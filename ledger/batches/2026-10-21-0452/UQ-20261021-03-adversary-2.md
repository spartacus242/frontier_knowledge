## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

A hospital stacks several checks on the path of one medication order: the ordering system fires (or does not fire) a decision-support alert that the prescriber accepts or overrides; a pharmacist verifies; a nurse scans a barcode at the bedside. Safety talk calls these layers, and layer talk invites multiplying their individual miss rates together to get the chance an error reaches the patient. The question is whether the misses are coupled — is a check more likely to miss an error precisely when the previous check already missed it — measured from the electronic trace each check leaves on the same order, expressed as a reliability-engineering coupling parameter, and converted into the factor by which the real all-barrier escape probability exceeds the multiply-the-miss-rates number. Two sub-asks matter for grading: the comparison must be against *that same cohort's* marginals, and the stratum of errors that got through everything and was never noticed must be bounded rather than ignored.
