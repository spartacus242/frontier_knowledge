# UQ-20261017-05 — Gate 4 adversary #1 log

## Restatement (my own words, before reading the record's framing closely)

Take physical quantities that have been measured many times (neutron lifetime, particle masses, coupling constants, etc.). For each published measurement, work out from the paper whether the analysis was done blind (and how), and compute how far the result sits from a trustworthy later value, in units of its own quoted error bar. Then ask: do blinded analyses' error bars match their actual misses better, worse, or the same as unblinded analyses' error bars, once you compare like with like (same quantity, similar year, similar precision)? If blinded and unblinded work are so segregated in time that you cannot compare like with like, say so instead.

The question is about the size/honesty of error bars, not about whether blinding shifts the central value.

## Prior spot-checks

- Henrion & Fischhoff 1986, Am. J. Phys. 54, 791-798, DOI 10.1119/1.14447: REAL, now READ in full (fetch 4). Content: Birge ratios for c (1.42; P<0.005 — this is Jeng's "1 in 200"), G 1.38, mu_p 1.44, alpha^-1 2.95/1.26, PDG particle data 1.24; calibration indices (41% within interquartile vs ideal 50%; 11% surprises outside 98% vs ideal 2%); recommended-values surprise index 57%; Birge ratio did NOT improve with era (1.47 pre-1941 vs 1.32 post-1947). Blinding appears ONLY as a suggested refereeing practice ("referees might be asked to scrutinize the account of an experiment 'blind,' before knowing its outcome"). Stratifies by era and by high/low accuracy, not by analysis protocol. Notably it says compiling data across more classes "can permit analysis of the importance of these factors" — i.e. the regress-calibration-on-covariates idea is stated, unexecuted, in 1986. Does NOT ask the candidate's question. Confirms the record's second-hand account.
- Croft & Dailey 2011 (arXiv:1112.3108): REAL, READ. 637 measurements of 12 cosmological parameters, 1990-2010, pulls against WMAP7 as a later high-precision reference; stratified by method (12 categories) and by era. Pre-2000 error bars underestimated by factor ~2 on average; post-2003 Omega_Lambda error bars look OVERestimated (chi2/point 22.7 with one 4-sigma outlier, 0.26 without — their numbers); sharp tightening after WMAP1 read as possible confirmation bias. Blinding appears once, as a remedy: "Wider use of blind analyses in cosmology could help to avoid this." NOT stratified by blinding. Record did not cite it.
- Bailey 2017 RSOS, Klein & Roodman 2005, Moustgaard 2020 BMJ, Rajan & Desai PTEP 2020, arXiv:2311.13542: all real; Klein & Roodman and 2311.13542 resurfaced in my queries 4 and 5 at the URLs the record gives. Not re-opened.

Provisional. Nothing Unresolved; attacks 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 Partial; novelty holds at Medium but no higher, with a second, closer design-neighbour (Croft & Dailey 2011) the record missed. Promotion conditions: (1) open Junk & Lyons, arXiv:2009.06864 (HDSR 2020) — surfaced by title only; (2) cite and differentiate Croft & Dailey 2011 and add cosmology to the frame; (3) replace the variance-multiplier c with a robust scale estimand as the primary, keep c as secondary; (4) add collaboration size to the declared controls; (5) produce a feasibility count of within-quantity, both-strata, blinding-ascertainable measurements post-2000 before claiming identifiability is likely.

Strongest killing point: Croft & Dailey 2011 already computed reference-based, era- and method-stratified error-bar calibration on 637 cosmological measurements, found a sign-changing era effect (under-estimation by ~2x pre-2000, over-estimation post-2003) coincident with the spread of blinding, and named blinding as the remedy — so the candidate is one covariate away from an existing paper, and the covariate it adds is the one most confounded with the era effect that paper documents.
