## Restatement (my own words, before reading the record's framing back)

Take normal, non-cancerous colon lining from living adults today who were born in different decades. In each person's colonic crypts, count the mutations carrying the two catalogued colibactin fingerprints (COSMIC SBS88 and ID18), keeping only the ones that sit early in the crypt lineage's history, i.e. the ones laid down in childhood. Does that count per crypt rise the later the donor was born? A rise would mean the childhood bacterial-genotoxin exposure that writes those mutations has been increasing generation by generation, making it a candidate carrier of the early-onset colorectal cancer surge; a flat profile would mean the epithelium's childhood mutational record did not change, and the surge has to be explained some other way.
