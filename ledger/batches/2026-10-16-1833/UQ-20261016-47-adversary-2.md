## Restatement (my own words)

A tokenizer is produced by running a frequency-driven fitting procedure (BPE/Unigram) over some slice of text. The candidate asks: treat that slice as a *sample*, not as a fixed recipe input. (a) If you draw k ≥ 5 independent same-size slices from one corpus and fit a tokenizer on each — same algorithm, same pre-tokenizer, same vocab size — how different are the resulting tokenizers (vocabulary overlap, merge-order agreement, and above all how differently they chop up held-out text, per domain), and how does that disagreement shrink as the slice grows? (b) If you then pretrain one model per slice on the *same* text, equalising bytes seen and FLOPs spent, how big is the spread in bits-per-byte and benchmark scores — bigger or smaller than (i) the spread you get from just changing the random seed with one fixed tokenizer, and (ii) the published gaps that the literature attributes to picking BPE over Unigram or 33k over 100k?

In one line: **does a tokenizer have a standard error, and if so is it big enough to swamp the effects papers currently report as design choices?**
