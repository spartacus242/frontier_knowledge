## Answers to the three questions

(1) YES. String A asserts compatibility ("consistent with") and selects nothing. As the paper lists them: on Bennu, (i) thermal fatigue (Molaro et al., 2020; Delbo et al., 2022; Lucchetti et al., 2024) or (ii) micrometeoroid impacts (Ballouz et al., 2020; Keller & Thompson et al., 2025); or (iii)-(iv) "earlier geologic processes within or on Bennu’s parent body, such as impacts or fluid reaction–driven volume changes". The count of four is a reading of the sentence's grammar — my own, labelled: two on-Bennu mechanisms named outright, plus one parent-body category illustrated by two examples. The "such as" leaves the parent-body category open-ended, so the paper's own list is not presented as exhaustive either. Bearing on the entry's leg 3 (the candidate's two-way disjunction is not exhaustive): the paper names two current-body mechanisms (thermal fatigue, micrometeoroid impacts) that the candidate's volume-change-versus-impact-shock frame has no slot for, and its "such as" adds an open end; the sentence supports leg 3 as the entry states it. Whether micrometeoroid impacts belong under the candidate's "impact shock" arm is a question about the candidate's definitions, not about the paper. The sentences around A (438-452, by opener and by F1/F4/F8) and C's paragraph select no mechanism; I did NOT read the Abstract or any Conclusions for a selection elsewhere, so "the paper selects none anywhere" is not a claim I can make — only that this sentence and its neighbours select none. The entry's attack-1 wording "lines 442-448 list four compatible mechanisms" is loose: the list is at 442-445; 445-448 is string B, which illustrates rather than lists.

(2) PARTLY. String B does propose a volume-change discriminant and does map it onto the two Bennu particle classes — hydration (volume-increasing) → polygonal patterns → angular stones; desiccation (volume-decreasing) → fractal or dendritic patterns → hummocky stones — citing Okamoto and Shimizu (2015) at the sentence's end. It does NOT propose the candidate's own discriminant. The candidate's discriminant separates volume-change cracking from impact-shock cracking by orientation isotropy, spacing against phyllosilicate-domain thickness, and crack area density rising with alteration grade. Ryan et al.'s sentence separates volume-INCREASING from volume-DECREASING reactions by pattern topology (polygonal versus fractal/dendritic); shock is nowhere in it (and nowhere in the document, F6); orientation, spacing and density are not in it. It is a discriminant within the volume-change family, on a different axis — as the record's P3 paragraph says and as VERIFY-01-a also noted. Force: "For example" makes the sentence one illustration of A's last-named mechanism, not a conclusion; the two "may have" modals make each half a possibility; nothing is tested or measured; the pattern words appear in no other sentence of the main text (F6), so the paper nowhere reports the polygonal/dendritic patterns as a Results finding — the sentence is a hedged proposal built on an embedded qualitative comparison. For the entry's wording: "a volume-change-geometry discriminant proposed and already mapped onto the two Bennu particle classes" (Strongest remaining objection) is accurate; "Prior 1 POSES, PROPOSES and PREDICTS the candidate's discriminant" (Novelty — how this differs) overstates, since the proposed discriminant is not the candidate's. The brief's "the candidate's own volume-change discriminant" inherits the overstatement.

(3) CONFIRMED on both points, and NO. "This observation suggests that" is the sentence's opener and scopes the claim to the single observation in S2 — the clast comparison between angular and hummocky particles — with "suggests" as the hedge. The trailing clause names the rejected alternative as "the presence or absence of putative clasts": clasts, not shock. "shock" occurs nowhere in the document (F6, my dedicated sweep; the record's and adversary 2's sweeps agree), and "impact(s)" occurs nowhere in lines 449-477 (F6). The sentence can support no claim about alteration grade versus shock: (a) its contrast is alteration regime versus clasts, for the DIFFERENCE in cracking between the two particle classes, not the mechanism that opened the cracks, which the paper leaves open at line 430 and in string A; (b) "regime" is categorical — two alteration regimes (~465) — not an ordinal grade, so even "alteration grade" is a step beyond the sentence's words (my reading of "regime", labelled); (c) "primary control on crack development", read with S2's "clasts strongly influences crack propagation", concerns what governed how cracks developed, not which external process drove them. The entry already treats this correctly in P2 ("Ryan et al. only suggest maps to alteration regime") and in "asserts a hedged alteration control".
