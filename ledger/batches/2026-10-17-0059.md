## What verification found

- **UQ-01: the kill is refuted, and the entry is rejected anyway on better ground.** Ambroz et al.
  (Small Methods 2018) contains no pore-size-dependent sign change in the habitual-minus-criteria BET
  difference. Its one directional statement runs the *other* way — for sub-7 Å IRMOFs the criteria
  values "would be higher" than standard-range ones — and the word "underestimate" never appears; the
  "overestimated" sentences are about micropore pore filling and NU-1103/1104's inflated monolayer
  loading, a different mechanism. Figure 5, which holds the numbers, could not be read through any
  route. Novelty moved the other way too: Osterrieth et al. (Adv. Mater. 2022) is a round robin on 18
  **supplied** isotherms sent to 61 labs, and compares participants with each other, never a published
  paper's own reported area against a recomputation from its own isotherm. So a fourth adversary ran
  cold with those facts handed over, completed eight new Disguise vocabularies (finding, again, no
  prior), and returned Unresolved on attack 4 for a reason nobody had reached: the NIST/ARPA-E ISODB
  has no reported-BET-area field, so the frame must be hand-harvested paper by paper; nothing shows
  the record-holders' raw isotherms are deposited anywhere; and Farha et al. (JACS 2012;134:15016,
  NU-110E at 7140 m²/g) says its areas are "subject to well-defined 'consistency criteria'" — so a
  near-zero offset is the *expected* result exactly in the stratum carrying the whole hypothesis.
  Attack 7 concurs: consistency criteria for MOFs date from 2006–2007, so "the tools are too recent"
  does not explain the silence.
- **UQ-02: the kill survives, but not on the legs it was argued from.** Of the three documents cited,
  CCRI/12-05 confirms persistence-until-change ("This presumes that there have been no significant
  changes in staff, equipment or measurement methods") and supersession ("the most recent replacing
  any earlier participation") — but it **forbids** the pooling the adversary attributed to it ("only
  one of these should be quoted"; "there is no averaging of results over several qualities"). Pooling
  appears only in CCQM IAWG-GD-01, as a participation record. And the 2016 MRA Review Recommendations
  address none of it — the words supersede, replace, valid and validity do not occur in the text — so
  citing it was an error and it is struck. The finding neither side had: **no document models
  persistence at all.** Nothing quantifies how a result persists, how an old one loses weight, or what
  cross-measurand extrapolation costs; validity is set by fixed time windows. So limbs (i)–(iii) are
  genuinely unmeasured, and the rejection now rests on limb (iv) alone — the degrees of equivalence do
  not feed the inherited uncertainty at all, so there is no quantity for a per-comparison treatment to
  leave out. The "asked and answered in policy" characterisation is struck: a presupposed persistence
  nobody has measured is not an answered question.
- **UQ-03: "asked and answered" becomes "asked and explicitly not answered".** Odrzywolek
  (arXiv:2002.12690) does carry "Tested unique formulas count must not exceed 1/sigma" verbatim — and
  then declines the candidate's question outright: "Above criterion do not provide any numerical
  estimate for probability of successful identification", with the equivalence problem "undecidable in
  general". The Ramanujan Machine's 10⁻⁴⁰ is not an acceptance threshold but a random-match probability
  for one case at a 10⁹ space and >50 digits. The Ramanujan Library's cutoff is empirical and hedged
  ("an RoI of 2 *seems* to comfortably separate random results from false positives"). The rejection
  therefore stands on triviality instead: at the precisions actually used — "tested numerically to 100
  digits", up to 2000 — the nominal rate is so far below anything observable in a corpus this size
  that the comparison is decided by the setup, and no engine reports a disproved conjecture.
