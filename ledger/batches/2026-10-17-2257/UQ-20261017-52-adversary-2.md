## Restatement (my own words)

Take a set of genes. Remove each gene's product two ways in the same cells: (a) tear the protein down in minutes-to-hours with an inducible degron, (b) destroy the gene with CRISPR so the protein fades away over several divisions. Read cell fitness and apply a threshold declared in advance. How often do the two methods disagree about whether the gene is "essential"? And — the part the candidate treats as decisive — once you have *measured* how much target protein is actually left in each arm at the moment you read fitness, and compare only arms matched on that residual level, does the leftover disagreement line up with how slowly the protein normally turns over (half-life), or with the cell switching on backup transcripts (transcriptional adaptation, which El-Brolosy et al. showed is triggered by decay of the mutant mRNA, not by loss of protein), or with neither? Underneath: when a map says a gene is essential, is that a fact about the gene or a fact about how fast the experiment removed its product?

## Pages opened (3 fetches)

- **P1 verified, publisher route**: https://rupress.org/jcb/article/224/2/e202409007/277169 — real, budding yeast, and the record's quotations are accurate: "fitness defects were observed for over 55% of essential ORFs (499 out of 906)"; "Whereas degradation resulted in a fitness defect for over 61% of essential proteins"; "Interestingly, the degradation of 38% of essential proteins did not result in a fitness defect." Explanations offered: "First, it is possible that in some cases degradation is incomplete, resulting in low protein levels" and "This suggests that conditional essentiality could explain the observed lack of fitness defects". **Correction to the record:** P1 does *not* raise protein half-life/turnover, and does not analyse transcriptional compensation, in relation to its discordance — the record's claim that "P1 and P2 each raise incomplete degradation / half-life / conditional essentiality" overstates P1. (My own arithmetic, labelled as such: 906 − 499 = 407, i.e. 44.9% of essential ORFs without a defect at the ORF denominator, versus the paper's stated 38% over proteins with detectable signal — so the published headline fraction is itself denominator-sensitive, ~38-45%.)
- **The record's COULD NOT READ paper now READ, via the DOI route the record did not try**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-33246-4 (Nat Commun 2022, "Systematic profiling of conditional degron tag technologies for target validation studies"). 16 POIs, ectopic POI-CDT fusions in HEK-293T. It is a technology benchmark, not a matched essentiality panel: 16 ectopic fusions, no fitness essentiality calls, no discordance rate.
