Question (canonical, V0 as supplied): For systems carrying two or more independently published CALPHAD assessments, does the ordering of those assessments by their own reported fit to the experimental data survive leave-one-study-out predictive evaluation on a common digitised experimental corpus, and how large is the gap between apparent fit residual and predictive residual?

Question (canonical, R1 — THE ONE PERMITTED GATE 1 REFORMULATION, ADOPTED): For a declared frame of systems each carrying two or more independently published CALPHAD assessments whose full parameter sets are retrievable, and on one commonly digitised experimental corpus with every datum attributed to its source study: (a) what is the Kendall tau between the ordering of the competing assessments by their APPARENT residual (recomputed on the pooled corpus under one pre-declared common metric, since the assessments' own reported fit is in general not a published number — see Gate 1) and their ordering by leave-one-study-out predictive residual under that same metric; (b) what is the distribution of the predictive-minus-apparent residual gap, in the metric's units and as a ratio; and (c) in how many systems, and which, does the top-ranked assessment change?

WHY R1, AND WHAT R1 ITSELF PRESUPPOSES (repair tested as hard as the clause it replaced): V0 orders assessments "by their own reported fit". One instance of the document kind was opened (Wang, Hu, Lucas & Latypov, arXiv:2601.11899, read in full) and it prints NO fit statistic at all — "Overall, we observe good agreement between our calculations and data reported in the literature", with the optimisation run in Pandat's PanOptimizer and no chi-square, weighted sum of squares or RMS deviation printed anywhere. If reported fit is not a number, the V0 ordering it asks about does not exist, and V0 is ill-posed as stated. R1 replaces it with a recomputed apparent residual and declares the recomputation part of the protocol. R1 now presupposes, in its own right: (i) that one common metric can be fixed over heterogeneous property types (invariant temperatures, activities, enthalpies of mixing, heat capacities) without the choice of weighting deciding the ordering — a weighting-sensitivity analysis therefore has to be inside the protocol, not after it; (ii) that each assessment's parameter set is complete enough to evaluate, which in the one instance opened is true only after chasing cited sub-system parameters (Table 7's Ga-La binary parameters are "adopted from Ref. [12]" and SGTE pure-element functions are not reproduced); and (iii) that study-level inclusion is recoverable, on which see presupposition P2 below. Both versions are recorded; no second reformulation is taken.

Alternative formulations: (1) Do competing thermodynamic descriptions of the same alloy system change places when they are scored on experimental studies they were not fitted to? (2) What is the out-of-sample penalty of a published CALPHAD assessment — the free-R analogue of a thermodynamic description — and does it reorder the assessments of a system relative to their apparent agreement?

Motivating gap: Cross-field transplant. Cross-validation has entered CALPHAD for a DIFFERENT object (dataset weighting inside one fitting loop), and the field's own "how far can I trust this description" question has been routed to a STRUCTURAL surrogate rather than a held-out residual: the credibility of a prediction is indexed by how many of the constituent subsystems are fully assessed and how far the composition sits from them (the FAB/FAT credibility criteria attributed to Senkov et al. and used in the Entropy 2018 reliability paper read for this record). No source opened here scores two or more independently published assessments of one system against experimental studies withheld from the fit, and no source opened here prints an apparent-minus-predictive residual gap or a rank correlation between fit-based and prediction-based orderings. The gap is therefore not virgin ground — the parent question "how reliable are CALPHAD predictions outside the assessed region" is asked, and partly answered by surrogate — but the candidate's two estimands are, on this record, uncomputed.

P4 — PRIMARY CONTESTED PRESUPPOSITION. A predictive residual is meaningful for a thermodynamically self-consistent model: a Gibbs-energy description is not a free-form regressor, so held-out data of the SAME property in the SAME composition region may be near-determined by the model form, making a same-property hold-out trivially satisfied. The protocol must declare its hold-out structure and argue it is not trivial; the informative structures are cross-PROPERTY (fit calorimetry, predict phase boundaries) and cross-COMPOSITION-REGION (fit binaries and one ternary region, predict another). Unresolved here and carried forward.

NOT presupposed: that any reordering implies an assessment is wrong; that newer assessments are better; that a single metric is the right one (weighting sensitivity is inside the protocol).

What would count as an answer: For each system in the declared frame, a table of (assessment identifier, apparent residual on the common pooled corpus under the declared common metric, leave-one-study-out predictive residual under the same metric), plus across the frame: (1) Kendall tau between the apparent and predictive orderings, with a bootstrap interval over systems and over hold-out folds; (2) the distribution of the predictive-minus-apparent gap, in the metric's units and as a ratio; (3) the count and identity of systems whose top-ranked assessment changes; (4) a weighting-sensitivity panel showing the tau under at least two pre-declared weighting schemes, since R1 presupposes the metric choice is not what decides the ordering. Significance — consequence: If tau is well below 1 and the gap is large, then apparent agreement — which in the one assessment opened here is not even a number, only the sentence "Overall, we observe good agreement between our calculations and data reported in the literature" — is not a valid selector between competing descriptions, and three things follow: database curation acquires a held-out scoring step, assessment papers acquire a reportable out-of-sample statistic the way a crystal structure acquires a free R, and the field's structural credibility surrogate (FAB/FAT subsystem counts, used in PRIOR 2) gains a measured competitor against which it can itself be scored. If tau is indistinguishable from 1 with a small gap, the answer is equally consequential in the other direction: apparent fit is vindicated as a cheap sufficient selector, the free-R analogue is shown to be unnecessary for this model class, and P4's suspicion — that a thermodynamically self-consistent description is so constrained by its functional form that same-property hold-out is near-determined — becomes a measured fact about the model class rather than a worry.
Significance — audience: Four communities, each with a documented instrument. (i) Database curators and assessment referees: PRIOR 3 shows a group collecting published assessments and concluding that "necessary changes in the databases are pointed out", so the act of choosing between descriptions is performed and published; a held-out statistic would enter exactly there. (ii) The SGTE and commercial database maintainers, whose selection practice has a written methodology page (Thermo-Calc's "assessment of thermodynamic data", returned by Q9 and NOT opened — the audience claim is therefore partly unevidenced and this is the specific document owed). (iii) The ESPEI / Bayesian-UQ line (Q7: Cu-Mg ESPEI paper, Bayesian UQ and information fusion, pure elements from 0 K with UQ), who already produce parameter posteriors and lack a published model-comparison criterion for competing descriptions; they are also the group most likely to have already done part of this, which is why those four unopened papers matter in both directions. (iv) ICME users, who inherit whichever description a database ships and currently have a structural proxy (how many subsystems are assessed, how far the composition sits from them) rather than a measured out-of-sample penalty.
