# C4 — Adversary 2 (harsh pass)

## Restatement (my own words)

Take every external URL ever cited in English Wikipedia, note the article's topic, the year the URL was added, and the kind of site it points to. Does the article's topic still predict how fast the URL dies once the year and the site type are held fixed? If yes, which topics? Secondary: same thing for "dead and no archive copy exists."

Put bluntly: this is the 2021 CJR/Zittrain New York Times section-by-section link-rot design re-run on Wikipedia with a survival model, plus a correction for the fact that Wikipedia editors and bots repair links (which the NYT does not).

## Attacks

| # | Attack | Objection | Best response | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Triviality | The answer is predictable from CJR: topic gaps are "in large part" age and source mix, so a page of reasoning gives "mostly no residual topic effect." | Prediction is not an answer. No Wikipedia topic-stratified rot figure exists in Tzekou 2011, IMC'22, Pew 2024, Baigutanova 2023 (all checked directly), and CJR itself never reports fully age-adjusted section effects, so even the NYT analogue is unsettled. The Wikipedia-specific repair process makes transfer non-obvious. | **Resolved** |
| 2 | Disguise | Rename it as "reference persistence by topic" (Wikimedia research vocabulary), "archive coverage by WikiProject" (web archiving), "maintenance-template prevalence by ORES topic" (Wikimedia tooling), or "source importance by topic" (Lewoniewski). Any of these may already contain the table. | 26 new queries across those vocabularies found nothing stratified. Nearest hits are Baigutanova 2023 (topic breakdown, but of citation need/risk, not liveness) and Lewoniewski's 70-topic reference study (importance/density, not liveness). Weakness: the Research Fund grant "Improving the Persistence of External References on Wikipedia" and the 2025 Signpost/Research Newsletter issues could not be opened; the grant is plausibly a follow-up by the IMC'22 group and could contain topic analysis. | **Partial** |
| 3 | Combinatorial novelty | It is exactly CJR's design (topic x age x domain type) with Wikipedia as corpus. The record admits "same design... different corpus" and "the step to Wikipedia is a transplant." The claimed interface problem — editor/bot repair censoring observed decay — is a nuisance parameter for the estimator, not a question anyone is asking. The question as posed is about URL hazard, which is a property of the cited web, not of Wikipedia; Wikipedia is just the sampling frame. | The Wikipedia-specific content is (a) censoring by repair, (b) the "dead and unarchived" outcome, which depends on IABot behaviour, and (c) encyclopedic topics absent from journalism (sport, pop culture, local history). But (a) is a method correction, (b) is a secondary outcome, and (c) is a different sample of the same design. The record does not formulate the genuinely interfacial question — the maintenance-vs-hazard race by topic — as the primary question; it lists it under Fecundity. | **Unresolved** |
| 4 | Presupposition failure | "Topic affects URL death after conditioning on domain type" presupposes topic is a causal unit for URL death. Topic can only act through source characteristics; a residual topic effect just measures how coarse the domain-type taxonomy is. Second presupposition: that archiving is "topic-blind and capacity-limited" so topic hazard would change allocation; in fact IABot/Wayback save-at-citation already archives essentially all new citations regardless of topic. | Presupposition 5 is explicitly flagged as the thing being tested, and alt formulation 2 states the proxy worry outright; a well-defined domain taxonomy (type x popularity) makes the residual interpretable as "source-level ephemerality not captured by type," which is still informative. The archiving presupposition is not stated in Gate 1 but is load-bearing in Gate 3. | **Partial** |
| 5 | Vagueness | Taxonomy choice (64 ORES topics vs ~2,000 WikiProjects, multi-label) can manufacture or erase effects; with millions of citation events every coefficient is "significant"; the HR >= 1.3 threshold is arbitrary. | A pre-registered taxonomy and an effect-size threshold are stated; either outcome is specified; "dead" is operationalised with known noise sources listed. This is as sharp as observational questions get. | **Resolved** |
| 6 | Significance inflation | The actionable consequence ("prioritise archiving by topic hazard") assumes archiving is rationed. It is not: Wayback save-at-citation plus IABot plus TARB (>30M links fixed) archive prospectively and topic-blind at near-zero marginal cost. The "no" branch ("target source types") is already current practice (IABot archives everything). So neither outcome changes tooling. Audience exists, but would shrug. | Topic hazard still matters for the pre-2016 backlog, for sources that defeat archiving (robots.txt, paywalls, soft-404s), and for the "unrecoverable" outcome; and it is a reportable verifiability-debt metric for WikiProjects. Researchers using Wikipedia citations as data would care about coverage bias. Plausible but unevidenced that any named audience has asked for it. | **Partial** |
| 7 | Reason for silence | "Remediation crowded out diagnosis" is plausible but incomplete: Wikipedia data is far more open than the NYT's, the Wikipedia Citations dataset (2020) and the reference-history dataset (2023) exist, and a Wiki Workshop student could do a crude version in a week. The more likely reason is that insiders expect the answer to be "it is source mix," so it is not worth asking — which makes the novelty a low-value gap, not a frontier. | Evidence does fit the remediation story (Community Tech project, IABot/TARB counts, Robust Links talk, all engineering). The disciplinary split (scientometrics studies journals, archivists study generic URLs, Wikimedia Research studies reference quality) is confirmed by my searches. But the "anticipated answer" counter-mechanism is not addressed by the record. | **Partial** |
| 8 | Dissolution | Under analysis "topic" dissolves into "source mix at finer granularity": refine domain type enough and the topic coefficient goes to zero by construction. The question then becomes "how coarse is your domain-type variable," which is a methods choice. | The record pre-empts this: a "no" result is declared informative (topic is the wrong unit; target source types). But that result is only weakly informative because it is already the operating assumption of every remediation tool. The question does not fully dissolve — a "yes" with a good taxonomy is a real finding — but a "no" nearly does. | **Partial** |

## Recommended verdict

No Unresolved on attacks 1, 2 or 4, so not Rejected. Unresolved on attack 3 (Combinatorial novelty: the primary question is CJR's NYT design transplanted to Wikipedia, with the Wikipedia-specific element demoted to a nuisance correction and a Fecundity bullet) → **Provisional at best**.

Conditions before any upgrade:
1. Someone with Meta-Wiki access must read the Research Fund grant "Improving the Persistence of External References on Wikipedia" (FY2022-23) and its outputs, and the 2025 Signpost "Recent research" / Research:Newsletter issues that the search surfaced. If any of them contains topic- or WikiProject-stratified dead-link rates, novelty collapses and the candidate is Rejected.
2. Reframe the primary question around the Wikipedia-specific interface: does editor/bot repair rate by topic keep pace with URL hazard by topic (the maintenance-vs-hazard race), with the CJR-style hazard model as the input rather than the output. That is a question no NYT study could pose and no remediation tool answers.
3. Replace the Gate 3 archiving-allocation consequence, which presupposes rationed archiving, with one that survives save-at-citation (e.g. topic-level unrecoverable loss in the pre-2016 backlog).
