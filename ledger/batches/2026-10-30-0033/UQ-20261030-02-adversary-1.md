# UQ-20261030-02 — Gate 4, Adversary #1

## Restatement (my own words, written before reading the attacks)

Take a human cell line that has been engineered so that exactly one copy of a tagged, switchable LINE-1 element sits at a known address on a known chromosome. Switch it on, let it jump, and map where the new copies land — without using the tag's own expression to find them. Now ask: after you have accounted for where the ORF2p endonuclease likes to nick, how open the chromatin is, when the region replicates and what its base composition is, does the number of new insertions falling in a given 100-kb stretch of genome still go up with how often that stretch physically touches the donor's address in 3D nuclear space? Take the headline estimate on the other chromosomes, where there is no linear distance to confound it. Then rebuild the line with the donor parked in the opposite A/B compartment and see whether the coefficient follows the donor. In short: does a jumping gene land near where it was made, or does it forget where it came from?

## The source my kill rests on

**Dooner HK, Belachew A, Burgess D, Harding S, Ralston M, Ralston E. "Distribution of unlinked receptor sites for transposed Ac elements from the bz-m2(Ac) allele in maize." Genetics 1994;136(1):261–279. DOI 10.1093/genetics/136.1.261.**
**READ LEVEL: ABSTRACT ONLY.** The OUP page says "This content is only available as a PDF" and the full text is paywalled; I did not reach the Results or any table. Author names are taken from the OUP front matter as served, not from a short form. Per this ledger's own rule, *an abstract-only read cannot carry a kill*, so nothing below is graded Unresolved on it.

**REGIME:** population = maize plants carrying the *bz-m2(Ac)* allele on chromosome 9S; unit of analysis = one transposed Ac (trAc) element, mapped by genetic recombination against markers; operating condition = somatic/germinal Ac excision from a single mapped chromosomal donor allele in planta; instrument = genetic linkage mapping, not sequencing and not any contact map; threshold = genetic linkage to *bz*, plus chromosome-arm assignment; category = donor–receptor site linkage / "physical association". **THE CANDIDATE IS OUTSIDE THIS REGIME.**

Quotations — each is a single short fragment as served by the summariser under its ~125-character cap. I did NOT assemble a multi-fragment reconstruction, and I did not verify any of them against the PDF:

- "transposes preferentially to sites that are linked to the *bz* donor locus" — ASSERTS the quantity (a donor-proximity preference).
- "about half of the Ac transpositions recovered from bz-m2(Ac) are in receptor sites not linked to the donor locus" — ASSERTS a complementary share; no numerator/denominator printed in the abstract for this one.
- "Ten trAcs mapped to 9L, i.e., Ac had transposed to sites physically, if not genetically, linked to the donor site." — number named, with its denominator stated separately in the abstract as 37 trAc elements mapped. **10/37** is the only donor-proximity fraction I can name with a printed location, and its location is the abstract, not a table I opened.
- "We found that Ac rarely transposes from bz-m2(Ac) to the homologous chromosome 9." — this clause **RECORDS A MOVE AWAY** from the naive 3D-proximity prediction: the homolog is the most donor-proximal DNA in a paired nucleus and is disfavoured. I flag it because quoting only the first two fragments would book this paper as a point in the candidate's favour when it is partly a point against.
- "has been taken to mean that a physical association between the donor site and nearby receptor sites occurs during transposition" — **SCOPE WARNING:** the opening words "has been taken to mean" attribute this interpretation to the prior literature, hedged, and it is the authors reporting a received model, not their own measurement. This is the hedged formulation and it is the one I quote.

What this does and does not establish. It establishes that the candidate's **hypothesis** — that a mobile element's insertion site is set in part by physical proximity to the locus that produced it — was stated in the literature, in those words, **32 years ago**, with a mapped single-copy chromosomal donor and a quantified linkage statistic. It does not establish any value of the candidate's headline quantity: there is no contact frequency, no observed/expected normalisation, no regression coefficient, no interval, no trans estimate that is not simply "unlinked", no 100-kb bin, no compartment relocation, and no retroelement.

Overlap in both directions, as required. Dooner's numerator class **MISSES** everything the candidate centrally includes: an element with an RNA intermediate and a cytoplasmic RNP stage, human cells, contact-frequency as the predictor, 100-kb resolution, a trans estimate distinguished from mere non-linkage, and a donor relocated between compartments. It **INCLUDES** what the candidate excludes: cut-and-paste excision, in which the donor DNA and the receptor DNA can be physically held together by transposase on contiguous or nearby DNA — a mechanism with no L1 analogue, because L1's intermediate leaves the nucleus as RNA. Its denominator (37 trAcs that recombined independently of *bz*) is a genetic-recombination-selected set, not all transposition events; the candidate's denominator is all recovered insertions genome-wide.

## Promotion condition, and the document-kind justification for it

What kind of document could settle the open novelty question: it must (a) cover engineered de-novo L1 insertions in human cells mapped genome-wide in large numbers, (b) vary or at least report the donor's chromosomal position, and (c) PRINT either a per-chromosome or cis-versus-trans insertion distribution relative to the donor's own chromosome. Flasch et al. 2019 is that kind: it is the field's large genome-wide de-novo engineered-insertion study, its title announces genome-wide insertion mapping, and such papers conventionally print a per-chromosome insertion distribution. I am reasonably confident on (a) and (c) from the title and the journal; I am **not** confident on (b), because I never read its Methods and therefore do not know whether its donor was episomal — if it was, it is NOT the kind of document that can settle this, for exactly the reason the record gives about P1. Say so rather than dress it as a lead: this nomination is justified by kind on two of three criteria and is a guess on the third. Sultana et al. 2019 Mol Cell is a second candidate of the same kind. Neither has been opened by anyone in this pipeline.
