Question (canonical): In dynamic total-body PET (axial FOV >= ~1.9 m) of N subjects per tracer, for a pre-specified tracer set, does the closure residual R(t) = A_inj(t) − SUM_organs Ahat_organ(t | thetahat) − E(t) − B(t) lie within its propagated uncertainty, where thetahat are per-organ compartmental parameters from the conventional independent per-organ fit, A_inj(t) is decay-corrected injected activity, E(t) is measured excreted/left-FOV activity and B(t) is a pre-registered BOUNDED (not freely fitted) non-segmentable remainder; and for each tracer where R(t) is significantly non-zero, which organ's parameters move when the fit is redone under a global conservation constraint, in which direction, and by how much in Ki, Vt, K1 and organ absorbed dose?
  (This is V2, the one Gate 1 reformulation. V1 as received: "When organ-level compartmental models are fitted independently per organ in the conventional way, do the fitted parameters reproduce the whole-body tracer mass balance that a single total-body PET acquisition now measures directly, and where they do not, which organs' published kinetic parameters are biased and in which direction?" V2 fixes the estimand, the uncertainty denominator, and forbids a free remainder; no scope change.)
Alternative formulations:
- Does total-body PET's simultaneous view of every organ falsify the independence assumption behind decades of single-organ PET kinetic modelling?
- Is injected activity conserved across independently fitted organ compartmental models, and how large is the closure residual by tracer and by time after injection?
- (Metrology register) What is the misclosure of the whole-body activity balance implied by conventional per-organ PET kinetic fits, and which organ's parameters does a constrained adjustment move?
Motivating gap: Total-body scanners (~2 m axial FOV, uEXPLORER class, from 2019) record every organ's TAC and the input function in one dynamic acquisition, so the tracer's whole-body balance is observable while organ kinetics are being fitted. The per-organ independent-fit convention is a fossil of the 15-25 cm FOV and has been carried into total-body data unchanged — three opened instances: Chalampalakis 2024 (eight organs, independent Patlak, never summed), vPET-ABC 2026 (~4.4M voxels fitted independently), Rainio/Knuuti/Klen 2025 (20 structures fitted separately, a joint multi-structure model named only as future work). Internal dosimetry does hold the conservation concept but computes MIRD remainder-of-body as injected minus measured organs, zeroing the residual by construction. Drug development must demonstrate radiolabeled dose recovery; PET never has.
Presuppositions: (1) LAFOV quantification accurate enough at organ level to close to a few percent, incl. FOV extremes — CONTESTED, and undermined by an opened source (Chalampalakis: early-frame underestimation from a fixed 0.75 scatter factor; organ Ki −12%/−16% at 25%/10% counts). (2) Organ volumes segmentable with bounded, propagatable error. (3) The non-segmentable remainder can be BOUNDED rather than freely fitted — KEY DESIGN THREAT, handled by pre-registration; a wide bound makes the test underpowered. (4) Excreted/left-FOV activity accounted for within the window. (5) Decay, dead-time, randoms corrections exact enough not to swamp R(t). (6) The imaged quantity is conserved (labelled metabolites imaged identically or measured) — CONTESTED, tracer-specific; V2 inherits a per-tracer restatement requirement. (7) Independent per-organ fitting is the live convention for total-body data — supported by three opened instances, not by a methods review. (8) Published Ki/Vt/K1 feed decisions at a precision where closure-sized bias matters — only partly established; weakest significance limb. (9) UNFLAGGED BY THE RECORD, added by adversary 2: that a non-zero R(t) is attributable to organ KINETIC parameters. The record supplies no identification argument for this. See strongest objection.
What would count as an answer: Per tracer, in dynamic total-body scans of N subjects, compute R(t) as defined, with uncertainty propagated from segmentation, scanner calibration and input-function error. Answered by (a) the distribution of |R(t)| relative to that uncertainty, per tracer and time window, and (b) for each tracer with R(t) significantly non-zero, which organ's parameters move under a global conservation constraint and by how much (% shift in Ki, Vt, K1, organ absorbed dose). A null — closure within propagated uncertainty for every tracer — is a real result: it licenses the independent-fit convention with evidence for the first time and yields an organ-level bound on LAFOV quantitative accuracy as a by-product.

(i) THE GATING ITEM IS SETTLED, AGAINST THE CANDIDATE. PMC10881693 is Smith et al., "Mitigating SUV uncertainties using total body PET imaging", Eur J Nucl Med Mol Imaging 51(4):1070-1078 (2024), reached on the first publisher attempt at https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-023-06503-x, and it performs limb (a). Its Methods construct the quantity by "counting the total image activity in all voxels" "while applying a correction for radioactive decay toward injection time"; its Eq. 1 sets "ActIMG is the total image-derived 18F-FDG activity (MBq)" against "ActINJ is the reported net injected activity (MBq)"; and its Discussion reports the result: "We found that TB image-derived 18F-FDG activity underestimated reported injected activity by 14%". The mass-balance character is confirmed by the physical account it gives of the shortfall - "Approximately 10% can probably be attributed to activity loss due to emptying the bladder before scanning", with the residual assigned to calibration ("Discrepancies in scanner calibration might explain the resulting percentage difference"). That is a whole-subject sum of measured activity, decay-corrected to injection time, set against the administered dose, with the discrepancy quantified and physically attributed. The record's own pre-registered condition therefore fires: novelty falls to Low and the verdict is Rejected, not Provisional.
(ii) Three qualifications travel with that YES, and none of them saves the candidate. The direction of use is inverted - the paper's aim is image-based QC, deriving injected activity from the image to substitute for an erroneous recorded value, so the dose comparison is the calibration step rather than a validation of the image against the dose. It is a cohort-level regression (a slope fitted on 25 scans, validated on 56 independent scans, 3 phantoms), not a per-subject recovery fraction. And the whole-subject sum holds for the total-body arm only; the half-body arm covers "skull vertex to mid-thigh", which is why its equations carry an extra patient-length term. Limb (a)'s measurement nonetheless exists, sits in the candidate's own journal family, and has a number.
(iii) The number must be carried in the right direction. 14% is ActINJ/ActIMG - 1: the reported dose sits 14% ABOVE the image total, so the image total sits 12.3% below the dose (1/1.14 = 0.877), i.e. the image recovered 87.7% of it, not 86%. The abstract's 4.8% is a post-calibration residual agreement on the 56 validation scans after the fitted coefficients are applied (4.8%/3.2% TB, 4.9%/3.1% HB, 6.7%/4.5% SAFOV), not the raw shortfall; conflating the two understates the raw discrepancy roughly threefold.
