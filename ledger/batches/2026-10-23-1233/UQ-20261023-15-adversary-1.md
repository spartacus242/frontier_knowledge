## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Take a pre-named population of infrastructure units from a register that records, per unit, when it entered service, when it left, and why (or lets you rebuild those). (i) Fit the realized time-in-service as a survival distribution, handling units whose start predates the register and units still standing, and report median, quartiles and the fraction reaching the code's declared design working life. (ii) Split the exits by a rule — "would more design resistance have prevented this exit?" yes/no/can't tell — and report shares plus how well two adjudicators agree. (iii) Take the code's target reliability, which is stated over a nominal reference period, and recompute the implied lifetime failure probability over the realized life distribution instead; report the change in reliability index, and in parallel report the figure under the reading that the reference period is only a load-statistics normalising convention (where the change should be zero).

The record's own triviality guard says (i) is routine and stakes the candidate on (ii) and (iii). So the kill test is: are (ii) and (iii) asked-and-answered somewhere the record did not look, in the candidate's own population?
