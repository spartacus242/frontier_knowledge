Question (canonical) — V2 after the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation: For macromolecular complexes whose ligand- or nucleotide-bound states are resolvable and countable in situ by cryo-electron tomography, and whose state is a reversible binding equilibrium on the timescale of the measurement (driven cycles admitted only with a declared steady-state rather than equilibrium null), does the measured in-cell occupancy of each bound state match the occupancy predicted from in vitro dissociation constants and the measured or bounded intracellular FREE ligand concentration, and what is the sign and magnitude of the signed log-ratio discrepancy across complexes?

Alternative formulations:
- Do in-cell structural state censuses agree with occupancies predicted from in vitro dissociation constants and cellular free-ligand concentrations?
- How wrong is the biochemical prediction of binding-state occupancy inside a living cell, when occupancy is counted structurally rather than inferred from an assay?
(V1 as intaked, and the reason for the reformulation — V1 rested feasibility on a GTP-consuming elongation cycle while excluding driven cycles — in batches/2026-10-17-2257/UQ-20261017-54-record.md.)

Motivating gap:
Subtomogram classification has made "fraction of complex X in state S inside an intact cell" a directly counted quantity (Xue et al.: 101,696 ribosomes, 356 tomograms; elongation classes 70% of localized ribosomes; 72%/67.5%/59.5% trapped under three drugs; polysomes 26.2%). Cell-scale kinetic models are parameterised from in vitro Kd on a single free pool. The record found no source using an in-situ census as a quantitative test of that transfer; the three terms the test needs (free ligand, classification error, vitrification fidelity) are each named in the literature and none assembled.

Presuppositions (8; full list in the record):
1. In-situ classification yields state fractions with a quantifiable error rate — CONTESTED (Evans et al. 2025: class-count populations "should not be used as quantitative evidence of the thermodynamics"; Xue et al. report no misclassification rate).
2. Intracellular FREE ligand measurable or boundable in the same cells — CONTESTED and binding (Xue et al.: "the exact concentrations of antibiotics inside cells cannot be determined").
3. Vitrification preserves the state distribution — CONTESTED, partly argued.
4. In vitro Kd exists for the same complex and ligand — established only case-by-case; adversary #1 verified that of the record's own three ligand cases only chloramphenicol supplies a Kd (Spt has a saturating concentration only; the "puromycin-like" IC50 is pseudouridimycin's, an RNA-polymerase number — two material corrections to the record).
5. The declared null is the appropriate reference — declared AS the object of test.
6-7. Counting statistics and physiological-state reproducibility — FLAGGED.
8. Driven cycles out of scope absent a declared steady-state null — SCOPE RESTRICTION, now in the canonical wording.

What would count as an answer:
For >= 3 complexes each with (a) an in-situ census with counting statistics, (b) an in vitro Kd under stated conditions, (c) a measured or bounded intracellular free-ligand concentration: a table of signed log(observed/predicted) occupancy with propagated uncertainty; a test of shared sign and magnitude versus complex-specificity; an error budget separating classification, free-concentration and affinity-transfer error.
