Domain(s): Medicine — critical care and anaesthesiology; medical-device validation and regulatory science. Adjacent: biomedical signal processing (catheter-transducer dynamics, waveform quality classification); measurement-comparison statistics / metrology.

## Question (canonical)

**v1 (intake, retained for the record):** In validation studies that certify noninvasive or continuous blood-pressure devices against an intra-arterial reference, what share of the error attributed to the device under test is contributed by the reference line's own dynamic-response distortion, and does any device's pass/fail verdict under the applicable standard change when the reference is restricted to arterial systems shown to be non-resonant?

**v2 (Gate 1 reformulation — the single permitted one, made only for well-posedness; this is the version graded):** For continuous or intermittent noninvasive blood-pressure devices evaluated against a fluid-filled intra-arterial reference under a named standard (ISO 81060-3:2022 for continuous automated measurement; its per-class analogues declared per dataset), restricted to datasets with retrievable per-pair records and per-line dynamic-response characterisation (fast-flush-derived where recorded, waveform-classifier-derived otherwise with classifier misclassification propagated): (a) for systolic, diastolic and mean pressure **separately**, how do the device's bias, the SD of paired differences and the mean squared paired disagreement differ between the non-resonant and resonant reference strata, expressed as the fraction of pooled mean squared disagreement contributed by the resonant stratum in excess of its sampling share; and (b) for how many devices does the standard's pass/fail statistic cross its threshold when the analysis is restricted to the non-resonant stratum?

Why v1 needed reformulating (three defects, all fixed in v2): "share of the error" did not say share of *what* (bias, variance, mean squared disagreement) and, taken as a latent apportionment between two error-bearing instruments, is **not identified** without replicates, a third method or a pseudo-truth — Bland & Altman state the only route they recognise is replication ("We can estimate this measurement error only by making repeated measurements by the same method"), so v1's headline quantity could have been unanswerable as posed; "the applicable standard" was unnamed while the device classes in scope are governed by different ones; and v1 did not separate SBP/DBP/MAP, which is the one split on which the answer most plausibly flips sign in importance.

## Alternative formulations

1. Is the fluid-filled arterial catheter-transducer system a fit reference standard for blood-pressure device validation, given that roughly a third of in-service radial lines are underdamped?
2. How much of the published disagreement between cuff or continuous noninvasive monitors and invasive arterial pressure is the reference's error rather than the tested device's? — **Note: this formulation grades Low, not Medium.** Romagnoli 2014 reports precisely this contrast at one site for oscillometric NIBP (systolic IBP−NIBP bias 28.5 mmHg in the resonant group vs 4.1 mmHg in the rest). Only the canonical v2 object (validation-dataset error budget, apportioned variance, standard verdict flips) survives Gate 2.

## Motivating gap

Settled claim inverted: invasive arterial pressure is the standard against which other blood-pressure measurement is judged. Romagnoli et al., Crit Care 2014;18:644 (read at the publisher by the intake agent; not re-opened by this agent, whose searches confirmed the ccforum.biomedcentral.com route and the article's existence) characterised 300 radial lines by Gardner's fast-flush method, classed 30.7% (92/300) resonant/underdamped, and derived a waveform-only detector (dP/dt_max cutoff 1.67 mmHg/ms; sens 77%, spec 97%, AUC 0.88). Its own setting-specific prevalences are internally inconsistent between text (30%, 27.8%, 33.8%) and Table 1 (21.7%, 29.4%, 48.9%); overdamping prevalence is not reported. The prevalence limb is answered and is not the candidate. The un-asked object is the **reference term in device-validation error budgets**: validation studies and the standards behind them treat the intra-arterial signal as definitionally the truth, and this agent found no study that classifies reference lines by fidelity inside a validation dataset, apportions the disagreement, or re-runs the standard's verdict.

## Presuppositions (status after Gate 1/2)

1. A nontrivial fraction of in-service arterial lines are dynamically distorted. — Established at one figure in one setting (30.7%, Romagnoli 2014). CONTESTED as to generalisability across populations and to femoral/brachial sites, and as to overdamping, whose prevalence that paper does not report. Partly corroborated independently: the medRxiv simulation paper drew its parameter ranges from fast-flush tests in 121 ICU patients and reports an IQR of 10–40 Hz for f_natural and 0–0.6 for Z_damping, stating that "a significant proportion of patients" fall in its high-error region but giving **no percentage**.
2. Resonance can be identified post hoc from a recorded waveform without a bedside fast-flush test. — CONTESTED, and now better supported than at intake: Michard (JCMC 2024) reviews an overdamping detector (Rinehart et al., 38 surgical patients, air-bubble-induced overdamping, sensitivity 98%/specificity 92%) alongside Romagnoli's dP/dt_max rule. Still CONTESTED for the specific regime that matters here — archived monitor waveforms at 125 Hz may not carry the resonant frequency, and the derivation cohorts were sampled far faster.
3. Published validation studies retain, or can regenerate, per-pair records permitting stratification by reference fidelity. — CONTESTED and untested by this agent; most publish Bland–Altman summaries only. This is the binding feasibility risk: the question may be answerable only prospectively, or on the subset with retrievable raw waveforms.
4. The applicable standard's pass/fail statistic is a function of the reference, so reference quality can move the verdict. — Established by the construction of such criteria (bias and SD of paired differences against the reference). **Not verified against the standard's text in this run: ISO 81060-3:2022 COULD NOT READ** (see access log). So whether 81060-3 already imposes any dynamic-response, flush-test or waveform-quality requirement on its invasive reference is an open factual question, not a checked absence. If it does, limb (b) narrows to pre-2022 datasets and to classes governed by other standards.
5. Mean arterial pressure is less distorted by a second-order catheter system than systolic pressure. — CONTESTED and load-bearing in the adverse direction. One opened source supports it in words: Michard 2024 — "The MAP also has the advantage of being relatively insensitive to damping phenomena." One opened source partly cuts against it: the medRxiv simulation declines to make that general claim, reports mean error "as much as 100 mmHg" in poorly damped low-frequency configurations, and warns that "it is possible to have a combination of positive and negative errors resulting in small mean error" — i.e. MAP's apparent robustness can be cancellation, not fidelity. v2 therefore tests SBP, DBP and MAP separately rather than assuming.
6. Device-validation studies do not currently check reference fidelity. — Still an **unverified absence**, now partially probed: four queries aimed at it returned no validation study doing so, and the medRxiv paper's only statement on practice is that "there is only qualitative characterization by visual inspection" and that the fast-flush test is not recorded. One registry record that could falsify it (NCT06518980, "Prospective Validation of Intraoperative Blood Pressure Monitors") COULD NOT READ.

## What would count as an answer

A stratified re-analysis over a pre-specified frame of device-validation datasets with retrievable paired records, every reference line classified by a pre-registered fidelity rule (fast-flush-derived where available, waveform-derived otherwise, classifier error carried through), reporting the device's bias, limits of agreement and the standard's pass/fail statistic within strata — yielding the fraction of pooled mean squared disagreement contributed by the resonant stratum and naming the devices, if any, whose verdict flips. A prospective validation with fast-flush characterisation of every reference line answers it directly. For the full apportionment (as opposed to the stratified contrast), a pseudo-truth is required — e.g. fast-flush-identified second-order inverse-filter reconstruction of each line's input pressure, with the reconstruction's own uncertainty propagated — and the answer must state which of the two estimands it reports. A null (error metrics indistinguishable across fidelity strata for systolic, diastolic and mean alike) is equally admissible and would retire the concern.

## Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, with sources)

**P1. Michard F. "Towards the automatic detection and correction of abnormal arterial pressure waveforms." J Clin Monit Comput 2024. doi:10.1007/s10877-024-01152-3 — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10877-024-01152-3 (OPENED).**
- Unit of analysis: none of its own — narrative commentary, "no datasets were generated or analysed". Its cited studies: patients (Rinehart, n=38 surgical; Foti, n=70 critically ill).
- Endpoint: detectability of abnormal waveforms (sens 98%/spec 92% for overdamping) and the effect of abnormal waveforms on *derived haemodynamic indices* — cardiac output overestimated by 79–91% with underdamping; dP/dt_max inflated by 84% on average.
- Reference/comparator: the corrected or resonance-filtered waveform, or an induced-artefact ground truth.
- Estimand: whether abnormal ABP waveforms can be detected and corrected automatically, and how much they distort derived indices.

**P2. "Intra-Arterial Blood Pressure Measurement: Sources of Error and Solutions." medRxiv 2020.08.29.20184275 (2020) — https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.08.29.20184275.full.pdf (OPENED; authors not captured, see access log).**
- Unit of analysis: a simulated arterial waveform per (f_natural, Z_damping) configuration of a virtual second-order fluid-filled catheter system; parameter ranges taken from fast-flush tests in 121 ICU patients.
- Endpoint: systolic, mean and diastolic error in mmHg, measured minus true input (e.g. systolic error 18.4 mmHg at f_natural 12 Hz / Z 0.25, falling to 1.0 mmHg at 20 Hz / 0.40).
- Reference/comparator: the synthesised true waveform — simulation ground truth, not any device and not any patient's other measurement.
- Estimand: the catheter system's own measurement error as a function of its dynamic parameters.

**P3. Bland JM, Altman DG. "Comparing methods of measurement: why plotting difference against standard method is misleading." Lancet 1995;346:1085–7 — https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/diffplot.htm (OPENED).**
- Unit of analysis: the subject measured by two methods (worked example: blood pressure).
- Endpoint: the paired difference and its plotted relation to the standard; expected spurious correlation −√((1−ρ)/2), ≈ −0.29 at ρ=0.83.
- Reference/comparator: a "standard" method explicitly **not** assumed error-free — a method "presumably more accurate" is noted as not necessarily so.
- Estimand: how to display and interpret method-comparison data when both methods err; it states that within-method measurement error is estimable "only by making repeated measurements by the same method" and discusses no third-method or known-reference-precision route.

**P0 (the intake's own anchor, listed for completeness, not re-opened by this agent).** Romagnoli et al., Crit Care 2014;18:644. Unit of analysis: patient (n=300 radial lines); endpoint: resonance prevalence and systolic IBP−NIBP bias (28.5 mmHg, LoA −2.9 to 59.6 in the resonant group vs 4.1 mmHg otherwise); comparator: averaged oscillometric NIBP from the same arm; estimand: prevalence and magnitude of resonance in one surgical/ICU population.

## Novelty — how this differs from each

- **vs P1:** P1's object is the waveform and the indices computed from it; the candidate's object is a *validation dataset's error budget* and a *standard's verdict*. P1 explicitly does not discuss validating noninvasive or continuous BP devices against an arterial reference, stratifies no validation dataset, and apportions no disagreement. Its correction machinery is, however, the obvious instrument for answering the candidate — which is what makes the absence pointed rather than comfortable: the detector exists, the error budget was never recomputed with it.
- **vs P2:** P2 answers "how wrong can the reference be?" in silico, with simulation truth and no device under test. The candidate asks "how much of a *device's* measured error, in real paired records, is the reference's — and does the verdict move?". P2 has no device, no patients' paired measurements, no standard, no strata, and reports no share of total disagreement. Its ranges would parameterise the candidate's sensitivity analysis; they do not answer it.
- **vs P3:** P3 is the method-owning field's statement of the general problem and shows the candidate's v1 apportionment is not identified without replication — which is why v2 reports a stratified contrast and declares a pseudo-truth route for the apportionment. P3 is generic, blood-pressure-illustrated statistics; it names no arterial line, no fidelity classifier, no standard, and no device class.
- **vs P0 (Romagnoli):** differs on all four attributes — unit of analysis (patient vs validation dataset/device), endpoint (prevalence and IBP−NIBP bias vs device bias/SD/MSE and pass-fail statistic), comparator (oscillometric NIBP as the foil that exposes the line vs device-under-test against a fidelity-stratified reference), estimand (prevalence and magnitude in one population vs error-share and verdict flips across a pre-specified frame). ## Significance

**Consequence.** If yes — a material share of the pooled disagreement sits in the resonant stratum and at least one device's pass/fail statistic crosses its threshold — then three things change. Validation protocols acquire a cheap, concrete inclusion criterion (record the fast flush; archive it), since the stratifying variable costs seconds at the bedside and is currently discarded. Part of the standing conclusion that cuff and continuous noninvasive monitors "miss hypotension" relative to invasive pressure becomes reference artefact rather than device failure, which changes who is to blame for a known clinical disagreement. And the perioperative-hypotension threshold literature, which derives action thresholds (e.g. MAP cut-offs) from invasive pressure, inherits a quantified distortion term it has not carried. If no — metrics indistinguishable across strata for systolic, diastolic and mean alike — the concern is retired with evidence, the invasive reference is strengthened exactly where it is most often questioned, and standards committees have a reason *not* to add a QC burden. Both answers are acted on, which is the test.

**Audience.** Named and reachable: manufacturers and their notified bodies for continuous noninvasive BP devices (volume-clamp, pulse-decomposition, cuffless wearables) whose market claims rest on invasive-reference validations; the IEC/ISO committee behind ISO 81060-3:2022, which must decide whether to specify reference-system dynamic response at the next revision; FDA reviewers assessing continuous noninvasive BP accuracy claims; and — a constituency the intake did not name — developers of cuffless BP algorithms trained on archived invasive waveforms (MIMIC-style corpora), whose *labels* inherit whatever distortion the lines carried, which q8/q10 showed is unmeasured in those corpora.

**Fecundity.** Opens: whether cuffless models trained on archived arterial waveforms have partly learned the catheter's transfer function rather than physiology; whether inverse-filter reconstruction of the reference should be mandated, and how its own uncertainty enters a validation's error budget; whether the ±5/±8 mmHg-class agreement criteria are attainable at all given reference uncertainty (a metrological floor question the standards do not pose); whether the reference term differs by cannulation site (radial/femoral/brachial), where no prevalence data exist at all; and whether overdamping — prevalence unreported anywhere found — moves verdicts in the opposite direction from resonance.

**Anchoring.** Touches the live and unresolved dispute over whether invasive arterial pressure may serve as a validation reference at all; the long-standing unexplained magnitude of cuff-versus-invasive disagreement; the perioperative-hypotension threshold debate; and the cuffless-BP validation-standards controversy, where the choice of reference is the central contested methodological point.

## Reason for silence

A disciplinary boundary plus an incentive gap, with a recording-practice mechanism underneath. Reference-line fidelity belongs to bedside critical-care monitoring QA — the fast flush is a nursing/anaesthesia manoeuvre, interpreted, as the opened simulation paper says, by "qualitative characterization by visual inspection", and **not recorded**: monitors do not store it, so until waveform-derived classifiers existed the stratifying variable was literally unrecordable in any retrospective dataset. Error budgets for device validation belong to clinical engineering, biostatistics and regulatory science. The two communities publish in different journals and neither owns the joint object. The incentive gap closes the loop: a manufacturer whose device passed has no reason to reopen the reference; one whose device failed cannot blame the reference without attacking the standard it must be certified against; and the standards themselves make the question hard to formulate, because naming the intra-arterial signal "the reference" puts its fidelity outside the analysis frame by construction. This is not "no one thought of it" — the components (Gardner's dynamic-response criteria since 1981, Romagnoli's classifier, Rinehart's detector, Michard's correction agenda, Grubbs' variance apportionment) all exist and have never been assembled across that boundary.

## Strongest remaining objection (flagged for Gate 4)

**Partial triviality by composition.** Romagnoli's systolic contrast (28.5 vs 4.1 mmHg) and the simulation's systolic error at realistic parameters (≈18 mmHg at f_natural 12 Hz, Z 0.25) may already let a competent reader predict limb (a) for *systolic* pressure to within the precision anyone needs, after which limb (b) is close to arithmetic for any device whose systolic criterion margin is small. The candidate's defence must be that the pass/fail statistic depends on the SD of paired differences and on the resonant stratum's *share* in each actual dataset, neither of which is predictable from those two numbers, and that MAP and diastolic endpoints — where the cancellation warning in P2 cuts against the MAP-robustness presupposition — are genuinely open. A Gate 4 adversary should also be handed the two COULD NOT READ items, since ISO 81060-3's text could falsify presupposition 4 and NCT06518980 could be this design already running.
