## Restatement in my own words

Two questions about the sticker on an antibody bottle.

(i) When a company prints "use at 1:1000 for western blot", is that number explained by how concentrated the liquid in the tube happens to be (so the number is really about the packaging), or by how tightly the antibody binds and how much of the target protein is in the recommended sample (so the number is really about the biology)? Settled by regressing log recommended dilution on stated stock concentration vs. a binding constant plus nominal target abundance, one row per product × application, against a dated datasheet, with product format and vendor as covariates.

(ii) Take published papers that used those same products. In what share of them can you actually work out how much antibody (mass or moles per volume) was in the incubation, using only the paper plus that dated datasheet? And where you can, how far apart are the real concentrations in papers that all wrote the same "1:1000" for the same catalogue number?

So: limb (i) is about the provenance of a vendor number; limb (ii) is about whether a dose survives into the published record. The unit of limb (i) is product × application; of limb (ii), the paper.
