## Question (canonical — Gate 0 intake version)

For units held in vapour-phase liquid-nitrogen storage, does a unit's recorded storage coordinate (canister, rack, height) together with the retrieval-event history of its neighbourhood predict its post-thaw outcome, and does between-facility variation in in-dewar thermal history account for part of the published between-facility spread in post-thaw survival for identical freezing protocols?

## Question (Gate 1 reformulation — one permitted, for well-posedness only; both versions recorded)

Within a single ART or cell-therapy facility, among cryostored units of one specimen class frozen under one protocol, what share of the residual between-unit variance in a per-unit post-thaw endpoint is attributable to the unit's static storage coordinate (vessel, canister, rack, height), estimated with cohort, calendar date, donor and operator adjusted and with re-racking and tank-transfer events as the identifying variation, and what is the effect size per 10 °C of a pre-specified time-weighted static storage temperature derived from the facility's own thermal map?

Reason for the reformulation: the intake version is a compound of three estimands with three incompatible designs (a within-facility position effect; a cumulative retrieval-exposure effect; a between-facility decomposition of published survival spread). The third limb has no identified estimand at all — "published between-centre survival spread" is a heterogeneous literature aggregate with no common denominator, no per-unit exposure data and no way to hold protocol, population and endpoint definition fixed, so no evidence could settle it as stated. The reformulation keeps the one limb that is both identified and not already answered: static coordinate. Note that the reformulation does **not** rescue novelty — see Gate 2 — it only makes the surviving limb well-posed.

## Alternative formulations

- Is a frozen sample's shelf position in the tank an unrecognised exposure variable for its post-thaw viability?
- Among cryostored units from the same cohort and protocol, how much of the residual variance in post-thaw outcome is attributable to storage location and to cumulative transient warming events, as opposed to the freezing protocol, the operator or the donor?

## Motivating gap (as intaken, with Gate 2 corrections marked)

Three things were asserted to be separately reported and never joined: (i) large vertical temperature gradients inside vapour-phase LN2 dewars, with upper/neck positions claimed to sit near the ~−130 °C glass-transition region; (ii) documented transient warming of neighbouring inventory during retrieval; (iii) substantial between-centre spread in post-thaw survival for one protocol, never attributed to storage position.

**Gate 2 corrections to the gap.**
- (i) is **partly false as stated.** The only two position-resolved temperature sources opened in this run both put the warmest routinely occupied position roughly 50 °C *below* the glass transition, not at it. Measured: CBS/BioLife V-1500 isothermal vessel, nine probes at three heights, 24 h averages top −184.7/−183.9/−184.6 °C, middle ≈−193 °C, bottom −194.8 °C; warmest single hourly reading −181.5 °C, against the paper's own cited −132 °C threshold. Modelled: Xiong et al. 2026 CFD of automated and non-automated LN2 biobanks, monitoring-point averages −187.9 → −183.2 °C (automated, empty → full) and −169.7 → −165.4 °C (non-automated), with between-point spreads growing 10.67 → 13.70 °C and 7.81 → 10.25 °C. So the gradient is real and of order 8–14 °C, and is **not** of order 60 °C. The intake's figures ("bases near −190 °C, upper/neck positions in the region of −130 °C") conflate the steady-state stored position with the *transient neck* temperature a unit passes through when lifted — Sansiñena et al. modelled the neck transient at −50 °C and −70 °C, which is where the devitrification risk lives.
- (ii) is confirmed, and (iii)'s link to (ii) **has already been joined and tested** — see Gate 2 prior 1.
- The intake's HONESTY FLAG was correct to flag the gradient figures as unverified. Re-sourced here, they do not support the mechanism they were offered for.

## Presuppositions (status after Gate 2)

1. Per-unit storage coordinate is recorded and retrievable. **ESTABLISHED** for modern inventory systems — Zhang et al. 2023 state a hospital electronic system logged each straw's cane, canister and tank position. CONTESTED for legacy archives, which hold the longest-stored units.
2. The vertical gradient puts upper positions at or above the devitrification/glass-transition region. **CONTRADICTED** by both position-resolved sources opened (warmest measured position −181.5 °C; warmest modelled monitoring-point average −165.4 °C; Tg −126 to −121 °C, devitrification onset −109 °C per Sansiñena et al.). This is the presupposition the mechanism rested on, and it is the one that failed. It survives only in a weaker form: a real but ~10 °C gradient, well below Tg, whose effect would have to run through some sub-Tg process rather than devitrification.
3. Retrieval/opening events are reconstructable. **ESTABLISHED** at least at one facility — Zhang et al. reconstructed per-straw event counts, including bystander counts accrued when a cane was moved for a neighbour, with explicit counting rules.
4. A per-unit post-thaw endpoint exists and is linkable. **ESTABLISHED** for ART and cell therapy; false for many research biobanks.
5. Storage position is not fully confounded with calendar time, cohort or donor. **CONTESTED and confirmed as the main design threat.** Zhang et al. found storage time strongly associated with their exposure count (P < 0.001), which is the same confound in its retrieval-event form.
6. Degradation above the glass transition is cumulative and monotone in temperature and time. **CONTESTED**, and now largely beside the point for the static limb, since no static position reaches the glass transition.
7. Any position effect is separable from protocol variance at achievable sample sizes. **CONTESTED** and now less likely: a 9,200-cycle study was unable to resolve an effect of the much larger retrieval exposure.

## What would count as an answer

A within-facility analysis of a few thousand units of one specimen class, each with (a) storage coordinate, (b) a thermal history either logged directly (loggers at several heights) or reconstructed from mapped gradients plus neighbourhood retrieval counts, and (c) a per-unit post-thaw endpoint; outcome regressed on coordinate and on a pre-specified time-weighted thermal-history summary with cohort, date, donor and operator adjusted, using re-racking and tank-transfer events as the identifying variation. The deliverable is a variance-component estimate for storage location plus an effect size per 10 °C of time-weighted storage temperature, judged against a pre-registered null band set from the facility's own replicate variability. A null in a facility with a documented gradient counts as an answer: it licenses current practice with evidence. (The between-facility limb, as noted at Gate 1, has no identified estimand and is dropped.)

## Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, all opened)

**Prior 1 — Zhang H, Ye D, Wu Y, Li Y, Huang X (2023).** "Effect of exposed-to-air frequency of cryopreserved embryo on clinical outcomes of vitrified-warmed embryo transfer cycles: a retrospective analysis of 9,200 vitrified-warmed transfer cycles." BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth, 17 Aug 2023, DOI 10.1186/s12884-023-05879-w. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10433674
- **Unit of analysis:** the vitrified-warmed transfer cycle (9,200 cycles, Jan 2011–Dec 2016: 3,313 D3 / 4,530 D5 / 1,357 D6), with the exposure assigned per straw/embryo.
- **Endpoint:** clinical pregnancy and live birth (primary); post-warming survival, implantation, positive hCG (secondary).
- **Reference/comparator:** EAF = 2 (the minimum possible: in once, out once), against EAF 4, 6, 8, ≥10.
- **Estimand:** adjusted odds ratio for the association of cumulative retrieval-exposure count with outcome, storage time among the adjustments; no causal estimand declared.
- **What it did with the candidate's machinery:** it built the per-unit cumulative retrieval-exposure variable *from the inventory system's logged cane/canister/tank coordinates*, and it counted **bystander exposure** — a straw not being warmed accrues two counts whenever its cane is moved for a neighbour. That is precisely the candidate's "retrieval-event history of its neighbourhood".
- **Result:** null. After adjustment, EAF was not associated with clinical pregnancy or live birth at any stage (e.g. D3, EAF ≥10: OR 0.867, 95% CI 0.672–1.119); the one significant D3 survival difference was judged not EAF-dependent by the authors.

**Prior 2 — Sansiñena MJ, Zaritzky N, Taminelli G, Santos MV (2014).** "Implications of glass transition in the devitrification process and storage management of vitrified oocytes and embryos." Universidad Católica Argentina. https://www.sidalc.net/search/Record/oai:ucacris:123456789-5487
- **Unit of analysis:** a modelled open-pulled-straw carrier plus DSC measurements on a vitrification solution — not a stored unit.
- **Endpoint:** glass transition temperature (−126 to −121 °C) and devitrification onset (−109 °C); modelled time to reach threshold on exposure.
- **Reference/comparator:** full LN2 immersion at −196 °C versus exposure at two arbitrary dewar-neck temperatures (−50 °C and −70 °C).
- **Estimand:** a deterministic heat-transfer time-to-threshold (order 30 s), not a statistical effect.
- **Result:** vapour-phase handling carries devitrification risk; handling in vapour should be brief. No survival data, no per-unit outcome, no storage position.

**Prior 3 — Xiong Z, Liu X, Zhu T, Yao L, Yang Z, Zhang Z, Xiao X (2026).** "Temperature Evolution in Liquid-Nitrogen Biobanks." Applied Sciences 16(15):7700, DOI 10.3390/app16157700. https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/temperature-evolution-in-liquid-nitrogen-biobanks/
- **Unit of analysis:** a monitoring point inside a CFD model of an automated and a non-automated biobank vessel, at empty/half/full load.
- **Endpoint:** vapour-phase temperature over time and spatial uniformity between monitoring points.
- **Reference/comparator:** load states against each other, and the two vessel designs descriptively (the authors warn the cross-design comparison is not normalised).
- **Estimand:** modelled final average temperature and between-point spread as a function of load — a simulated physical field, not an exposure–outcome association.
- **Result:** averages −187.9 → −183.2 °C (automated) and −169.7 → −165.4 °C (non-automated); spreads 10.67 → 13.70 °C and 7.81 → 10.25 °C; warmest locations most load-sensitive, so non-uniformity grows with loading. No sample outcome of any kind.

Supporting (not graded as a prior, position-resolved measurement): CBS Cryogenic Solutions / BioLife Solutions, "Isothermal Temperature Uniformity Testing", WP-CBS-230500_R01, 08 Mar 2023 — nine probes at three heights in a V-1500 over 24 h: top ≈−184 °C, middle ≈−193 °C, bottom −194.8 °C, warmest hourly reading −181.5 °C. Vendor-published, near-empty chamber, no retrieval transients. https://www.biolifesolutions.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/CBS_Isothermal_Temperature_Uniformity_Testing_White_Paper_030823.pdf

## Novelty — how the candidate differs from each

- **From Prior 1 (the fatal one):** the candidate's retrieval-event limb is not merely adjacent, it is the same question with the same data source in the same field, already answered null at n = 9,200. The differences are narrow: Zhang's exposure is an event *count* rather than a time-weighted thermal integral; its primary endpoints are clinical rather than a per-unit post-thaw viability readout (though post-warming survival is a secondary endpoint); and it recorded canister/rack/height but, by the paper's own account, **did not analyse the static coordinate**. So the candidate retains exactly one unexplored element — static coordinate as a covariate, and a variance share for it — bolted onto a limb that is closed. A limb-level novelty claim cannot survive a published null on the dominant exposure in the same mechanism.
- **From Prior 2:** Sansiñena et al. establish the physical threshold the candidate's mechanism needs, and in doing so locate the risk in the *neck transient* during handling (−50/−70 °C), not in the static stored position. This relocates the candidate's own mechanism onto the limb Prior 1 then measured and found null. The candidate differs by asking for a per-unit statistical association rather than a deterministic threshold time — but it inherits Prior 2's physics, and that physics does not support the static limb.
- **From Prior 3:** Xiong et al. supply the exposure field (an ~8–14 °C vertical spread) that the candidate proposes to use as its reconstructed exposure, with no outcome attached. The candidate differs by joining such a field to per-unit outcomes. But the magnitude Prior 3 reports is roughly an order of magnitude too small to reach the Prior 2 threshold, which removes the proposed mechanism and leaves the candidate asking whether a ~10 °C difference, 35–65 °C below Tg, moves a post-thaw endpoint — a much weaker and less motivated question than the one intaken.

## Novelty confidence: **Low**

A near-duplicate exists for the question's dominant limb, in the candidate's own literature and its own words, found by the mandatory own-field title query: Zhang et al. 2023 operationalised per-unit cumulative retrieval exposure *including neighbourhood/bystander events* from logged storage coordinates, regressed post-warming survival and clinical outcomes on it with storage time adjusted, across 9,200 cycles, and reported a null. It explicitly held the candidate's coordinate data and chose not to analyse it. The residual static-coordinate variance-share limb was not found published (query 6), so it is not a *complete* duplicate — but on the ledger's own rule ("Low — likely asked; a near-duplicate was found"), and given that the motivating presupposition for the residual limb is contradicted by both position-resolved sources opened, Low is the honest grade. This is a successful Gate 2, not a failed one.

**Asked-but-open?** No — asked and *answered* (null) on the retrieval-event limb. It is therefore not routable to the open-questions project. The static-coordinate variance share is unanswered but is a sub-limb of a closed question with a falsified mechanism, not an open problem of record.

## Significance — consequence

Weak, and weakened further by Gate 2. If the static-coordinate term were non-zero, ART labs, cord-blood banks and cell-therapy facilities would have to treat shelf height as a quality attribute: stratified release testing, position-aware allocation of the highest-value units, position as a covariate in retrospective outcome studies, and a new entry in ISBER/FACT/AABB storage guidance. If zero, current practice is licensed with evidence rather than assumption. But the informative content of either answer has already been largely pre-empted: a null on cumulative retrieval exposure — the *larger* thermal insult, since a lifted unit passes through a −50 to −70 °C neck while a statically stored one sits 35–65 °C below Tg — makes a non-zero static-position effect physically hard to credit. The candidate's remaining consequence is a tightened confidence interval on something already believed, for reasons now sourced rather than assumed.

## Significance — audience

Real but unlikely to act. ART laboratory directors and embryologists (the community Zhang et al. wrote for, and which has already absorbed that null), cord-blood and cell-therapy QA, biorepository operations, and the standards bodies (ISBER Best Practices, FACT-NetCord, AABB, HFEA). Vendors (BioLife/CBS, Azenta, Air Liquide) already publish uniformity and excursion data as a commercial argument, so the measurement half has an owner with an incentive — which is part of why the gradient is well characterised and the outcome link is not. No group among these would change a procedure on a variance-component estimate for a ~10 °C sub-Tg difference.

## Significance — fecundity

Thin. A positive result would open: what sub-Tg process could be dose-responsive at 10 °C (molecular mobility in the glassy state, ice recrystallisation kinetics well below Tg); whether archival cohorts need position as a retrospective covariate; whether "storage time" effects reported across the literature are partly position effects in disguise, since position and date are confounded by sequential tank filling. That last one is the most interesting derivative and is not foreclosed by Prior 1. A null adds essentially nothing that Prior 1's null does not already supply.

## Significance — anchoring

Touches: the unresolved "does cryostorage duration harm embryos/units" literature (where position–date confounding is a live and unexamined alternative explanation for reported duration effects — the one genuinely under-asked thread here); the vapour-phase versus liquid-phase storage debate; the unexplained between-centre spread in post-thaw survival; and the preanalytical-variable programme in biospecimen science (SPREC and successors), which codes storage temperature but not position.

## Reason for silence

The candidate is largely **not silent** — it was asked and answered in 2023 in its own field's words, which is itself the Gate 2 finding. For the residual static-coordinate sub-limb, the credible mechanism is a **cross-system incentive and ownership gap compounded by a reasoned dismissal**: storage coordinate lives in facility operations' inventory/LIMS systems while the per-unit endpoint lives in clinical or QC systems, and the join is nobody's funded deliverable; a positive finding would constitute a self-reported storage-quality defect with licensing consequences (HFEA, FACT-NetCord, AABB), so facilities have a standing disincentive to look; and — decisively — practitioners have a *principled* reason to treat position as irrelevant, because the published thermometry puts every occupied position tens of degrees below the glass transition. That last is not "no one thought of it": it is a field correctly inferring from its own physics that the variable cannot matter, and then (in Prior 1) testing the larger version of the insult anyway and finding nothing. A silence with a sound reason behind it is weak evidence of an un-asked question.
