## Restatement (my own words, before engaging the record's framing)

Take placebo-controlled drug trials that have already finished, in a drug class that people could also obtain outside the trial while it was enrolling. Fix the list of trials before touching any specimen. Then thaw the control-arm blood drawn at the trial's own scheduled visits and run one pre-specified validated assay for the drug class. Report three numbers plus one accounting figure:

1. what share of control-arm participant-visits are positive for the class — stated alongside the assay's detection limit, its false-positive rate, and how long after a dose it can still see the drug *compared with the gap between visits*;
2. how well that positivity agrees with what the same participant's own concomitant-medication form says;
3. how far, and in which direction, the trial's headline effect estimate moves when recomputed under one contamination-adjusted estimand fixed in advance of seeing any assay result;
4. what share of the pre-declared trial list the specimen custodians refused to release, so the denominator can be audited.

A measured positivity indistinguishable from the assay's false-positive rate is declared in advance to be a real answer, not a failure.

### (a) Clause "release permissible under the governing consent" — holds, but NARROWER than conditioned on

Quotations are as served by the summariser from the protocol PDFs; spacing in the SOUL PDF was reported as irregular and normalised by the summariser.

SOUL flowchart carries rows "Biosamples for future analysis (biobank, genetics)" and "Biosamples for future analysis (biobank, biomarkers)", with footnote m: "Only applicable for patients that have provided informed consent for biosamples for biomarkers and genetic analyses." Footnote n, on the biomarker row: "Only applicable for the year two visit (V 12)."

STEP-HFpEF §7.2 (subject discontinuation/withdrawal): a withdrawing subject "may request destruction of any samples taken and not tested."

Three specific narrowings follow, and this is my strongest sourced finding:

1. **Optional, separate consent.** The retained-for-future-research series exists only for patients who gave a *second* consent. The releasable set is therefore a self-selected consenting subset of the control arm, not the control arm. The question's denominator ("control-arm participant-visits") silently assumes the specimen series tracks the randomised series; in this instance it does not.
2. **Consent scoped to a different purpose.** The consent is scoped on its face to "biosamples for biomarkers and genetic analyses." A drug-level screen for the study class is neither a biomarker measurement nor a genetic analysis. So release for the candidate's purpose is a request *outside the stated scope of the consent*, not a request within scope that a committee might refuse. The question's limb-4 accounting — "the share of the declared frame whose governance refused release" — has no slot for "the purpose was never within the consent's scope," which is a different and more fundamental obstacle than refusal.
3. **GRANULARITY gap on the unit.** In SOUL the biomarker biobank row is "Only applicable for the year two visit (V 12)" — a single designated visit. The question wants a proportion across *scheduled visits*. Per the ledger's rule, a classification or collection axis that exists but stops short of the candidate's grain is reported as a granularity gap, neither as an absence nor as a known: the releasable object here is one specimen per consenting patient, not a visit series.

## Single strongest killing point

The consent-scope and granularity finding from the one biobank instance I opened, which is also the check the record names as its cheapest unrun one. In SOUL the retained-for-future-research control-arm specimen series exists only for patients who gave a separate optional consent, that consent is scoped on its face to "biosamples for biomarkers and genetic analyses," and for biomarkers it covers one designated visit — footnote n: "Only applicable for the year two visit (V 12)." A drug-level screen for the study class is neither a biomarker nor a genetic analysis. So the obstacle is not that governance would *refuse* release but that the candidate's purpose falls outside the consent's stated scope, and limb 4's auditable denominator — "the share of the declared frame whose governance refused release" — has no category for that. Meanwhile the question is denominated in control-arm participant-*visits* while the releasable object in this instance is one specimen per consenting patient. If this generalises, the question's unit of analysis does not exist as a releasable thing in its own motivating trial family.

**Multi-leg disclosure.** This kill has three legs and they are not equally sourced. The leg I would stand on if the others fell is the **granularity leg**: footnote n is a direct quotation fixing the biomarker biobank to a single visit against a question denominated in visits, and it stands on its own. The **consent-scope leg** rests on reading the phrase "biomarkers and genetic analyses" as exhaustive of the consented purpose, which the footnote's wording supports but the unread Appendix 7 governs. The **optional-consent leg** (releasable subset is self-selected) is sourced by footnote m but I have not established its uptake, so it bounds nothing quantitatively. All three are one trial's protocol read at footnote level; the second instance, STEP-HFpEF, corroborates only that a retention appendix exists and that withdrawn subjects may request destruction of untested samples.
