Question (canonical) — V2, adopted at Gate 1 (the single permitted reformulation; V1 recorded below):
For a panel of enzymes purified by the standard published routes and declared before any measurement, (a) what is the distribution of the catalytically active fraction of protein in the preparation — stoichiometric active-site titration or single-turnover burst amplitude against total protein measured by the same assay the source papers used, with the titration endpoint declared operationally in advance — reported with per-enzyme and per-route variance components; and (b) what is the MAXIMUM share of the published between-report log10 kcat variance for those same enzymes that a between-preparation active-fraction distribution of the measured magnitude could generate, computed as a variance budget under a declared exchangeability assumption between the panel's preparations and the source papers' preparations, with the residual assigned to assay conditions after adjusting for declared temperature, pH, buffer, ionic-strength and substrate covariates?

V1 (intake, superseded): "...and what share of the order-of-magnitude spread in published kcat values for those same enzymes is attributable to that unmeasured fraction rather than to assay conditions?"
Reason for reformulation (well-posedness only, no change of subject): V1's limb (b) is an ATTRIBUTION of historical variance to a quantity measured on today's preparations. The preparations behind the published kcat values no longer exist and were never titrated, so no measurement on a fresh panel identifies their active fractions; the attribution estimand is not identified by any data the design can collect. V2 replaces it with an identified quantity — an upper bound on the share such a distribution COULD generate — and moves the unfalsifiable step into an explicitly declared exchangeability assumption rather than leaving it inside the estimand. V2 is strictly weaker and strictly answerable; V1 is not answerable as written.

Alternative formulations: When biochemistry writes kcat = Vmax/[E], how often is [E] the concentration of enzyme that actually works, and how much of the kcat literature's disagreement could that substitution account for? / Is the active fraction of a purified enzyme preparation a reproducible property of its purification route, and how large a share of the spread in published turnover numbers could its distribution generate?

Motivating gap: Turnover numbers are computed by dividing Vmax by a total protein concentration from a bulk assay (A280, Bradford, BCA), which presupposes that essentially every molecule counted is catalytically competent. Active-site titration exists because that is not guaranteed, and reporting standards ask for enzyme concentration and activity, yet titration is rarely performed. Gate 2 finding: the mechanism is EXPLICITLY NAMED in the candidate's own field's reproducibility literature (Prior 1) and in the kapp-vs-kcat literature (Prior 2) and then dropped without measurement, and a bespoke titrant for a single enzyme family is itself a full Chemical Science paper (Prior 3). So the gap is better described as ACKNOWLEDGED-BUT-UNQUANTIFIED than as un-noticed, and the record is written that way. Newly consequential because kcat compilations (BRENDA, SABIO-RK) are now ground truth for ML kcat predictors and parameters in enzyme-constrained genome-scale models, both of which would propagate a systematically low kcat as if it were biology — and because the active fraction is correlated with purification route, which is correlated with tag and host, which is correlated with sequence family, a route-driven bias is learnable by a sequence-input predictor as signal rather than averaging out as noise.

Presuppositions:
P1 kcat is routinely computed from total rather than active enzyme concentration — ESTABLISHED as standard practice at the level of convention (kcat is defined operationally as Vmax/[E]total); the share of papers that do otherwise is part of the answer, not an assumption. Not independently audited in this Gate 2.
P2 An "active fraction" is well defined, i.e. molecules partition into competent and incompetent rather than varying continuously in activity — CONTESTED, and Gate 2 found the contesting evidence in print: Prior 4 (Craig 2012, opened) measures a CONTINUOUS distribution of per-molecule catalytic activity for one enzyme and reports no zero-activity class at all. The partition is therefore operational, fixed by the titration endpoint, and must be declared in advance; it is not a natural kind.
P3 A stoichiometric titrant or burst-phase assay exists for the panel's enzymes — a declared SELECTION CONSTRAINT on the panel, not an assumption about biology, and Gate 2 shows it is the binding one: Prior 3 is an entire Chemical Science paper devoted to building one titrant for one family (alpha-amylases). It restricts the population the answer applies to, and that restriction is severe and non-random (families with titrants are the mechanistically best-studied ones).
P4 Purification routes are reproducible enough that active fraction has a route-level variance component rather than being preparation-idiosyncratic — CONTESTED; no opened source estimates such a component, and Prior 3's one large deficit (PPA) is attributed by its own authors to storage degradation, i.e. to preparation history rather than route.
P5 The published kcat spread for one enzyme is not already fully explained by assay temperature, pH, buffer, ionic strength and substrate identity — CONTESTED, and the competing explanation that gives the question its content. Gate 2 found NO published attribution in either direction: Prior 1 is explicit that it offers none.
P6 Total-protein assays are not themselves biased in a way that mimics an active-fraction deficit — must be measured against an orthogonal quantitation, not assumed. Unaddressed by any opened source; a second instrument arm is required, not optional.
P7 Michaelis-Menten parameterisation is appropriate for the panel's enzymes under the assay conditions used — assumed, standard, and declared.
P8 (new, forced by V2) The panel's preparations are exchangeable with the source papers' preparations in respect of active fraction — CONTESTED and unfalsifiable from the panel alone; V2 declares it as an assumption and reports limb (b) as a bound conditional on it, rather than hiding it inside an attribution.

What would count as an answer: a prospective panel with enzyme list, purification routes, titration method, titration endpoint definition, and total-protein method all declared in advance, reporting (a) the measured active-fraction distribution with per-enzyme and per-route variance components, each total-protein value cross-checked against one orthogonal quantitation (P6); and (b) a variance budget for the published between-report log10 kcat spread for those same enzymes, giving the maximum share an active-fraction distribution of the measured magnitude could generate under the declared exchangeability assumption, with the residual assigned to assay conditions after covariate adjustment, each bounded separately with its own uncertainty. A measured active fraction near 1 with small spread vindicates the presupposition and forces the kcat spread onto assay conditions and extraction error — an equally decisive answer, and a positive licence for kcat-trained predictors on this axis.

Reason for silence: three mechanisms, the first two evidenced from opened sources and the third structural.
(1) MEASUREMENT COST WITH A BESPOKE INSTRUMENT PER FAMILY. Active-site titration is not a general assay: a stoichiometric titrant must be designed for each mechanistic class, and Prior 3 is an entire Chemical Science paper that delivers ONE titrant for ONE family and validates it on four preparations. A cross-family panel therefore costs a reagent-development programme, not an experiment, which is why P3 is the binding constraint and why the only published numbers in this measurement class sit inside a reagent-development paper.
(2) A DISCIPLINARY BOUNDARY WITH NOBODY ON BOTH SIDES. The method belongs to mechanistic enzymology and chemical biology (titrant chemistry, burst kinetics); the consequence belongs to kinetic-parameter curation and systems biology (compilations, ecGEMs, ML predictors). The literature that sits between them — enzyme-reaction reproducibility — names the mechanism and moves on: Prior 1 flags inactive enzyme as a kcat bias and the words "active site titration", "active enzyme concentration" and "active fraction" appear nowhere in it. Prior 2 names the active/inactive distinction in one clause and renames its rates apparent instead of measuring the fraction.
(3) A DEFINITIONAL ESCAPE HATCH THAT REMOVES THE ERROR. Because kcat is defined operationally as Vmax divided by the enzyme concentration the author measured, a low active fraction makes no individual paper wrong by its own stated convention. There is therefore no erratum to issue and no reviewer objection to raise — only a convention to question, and conventions have no owner.
