Question (canonical): For a pre-declared frame of NORAD-identified Starlink satellites labelled by launch group, design generation and target shell, under a failure proxy declared before any data pull (decay onset within T days of launch with no operator decommissioning signature) plus two pre-declared alternative proxies as a sensitivity axis, fit a discrete-time competing-risks hazard model with a launch-group random intercept, conditioning on each unit's cumulative geomagnetic exposure over its own orbit-raising window, its target shell and its design generation: what is the interval estimate of the between-launch-group intercept variance tau^2 (and the implied median hazard ratio between two randomly drawn launch groups), and what does propagating that tau^2 through a standard debris-environment / PMD-compliance model do to the predicted distribution — not merely the mean — of non-disposed objects? (V2, the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation, scored as canonical; V1 named the manufacturing lot, which no public record identifies. Both in the record.)

Alternative formulations:
- Are mega-constellation satellite failures exchangeable across production batches, or are some batches "lemons" in a statistically identifiable sense?
- How much of the observed between-launch dispersion in Starlink early deorbit rates is attributable to batch-level manufacturing/screening variance rather than to environment, operations and design revision?

Motivating gap: Mega-constellations are the first case with per-unit public outcomes for thousands of nominally identical spacecraft, and batch-level dispersion is already reported. The academic state of the art on fleet failure is a pooled, unstratified Kaplan-Meier (arXiv 2603.25835, READ: mean daily failure probability 0.0128%; McDowell starlife, READ). The one batch loss with an accepted cause (Group 4-7, Feb 2022) is environment-level, which is why a residual group-level component is the un-asked part. Debris and PMD-compliance models propagate a single scalar reliability parameter (ISO 24113-style 95% PMD success), so a variance component would change the predicted tail, not only the mean. Correction carried from Gate 4: a published per-launch tabulation of early-life survival does exist (see priors) — the gap is the conditional variance component, not the absence of per-launch data.
