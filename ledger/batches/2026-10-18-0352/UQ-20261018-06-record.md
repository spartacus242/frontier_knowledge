Question (canonical) — V2, the one Gate 1 reformulation, taken as canonical:
For ONE measurand per network, over a frame of (station, event-window) pairs declared before any data pull — stations with continuous telemetry and machine-readable outage/QC flags, event windows defined by an exogenous event catalogue (storm track, flood wave, named cyclone) and not by the station's own record — and with each pair's realized peak magnitude y assigned from evidence independent of that station's instrument (high-water marks, post-event survey, neighbouring stations, hindcast reanalysis, each carrying a declared error model): (a) what is the hazard h(y) that the instrument ceases to report inside the event window, as a function of y in the measurand's own units, with confidence intervals, and is h flat in y; and (b) re-fitting GEV/POT with that censoring modelled explicitly — as a non-ignorable selection model, and alternatively by treating a lost peak as interval-censored below by the last recorded value — what is the signed change in the 50- and 100-year return levels against the conventional fit on the published annual-maxima series?

Question (V1, as intake; recorded per Gate 1):
In environmental monitoring networks whose instruments record the very extremes used to set design values, what is the hazard of an instrument ceasing to report inside the event window as a function of the realized magnitude it is measuring, and how much does that measurand-dependent censoring bias published annual-maxima series and return-level estimates?

Why reformulated (well-posedness only): V1 left three things undefined that decide whether an estimate exists — which measurand (V1's "networks" plural bundles surge, discharge and gust into one h(y), which is not one ordered magnitude variable), how the event window is drawn (if drawn from the station's own record, the window is defined by the thing whose absence is the outcome, and the estimand is circular), and where y comes from at a station that failed. V2 fixes one measurand per network, makes the window exogenous, and requires a declared error model on the reconstructed y. No change of subject: the estimand is still instrument survival as a function of the measurand, plus its propagation to return levels.

Alternative formulations:
- Do stream gauges, tide gauges and anemometers fail because of the events they are measuring, and by how much does the consequent loss of the largest observations depress estimated extreme quantiles?
- Treating instrument survival as an outcome in the units of the measurand, is missingness in extreme-value records ignorable, and what is the selection model that is being assumed when it is treated as such?

Motivating gap (as intake, with Gate 2's correction appended):
Instrument loss at the peak is reported constantly in passing and never aggregated: USGS flags stage/discharge as estimated or the gauge as destroyed in flood reports, NOAA tide gauges have dropped out at the surge maximum in major landfalls, anemometers are noted in post-storm surveys as having failed before the maximum gust, strong-motion instruments clip. The intake held that the extreme-value literature treats record gaps as ignorable, and named arXiv:2506.15964 (weighted GEV estimators under missing observations) as the nearest thing found, "without letting missingness depend on the measurand."
GATE 2 CORRECTION — the intake's gap statement is false as written, on the exact attribute it relied on. The companion paper to the one it cited (McVittie & Murphy, arXiv:2506.15970, surge at three Atlantic-Canadian tide gauges) tests and rejects MCAR, names this candidate's mechanism in its own words ("a failure of the equipment was attributed to the high intensity waves"; "the loss of power and flooding of the measurement apparati can result in gaps in the observed data series"), builds censoring-based GEV estimators whose censoring probabilities are magnitude-dependent, and reports the downstream return-level consequence. Separately, Simpson & Northrop (arXiv:2512.15429, UCL) pose limb (b) as an explicit open need in the field's own words, naming the candidate's own example. Both were missed by the intake's two searches.

Presuppositions (as intake, re-graded against what was opened):
1. Event-window outages are recorded in network metadata with enough timing and QC detail to distinguish an outage from a genuine low reading. CONTESTED; unchanged by Gate 2 (true for USGS estimated-record flags, weaker elsewhere). Nothing opened quantifies the flag's reliability.
2. The realized peak at a failed station can be reconstructed from evidence independent of that instrument well enough to condition the hazard on magnitude. CONTESTED and the identification crux. Gate 2 found the crux live in print rather than resolved: McVittie & Murphy do not reconstruct y at all — they impute the censoring probability from the fitted GEV CDF or the empirical CDF of the observed values, which is a distributional assumption standing where this candidate wants a measurement.
3. Instrument failure is at least partly caused by the measurand rather than only by calendar-correlated maintenance and funding. What would count as an answer:
An estimated failure hazard h(y) with confidence intervals over the pre-declared frame, in the measurand's own units, plus a quantified downstream effect: re-fitting GEV/POT with the censoring modelled explicitly — as a non-ignorable selection model, or by treating a lost peak as interval-censored below by the last recorded value — and reporting the signed change in 50- and 100-year quantiles against the conventional fit. A clean negative answers it too: h(y) flat in y and return levels moving by less than a pre-declared tolerance would show network missingness is ignorable for extremes and close the question. Gate 1 passes on this criterion; the question is not answerable identically regardless of evidence, and the negative is informative.

Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, with sources, all opened):
P1 (NEAREST on limb (b), and the posing sentence) — Simpson, E. S. & Northrop, P. J., "Accounting for missing data when modelling block maxima", arXiv:2512.15429v1, Dept. of Statistical Science, UCL (read in full via arxiv.org/html/2512.15429v1; venue not stated in the text).
  UNIT OF ANALYSIS: the block (year, or season with n = 90 in simulations), carrying its count of non-missing values; applications are 162 years of declustered sea-surge heights at Brest and 27 years of daily maximum ozone at Plymouth.
  ENDPOINT: GEV (mu, sigma, xi) and r-block return levels; the simulation targets the 100-block return level.
  REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: four alternatives plus an unattainable benchmark — "naive" (ignore missingness), "discard" (drop blocks >10% missing), "weight1"/"weight2" (the weighted likelihoods of McVittie and Murphy), and "full" (the complete pre-missingness data).
  ESTIMAND: the bias in GEV parameters and return levels induced by missing observations within blocks, and its removal by a block-level non-missing-count adjustment — explicitly under ignorability: "We assume here that observations are missing completely at random in each block" and "must be non-informative, meaning that whether or not an observation is missing does not depend on its value." Reported numbers: 100-block return-level mean bias 0.025 (full) / 0.037 (adjust) / -0.090 (naive) in the exponential case; Brest 100-year level 104.5 cm adjusted vs 104.2 cm naive; Plymouth 216 vs 192 ug/m3 with xi moving from -0.28 (naive) to 0.00.
  DECISIVE CONTENT: the Discussion poses this candidate's limb (b) as an open need, in the candidate's own example, verbatim — "It is also quite likely for informative missingness to occur in environmental applications," / "but this is not accounted for in our current approach." / "It is easy to imagine situations where the most extreme events are the ones that are the hardest to reliably record," / "e.g., river flow gauges being damaged by fast-running water, and there is a need to be able to capture such phenomena." / "To account for this, one could attempt to explicitly model the missingness mechanism and incorporate this into the GEV model." / "One option is to take a regression-based approach, exploiting covariates that help to explain" / "whether or not values are missing." (Quoted from a second, targeted verbatim fetch, not from a summariser's paraphrase.)
P2 (NEAREST on execution) — McVittie, J. H. & Murphy, O. A., "Estimating Extreme Wave Surges in the Presence of Missing Data", arXiv:2506.15970v1 (read via arxiv.org/html/2506.15970v1; year not stated in the part read, cites 2024 work and uses data through 2023). Companion to arXiv:2506.15964, the paper the intake named as its nearest neighbour.
  UNIT OF ANALYSIS: block maxima of de-tided storm-surge series at three Atlantic-Canadian stations (Saint John, Yarmouth, Port-Aux-Basques); simulation blocks of 50 or 100 observations.
  ENDPOINT: GEV parameters and return levels — the 50-year level in simulations, the 20-, 50- and 100-year levels on the station data.
  REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: the standard observed-data likelihood that ignores missingness, plus the field practice of discarding blocks above a ~10-15% missingness threshold (attributed to Beck et al. 2020); its own alternatives are hard censoring, soft-unconditional, soft-conditional and EM.
  ESTIMAND: GEV parameters and return levels under missingness treated as CENSORING of the block maximum rather than as ignorable absence — with the mechanism explicitly not assumed MCAR ("In all cases, there was no evidence that the missing data mechanisms were MCAR"), the candidate's own causal story named as its Scenario III ("a failure of the equipment was attributed to the high intensity waves"; "The loss of power and flooding of the measurement apparati can result in gaps in the observed data series"), and a reported downstream effect: EM and conditional soft-censoring "predict a 20-year return level almost 1m higher" than the observed-likelihood fit at Saint John, while hard censoring gives "grossly inflated" levels.
  WHAT IT DOES NOT DO: it does not estimate the failure probability against measured magnitude. The censoring probabilities delta-hat are taken from the fitted GEV CDF or the empirical CDF of the observed values — a distributional assumption, not an estimate of h(y) from network data — and no y is reconstructed at a failed station.
P3 (nearest in the field that owns the METHOD for measurand-dependent missingness) — Bragato, P. L., "Regression Analysis with Truncated Samples and Its Application to Ground-Motion Attenuation Studies", BSSA 94(4) (landing page seismosoc.org/Publications/BSSA_html/bssa_94-4/03083.html opened; volume 94 implies 2004, inferred not confirmed on the page).
  UNIT OF ANALYSIS: individual ground-motion records (PGA) paired with magnitude and distance.
  ENDPOINT: the coefficients of a PGA attenuation relation.
  REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: standard lognormal regression on the untruncated sample, assessed on synthetic and real data.
  ESTIMAND: unbiased attenuation-law parameters from a sample truncated by the recording system itself — STA/LTA station triggering, with thresholds that "change randomly over time", handled by randomly truncated regression, explicitly without needing to know which stations failed to trigger.
  DIRECTION AND ENDPOINT BOTH DIFFER: the truncation bites at the LOW end (weak motions fail to trigger), the endpoint is a predictive attenuation model rather than a return level, and the missingness probability is never estimated as a function of amplitude. The clipping/instrument-damage end — records lost because shaking was too strong — is absent from this page; the Strathclyde PGV/PGD-recovery work found at query 6 addresses it as signal reconstruction on individual records, not as a hazard or a bias in a quantile.

Significance — consequence: If h(y) rises steeply in y, every published annual-maxima series in the affected networks is a truncated sample of its own tail and the conventional GEV/POT fit is biased low in a direction that current practice does not correct; P2's ~1 m shift in a 20-year surge level at one station is an existence proof that the correction is not cosmetic. If h(y) is flat in y, the field's standing ignorability assumption — which P1 states as an assumption and flags as doubtful — is licensed empirically for the first time, which is worth having precisely because it is currently asserted. The weakness, after Gate 2, is that this consequence is now largely P1's and P2's consequence rather than the candidate's: what the candidate adds is a measured h(y) in place of an imputed delta-hat, so its marginal consequence is the difference between an assumed and an estimated censoring probability. That difference is argued, not asserted: P2's censoring probabilities come from the same GEV being fitted, so the correction is self-referential, and an externally estimated h(y) would break that circularity. Whether breaking it moves a 100-year quantile by a reportable amount is unknown.
Significance — audience: network operators who own the only data for limb (a) — USGS (estimated-record and destroyed-gauge flags, and the disaster-recovery appropriations that pay for replacement), NOAA CO-OPS, the DBCP buoy programme, national met services and the wind-engineering post-storm survey community — would act by publishing outage-with-magnitude metadata in a form an analyst can condition on, and by siting/hardening decisions aimed at the magnitude range where h(y) turns up rather than at uptime in general. Reason for silence (candidate mechanism, offered as required even though the candidate is rejected): a disciplinary boundary that runs exactly between the two limbs, plus a conceptual bridge that was built so recently it explains the silence ending rather than persisting. Limb (a)'s data live in operators' asset-management and telemetry systems — outage tickets, QC flags, equipment-replacement appropriations — where the governing question is uptime and repair cost and where the measurand at the moment of failure is not an analysis variable; the USGS disaster-recovery page is the shape of this, publishing counts of destroyed gauges with no denominator and no magnitude. Limb (b)'s estimand lives in extreme-value statistics, where missingness entered as a nuisance to be weighted or discarded (the ~10-15% block-discard rule P2 attributes to Beck et al. 2020) and where the apparatus for non-ignorable selection was never pointed at the instrument. Neither community holds both halves, and the measurement that would join them — the realized peak at a station that stopped reporting — is by construction the one number the failed instrument did not produce, so it exists only as a reconstruction that the data owner has no reason to compile. What makes this mechanism credible rather than convenient is that it predicts its own expiry: the bridge was crossed in 2025 from the statistics side (P1 posing it, P2 executing a version of it), which is precisely when the question stopped being un-asked. A reason for silence that dates the silence's end to the year the literature actually moved is doing explanatory work; it is also why the Low grade is not an artefact of search luck.
