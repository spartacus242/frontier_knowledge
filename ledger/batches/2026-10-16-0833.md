## Verification pass: what it changed

Five fresh-context checkers, one per load-bearing prior or kill, each handed the claim and its source but not the reviewer's reasoning (93 queries; one checker lost 9 of 23 calls to access failures, below). Every one of the five entries needed correction, and two verdicts changed.

- **UQ-21 Rejected → Provisional.** The Low novelty grade rested on two priors and one is not what the record took it for. BMJ Open 2025;15(6):e097097 does not ask the census limb: it is a thematic synthesis of IDEAL/IDEAL-D-citing studies in which the modifications counted are predominantly surgical (procedure 66.6%, device 22.1%, patient selection 11.3%), the device modifications cover "components, number used, size, and how the device is applied" with no mention of software or firmware, the record source is published reports and protocols rather than manufacturer or regulatory records, and it links nothing to effect estimates. SMART is a reporting checklist. The NeOProM reading, by contrast, is confirmed verbatim — oximeter software era was one of nine pre-specified subgroup factors across five trials, interaction P = .03 on death at 18–24 months — so limb (ii) IS already answered for the oximeter family and that novelty claim is struck, while limb (i) was found asked nowhere.
- **UQ-23 Rejected → Provisional.** Fomina et al. 2023, the fatal Disguise kill, is a presence-of-evidence census with no design criterion of any kind; reading its 51%/2% as having settled design *adequacy* conflates "no study exists" with "the existing study's data are unfittable". Meer et al. 2015 is a genuine prior the record did not cite — it audits lactation PK studies against FDA plus ILCA and already reports two of the defects a residue-depletion lens would claim — so the scope and the standard, not the question, are what remain novel. The presupposition kill was also downgraded: the EMA machinery is limit-dependent, but the guideline's own footnote 1 licenses "another reference value" where no MRL exists, which makes it an assumption to declare rather than a false premise.
- **UQ-22's kill corrected and strengthened.** The record's "never measured" is refuted: Takao et al. 2016 (Front Behav Neurosci 10:99) measured removal-order effects on rectal temperature, hot-plate latency, elevated-plus-maze open-arm time and plasma corticosterone in roughly 1,700 control C57BL/6J mice, nine years before the prior the record led with. Schipper et al. 2025 is therefore neither first nor strongest, and its Methods could not be read, so whether its "testing order" is physical catch order at all is unverified.
- **UQ-24's feasibility objection was half wrong.** By-date retrieval IS implemented for currently-live IBM QPUs — `backend.properties(datetime=...)` maps to the REST parameter `updated_before`, is documented under "Retrieve historical data", and is exercised by a live CI test as of qiskit-ibm-runtime 0.50.0 (2026-09-24) — so the record's claim that it raises `NotImplementedError` is withdrawn. What carries the objection instead is retired devices (`IBMRetiredBackend.properties()` returns None; issue #2255 open since 2025-05-08; no bulk download, no archive; `fake_provider` snapshots carry no date) plus an undocumented retention depth. Also: IonQ does expose dated per-characterization history by REST, and AWS Braket states outright that historic calibration data is unavailable.
- **UQ-25's kill split in two.** Limb (b) is answered, with three corrections: Ain 1996 restricted a *set* of strengths rather than discontinuing one, the Ontario intervention removed five products at once, and the Ontario studies measured dispensing and dose trajectories rather than clinical outcomes — Guan et al. themselves call for patient-level outcome work. Limb (a), the variance share, has not been computed in any drug class, but neither has it ever been posed: the phenomenon is named in print and its coarser form is already answered descriptively (Zhang 2021: 22% of prescriptions not fillable on the marketed lattice).
