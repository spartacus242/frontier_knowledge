Alternative formulations:

Motivating gap:
Large-scale, dated, geolocated GNSS jamming and spoofing has been documented since 2022 over the Baltic, eastern Europe, the eastern Mediterranean and the Middle East (EASA SIB 2022-02, now R4; ICAO APAC 2025 and ICAO SAM 2026 "GNSS Vulnerabilities and Threats" briefings; IATA/ICAO MID summary reporting). The physical coupling to the meteorological product is direct: an aircraft-derived wind is the vector difference between the ground-velocity solution (on most modern airframes a hybrid IRS/GNSS solution) and the air-data true-airspeed/heading vector, and the report's position and time stamp are GNSS-derived for the surveillance-sourced stream. So interference can corrupt the wind value and can separately mis-place an otherwise correct temperature value by hundreds of kilometres. The volume at stake is large and growing: EMADDC alone produces about 55 million Mode-S EHS wind and 32 million temperature observations per day over European airspace (de Haan et al., AMT 2025) — i.e. the stream most exposed to the affected airspace is the one the meteorological community is actively expanding. Aviation has characterised the anomaly extensively as a navigation-safety and surveillance-integrity matter and has quantified its incidence at fleet scale; nothing found joins that exposure to observation-quality or analysis-impact statistics.

Presuppositions (E = established, C = contested/flagged):
1. (C, in degree) Aircraft-derived wind depends on a GNSS-informed ground-velocity solution. Pure-inertial or IRS-only-reverting airframes would show an unaffected wind value but still a mis-placed report. The per-fleet split must be established from avionics documentation (type certificate data sheets / AFM / FMS-GPS fit by type), not assumed. This is also the practical feasibility limit on limb (i)'s stratification: Mode-S ICAO address -> registry -> aircraft type is tractable; type -> navigation fit is documentable but laborious, and sub-type retrofit variation may be unresolvable.
2. (E, with a split) Position and time stamps in the assimilated stream are GNSS-derived. Holds for Mode-S/ADS-B-sourced observations; AMDAR positions come from the FMS navigation solution (hybrid, hence partially GNSS-derived). Confirmed as an architectural fact by the derivation described in de Haan et al. 2025 (wind from the air and ground vectors) and by the receiver-side timestamping noted in that paper's public review.
3. (C and load-bearing) Interference exposure can be geolocated and dated independently of the outcome. The usual exposure proxies are themselves GNSS-derived — this is now documented, not merely suspected: the Aireon anomaly product builds its indicators from PIC/NIC, Field Type Code 0, duplicate addresses and >20 NM position error, and GPSJAM-style maps are the percentage of aircraft in a hex cell reporting low navigation accuracy. Exposure misclassification would therefore be correlated with the outcome. V2 requires an exposure index built from non-GNSS evidence; Aireon's Independent Position Check (a TDOA-plus-kinematics reference track that does not rely on the aircraft's GPS) is a partial exception usable as a validation layer rather than the primary index.
4. (E, to be confirmed per centre) NWP centres retain observation-feedback archives for the window at per-report granularity including QC rejection flags (ODB-style feedback at ECMWF; equivalent at Met Office and NCEP).
5. (C) Enough aircraft reports exist inside affected airspace for power. Several affected regions saw traffic collapse or rerouting, which reduces sample size and confounds exposure with route, airline and aircraft-type mix; the Baltic and eastern Mediterranean retain high traffic and are the plausible analysis regions.
6. (C — and partly the object of measurement, not a premise) Operational QC is imperfect, so some corrupted observations are assimilated. Limb (iii) measures this; the question must not assume it.
7. (C) The NWP background is a usable reference in the affected regions, i.e. not itself substantially degraded by the same contaminated observations. Circularity to be broken with a reanalysis background or a data-denial reference analysis.

What would count as an answer:
A matched-exposure analysis over a pre-declared window and airspace using archived observation-feedback data (ECMWF/Met Office/NOAA feedback archives; E-AMDAR and national Mode-S/EMADDC archives) joined to a non-GNSS-derived interference exposure index, reporting: (i) exposed-versus-unexposed differences in O-B bias and standard deviation for u/v wind and temperature, stratified by aircraft navigation configuration; (ii) a rate of gross positional misassignment, detectable as kinematic inconsistency between successive reports from one airframe (and cross-checkable against an independent TDOA reference track); (iii) the fraction of corrupted reports surviving operational QC, the resulting analysis increments, and a data-denial OSE giving the forecast-score consequence. A clean null answers it equally: exposed and unexposed departure statistics indistinguishable inside a pre-declared equivalence band, with no excess QC rejection rate, means the interference does not reach the analysis and the question closes.

Significance — consequence:
If yes (exposed reports show excess bias/sd or gross misplacement and a material share survives QC): the operational consequence is immediate and specific. Variational QC and blacklisting are organised by platform ID and variable, not by airspace exposure, so the fix is a new kind of QC predictor — an exposure-conditioned rejection or error-inflation rule — and the affected volume is large (tens of millions of Mode-S-derived obs/day over Europe, the region containing the documented exposure). A mis-placed-but-physically-valid temperature is the worst case: it passes a departure check at the wrong location and injects a coherent, geographically displaced increment, which is precisely the error mode that background checks are poor at catching. It would also give aviation-safety and spectrum regulators a quantified non-safety cost term for interference, currently absent from the EASA/ICAO framing.
If no (clean null inside the equivalence band, no excess rejection): the question closes with real value — it licenses the ongoing expansion of Mode-S/ADS-B-derived observations into exactly the airspace where interference is worst, and it documents that avionics reversion plus existing QC is adequate, which is an argument the meteorological programmes cannot currently make with evidence.

Significance — audience:
(1) NWP observation-monitoring and data-assimilation groups (ECMWF, Met Office, DWD, Météo-France, KNMI, NCEP) — they would add an exposure field to feedback-archive diagnostics and potentially an exposure-aware QC rule; these are the people who already run the obstat/monitoring machinery query 6 surfaced. (2) The EUMETNET E-AMDAR and EMADDC programmes and the WMO JET-ABO — owners of the stream and of the guidance material; F5 shows their published record has not touched this. (3) Aviation-safety and spectrum regulators (EASA, ICAO, national CAAs) — a documented meteorological-product cost changes the cost-benefit of mitigation and the content of SIBs. (4) Secondarily, the ADS-B integrity community (Aireon, OpenSky, GPSJAM) — their products become inputs to a meteorological QC chain, which is a new use and a new validation requirement for them.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: how should assimilation QC treat an observation whose *position* rather than *value* is in error (a measurement-geometry rather than measurement-error problem, barely formalised for in-situ data)? What is the general sensitivity of any navigation-dependent observing system — ships' AIS-positioned reports, buoys, radiosonde GPS winds, UAS/wind-profiling drones, mobile-phone pressure networks — to navigation-infrastructure degradation, and should observation error covariances carry a navigation-integrity term at all? Does interference exposure need to be a routine, archived metadata field on aircraft reports (as flight-level and aircraft type are)? And for the satellite-navigation side: can meteorological O-B departures serve as an *independent* interference detector, since they are built from a non-GNSS physical reference (the air-data vector plus a model background) — inverting the question.
Significance — anchoring:
Touches: (a) the known, unresolved AMDAR/Mode-S temperature bias and error-characterisation problem (WMO temperature-bias task team; P1's AMDAR +0.5 K); (b) the general problem of correlated and non-Gaussian observation error in assimilation, where a jamming episode is a natural source of spatially and temporally clustered gross error; (c) the ECMWF aircraft data-denial literature, which quantified what losing aircraft data costs but not what keeping corrupted aircraft data costs; (d) the open aviation question — IFALPA's framing of interference as having "emerging unknown impacts on aircraft systems" — of what the full downstream footprint of interference is.

Reason for silence:
Primarily a disciplinary boundary with an asymmetric handoff. The exposure is documented, dated and mapped in aviation-safety, surveillance and spectrum channels (EASA SIBs, ICAO working papers, Aireon/GPSJAM products) whose outputs are not inputs to any meteorological workflow; the outcome lives in NWP feedback archives organised by platform ID, variable and date, with no airspace-exposure dimension to condition on. Neither side's monitoring is indexed by the other's key, so the join nobody has done is literally the join neither database supports. Two reinforcing mechanisms: (i) a new-data element — large-scale geolocated exposure products date only from 2022 (GPSJAM) and 2025 (Aireon's anomaly indicators), and the Mode-S/ADS-B meteorological stream reached operational volume over the same window (P1, 2025), so the two series have barely coexisted long enough to cross; (ii) an incentive and instrumentation gap in QC practice — operational QC is judged by whether the analysis is protected, not by characterising what it rejected and why, so variational QC and blacklisting absorb exactly this kind of corruption silently and remove the visible symptom (degraded forecasts) that would prompt the question. The rejection-rate time series that would show it exists in the archives but has no owner who looks at it by airspace.
