Question (canonical): For each of a pre-declared set of medication-error classes, restricted per class to orders whose barrier-capability set holds >=2 barriers (CPOE alert, pharmacist verification, barcode administration) with per-barrier outcome joinable to the order record: (a) estimate P(barrier k+1 fails | barrier k failed) against the same cohort's marginal P(barrier k+1 fails), with intervals; (b) express the coupling as a CCF beta factor (or alpha-factor set) with an interval; (c) report the ratio of realised all-barrier passage probability to the prediction from that same cohort's own marginals; (d) bound the unobserved "passed every barrier, never detected" stratum with declared assumptions; (e) record whether any of the site's own published barrier/FMEA/business-case analyses states a product of barrier failure probabilities. (V2; V1 and the Gate 1 reformulation rationale are in the record.)
Alternative formulations:
- Are a hospital's successive medication checks statistically independent, and if not, how much does adding another check really reduce the chance an error reaches the patient?
- Estimate the common-cause coupling between CPOE decision support, pharmacy verification and barcode administration for matched error classes, and the error in independence-based barrier calculations that follows.
Motivating gap: Medicine imported defence-in-depth as a qualitative metaphor; the apparatus that makes it quantitative (beta factor, IPL independence criterion, THERP dependence assessment) returns zero healthcare applications, while the field measures only per-barrier marginals. Corrected at Gate 4: the field's own professional literature does state the arithmetic — David U, "Double-checking: does it work?", Can J Hosp Pharm 2003;56(3) (ISMP Canada PDF, opened in full by A1) prints "the checking error rate x the task error rate" and "5% of 5%, or only 0.25%", and in the same column "not all double checks are truly independent" and "there is a high probability that the 2 individuals doing the checking will make the same error". The record's secondhand "ISMP Canada 2005, 1 in 400" is this 2003 column; the product stated is task-error x one check, not a product across barriers.
Presuppositions: (1) each barrier's per-order outcome electronically recorded and joinable — CPOE/BCMA established in principle, pharmacist-verification content granularity CONTESTED and unevidenced either way (leading feasibility risk); (2) a ground-truth error label assignable independently of the barriers — CONTESTED and central; a rule-computed label for CDS-covered classes reuses barrier 1's rule, so only blinded re-review is independent (A3); (3) >=2 barriers nominally capable per class — CONTESTED, and A3's own class analysis finds few classes with three capable barriers; (4) the field's reasoning multiplies barrier failure probabilities — now EVIDENCED at task x one check (CJHP 2003), still imputed across barriers; (5) event counts sufficient for the needed precision — CONTESTED, needs a power statement (Galanter: 32 intercepted wrong-patient errors in 127,320 alerts); (6) fixed barrier order — CONTESTED, order-of-arrival must be modelled.
What would count as an answer: per class, limbs (a)-(e) above with intervals; a beta indistinguishable from zero reported against a stated minimum detectable coupling answers negatively and is substantive. Defect carried forward (A2): limb (a)'s within-cohort marginal is not observable in a filter cascade — every error reaching barrier k+1 is already one barrier k failed to stop, so conditional and marginal coincide; the marginal requires a between-cohort comparator in which the upstream barrier was inactive (rule never configured, pre-implementation period, unit without BCMA). Positive-control injection is not a route for live orders.

**The two load-bearing quotations belong to a different article than the entry implied, and the correction matters.** "both people may be affected by the same disturbances in the environment" and "full independence cannot be achieved for checks" are the words of the commentary at BMJ Qual Saf 2020;29(7):536, not of the Koyama systematic review at 29(7):595. They are two articles in the same issue, the first commenting on the second. Attributing them to Koyama et al. would have put the commentary's framework-building claims into the mouth of the review it criticises for not distinguishing checking methods. The entry had them as unattributed, which was honest; they are now attributed, and the attribution is to the commentary. Two cautions carried forward: the second string is cut after "checks" where in place it is a "Therefore..." premise continuing into "and optimising independence works differently:", so quoted bare it reads as a verdict; and front matter for both articles remains COULD NOT READ, which is not NOT FOUND — both bodies were served.

**The decisive methodological question about Ford et al. 2012 could not be reached, and that is a different finding from the paper being silent.** Three publisher routes refused: doi.org returned HTTP 429 with a do-not-retry instruction, ScienceDirect was robots-disallowed, and the journal's own article page returned a client error. Two of the entry's figures for this paper are now marked unchecked above rather than carried, and one of its claims — every individual check below 50% — is withdrawn as an inference that does not follow from the sentence it was drawn from.
