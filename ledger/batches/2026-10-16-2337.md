## What verification found

- **UQ-61: the kill is confirmed verbatim, and the routed row with it.** All three strings are in
  Feldtkeller & Rudwaleit (*Ann Rheum Dis* 2014;73(12):e77): the DANBIO analysis "neglected … all the
  patients with recent symptom onset who were not yet diagnosed", its average delay is "a realistic
  approximation only for former disease-onset decades", and "in order to demonstrate this reduction
  reliably, we need another 20 years passing by." The letter proposes no correction — waiting is its
  only remedy — so the question is asked in print and unanswered there, which is the asked-but-open
  shape exactly. Birrell et al. 2012 is confirmed as a Bayesian back-calculation that identifies
  time-to-diagnosis and undiagnosed prevalence jointly *because* CD4 at diagnosis supplies a
  time-since-infection clock. Rare disease has no counterpart, which is why the entry's P5 survives
  and why the routed row is narrowed to the limb that needs no incidence-anchored frame.
- **UQ-62: the figures hold, and the unreadable residual turned out to close the limb this batch was
  about to route.** Borad et al. 2025 is confirmed as printed — "28.1% (78/278) of those STS pairs and
  18.5% (12/65) of those MTS pairs grew commensal organisms in both BC sets (P = .11)" — but the
  denominator is commensal-positive pairs, the quantity is a proportion rather than an agreement
  statistic, nothing tests independence, and the authors concede "We also did not adjudicate whether
  patients had true bacteremia". Their Discussion only *poses* the consequence: single-site sampling
  "may increase the risk of misclassifying patients as having true bacteremia". That looked routable.
  The second pass then identified the ICHE paper the batch could not open: Senger, Saccozza & Yüce,
  *Infect Control Hosp Epidemiol* 2007;28(8):992-996, doi 10.1086/518753, which scores CDC criteria
