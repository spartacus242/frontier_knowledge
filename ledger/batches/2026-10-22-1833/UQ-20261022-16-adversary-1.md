## Restatement in my own words

Take some open-weight models as they ship. Two bad sets of vocabulary IDs exist for each: U, tokens a detector says the model was (nearly) never trained on; R, tokens the tokenizer can never produce from text. Give the model ordinary prompts and let it generate freely from its own output. Then: (i) how much next-token probability, before any top-k/top-p/min-p cut, sits on U and on R per step (mean and upper tail); (ii) after applying the sampler the release itself declares in `generation_config.json` or its model card (or a substitute arm, labelled as such, when none is declared), on what share of steps is at least one U-token (or R-token) still eligible to be sampled; (iii) how many U- and R-tokens actually come out per million generated tokens, compared with a control set of tokens chosen to have the same unigram frequency profile. Short form: do shipped samplers already seal the glitch-token hole on the output side, or does it leak, and by how much?

## Attack 4 — presupposition clauses, written out and tested

Every clause the question needs true before it can be asked, as distinct from what it would measure:

C1. Shipped open-weight releases carry a non-trivial detector-defined set U. — TRUE (sourced). GlitchMiner Table 2 (F3, full read): e.g. 1839 tokens detected at @2000 for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, 302 for Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3; record's full read of Land & Bartolo Table 1 (24 model rows) concurs. Both are vocabulary-item counts, which is exactly what C1 needs.

C2. Shipped tokenizers carry a set R of unreachable items. — TRUE in kind on the record's full-text quotes from Land & Bartolo (Sec. 3.2: "over 1,400 manually added tokens of emojis are categorized as unreachable" for Command R; "over 200 unreachable tokens" for OLMo). I did not open that paper myself; I rely on the record's quoted lines. R may be empty for some tokenizers; the question reports per release, so an empty R is a result, not a failure.

C3. Releases ship a declared default truncation rule in `generation_config.json` or model card. — CONTESTED on two instances I opened, and CONVERTED by the question. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (F1) ships `do_sample: true, temperature 0.7, top_p 0.8, top_k 20`; Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 (F2) ships only bos/eos and no sampler at all. So the clause is true of some releases and false of others. The canonical wording already provides "an explicitly declared substitute arm recorded as such for releases that ship none", so this clause is a reported case-split, not an assumption: MEASURAND EXEMPTION APPLIES to C3 and attack 4 does not reach it. Two residual wording gaps, neither fatal: (a) Qwen's declared default includes `repetition_penalty: 1.05`, which is a history-dependent logit warp applied before truncation and is not a "truncation rule"; the question must say whether the declared arm includes it (it changes the pre-truncation mass in limb (i) as well as support in limb (ii)); (b) for an artifact like Mistral's with no `do_sample`, HF `generate` defaults to greedy, under which post-truncation support is the argmax alone and limb (ii) degenerates to "is the argmax in U" — the substitute-arm clause absorbs this, but the record should say the substitute arm is what carries the Mistral-class releases, i.e. for an unknown share of releases the "release's own" arm does not exist.

C4. Per-step post-truncation support membership is a well-defined quantity under top-p/top-k/min-p/temperature. — TRUE in kind: Awad et al. (record's Prior 1, full read) build and run exactly this instrument on shipped defaults; GlitchMiner's silence on samplers (F3) does not contradict it.

C5. A control set "matched on unigram frequency profile" exists for U and for R. — CONTESTED, by my own inference from a quoted definition (labelled as such). Land & Bartolo define unreachable tokens as "those that are never produced as a result of tokenizing text" (record's verbatim quote). It follows that every member of R has unigram frequency exactly zero in ANY tokenized corpus, so a frequency-matched control for R must be drawn from the zero-count bin, which after excluding U ∪ R may be empty or tiny, and whatever remains in it is itself indistinguishable from U by the frequency criterion. For U the problem is softer (U is "under-trained", near-zero rather than zero, and the matching corpus would be a proxy, not the training set) but the same confound applies: the matching variable is the variable that defines the set. This is a limb (iii) null-design defect, not a question-killing clause — limbs (i) and (ii) do not depend on it, and limb (iii) could be reported against a differently defined null (e.g. tokens ranked just above the detector threshold, or matched on output-embedding norm). I did not open a source that states this problem; it is derived from the quoted definition.

C6. "Free self-conditioned generation" on a "pre-declared prompt set" is a defined operating condition. — TRUE trivially; standard practice.

C7. "Under-trained" is detector-relative. — handled by the question (report per detector); P2 in the record quotes both detector definitions. Not a failure.

No clause the question cannot survive without came back FALSE or CONTESTED on a source I opened. C3 is contested on two opened instances and the question already converts it into a reported case-split; C5 is contested by inference and affects only the null of one limb.
