Question (canonical): Across the published in vitro reconstitution literature for a declared process family, what is the distribution of the ratio between each component's concentration in the reconstitution and its measured concentration in the cell where the process is claimed to occur, and in what fraction of reconstitutions does the demonstrated sufficiency fail when every component is held at its cellular concentration?

Alternative formulations: Does the reconstitution literature show that purified components are sufficient for a cellular process, or only that they are sufficient at concentrations the cell never reaches? / For each published reconstitution, how far above cellular concentration was each component pushed to make the process run, and does that fold-excess predict whether additional cellular factors were later found to be required? (Gate 1 reformulation V2 — one named process family, a declared comparator ladder of cell-average / compartment-average / local-enrichment allowance <=10^2, and a bench subset re-run with a pre-declared detection limit — is in the record; it was scored, not filed as a separate candidate.)

Motivating gap: Intake inverted the presupposition behind "purified components are sufficient", claiming the worry is argued only qualitatively in the condensate subfield and "nowhere quantified as a distribution over a frame". That central negative is false for the leading limb: the fold-excess distribution was published in 2021 over a declared frame, with the candidate's own PaxDb comparator, inside the candidate's own declared secondary subfield.

Presuppositions: (1) reconstitution papers report component concentrations in extractable units — contested and evidenced biased (Prior 1 could form the ratio for 75/89 frame proteins, 30 of those lacking a usable Csat, even with four curated registries); (2) a matched cellular concentration exists per component — contested; (3) cell-average total concentration is the right comparator — contested and resolved against the candidate by its own nearest prior; (4) "sufficiency fails at cellular concentration" is decidable against a declared detection limit — designable, but Prior 2's conditions were tuned post hoc; (5) the in vitro process is the cellular process — contested case by case, no adjudication rule; (6) later discovery of a required factor is codable without hindsight bias — no precedent found; (7) buffer, crowding and temperature are secondary to concentration — contested and probably false (Prior 2's best match needed pH 5.8, 30 C, 300 mM KOAc, ATP). Full list in the record.

What would count as an answer: a pre-declared frame; per (paper, component) in-vitro concentration paired with a matched cellular concentration, each carrying its uncertainty; a log-fold-excess distribution plus the fraction of reconstitutions above pre-declared thresholds; a bench subset re-run with every component at cellular concentration against a declared detection limit, with buffer/crowding/temperature as declared arms; and a pre-registered association test between maximum fold-excess and later identification of required factors. Intake rightly allowed the symmetric answer (small fold-excesses, sufficiency surviving) as equally decisive — and that answer is already partly on the record and mixed.
