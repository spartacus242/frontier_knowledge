## Gate 0 — Intake

### Question (canonical, as submitted)

For aliquot sequences under the sum-of-proper-divisors map, is the per-step multiplicative drift conditional on survival equal to the unconditional aliquot constant — that is, does the mean of log(s(N)/N) over iterates N of sequences that have already survived k steps or reached d digits change with k or d, and is any change accounted for by the changing occupancy of Guy-Selfridge drivers among the survivors?

### Alternative formulations (as submitted)

1. Do long-surviving aliquot sequences drift upward more strongly than a random even integer of the same size, and by how much — the hazard-rate question that reliability analysis asks of any surviving population?
2. Is the aliquot constant a stationary drift for the iterated map, or only an average over starting values, and what is the drift in the quasi-stationary distribution of surviving iterates?

### Motivating gap (re-stated, with my own read levels)

Bosma & Kane compute an **unconditional** average over starting values and say in their own Remark 2.3 that it "needs to be combined with the persistance of drivers as in [7]" (ref. [7] = Guy & Selfridge). Bosma 2016 computes the **survival counts** by digit threshold and no growth rate. The candidate's unit is an **iterate conditioned on survival**, which lies outside both. Verified against both papers at full text (below). What the candidate's intake did **not** know, and what Gate 2 found, is that Pomerance has a paper proving the per-step drift at step k equals the same constant for every fixed k. That is the near-duplicate.

## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

### Defects in the submitted wording

1. **"survived k steps" is undefined.** Survival must be stated: not yet absorbed at 1 and not yet entered a cycle (perfect, amicable or sociable). For even seeds the practical absorption route is a parity switch, which Pomerance states happens "if and only if one hits a square or its double" (Section 1, p. 2) and calls "rare".
2. **The pre-declared frame is named and not generated.** The submitted wording requires "a declared size window in which every iterate of every sequence in the frame is fully factored" and never states the window or the rule that makes it. LEDGER.md's Gate 1 requires a set to be enumerated or generated by a stated rule.
3. **"the aliquot constant" is not pinned** to a definition or a value.
4. **"Guy-Selfridge drivers"** is a pre-declared class that must be enumerated or generated.
5. **No effect-size threshold** is declared for "changes with k or d", so the first limb could be answered either way by a small enough deviation.

### Reformulation (the one permitted at this gate; both versions recorded)

> Over the frame of **all 499 999 even starting values n < 10^6**, each sequence iterated until termination at 1, a cycle, or a first iterate of 100 decimal digits — i.e. Bosma 2016's computed frame, inside which every iterate is fully factored by construction — let a row be one iterate N, labelled with its step index k, its decimal length d, a survived/absorbed flag, and its driver class D. Does the mean of log(s(N)/N) over surviving rows vary with d across 10 <= d <= 99, and does it vary with k; and is any variation reproduced by the decomposition sum_D P(D | survived, d) * E[log(s(N)/N) | D], where D ranges over the Guy-Selfridge driver classes together with a no-driver class and an odd-iterate class?

**Definitions now pinned.** lambda := the constant of Bosma & Kane's Theorem 1.1, with their bound `lambda < -0.030` (Corollary 5.10, p. 16) and geometric mean `mu <= 0.969 ...` (Theorem 1.1, p. 2). Driver classes: enumerated in Bosma 2016 as "the even perfect numbers and 2, 24, 120, 672, 523776", generated by the rule it states — a divisor 2^k * m of an even n with 2^k || n, odd part m dividing sigma(2^k) = 2^(k+1) - 1, and 2^(k-1) dividing sigma(m). **Which of the two I did: both — the finite list is enumerated and the generating rule is stated.** Caveat recorded below: I have this rule **second-hand from Bosma 2016 and in a damaged extraction**, not from Guy & Selfridge 1975, which I could not open.

### What the repair itself presupposes (tested as hard as the clause it replaced)

- **That the frame's iterates are available or regenerable.** Bosma 2016 pursued every seed below 10^6 to 100 digits, which cannot be done without factoring each iterate, so the factorisations existed; whether they were retained is a data-availability matter and not a mathematical one, since every iterate is recomputable. Acceptable.
- **That the d-bins hold enough rows.** Bosma's printed table gives 247 191 even sequences surviving at d = 10 and 169 548 at 100 digits. **My own arithmetic:** at |lambda| ~ 0.0332597 per step in natural log, a sequence crosses one decimal digit only every log(10)/0.0332597 ~ 69.2 steps, so each surviving sequence contributes of order tens of rows per digit class. Rows are ample.
- **That the 100-digit ceiling does not bias the top bins — it does, and the repair makes this sharper rather than vacuous.** A row at d = 99 whose image exceeds 100 digits is the last observed row of its sequence, so the frame is right-censored at the top by a rule that depends on the very ratio being averaged. That is informative censoring **inside** the window, and it is now a concrete, bounded nuisance rather than an abstract one. This is the labelled prerequisite limb.
- **That the driver class is defined for every row.** It is not: drivers are defined for even n. Odd iterates (reached only via a square or twice a square) need their own class, which the reformulation adds.
- **The repair does not buy well-posedness with a record frame.** The frame is "all even n < 10^6" — a rule over integers — and the rows are integers. Naming Bosma 2016 names who computed them, not what they are.

### What would count as an answer

The measured conditional drift E[log(s(N)/N) | survived, d] and E[... | survived, k] on the declared window, with the driver decomposition above, set against lambda with sign and magnitude and against a stated effect-size threshold. Alternatively a theorem: that the quasi-stationary drift of surviving iterates differs from lambda, or that it cannot. **Note what the literature already supplies here (Gate 2):** a theorem of almost exactly the second shape already exists for every **fixed** k.

### Presuppositions

1. **Practical and CONTESTED, and it is the transplant's own content.** s(N) requires N's full factorisation, so observability is size-dependent and the censoring is informative. **Status after the reformulation: largely discharged inside the window and sharpened at its edge** — within the 10^6/100-digit frame every iterate is factored by construction, and the residual censoring is the right-censoring at d = 99 named above.
2. **ESTABLISHED as an average, and now verified at full text.** Bosma & Kane, Theorem 1.1 (p. 2): "The geometric mean mu of s(2n)/2n over all positive integers n exists"; Corollary 5.10 (p. 16): "lambda < -0.030". Whether lambda is also the surviving-iterate drift is the question and is not assumed.
3. **ESTABLISHED but only SECOND-HAND.** Drivers are well defined and computable from an iterate's factorisation. Source actually opened: Bosma 2016's reproduction of the definition and its list of the known drivers. **Guy & Selfridge 1975, the primary source, is COULD NOT READ (routes below), so this presupposition rests on a secondary reproduction with a garbled extraction.**
4. **MY OWN READING, NOT A SOURCE FINDING — now PARTLY GROUNDED and partly still mine.** That both sides of the Catalan-Dickson / Guy-Selfridge dispute turn on conditional survivor behaviour. *Grounded:* Pomerance's Introduction (p. 2) reads the sign of the constant as "evidence in favor of Catalan-Dickson and against Guy-Selfridge", so the inferential load on this one average is on the record; and his Section 1 (p. 2) states the Guy-Selfridge mechanism as "certain divisors (such as 24), known as 'drivers', persist for long stretches in the sequence". *Still mine:* that the dispute is *best* read as a disagreement about survivor-conditioned behaviour. I could not open Guy & Selfridge 1975 to ground it from the primary side, and I say so rather than inheriting the claim.
5. **Estimability — the intake's figure was WRONG and is corrected here.** The intake said "about 20% of even starts exceeding 20 digits". Bosma 2016 actually prints 169 548 even sequences still open at 100 digits against a stated 499 999 even starting values, i.e. **33.9% (my arithmetic)**, and "At 100 digits, there are still 9327 different open main sequences." Pomerance independently characterises Bosma's result as "about 1/3 of the even seeds" surpassing 10^99. Estimability is amply satisfied; the intake's number is struck.

### 4. The near-duplicate, with numerator / denominator / overlap in both directions

**The headline limb is already a published theorem.** Pomerance proves that the per-step mean log-growth taken at step k equals the *same* constant as the unconditional average:

- **Theorem 1.1 (p. 3), verbatim:** "The average value of log(s2(2n)/s(2n)) is asymptotically equal to the average value of log(s(2n)/2n)." The displayed formula, as the summariser reconstructed it from a garbled layout and labelled as a reconstruction: (1/x) sum_{2<=n<=x} log(s^2(2n)/s(2n)) ~ (1/x) sum_{1<=n<=x} log(s(2n)/2n) ~ beta.
- **Conjecture 2.3 (p. 5), verbatim:** "If A is a set of natural numbers of asymptotic density 0, then s^{-1}(A) has asymptotic density 0."
- **Theorem 2.4 (p. 5), verbatim:** "Assuming Conjecture 2.3, then for each integer k >= 2 there is a set Ak of asymptotic density 1 such that" — followed by (1/x) sum_{n<=x, n in Ak} log(s^k(n)/s^{k-1}(n)) -> beta as x -> infinity.
- **The number, and where it is printed:** "beta ~ -0.03" — Introduction, p. 2, in the sentence "Namely, they showed that there is a real number beta such that ... and that beta ~ -0.03 is negative", repeated in Section 2, p. 3. Units: natural log per step, dimensionless. Denominator: **x, a count of seeds n <= x.** The sharper digits are Bosma & Kane's, not his: `lambda < -0.030` (Corollary 5.10, p. 16) and computed partial averages approaching "-0.0332597045..." at N = 3.953 * 10^9 (Example 3.14 table, p. 10).

**(a) Pomerance's NUMERATOR CLASS, from his own statements.** log(s^k(n)/s^{k-1}(n)) — the natural log of the one-step multiplicative ratio taken at the k-th application of s. **Exclusions the definition states:** for k >= 2 the average is restricted to n in A_k, a density-1 set built from A = "the set of integers n such that for each m <= log2 n/(log3 n) [extraction damaged] 2 there are at least 5 distinct primes p || n with p == -1 (mod n)" — *reproduced as served; the summariser flagged that the modulus is printed as n where the argument needs m, and I do not correct it* — minus the s-preimages of A's complement.

**(b) Pomerance's DENOMINATOR, checked against the near neighbours it is not.** It is **x, a count of STARTING VALUES** (Theorem 1.1: even seeds 2n with n <= x; Theorem 2.4: seeds n <= x lying in A_k). It is **not** a count of iterates, **not** a count of survivors, and **not** a count of iterates in a size class. Near-neighbour discrimination: *seeds-up-to-x* (Pomerance, Bosma-Kane) vs *sequences open at digit threshold d* (Bosma 2016, a count and not an average of a ratio) vs *iterates at d digits conditioned on survival* (the candidate).

**(c) THE OVERLAP, both directions.**

*What Pomerance's class MISSES that the candidate includes.* (i) **Iterates binned by their own size far above the seed bound** — his limit is x -> infinity with k fixed, so an iterate's size tracks the seed bound; the regime that motivates the question (276 at 175+ digits from a 3-digit seed) is never reached. (ii) **Any variation of the drift with k, and the k -> infinity / quasi-stationary limit** — each theorem fixes k *before* letting x -> infinity and returns the same beta for each fixed k; it licenses no interchange of the two limits, which is precisely the content of "does it change with k". (iii) **Conditioning on SURVIVAL.** A_k is an *arithmetic regularity* set (a prime-factorisation condition), not the event "not yet terminated and not yet cycled". (iv) **The density-0 set**, which his own remark says carries the bulk of the mass: immediately after Theorem 2.4's proof (p. 6) the text reads "We do not have an analogue of Theorem 2.4 for log(sk(n)/sk-1(n))" "since presumably the sum of these terms is principally supported on a set of n of density 0." (v) **Any driver decomposition** — see the enumerated absence below.

*What Pomerance's class INCLUDES that the candidate excludes.* (i) **Odd n** — Theorem 2.4 ranges over all n <= x in A_k, not only even n, whereas the candidate's survivors are essentially all even (Pomerance, Section 1, p. 2: "It is rare for a sequence to switch parity, this occurs if and only if one hits a square or its double"). (ii) **Seeds whose sequences terminate or cycle later**, which the candidate's survival conditioning removes. (iii) **Iterates of every size up to about x**, unbinned.

**(d) Internal consistency of the priors, checked before leaning on them.** Two flags, reported rather than resolved. **Pomerance:** the sentence served after Theorem 2.4's proof denies having "an analogue of Theorem 2.4 for log(sk(n)/sk-1(n))", which is self-referentially inconsistent, since Theorem 2.4 *is* about that quantity; the coherent reading is that the missing analogue is of **Theorem 1.1** — the *unrestricted* average for k >= 3 — but I report the string as served and flag it rather than correcting it. **Bosma-Kane:** Theorem 1.1 prints "mu <= e^{-0.03...}" and "mu <= 0.969 ... < 1" while Corollary 5.10 prints "lambda < -0.030"; **my own arithmetic:** e^{-0.030} = 0.9704, so 0.969 is strictly stronger than -0.030 yields and the elided digits must run further (e^{-0.0315} = 0.9690). Also flagged by the summariser: Example 3.14 indexes by "2n <= N" while Theorem 3.7 normalises by 1/N over n = 1..N, so the -0.0332597045... figure's denominator is not the same N as the theorem's.

**Clause asserted, denied, or moved away from.** Every quotation above sits in a clause that **asserts** the quantity, with two exceptions I name because the rules require it: the "We do not have an analogue" sentence **denies** that the unrestricted version is available, and Bosma-Kane's Remark 2.3 opener "To draw conclusions about the Catalan-Dickson conjecture, one needs more than just the arithmetic or geometric mean of sigma(n)/n" **scopes the whole remark to the inferential gap** — dropping that opener would turn a statement about what is insufficient into a free-standing programme. Likewise Bosma-Kane's "Iterating s provides long sequences of more or less random numbers of similar size" (p. 2) is a **remark about computation**, not a result about the iterate distribution, and must not be quoted as one.

## Gate 3 — Significance

**Consequence (yes/no).** If the survivor-conditioned drift at large d differs from lambda, then the sign of lambda stops being evidence about the regime that decides Catalan-Dickson, because the sequences in doubt are survivors at 100+ digits and not random even seeds. That inference is on the record and is not my construction: Pomerance's Introduction (p. 2) reads the negative constant as "evidence in favor of Catalan-Dickson and against Guy-Selfridge". If instead the drift is flat in d and k, the Guy-Selfridge driver-persistence objection is quantified away rather than argued away, and Bosma-Kane's Remark 2.3 — "This needs to be combined with the persistance of drivers as in [7]" — is discharged with a number. Either answer moves a named inference, and the driver decomposition says *through which mechanism*.

**Audience.** Two communities, and they would act differently. (i) The analytic side working on s-iteration (Pomerance; Pollack-Pomerance) — a measured d-dependence would tell them whether the density-0 set their theorems exclude is where the drift lives, which is the obstruction Pomerance names himself. (ii) The computational aliquot projects (Bosma's frame; the Jacobson-Mosunov line; the distributed factoring effort behind the 276 sequence) — a driver-conditional drift is directly actionable in deciding which open sequences are worth further factoring effort, since factoring cost rises steeply in d and the driver class is observable before the next step is taken.

**Fecundity.** It opens the quasi-stationarity question for arithmetic dynamics as such: is there a Yaglom-type limit for the s-map, and on what state space? Query 6 found that vocabulary entirely unapplied here. It makes Pomerance's Conjecture 2.3 and his density-0 support remark quantitative rather than presumptive. And it transposes immediately to other arithmetic iterations with absorbing states — sigma-iteration, Euler-phi iteration, 3x+1 — where "is the drift of survivors the drift of the population?" is the same question with different absorption.

**Anchoring.** Catalan-Dickson versus Guy-Selfridge; Pomerance's explicitly-unavailable unrestricted average for k >= 3; his Conjecture 2.3; the 276 sequence ("thousands of iterates have been computed, reaching beyond 175 decimal digits"); Bosma-Kane's Remark 2.3.

**Score: Significance 2/3.** Argued, with a named audience and a stated action. Held below 3 because the fixed-k half of the headline limb is already settled, so the live headroom is the d-binned/quasi-stationary half plus the driver decomposition, and the acting community is small.

## Reason for silence

A technical obstruction sitting on a disciplinary boundary, and the obstruction is visible in the prior itself. The analytic machinery that makes lambda tractable is Davenport-type density of t-abundant numbers, which applies to averages over *starting values*; the survivor-conditioned object needs the orbit data, which lives with the distributed-computing aliquot projects, and the natural formalism for it — quasi-stationarity, hazard conditional on survival — belongs to probability and reliability, whose vocabulary query 6 shows has never been pointed at this map. Pomerance's paper is the proof that this is an obstruction and not an oversight: the analytic side reached for the step-k version, got it only on a density-1 set, and stopped exactly where the density-0 set begins, saying so ("presumably the sum of these terms is principally supported on a set of n of density 0"). Bosma 2016 had every iterate in hand and computed counts rather than ratios. Nobody was positioned to hold both halves at once. **Not** "no one thought of it": for the fixed-k limb, someone did, and proved it.
