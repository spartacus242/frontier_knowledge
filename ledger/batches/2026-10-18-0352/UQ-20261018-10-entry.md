Domain(s): Other STEM — electrical metrology / power engineering; secondary legal metrology (method owner: sampling inspection in legal metrology)
Question (canonical): For one declared national installed base of static residential electricity meters, taking a probability sample stratified by meter model, firmware revision and build year from a distribution utility's asset register, and applying a pre-declared library of residential load mixes synthesised from a published appliance current-waveform set against a reference instrument whose immunity to those same stimuli is demonstrated rather than assumed: what is the population-weighted distribution of relative registration error per mix (mean with CI, plus lower and upper tail quantiles), and, under a declared national load-composition model, what fraction of billed residential energy does that distribution imply is misregistered? (v2; the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation of v1, which asked the same thing without a sampling frame, a declared mix library, a demonstrated-immune reference, or the conditional status of the billed-energy limb.)
Alternative formulations:
1. Do the waveforms used in meter type approval and periodic sample testing represent the loads meters actually see, and what does the installed base register under realistic household load mixes?
2. Is there any population-level estimate of static-meter over- and under-registration, as opposed to laboratory demonstrations that large errors can occur?
Motivating gap: The field is dense with how-to-answer apparatus (IEC 62052/62053, EN 50470, utility periodic sample testing, verification procedures) and appears empty of the population value. The anomaly is on record — Leferink/Keyer/Melentjev reported -32% to +582% on mixes of common consumer electronics (IEEE EMC Magazine, 2016), disputed, partly replicated by VSL/UTwente, and followed by standards activity — and the party holding the asset register has publicly asserted the null from complaint counts without an estimate.
Presuppositions: P1 effect real, not a 2016 setup artefact — now supported independently of 2016 (VSL/UTwente deck; INRIM 2024 found one of six models at about +25% under a recorded field waveform; arXiv 2601.16612 finds it across manufacturers). P2 error reproducible enough to pre-declare a mix — supported in outline (MeterEMI waveform library and testbed). P3 register supports model/build-year strata — EVIDENCED (Dutch steekproefcontrole; Eversource files lot sizes from installed meter counts per model); firmware strata unevidenced. P4 a demonstrably immune reference exists — established in outline, to be shown per mix. P5 in-service testing uses conditions under which the effect cannot appear — now evidenced twice beyond the Dutch document (Eversource's NH PUC filing states no amperes, power factor or waveform; arXiv 2601.16612: verification is "performed under ideal conditions or with simple signal models", IEC 62053-24 testing "only a 5-th order harmonic") but still by omission. P6 misregistration not already absorbed in loss accounting — UNEXAMINED; a loss-accounting sweep found no decomposition of unaccounted-for energy into a metering component. P7 household composition has shifted — in outline only; no household field-waveform evidence was opened, and the one field waveform tested (INRIM) came from a 50 kW PV site, not a home.
What would count as an answer: an error distribution measured on a probability sample of in-service meters drawn by model/firmware/build-year strata from a real asset register, against a reference with demonstrated immunity, under a pre-declared mix library — population-weighted mean relative error with CIs and tail quantiles per mix, plus a conditional energy-weighted misregistered fraction under a declared composition model. The null (distribution inside the class limit, thin tails) is pre-specified, decisive and publishable, and is what the incumbents already assert.

PRIOR 2 — Hartman & van den Brom, VSL/UTwente FHI deck 2023: unit = meter specimen x load waveform, 24 meters "representing the installed base in the Netherlands", >70 investigated; endpoint = error sign and mechanism by edge/phase case and waveform feature; comparator = traceable Yokogawa WT500 on a 140-TMX source; estimand = per-specimen conditional error, plus an unreported "Impact study in the Netherlands based on statistics". PRIOR 3 — in-service statistical sample testing as actually practised: Netbeheer Nederland "Steekproefcontrole Energiemeters" (2024) and Eversource's 2021 Meter Testing Program report to the NH PUC, docket 18-162: unit = a meter population/lot by model, kind and build year, sampled from the installed count; endpoint = conformity, and under ANSI/ASQ Z1.9 also per-lot sample mean, estimated lot sigma and estimated percent nonconforming each side of the limits (e.g. Centron lot 476,038, n=201, mean 100.001%, s=0.0951); comparator = the legal maximum permissible error under standard registration test points; estimand = accept/reject plus, in the US variables plans, a population error distribution. Fourth, recorded as an answer-claim not a study: Netbeheer Nederland's 2023 grid-operators' response — tens of complaint cases across >8 million households, estimand an assertion of absence in practice with no measurement design.
Novelty — how this differs from each: vs PRIOR 1, MeterEMI builds the instrument this question would use and stops at the test method; nothing opened contains a per-model comparison table, a sample size or a fleet figure. vs PRIOR 2, lab convenience selection of types vs probability sample from a register; per-specimen error vs population-weighted distribution with CIs and tails; no energy weighting; exploratory rather than pre-declared mix. vs PRIOR 3 — and this is the correction the record did not have: the record graded novelty partly on the claim that acceptance sampling "cannot produce an error distribution no matter how many meters it tests". That claim is FALSIFIED by a document adversary 2 opened; Z1.9 variables plans publish exactly that object annually, by model, from the asset register. Strike it. What survives is narrower: the surviving difference is the substituted stimulus (declared realistic high-crest-factor mixes vs standard registration test points, which the same sources show are sinusoidal or single-harmonic) plus limb 2's energy weighting, neither of which any of the 29 queries found measured. The stimulus substitution is not cosmetic — INRIM 2024's +25% model is in class under EN 50470's 5th-harmonic test and far outside it under a recorded field waveform — so this is not judged a covariate or instrument swap on an asked question; but novelty now rests entirely on it.
Novelty confidence: Medium
Significance — consequence: if the distribution has material mass outside the class limit under declared realistic mixes, the in-service sampling regime is measuring under a stimulus that cannot reveal it (so conforming and misregistering populations are indistinguishable under it), the draft waveforms handed to CENELEC/IEC/OIML become calibrated against measured population harm rather than precautionary, and a two-signed error opens a transfer-between-customers problem rather than a net-overcharge one. The null is equally consequential and is the incumbents' own position: it closes a dispute running since 2016 and redirects the work to specific appliance classes.
Significance — audience: NMIs already staffed on this object (VSL, PTB, NPL, METAS, INRIM); verification and market-surveillance authorities with a standing annual decision the answer feeds (RDI's rejected-population lists, UK OPSS/IST, US state PUCs receiving annual meter-testing filings, German/Austrian recertification-extension sampling); the standards bodies named by MeterEMI; DSOs and energy regulators; consumer bodies. They are demonstrably already acting with the estimate missing.
Significance — fecundity: whether registration error is socially patterned (dimmer/LED/heat-pump/EV penetration, income); whether decades of steekproefcontrole and Z1.9 data can be re-analysed as error distributions; the sign distribution and hence cross-subsidy vs loss; how much DSO non-technical loss is misregistration; transfer to gas, water, heat, PV and EV submetering and to the 2-150 kHz band; how to make a mix library auditable so "realistic" is contestable rather than the testing party's choice.
Significance — anchoring: the 2016 Leferink anomaly and its unresolved dispute; the 2-150 kHz supraharmonic emission/immunity gap; the acceptance-sampling-versus-estimation distinction in legal metrology; MID 2014/32/EU and the OIML R46 revision; non-technical-loss accounting.
Reason for silence: partly broken, and this is why attack 7 is Unresolved. Mechanism 1 (disciplinary boundary, the method field's estimand being incapable of an error distribution) is false as stated — see PRIOR 3. Mechanism 3 (the register-holder prefers a regime that cannot produce the estimate) is jurisdiction-bound: in several US states that party is compelled to file the estimate publicly. What survives is mechanism 2, instrument: a programmable realistic-mix source plus a transportable reference with demonstrated immunity to the same stimuli arrived only with MeterEMI's testbed (2018-2021), and the EMC and revenue-metering literatures do not cite each other. That explains why the measurement is hard, not why nobody posed it, and it does not account for the eight years since 2016; the remaining support for silence is that the question could not be found, which is the novelty finding restated.
