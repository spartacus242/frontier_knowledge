## REGIME

The document's own regime, in its own words: it documents **"the v5.2 ACOS/OCO-2/OCO-3 absorption coefficient (ABSCO) tables"** (Sec. 1.1), i.e. tabulated absorption cross sections on a grid of pressure, temperature, broadener VMR and wavenumber, over 12745-13245 cm-1 (O2) and 4700-6500 cm-1 (the CO2 bands and H2O), at 0.01 or 0.002 cm-1 resolution (Table 1). The unit of analysis is **a band-wide table of absorption cross sections**, paired with a named Level-2 algorithm version (v5.2 <-> L2 v11; v5.1 <-> L2 v10). The scalings are documented under the heading **"Scaling of absorption cross-sections."**

**The claim does not sit cleanly inside that regime.** The claim's unit is the *line parameter* ("empirical intensity scale factors", "reads no HITRAN-frame parameter"); the document's unit for three of the five numbers is the *cross-section table*. See DEFECT 1, which is a numerator-class mismatch, not a quibble.

**Numerator class and denominator, for the numbers the claim leans on.**
- *Numerator class as the document defines it* for 1.0048, 0.994, 0.9875: a dimensionless multiplicative factor on the **published absorption cross-section table** for one band, applied within the Level-2 algorithm. What that class **INCLUDES that the claim excludes**: every band-wide multiplicative error the retrieval can see - continuum, collision-induced absorption, instrument line shape, radiometric gain, the a priori airmass dependence - all of which are degenerate with an intensity error against a single scalar. The 1.0048 case is explicit about this: it is fit to *surface pressure* bias, so it absorbs anything that biases retrieved surface pressure. What that class **MISSES that the claim needs**: it does not touch any individual line's intensity parameter, and it cannot represent a transition-dependent intensity error, which is the error structure a per-line census is about.
- *Numerator class* for 1.014: this one IS intensity-framed, and the document says so - "a ratio between line intensities in the Devi et al. (2016) database and reference intensity measurements." Its denominator is **the Devi et al. (2016) database's own intensities for that band**, not HITRAN's, and not the atmosphere. What it MISSES: everything non-intensity in Devi et al. (2016), which passes through untouched. What it INCLUDES that the claim excludes: a laboratory determination (Polyansky et al. 2015, NIST) as the *target*, i.e. the reset moves the table toward a laboratory value, not away from laboratory values.
- *Denominator check against near neighbours*: 0.981 is a factor on the **H2O** cross sections and was fit to a ~2% low bias in **total column water vapor**, not in XCO2 or XO2. It is in the claim's list but it is not an O2 or CO2 number at all, and it is the only one of the five whose target quantity is a different retrieved species.

## DEFECTS FOUND

1. **"Empirical intensity scale factors" mis-names three of the five numbers.** Section 2.1 is titled "Scaling of absorption cross-sections," and Table 1's row is "ABSCO scaling." 1.0048, 0.994 and 0.9875 are applied to the *published table* inside the Level-2 algorithm, not to line intensities; 1.0048's reference quantity is retrieved surface pressure in hPa, not an intensity. Only 1.014 is defined as an intensity ratio in the document's own words. A band-wide cross-section multiplier is one word away from an intensity reset and is a different quantity - it is degenerate with continuum, CIA, ILS and radiometric gain.

2. **The page range is wrong.** Page 5 is "1 Introduction" / "1.1 Scope and Background". The figures are on **pp. 6-8** (Sec. 2.1 text pp. 6-7, Table 1 p. 8), with 0.981 repeated on p. 9. Correct "pp. 5-8" to "pp. 6-8".

3. **"Empirically resets every band's intensity scale from ATMOSPHERIC references" is false for the weak CO2 band, which is the band carrying the largest factor.** 1.014 "represents a ratio between line intensities in the Devi et al. (2016) database and reference intensity measurements from our NIST ABSCO team members, described in Polyansky et al. (2015)." That is a laboratory-to-database ratio; the TCCON comparison is a subsequent test of it. Additionally the strong band's v5.2 table is anchored to "the ab initio intensities reported in Zak (2016)" via Fleurbaey (2020) before any scaling. The document never draws the atmospheric/laboratory contrast the entry attributes to it.

4. **"Reads no parameter as shipped" is false even where no HITRAN edition is named.** "They were determined from the Devi et al. (2016) database, then subsequently scaled by a constant factor of 1.014" - one band-wide scalar leaves positions, widths, temperature dependences, speed dependence and line mixing as the Devi database ships them. Same structure for H2O: ATM18 read as shipped, scaled by 0.981. What ABSCO does is substitute one compilation for another and retune a single scalar per band, not escape compilation-frame parameters. The narrow claim ("no **HITRAN**-frame parameter for O2 or CO2") survives on this document; the broader framing in the entry's WHY-IT-MATTERS text ("reads no **compilation-frame** parameter for O2 or CO2") does not - it is contradicted by the Devi-database and ATM18 sentences on the same pages.

5. **The document is SILENT, not negative, on HITRAN overlap - and the overlap is the whole of Leg B.** It nowhere compares its values to HITRAN's, nowhere says HITRAN values were rejected, and nowhere states that Devi et al. (2016), Payne (2021) or Drouin's unpublished fit differ from the corresponding HITRAN entries. "Not labelled HITRAN" is a citation-frame fact; Leg B needs a determination-level fact. **NOT CHECKED HERE** (out of this task's named source, and I kept to budget): whether Devi et al. (2016) and the Payne/Drouin O2 work are themselves among HITRAN2020's adopted sources for these same bands, and whether the 1.014 target (Polyansky et al. 2015, NIST) is the same lineage HITRAN's 1.6 um CO2 intensities trace to. Named routes: HITRAN2020 (Gordon et al., JQSRT 277 (2022) 107949) CO2 section, and P1, the PriSpecTemp literature study already in this entry, whose per-band tables the entry quotes as saying "The line intensities in the ABSCO 5.1 database [77] for the 30013 band are data from Ref. [78] rescaled by 1.4%" - a sentence that, on its face, describes ABSCO's 1.014 as a rescaling of a *shared* laboratory determination rather than an independent one. If that resolves the way the ABSCO text hints, Leg B inverts: the flagship would be anchored to the same primary determinations as the compilation, with a different label on top.

6. **The behaviour is one version old and the document says so.** "For version 10, the scaling factors applied within the Level 2 algorithm to ABSCO v5.1 tables were 1.000 and 1.004 for the weak and strong bands, respectively. However, for that version, no effort was made to account for the overall persistent bias of retrieved XCO2 through spectroscopic adjustments." In the immediately preceding operational pairing the weak band's algorithm-level factor was unity and the team disclaims spectroscopic bias tuning. "The flagship team already resets every band's intensity scale" (entry line 15) overstates a practice the document dates to v5.2/L2 v11 and describes as a decision taken for that version.

7. **0.981 is in the claim's list but is not an O2 or CO2 number.** It scales the H2O cross sections and was fit to a ~2% low bias in total column water vapor. Listing it among factors that show "the flagship reads no HITRAN-frame parameter for O2 or CO2" pads the count. It also cuts the other way: H2O's parameters were "HITRAN 2012" as recently as v5.1 (Table 2), which is document-internal proof that ABSCO reads a HITRAN edition as shipped when it chooses to.

8. **A HITRAN line list IS shipped into both CO2 windows, for CH4.** "CH4 tables using the HITRAN line list are now also available; they are computed over both CO2 spectral windows." That does not touch the claim as narrowly worded (O2 or CO2), but it undercuts the inference the entry draws from it - that "the shared compilation is not the dependency the retrievals actually have." Hedge, in the source's own words: "now also available" states availability; I did not establish that OCO-2 L2 v11 operationally uses them. PUBLISHED IS NOT PERFORMED.

9. **Citation front matter.** The entry asks for "the ABSCO v5.2 ... release documentation or technical description." The document is **"ABSCO User Guide," Payne and Oyafuso, Jet Propulsion Laboratory, California Institute of Technology, revision date February 25, 2022**, and it carries **no printed version number** - "v5.2" is in the filename and in the body's scope sentence only. It is not a release note and has no DOI. Cite it by that title, those two names from the front matter, and that revision date.

## BEARING ON THE LEDGER ENTRY

**Verdict on the claim: MOSTLY holds on its numbers, FAILS on two of its words and on its inference.**

- The five figures are all printed, in the bands the entry assigns, in the order the entry gives for the weak band (1.014 baked in, then 0.994 on top). **Narrow the page citation** from "pp. 5-8" to "pp. 6-8".
- **Strike "intensity" from "empirical intensity scale factors."** The document's own heading is "Scaling of absorption cross-sections," and three of the five are band-wide multipliers on the published table applied inside the Level-2 algorithm - 1.0048 fit to a surface-pressure bias in hPa. Only 1.014 is an intensity ratio in the document's words. Leg B should say "band-wide cross-section scale factors," which is weaker for its purpose: a single scalar per band is degenerate with continuum, CIA, ILS and gain, and cannot stand in for a per-transition intensity reset.
- **Strike "from atmospheric references" as a universal.** Narrow it to 1.0048, 0.994, 0.9875 and 0.981. The weak band's 1.014 is a laboratory-to-database ratio against NIST reference intensities (Polyansky et al. 2015), and the strong band's v5.2 table is anchored to Zak (2016) ab initio intensities via Fleurbaey (2020). The document draws no atmospheric-versus-laboratory contrast; the entry supplies it.
- **Hold, narrowly, the "no HITRAN-frame parameter for O2 or CO2" half.** On this document that is correct for v5.2: HITRAN appears only as the CH4 line list, as netCDF gas/isotope indices, as superseded v5.1 H2O, and in one reference entry. It appears nowhere in the O2 or CO2 parameter rows.
- **Strike the broader version of Leg B as the entry's WHY-IT-MATTERS text words it** - "reads no compilation-frame parameter for O2 or CO2." It reads the Devi et al. (2016) database for the weak band and ATM18 for H2O as shipped, modifying one scalar each. It swaps compilations; it does not leave compilation-frame parameters behind. The entry's own line 15 already concedes the line-shape and mixing parameters "are NOT empirically rescaled" - that concession is the whole weak-band parameter set except intensity, and it should be stated in Leg B itself rather than two sections away.
- **Convert the decisive residue to a promotion condition.** Leg B's force rests on something this document does not say: that ABSCO's named sources are *different determinations* from HITRAN's for the same bands, rather than the same primary work under a different label. The document is silent, and silence is not denial. Promotion condition (d) should add: resolve whether Devi et al. (2016) and the Payne (2021)/Drouin et al. (2017) O2 work are among HITRAN2020's adopted sources for the 1.61 um CO2 and 0.76 um O2 bands, and whether the 1.014 target (NIST, Polyansky et al. 2015) is the same lineage as HITRAN's intensities for that band. The entry already holds the instrument for this in P1, which it quotes as describing ABSCO 5.1's 30013-band intensities as "data from Ref. [78] rescaled by 1.4%". If ABSCO's reset moves the table *toward* the same determination the compilation uses, Leg B inverts and the common-mode motivation is restored rather than defeated.
- **One factual correction to the entry's history.** Entry line 15 and line 19 assert that the flagship team "already resets every band's intensity scale from atmospheric references" as settled practice. The document dates it to v5.2/L2 v11 and prints the contrary for the version before: "for that version, no effort was made to account for the overall persistent bias of retrieved XCO2 through spectroscopic adjustments."
- Net effect on the entry's disposition: Leg B survives as a real and sourced objection, but at reduced strength - it is an objection about *one* parameter (intensity), scaled *per band* by a single scalar, with *one* of the four anchors being a laboratory value, and with the determination-level overlap with HITRAN unresolved. It is no longer obviously "substantively the strongest objection"; Leg A, which the entry calls decisive and self-standing and which rests only on the record's internals, is untouched by anything here and should carry the verdict.
