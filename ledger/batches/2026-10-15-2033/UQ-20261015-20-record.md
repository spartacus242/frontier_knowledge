Domain(s): Biology / molecular methods — PCR primer and multiplex-panel design; neighbours: clinical and environmental diagnostics (pathogen surveillance panels, tiled amplicon sequencing, oncology MRD assays), bioinformatics tool building, DNA data storage (random-access PCR), CRISPR guide-activity prediction (structural analogue)

Question (canonical, V0 as submitted): Is the amplification efficiency of a primer pair a transferable property of its sequence and template context — predictable across polymerase/buffer systems and across multiplex contexts — or is it specific to the panel and chemistry in which it was measured, as judged by massively parallel efficiency measurement of the same thousands of primer pairs in several chemistries?

Question (canonical, V1 — one Gate 1 reformulation, adopted, for well-posedness only: fixes the estimand and pre-commits numeric bands): For a designed library of N >= 3,000 distinct primer pairs on identical synthetic templates, with per-pair efficiency defined as log2 relative yield at a fixed cycle number against a common spike-in set and estimated separately within each (chemistry x multiplex-density) cell for >= 3 polymerase/buffer systems and >= 2 multiplex densities: (a) what are the variance components for pair, chemistry, pair-by-chemistry, density and pair-by-density; and (b) what is the out-of-sample r^2 and Spearman rho of a sequence-feature model trained in one chemistry/density cell and tested in every other? Pre-committed bands: TRANSFERABLE = pair component >= 50% of non-residual variance AND cross-cell r^2 >= 0.5 in all pairwise transfers; NOT TRANSFERABLE = pair-by-chemistry component >= pair component OR cross-cell r^2 < 0.2 in any transfer; RANK-ONLY TRANSFER = rho >= 0.5 with r^2 < 0.2 (atlas usable for ranking, not for magnitudes). Both versions recorded; V1 is the version scored.

Alternative formulations: (1) Would a public atlas of measured primer-pair efficiencies be useful outside the exact assay in which the measurements were made, or does efficiency simply not transfer? (2) How much of the variance in measured amplification efficiency among thermodynamically matched primer pairs is reproducible, sequence-driven signal that generalizes across polymerase, buffer and multiplex density, and how much is context-specific interaction?

Motivating gap (as submitted, with Gate 2 corrections): Primer design is dense with how-answers and thin on why-questions: dozens of design tools and rule sets (Tm matching, GC clamp, 3'-end stability, dimer/hairpin avoidance) and a large literature on rescuing specific dropouts, including repeated empirical ARTIC SARS-CoV-2 primer revisions. The submitted gap claimed no measured, generalizable account of why thermodynamically equivalent pairs differ by orders of magnitude, and no test of whether efficiency is transferable. Gate 2 falsifies the second clause in two directions: (i) the weak transferability question was asked and answered qualitatively in 2018 with one primer pair across ten polymerases (Witte et al., P3: transfer can fail catastrophically, up to 10^6-fold sensitivity loss, with an explicit target-by-polymerase interaction the authors could not attribute); (ii) the 10^4-scale, sequencing-readout version was executed in 2024-25 for template-driven bias under a shared primer pair, including a two-workflow cross-condition model-transfer test that largely failed (P1), and in 2025 for 360 distinct primer sets / 141 targets within what appears to be one chemistry (P2, PrimerCast). What remains genuinely unlocated is the pair-level variance decomposition with chemistry and multiplex density as crossed factors.

Presuppositions:
P1 Pooled measurement with a sequencing readout yields an interpretable per-pair efficiency, i.e. pool-level competition separable from intrinsic efficiency — CONTESTED; load-bearing. Partial external support: P1-source fits per-sequence efficiencies to an exponential PCR model from serial-amplification NGS and validates 4 sequences by singleplex qPCR (poor amplifiers confirmed, one-way ANOVA F(4,10)=252, p=5e-10), but that design has one shared primer pair, so inter-primer competition — the dominant term in a real panel — is untested. Must be validated against singleplex qPCR on a calibration subset.
P2 "Efficiency" is a definable scalar per pair within a given chemistry — established for singleplex qPCR; CONTESTED in multiplex, where yield depends on competitors. V1 defuses this by indexing the estimand to the (chemistry x density) cell, at the cost of making "the" pair efficiency a vector rather than a scalar.
P3 Thermodynamic matching is good enough that "equivalent-on-paper" pairs form a real contrast set — established by existing design tools, but model-dependent: the strata are defined by whichever nearest-neighbour model is used, so the contrast set is a property of the chosen tool.
P4 The identical pool can be run across >= 3 polymerase/buffer systems and >= 2 multiplex densities on identical templates — feasible; commercial pooled synthesis plus NGS readout is the enabling instrument and is in routine use (P1, P2).
P5 Template secondary structure and flanking context can be varied independently of primer sequence — partly; requires synthetic templates.
P6 (added at Gate 2) Chemistry factors are separable. P1-source confounds polymerase with annealing (54 vs 60 C) and denaturation (95 vs 98 C) temperature, so its "workflow" effect is not a polymerase effect. The candidate must vary polymerase/buffer with thermal profile held fixed, or it inherits the same confound.
Not presupposed: that a transferable predictor exists. Non-transferability is a substantive answer that redirects effort from shared atlases and sequence-only predictors to panel-local calibration.

What would count as an answer: A designed pool of >= 3,000 primer pairs spanning matched thermodynamic strata on identical synthetic templates, measured for per-pair log2 relative yield against a common spike-in set in >= 3 polymerase/buffer systems (thermal profile held fixed) and >= 2 multiplex densities, with singleplex qPCR validation on a calibration subset of >= 24 pairs spanning the measured efficiency range. The answer is (a) a crossed-random-effects variance decomposition into pair, chemistry, pair-by-chemistry, density and pair-by-density components with CIs, and (b) cross-cell out-of-sample r^2 and Spearman rho for a sequence-feature model trained in one cell and tested in each other, against a permutation null. Verdict by the V1 bands above. A dominant pair component with small interaction licenses shared efficiency atlases and sequence-only predictors; a dominant pair-by-chemistry interaction answers "not transferable" and licenses only panel-local calibration; RANK-ONLY TRANSFER is the pre-registered middle outcome with its own distinct action (atlases for triage, not for magnitudes).

P1. "Deep learning uncovers sequence-specific amplification bias in multi-template PCR" (preprint PDF opened: bioRxiv 10.1101/2024.09.20.614030; ETH Zurich and University of Stuttgart; method named CluMo; title/authors/year not printed in the fetched excerpt, so the title above is the one returned by search 4, and the published version appears in search results as Nature Communications 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-64221-4 — that page could not be opened and the author list is NOT verified). Design verified from the PDF: two Twist pools of 12,000 random 108-nt sequences (GCall unconstrained, GCfix 50% GC) with shared 0F/0R adapter primers; six iterations of 15 cycles (15-90 cumulative) with iSeq 100 NGS at each iteration; per-sequence efficiencies fit to an exponential PCR model; ~2% (~240 per pool) classed as poorly amplifying; a 1,000-sequence reproducibility pool; a 12,000-sequence external-validation pool (10,000 random + 2,000 motif-inserted); singleplex qPCR on 4 individually synthesised sequences in triplicate dilution series (poor sequences significantly lower, F(4,10)=252, p=5e-10; 5'-degenerate primers rescued #09807 from 68+/-2% to 81+/-5%); two workflows (KAPA SYBR FAST / 54 C anneal / 95 C denature vs Q5 Hot Start / 60 C / 98 C); five external DNA-storage datasets (Erlich, Koch, Song, Choi, Gao). Cross-condition transfer reported as AUROC/AUPRC: GCall<->GCfix transfer well; Koch-related 0.71/0.17; Erlich/Gao 0.87/0.17; an Erlich-trained model on the external workflow 0.43/0.02. Stated conclusion: bias depends on individual oligo sequence rather than pool composition, motif-driven adapter self-priming replicates across laboratories when conditions match, but there is "strong workflow dependence" and polymerase and adapter choice modulate or override the effect. No formal variance decomposition.

Significance — consequence: TRANSFERABLE (pair component dominant, cross-cell r^2 >= 0.5) licenses three things that are currently done on faith: pooling measured efficiency labels across chemistries to train sequence-only predictors (P2's dataset would gain external validity it does not claim), publishing a shared efficiency atlas as a design resource, and carrying a prior from a pair validated in chemistry A into chemistry B so revalidation can be reduced rather than repeated. NOT TRANSFERABLE (interaction >= pair component, or any transfer below r^2 0.2) retires the shared-atlas programme, converts the design problem into a calibration problem — panels ship with an in-chemistry efficiency measurement kit instead of inheriting one — caps the reported accuracy of every sequence-only predictor as a within-chemistry number, and gives standards bodies a quantitative basis for requiring revalidation on master-mix change. RANK-ONLY TRANSFER is the branch current practice has no name for: atlases survive as triage tools and die as quantitative ones. The decision-relevant content is the fraction, not the sign: P1 and P3 already make "some interaction exists" the expected answer, so a yes/no framing would add little and the magnitude split is where the information is.
Significance — audience: primer-design tool builders (Primer3 / qPrimerDB / PrimerBank / PrimerCast lineage), who must decide whether to train on pooled or chemistry-stratified labels and what accuracy to claim; multiplex-panel developers in pathogen and wastewater surveillance, tiled-amplicon sequencing (ARTIC-class schemes) and bespoke oncology-MRD panels, who currently rebalance empirically per panel per chemistry; reagent vendors, who hold the only existing cross-chemistry per-pair data as internal QC; and standards bodies writing panel-validation requirements (MIQE/dMIQE successors, CLSI MM-series, ISO 20395), who specify that efficiency be reported but have no rule on when a measured efficiency stops being valid. Reaction risk, stated plainly: panel developers may regard the answer as already known from P3 and from their own bench experience, and act no differently whichever branch wins.
Significance — fecundity: whether the transferable component is merely what thermodynamic predictors already capture (Tm, GC, 3'-end stability), which would mean the portable part is the uninteresting part and the interesting part is the non-portable one; whether pair-by-chemistry interaction is low-dimensional (a few chemistry archetypes), which would license few-shot calibration from a small anchor set rather than full remeasurement; whether multiplex-density dependence is pairwise (dimer-driven, predictable from pair-pair features) or collective (primer/dNTP/polymerase depletion); whether the same decomposition holds for hybridisation-capture probes, isothermal (LAMP) primer sets and CRISPR guides, which would make "is measured oligo activity portable" one estimand across oligo technologies rather than five separate folk literatures.
Significance — anchoring: ARTIC V4/V4.1 dropout and the repeated-revision pattern (search 9); P3's unattributed target-by-polymerase interaction ("remains unclear" which factor); P1's out-of-workflow transfer failure (AUROC 0.43, AUPRC 0.02) sitting beside its claim that bias is sequence-driven rather than pool-driven — an unresolved tension in one paper, which is the candidate's sharpest anchor; the parallel, documented failure of sgRNA-activity models to transfer across datasets and platforms, handled there as transfer learning rather than as a variance question (search 10); and the standards gap in which efficiency must be reported but its domain of validity is unspecified.

Reason for silence: a cost-and-incentive mechanism layered on a very recent instrument, with the instrument clause only partly intact. Pooled oligo synthesis with an NGS readout made 10^4-scale efficiency measurement possible only in the last few years, and Gate 2 shows the instrument arrived and was promptly spent — twice — on within-regime predictors (P1 in 2024-25, P2 in 2025), not on a transferability matrix. The reason it was spent that way is structural, not oversight: a crossed design multiplies synthesis and sequencing cost by (chemistries x densities) while, under the prevailing incentive, returning no new predictor and possibly destroying the one you have, since a large pair-by-chemistry interaction invalidates the headline r^2 of the paper you are writing. Ownership compounds it: the only parties who routinely run the same panel across master mixes are reagent and panel vendors, for whom a published matrix showing that a competitor's enzyme reorders their panel is a commercial liability rather than a paper, so the data stay in internal QC; and the question straddles a boundary between method developers (who fix one chemistry by design and publish the panel) and tool builders (who need labels and take whatever single-chemistry set exists). Finally, the qualitative answer already in print (P3's title-level conclusion that enzyme replacement can destroy an assay) functions as received wisdom that makes the quantitative version look settled: everyone knows chemistry matters, so nobody measures how much. Weakness to concede to the adversary: this is not a case of an unaskable question. The question was askable in 2018 and has been answerable since roughly 2023; "received wisdom plus cost plus vendor ownership" is a credible mechanism but a weaker one than a genuine instrument barrier, and it coexists with two 2024-25 papers that came within one design choice of answering it.

Scores: Well-posedness 2/3 — V1 names the estimand, the estimator, the cells, the validation subset and three disjoint pre-committed bands, so the evidence that would settle it is statable; held off 3/3 because the load-bearing presupposition P1 (separating intrinsic pair efficiency from pool-level competition) is contested and, if it fails, the "pair" variance component is not interpretable as intrinsic efficiency rather than merely being estimated to be small — and because P3's thermodynamic strata are defined by whichever nearest-neighbour model is chosen, so the "equivalent-on-paper" contrast set is tool-relative.
Significance 2/3 — the three branches map to genuinely different actions for four named and already-engaged communities (predictor builders most cheaply: P2's dataset plus one extra chemistry), and the fecundity items are real. Held off 3/3 because the expected answer is pre-figured by P1 and P3, because the only community with a standing decision to make is the tool builders, and because panel developers already behave as if efficiency were panel-local, so the NOT-TRANSFERABLE branch — the likelier one — changes their practice least.
