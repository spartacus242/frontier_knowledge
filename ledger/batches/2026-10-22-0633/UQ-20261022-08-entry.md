Domain(s): Medicine — clinical informatics / health-systems data infrastructure (target field). Adjacent: digital preservation (method vocabulary), RWE regulatory science, research-network data curation (OHDSI/Sentinel/PCORnet). Source method: cross-field transplant.
Question (canonical): For a health system that replaced its EHR, with a queryable legacy archive and a declared look-back window W before cutover date T: (i) per pre-declared fact class (problem-list entries, medication orders, allergies, quantitative labs with units, immunisations, procedures, notes), what fraction of the facts present in the SOURCE system's structured tables within W is present, queryable and value-identical in the TARGET system's computable record, under a correspondence rule fixed before any data pull, with "no admissible correspondent in the target model" counted as its own category rather than dropped; and (ii) on at least three named longitudinal estimands (incident-diabetes rate, prevalent ACE-inhibitor exposure, Charlson comorbidity prevalence) computed from the target system's standard research extract, is there a level or slope discontinuity at T exceeding a materiality bound declared in advance, where "artefactual" is established by re-deriving the same estimand for the same patients from the legacy archive and by a pre-declared no-cutover control window of equal length (plus a contemporaneous non-converting control site where available)?
Reformulation note (Gate 1): one reformulation used; V0 retained as the lay/intake phrasing. V0 left the denominator uncountable ("the pre-cutover clinical record"), asserted causation in the word "artefactual", and set no materiality bound. V2 fixes the denominator to source-system structured tables within W, adds the legacy re-derivation and no-cutover control window as attribution arms, and requires the bound pre-declared so a null is reportable.
Alternative formulations:
- (V0, intake) When a health system replaces its EHR, what fraction of the pre-cutover clinical record is never brought into the new system's computable record at all, per pre-declared fact class, and does that remainder produce artefactual discontinuities at the cutover date in the incidence, exposure and comorbidity measures that EHR-derived research and quality reporting compute?
- (lay) After a hospital switches EHR vendors, how much of its patients' past survives as a usable number rather than a scanned image or a decommissioned archive, and what does the missing part do to the statistics drawn from that record?
- (method field's words) Applying significant properties, migration fidelity and fixity to EHR conversions: what are the per-fact-class retention rates in the target system, and what discontinuity do they induce in named longitudinal estimands at the cutover boundary?

What would count as an answer: per fact class, a measured retention fraction (present, queryable, value-identical) at two or more systems with DIFFERENT source/target vendor pairs, correspondence rule fixed before any data pull and correspondence failures reported as their own category; plus a pre-registered discontinuity test on three named estimands at T against a bound declared in advance, with legacy re-derivation and no-cutover control window as attribution arms; plus a stated count of candidate sites that could not supply a legacy denominator at all. Near-complete retention with no discontinuity beyond the bound is a clean, informative negative: it would license the current unevidenced practice of pooling across cutovers.
