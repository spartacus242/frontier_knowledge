## 0. Restatement (my words, before touching the record's framing)

Take the published phylogenetics literature as a corpus of *pairs* of papers that overlap in taxa. For each pair, reconcile taxon names, cut down to the shared taxa, and ask: of the clades one paper printed with a ≥95% support label (nonparametric bootstrap, UFBoot, or posterior probability), how many are topologically incompatible — under one declared bipartition-conflict rule — with a clade the *other* paper also printed at ≥95%? Report that rate separately for each support type, split by how much data the two papers share and by era, read it as a floor on how often at least one of the two ≥95% labels is wrong, and hold it against a nominal error rate declared beforehand for each support type.

Compressed: **has anyone ever scored the published "95% support" label the way a meteorologist scores a 95% forecast — using another published paper, rather than a known or reference truth, as the scorer?** The novelty claim is about the *comparator* (another paper, no truth needed) and the *unit* (a pair of published labels), not about the phenomenon, which the field asserts qualitatively all the time.

I am **not** nominating anything else; the remaining unopened returns are either single-clade case studies or methods papers, and a nomination I cannot justify by kind would be a guess.

**Single strongest killing point (Partial, honestly graded):** the question's closing limb — "how does that rate compare with a per-method nominal benchmark declared in advance" — has **no referent for the nonparametric bootstrap**, the support type that dominates the published record. The founding paper's own target is data-scoped, not the true tree (Felsenstein 1985, p. 786: the interval contains "not the true phylogeny, but the phylogeny that would be estimated on repeated sampling of many characters from the underlying pool of characters"), and Kumar et al. 2012 state flatly that "the bootstrap support value has no simple frequentist interpretation as a probability". Fold in C7 — incompatibility need not imply error where the two studies' estimands differ (Larson et al.: discordance "due to error, ILS, and/or genetic exchange among lineages") — and the headline number risks being an uncalibratable rate compared against a benchmark the field never set. It does not reach Unresolved, because the same page of Felsenstein 1985 *does* derive a confidence figure about the true tree (95% and 95% giving "as little as 90% confidence in the statement that they are both present in the true tree"), posterior probability keeps a clean benchmark, and the share of estimand-mismatched pairs is a quantity nobody has measured.
