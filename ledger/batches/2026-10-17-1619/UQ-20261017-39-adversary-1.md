## Restatement (my words)

Take a ward where the same patients carry a wearable continuous monitor AND receive the ordinary nurse-scheduled observation rounds. Compute NEWS2 from each stream (the continuous stream collapsed to one score per time window by a pre-declared rule, limited to the parameters both streams share). For each stream, measure how well the unchanged published thresholds behave (calibration intercept and slope, discrimination, alerts per patient-day, PPV, lead time), work out the recalibration that makes the continuous stream reproduce the nurse stream's operating point, and — using the nurse data alone — test whether HOW OFTEN nurses chose to measure predicts deterioration beyond WHAT they measured. Headline intent (V0, kept in V1's last clause): how much of an early-warning score's apparent prognostic value is the nurse's decision to measure rather than the physiology recorded.

## What the newly opened paper says (Wilson et al. 2025, as read on the publisher page)

- 48 Covid-19 inpatients, 204 patient-days, 41 contributing ward NEWS2 data; four Isansys wearables; nurses recorded vitals 1–12-hourly on the ward. Both streams on the same patients.
- Partial NEWS2 (HR, RR, temperature, SpO2; consciousness and air/oxygen omitted; BP in a small subset) computed from the wearable as the median of readings in the 5 minutes preceding each nurse measurement — i.e. a paired-at-nurse-time design, not a per-window design.
- Agreement within prespecified limits: HR ±5 bpm 59.7%; RR ±3/min 38.5%; temperature ±0.3 °C 24.4%; SpO2 ±2% 32.9%.
- 613 ward NEWS2 pairs: median nurse NEWS2 1 (IQR 1–2); median wearable NEWS2 4 (IQR 3–6).
- Wearable alerts at unchanged thresholds: NEWS2 >=5, 262 alerts, 86% (225/262) false positive against the nurse score; NEWS2 >=7, 92 alerts, 89% (82/92) false positive. (My own arithmetic from those counts: PPV against the nurse-alert reference ≈ 14% and ≈ 11%; the paper gives no CI.)
- Not reported: sensitivity/specificity against outcomes, calibration, AUROC, lead time.
- Authors' conclusion, as quoted by the page: "data from wearable sensors should not be used in existing track and trigger systems"; they recommend scheduled trend reviews or re-developed thresholds.

Strongest single killing point: Wilson et al. 2025 (BMC Digital Health 3:13), read on the publisher page, already ran the candidate's alternative formulation #2 on a paired cohort with unchanged NEWS2 thresholds — median wearable NEWS2 4 vs nurse 1, 86% of wearable >=5 alerts false — and attributes it to measurand disagreement (RR within ±3/min in 38.5% of pairs), concluding wearable data "should not be used in existing track and trigger systems"; this both duplicates the operating-point limb a second time and shows the headline "timing rather than physiology" attribution is swamped by a term the paired design cannot remove.

Routing: the within-nurse-stream limb ("does clinician-chosen observation intensity carry prognostic information beyond the recorded values, and what share of a ward EWS's AUROC is credit for the decision to measure?") is a distinct, identified question whose nearest priors are Jarvis 2015, Agniel 2018 and Briggs 2024; it should be re-entered at Gate 0 as its own candidate with no wearable in it.
