Question (canonical): Now that the complete set of solutions to x^3 + y^3 + z^3 = k up to height around 10^15 to 10^16 is known for every admissible |k| <= 1000, is the per-k representation density — the count's slope against log of the height — determined by the local singular series alone, or does the residual after dividing out the local prediction correlate with a global invariant of the pure cubic field Q(k^(1/3)), such as its class number, its regulator, or the 3-part of its class group?
Reformulation note (Gate 1, the one permitted; the wording actually judged): fix B = 10^15 and a stated counting convention, define r_k = n_k(B) / (c_k^loc log B) with c_k^loc built from local densities alone, and ask whether r_k has nonzero rank correlation, against a permutation null conditioning out the mod-9 class and cube-factor structure, with any of exactly three pre-declared invariants of K = Q(k^(1/3)): h(K), R(K), 3-rank of Cl(K). Plus a labelled prerequisite limb: is the prediction in use (Booker-Sutherland's rho_sol) purely local, or does its construction already involve global data of K? The original's fourth invariant ("unit index of the associated norm form") was struck as unenumerated. Terminal estimand = the rank correlation.

Alternative formulations:
1. Do the newly complete three-cube solution tables show a per-k representation density that local densities fail to predict, and does the discrepancy track the unit and class-group arithmetic of Q(k^(1/3))?
2. Is the solution-poverty of small k such as k = 3 a fluctuation of the predicted logarithmic law, or the shadow of a global invariant the singular series cannot see?

Motivating gap: Exhaustive searches now give per-k solution lists to heights near 10^15-10^19, turning the per-k representation density from a heuristic parameter into a measurable one; individual k deviate startlingly (k = 3 has three known solutions; k = 33 and k = 42 fell only at very large heights), and solutions at fixed x + y + z are governed by norm forms in Q(k^(1/3)) where units and the class group act.
