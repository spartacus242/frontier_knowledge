Source method: presupposition inversion

Question (canonical, Gate 1 reformulation — the version to carry forward):
In a paired report–image corpus with intact patient/study series order (e.g. MIMIC-CXR), restricted to *carry-forward-eligible assertions* — a positive assertion of a finding class F in a follow-up report R_t where F was also positively asserted in that patient's immediately preceding report R_{t-1}, and where F is adjudicable from a single image (pure change-descriptors such as "stable", "improving", "resolved" are excluded from the numerator and counted separately): (a) what fraction of such assertions does a blinded multi-reader panel (>=3 readers, current image alone, no report, no prior image) call absent, after subtracting a reader-noise floor estimated on positively-asserted findings in index exams read under identical blinding; (b) is the report-label false-positive rate on follow-up exams higher than on index exams for the same finding classes and the same case-mix, and is the excess concentrated in finding classes with high resolution rates (consolidation, effusion, oedema, pneumothorax, line/tube position) rather than in persistent structural classes (cardiomegaly, chronic fibrosis); and (c) how much does measured performance of a standard classifier trained and evaluated on report-derived labels change when follow-up exams are excluded, or re-labelled by the panel, versus a matched exclusion of a random equal-sized subset?

Question (Gate 0 intake version, recorded as required):
In paired report–image corpora of serial imaging studies, what fraction of the findings asserted in a follow-up report is carried forward from the prior report without support in the current image (adjudicated by blinded re-read of the current image alone), and does that inherited content account for a directionally biased share of the label error in report-derived imaging-AI datasets?

Gate 1 note — why the reformulation was needed (well-posedness only, no scope change):
The intake version left three things unfixed, each of which could be answered differently without the result changing: the unit and denominator of "fraction of the findings asserted" (per report? per finding class? including change-descriptors that cannot be adjudicated on one image?); the reference standard's own error rate, which must be measured in the same design or the numerator is uninterpretable; and the contrast that carries the directional claim (index vs follow-up at equal case-mix, against a random-exclusion control rather than against nothing). The reformulation fixes the estimand to carry-forward-eligible assertions, specifies the blinded >=3-reader panel plus an index-exam noise floor, and makes the model-performance limb a differenced comparison. The scientific content is unchanged.

Alternative formulations:
1. Does a radiology report describe its own image, or partly the previous one — and how much of the "ground truth" in report-labelled datasets is inherited rather than observed?
2. When report-derived labels disagree with blinded image re-reads, how much of the disagreement is explained by the exam being a follow-up rather than an index study, and does it skew toward findings that had already resolved?

Motivating gap:
Every large report-labelled imaging corpus (MIMIC-CXR, CheXpert, PadChest and successors) and a substantial share of device-validation reference standards derive labels by NLP over free-text reports. The load-bearing presupposition, nowhere stated, is that a report describes the study it is attached to. Radiologists read serial exams with the prior report in hand; the clinical-documentation literature establishes that content propagates forward between notes; and the imaging-AI label-noise literature measures total label error but partitions it into NLP extraction failure (negation, uncertainty, label hierarchy) and reader disagreement, so inheritance across exams in a series is not a category in the field's own error taxonomy. The inversion is actionable because its predicted consequence is directional rather than symmetric: a finding that has resolved but is restated persists as a positive label on a negative image, so error should concentrate in follow-up exams and in resolving findings, and a model trained and scored on report labels would be partly rewarded for reproducing inheritance. MIMIC-CXR is heavily weighted toward ICU serial imaging, which maximises the exposure.

Presuppositions (established / contested):
- The current image alone suffices to adjudicate presence of a finding — CONTESTED for findings defined by change ("stable", "improving", "resolved"). FIXED by the reformulation: change-descriptors are excluded from the estimand and reported as a separate stratum.
- Study-series linkage (which exam precedes which, same patient) is available — ESTABLISHED for MIMIC-CXR-style corpora via subject_id/study_id and StudyDate; CONTESTED for scraped or de-linked datasets, and must be checked per corpus.
- Blinded re-read is an adequate reference standard — CONTESTED; it carries its own error rate. FIXED by requiring >=3 readers, reported inter-reader agreement, and an explicit index-exam noise floor that the carry-forward estimate is netted against. If the floor and the raw rate overlap, the answer is negative.
- Report-derived labels are genuinely used as ground truth in artefacts whose measured performance would change — ESTABLISHED for training corpora and public benchmarks; PARTLY CONTESTED for regulatory validation sets, where reference standards are often bespoke re-reads and the exposure must be established per submission rather than assumed.
- Carry-forward is distinguishable from genuine persistence of a finding — CONTESTED and central. FIXED by restricting the numerator to assertions the blinded panel calls ABSENT; a restated finding that is really still present is correctly labelled and is not in the numerator.
- Reports and images are time-aligned and not scrambled by de-identification in ways that destroy series order — GENERALLY ESTABLISHED (date-shifting in MIMIC is per-patient and order-preserving), needs per-corpus verification.
- Mechanism-agnosticism: the question measures inheritance as an outcome (report asserts, image does not support, prior asserted) and does not require that the radiologist literally copied text. Template reuse, anchoring, and genuine clinical-context inference all land in the same numerator. This is a deliberate scoping choice, not an oversight; attributing mechanism is a separate study.

What would count as an answer:
A blinded multi-reader adjudication on a stratified sample of index versus follow-up exams from a paired report–image corpus, reporting (a) per-finding-class carry-forward rates with reader-agreement statistics and inter-reader CIs, netted against the index-exam noise floor; (b) the index-vs-follow-up difference in report-label false-positive rate with its direction and its concentration across finding classes (excess false positives on high-resolution-rate findings being the predicted signature; a symmetric or null pattern being the falsifier); and (c) the change in measured classifier performance when follow-up exams are excluded or re-adjudicated, differenced against a random-exclusion control of equal size. A carry-forward rate statistically indistinguishable from zero above the reader-noise floor, or an error excess with no directional structure across finding classes, answers the question negatively and closes it.

Novelty — three nearest prior formulations and how the candidate differs:
1. VisualCheXbert (Jain et al., CHIL 2021, arXiv:2102.11467) — nearest prior overall, and genuinely close. Its question: how much do report-derived labels disagree with radiologist image labels, and can a text model be trained to predict image labels instead? Difference: (i) it measures TOTAL report-vs-image disagreement and names carry-forward from prior studies as one of several post-hoc explanations in a discussion list, without measuring it, isolating it, or testing it; (ii) it has no series-order variable — no index-vs-follow-up contrast, no requirement that the finding was asserted in the preceding report, so the carry-forward estimand is not constructed; (iii) it has no reader-noise floor netting and no directional prediction across finding classes; (iv) its response to the phenomenon is to absorb it into a better labeller (fit image labels from text) rather than to characterise it as a structured, exam-position-dependent bias in existing corpora and benchmark scores. The candidate's claim to novelty is therefore that the mechanism is NAMED but never POSED as a question; this is the reason novelty is Medium rather than High.
2. Repository Supervision Auditing (Agudelo-Londoño et al., arXiv:2608.10084, 2026) — nearest prior on method. It does exactly the adjudication design the candidate needs (report-derived labels vs expert image annotation, MIMIC-CXR) but for one finding class (cardiomegaly), with no series structure, and its own explanation of disagreement is non-mention — i.e. report false NEGATIVES, the opposite direction from the candidate's prediction. Cardiomegaly is also the worst possible class for the candidate's hypothesis (persistent, structural, low resolution rate), so this paper neither tests nor bears on the directional claim.
3. The prior-reference line in report generation (Ramesh et al., arXiv:2210.06340, 2022; Li et al., arXiv:2602.23676, 2026) — nearest prior on the phenomenon. These establish that reports routinely reference priors and that this corrupts generation training, and they respond by DELETING prior references from report text or steering models away from producing them. Difference: the object of concern is generated text fidelity, not label validity; prior-referencing content is treated as a nuisance to be removed, never as evidence that the attached image may not support the assertion; neither work adjudicates against images, stratifies index vs follow-up, or asks what the inherited content does to classification labels or to measured model performance. This is the clearest instance of the phenomenon being neutralised rather than measured.
4. (Fourth, for completeness) Rafferty & Rajan, arXiv:2509.15107 — not a competing formulation but the field's own stocktaking of CXR label quality, opened and confirmed silent on this category. Its silence is the main positive evidence that the question is absent rather than merely hard to find.
No source in this record is cited unverified; every one above was opened by fetch. Significance — consequence (yes/no):
If YES and the net rate is material: a specific, correctable defect is identified in the reference standard of the field's most used imaging corpora, with a known sign. Report-derived positive labels on follow-up exams are inflated for resolving findings, which means (i) published sensitivity/specificity on these benchmarks is not an estimate of image-reading performance but partly of inheritance-reproduction, (ii) models are actively trained to predict what a radiologist would have written given the prior, which is a different and clinically worse target than what is on the film, and (iii) benchmark leaderboards built on these labels have a systematic, exam-position-dependent bias rather than symmetric noise — so the usual "label noise attenuates but does not bias" defence fails. Remedies are cheap and immediate: curate index-only splits, flag follow-up exams, or re-adjudicate them. If NO (rate indistinguishable from the reader-noise floor, or excess with no directional structure): that is also consequential and closes a live worry — it would license the existing practice of mining serial exams indiscriminately, and would redirect label-quality effort to the non-mention/false-negative channel that arXiv:2608.10084 points at, strengthening rather than weakening the case for report mining.

Significance — audience (who would act, and how):
- Dataset curators (MIMIC-CXR/PhysioNet, CheXpert, PadChest, hospital-scale successors): would add a series-position and prior-assertion flag to released label files, and could ship index-only benchmark splits. This is a metadata change they are already technically able to make (subject/study IDs exist), so the barrier is knowing it matters, not capability.
- Benchmark and challenge organisers: would stratify or exclude follow-up exams in evaluation sets, and would have to re-score existing results if the effect is large.
- Regulators and notified bodies assessing imaging-AI claims, and journal reviewers/editors applying CLAIM/STARD-AI style checklists: would ask whether a submitted reference standard derives from reports, and if so whether follow-up exams were adjudicated. A finding with a known direction gives them a concrete question to add; a null closes it.
- Radiology QA and peer-learning programmes (RADPEER-style): a measured carry-forward rate per finding class is directly usable as a QA metric and as a target for the "read without the prior first" intervention, which is advocated on cognitive-bias grounds (query 2) but has no measured effect size on this outcome.
- Model developers: would reconsider training targets and the use of prior-conditioned inputs.

Significance — fecundity (what becomes askable):
- Is carry-forward rate a reader-level, site-level or template-level property — i.e. does it track individual radiologists, PACS report templates, worklist pressure, or shift time? That becomes measurable only once the rate is defined.
- Does a measured carry-forward rate predict downstream clinical action (repeat imaging, antibiotic continuation, line repositioning), i.e. does label inheritance also cause care inheritance?
- Do prior-conditioned report-generation models, now a live line, inherit and AMPLIFY the rate relative to their training distribution — and is the "prior-comparison hallucination" the generation literature is suppressing partly a faithful reproduction of a real human behaviour?
- Does the same estimand exist in pathology addenda, echo reports, and dermatology serial photography — the design transplants directly, and query 7 found it unmeasured in echo.
- Can carry-forward be detected automatically (prior-report text overlap plus image-model disagreement) well enough to triage serial exams for adjudication at corpus scale, turning a sampled audit into a corpus-wide correction?
- Is the reader-noise floor itself prior-dependent — do blinded readers systematically differ from priored readers in a direction, which would recast "radiologist-level performance" claims generally?

Significance — anchoring (existing open problems and anomalies it touches):
- The unexplained magnitude of report-vs-image label disagreement: VisualCheXbert's kappa gaps and arXiv:2608.10084's near-zero cardiomegaly agreement are anomalies the field has not fully accounted for; the candidate supplies a testable component with a predicted sign, and its prediction is distinguishable from the non-mention channel (opposite direction).
- The gap between report-labelled benchmark performance and prospective clinical performance of imaging AI, which is a standing puzzle in the deployment literature.
- The "hallucinated priors" problem in report generation (arXiv:2210.06340, arXiv:2602.23676): the candidate reframes a known preprocessing nuisance as a possible validity defect in labels, connecting two subfields that currently treat the same textual phenomenon with incompatible purposes.
- The clinical-documentation copy-forward/note-bloat literature (query 4), which has an established propagation finding in notes and no bridge to imaging labels.
- Cognitive-bias work on anchoring and prior-report framing in radiology (query 2), which is qualitative and lacks an outcome measure; this question supplies one.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism — disciplinary boundary plus "the fix absorbed the question"):
Three mechanisms compose, and none is "no one thought of it".
(a) Unit-of-analysis artifact at a disciplinary boundary. The corpora are consumed as flat (image, label) rows. Series order is physically present in the identifiers but absent from the analysis frame of everyone who studies label quality; the people who own the error taxonomy (ML groups) inherited it from NLP extraction — negation, uncertainty, label hierarchy — and the people who own carry-forward as a concept (clinical-documentation researchers, radiology QA) do not work on datasets and have no reason to ask what a corpus's labels inherit. The category is therefore not contested; it is simply not in either field's error list, which is exactly what arXiv:2509.15107's silence shows.
(b) The fix absorbed the question. The one subfield that DID notice that reports talk about priors — report generation — encountered it as a text-fidelity nuisance and solved it by deleting prior references from report text (arXiv:2210.06340) or steering models away from emitting them (arXiv:2602.23676). Once the phenomenon has an accepted remedy, it stops being an object of measurement: a quantity that gets stripped in preprocessing is never estimated. This is the strongest single explanation, and it is evidenced rather than speculated.
(c) Incentive gap and ownership vacuum. The finding would retroactively discount performance numbers on the benchmarks the field is built on, and it falls between actors: curators did not create the labels' clinical content, reporting radiologists are not evaluated on dataset validity, and model developers have no incentive to audit the standard their scores rest on. An audit also costs blinded multi-reader radiologist time with no publication-shaped reward for a null, which is the predicted outcome under the field's current assumptions.
Residual worry attached to this field: VisualCheXbert names the mechanism in 2021 and nobody followed it up, which is consistent with (b) and (c) but is also the kind of trace that a fresh adversary should attack — a follow-up may exist in radiology-QA or health-services vocabularies that these eleven queries did not reach.
