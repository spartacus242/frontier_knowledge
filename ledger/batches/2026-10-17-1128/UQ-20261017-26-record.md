Question (canonical, Gate 0 as intaken): For deployed speculative-decoding implementations documented as lossless, how large is the excess deviation between the served output distribution and the target model's own output distribution once a precision-, kernel- and batch-composition-matched non-speculative baseline is subtracted, and does that excess reach the size of the smallest model-to-model differences the field reports as findings?

Question (canonical, Gate 1 reformulation — the version graded; one reformulation used, for well-posedness only):
For one production serving engine and one open target/draft pair at fixed weights, precision, temperature and engine version, define the treatment as the engine-level speculative-decoding switch and define matching at the REQUEST level (identical co-tenant request mix and arrival schedule), explicitly conceding that the target forward pass's tensor shape is part of the treatment and cannot be matched:
 (i) what is the estimated distributional distance D(spec-on, spec-off) between the two served completion distributions on a fixed prompt set, with a permutation null;
 (ii) what is D(spec-off, spec-off') between two non-speculative runs whose request-level composition differs over the same declared range — the nuisance baseline;
 (iii) what is the excess (i) − (ii) with a confidence interval; and
 (iv) does that excess exceed a pre-declared yardstick — one quantization step on the same instrument, and the smallest benchmark difference the field publishes as a result?
Reason for the reformulation: the Gate 0 phrase "batch-composition-matched" names a match that the mechanism forbids (the speculative target pass scores K draft positions in one forward pass, so no non-speculative run shares its batch shape), which would have made the central contrast unachievable as written rather than merely difficult. The reformulation moves the match to the request level, puts the shape difference inside the treatment where it belongs, and names the yardstick as pre-declared rather than as a literature quantity to be found later. Both versions recorded.

Alternative formulations:
1. Does turning speculative decoding on change what a served model actually samples, over and above the change caused by request/batch composition alone?
2. What is the measured size of the gap between "algorithmically lossless" and "lossless as served" for speculative decoding, expressed as a two-sample distributional distance with a permutation null?

Motivating gap: ANCHOR OPENED AT FIRST HAND (previous run, carried in intake): vLLM's Speculative Decoding documentation (docs.vllm.ai/en/v0.8.2/features/spec_decode.html) ships a correctness claim ("algorithmically validated to be lossless"; "theoretically lossless up to the precision limits of hardware numerics") next to its own statement that floating-point error and batch size may shift output distributions and that token log-probabilities are not stable across runs. The engine therefore documents the failure of its own claim's premise and asserts the residual is small without measuring it. The intake's further claim — that published speculative-decoding measurement is of acceptance rate and speedup, not distributional fidelity — was tested this run and is TRUE OF THE SYSTEMS LITERATURE (confirmed on arXiv:2601.11580, a vLLM-wide speculative-decoding measurement study whose endpoints are speedup, acceptance length and time breakdown, with no fidelity endpoint), but FALSE AS A STATEMENT ABOUT THE QUESTION BEING UNASKED: two sources answer the fidelity question directly and disagree (see Novelty). The intake's two unsourced recollections are now both sourced: batch-composition dependence of vLLM outputs (Thinking Machines Lab, "Defeating Nondeterminism in LLM Inference", 10 Sept 2025 — opened at first hand by this ledger's own adversaries for UQ-20261016-06; corroborated independently this run by arXiv:2605.29979, which shows engine/attention-backend/hardware numerical deviations are observable in generated text and reliably classifiable even at non-zero temperature), and the existence of an adequately powered two-sample test for text-generating distributions (arXiv:2410.20247, MMD with a Hamming string kernel, median 77.4% power at ~10 completions per prompt over 20-25 prompts).

Presuppositions (status after Gate 1):

P2. An adequately powered two-sample test for text-generating distributions exists — NOW ESTABLISHED AND SOURCED (arXiv:2410.20247, read this run: MMD + Hamming string kernel, median 77.4% power against a range of distortions at ~250 completions). Was "recalled, not sourced" at intake.
P3. The speculative and non-speculative paths can be run with everything else held fixed — FALSE AS STATED, and the reformulation's subject: the target pass's shape is intrinsic to the treatment. Survives only as request-level matching with the shape difference inside the treatment. Practitioner report (dev.to, read this run) names exactly this: "Different batch shapes, different kernel paths, different reduction order."
P4. Deployed endpoints evaluated as "the model" do use speculative decoding — CONTESTED for closed providers (disclosure is partial); supported for engine defaults and self-hosted stacks (title-level evidence this run that Ollama 0.34 turns speculative decoding on by default — NOT READ, title only, so it grounds nothing beyond a pointer).
P5. A deviation of the size at issue would matter to someone — Gate 3's burden; partly discharged by the yardstick machinery existing (arXiv:2411.00640; arXiv:2608.22331, both title-level only this run) and by the practitioner report's 1.2% figure.

What would count as an answer: Pre-registered measurement on >=2 open target/draft pairs and >=2 engines reporting (a) D(spec-on, spec-off) on a fixed prompt set with a permutation null; (b) D(spec-off, spec-off') with request composition varied — the baseline; (c) the excess (a)-(b) with a CI; (d) the excess calibrated against a known-size perturbation (one quantization step on the same instrument) and against the smallest published benchmark gap. A null — excess indistinguishable from the baseline at stated power, with the interval tight against the yardstick — answers it as decisively as a positive finding. If the excess is large, the answer names the responsible implementation choice (acceptance-test arithmetic, draft/target precision mismatch, tree attention) by one-at-a-time ablation.

Asked-but-open: YES, in a specific and routable sense. The question is asked (A, D, vendor documentation, practitioner discourse) and the two available answers conflict — a null at p = 0.976 on a non-GPU custom implementation with marginal-frequency bins and no power analysis (A), against 1.2% of outputs differing on vLLM with no statistical test and no baseline (D). Route to the open-questions project: tractable at modest cost (one or two open target/draft pairs, one or two engines, open weights, an off-the-shelf MMD instrument from B, a yardstick from the eval-noise literature), and the conflict between A and D is a publishable hook on its own.

Significance — consequence: If the excess is non-zero and of D's order (~1% of outputs at greedy), then (a) every offline evaluation run through a non-speculative path is measuring a different sampler from the one served, which is a validity defect in exactly the evaluation practice the field uses to qualify models; and (b) a model-equality audit of the B kind would flag a provider as serving a changed model when it had only enabled a documented-lossless optimization — that is, the auditing instrument's false-positive rate depends on an unmeasured quantity. If the excess is indistinguishable from the composition baseline, the vendor claim is vindicated as served, auditors can treat the speculative switch as inside the nuisance envelope, and eval harnesses need not match the serving path. Both outcomes change a practice. Argued, not asserted: B's power figures (77.4% at ~250 completions against quantization-scale distortions) mean the audit false-positive concern is quantitative, not rhetorical — a deviation of D's size is in the detectable range of the instrument the auditing community actually uses.
Significance — audience: (a) model-equality auditors and third-party API monitors (B's community) — would have to declare the serving-stack nuisance envelope before reading an MMD rejection as a model change; (b) evaluation-harness maintainers — would have to match the serving path, not just weights and decoding parameters; (c) serving-engine maintainers (vLLM, SGLang, Ollama) — bears on whether a losslessness claim can be documented without a measured residual, and on whether speculative decoding should be a default; (d) teams running offline-to-online qualification, which is where D's reported customer incident arose.
Significance — fecundity: makes askable whether prefix-cache hits, tensor-parallel degree, chunked prefill and continuous-batching scheduling form a family of channels with a common signature and a common nuisance envelope; whether an engine can ship a certificate — a measured and bounded residual — alongside a losslessness claim; whether the sampler-testing tradition (query 6/8: Barbarik and successors, conditional-sampling testers with provable guarantees) transfers to LLM decoding paths, which would give engine CI a formal fidelity test rather than a greedy-equality smoke test; and whether acceptance-rate-maximizing SD designs trade fidelity for throughput in a measurable way.
Significance — anchoring: (i) the batch-invariance result and the reproducibility-of-serving problem; (ii) model-equality testing and API-drift auditing; (iii) eval validity and the benchmark noise floor (arXiv:2411.00640, arXiv:2608.22331, title-level); (iv) the A-vs-D contradiction, which is a live unreconciled anomaly; (v) this ledger's own UQ-20261016-06, whose fecundity field explicitly named speculative decoding as the next channel to ask about — so this candidate is a derived question of an existing Provisional entry, which is a ledger-internal near-duplicate of motivation if not of estimand, and is recorded as such.

Reason for silence: NOT APPLICABLE as stated — the question is not silent. It is voiced in vendor documentation, answered in a preprint, answered differently in practitioner writing, and discussed in production blogs. What is missing is the rigorous version, and the candidate mechanism for THAT gap is an incentive-and-credit gap crossed with a disciplinary split: losslessness is a theorem, so the systems community treats fidelity as settled by construction and spends its measurement budget where the credit is (acceptance rate, speedup — E is the exemplar), and a well-powered null on fidelity publishes nowhere; meanwhile the two communities that own the method — distribution/sampler testing with provable guarantees (Barbarik lineage) and model-equality auditing (B) — share no venue with the kernel and scheduler engineers who own the exposure, and the auditing community has a positive incentive to treat any detected deviation as a finding about the model rather than as a nuisance envelope to be characterized. This is a plausible mechanism and it is argued from what the opened sources do and do not measure; it is not independently evidenced.
