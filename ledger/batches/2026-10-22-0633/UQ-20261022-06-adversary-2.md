## Restatement in my own words

When a hospital laboratory sends out a number for a test, that number is sometimes not the first number the
analyser produced for that test on that same tube: the tube was re-run, diluted, repeated because a delta
check or a critical value fired, or confirmed on a second instrument, and the later number is the one that
left the building. Two things are being asked. First, how often does that happen — per analyte, and broken
down by which rule caused it — in at least two accredited laboratories on different middleware vendors,
under a rule for "first measurement" written down before anyone looked at the data. Second, if you rebuilt
three population numbers from the *first* measurements instead of the *released* ones — the reference-interval
limits, the fraction of results that cross a clinical decision limit, and the patient-to-patient change used
for monitoring — would any of them move by more than a margin fixed in advance? Plus a side result: how many
laboratories cannot even retrieve the measurement that got overwritten. In short: how much of what a laboratory
reports is a second opinion it gave itself, and does that quietly bend the statistics everyone computes downstream.
