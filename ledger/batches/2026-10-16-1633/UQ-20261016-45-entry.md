Domain(s): STEM — computer-assisted mathematics and formal methods; computer-architecture reliability (silent data corruption, soft errors). Source method: silence mapping.

Question (canonical): (V1, Gate 1 reformulation, adopted) Over a defined frame of published computer-assisted mathematical results, (i) what interval on each result's hardware-fault exposure follows from its reported core-hours, hardware and re-check history combined with a stated fleet SDC rate carried at its own order-of-magnitude uncertainty; and (ii) under a pre-registered fault model, what is the fail-silent fraction of a formally verified certificate checker — the share of injected faults under which it outputs "verified" where no valid derivation exists — against the shares that crash or correctly reject?
(V0, intake: "what is the probability that a published computer-assisted result is wrong for hardware rather than logical reasons…". Reformulated because presupposition 6 concedes hardware-caused and logical wrongness are not separable in the historical record, while the answer criteria ask for an exposure interval, not a probability of wrongness. Reformulation does not rescue novelty.)

Alternative formulations:
1. Does re-checking a certificate with a small verified checker reduce a computer-assisted proof's hardware-fault exposure, or concentrate it into one long unreplicated run?
2. Once the executing machine is admitted to have a measured, core-local, reproducible corruption rate, what does a machine-checked theorem's trusted base actually contain?

Motivating gap: As intaken — the formal-methods trust literature is dense with how-answers (minimal kernels, de Bruijn criterion, independently implemented and verified checkers) and silent on the machine they run on, while the architecture community now measures production-fleet corruptions that are often core-local, data-dependent and reproducible, the regime in which "run it again" and "check the certificate" do not help. After Gate 2 — the mapped silence is real for the kernel/TCB how-literature (independently confirmed by adversary 1: Lean 4.29 "Validating Proofs" enumerates its trusted base with no hardware item) and false for the candidate's field taken as a whole: the question is named and twice quantitatively estimated in the mathematics-of-trust literature. The silence mapping surveyed the wrong shelf.

Presuppositions: (1) published fleet SDC rates transfer to the machines these computations ran on — contested; rate families differ by orders of magnitude and academic/consumer rates are not established. (2) corruption is often core-local and reproducible rather than independent-transient — reported by fleet studies, share contested, load-bearing, and the one presupposition that genuinely post-dates the priors: it invalidates Lam's remedy (re-run affected cases) and Hales' reassurance (E8 repeated ~5x). (3) a verified checker's correctness theorem is conditional on a machine model SDC violates — established by inspection, but already said aloud (Hales §3.4; FOM 2000/Bauer), so the candidate's "rarely said aloud" claim fails. (4) fault injection is a valid proxy for field corruption — contested, and the sharpest part of limb (ii). (5) core-hours, hardware and replication history of published results are recoverable — partly established; Hales demonstrates it anecdotally for E8, the projective plane of order 10 and the four-colour theorem. (6) hardware-caused wrongness is distinguishable from logical/software error — inside the injection experiment yes; in the historical record contested, and Lam already performs a design-time version of the separation.

What would count as an answer: Two measurements reported together. (i) A census over a defined frame recording, per result, core-hours, hardware and whether the certificate check was ever repeated on independent hardware or a different architecture, combined with a stated fleet SDC rate to give an exposure interval carrying that rate's order-of-magnitude uncertainty. (ii) A fault-injection campaign against one or more verified certificate checkers under a pre-registered fault model, reporting the fail-silent fraction against the crash and correct-reject fractions, with sensitivity analysis over the fault model. A fail-silent fraction effectively zero (corruption derails control flow) is an equally admissible settlement.

The Gate 2 kill holds on the source that carries it. The second source is weaker than the record states in three of four respects, and the routing row the batch proposed is withdrawn.
(i) HALES IS CONFIRMED ON EVERY CLAIM, including the arithmetic. Section 3.5 is titled "Soft errors" and concerns cosmic-ray-induced transient hardware error in computer-assisted mathematics. It carries out the attributed computation — 77 hours, 64 GB, 10^3 FIT/Mbit, 1 FIT = 1 error per 10^9 hours — and both quoted sentences appear verbatim: "In software that has been thoroughly debugged, soft errors become the most significant source of error in computation", and the claim that the probability of a soft error inside a proof assistant can be much higher because soft errors scale with execution time. The Denver-versus-Boston 3x elevation comparison appears, in footnote 15 rather than the body. Its estimand is a single point expected value of the form FIT rate x memory x wall time, with no uncertainty interval, no measurement of the actual machine, and no link from an error count to the probability that the mathematical conclusion is wrong.
(ii) Three corrections to how the record quotes Hales. The paper's own printed figure is approximately 39.4 errors, in a displayed equation; "about 40" is its rounded prose, not a second independent estimate. Redoing the arithmetic gives 39.42 with decimal GB (64 x 8000 = 512,000 Mbit) and 40.37 with binary GB (524,288 Mbit), which pins the paper to the decimal conversion. And the 77-hour E8 character-table computation is attributed in Section 3.5 to the Atlas Project, cited [Ada07], not to Vogan.
(iii) LAM 1991 NEEDS NARROWING ON THREE OF FOUR POINTS, and the record leans on it harder than it will bear. The CRAY-1A rate is literature-reported — the machine is "reported to have such errors" — not a rate the authors measured; and the run length is explicitly unrecorded ("we did not keep track of the computer time used on the CRAY"), a guess of 3,000 hours later revised to about 2,000, so "a measured error rate over a known run" overstates both inputs. The cross-check is "two different programs to cross check selective sample cases", not two independently written programs, not a full independent re-run, and not framed by Lam as separating hardware from software failure modes. And "infinitesimal" is scoped to the probability of hardware errors affecting ALL the A2's that a real plane would generate, not to an unqualified probability that the result is wrong. What does hold verbatim is the fourth point: Lam declines the word proof ("these are not proofs in the traditional mathematical sense") and asks for independent verification. Lam's two printed probability values are rendered as inline images in the available edition and could not be read, so no specific numeric probability from Lam is verified here.
(iv) THE SECOND LIMB IS CONFIRMED UN-ASKED. No published fault-injection study of a formally verified proof checker or proof-assistant kernel was found — nothing measuring what fraction of injected hardware faults make such a checker report success on an invalid certificate rather than crash or reject. That is an absence, not an answer, and it is what makes limb (ii) a candidate for fresh intake rather than material for this entry.
