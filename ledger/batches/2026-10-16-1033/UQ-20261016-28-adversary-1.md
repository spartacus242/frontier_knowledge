## 1. Restatement in my own words (not the record's framing)

Take a set of hospital/primary-care laboratories that have been measuring the same handful of blood tests for fifteen years or more. Treat each laboratory like a weather station: its series of results is a mixture of a real signal (what is happening to the patients) and artefacts of its own hardware history (a new analyser arrives, a reagent is reformulated, a calibrator lot turns over, a method generation is replaced). Climatology refuses to read a trend off such a series until it has been "homogenised": you compare the suspect station against its neighbours, difference the two series so the shared signal cancels, and test the difference for steps; a step in the difference means the suspect station, not the weather, changed. Then you subtract the estimated step and re-publish the trend with the adjustment uncertainty carried through.

The candidate asks three things. (a) If you run that exact machinery on clinical laboratories — using other laboratories drawing from overlapping patient catchments as the "neighbours" — where does it say the steps are, and how big are they? (b) Then go and dig up what the laboratories' own service records say about when analysers, methods and calibrators actually changed, and score the blind detector against that: how often did it find a real change, how often did it cry wolf, and were the step sizes it estimated the right sizes? (c) Finally, take a list of already-published papers claiming "X has been rising/falling over the last two decades" that rest on these records, re-run their trends on the step-corrected data, and report how much of each published trend was the laboratory rather than the patients, and how many of the claims keep their sign and their p-value.

The third limb is the headline; the second is the part that makes it a test of the transplant rather than an assertion of it. Plain version: how much of the long-run change we think we see in blood test results is the machines changing, and can you find out without having banked the old blood?

## 3. Primary-source findings I obtained myself (used across the attacks)

**(i) Poh/de Lusignan is a weaker prior than the record concedes.** The record's Gate 2 says "the detection limb is not new" and grades novelty Low partly on that basis. I opened arXiv 1409.1542 and the characterisation needs correcting in the candidate's favour. The unit of analysis is the individual patient's paired series: "We shall call this paired measurement time series the renal function time series of the patient." The reference is internal and depends on a derived quantity: the detector maps a self-recalculated MDRD eGFR onto the laboratory-reported eGFR and counts how many mapping functions are needed — "Performing this mapping from g_self onto g_lab for a series of two or more measurements enables w1 and w0 to be calculated." Peer practices are explicitly NOT a reference; practice grouping instead assumes one shared laboratory: "As all practices sent laboratory samples to a single laboratory this change will affect all eGFR measurements reported by that practice." There is no ground truth — "No codes were identified in the Read code system which enable recording of creatinine assay method" — and no operating characteristics; validation is a plausibility argument, "Both of these factors make this observation highly unlikely to be due to an actual physiological change in these patients." No correction and no trend re-estimation: "Identification of these changes in eGFR calculation method is the first step towards backwards calibration" and "Our method is the first step to generating a back calibration algorithm."
Consequence: what Poh detects is a change in the **eGFR reporting equation**, an event class that is only visible because the laboratory reports both a raw measurand and a derived quantity. It does not detect analyser, reagent or calibrator change, and it cannot be run at all on haemoglobin, albumin or cholesterol. So limb (a) of the candidate — site-referenced detection for analytes with no equation attached — is not executed by Poh, and limb (b) is not executed by anyone I found.

**(ii) The "known date, step dummy, no reference series" design already exists in environmental monitoring.** Chesapeake Bay Program, appendix 2.7a: the 1998 TSS laboratory change is handled with a model term where "LabChange ... takes values of zero before the Laboratory Change and values of one afterwards", which "lets the data define the magnitude of the adjustment factor", the step being station-specific because it is "a function of the salinity at the station". No detection (the date is given), no reference station, no split samples (the document notes the traditional approach is to "conduct a paired sample methods comparison study" and that this method does not), and the historical values are not rewritten — only the model intercept shifts. The document also warns the method "cannot be applied until several years of data have been collected in the post methods change period." This is the exact structural twin of ARIC/Parrinello and of the Sternberg bridging approach: known date, estimated offset, trend read off the corrected model. It is a cross-field precedent for limb (c) and a non-precedent for limbs (a) and (b).

**(iii) Regime check on detection power, and it cuts in the candidate's favour.** Lindau & Venema, "The joint influence of break and noise variance on the break detection capability in time series homogenization", ASCMO 4, 1–18, 2018: "For SNRs below 1, the multiple breakpoint search algorithm fails under typical conditions"; "The results for low SNRs obtained by this algorithm do not differ much from random segmentations"; "If the SNR becomes larger than 0.5, the situation improves rapidly and above a SNR of 1 break detection performs reasonably." They note the SNR for monthly temperature is about 0.5 — i.e. climatology's own flagship application sits in the marginal band. I checked whether the clinical regime is worse or better and conclude, as my own reasoning (labelled as such), that it is plausibly better on this axis: a site reporting hundreds to thousands of results per analyte per month has a standard error on its monthly location statistic one to two orders of magnitude below the within-person biological spread, while documented breakpoints are large (ARIC, per the record, >10% for creatinine and uric acid). So an SNHT-style attack is not obviously underpowered. I therefore decline to kill the candidate on power grounds, and record that the real difference-series "noise" is not sampling error but differential case-mix drift between sites, which is autocorrelated and has no climatological counterpart — an open empirical quantity, which is what P2 says.

**(iv) "Blind calibration" is the candidate's design under another name.** Search 10 found a named problem family — blind drift calibration of sensor networks (arXiv 1707.03682, arXiv 2111.09417, arXiv 2205.01988) — defined as recovering per-node offsets and drifts from network redundancy with no reference instrument. I did not fetch these (budget), so I rely on titles and abstractive search snippets only and will not quote them. It matters anyway: the record's query 7 asked whether pairwise homogenisation had been transplanted out of climatology and got "climatology and only climatology", concluding the design is unique to climate science. That conclusion is an artefact of searching for climatology's vocabulary. The design has an independent, mature home in signal processing.

**(v) A structural identifiability limit on the transplanted design, which the record does not list.** This is my own derivation, labelled as such, and it is an identity rather than an empirical claim. A pairwise-reference procedure tests the differenced series d_ij(t) = x_i(t) − x_j(t). If every site in the frame shifts by the same amount delta at the same time t0 — as happens when a vendor reformulates a reagent nationally, when a calibrator is re-traceable to a new reference material, when IDMS-traceable creatinine calibration propagates through a country's laboratories, or when a reporting equation is swapped by guideline — then d_ij(t) is unchanged at t0 for every pair, and the break is invisible to relative homogenisation by construction. Climatology has the same blind spot for network-wide instrument transitions, and manages it with absolute methods and metadata. The empirical premise that this class is large in laboratory medicine is supported by what I opened: Poh found an inferred method change in 80 of 127 practices with dated changes (the technical report's figure), i.e. a near-simultaneous sweep through a small number of serving laboratories in a 2006–2010 window, and the record's own account of ARIC has HDL moving by method generation (magnesium-dextran precipitation to direct enzymatic) — a vendor-era change, not a site accident. Laboratory method changes are *more* synchronised across sites than weather-station changes, because manufacturers, standardisation programmes and guideline committees act on a whole country at once.

**(vi) The record's audience claim (ii) is factually wrong as stated.** UK Biobank Data-Dictionary coding 165, "Assay correction level", fetched: it "Indicates the type of assay correct that was applied to a biochemistry result", with values 0 "None", 1 "Date and aliquot correction", 2 "Date correction only", and it is attached to 30 biochemistry fields including creatinine and cholesterol. So a major biobank already distributes corrected values plus a per-result flag saying which correction was applied — not merely "assay-era indicators but no corrected values" as the record asserts. The mitigation is real but narrow: UK Biobank's biochemistry came from a single central laboratory in one assay campaign with injected QC, so "date correction" is within-campaign drift correction, the physical-bridge paradigm again, and says nothing about multi-decade multi-site method generations.

## 4. The eight attacks

### Attack 1 — Triviality: can it be answered in a page from existing knowledge?

**Objection.** The answer's direction is already in print and the record admits it. ARIC moved one visit's CKD prevalence from 21.7% to 1.3% by recalibrating creatinine; the newborn-screening literature (my search 14, which returned a cluster of papers across adc.bmj.com, Frontiers in Endocrinology and Int J Neonatal Screening attributing rising CH incidence to assay and cutoff change — titles only, not opened) already attributes secular trends to measurement change; Poh found a step in 90% of practices. So limb (c)'s qualitative answer — "yes, a material fraction, sometimes most of it" — is a one-paragraph inference from existing numbers, and limbs (a) and (b) are engineering.

**Best response.** None of that gives the quantities asked for, and the asked-for quantities are not inferable. Limb (b) is an operating characteristic: sensitivity and specificity of a particular detector on a particular corpus against retrieved logs. No amount of knowing that breakpoints are big tells you whether a differenced-series test finds them, because that depends on an unmeasured quantity — the autocorrelation and magnitude of differential case-mix drift between co-catchment sites. My own regime check (finding (iii)) shows the SNR question has an answer that is neither obvious nor the climatological one, and Lindau & Venema's own field sits at SNR ~0.5, which is exactly where "do not differ much from random segmentations" begins. Limb (c)'s *per-claim* arithmetic cannot be done from the armchair either: the fraction of a given published trend attributable to offsets depends on where the breaks fall relative to the trend window, and ARIC's 25-year scheduled-visit structure gives no information about that for an open-cohort EHR series. And the one thing that would make the whole thing trivial — "the logs do not exist, stop" — is itself an empirical finding nobody has published.

**Status: Resolved.** The question is not answerable in a page. I record the caveat that limb (c)'s *sign* is largely foreseeable, so the paper's surprise value rests on limbs (a) and (b).

### Attack 2 — Disguise: is it a known question renamed?

**Objection.** Translate it out of climatology and clinical chemistry and it is a question several fields ask routinely, with established names. (1) Signal processing: "blind drift calibration of a sensor network" — recover each node's offset and drift from network redundancy with no ground truth (arXiv 1707.03682, 2111.09417, 2205.01988). That is the candidate's transplanted design, stated generally, and the record's conclusion that pairwise referencing exists only in climatology is an artefact of having searched only for climatology's words. (2) Educational measurement: "item parameter drift" and "scale drift" detection against anchor items (ABIM, ETS "Multiple Linking in Equating and Random Scale Drift", Battauz 2022), and — closer to limb (c) — OECD's PISA trend tables, which routinely add a "link error" to the standard error of every cross-cycle comparison precisely so that instrument-change uncertainty is propagated into the published trend. The record lists that propagation as untried fecundity item (c); it is standard practice in two other fields. (3) Environmental monitoring: the "did the lab change or did the river?" literature, including the Chesapeake laboratory-change term I opened. (4) Metabolomics: batch/drift correction with intrastudy QC samples. (5) The candidate's own field already uses peer laboratories' patient medians as a comparator (Danish patient-median work; the 2025 international patient-based EQA programme). So the question is "detect and correct instrument-induced steps in a network of instruments measuring the same latent quantity, then re-report the trend" — asked and answered, repeatedly, under five other names.

**Best response.** Each renaming is missing precisely the thing that makes the candidate hard, and in each case the missing thing is the same. Blind calibration assumes the nodes observe a common field with known spatial structure and that nodes do not move; the clinical analogue of "node moves" is "the patients change", and no blind-calibration formulation I could find addresses a reference node whose *sampled population* shifts at the same instant as its hardware (the record's P7). Educational measurement's anchor items and PISA's link error both require a deliberately administered common instrument — anchor items are a physical bridge, the test-theory equivalent of ARIC's banked aliquots; the candidate's premise is that no bridge was ever laid. Chesapeake, ARIC and Sternberg all take the change date as given; I verified this for Chesapeake from the document itself ("LabChange ... takes values of zero before ... and one afterwards"). Metabolomics QC samples are, again, a physical bridge. The clinical patient-median line is prospective single-analyte QC against a peer group with a known current method, not retrospective breakpoint dating in a fifteen-year record. And limb (b) — operating characteristics of blind detection scored against independently retrieved hardware change logs — I did not find in any of the five vocabularies. The nearest is in geodesy/climatology statistics: Nguyen 2024 on attribution of detected change-points, and arXiv 1511.07238 on change-point detection with partial metadata. I did not open either, so I claim only that they exist and are relevant.

**Status: Partial.** The question's *method* is a known method under at least three other names, which lowers methodological novelty further than the record's Gate 2 did, and one renaming (blind sensor-network calibration) directly falsifies the record's "no transplant out of climatology exists in any domain" finding. But the *question as posed* — this design, on indication-driven multi-site clinical records, scored against retrieved service logs, with pre-registered published claims re-estimated — I could not find under any of the names I tried. Not Unresolved.

### Attack 3 — Combinatorial novelty: is it Field A x Field B with no problem at the interface?

**Objection.** "Apply SNHT to lab data" is a method-shopping exercise. Pick an algorithm from a journal set, point it at a new table, report numbers. The interface contributes nothing.

**Best response.** There are at least three interface problems, and I can source or derive each. First, and the strongest point in the candidate's favour: the differencing identity that makes relative homogenisation work in climatology assumes the *signal* is shared and the *breaks* are not. In laboratory medicine both halves are inverted relative to climate. The shared signal (population health) is weak and slow; the breaks are strongly synchronised across sites because vendors and standardisation bodies act nationally — see my finding (v). That inversion is not a nuisance, it is a structural statement about what the transplanted estimator can and cannot see, and it has to be worked out before any correction is admissible. Second, the "reference station moves with the instrument" problem: a station relocation does not change the weather, but an analyser purchase bundled with a new service line changes the sampled patients, so the break and the signal shift together at the same site at the same instant. Nothing in blind calibration or in climate homogenisation has this. Third, detection-power regime: Lindau & Venema give a hard threshold (fails below SNR 1, random-like at low SNR) and their own flagship sits at 0.5; the clinical regime has a wholly different variance decomposition (negligible sampling error, non-negligible differential case-mix drift), so the threshold has to be re-derived, not imported. These are genuine unsolved problems located exactly at the seam.

**Status: Resolved.** There is a real problem at the interface, and it is sharper than the record states, because the record's P2 only asks whether sites share the *signal* and never asks whether they share the *breaks*.

### Attack 4 — Presupposition failure: does it smuggle in a false or contested assumption?

**Objection.** Two candidates, one of them unlisted and probably false.
(A) The unlisted one. The question presupposes that site breakpoints are idiosyncratic enough to show up in a differenced series. By the identity in finding (v), any break common to all frame sites at the same time cancels in every pairwise difference and is undetectable. The largest and most consequential breakpoints in clinical chemistry are of exactly that kind — national IDMS-traceable creatinine recalibration, a manufacturer reformulating a reagent, a guideline swapping MDRD for CKD-EPI, an assay re-traced to a new WHO International Standard. Poh's own data are consistent with this: a method change inferred in 80 of 127 practices with dated changes, clustered in a short window, because a small number of serving laboratories changed. So the procedure in limb (a) is blind to the dominant break class, limb (b)'s sensitivity is then being estimated against a log set dominated by events the design cannot in principle detect, and limb (c) will systematically under-attribute trend magnitude to breakpoints. The record's three decision-relevant outcomes include "trends unchanged within CI licenses raw-series trend claims" — and that is precisely the outcome a design blind to synchronised breaks will tend to produce for the wrong reason. A falsely reassuring licence is worse than no study.
(B) The listed-but-possibly-empty one. R1 repairs well-posedness by restricting to "analytes with documented commutability across the method generations present". Commutability assessment is a property of candidate reference materials evaluated against clinical samples, and the formal apparatus for documenting it is recent; for method generations that were retired fifteen or twenty years ago, documented commutability assessments may simply not exist, in which case the pre-specified frame is empty or near-empty and the question cannot be instantiated.

**Best response.** On (A): the identity is true but its scope is narrower than the objection implies. Analyser replacement, reagent lot turnover, calibrator lot change, maintenance and a laboratory consolidating onto a different platform are site-idiosyncratic in timing, and these are frequent — they are what the service logs are full of. The design is blind to the synchronised subclass only. A competent execution would say so in advance, partition the log into synchronised and idiosyncratic events, estimate sensitivity separately within each, and report that the synchronised class requires an absolute method or external metadata — which is exactly what climatology does and exactly the kind of finding the record's "negative answer is informative" clause anticipates. The objection converts a hidden assumption into a required design element; it does not void the question. On (B): the restriction can be read as "analytes whose method generations are known to measure the same measurand" rather than "analytes with a filed CLSI-format commutability study", and creatinine, haemoglobin and total cholesterol all have standardisation histories that make that judgement defensible. Also, ARIC's creatinine is commutable and standardised and *still* showed >10% bias, so the restriction does not select away the effect.

**Status: Partial.** I considered Unresolved on (A) and stopped short for two reasons: the false assumption is false only for a subclass of breakpoints, and I did not open a source quantifying what share of fifteen-year laboratory breakpoints are network-synchronised versus site-idiosyncratic, so the kill is unsourced at the quantitative step — and an unsourced kill is Partial by the standard I am bound to. But this is the attack that did the most damage, and (A) is an assumption the record does not list anywhere in P1–P8. The question should not be recorded without it added as P9, flagged CONTESTED and probably false in part.

### Attack 5 — Vagueness: could it be answered no matter what the evidence shows, or never?

**Objection.** Two of the three named deliverables are not identified estimands. (i) Specificity, as the record itself concedes at P8: with incomplete logs, "no documented change" is not "no change", so the false-positive rate is not estimable; R1 does not repair it and the record scores well-posedness 2/3 for this reason. (ii) Worse, and unacknowledged: "the fraction of each published trend's magnitude attributable to breakpoint offsets" is a counterfactual quantity that depends on the detector that produced the offsets. If detection has non-trivial error — which limb (b) exists to measure — then the attributed fraction is a function of the detector's errors, not of the world, and the same corpus yields different "fractions attributable" under SNHT, pairwise comparison and ACMANT with no principled way to choose. The record names three algorithms as alternatives, which guarantees three answers. On top of that, "K sites" is left as a free parameter and the size of the "pre-registered list of published claims" is unspecified, so the frame is not actually fixed by the question text.

**Best response.** The attribution limb is identified *conditional on* the detected break set, and reporting it conditionally, with the limb-(b) operating characteristics in hand and with sensitivity analyses across detectors, is standard practice: climatology reports adjustment uncertainty from exactly this source and propagates it into trend confidence intervals, and PISA's link error is the same manoeuvre in another field — so the estimand has two mature precedents for how to report it honestly. The specificity defect has a known technical remedy that the record did not find: change-point detection with partial information on change-point times is a studied problem (arXiv 1511.07238, not opened), which means P8 is a design gap rather than an impossibility. And "K" being free is a feature of a pre-registration template, not vagueness: the frame is fixed at registration, which is what R1 says.

**Status: Partial.** One of three deliverables is not estimable as written (record concedes), a second is detector-dependent in a way the record does not concede, and the fix in both cases is a further design step the question does not specify. It is not infinitely elastic — limb (a) and the sensitivity half of limb (b) are sharp — so this is not a kill.

### Attack 6 — Significance inflation: is the audience real, would they act either way?

**Objection.** The audience is partly mis-described and partly already served. The record claims biobank curators "currently ship assay-era indicators but no corrected values"; I fetched UK Biobank's own data dictionary and that is wrong — coding 165 "Assay correction level" is attached to 30 biochemistry fields, with values "None", "Date and aliquot correction", "Date correction only", so a flagship biobank ships corrected values and a per-result correction flag. The clinical-chemistry audience is also already acting, with a different and better instrument: peer-group patient medians and patient-based EQA (the 2025 international programme), which is the peer-reference idea operationalised prospectively. And on acting either way: the record itself concedes ARIC made the general point with an effect "larger than any secular effect anyone would claim" and it "did not propagate". A finding that failed to move the field once is unlikely to move it the second time, especially when the second version is weaker (no banked aliquots).

**Best response.** The UK Biobank correction is a within-campaign drift correction from a single central laboratory with injected QC, i.e. the physical-bridge paradigm; it is evidence that curators will act when they have a bridge, and silent on the multi-decade multi-site case where they do not. Patient-based EQA is prospective and tells a laboratory it is currently off its peer group; it does not date a step in 2011 and does not correct a published trend. The audience that would actually act is narrower than the record's five bullets but real: the specific authors of the pre-registered trend claims, and the journals that would get a methods requirement, have no choice about reading a result that names their papers — which is also the incentive problem in attack 7. And the negative outcome is genuinely cheap-useful: "for these analytes at this site density, raw trends are safe" is a result a methodologist can cite to avoid work.

**Status: Partial.** The audience is real but the record overstates it on a checkable point, and the record's own concession that ARIC did not propagate is the strongest evidence against action.

### Attack 7 — Reason for silence: why has no one asked it?

**Objection.** The record offers disciplinary boundary, incentive gap and missing metadata. But if the method exists in signal processing as blind calibration, in educational measurement as scale-drift detection, in environmental monitoring as lab-change modelling, and in clinical chemistry itself as peer patient medians, then "nobody could see it" is not available — four communities saw it. What is left looks like "nobody has bothered", which the brief rules out.

**Best response.** The mechanism survives, and I can now source the material half of it myself rather than relying on the record. Poh et al. state flatly that "No codes were identified in the Read code system which enable recording of creatinine assay method" — the station-metadata half of the climatological method had no retrievable counterpart in a 127-practice, 951,764-record extract, so an investigator who tried would stop at the feasibility step, which is a material barrier, not an attitude. The boundary half also survives in sharpened form: the method's homes are NCEI/ASCMO (Menne & Williams 2005, Lindau & Venema 2018) and sensor-network signal processing (arXiv 1707.03682 et al.); neither is read by laboratory medicine, and the clinical community's own peer-comparison tool (patient medians, patient-based EQA) grew up inside a *quality control* frame whose purpose is to tell a laboratory to fix itself today, not to rewrite its past — so the operation is unavailable in that framework for a reason of purpose, not ignorance. And the incentive half is the sharpest: the product of this work is the invalidation of other people's published trends, plus a new methods burden. The four communities that have the method each have a different reason not to point it here.

**Status: Resolved.** A credible, now independently sourced mechanism: missing metadata as a hard feasibility stop, plus two disciplinary boundaries, plus an incentive structure that rewards nobody. I note that what the mechanism explains is the silence on the narrow residue; the broad question was not silent, as the record honestly says.

### Attack 8 — Dissolution: would it dissolve under analysis rather than be answered?

**Objection.** Two dissolution paths. (a) R1's own repair may dissolve the interest: restricting to commutable analytes selects for the cases where a method change is a subtractable offset, i.e. the cases where the correction is a nuisance parameter rather than a threat, so the answer could reduce to "where it was safe to subtract, subtracting did not change much". (b) The synchronised-break identity (finding (v)) dissolves limb (a) into "we detected the small site-local breaks and were structurally blind to the big national ones", at which point limb (c) is not an answer about trends but an artefact of the detector's blind spot, and the right conclusion is "you cannot do this without metadata" — which is a feasibility report, not an answer to the question asked.

**Best response.** On (a): commutable does not mean unbiased, and the record's ARIC numbers are the counterexample — standardised, commutable creatinine carried >10% bias and moved a prevalence estimate by an order of magnitude. On (b): a study that lands on "pairwise referencing recovers idiosyncratic breaks with sensitivity s and is blind to synchronised ones" has answered a real question with a real number — it tells you how much of laboratory history is recoverable from the data alone, which is the record's fecundity item (b), and it tells biobank curators which of their breakpoints they must document because no statistic will recover them. That is a result, not a dissolution.

**Status: Partial.** Path (b) is a live risk: the question could reduce to a feasibility statement plus a structural impossibility proof. That is publishable and useful, but it is not the question's stated deliverable, and a candidate whose most likely outcome is a reformulation of itself is weakened.

## 5. Closing: the single strongest surviving objection

The strongest surviving objection is the unlisted presupposition identified in Attack 4(A) and derived in finding (v): a pairwise-reference design can only see breaks that are *not* shared across the frame, because a simultaneous common shift cancels identically in every differenced series — and in laboratory medicine the largest and most consequential breaks are precisely the shared ones, since manufacturers, standardisation programmes and guideline committees change a whole country's laboratories at once, whereas weather stations are re-sited one at a time. This inverts the condition that makes the climatological transplant work, it is nowhere in the record's P1–P8, and it biases the study's most quotable deliverable in the most dangerous direction: limb (c) will under-attribute published trend magnitude to breakpoints, and the record's own first decision-relevant outcome — "trends unchanged within CI licenses raw-series trend claims" — is the outcome this blind spot manufactures. I stopped at Partial rather than Unresolved only because the blindness covers a subclass rather than the whole, and because I did not open a source quantifying the synchronised/idiosyncratic split; a reviewer with that number could make this a kill. The candidate should not be recorded without a P9 stating this assumption, and limb (b) should be required to report sensitivity separately within synchronised and idiosyncratic event classes.

Two corrections to the record, both from sources I opened, run the other way and should be carried forward: Poh/de Lusignan detects a change in the *eGFR reporting equation* using a within-patient reference that exists only for derived quantities, with no ground truth, no operating characteristics, no correction and no trend re-estimation — so limb (a) for analytes without an attached equation is less covered than Gate 2 conceded; and UK Biobank does ship corrected biochemistry values with a per-result "Assay correction level" flag, so the record's audience bullet (ii) is wrong as written. Separately, the record's finding that pairwise referencing has never been transplanted out of climatology is an artefact of searching in climatology's vocabulary: the same design is a named problem family ("blind drift calibration") in sensor-network signal processing.
