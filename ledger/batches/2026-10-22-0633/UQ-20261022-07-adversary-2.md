## Restatement in my own words

Take trials that have already finished, where (a) the drug being tested was also gettable off the street / off-label / from a compounder or telehealth seller while the trial was recruiting, (b) blood or urine from the control arm is still in a freezer from the ordinary study visits, and (c) somebody is allowed to thaw it for a question the original consent did not name. Before anybody opens a freezer, write down which trials are in the frame and which estimand you will use. Then ask three things. First, a rate: out of all control-arm visits, at how many does a single pre-specified assay find the study drug class — with the assay's detection limit, its false-positive rate, and how long after a dose it can still see anything, stated against the gap between visits. Second, agreement: when the assay says yes, did that participant's own concomitant-medication form also say yes. Third, a correction: how far, and in which direction, does the published primary result move when you re-run it under the pre-registered contamination-adjusted estimand. And report how much of the frame was refused by ethics/biobank governance, so the denominator is auditable.

In short: trials verify adherence in the treated arm with chemistry and verify non-exposure in the control arm with a questionnaire; the question asks what chemistry would say about the control arm, and what that does to the answer.
