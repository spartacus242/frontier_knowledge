## Restatement (my words, not the generator's)

Take one human cell line and a list of at least a hundred genes. For each gene, remove its product two ways: slowly, by cutting the gene and letting the existing protein dilute out over several divisions (CRISPR knockout), and fast, by tagging the protein with a degron and adding ligand. Measure how much target protein is actually left in each arm at the moment you read fitness, and apply an essentiality threshold fixed in advance. Question: among genes where the two arms end up at the *same* residual protein level, how often do the two methods still call essentiality differently — and does whatever disagreement remains line up with (a) how long-lived the protein is, (b) how much compensating transcription the mutant allele triggers (which is why a knockout arm containing RNA-less alleles as well as premature-stop alleles is demanded), or neither? Underneath sits a slogan: is "essential" a fact about the gene, or an artefact of how fast you take its product away?

So the unit is a gene in one cell line; the endpoint is a fitness-derived binary call; the comparator is the same gene in the other perturbation modality; the estimand is a discordance rate conditional on measured residual protein, plus two regression coefficients.
