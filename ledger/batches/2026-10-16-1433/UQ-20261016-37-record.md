Question (canonical) — V0, as intaked:
Taking the certified sterility assurance level of 10^-6 as a frequency claim about released units rather than as a validation convention, is the ascertained record of intrinsic non-sterility in terminally sterilized product — release sterility-test failures, recalls, and confirmed intrinsic-contamination outbreaks — quantitatively consistent with 10^-6 across the annual released fleet, and if it is not, is the discrepancy attributable to the log-linear inactivation extrapolation that generates the number?

Question (canonical) — V1, Gate 1 reformulation (one permitted; made only for well-posedness: converts the test to an explicit one-sided bound, separates measurement from mechanism attribution, and fixes the frame):
For terminally sterilized products certified at a PNSU/SAL of 10^-6, over a fixed window with an enumerated released-unit denominator: (i) what one-sided lower bound on the realized probability of intrinsic non-sterility per released unit does the ascertained record of release sterility-test failures, non-sterility recalls and confirmed intrinsic-contamination outbreaks support, after an explicit attribution rule excluding aseptically filled product, post-sterilization container-closure breach and in-use contamination, and under a stated ascertainment-probability interval; and (ii) if that bound exceeds 10^-6, is the excess reproduced in direction and order of magnitude by recomputing PNSU for the same cycles under published non-log-linear (shoulder/tailing, Weibull) inactivation parameters in place of the log-linear extrapolation used at release?
Both versions are recorded; V1 is the operative formulation for Gates 2-3.

Alternative formulations:
- Does the realized non-sterility rate of terminally sterilized product match the one-in-a-million that licensed its release, and has anyone ever done that arithmetic?
- What interval on the true probability of a non-sterile unit does the observed population of sterility failures support, and how does it compare with the extrapolated SAL used at release?

Motivating gap:
Every terminally sterilized unit is released on a probability (PNSU/SAL, conventionally 10^-6) obtained by extrapolating an inactivation curve roughly six logs below the smallest measurable survivor endpoint: biological indicators carry ~10^6 spores, so the demonstrable range is 2-3 logs while the claim is 12 logs of kill. The presupposition inverted is not the kinetics but the epistemic status of the number — it is treated as unfalsifiable by construction, so the one check available (comparing it against the realized record over a fleet of order 10^10-10^11 units a year) is, on the intake hypothesis, never attempted. The inadequacy of log-linearity was flagged at intake as prior art (shoulder/tailing is standard in food-processing inactivation modelling; the mechanistic-versus-vitalistic debate is old). Gate 2 found that the epistemic limb is prior art too — see below.

Presuppositions:
1. SAL/PNSU is intended as a frequency statement about released units, not solely as a process-validation metric. CONTESTED, and fatal if false: on the validation-only reading the question dissolves rather than being answered. Gate 2 weakens this presupposition: the standards-facing literature opened here presents SAL as a design and validation target (Manufacturing Chemist 2002) and the leading critique treats it as a validation requirement whose proof is impossible (von Woedtke & Kramer 2008), not as a frequency estimate anyone defends.
2. The annual released fleet of terminally sterilized units certified at 10^-6 is enumerable to within an order of magnitude from regulatory and industry production statistics. PARTIALLY SUPPORTED: search results point to AdvaMed and American Chemistry Council materials asserting a US ethylene-oxide-sterilized device volume of order tens of billions per year, with EtO covering roughly half of US device volume. NOT VERIFIED BY OPENING — these appeared as search results only and the fetch budget was spent; the 10^-6-certified subset is a further unresolved restriction, since device categories differ in certified SAL.
3. Confirmed intrinsic non-sterility events are ascertainable with a quantifiable and boundable ascertainment fraction. CONTESTED and the hardest point: most non-sterile units cause no detectable event, so the comparison is one-sided — an observed rate above 10^-6 falsifies, an observed rate below it does not confirm. Gate 2 finds this point already argued in the field: von Woedtke & Kramer state it is "practically impossible to find a difference in the rate of infection" between product at 10^-6 and at 10^-3.
4. Events can be attributed to terminal-sterilization failure rather than post-sterilization container-closure breach, aseptically filled product, or in-use contamination. UNRESOLVED by this Gate 2: the outbreak literature surfaced (contaminated IV fluids; contaminated antiseptics scoping review) is organised by product and hazard, not by sterilization modality, and no attribution rule of the required kind was found.
5. Measured non-log-linear inactivation parameters exist for the relevant organism-matrix-cycle combinations and can be used to recompute PNSU. PARTIALLY SUPPORTED: Weibull inactivation modelling is mature and tabulated in food-processing work; whether parameters exist for the specific industrial cycles and BI organism-carrier-matrix combinations in the 10^-6 frame was not established.
6. 10^-6 is the claim actually made for the products in the frame (some categories are released at 10^-3), so the frame must be restricted to 10^-6-certified products. SUPPORTED as a framing constraint: EN 556 applies 10^-6 to items labelled sterile, while some US non-invasive devices are at 10^-3 (Manufacturing Chemist 2002); von Woedtke & Kramer propose a 10^-6 / 10^-4 / 10^-3 tiering.
7. Release sterility testing has characterised sensitivity, since otherwise the numerator's ascertainment cannot be bounded. CONTESTED: the field's own position is that final inspection cannot detect contamination at these levels (von Woedtke & Kramer; Akers & Agalloco), which caps the sterility-test contribution to the numerator at near zero and throws the whole numerator onto recalls and outbreaks.

What would count as an answer:
Four deliverables. (a) A defensible denominator: released terminally sterilized units certified at 10^-6 over a fixed window, from production and regulatory statistics, with a stated range. (b) An ascertained numerator of confirmed intrinsic non-sterility over the same window, with an explicit ascertainment-probability interval and an attribution rule separating terminal-sterilization failure from aseptic-process and post-sterilization causes. (c) The resulting interval on the realized non-sterility rate. (d) A recomputation of PNSU for the same cycles under published non-log-linear inactivation parameters. Answered one way if the realized-rate interval excludes 10^-6 and the recomputation reproduces the direction and rough magnitude of the gap; answered the other way if the realized interval contains 10^-6 and the non-log-linear recomputation stays at or below it. Because the numerator is an undercount, the answer must be stated as a one-sided bound, not a point estimate.

- Endpoint: whether SAL 10^-6 is experimentally provable and evidence-based; plus a normative proposal to tier SALs at 10^-6 / 10^-4 / 10^-3.
- Reference/comparator: the highest experimentally accessible inactivation level (~10^-2, with one log of extrapolation to 10^-3) against the required 10^-6; and, asserted rather than measured, infection rates at 10^-6 versus 10^-3.
- Estimand: none. The claims are argumentative and normative — "a practical proof of the required level of sterility assurance of 10-6 is not possible"; the SAL is "neither evidence based nor can it be accurately ascertained in experiments." No denominator, no estimator, no interval.

Source showing it is open: von Woedtke T, Kramer A, "The limits of sterility assurance," GMS Hygiene and Infection Control 3(1):Doc19, 2008, https://journals.publisso.de/en/journals/hic/volume3/dgkh000117 — poses the provability question and leaves the empirical comparison undone, calling it practically impossible. Significance — consequence:
If the realized-rate interval excludes 10^-6 and the tail-aware recomputation reproduces the gap in direction and magnitude, then parametric release of terminally sterilized product rests on a number wrong in a known direction, and the overkill justifications underlying ISO 11135/11137/17665 and USP <1222>/Ph.Eur. 5.1.1 would need a tail-aware PNSU rather than a log-linear one — a change to the arithmetic of release, not merely to its rhetoric. If the interval contains 10^-6 and the recomputation stays at or below it, the convention is vindicated as a conservative bound, and the standing critique (P1; and the broader unprovability argument of P2) loses its practical force — which would settle a live standards dispute, since P1's concrete proposal is to relax the requirement to 10^-4/10^-3 for whole product classes on the strength of that critique. Either way the answer is consequential precisely because a normative proposal is already on the table that the arithmetic would support or defeat. The asymmetry is the real limit: a null result is weak evidence, because the numerator is an undercount by construction.
Significance — audience:
Sterilization standards committees (ISO/TC 198 and the AAMI sterilization working groups that own 11135/11137/17665), pharmacopoeial expert committees for USP <1211>/<1222> and Ph.Eur. 5.1.1, notified bodies and FDA CDRH/CDER reviewers who grant parametric release, and the hospital infection-control community that must reason about device-attributable infection. The action is concrete and identifiable: the tiered-SAL proposal of P1 has been in the literature since 2008 and has not been adopted; a realized-rate bound is the evidence its adoption or rejection currently lacks. Against that, the same committees may legitimately hold that SAL is a process criterion by design, in which case they would not act — this is the main route by which the significance claim deflates, and it is the same route as presupposition 1.
Significance — fecundity:
Makes askable: (i) what the ascertainment fraction for intrinsic non-sterility actually is, and what surveillance design would make SAL falsifiable at all — a question P1 forecloses by assertion; (ii) what conservatism is purchased by requiring 12 logs rather than 6, in cost, in heat or dose damage to product, and in foregone adoption of low-temperature modalities; (iii) the general form — for any certified-but-unobservable probability (aviation 10^-9/hour failure budgets, software reliability targets, PRA core-damage frequencies), what one-sided bound does the operating record support — which connects sterilization science to the method-owning fields found at queries 5 and 6.
Significance — anchoring:
The mechanistic-versus-vitalistic survivor-curve dispute; the EMA Annex 1 physical-versus-biological-evidence dispute (Agalloco 2019, "The Bugs Don't Lie"); P1's unadopted tiered-SAL proposal; the detection-limit argument of P2; and the general problem of validating extrapolated low-probability failure claims against operating experience in nuclear PRA and reliability demonstration testing.

Reason for silence:
Candidate mechanism: a split of custody reinforced by an authoritative foreclosure. The number is a regulatory release criterion rather than a scientific estimate, so the parties who could assemble the denominator — manufacturers and contract sterilizers — have no incentive to construct a falsification test of their own release basis, while the parties who hold the numerator — recall and outbreak surveillance — are organised by product and hazard class, not by sterilization modality, so no single body owns both halves of the fraction (query 8 shows the outbreak corpus has exactly this shape). On top of that disciplinary/incentive gap sits a specific foreclosure: when the field did state the problem authoritatively (P1, 2008), it concluded that the field-outcome comparison is practically impossible, which converted an open empirical question into a closed rhetorical one and removed the prompt to attempt the arithmetic. That is also why the silence is only partial, and why the correct verdict here is asked-but-open rather than un-asked: the question was posed, answered in the epistemic register, and then its empirical register was declared unreachable rather than tried.
