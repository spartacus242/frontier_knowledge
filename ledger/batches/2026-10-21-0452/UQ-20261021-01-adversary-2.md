## Restatement in my own words

Patients take their medicine more reliably in the few days before a clinic appointment. If the
trial's outcome is a biomarker that reacts within days (office BP, LDL, HIV RNA, IOP, INR), then the
number measured at the visit is partly a reading of that pre-visit burst of dosing rather than of the
patient's ordinary dosing. The question asks: how big, and in which direction, is the gap between
(i) the treatment effect a trial reports from visit measurements and (ii) the effect those same
patients' doses would have produced had the same number of doses been spread evenly through the
interval instead of bunched before the visit — reported per randomised arm and as a share of the arm
difference, against a pre-set margin, with HbA1c (too slow to respond) as the control case where the
gap must come out at zero. In one line: is some of a drug's measured efficacy an artefact of the
appointment calendar, and how much?

## My own arithmetic (labelled as mine, with the source's uncertainty carried)

Tousset's model is, in substance, the exposure-response map the candidate says it would have to declare, fitted on a *pre-visit* exposure window in one of the candidate's own index indications. Taking a saturating form E(x) = Emax*x/(E50+x) (**the form is my assumption; the abstract reports an Emax and an E50, not the equation**), the local slope is Emax*E50/(E50+x)^2 mmHg per unit exposure fraction.

- At point estimates (Emax 24 mmHg, E50 0.51) and x ~ 0.95 (Poleon's optimal adherers): 24*0.51/1.46^2 = 5.74 mmHg per unit = **0.057 mmHg per adherence percentage point**.
- Poleon's pre-visit elevation in that subgroup is 97.7 - 94.0 = **3.7 pp**, confined to the Pre3-1 window. Applied across the whole exposure window: **~0.21 mmHg**. Pro-rated as 3 elevated days inside Tousset's 10-day window: **~0.06 mmHg**.
- Carrying Tousset's stated intervals at x ~ 0.9: slope between 11*0.26/1.16^2 = 2.13 (0.021 mmHg/pp) and 37*0.76/1.66^2 = 10.21 (0.102 mmHg/pp), i.e. **0.08-0.38 mmHg** window-wide, **0.02-0.11 mmHg** pro-rated, for a 3.7 pp elevation.

Against antihypertensive arm contrasts of order 8-15 mmHg office SBP this is **under a few percent, in one arm, before any between-arm cancellation**. Scope limits I will not paper over: this transplants a glaucoma eyedrop entrainment into a hypertension pill model; Tousset's cohort is treatment-*resistant* hypertension, so its Emax need not generalise; HIV RNA (threshold-like) and INR (time-in-range) are not covered and could behave quite differently; and the counterfactual is my slope approximation, not the candidate's declared redistribution computed on patient-level records.
