Domain(s): Medicine — primary care / general internal medicine; clinical informatics; diagnostic safety. Adjacent: pharmacoepidemiology (code-based phenotype validity).
Question (canonical): For label/result pairs with a consensus disconfirming rule computable from routine data — (a) COPD code + post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC >= 0.7, (b) hypothyroidism code + normal TSH >= 6 weeks after last levothyroxine supply, (c) coeliac code + negative serology and negative biopsy on a documented gluten-containing diet — what is the 24-month cause-specific hazard and cumulative incidence of a recorded retraction event (problem-list removal, "resolved"/inactivated status, exclusion code; pre-declared fallback, reported separately: >= 180 days without disease-specific prescribing) after the first disconfirming result, against matched person-time in labelled patients with no such result, and what share of each group is still on disease-specific therapy at 12 and 24 months? (V2, the Gate 1 reformulation; asthma + negative challenge and heart failure + normal natriuretic peptide are pre-specified secondary pairs; penicillin allergy + negative challenge is the positive control.)
Alternative formulations:
- Are clinical diagnoses falsifiable in practice — what is the hazard of a label being removed once the test that defines it comes back against it?
- When the chart already contains the result that rules a condition out, how long does the condition stay on the problem list and on the prescription?
- V1 (intake, failed Gate 1: "objectively disconfirming evidence" undefined for two of five pairs; outcome stated without naming the data model that emits it): In routine care records, once a chronic-disease diagnosis has been entered, what is the rate at which it is retracted after objectively disconfirming evidence appears in the same record, and does retraction change the disease-specific treatment the patient goes on receiving?
Motivating gap: Repeated studies find a large minority of chronic-disease labels unsupported by their defining criteria (Aaron 2017 ruled out current asthma in 33.1%, 95% CI 29.4-36.8, of 613 completers), but de-labelling there was a research intervention. The intake's framing — nobody asks what routine care does when the disconfirming result is already filed — was falsified at Gate 2 for the index pair: Fortis 2016 asked exactly that for COPD and reported both limbs. What survived was narrower (multi-pair, time-to-event, comparator-controlled, recorded-removal-event endpoint) and did not survive Gate 4's independent sweeps.
Presuppositions: P1 dated diagnosis histories retrievable INCLUDING removal events — recorded as the weakest point at Gate 2; both adversaries EVIDENCED it instead (Jones 2024 built a 16,023-patient CPRD GOLD cohort indexed on first "resolved asthma" code; NHS Digital publishes a QOF "COPD resolved" refset). P2 a computable disconfirming rule per pair — holds for COPD by GOLD's definitional convention; adversary 2 found the hypothyroid rule mis-specified (fires while the patient is still supplied, since ">= 6 weeks after last supply" ignores days'-supply) and the coeliac rule effectively uncomputable (gluten intake lives in free text; labelled patients are gluten-free by default). P3 the result was visible to a clinician — contested; Rimawi 2013 records "no attempt to convey the result" outside the index system. P4 labels are intended to be falsifiable — contested; partly rebutted (a register carrying resolved codes is by construction a register of current disease). P5 retraction is not scored as the good outcome — descriptive only. P6 confounding by record continuity is a design problem (prevalent-user and immortal-time structure), not a covariate problem.
What would count as an answer: A pre-registered multi-database cohort (CPRD/OpenSAFELY, a US EHR network, a Nordic registry) reporting per pair the 24-month cumulative incidence of recorded retraction after the first disconfirming result against matched no-result person-time, the share still on disease-specific therapy at 12 and 24 months in each arm, penicillin allergy as positive control, and the prescribing-cessation analysis where removal events are not emitted. A hazard indistinguishable from comparator with therapy continuing answers it one way; a materially raised hazard with therapy stopping answers it the other. Removals never being recorded would falsify P1 and return the question to Gate 1.

Significance — consequence: If the hazard is indistinguishable from matched no-result person-time and therapy continues, every code-defined chronic-disease denominator (QOF-style registers, phenotype libraries, HCC-style risk adjustment, code-based trial recruitment) carries a false-positive fraction that is absorbing rather than transient, and de-labelling trials need a maintenance arm. If the hazard is raised and therapy stops, the one-third prevalence figures measure a queue being cleared and the overdiagnosis reading weakens. Deflated: for COPD the direction is already published.
Significance — audience: EHR-phenotyping and pharmacoepidemiology methodologists; primary-care quality bodies that already own resolved/exclusion coding (NHS Digital copdres_cod; vendor inhaler alerts keyed to "dormant or resolved coding"); de-labelling trialists in respiratory, endocrine and allergy medicine; diagnostic-safety researchers; payers using diagnosis-based risk adjustment. Judged real by both adversaries (attack 6 Resolved twice).
Significance — fecundity: whether the hazard varies by who filed the result (own practice vs external lab) makes result-acknowledgement auditing an exposure and tests P3; whether retraction is faster where the EHR emits a removal event turns the data model into a treatment; whether "history of" conversion rather than deletion dominates creates a measurable intermediate category.
Significance — anchoring: the misdiagnosis-prevalence anomaly (Aaron 2017; the COPD spirometry series); the problem-list literature's asymmetry between additions and deletions; penicillin-allergy delabelling and relabelling; code-based phenotype validity and chronic-flag persistence in claims.
