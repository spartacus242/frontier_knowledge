## Restatement (my own words, before reading the generator's framing twice)

Pull old clinical genetics reports that announced a recessive disease on the strength of two heterozygous mutations in one gene, in cases where nobody ever checked which chromosome copy each mutation sat on. Go back to the frozen DNA and look directly with long reads. Count what fraction had both mutations on the same copy — meaning the patient was a carrier, not a compound heterozygote, and the report was wrong. Then, among those, count how many are still genuinely affected because a second hit (deep intronic, structural, methylation) was sitting undetected on the other copy. Report the first number as "phase was wrong" and the second as "the diagnosis was wrong," with confidence intervals, broken out by how far apart the two variants were, by gene, by era, and by whether the lab leaned on a population cis/trans prior. Measure it on the DNA you can actually retrieve, and say what fraction of issued reports that is.

In one line: **how often was "compound heterozygous" a mistake, now that phase is directly observable?**

### The decisive extraction (Guo et al. 2024, preprint full text)

- 627 patients from the Broad Center for Mendelian Genetics, each with a **confident or strong candidate diagnosis** (i.e. already-made recessive diagnoses, the candidate's target population).
- Phase estimable for **293**; of these: **281 (95.9%) in trans, 7 (2.4%) in cis, 5 (1.7%) indeterminate**.
- Trio sequencing available for **168 of the 293** (so ~43% were proband-only — the un-phased subset the candidate's presupposition 1 needs).
- Six of the seven cis calls came from proband-only sequencing; the seventh was trio-refuted ("which confirmed the variants to be in trans"), and the authors concede "Despite one error and several indeterminate predictions."
- For the cis cases, **"the responsible clinician was contacted."**
- They note "we have only tested our phasing accuracy in a clinical setting in a retrospective manner."

The record's Gate 2 wrote its own kill condition: *"if Guo's validation includes a count of presumed-causal pairs their method or trio data called cis, that count is a partial answer in miniature and would push novelty to Low."* It does. 7/293 (2.4%), retrospective, on issued research-clinical diagnoses, with clinician recontact.
