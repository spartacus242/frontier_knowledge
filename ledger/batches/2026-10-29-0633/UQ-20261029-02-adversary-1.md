## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Take a real elemental solid and let it melt as a set of nanoparticles of known radius between 2 and 20 nm. The melting temperature falls with radius; the standard fit assumes the crystal–melt interfacial free energy per unit area is one number independent of how sharply the interface is curved. The candidate asks whether that number itself changes with curvature — whether there is a first-order (1/R) correction, called a solid–liquid Tolman length — what its sign is (does a small crystal's interface cost more or less per unit area than a flat one), and how large it is compared with the thickness of the interface. The repaired form (V2) narrows this to: from experimental Tm(R) on at least two real elemental metals, with premelting and substrate terms constrained by a freestanding-versus-supported comparison, what is the fitted delta_sl in nm and in sign?
