## Restatement (in my own words, before attacking)

When a popular open-weight instruct model lands on the Hugging Face hub, the big weight files normally stop changing, but the small text files that decide how a conversation is turned into token IDs and when generation stops — `tokenizer*`, the chat template, `generation_config.json`, `special_tokens_map.json`, `added_tokens.json` — keep getting edited. Two empirical questions follow. (a) Prevalence: across the N most-downloaded such repositories, how many received at least one post-weight-freeze commit to those files that actually changes behaviour, where "actually changes behaviour" is decided by rendering a fixed prompt set under both revisions and comparing token-ID sequences and stop/EOS configuration, not by eyeballing a diff? (b) Magnitude: with the weight bytes identical and harness, runtime, seeds and decoding pinned, how big is the earliest-vs-latest paired score-delta distribution on a suite split into format-sensitive and format-robust tasks, measured against (i) a same-revision repeat-run noise floor obtained in the same study and (ii) the sizes of improvements that model-comparison papers routinely present as evidence?

Underneath: does a bare model name identify the system a benchmark number refers to, or does it only identify a repository whose behaviour-determining configuration moved after publication?

### Pages opened (4 fetches)

- https://unsloth.ai/blog/phi4 — no isolated tokenizer/template/EOS before-after scores; fixes bundled with an architecture port.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11694 ("The Wording Effect") — varies *problem phrasing* (BenchDrift: linguistic/referential/pragmatic/structural), GSM8K / MMLU / MATH-Hard, 8 models; never varies model artifacts, never mentions repository commits. **Not a duplicate.**
- https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08765 ("Benchmark Scores Are Pipeline-Dependent") — **holds model weights fixed and varies prompt template / chat formatting, decoding, max-new-tokens and stop sequences, generation-config defaults inherited from the backend, extraction/scoring, denominator and aggregation policy.** Single-factor effects include a `"\n"` stop sequence firing inside Qwen3.6's reasoning worth **85.9 pp**, decoding drift 40 pp, logprob-vs-generative scoring 40.9 pp; whole-pipeline standardisation moves benchmark means by +1.3 to +32.9 and shifts 9 of 10 models by ≥3 ranks on at least one benchmark. It **does not** vary tokenizer configs, **does not** vary repository revisions (it records resolved HF commit hashes for reproducibility only), **does not** estimate repeat-run variability (explicitly declines; greedy decoding, seed 42, question-level bootstrap only), and **never** compares its effects to margins claimed in published papers.
- https://tairc.substack.com/p/version-drift-why-yesterdays-evaluation — argues "a model name is not a fixed identity" and recommends an identity record plus retest triggers, but never mentions tokenizer/template/generation-config artifacts, reports no numbers (its only empirical cite is Chen–Zaharia–Zou on GPT-3.5/4 March-vs-June), and asks for no census.

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional** — nothing is Unresolved (so not Rejected), but five of eight attacks land Partial and novelty no longer holds at Medium-with-confidence: a single new-vocabulary sweep surfaced arXiv:2609.08765, a same-season own-field paper that already does the fixed-weights / varying-stop-and-template measurement, which is precisely the Gate 2 failure signature the ledger warns about. Promotion to Validated would require: (i) re-running Gate 2 with 2609.08765 and arXiv:2405.14782 as nearest priors and re-stating the delta against them; (ii) redefining weight-freeze and byte-identity on tensor-value hashes rather than file SHAs; (iii) a specified sampling frame for the claimed-margin distribution; (iv) a rewritten reason-for-silence that explains the perturbation-set provenance gap rather than claiming an unowned join.

**Single strongest killing point:** arXiv:2609.08765 "Benchmark Scores Are Pipeline-Dependent" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08765) holds model weights fixed and varies chat formatting, stop sequences and generation-config defaults, reporting a single stop-sequence setting worth 85.9 pp and whole-pipeline mean shifts up to +32.9 with 9/10 models moving ≥3 ranks — so the candidate's headline claim ("config-only changes can swamp the margins papers treat as evidence, with weights identical") is already in print in the candidate's own field; what remains un-asked is only the prevalence census and the provenance of the perturbation set, a narrower and less consequential question than the record argues for.
