## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (canonical, as submitted): For shipped sub-quadratic language models whose recurrent state is a fixed-size tensor, how many bits about the processed prefix are recoverable from that state, and what fraction of its nominal capacity (state dimension × numeric precision) does the model actually realize?

Alternative formulations:
- Do state-space and linear-attention language models use their finite state efficiently, or do they realize only a small fraction of its nominal information capacity — and is that fraction invariant across model families?
- What is the measured prefix-information content of a Mamba-style recurrent state as a function of prefix length, and how does it scale with the state dimension within one family?

Motivating gap: the transformer-versus-sub-quadratic argument is conducted entirely in terms of a finite recurrent state, and every bound I opened states that state size limits what can be recalled without any realized figure to compare against: Arora et al.'s Theorem 3.1 ("Any recurrent model depending causally on input u ∈ {0, 1}^{N×d} requires Ω(N)-bits in state size to solve MQAR") and Jelassi et al.'s introduction ("GSSMs cannot accurately copy strings with more bits than the size of the latent state") both treat state size AS bits, which is the identification the candidate proposes to measure rather than assume. The instrument that makes the state itself measurable — implementations exposing a readable and writable complete fixed-size state at 7B and above — is about two years old.

## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

What would count as an answer: for at least three models across at least two families, recoverable-bits curves versus prefix length obtained from a declared decoder family applied to the exposed state, reported against the nominal bound, with the utilization fraction, its confidence interval, and its scaling in state dimension within one family. A family-invariant fraction answers one way; a fraction that rises with state dimension or with training answers the other.

**One reformulation taken (the permitted one), for well-posedness only.** The submitted wording leaves three pre-declared sets unenumerated — the decoder family, the prefix family and the state boundary — which is the defect the LEDGER records for an un-enumerated pre-declared list. Both versions recorded; the canonical question above is unchanged for the purposes of Gate 2.

Reformulated wording: *For each of (i) a Mamba-2-class pure-recurrent release, (ii) Falcon-Mamba, and (iii) a hybrid (Zamba2 or Nemotron-H), run the model on prefixes of length L ∈ {16, 64, 256, 1024, 4096} drawn i.i.d. uniform over the model's own vocabulary (so prefix entropy is L·log2|V| bits by construction); harvest the complete exposed carry as enumerated in the implementation (per layer: `ssm_state` AND `conv_state`; for the hybrid, with the attention KV path declared either ablated or counted); train a declared decoder family — (D1) a linear readout per token position, (D2) a fixed-width MLP readout, (D3) a state-initialised autoregressive decoder of the same architecture — to recover the prefix tokens from the state alone, and report recovered bits (L·log2|V| minus the decoder's per-position cross-entropy summed over positions, in bits) divided by nominal carried bits (Σ_layers numel(ssm_state)+numel(conv_state), times the stored dtype's bit width), with a bootstrap interval, at each L and for each state dimension within the family.*

**What the repair itself presupposes, tested as hard as the clause it replaces:**
- R1. That the hybrid's denominator can be declared without making the question about a convention. **Partly unmet.** From instance 3 above, the hybrid's attention carry grows with L, so "fraction of a fixed nominal capacity" is only well-defined for the recurrent part; the honest frame restricts the headline estimand to the recurrent-only carry with the attention path ablated, and reports the hybrid's total-carry number separately as a descriptive figure. This is why well-posedness scores 2, not 3.
- R2. That a decoder trained to invert the state is feasible at ≥7B. Established at 130M–1.4B only (Hossain et al. trained a per-length state-initialised decoder on 282M tokens); nothing I opened does it at 7B. Flagged as a resource presupposition, not a fact.
- R3. That the three-member decoder family's best member is a *useful* lower bound (non-vacuous: materially above zero and below nominal). Unestablished in either direction by anything opened; it is the measurement's own risk and is why the headline must be reported as a lower bound on utilization.
- R4. That uniform-random prefixes do not collapse the measurement by being wholly off-distribution. Unestablished; the reformulation therefore keeps a natural-text arm as a secondary curve whose entropy is not known by construction and is reported as a decoder-vs-decoder comparison only, never as a bits ratio.

## Gate 3 — Significance

**Consequence (argued).** The sub-quadratic programme's central claim and its central criticism are the same sentence — a fixed-size state must eventually lose the prefix — and both sides argue it with the state's SIZE standing in for its CONTENT. Arora's Theorem 3.1 needs Ω(N) bits "in state size"; Jelassi's introduction says GSSMs cannot copy strings "with more bits than the size of the latent state"; Chen's fits put a token length against a count of state elements; Arora's Figures 2 and 3 put accuracy against state bytes. If the realized fraction turns out to be small — say a few percent — then every one of those arguments is pointed at the wrong object: the binding constraint is the learned write/read code, not the tensor, and the productive architectural move is a better state code (gating, delta rules, an explicit state-reconstruction objective, state distillation) rather than a bigger state, which is the expensive move everyone currently makes. If the fraction is near 1, the opposite: the wall is information-theoretic, state growth is the only lever, and hybrid attention retention is vindicated as necessary rather than transitional. If the fraction is family-invariant, it is evidence of a common coding regime that training recipes do not escape; if it rises with state dimension or with training tokens, utilization becomes a scaling-law target with an a priori ceiling — a rare thing in this literature, because the denominator is known exactly rather than fitted.

**Audience.** (i) Architecture groups shipping recurrent and hybrid LMs — the teams behind Mamba-2-class releases, Falcon-Mamba, Jamba, Nemotron-H, Zamba2 — who currently choose `d_state`, `headdim` and layer mix against accuracy-per-byte curves and would gain a realized-bits axis that says whether a given increase buys capacity or buys slack. (ii) KV-cache-compression and long-context systems researchers, who compare a growing cache against a fixed state in bytes and have no realized-information basis for the comparison; a utilization figure converts their bytes axis into a bits axis. (iii) Interpretability researchers probing recurrent states — Hossain et al. are exactly this community, and they would get a normalisation for the reconstruction scores they already compute. (iv) Quantization practitioners: if utilization is a few percent, storing the state in 4 bits should cost nothing measurable, which is a directly testable prediction with deployment value.

**Fecundity.** Is utilization improvable by an explicit state-reconstruction auxiliary objective, and does improving it transfer to long-context benchmarks? Does utilization predict long-context performance better than state bytes? What is the gap between a decoder lower bound and a tighter two-sided estimate of recoverable information (a sandwich problem, by analogy to variational-bound tightening elsewhere)? Does the convolution state carry a disproportionate share of recoverable bits relative to its size — a question the code read makes askable and nobody has posed? In a hybrid, how do recurrent bits and attention-cache bits trade at fixed total carry? Does utilization differ between a uniform-random prefix and natural text, and by how much — i.e. is the state's code distribution-matched?

**Anchoring.** Directly on three opened sources' own unmeasured premises: Arora's Ω(N)-bits requirement, Jelassi's bits-equals-state-size identification, and Chen's exponential T_recall(N_S) fit, none of which has a realized figure to be compared with. Also on the reservoir-computing total-capacity framework, where a realized-fraction-of-nominal measurement is standard practice for untrained dynamical systems — the transplant direction is physics/neuromorphic → AI architecture, and the primary source for it is Unverified here (query 6 returned only citing works).

**Reason for silence (candidate mechanism, three parts).** (1) **Instrument age.** A complete, readable and writable fixed-size state at ≥7B is new: the implementations I opened expose it as a first-class object (`allocate_inference_cache`, `get_recurrent_state`, `update_recurrent_state`), and that API, at that scale, is roughly two years old; before it, probing a recurrent state meant small LSTMs, where nobody would ask about a shipped model's deployment ceiling. (2) **A disciplinary split that puts the ratio in nobody's hands.** The numerator and the denominator are owned by different communities: the nominal bound belongs to theorists working in circuit complexity and communication-complexity lower bounds (Merrill et al.; Arora's Theorem 3.1; Jelassi's argument), who state bounds and do not measure; the measurement belongs to probing and interpretability, whose venue currency is accuracy, token-level loss and content-selectivity (Hossain et al. built the instrument and reported ROUGE and omission rates); and the capacity-fraction methodology itself lives in reservoir computing, in physics and neuromorphic venues that this literature does not cite. Each side has half the fraction. (3) **An incentive gap.** A utilization figure well below 1 is nobody's benchmark win — it is a criticism that lands on every release at once, including the measurer's, and the publishable surrogate (accuracy against state bytes) is cheaper to produce and easier to present as progress. That is a mechanism, not "no one thought of it": the people positioned to measure it are the people whose results it would relativise.
