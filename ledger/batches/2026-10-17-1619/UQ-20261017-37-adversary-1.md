# UQ-20261017-37 — Adversary 1 log

## Restatement (my own words)
Take cluster-randomised and stepped-wedge trials run inside health systems whose intervention works through clinicians (training, decision support, ordering/prescribing nudges). (a) Using provider identifiers (rosters, payer/EHR provider IDs) rather than what the paper says, in how many clusters is there at least one clinician who, in the same calendar window, is also seeing patients in a cluster that is in the other treatment state, and what share of all patient contacts do such clinicians deliver? (b) For trials where those identifiers can be obtained, re-fit the primary analysis with a crossed clinician random effect (or an interference-aware estimator) and report how far the point estimate and CI move from the published ones.

In plain terms: how often do clinicians straddle the arms of a cluster trial, and does it matter to the answer? That plain-terms version is the one I attacked, because the candidate's specificity (provider IDs, cluster/contact denominators, within-trial re-analysis) is an instrument, and a Disguise attack has to ask whether the underlying question has already been asked under another name.

## Recommended verdict
**Rejected** (Disguise Unresolved; Reason-for-silence Unresolved). Key kill: the question is an asked-but-unanswered one wearing a new instrument — Keogh-Brown et al. (2019) re-asked both limbs; the candidate's residue is provider-identifier measurement plus a sensitivity re-fit, i.e. a protocol, not a question.
