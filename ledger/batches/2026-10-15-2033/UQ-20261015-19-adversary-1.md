## Restatement (my words, not the generator's)

Take the biobanks that hold both a person's genome and the lab results their doctors actually ordered. For a substance measured by an antibody-based test, ask: does the person's own coding variation in the gene that makes that substance change the number the lab reports? Then try to tell apart two reasons it might — the test's antibody failing to grab an altered protein (a measurement defect) versus the altered protein genuinely being present in different amounts or behaving differently (real biology) — using two tells: the effect differing between analyzers measuring the same thing, and the effect shrinking into a pre-declared "no real difference" band when the same people are measured by a method that does not depend on antibody recognition. Finally, count how often this pushes a person across a line that triggers a diagnosis or treatment, per 100,000 tests, and whether carriers' subsequent care differs. If too few analytes have usable analyzer metadata, report that census instead.
