## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Everybody agrees the *resting* temperature of a healthy person has drifted down over the last century or so (Protsiv's ~0.03 °C per birth decade). Nobody has checked what happened to the *height of the fever itself*. So: take people with a confirmed infection, grab the hottest temperature recorded before anyone gave them paracetamol, and subtract the resting temperature of non-infected people measured on the same wards with the same thermometers at the same hour. Does that gap shrink year on year? If it does not, the thermometer's zero moved and the patient did not — move the fever cut-off and you are done. If it does shrink, the body is mounting a smaller fever for the same infection, and moving the cut-off buys you nothing, because the signal itself is smaller rather than merely offset.

Two things are worth saying about the restatement. First, the thing being claimed as new is not "has temperature fallen" but a *subtraction of two slopes* — a bookkeeping operation on an already-published trend. Second, "amplitude of the febrile response" is doing a lot of silent work: it only means something if fever is an additive displacement of a resting level, which is precisely what thermal physiology has argued about for fifty years.
