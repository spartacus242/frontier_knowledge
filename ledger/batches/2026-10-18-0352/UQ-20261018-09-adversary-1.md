## Restatement (my words)

For the five small bodies where a spacecraft has physically touched or struck the surface (Itokawa, 67P, Ryugu, Bennu, Dimorphos): if you use only publications dated before each encounter and run the standard remote-sensing estimators (thermal inertia -> grain size -> cohesion; spin-limit cohesion; radar polarisation ratio; boulder size-frequency; slope stability), what interval would each have honestly issued for near-surface cohesion (and for penetration resistance at a stated depth)? Did the post-encounter constraint — graded as a direct measurement, a one-sided bound, or a model-inverted parameter — fall inside that interval, and in which direction was the miss? And is the interval tighter than the strength distinctions that mission requirement documents actually draw, those thresholds being quoted rather than assumed?

## Evidence I will lean on (verbatim or near-verbatim, from pages I opened)

Groussin et al. 2019 (F1), 67P mechanical strength: remote-sensing values from OSIRIS imaging — "tensile strength 3-15 Pa and compressive strength 30-150 Pa at 5-30 m scales" (Groussin 2015), cohesion 1-2 Pa at tens-of-metre scales (Vincent 2017), overhang minimum tensile strengths 0.02-1.02 Pa (Attree 2018a). In-situ Philae values — CASSE Hertzian compressive strength 3.5-12 kPa; Biele 2015 bounce excavation uniaxial compressive strength "unlikely to exceed about 10 kPa"; MUPUS penetration failure "at least 2 MPa"; Knapmeyer 2018 via assumed E/sigma_c and sigma_c/sigma_T ratios gives compressive 18 kPa to 9.8 MPa. The review states strength is "a scale dependent parameter", that remote estimates rely on model assumptions, that for Deep Impact the experiment is "compatible with any shear strength in the range 0 - 12 kPa" (Holsapple & Housen 2007), and that applying the MUPUS result to derive material properties is "very challenging" because laboratory data are scale dependent. The review does NOT frame any of this as a prediction audit and does not quantify a pre-landing-vs-Philae error.

My own arithmetic on those numbers (labelled as mine, not the review's): remote compressive 30-150 Pa against in-situ CASSE 3.5-12 kPa is a gap of 1.4 to 2.6 decades (log10(3500/150) = 1.37; log10(12000/30) = 2.60); against MUPUS >= 2 MPa it is >= 4.1 decades (log10(2e6/150) = 4.12). The remote values are at 5-30 m scale and the in-situ at cm scale, so the review attributes the gap to scale dependence rather than estimator error; the gap therefore depends entirely on which scale one declares commensurable.

Raducan 2024 abstract (F2): Dimorphos cohesion "less than a few pascals", "similar to Ryugu and Bennu"; model-dependent. Record's prior 4 (Rivkin 2024): remote spin/slope estimator gives Dimorphos surface cohesion "<0.9 Pa", Didymos "<=1 Pa".
