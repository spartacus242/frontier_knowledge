Domain(s): Computer science — programming-language runtimes and memory management (method transplanted from demographic age–period–cohort analysis)

Question (canonical): For a managed-runtime program traced to exact object death times on a single declared clock (bytes allocated), does the death hazard of a heap object depend only on its age, or do the identifiable curvatures of the death-time (period) and allocation-epoch (cohort) axes differ from zero — reporting (i) second-difference curvature sequences for the period and cohort axes with intervals, (ii) a pre-declared likelihood-ratio test of the age-only submodel, and (iii) nested deviance reductions under ALL orderings of the three axes, since under p = c + a no unique split exists? (This is V2, the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation, and the version scored; V1 asked in its third clause for a single deviance "split" that the exact dependence it invokes makes non-identifiable.)

Alternative formulations:
- Is the age-specific mortality of heap objects stationary in program progress, or does the rate at which objects of a given age die change with when in the run they are dying and with when they were allocated?
- Lay heap-object deaths on a Lexis surface with an at-risk denominator: do the off-age axes carry estimable curvature beyond age, and how much deviance?

Motivating gap: The generational hypothesis is an age-only statement about mortality. The field has defined the hazard, measured its age marginal, and printed the raw two-way death tabulation — but no at-risk-denominated rate off the age axis, no period or cohort curvature, no deviance decomposition, and no statement of the identification obstruction (re-confirmed on all three priors at full text by verification 08-b, which adds that prior 2 does assert the DIRECTION in print — "The survival rate is certainly not uniform during a program run," — of the nursery survival rate, qualitatively, with no rate off the age axis, no model and no number; the absence is over opened documents only and is bounded under "how this differs"). Dolan (ISMM 2025, abstract) states that of the generational hypothesis "it is not obvious how it can be measured as a property of a program (independent of a particular GC strategy)" and proposes lifetime dispersion (Gini) instead — a move away from a hazard framing. Correction carried from adversary 3: the record's claim that the field "has not named the cohort axis" is struck — Yak (OSDI 2016) names it as the "epochal hypothesis".

Depth (object test; deletion test): Object — the answer would be a fact about the heap objects of running programs and their mortality (a running system behaving; on the pass list). Deletion — if every paper, database and protocol vanished tonight while runtimes and released software stayed as they are, running programs and their heaps would still fix the hazard surface. Presuppositions:
1. Exact object death times are obtainable on a bytes-allocated clock. ESTABLISHED, sourced at full text by two adversaries on Merlin (TOPLAS 2005/2006): "A perfect trace ... includes the birth and death time of all objects, measured in bytes allocated"; death time is "the time interval immediately following the one when it was last reachable". Cost 70–300x tracing slowdown; "exact" means exact to the allocation at which the clock ticks. LOAD-BEARING for the estimand, per verification 08-a (the checker's own reasoning, no source): with deaths observed only at GC boundaries p = c + a still holds for the true and for any imputed pair, but the period axis is coarsened to a collector-defined grid (roughly regular in bytes for a fixed-size nursery, irregular for major collections), exposure within an interval is overcounted, the unequal-interval problem of presupposition 3 is imported unless all three axes are coarsened alike, and the estimand becomes collector-dependent — the period axis is then the collector's, not the program's. Prior 1's surface is of that kind: Jones & Ryder "force full collections at 64 KB intervals" and "Hence object allocation times are accurate, but death times are only accurate to this granularity." (08-b, full text).
2. p = c + a exactly on one clock. True by arithmetic, and sourced in that both birth and death are measured in bytes allocated.
3. Under that exact dependence the identifiable quantities are the second differences of each axis PLUS a linear plane (level and two slope combinations); what is not identifiable is the attribution of the linear trend to any one axis. CONFIRMED IN SUBSTANCE by verification 08-a — one reader, one retrieval tool (the WebFetch summariser), three documents on three hosts — after being UNVERIFIED by the record and all three adversaries (title level arXiv:2112.08299, 2312.09698; abstract level, adversary 1, "the structural link identification problem"). Primary opened at FULL TEXT: Kuang, Nielsen & Nielsen, "Identification of the age-period-cohort model and the extended chain ladder model", Nuffield College working paper dated "20 November 2007" (08-a identifies it as the Biometrika 95 (2008) 979–986 paper; the working paper names neither series nor journal): "It has long been appreciated that this parametrization is not identified."; "linear trends in αi, βj, γi+j−1 can be added without changing the value of µij" (attributed there to Carstensen 2007); Theorem 1's canonical parameter ξ — the three second-difference sequences plus three initial points — is a "maximal invariant function of θ under g."; "The second differences ∆²αi, ∆²βj, ∆²γi+j−1 do however, have a unique interpretation."
