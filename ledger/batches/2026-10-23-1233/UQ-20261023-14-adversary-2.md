## Restatement (my own words, before engaging the record's framing)

Pick time windows during which GNSS was demonstrably misbehaving — jammed, spoofed, or broadcasting bad parameters — where the proof that it misbehaved comes from somewhere other than the clocks you are studying (regulator interference bulletins, NOTAMs, aviation-derived interference maps, NAGUs, constellation/ground-segment anomaly notices). For each window, take the deployed GNSS-disciplined clocks and timing receivers that are logged finely enough (a sample at least every 15 minutes, 30 days around the window) and whose logs say which model or holdover-oscillator class the unit is, and whose job is governed by a document that states a numeric time-error limit. Two numbers come out: (1) how big the time error actually handed to the dependent application got, as a distribution per model and per window, and (2) what share of units blew past the numeric limit its own governing document states, with a binomial interval and with an honest statement of how much of the intended denominator was lost to missing logs.

In one line: **not "how badly can a receiver be made to fail on a bench", but "how badly did the fleet actually fail in the field, by model, against the limit its standard prints".**

## Verdict

**Recommended verdict: Provisional.** Nothing Unresolved on any attack, so no rejection ground; but Validated is blocked three ways — novelty is Medium with a newly surfaced close shape-neighbour (Cao & Veitch 2018) that I **could not read past a paraphrased abstract**, well-posedness is 2/3 on the record's own scoring with CL1(c)/CL3/CL4 unverified, and attacks 2, 4, 5, 7 and 8 are Partial rather than Resolved.

**Promotion conditions, in the order I would spend budget on them:** (1) read Cao & Veitch 2018 in full at a route that renders — IEEE Xplore via DOI 10.1109/INFOCOM.2018.8486286, or an author copy — and check its grading thresholds, its numerator class and its denominator against the candidate's, in both directions; (2) resolve the impacts.ucar.edu item "The effects of the January 2016 UTC offset anomaly on GPS control…" to a DOI and open it (returned twice in my own sweep, opened neither time; by title the journal sibling of the NIST deck and the single likeliest document to print per-receiver values for the candidate's own epoch class); (3) open one limit-bearing document at clause grain to settle CL1(c)/CL3/CL4.

**What KIND of document could settle the attack I could not close** (attack 8's second half — whether jamming epochs are externally datable at a single site's grain), stated before naming one: it must cover *ground-level fixed sites* rather than aircraft, must *vary or perturb* the inference from aviation- or regulator-derived exposure to a site's own interference environment, and must *print* a spatial validity statement or a ground-truth comparison. An ADS-B-derived interference product's own methodology/FAQ page, or a regulator's bulletin stating the geographic resolution of its interference reports, is that kind. **I did not open either and I am naming a kind, not a source — this is a lead by kind, which is a guess until someone opens an instance.** I hand that kind to the next reader in exactly those words.

**Single strongest killing point (the leg my verdict would stand on if the others fell):** the epoch classes trade off against each other — the anomaly epochs whose dating is cleanest deliver a **common-mode constant** (13 µs, identical across all affected units in all three documents that print it), so limb 1's cross-model distribution is near-degenerate there, while the model-dependent spread lives in the holdover/rejection branch and in jamming epochs, for which no source I opened dates exposure at a single site's grain. It is **Partial, not a kill**, because the holdover branch is demonstrably non-degenerate in those same documents (~1 in 8 of NIST's ~80 receivers unaffected, with no explanation; "Affected all SSU 2000 units" against P1's "No adverse impact") and because the second half of the argument is my own reasoning with the grounding search unrun.
