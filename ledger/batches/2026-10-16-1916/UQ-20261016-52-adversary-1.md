## Restatement (my words, not the record's)

Researchers compare a released "chat" model against its released "base" sibling and call the difference "what post-training did". The candidate asks: is the released base already partly behaving like a chat model (refusing harmful requests, liking its sibling's chat template, obeying zero-shot instructions, calling itself an assistant)? If so, how much of the base-to-chat gap is already closed in the base, does that fraction grow with release date and with the releaser's admitted mid-training/annealing on instruction-like data, does it track the share of instruction-formatted text in the (open) pretraining corpus, and when you swap the released base for a pre-anneal or pre-2023 base, do published "post-training adds X" numbers move outside their own error bars?

## Recommended verdict

Provisional at best: no attack Unresolved, but Disguise, Presupposition, Reason for silence and Dissolution are all Partial, and Gate 2 novelty should be re-graded downward because two closer priors (arXiv:2503.20783, arXiv:2506.20512) were missed — the candidate's defensible novelty is confined to the safety/persona/dated-frame/corpus-share extension of an argument already made and acted on in the RL-for-reasoning literature.

Single strongest killing point: Liu et al. 2025 ("Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training", arXiv:2503.20783) already (i) measures a limb-(i) behaviour (zero-shot answering without template) across base families, (ii) attributes it to QA-formatted pretraining and names a "nearly pure base model" as the cleaner control, and (iii) re-estimates post-training (RL) gains on that cleaner control and concludes the published gains are overstated — so the record's claim that the control-validity framing "returns nothing in ML's own words" and its reason-for-silence mechanism (1) are both false for the capability endpoint; what remains un-asked is only the safety-side extension.

What would promote it: re-run Gate 2 against 2503.20783 and 2506.20512 and narrow the canonical question to the refusal/persona/template limbs with the dated frame and corpus-share test; verify from the OLMo 2 full report whether Dolmino includes instruction-tuning data and whether stage-1 checkpoints are released (which would also fix presupposition 1 and mechanism 2).
