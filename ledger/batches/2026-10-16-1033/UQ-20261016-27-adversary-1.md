## 1. Restatement in my own words (not the generator's framing)

Take one vaccine product and one population in which each administered dose can be traced to the
factory batch it came from, and in which each recipient's later infection or severe-disease outcome
is independently ascertained. Throw away the unvaccinated entirely. Among the vaccinated only, fit
a model of outcome risk with a random intercept for batch, nested inside the shipment consignment
the batch travelled in, holding constant when the person was vaccinated, how long ago, which dose,
where they live and who they are. Ask one thing: is the spread of those batch intercepts bigger
than chance, and if not, how small can the data prove the spread to be?

The trick the candidate rests on is that you never need a per-batch control group: if everyone's
comparison group is the same unvaccinated population, that term is a constant across batches, so
the dispersion of batch intercepts in the vaccinated stratum alone carries the dispersion of
protection across batches. The estimand is a dispersion, not an accusation about any batch, and the
candidate pre-commits to counting "we could not tell" as a result about the registries rather than
as a null about batches.

What is genuinely new here is not the topic (do batches differ?) but the object: a variance
component on a clinical endpoint over the whole distributed batch population, rather than an
equivalence test on a surrogate over a handful of batches.

## 3. What the opened pages actually say

**(a) Seven-lot phase IV consistency trial (PMC10796506).** "Seven commercial-scale lots from three
workshops: one lot from workshop 1 and three each from workshops 2 and 3"; described as "a
randomized, double-blind, controlled, phase IV clinical trial"; primary endpoint neutralising
antibody GMT at day 28, no efficacy against infection reported; criterion "equivalence is considered
to be fulfilled when the 95% confidence interval (CI) for the inter-group GMT ratio were between
0.67 and 1.5"; lots entered as **fixed** effects ("vaccines groups (lots or workshops) were the
fixed effects"); per-lot GMTs ranged 118.78 to 134.97; **no between-lot variance component or SD
across lots is reported**.
*My own arithmetic, flagged as mine:* 134.97/118.78 = 1.136, i.e. a 13.6% spread of point estimates
across 7 lots. I deliberately do **not** convert this to a between-lot SD: each lot GMT carries its
own sampling error, which inflates an observed range, and the paper gives me no per-lot interval to
carry through.

**(b) Dose-fractionation systematic review (PMC9595080).** The authors describe "the non-linear
dose-response relationship of vaccination" and that "vaccine-induced nAbs varied substantially
across dose fractions, number of dosages, and vaccine platforms." Two half-doses of **mRNA** gave
"2.6 (95% CI, 2.1 to 3.3...)-fold of the nAbs"; two half-doses of **inactivated** vaccine gave
"0.28 (95% IC 0.20 to 0.37)-fold of nAbs", with projected efficacy "61% (95% CI, 51% to 70%)"
against symptomatic infection. Crucially: "we were not able to validate predictions for fractional
doses due to lack of data", and all efficacy figures are model projections from a correlate, not
measurements. (Figures above are the authors' stated estimates with their stated intervals; I quote
them as projections, not as measured efficacy.)

**(c) MMWR 1984 spot-check (cited prior #5).** Real and correctly located: MMWR June 22 1984,
33(24):349, "Measles Outbreak among Vaccinated High School Students -- Illinois." The faulty-lot
sentence is verbatim as the record has it: "This finding suggested a cohort effect, such as a faulty
vaccine lot or particular provider, but none was found." **But** the record twice puts in quotation
marks "detailed lot information was not available from the providers"; the source actually reads
"Although detailed vaccine information was not available from providers of vaccine". The record
narrows *vaccine* to *lot* inside quotation marks. Substance survives; the verbatim quote does not.
Other priors spot-checked indirectly: query 9 confirms the lot-consistency trial genre and
NCT-document style of prior #2; query 2 confirms the German/PEI regulatory setting of priors #3–#4.
I did not independently open PMID 29580639 or the MDPI record within budget.

Cancelling a common unvaccinated reference in a multiplicative model leaves the between-lot SD of log *risk* among the vaccinated, i.e. the SD of log RR = SD of log(1 − VE).
