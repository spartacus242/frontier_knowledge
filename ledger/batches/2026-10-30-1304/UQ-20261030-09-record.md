## Question (canonical, as submitted)
In human tumours resected after neoadjuvant chemotherapy with a drug that intratumoural bacteria can inactivate, are the surviving viable tumour cells spatially co-located with bacteria-colonised microniches at the 10–100 µm scale, and does local bacterial load grade the local probability of tumour-cell survival?

## Question (reformulated at Gate 1 — the one permitted reformulation; both versions recorded)
In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma resected after a **gemcitabine-containing** neoadjuvant regimen, is the per-microregion probability that a tumour cell is residual-and-viable graded by local bacterial occupancy at the 10–100 µm scale, net of necrosis fraction, hypoxia marker and distance to nearest perfused vessel?
Reason for the repair: the submitted clause "a drug that intratumoural bacteria can inactivate" pre-declares a drug set and enumerates none — the exact well-posedness defect LEDGER.md records from 2026-10-23. See "Pre-declared set" below.
**What the repair itself presupposes** (tested as hard as the clause it replaced): (i) that post-neoadjuvant *gemcitabine-regimen* PDAC resections with tissue suitable for spatial bacterial profiling exist in ≥2 independent series — established for ONE series from a source I read in full (May et al. 2025: 3 FFPE patients on neoadjuvant gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel plus 18 frozen-tissue patients after gemcitabine-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy, 9 long-term and 9 short-term survivors), NOT established for a second (the ESMO 1511P abstract that would speak to it was returned by my own search and NOT opened); (ii) that residual viable tumour is abundant enough per specimen for a distance-graded analysis — **this is my own domain inference, not a source finding**; no source I opened prints a residual-viable-tumour fraction for post-neoadjuvant PDAC, and the query that would have grounded it ("CAP tumour regression grade distribution post-neoadjuvant PDAC resections") was not run; (iii) a cost the repair incurs: contemporary PDAC neoadjuvant practice is substantially FOLFIRINOX rather than gemcitabine-based, so restricting the drug set shrinks the specimen pool — also my own inference, unsourced here.

## Alternative formulations
1. Do bacteria inside a human tumour create drug-free sanctuaries in which tumour cells live through chemotherapy?
2. At micrometre resolution in post-treatment human specimens, what is the distance-resolved cross-correlation between bacterial niche occupancy and residual viable tumour cells, net of necrosis, hypoxia and vessel distance?

## Motivating gap
Multiplex placement of bacteria and host cells in the same human tumour section at cell-level resolution now exists and has been applied in human PDAC and in OSCC/CRC. Bacterial inactivation of gemcitabine is an established in-vitro/mouse mechanism. The spatial prediction that follows — residual disease should sit where the bacteria are — is not tested in any post-treatment human series I could open, although treatment-response grading of post-neoadjuvant specimens is routine pathology. Two of the four papers I read in full do the spatial bacteria/host measurement and neither carries a treatment axis: in both, the served text does not state whether the resected tumours were treatment-naive or pre-treated (that is a statement about my read of those texts, not a quotation from them).

## Presuppositions (each marked established / contested, with the read level behind the mark)
1. **Bacteria are present in a substantial share of the relevant human tumours AT THE TIME OF SURGERY, i.e. after chemotherapy and any peri-procedural antibiotics.** CONTESTED, and now contested *with a measurement against it*, not merely unmeasured. May et al. 2025 (read FULL TEXT) assayed 69 samples including 21 post-neoadjuvant gemcitabine-regimen patients and report bacterial DNA "generally low, comparable to those seen in negative controls" (Abstract), concluding that the findings "point against, but do not exclude" a gammaproteobacterial role in gemcitabine response (Discussion). **Regime of that result, both directions of overlap:** numerator = bulk 16S-qPCR/sequencing signal from DNA extracted at discrete sampling locations; the paper itself states "microbial extractions were performed from discrete locations within the tumor"; denominator = 69 tissue samples (30 FFPE / 39 frozen) from ~30 patients at one centre; no spatial localisation anywhere in it. What that class MISSES that the candidate includes: focal, micrometre-scale occupancy, which a low bulk mean does not exclude — Geller et al. print a human-tissue density of about one bacterium per 146 human cells (95% CI 53–201), and a low mean with spatial clustering is the candidate's own premise. What it INCLUDES that the candidate excludes: signal attributable to contaminants, which the paper concedes ("We found a low microbial load that is partially attributable to contaminants") and does not quantify. **This two-directional argument is my own reasoning about regime, labelled as such; no source states it.** Net: presupposition 1 is the candidate's biggest factual risk and the entry is right to demand establishment on actual specimens first.
2. **Bacterially mediated drug inactivation reaches local concentrations that matter in human tissue.** CONTESTED — and this is now settled as *not established*, from a full-text read rather than from the intake's recollection. In Geller et al. 2017 no gemcitabine or dFdC/dFdU concentration is reported inside human tumour tissue; HPLC-MS/MS was run on conditioned medium and bacterial incubation medium, and the in-vivo work is a subcutaneous MC-26 syngeneic colon model in BALB/c mice, an *E. coli* Nissle 1917 colonisation model, and an implantable drug-delivery microdevice. Intratumoral drug levels are not reported even for the mice. PUBLISHED-IS-NOT-PERFORMED: the mechanism is **demonstrated in model systems and predicted for human tissue; the human-tissue concentration is not measured anywhere I opened.**
3. **Viable residual tumour cells can be distinguished from treatment-effect debris in the same section used for bacterial detection.** CONTESTED; not operationalised by the candidate (no marker rule stated) and not supplied by any source I opened.
4. **Contamination and ambient-bacteria artefacts can be controlled well enough to support a spatial claim.** CONTESTED; carried as the LABELLED PREREQUISITE limb (control sections, kitome/reagent controls, spatial permutation nulls), never as the finding. Relevant read: May et al. use extraction- and PCR-negative controls per batch and SCRuB decontamination but report neither the reads removed nor the contaminant fraction; Galeano Niño et al. validated probes and used a UMI ≥3 cut-off but, in the text served to me, describe no extraction or reagent blanks for the sequencing workflows.
5. **The association is not simply both features tracking necrosis, hypoxia or vessel distance.** Measured covariates in the design, not assumptions. Worth noting that no opened source supplies them alongside bacteria: Li et al. 2024 *exclude* necrosis from analysis ("Regions containing adipose tissue, necrotic lesions, and artefactual staining were excluded from analysis", STAR Methods), treat hypoxia only as a transcriptional gene set, and use vessel *presence* rather than any distance metric.
6. **Enough post-neoadjuvant resection specimens with suitable tissue exist in at least two independent series.** PARTIALLY ESTABLISHED, one series only (May et al. 2025, enumerated above). The second series is NOT enumerated; an unenumerated "such specimens exist" is not established here, and I name the unopened item that would settle it (ESMO 2024 abstract 1511P).

## What would count as an answer
In ≥2 independent series of post-neoadjuvant gemcitabine-regimen PDAC resections: per-section maps of bacterial occupancy and of viable tumour cells, reported as the odds of a residual viable tumour cell lying within versus beyond a pre-specified distance of a bacterial niche, with necrosis fraction, hypoxia marker and distance to nearest perfused vessel as measured covariates, and a null from label permutation preserving each section's spatial autocorrelation. A distance-graded excess surviving the covariates is positive and makes local bacterial inactivation a candidate mechanism of patchy residual disease; no excess is a clean negative removing a leading spatial explanation. The prerequisite limb reports the contamination/detection bound alongside; an effect smaller than that bound is reported as indeterminate rather than as either answer.

## Significance — consequence
Yes-answer: local bacterial drug inactivation becomes a candidate mechanism for the patchiness of residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and a co-intervention becomes testable — Geller et al.'s own discussion already says antibiotic combinations "merit additional exploration in the pre-clinical and clinical setting", but with no spatial target to aim at. No-answer: a leading spatial explanation is removed, and together with May et al.'s bulk-negative result it would largely close the gemcitabine-sanctuary hypothesis in human tissue, redirecting effort to cell-intrinsic quiescence and perfusion-limited penetration. An effect below the contamination bound is reported as indeterminate, which is a third, honest outcome the design pre-commits to.

## Significance — audience
Pancreatic and GI medical oncology (who choose and sequence neoadjuvant regimens and currently have no spatial account of residual disease); surgical pathology (who already grade treatment response on these exact sections, so the marginal cost is one added channel, not a new specimen stream); cancer-microbiome groups actively publishing on human tumour tissue (the Bullman, Straussman, Manji/Uhlemann and Vonderheide/Li groups are all represented among the four papers read, so the audience is real and currently working on the adjacent measurement). Note for the count-the-groups rule: the four papers are four independent groups, not one group citing itself.

## Significance — fecundity
Opens: whether the bacterial niche and the immune-exclusion niche are the same niche (Li et al. find bacteria where CD8 infiltration is higher, which predicts the OPPOSITE sign for survival than the sanctuary hypothesis — a genuine testable tension between two opened sources); whether lower Ki-67 near bacteria (Galeano Niño et al.) means bacterial niches are quiescence niches, giving a non-pharmacokinetic route to the same spatial prediction; whether the same distance-graded analysis applies to other drug/organism pairs once a second member of the drug set is established; and whether focal occupancy reconciles the bulk low-biomass findings with the spatial ones.

## Significance — anchoring
The patchiness of pathological response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy in PDAC; the unresolved tumour-microbiome low-biomass controversy (May et al.: "Efforts to replicate these studies have been difficult", and their findings "point against, but do not exclude"); drug-penetration-limited response and the old clostridial literature on bacterial preference for hypoxic/necrotic tumour regions, which makes the hypoxia/necrosis confounder the historically expected pattern rather than a nuisance invented for this design.

## Reason for silence
A disciplinary boundary that runs through a single glass slide, plus an instrument that arrived after the cohorts were built. Treatment-response grading is done by surgical pathologists on H&E; bacterial detection lives in microbiome laboratories working on bulk nucleic acid; the two communities do not share a section, and the bacterial channel is simply never in the slide the pathologist grades. Reinforcing this: the low-biomass controversy has made microbiome groups defensive about human-tissue claims, which pushes the field toward cleaner, treatment-naive specimens — and in both spatial papers I read in full, the served text does not state the specimens' treatment status at all, which is what a field that does not treat treatment as an axis looks like. "No one thought of it" is not invoked.
