Source method: presupposition inversion

Question (canonical) — V1, as intaken:
Is gene essentiality a property of the gene or of the rate at which its product is removed — for a matched gene panel assayed in the same cells by acute degron-mediated depletion and by CRISPR knockout, what fraction of essentiality calls disagree, and does the disagreement track protein half-life and the induction of compensatory transcription?

Question (canonical) — V2, the one reformulation permitted at Gate 1 (well-posedness only; V1 retained above):
For a matched panel of >= 100 genes in one human cell line, each assayed both by acute degron depletion and by CRISPR knockout, with **residual target protein measured in both arms at the fitness readout** and with a knockout arm that includes RNA-less alleles (promoter/whole-locus deletion) alongside PTC alleles: what is the discordance rate in pre-declared essentiality calls **conditional on matched residual-protein depth**, and after conditioning on measured depth, does the residual discordance load on (a) measured protein half-life, (b) measured mutant-mRNA level / transcriptional-adaptation signature, or neither?
Reason for the reformulation: in V1 the decisive coefficient (protein half-life) is not identifiable, because a long half-life also makes a degron harder to deplete — half-life and residual depletion depth are collinear by construction, and the two proteome-scale yeast studies read below attribute most of their discordance to depletion depth rather than to biology. V2 also separates kinetics from the mechanism El-Brolosy et al. actually demonstrated (mutant-mRNA decay, not slow protein loss) by adding an RNA-less knockout arm.

Alternative formulations:
- Does the standard essentiality call depend on removal kinetics, so that genes scored non-essential under gradual loss are essential under abrupt depletion, or the reverse?
- How much of a published essentialome is a statement about the dilution timescale of the perturbation rather than about the gene's function?

Motivating gap:
Inverts the presupposition behind every large essentiality map: that removing a gene and reading fitness days later measures the gene's requirement. In CRISPR knockout the protein disappears by dilution over several divisions; degron systems (AID/AID2, dTAG) remove protein in minutes to hours and are now library-scale. Two literatures say the timescale might matter — knockdown-versus-knockout discordance and transcriptional adaptation triggered by mutant mRNA degradation. The bookkeeping question that follows — what share of an essentialome is kinetics rather than function — looked unposed at intake (one screening query returned no study doing it). **Gate 2 overturned that**: the comparison has been run at proteome scale in *S. cerevisiae* and the fractions are published.

Presuppositions (status after Gate 1/2):
1. Degron and knockout perturbations can be made comparable in depth of depletion — CONTESTED, and now **partly falsified as an assumption**: both yeast degron-library papers name incomplete degradation as a leading explanation of their own discordance, and one reports a depletion-extent stratification of essential proteins. V2 handles this by measuring residual protein instead of assuming comparability.
2. Degron tagging does not itself perturb function or stability enough to confound — CONTESTED; the one systematic technology-benchmarking paper that would bear on this COULD NOT BE READ (see below), so this stays contested on no evidence either way.
3. Essentiality is operationalisable as a continuous fitness score on a common scale in both systems with a threshold declared in advance — supported in practice: the yeast work shows the threshold choice drives the answer (69% recovery at relative growth < 0.5 vs 4% novel calls at < 10%), which makes pre-declaration load-bearing rather than cosmetic.
4. Protein half-lives for the panel are available or measurable in the same cells under the same conditions — plausible (proteome half-life datasets exist in human cells), but see the collinearity problem in presupposition 1.
5. Compensation, if it occurs, is detectable at transcript or protein level within the assay window — CONTESTED, and mis-specified in V1: the demonstrated trigger of transcriptional adaptation is mutant-mRNA degradation, **not** slow loss of protein, so "compensation" is not a kinetic variable and must be measured separately from half-life.
6. Enough genes can be endogenously tagged at scale, and the asymmetry that viable knockout clones of essential genes cannot be made must be handled by design — stands; this is the real barrier in human cells and the reason the yeast libraries came first.
7. The question is not already settled by the existing knockdown-versus-knockout literature — FLAGGED at intake as the principal novelty risk. **The risk landed, but from a different direction**: not knockdown-vs-knockout, but genome-wide degron-vs-deletion in yeast.
8. Cell-autonomous fitness in culture is an acceptable endpoint (declared scope: non-clinical) — stands.

What would count as an answer:
A matched panel of >= 100 genes in one cell line, each inducibly degradable and separately knocked out, fitness endpoint and threshold declared before data collection, yielding (i) the discordance rate with CIs; (ii) its direction; (iii) a regression of discordance on measured protein half-life and on measured compensatory transcript/protein change, with the half-life coefficient as the decisive quantity — in V2, estimated after conditioning on measured residual-protein depth, which is what makes the coefficient interpretable; and (iv) a projection of how many calls in a public essentiality map the measured rate would reclassify. Small, half-life-independent discordance → essentiality is a gene property; large discordance predicted by half-life → removal kinetics becomes a declared parameter of every essentiality map.

Significance — consequence:
If a large, half-life-predicted discordance survives conditioning on depletion depth, removal kinetics becomes a declared parameter of essentiality maps, and a share of DepMap-style calls would carry a kinetics caveat; target-validation practice would have to state which kinetic regime a dependency was established in. If discordance is small or is fully explained by residual protein, the opposite and more useful conclusion follows: the yeast 31-38% figure is a technology artefact of incomplete degradation rather than a fact about essentiality, and the published fractions should not be read as "essentiality depends on how fast you remove the protein". Both outcomes are informative, which is the main thing V2 buys over V1.

Significance — audience:
(a) Human functional-genomics screen groups and DepMap-type map builders, who would have to declare a kinetic regime and possibly re-annotate borderline calls; (b) chemical-biology/pharma target-validation groups choosing between degrader and knockout evidence for a target, who currently treat the two as interchangeable support; (c) the yeast degron-library authors, whose own unexplained 31-38% would be decomposed. Audience (b) is the one that would change practice fastest, and the discordance direction found in yeast (knockout-essential genes tolerating acute depletion) is precisely the case that matters to them, because it is the case where a degrader would fail on a validated genetic dependency.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: is the depletion-depth dependence of the fitness response a measurable dose-response curve per gene (a per-gene "essentiality reserve")? Does transcriptional adaptation have a fitness consequence at all, which P3 never tested? Can a kinetic class be predicted from protein half-life plus paralog structure, allowing essentiality maps to flag kinetics-sensitive calls without re-screening? Does the RNA-less-vs-PTC allele distinction change fitness calls in human screens, where almost every knockout is a PTC allele?

Significance — anchoring:
Touches the knockdown-versus-knockout discordance problem; the transcriptional-adaptation literature; the bypassable/quantitative-essentiality line (essentiality as context-dependent); CRISPR-screen blind-spot work (paralog buffering, essential-gene call bias); and the degrader-versus-genetic-validation gap in target validation.

Reason for silence (for the residue that is actually un-asked — the human-cell, depth-conditioned, half-life-resolved version):
A design asymmetry plus a disciplinary boundary. The asymmetry is real and not rhetorical: a clonal knockout of an essential gene cannot be made viable, so the matched panel must be built from inducible knockouts or pooled measurements rather than paired clones, which is exactly the construction step the yeast collections did not have to solve (they used an existing deletion collection and a heterozygous/temperature-sensitive workaround for essentials). The boundary is that the degron libraries were built by yeast cell biologists whose reference annotation is YKO, while essentiality maps are built by human cancer functional genomics whose reference is CRISPR knockout — the two communities publish in different journals and neither owns the cross-modality bookkeeping. A third, weaker component: the decisive covariate (protein half-life) is collinear with the technology's main failure mode (incomplete depletion), so the analysis is unattractive until residual protein is measured per gene, which only became routine with quantitative targeted proteomics. Note that this mechanism explains silence in *human cells* only; it does not explain silence about the question as a whole, and indeed the question was not silent.
