Question (canonical):

**V2 (Gate 1 reformulation — the one permitted, for well-posedness; scored as canonical).** Using single-molecule protein sequencing of full-length chains in an unstressed cell, what is the distribution of the number of amino-acid misincorporation errors per individual polypeptide molecule, and does it depart from the **site-independent (Poisson-binomial)** null built from the position- and codon-specific error rates measured in the same cells — i.e. are translation errors clustered onto a minority of molecules **over a window longer than one tryptic peptide**, and what fraction of molecules carry two or more errors?

**V1 (as intake, retained per Gate 1 rules).** Using single-molecule protein sequencing, what is the distribution of the number of amino-acid misincorporation errors per individual full-length polypeptide in an unstressed cell, and does it depart from the independent-error (Poisson) expectation implied by the per-site error rate measured in the same cells — that is, are translation errors clustered onto a minority of molecules?

**Why the reformulation was needed (both grounds from opened sources, not from preference).**
1. *The null in V1 is underspecified.* Mordret et al. 2019 (opened, full text) show per-site error rates spanning orders of magnitude and varying systematically with position sensitivity and protein abundance, so a single-lambda Poisson built from a proteome-average per-site rate is the wrong comparator. The correct independent-error null is the Poisson-binomial over each molecule's own position-specific rates — which is precisely the object Landerer et al. 2022 compute under their stated site-independence assumption. V2 names it.
2. *V1's implicit gap claim is too wide.* V1's motivating text asserts peptide-level MS "cannot see how many errors one full-length chain carries". That is right about the chain and wrong about co-occurrence as such: within one tryptic peptide, two to four substitutions on the *same molecule* are detectable by bottom-up MS, and Wohlgemuth et al. 2021 did exactly that and tested them against the independence product. V2 therefore narrows the gap to its two surviving limbs — the **window** (beyond one tryptic peptide, out to the full chain) and the **condition** (unstressed).

Alternative formulations:
- Does mistranslation produce many slightly wrong proteins or a few badly wrong ones?
- Is the proteome's per-molecule error load site-independent, and what fraction of molecules carry two or more errors — measured, not inferred from the bulk rate?

Motivating gap:

Measured mistranslation is an aggregate ratio because the instrument has been bottom-up MS, whose unit of observation is the tryptic peptide. The gap has three sourced parts.

- **The canonical unstressed map runs no co-occurrence test.** Mordret et al. 2019, *Mol Cell* 75:427–441 (READ LEVEL: full text, via the author-copy PDF at longitude.weizmann.ac.il). Error rate estimand quoted as served: *"estimated as the ratio of intensity between the dependent and base peptide"*; overall rate *"around one misincorporation event per 1,000 translated residues"*. The paper runs no co-occurrence, clustering or Poisson-departure analysis. Its nearest-neighbour object is site recurrence **across** molecules — *"same protein positions tend to repeatedly misincorporate the same amino acid"* — which is a different object from error count **within** a molecule.
- **The instrument truncates the object.** Wohlgemuth et al. 2021 (READ LEVEL: full text, via Göttingen repository mirror of the published PDF) state of their own observed clusters that some are *"likely to supersede the length of average tryptic peptides"* — the measuring window is shorter than the thing being measured, in the authors' own words.
- **The quantity has been computed under the null but never measured.** See the quantitative-limb entry below: Landerer et al. 2022 print a per-molecule "at least one error" fraction derived from literature per-site rates under an explicit independence assumption. Nothing opened measures the distribution.

**Correction to the intake record, recorded rather than silently fixed.** The intake wrote that a clustered distribution "is the regime proteostasis models assume and have never measured". The second clause is false as stated: departure from independent-error expectation has been measured, at up to a 60,000-fold enrichment, under aminoglycoside stress at the peptide unit (Wohlgemuth et al. 2021). What is unmeasured is the unstressed, full-chain, per-molecule **count distribution**. The candidate's gap survives only in that narrowed form, and V2 states it that way.

Presuppositions:
1. **CONTESTED, and the hardest constraint.** Single-molecule protein sequencing can read a full-length chain end to end at a per-residue error rate low enough to separate reading error from translation error. Translation error is ~10^-3 per residue (Mordret 2019, opened), so the instrument must resolve a signal three orders of magnitude below unity against its own much larger per-residue error. The multi-pass nanopore work (bioRxiv 2023.10.19.563182) is the enabling route and was **not opened by this agent** — READ LEVEL: title and search-snippet only, grounds nothing in either direction. A declared discrimination model plus known-sequence standards is required, and whether current platforms clear this bar is unestablished here.
2. **CONTESTED by this record's own reading.** That mistranslation is frequent enough to be detected per molecule at achievable throughput. From the opened numbers, lambda is small: ~1 error per 1,000 residues over a ~300-residue chain gives a mean near 0.3, and the Weizmann release for the same work puts it at *"almost one mistake per protein"*. Under Poisson at lambda = 0.3, molecules with two or more errors are a few percent of the population, so the design needs a power calculation under both the independent and the clustered hypothesis, and the absolute number of molecules at stake may be small even if the relative departure is large.
3. **CONTESTED.** Sampled molecules are full-length mature products, not degraded fragments or nascent chains; selection on both termini is required.
4. **CONTESTED — a real confounder and a second question in its own right.** Degradation has not already removed multiply-erroneous molecules before sampling, which would bias the observed distribution toward independence. V2 does not resolve which estimand is wanted (errors per *synthesised* molecule versus per *surviving* molecule); these differ under this confounder, and that unresolved choice is the main well-posedness deduction below.
5. **Established as the right comparator, and now named correctly.** The independent-error null, with position- and codon-specific rates estimated in the same cells and growth condition. Landerer et al. 2022 state the assumption explicitly — *"The explicit site independence assumption of our model allows us to calculate the probability of error free translation"* — so the null is already a published construct, not an invention of this candidate.
6. A bulk per-site frequency and a per-molecule count are commensurable once chain length and site composition are accounted for — holds under (5) by construction of the Poisson-binomial.
7. "An error" is individuated the same way by both instruments: a substitution relative to the encoded sequence, excluding post-translational modification and genetic variation in the same cells. Flagged as **stated in words, not operationalised** — bottom-up MS individuates a substitution as a dependent/base peptide intensity ratio, which is not the same individuation as a per-residue single-molecule call.

What would count as an answer:
A measured per-molecule error-count distribution with its confidence interval, tested against a pre-declared site-independent (Poisson-binomial) null built from position- and codon-specific rates measured in the same samples; known-sequence standards bounding instrument reading error; a stated detection limit for molecules carrying two or more errors; the fraction of molecules with at least two errors, with uncertainty; and a declared choice of estimand with respect to presupposition 4 (synthesised versus surviving molecules). A distribution consistent with the null, reported with the power to exclude clustering above a declared effect size, answers it negatively and settles it.

Significance — consequence:
**If clustered (yes):** two published quantities move in a specified direction. P2's *">15% of all proteins contain at least one"* and its summed per-protein fitness burden are both computed under site independence, so clustering would redistribute the same total error count onto fewer molecules — lowering the fraction affected while raising the per-affected-molecule severity, which is the quantity that matters for aggregation seeding and dominant-negative action, since both are superlinear in the number of destabilising substitutions a single chain carries. It would also extend P1's mechanism (a ribosome that errs is more likely to err again, E_next 0.05–0.25 under drug) from a drug-induced phenomenon to a constitutive property of elongation. **If independent (no):** P2's independence assumption is validated outside the stressed regime, P1's processivity is confined to aminoglycoside-perturbed decoding, and per-molecule error load becomes safely computable from bulk rates — which retires the question and licenses the existing modelling practice explicitly rather than by default.
**Honest bound on the stake:** at the opened unstressed rate (~1 per 1,000 residues, "almost one mistake per protein"), lambda is small, so even a large *relative* departure moves a small *absolute* share of molecules. The candidate does not bound the effect size that would matter, and this is the main reason significance is scored 2 rather than 3.

Significance — audience:
1. **Proteostasis and aggregation modellers** — would change the input distribution of their misfolding-load models from a bulk rate to a per-molecule distribution, which is where chaperone-capacity and seeding thresholds are nonlinear.
2. **Translation-fidelity groups (the Rodnina, Pilpel and Toth-Petroczy lines above)** — P1's authors already quantified a conditional next-error probability under drug and did not extend it to unstressed full-length chains; this is the experiment that would.
3. **Biomanufacturing sequence-variant control** — current practice quantifies variants per site (query 11); per-molecule variant load is the regulatorily relevant heterogeneity for a product, and no opened source reports it.

Reason for silence:
**An instrument boundary, stated in the prior literature's own words, plus a disciplinary split.** The measuring instrument has been bottom-up MS, whose unit of observation is the tryptic peptide, so the observable window is a fraction of a chain — and P1's authors say outright that some of the clusters they detect are *"likely to supersede the length of average tryptic peptides"*, i.e. the instrument truncates the object. Multi-pass single-molecule nanopore reading of long protein strands has only recently made a long-chain per-molecule read conceivable (bioRxiv 2023.10.19.563182 — not opened by this agent). The split reinforces it: the community that *measures* fidelity (MS proteomics, P1 and P3) reports ratios at sites, while the community that *uses* per-molecule error load (molecular evolution and proteostasis modelling, P2) obtains it by assuming independence, so neither side's normal output makes the per-molecule distribution a measurable it is anyone's job to measure. This is a mechanism, not "no one thought of it" — and it is partly falsified for the stressed regime, where the instrument's short window happened to be long enough, which is exactly where the question did get asked.
