## Restatement in my own words

Reference genomes made by the current long-read pipeline routinely include a step whose whole job is to throw away sequence it judges to be a second copy of the same haplotype rather than a second gene. The candidate asks two things. (1) When that step fires, how often is the thing it throws away actually a real, very recently born gene copy rather than a haplotype artefact — measured as a rate, against a per-locus copy-number instrument on the same individual, with the purge step as the only thing varied in the pipeline, inside a paralogue-identity band declared in advance. (2) If you put the thrown-away copies back into the annotations that published gene-family turnover analyses were built on, do the published gain/loss rates and the youngest end of the paralogue-age distribution move outside their own stated intervals and outside the intervals you get after CAFE's error model has had its say. In short: is the default cleaning step quietly eating the youngest duplicates, and if so does the comparative-genomics literature built on those assemblies have a bias of known sign?
