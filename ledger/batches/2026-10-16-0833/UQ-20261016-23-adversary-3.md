# Gate 4 — Adversarial inspection (adversary #3, harder rerun)
ID: UQ-20261016-23 · Date: 2026-10-16

## Restatement (my words, not the generator's)

Take a defined list of the medicines most often prescribed to breastfeeding women. Then ask two things.

**(A) An audit.** Food-animal regulators will not let a dairy product go to market after treatment until a *residue-depletion study* of specified design has been run and a withdrawal period computed from it. Take that study design as a fixed yardstick — minimum number of milked subjects, how many milk samples and how far apart, whether the depletion was followed far enough down, whether the assay could see low enough, whether dosing matched the licensed regimen — and ask, drug by drug, how much of the existing published human milk concentration-over-time literature would have passed it.

**(B) A recomputation.** For whichever drugs pass, run the veterinary statistical rule (an upper one-sided 95/95 tolerance limit) against several candidate infant-exposure ceilings stated in advance rather than one contested one, and lay the resulting wait-intervals beside the intervals mothers are currently told, to see the signed size and direction of the gap.

So: *is human lactation pharmacology's raw evidence good enough to meet the standard a dairy regulator imposes, and if the same arithmetic were run on it, would the answers match today's advice?* Limb A is a bookkeeping census against an imported yardstick; limb B is an arithmetic re-derivation. The record's claimed firewall is that limb A stands alone and needs no agreed exposure ceiling.

## What the opened sources say (quoted)

**Meer DH, Wieringa A, Wegner I, Wilffert B, ter Horst PGJ. "Lactation studies of anticonvulsants: a quality review." Br J Clin Pharmacol 2015;79(4):558–565, doi 10.1111/bcp.12524.** Reviewed "the quality of the available lactation studies for 19 anticonvulsants" (62 studies) "against the guidelines of the Food and Drug Administration (FDA)" and ILCA. Faults named: "the lack of information on sampling methods, the number of samples in a particular dose interval" and "the low number of study participants". Counts reported: "25 of 62 studies were case reports, letters or abstracts"; "no study on anticonvulsants had both the absolute infant dose (AID) and milk to plasma ratio (M : P) correctly assessed". No veterinary, residue-depletion, MRL, tolerance-limit or interruption-interval content.

**Fomina YY, Byrne JJ, Spong CY. "Evaluating strength of recommendations for commonly administered medications in lactating women." J Matern Fetal Neonatal Med 2023;36(1):2163626, doi 10.1080/14767058.2022.2163626.** Graded every drug in LactMed as of Dec 2020, plus "the most commonly used medications in lactating women as defined by prior literature". "1408 medications were evaluated"; "714 (51%) had no associated data, 664 (47%) had minimal-moderate data, and 30 (2%) had strong data." "Strong data was classified as availability of research studies in all four LactMed categories" (maternal drug levels, infant drug levels, effects on infants, effects on lactation). No interruption intervals, exposure limits, RID thresholds or veterinary content.

**EMA CVMP, adopted guideline on determination of withdrawal periods for milk, rev. 1.** Design: "Data should be available for a representative sample of n animals, with n≥20"; "should be carried out with animals milked twice a day"; "between milking intervals of 12 hours are to be preferred"; "the last administration of the compound shall be 12 hours (one milking period) before milking 1"; "four-quarter composite samples should be collected from individual cows at each time point"; selection with attention to factors "known to be important, such as milk yield". Statistic: the harmonised method is **Time-To-Safe-Concentration**, with the withdrawal period "calculated as the 95/95 tolerance limit, i.e. the upper 95% confidence limit of the 95th percentile", where "TTSC points are assumed to arise from a log-normal distribution"; log-linear regression on concentrations is a "modification of the FDA method" relegated to Annex I, **not** the harmonised approach. Limit-relative criteria: "provided that the LOQ is sufficiently below or equal to the MRL1"; "if the last concentration in the data set is still above MRL1, TTSCi cannot be calculated." Sparse-data fallback: "If most or all residue concentrations are below the LOQ, no meaningful calculation of a 95/95 tolerance limit is possible" → "it is acceptable to establish a withdrawal period of 12 hours."

**FSANZ, "Medicines or drugs of abuse in human milk and human milk products", imported food risk advice to the Department of Agriculture, Water and the Environment, Aug 2019 (updated Oct 2019).** Sets no MRL/ADI for medicine residues in human milk. Records the donor-milk withholding convention: "the deferral period would be five times the half-life of the medication" (HMBANA), with "deferral periods of two months" for certain agents, and for imaging compounds "Breastfeeding should be interrupted to avoid infant exposures greater than 1 mSv", "Recommended time periods range from none to greater than 3 weeks."

**Prior spot-check.** Monfort et al. 2025 (Front Pharmacol, 10.3389/fphar.2025.1602018) — real, independently re-surfaced at frontiersin.org and the PAGE abstract list. The radiopharmaceutical line (record's prior #3) — corroborated in substance by the FSANZ quotes above (1 mSv constraint; interruption periods up to >3 weeks). Record's prior #2 — **unverified, by me as well as by the record.** One factual correction: the record describes the imported statistic as "an upper one-sided tolerance limit on the depletion curve"; the adopted guideline's harmonised statistic is a tolerance limit on **per-subject crossing times** (TTSC), not on the curve. That mis-description is what generates the error in attack 4.

## Recommended verdict

**Rejected** — Unresolved on attack 2 (Disguise) and attack 4 (Presupposition failure), either of which is fatal under Gate 4; attack 7 is Unresolved as well, which independently sends the novelty finding back to Gate 2.

**Single strongest killing point.** Fomina et al. 2023 (J Matern Fetal Neonatal Med 36(1):2163626) already ran limb A's census on the candidate's own population, own source and own drug-list concept — all 1408 LactMed medications plus "the most commonly used medications in lactating women" — and published the number the record said it could not obtain: "714 (51%) had no associated data, 664 (47%) had minimal-moderate data, and 30 (2%) had strong data"; Meer et al. 2015 (Br J Clin Pharmacol 79(4):558–565) had already audited 62 lactation studies across 19 drugs against an externally published design standard, faulting participant numbers and samples per dose interval. The record's explicit novelty ground — that the census framing "was not found" — is therefore false, and each paper surfaced on a single query in a vocabulary the record never tried. What is left of limb A is a stricter yardstick applied to an answered question; what is left of limb B was never verified by anyone in this record.

**Routing if rejected as already-asked.** Limb A belongs with the in-field lactation-data-quality audit genre (Meer 2015; Fomina 2023; concePTION/Milk4baby, Monfort 2025). The one element I could not find anywhere, and which could be re-posed as a narrower candidate, is limb B's statistical core: *can a per-subject time-to-safe-concentration tolerance limit be estimated from sparse, pooled human milk literature data, and at what loss of coverage?* That is a method question, not a census, and it survives both of my fatal attacks.
