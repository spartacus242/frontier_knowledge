# UQ-20261017-25 — Adversary 2 log

(Second adversary. First adversary's log not read, per instruction.)

## Restatement in my own words

Engineers and hydrologists publish, on a dated document, a number for a named place that is advertised as having a stated chance of being beaten in any year — the 50-year wind speed, the 1%-annual-chance discharge, the 100-year wave height. The candidate asks: if you collect many such published numbers together with the date each was published, and then count how often they were actually beaten in the years *after* publication, does the count match the advertised chance? And if it does not, how much of the gap is because the method used at the time produced a number that was wrong on the very data it was fitted to, how much is ordinary sampling noise given short records, and how much is the climate having moved since? Equivalently: score a published design value the way a meteorologist scores a probability forecast, and attribute the miscalibration.

That is a coverage/calibration test of an issued quantile, pooled over sites, plus a three-way variance attribution.

## Primary-source quotations the attacks rest on

From Tasan et al., *Geophys. J. Int.* 2014 (opened in full):
- objective: "compare the distribution of the expected number of sites with exceedance with the observed number" (Abstract)
- nominal rate is the input: "The acceleration thresholds with a given probability p of exceedance in a given time window are inferred from the hazard curves at all sites" … "Therefore the binomial distribution gives the expected number of sites with exceedances" (Sect. 2.1)
- return periods tested directly: "We tested AFPS2006 also at fixed return periods" (Sect. 3.3)
- the artefact tested is an issued code model: "The MEDD2002 model has been derived for the official French seismic building code" (Sect. 3.2); "We do not question any of the decisions taken in these studies. We simply use these models and test them" (Sect. 3.2)
- dependence handled by thinning, not modelling: "Acceleration occurrences must be independent from one site to the other" (Sect. 2.1); "stations located closer than 10 km to another rock station have been discarded" (Sect. 3.1)
- attribution not achieved: "the discrepancies between observations and predictions can generally have more than one explanation" (Conclusions)
- and the power caveat: "very different models may be consistent with the observations" when the expected count is near zero (Sect. 3.3)

From the SSA abstract "Do PSHA Maps Overpredict or Are There Shaking Deficits in the Historic Record?" (opened):
- the nominal-rate conversion the candidate uses: "p = 1 – exp(-t/T)"
- the pooled statistic: M0 is based on "the fraction of sites where modeled ground motion is exceeded," compared with the fraction the exponential model predicts
- the unresolved attribution, listing model overprediction, biased-low observations and a less active observation period: "Whether this reflects too-high models and/or biased data remains an important question."

**My own arithmetic, labelled as such.** 40 post-issue years at nominal p = 0.01 gives 0.4 expected exceedances per site, and per-site probability of at least one exceedance 1 − e^(−0.4) = 0.33 — the record's power figure checks out and matches the SSA abstract's exponential form. Scope check in the candidate's favour: Tasan's near-zero-expectation warning is for 62 thinned rock stations; a hydrological frame on the scale of Kjeldsen & Prosdocimi's 842 UK gauges × 40 yr × 0.01 would carry ≈337 expected exceedances before dependence, so Tasan's "very different models may be consistent" regime is **not** the candidate's regime. Effective sample size after the overdispersion Kjeldsen & Prosdocimi report is unknown and could be far smaller; I am not quoting a corrected figure I cannot derive.

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional** — nothing Unresolved on attacks 1, 2 or 4, but attack 7 (reason for silence) is Unresolved on opened primary sources, which under the Gate 4 rule also puts the Medium novelty grade back in question; promotion requires a fresh Gate 2 sweep for seismic-style issued-quantile coverage testing inside hydrology/wind and the opening of the three items Gate 2 left unread.

**Single strongest killing point:** the sibling design-value field already ran this experiment on official code-issued values — Tasan et al. 2014 compares "the expected number of sites with exceedance with the observed number" for the model "derived for the official French seismic building code," and the SSA M0 metric scores "the fraction of sites where modeled ground motion is exceeded" against p = 1 − exp(−t/T) — which simultaneously falsifies the record's negative that no one scores issued design values on the rate scale, inverts its fecundity claim that seismic maps are the *next* frame, destroys all three reason-for-silence mechanisms, and shows by twenty years of experience that the candidate's load-bearing presupposition 5 is not separable in practice ("Whether this reflects too-high models and/or biased data remains an important question").
