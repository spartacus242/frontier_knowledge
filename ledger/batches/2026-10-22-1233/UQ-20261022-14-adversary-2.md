## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take the reservoirs and lakes that SWOT sees and whose operator publishes (a) a level record with a named vertical datum or a published conversion to one and (b) an operating rule with a flood-pool / spillway-crest / freeboard threshold. For each one, compute r = SWOT's geoid-referenced water-surface elevation minus the operator's reported level after pushing that level onto the same geopotential datum *using the conversion the operator itself publishes*. Then report three things: the spread of r across that declared set with uncertainties; whether r holds still over the mission (steps, trend, checked against local vertical land motion where available); and, restricted to the size classes where SWOT's quoted accuracy is finer than the operating margin, the fraction of the set where |r| is bigger than that margin. A small, stationary r is a complete answer and licenses current practice.

In one line: *do the gauge numbers dams are run on sit where the structure's rule assumes, and can SWOT now audit that at population scale?*

## 3. Attack 4 worked properly — conditioning clauses as a numbered list, each graded against a document I opened

These are clauses the question needs to be TRUE before it can be asked at all, as distinct from anything a study would measure.

1. **SWOT delivers a geoid-referenced WSE for reservoirs of the relevant size.** TRUE (sourced): the isprs paper works a 0.7 km² reservoir from L2_HR_Raster_100m and states SWOT is referenced to EGM2008.
2. **Some operators publish a level record with a named vertical datum or a published conversion to one.** TRUE (sourced): USGS-07312109 states the gage datum as NGVD 29 and publishes a conversion (+0.82 ft to NAVD 1988). This clause the Gate 2 record left UNVERIFIED and treated as the reason it withheld 3/3 on well-posedness; on the instance I opened it comes back TRUE, which *helps* the candidate. Note also that V2 converts datum-documentation yield into a reported primary result, so even where it fails it is a measurand and attack 4 does not reach it.
3. **For an included water body, the operator's OWN stated conversion is sufficient to put the reported level onto the geopotential datum SWOT uses, so that a non-zero r is a defect in a chain the operator owns.** **FALSE on both instances I opened.** The USGS conversion chain terminates at NAVD 88 — a levelling datum — and the page publishes nothing geoid-referenced; closing the last leg requires a third-party transformation model which, on the only source I opened about it, is explicitly "a transformation rather than an equipotential surface" whose surface is itself "a bias, a continental tilt, and local warping from the bench marks". And in the one published SWOT-versus-reservoir-operator comparison I opened, the operator's frame was not merely unconverted but unavailable: "information on the height reference frame of the gauge station was not available", so the authors assumed a frame, bridged it with a geoid difference they do not print, and flagged a candidate frame shift of −0.18 m against their own measured difference of −0.08 m. UNCHECKED (0 fetches left): whether a USACE/USBR water-control page supplies a geoid-referenced conversion — if one does, this clause could be rescued for part of a US frame.
4. **Operating rules state a MARGIN that |r| can be compared against.** UNVERIFIED-tending-FALSE: the page I opened publishes no threshold at all; the record's own two operator instances publish thresholds with no datum and no tolerance; and the record itself concedes that "what a declared margin for referencing error should be in an operating rule... no opened document contains". An elevation threshold is not a margin; threshold-minus-current-level is a time-varying quantity, not a referencing tolerance. Graded UNVERIFIED on what I opened, so it kills nothing on its own; it feeds attack 5.
5. **SWOT's stated WSE accuracy is quotable by size class.** UNVERIFIED on what I opened (the isprs paper quotes no mission requirement; I did not open a product handbook).
6. **A local vertical-motion constraint exists at some included structures.** UNVERIFIED on what I opened.

Clause 3 is the one the question cannot survive without, because it is exactly the repair V2 made at Gate 1: V1 was rejected as "an undecomposable mixture", and V2's whole claim to well-posedness is that defining r against the operator's own stated conversion makes a non-zero r "a defect in a chain somebody owns". On the documents I opened, that conversion does not exist — the operator's chain stops at a national levelling datum and the geopotential leg belongs to a national transformation model nobody at the reservoir owns. So V2 re-inherits V1's defect under a different label, and the leading term of r in any US frame is a datum-transformation field, not a gauge defect.
