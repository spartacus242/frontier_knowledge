Question (canonical):
V1 (intake) — For a declared frame of in-service heat exchangers in one process-service class, with monitored duty, flows and inlet/outlet temperatures and retrievable design datasheets, what is the empirical distribution of the realized fouling resistance against the tabulated fouling resistance used in that unit's design, and — restricted to units whose surface area was fouling-allowance-controlled — what share of installed heat-transfer area is excess area that the realized fouling never calls into use?

V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted; scored as canonical) — For a declared frame of in-service heat exchangers in one process-service class, with monitored duty, flows and inlet/outlet temperatures and retrievable design datasheets, what are the bias and spread of the distribution of realized fouling resistance over the tabulated fouling resistance used in that unit's design, under a propagated uncertainty budget with a stated detection floor; and, restricted to units whose surface area is verifiably fouling-allowance-controlled on the datasheet, what is the SIGNED area-sizing error implied by that distribution — excess installed area where the allowance over-predicted, deficit where it under-predicted?

Reason for the reformulation (well-posedness only): V1's second limb presupposed the sign of the error by asking only for "excess area." The nearest prior art (Sloley 2019, below) reports realized fouling factors at roughly three times TEMA values in crude service — i.e. under-prediction — so V1 would have been unanswerable in the index service class while V2 is answerable whichever way the distribution falls. Both versions recorded per Gate 1.

Alternative formulations:
- Do the tabulated fouling factors that designers take from standards predict the fouling that actually occurs, and by how much are exchangers consequently over- or under-sized?
- Treating the design fouling allowance as a prediction and the monitored thermal resistance as its outcome, what are the bias and the spread of that prediction across a service class?

Motivating gap:
Process-plant design inverts a settled claim without testing it: the fouling allowance taken from a standard's fouling-resistance table is used as though it represented the fouling the unit will experience, yet those tabulated values ship without a distribution, an uncertainty or a stated provenance, and the fouling literature quantifies the cost of fouling at macroeconomic scale while continuing to treat the allowance as a margin rather than as a forecast to be verified.

Gate 2 correction to the motivating gap: the provenance half of this gap is not an unnoticed gap — it is a published observation in the candidate's own trade literature. Sloley (Chemical Processing, 2023-01-05, FULL TEXT read) writes of the TEMA values that "they were originally developed with very little, or perhaps even no data, to back up the values." and that the original values were "based on a consensus of experience from a variety of people but used no full analysis in deciding the fouling values." Aguirre (Chemical Processing, 2006-02-16, FULL TEXT read) states that "The original TEMA fouling factors were not only meant to include the real effect of fouling" but were "thus representing a combination of a fouling and safety factor". The candidate's premise is correct and sourced; it is the premise of an existing conversation, not a silence.

Presuppositions (as intaken, with Gate 2 disposition):
1. A standard (TEMA's fouling-resistance table being the index case) tabulates fouling resistances and designers use them as the allowance. ESTABLISHED, but NOT by a quotation from the standard itself — Gate 2 did not open TEMA (not freely retrievable; no fetch budget left after the five priors). What was established, from sources opened: the table exists, dates to the 1941 first edition, is at the 10th edition, carries "Recommended Good Practice" values, has "changed very little from 1941 to today", and TEMA itself says the values "should be used only when more accurate information is not available." (Sloley 2023 and Aguirre 2006, both full text). Recording the standard's own wording as NOT QUOTED rather than as a check that passed.
2. Realized fouling resistance is identifiable from operating data given a credible clean-condition reference. CONTESTED at intake; Gate 2 found the contest stated in the field's own words and it is sharper than the intake allowed: Sloley 2023 (full text) states that "the plant calculated fouling factor... includes the results of all errors and uncertainties." That is a documented statement that the quantity routinely computed is not the quantity the question names. The uncertainty budget is therefore load-bearing, as the intake said, and the detection floor is the part most likely to decide whether V2 has an answer at all.
3. The design fouling allowance for each specific unit is retrievable from datasheets. CONTESTED, unchanged — operator/vendor-held, so the frame is a cooperating-operator archive and not a random sample of the installed base. Note that every empirical source found operates under exactly this constraint (four units; "published crude unit reports").
4. A single lumped fouling resistance is the right measurand. CONTESTED for heavy fouling, unchanged. Not addressed by any source opened.
5. Fouling resistance within a run between cleanings is stationary or has an estimable trajectory. CONTESTED, and Gate 2 found the opposite emphasis in the field: Sloley 2019 (abstract) — "Fouling rates may vary dramatically from unit to unit and from exchanger to exchanger within the same unit." That is between-unit heterogeneity, not within-run non-stationarity, so it does not settle this presupposition either way; recorded as unaddressed.
6. Excess area is a meaningful counterfactual. CONTESTED, unchanged, and now partly inverted: Sloley 2019 (abstract) says "Higher fouling shifts optimum heat recovery investment to higher capital and lower energy recovery." — i.e. in crude service the field's own reading is that the allowance is too SMALL, which is why V2 makes the limb signed.
7. Monitored duty is attributable to the exchanger rather than to an upstream change in service. CONTESTED, unchanged. Not addressed by any source opened.

What would count as an answer:
A per-service-class distribution of realized over tabulated fouling resistance with a propagated uncertainty budget and a stated detection floor, plus the implied signed area-sizing error for the allowance-controlled subset. A distribution centred on 1 with modest spread vindicates the tables and is a full answer. A demonstration that routine plant instrumentation cannot identify the measurand at useful precision is also an answer — it would say that the standard's central design input is, as deployed, untestable, which is directly actionable for the committees that maintain it.

Significance — consequence: If the distribution is centred near 1 with modest spread, the tables are vindicated as forecasts and the long-running practitioner complaint (priors 1-3, Aguirre, Sloley 2023) is answered. If it is centred well above 1, as prior 1's four crude units suggest, the allowance systematically under-predicts and the design consequence is deficit area and earlier cleaning, not waste; if well below 1, as prior 3's lab result suggests for HVAC water, the allowance buys area the plant never uses, at capital cost and — per the field's own argument that excess area lowers velocity — at the price of faster fouling. A demonstration that routine instrumentation cannot resolve the measurand at all is the third answer and the most consequential one, because it would say the standard's central design input is untestable as deployed.
