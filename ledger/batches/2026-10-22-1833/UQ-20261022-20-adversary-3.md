## Restatement (my own words, before reading the generator's framing twice)

Pick open-weight model families that have shipped the context window upward at least twice (so the same family gives you both the old number, the new number, and both sets of weights). For each doubling, ask four separate things. (a) On a specific named public log of real requests, broken out by kind of workload, what fraction of requests are longer than the OLD limit? (b) Taking only those over-long requests, does running them at the new limit actually produce better task outcomes than chopping or compressing them down to the old limit (with a retrieval-augmented chop as the fair comparison)? (c) What does a request cost to serve at the new window versus the old one? (d) Did the family's short-prompt quality drop between the pre- and post-extension checkpoints? The implied verdict: if (a) is tiny, (b) is null and (d) is negative, the last doubling destroyed value and the announced number is positioning rather than engineering.

In short: **was the window ever the binding constraint, and what did raising it buy per unit of cost** — asked as an ex-post audit of a shipped product number rather than as a capability benchmark.
