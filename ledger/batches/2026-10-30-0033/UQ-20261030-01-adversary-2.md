## Restatement in my own words

Take a mammalian cell line that divides into two equal daughters (not a stem cell dividing asymmetrically). Watch single divisions. In each dividing cell, three kinds of cellular garbage are labelled at once: worn-out/depolarised mitochondria, blobs of misfolded protein in the cytosol, and lysosomes loaded with the brown non-degradable autofluorescent pigment lipofuscin. For each division, decide which daughter is "daughter 1" by one rule that has nothing to do with the garbage (the daughter that inherits the older of the two mother centrioles), and compute, for each garbage type, how lopsidedly it was split, as a signed fraction corrected for the two daughters' volumes. Then ask whether the lopsidedness of the three types goes the same way in the same division. Three readings: all three pairwise rank correlations positive = the cell has ONE disposal axis and one daughter is the designated dustbin; correlations indistinguishable from a null made by shuffling which species label belongs to which division = each garbage type is handled by its own independent machinery; any correlation negative = the cell deliberately sends different garbage to different daughters.

In short: is "the damaged daughter" one thing, three things, or a mistake?
