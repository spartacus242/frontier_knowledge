## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take an agent benchmark where every knob you can pin is pinned: greedy decoding, fixed seeds, one harness, tool calls recorded once and replayed verbatim. The only thing still wobbling is the arithmetic inside the serving engine, because GPU reduction order in matmul/attention/RMSNorm depends on what else happens to be in the batch. Two questions. (i) How much run-to-run spread in task success does that arithmetic wobble actually produce — operationally, the difference between replicate spread when you deliberately shuffle batch composition and replicate spread when you force batch-invariant kernels? (ii) Is the wobble fair — does the shuffled arm average to the same success rate as pinned numeric paths do — or does it sit systematically *below* them, by an amount that grows the longer the rollout? Behind (ii) is a claim about dynamics, not arithmetic: in a long rollout, a flipped token can push the agent into a hole it never climbs out of, while a lucky flip rarely buys a win, so extra chances to flip should cost expected success. Flat/zero gap kills that claim.

That is the question I attacked. I treated nothing in the record's framing as settled, including its claim that no counterfactual existed before batch-invariant kernels shipped.

### What the missed prior actually contains (arXiv:2506.09501)
- Measures Std@Acc across 12 numeric configurations (2 GPU types × 2 GPU counts × 3 batch sizes) under **greedy decoding**: up to **9.15 pp** on AIME'24 under BF16 vs **≈0 under FP32**; >90% of MATH500 examples diverge under BF16 (96.6%) vs 2.2% under FP32; output-length SD 9,189 tokens under BF16 vs 0 under FP32.
- Uses a **pinned high-precision counterfactual** (full FP32, and its own LayerCast = BF16 weights / FP32 compute) as the control arm, and cites the Thinking Machines batch-invariance work in §2.2 without evaluating it.
- Reports **per-configuration mean accuracies in its tables** (e.g. Table 10, Table 25, where LayerCast's means track FP32 on several benchmarks) but **never analyses them as bias** — it reports stability, divergence index and top-1 probability variance.
- Divergence position moves *later* in the sequence at higher precision (Fig. 5), and it cites prior work on a negative correlation between output length and consistency.
- Scope: single-prompt reasoning/coding benchmarks only (AIME'24, MATH500, LiveCodeBench, GPQA-D). No tool use, no multi-step agent, no horizon trend as an estimand.

### Spot-checks of the record's cited priors
- Prior A (arXiv:2602.07150, Bjarnason/Silva/Monperrus, "On Randomness in Agentic Evals", 6 Feb 2026) — **real and accurately characterised**: ~60,000 SWE-Bench-Verified trajectories, pass@1 varying 2.2–6.0 pp, SD >1.5 pp at temperature 0, token-level early divergence, recommends multi-run/pass@k/pass^k.
