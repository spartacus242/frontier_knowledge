## Restatement (my own words)

Take a training run that crashed and resumed. The run claims — by manifest, or by "seed + loader + world size" — a particular ordered list of documents it consumed. Question (a): how far off is the list it *actually* consumed? Report it two ways: as a bag-of-documents error (never seen; seen more times than claimed, as a fraction of nominal) and as an order error (how far documents moved from their claimed slot), per restart and summed, with the recovery semantics at each restart named and resharding events separated. Question (b): with a pre-registered test and a stated detection floor, do documents sitting next to a restart boundary show different memorization or trajectory-specific influence than matched documents far from any boundary — i.e. does the restart leave a dated fingerprint in the weights?

Short version: what are the delivery semantics of a production training dataloader across crash recovery, measured in documents rather than in minutes, and is the breach visible in the model?

## The load-bearing evidence, quoted

**A. Megatron-LM #7645 (https://github.com/NVIDIA/Megatron-LM/issues/7645), opened 2026-09-24, state open.** Title: "cyclic dataloader with data sharding repeats and skips samples when resumed at a different data-parallel size". Body, verbatim extracts:

> "The checkpoint stores only `consumed_train_samples`, and `build_pretraining_data_loader` rebuilds each rank's `MegatronPretrainingRandomSampler` from it. With `data_sharding=True`, each rank walks a permutation of its own contiguous bucket. `bucket_size`, `bucket_offset` and `start_idx` all depend on `data_parallel_size`. After a DP change, the resumed ranks walk different buckets from a different offset, so the samples they skip are not the samples the run already consumed in that epoch."

> "This runs on CPU and needs only the sampler class (checked against `main` @ ec806b2)."

Its output, verbatim:

> `data_sharding=True, DP 4 -> 2 at step 5: 58 duplicated, 58 never drawn in epoch 0`
> `data_sharding=True, DP 4 -> 8 at step 5: 56 duplicated, 56 never drawn in epoch 0`
> `data_sharding=False, DP 4 -> {2,8} at step 5: 0 duplicated, 0 never drawn in epoch 0`

> "A sweep over every resume step and every DP change among {1, 2, 4, 8} fails at nearly every resume point, for both divisible and non-divisible dataset sizes."

> "**Expected behavior** After a resume at any supported parallel configuration, every sample of the current epoch is drawn exactly once. Alternatively, the resume should fail loudly if the data order cannot be preserved."

> "`--dataloader-type single`, the default, is not affected: it held under every DP change in the same sweep."

Companion issue #7646 does the same for `--no-data-sharding` with a non-divisible dataset size ("DP 1 -> 8 at step 15: 19 duplicated, 19 dropped vs. the uninterrupted run").

Scope check on my own use of this: these are sampler-level simulations on toy datasets (N=250/256), not realized counts from a production run, and they cover one loader (Megatron `cyclic`) and one fault class (DP change at resume). They do not establish a number for any real run, and they say nothing about limb (b). What they do establish is a method claim: for this loader, realized-vs-claimed multiset divergence at a resharding restart is a closed-form function of (resume step, DP before, DP after, sharding flag, dataset size) **obtainable by reading the sampler**, with exact duplicate/drop counts, and the exactly-once property stated as the expected behaviour of a training dataloader.

**B. Hubble (arXiv:2510.19811), opened.** Verbatim from the paper: perturbation doses "we randomly assign examples to be duplicated {0x, 1x, 4x, 16x, 64x, 256x}"; insertion "Our insertion attempts to simulate training as if the perturbation was a regular document included in training"; timing arm "we train six 1B models on 100B tokens where perturbations are inserted only during specific timeframes", windows "(0, 25), (25, 50), (50, 75), (75, 100)". Findings: "When perturbations are inserted in only the first quarter of training, the final model does not memorize the data." / "When all perturbations are inserted in the last quarter of training, more data is memorized." On detectability at the dose a restart actually produces: membership inference on members duplicated only once gives near-random AUC, while loss "can show statistically significant differences in memorization at lower duplicate counts". No formal power analysis or per-document detection threshold.

**C. Jagielski et al. (arXiv:2207.00099), opened.** "the later a point is seen, the longer it takes to forget" (with "no clear trend on the C4 dataset"); "examples injected before the decay are forgotten significantly slower than examples injected after"; "the most recently seen examples are most vulnerable to privacy attacks"; "data duplicated many times is forgotten more slowly than data duplicated few times".

Scope check on my use of B and C: neither studies fault-induced perturbation, neither has a restart timestamp, and Hubble's dose grid skips the 2x point that a single rollback produces (it jumps 1x -> 4x), so I am *not* claiming limb (b) has been run. I am claiming its design (insert documents at a chosen training position, vary exposures, measure per-document memorization against controls) and its dose-response are published, so limb (b)'s answer is largely interpolable, and — per C — the restart-adjacent vs away-from-boundary contrast is confounded by plain position-in-training, which C measures.
