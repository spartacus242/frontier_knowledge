## The reversal: UQ-20261022-06

The kill was single-leg: Bhattarai & Thapa, ACCLM 2026;5(1):18-25, was held to print the candidate's quantity, a share of 701 (0.73%) in 95,757. A fresh-context checker read it in full and the ground does not hold — in its words, "it is a different numerator on a different denominator with only partial overlap."

- The paper's repeat is "a re-analysis of the same analyte from the original sample aliquot performed before the initial result was reported", explicitly EXCLUDING repeats on clinician request after reporting or on a new sample.
- The overlap fails in **both** directions. The 701 **misses** new-sample repeats, post-report repeats, cross-instrument confirmation (no second analyser exists in the paper; every search term NOT PRESENT) and delta-check repeats ("delta" appears nowhere). It **includes** a class the candidate excludes: where the first run was voided by an analyser error flag (37.09%), a QC failure or being out of linearity (9.55%), the released value IS the first *valid* measurement — "up to 46.6% of the 701".
- The denominator is tests **PERFORMED**, not released results, confirmed against "ordered" and "released" on direct question.
- It prints the share **REPEATED**, not **REPLACED**: "the replacement share is printed nowhere".

The paper also carries **three mutually inconsistent corpus totals** (95,757 / 93,706 / 88,706 — Table 3's printed total does not match the sum of its own nine rows), two rates for one numerator (0.73% and 0.75%), and a Methods inclusion list that is not the same four categories as the Results decomposition. So the composition of the 701 cannot be reconstructed from what it prints. Everything else about the paper verified: front matter, the verbatim abstract string, all 11 spot-checked Table 2 values, Table 4's eight significant analytes, the article type. The batch's own "never saw a rendered table" caveat is retired. This is a fit failure, not a citation error.

## UQ-20261022-09: the kill nobody could read

The checker sent to read O'Malley et al. 2021 in full was **served no article text at any level**, at any route, and returned only the identifier triple. Under this ledger's Unverified rule an unrun check cannot reject, so the Gate 2 Low grade is **demoted from a finding to Unverified** and attack 2's Unresolved falls with it. The verdict is unchanged because **attack 4 was Unresolved from both adversaries independently**, which is fatal on its own — but what was a three-leg verdict anchored by a published number is now a one-leg verdict resting on an instrument-availability finding.

The sharper point is that confirming the prior's numbers would not have saved the kill. This candidate's estimand is the effect of a human settings edit **net of the controller's own concurrent adaptation**, and a before-and-after contrast across users measures the edit plus whatever the algorithm did to itself over the same window, confounded by design unless the authors say they separated them. So the promotion condition is written against that clause, not against the figures: a sentence in which the authors state they isolated or adjusted for automatic adaptation.

Meanwhile the entry's self-accused "chief unrun check" was at last identified, in two calls: **Messer LH & Breton MD, "Therapy Settings Associated with Optimal Outcomes for t:slim X2 with Control-IQ Technology in Real-World Clinical Care", Diabetes Technology & Therapeutics 2023;25(12):877-882, doi 10.1089/dia.2023.0308**. Still not opened — could-not-read, not not-found. And on abstract-level evidence it is a **between-person cross-sectional regression on settings LEVELS** across 20,764 users whose strongest predictor is a behaviour, so it sits outside the regime of a within-person edit-effect claim; its own "lever to pull" conclusion makes exactly that leap, and a reader who takes the conclusion at face value inherits it.

## The process fix worth more than any single check

**Record a source by DOI plus first author plus year plus title, never by PMCID alone.** Three PMCIDs in this batch were recorded as "could not read at any route". Each converted in **one** call at `ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/utils/idconv`; two then opened, and one of those — **PMC11741007** — turned out to be UQ-08's **own fourth prior**, Pageler 2016, carried twice in the same entry under two identifiers without either agent noticing. PMC is the one identifier this container cannot open, which is a sufficient explanation for seven retrievals of one paper without a single read.
