## Question (canonical, as submitted)

In F_q[t], with monic polynomials of each degree ordered by their coefficient string and two monic irreducibles called consecutive when no irreducible lies between them, does the pair of residue classes of consecutive irreducibles modulo a fixed monic m show the same signed under-representation of equal classes that Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan found for consecutive primes modulo q, or is the bias absent?

## Question (REFORMULATED at Gate 1 — the one permitted reformulation; both versions recorded)

Fix a prime q in {2,3,5} and identify F_q with the residues {0,1,...,q-1} mod q. Order the monic polynomials of degree n by the integer value of their coefficient string read **constant-term-first as the least significant digit**, i.e. send f = t^n + sum_{i<n} a_i t^i to the integer sum_{i<n} a_i q^i, and order by that integer (degree is the size parameter; the order is total within each degree and degrees are taken in increasing order). Call two monic irreducibles of degree n *consecutive* when no monic irreducible of degree n lies strictly between them in that order. For each monic m of degree 1, 2 or 3 and each class a in (F_q[t]/m)^x, define

  delta(a,a; m, n, q) = P(a,a; m, n, q) / ( N_pairs(n,q) / phi(m)^2 ) - 1,

where P(a,a;...) counts ordered consecutive pairs (f, g) of degree-n monic irreducibles with f = g = a mod m and both coprime to m, N_pairs(n,q) counts all such ordered consecutive pairs with both members coprime to m, and phi(m) = |(F_q[t]/m)^x|. **Question:** is delta(a,a; m, n, q) systematically negative, as the Lemke Oliver–Soundararajan deficit in Z is reported to be, and how does it behave as n grows at fixed q; and does its sign and magnitude change when the SAME irreducible set is re-ordered by a second structured rule (the coefficient string read leading-coefficient-first, i.e. the reverse digit significance) that leaves every local correlation among irreducibles untouched while changing the position-to-residue map?

Reason for the reformulation (well-posedness only): (i) "ordered by their coefficient string" does not fix an order until the ordering of F_q's elements and the digit significance are both fixed, and those two choices are the variable the question's own mechanism limb turns on; (ii) the normalising expectation was named but not written; (iii) the submitted order-randomisation control is a null calibration rather than a mechanism discriminator (see Presuppositions, item 6), so the discriminating comparison is stated explicitly as two structured orders.

**What the repair itself presupposes, tested as hard as the clause it replaced:** that a canonical ordering of F_q exists with no arbitrary choice. This holds for q in {2,3,5} precisely because all three are *prime*, so F_q = Z/q carries the residue order; it FAILS for prime-power q (q = 4, 8, 9), where a basis of F_q over its prime field must be chosen and the order becomes basis-dependent. The reformulated question is therefore stated only for prime q, which coincides with the candidate's own declared feasibility set {2,3,5} — the repair does not widen the frame it was asked to fix, and any extension to prime-power q would need a separate declaration. The repair adds no record frame: both versions quantify over polynomials, not over documents.

## Alternative formulations

1. Is the consecutive-prime residue bias a consequence of local k-tuple correlations alone, or does it require the archimedean ordering of Z — as decided by the function-field model, where the needed correlation statements are theorems rather than conjectures?
2. For monic irreducibles over a finite field, do successive irreducibles repeat their residue class modulo a fixed monic less often than chance, and how does the deficit behave as the degree grows?

## Motivating gap

Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan, "Unexpected biases in the distribution of consecutive primes", PNAS 113 (2016), report a large, slowly decaying deficit of consecutive primes lying in the same class mod q, with a Hardy–Littlewood-based heuristic reproducing it. The heuristic attributes the bias to local correlations plus counting. The only arena where it has ever been measured is Z, where the correlations are conjectural and the ordering is forced. F_q[t] separates the two ingredients: k-tuple-type correlation statements are theorems in the large-q regime, equidistribution of degree-n irreducibles in classes mod m is exact, and the within-degree ordering is a free choice — so the measurement discriminates mechanism rather than re-confirming the phenomenon.

## Significance — consequence

If delta is systematically negative with the transported HL magnitude and is **stable** across the two structured orders, the bias is a consequence of local correlation structure that survives replacing the archimedean ordering of Z with a non-archimedean, digit-wise one — which converts LO-S's heuristic attribution into a claim with an independent instance, and makes the Z phenomenon an instance of something about prime-counting in any arithmetic of this shape. If delta **changes sign or vanishes** under one of the two orders while the irreducible set and all its correlations are identical, then the ordering is load-bearing and the Z bias is partly a fact about how Z is enumerated rather than about the primes alone — which would be the more interesting of the two outcomes and would bear directly on how the LO-S heuristic should be read. A third outcome, delta near zero in both orders, would say that the deficit needs the specific interaction of the archimedean gap distribution with residue cycling, and would sharpen rather than answer the mechanism question.

## Significance — audience

The function-field analogue community that has systematically transplanted prime statistics into F_q[t] — the Keating–Rudnick-style short-interval and correlation school, Sawin and Shusterman's large-q programme, and the function-field bias group whose papers this pass returned (Cha; Devin and Meng; the authors of 2302.13665 and 2509.16142; the "Ties in Function Field Prime Races" group) — would act on a positive result by adding an order-dependent statistic to a repertoire that is currently order-free, and would act on a negative one by treating LO-S as arena-specific. The LO-S lineage in Z (the group behind P1 is the clearest instance, having already moved the statistic to another sequence) would gain the first test of its heuristic in an arena where the inputs are not conjectural at large q.

Newly askable: the behaviour of delta in the large-q limit, where the correlation input may be theorem-grade and a closed form becomes provable rather than heuristic; the full order-dependence map, i.e. which classes of total orders on monics of degree n produce the deficit and which kill it, which is a new and self-contained combinatorial question about orders rather than about primes; the same statistic for consecutive irreducibles in short intervals and for Chebotarev classes in function-field extensions; whether the known function-field race and tie phenomena (P3, 2603.21005) have adjacency-conditioned refinements; and, back in Z, whether an order on the integers other than the usual one (e.g. digit-reversal in some base) changes the LO-S deficit, which this pass's sweeps suggest nobody has asked either.

## Significance — anchoring

Touches the LO-S phenomenon and its unsettled mechanism; the Hardy–Littlewood k-tuple conjecture and its function-field counterparts (Sawin–Shusterman's twin-prime and Chowla theorems over F_q[T], Annals 2022, and the large-q correlation literature); the function-field Chebyshev-bias and prime-race family descending from Cha; and the general question of which features of prime distribution are arithmetic and which are artefacts of enumeration — the same question that motivates the function-field model as a whole.

## Reason for silence

A disciplinary boundary with a specific shape, not an absence of curiosity. The function-field model is used because it makes statistics *provable*, and every prime statistic transplanted into it so far — races, ties, Chebyshev bias, variance in short intervals, k-tuple correlations — is **order-free**, so the transplant is canonical and the theorem is about the ring. The LO-S statistic is the first prime statistic of interest that is irreducibly order-dependent, and in F_q[t] the required order must be *stipulated*. To a community whose reason for working in F_q[t] is to remove arbitrary choices, stipulating an order looks like importing one, so the transplant reads as ill-defined rather than as informative — which is exactly inverted, since the arbitrariness is the instrument: it is what lets the ordering be varied while the correlations are held fixed. Secondarily, the statistic requires exhaustive enumeration with a declared order rather than the L-function machinery the field's toolchain is built around, so it falls between the computational and the analytic workflows. "No one thought of it" is not claimed.

## Scores

**Well-posedness: 3/3.** After the one permitted reformulation the order is fixed by an explicit rule (with the prime-q restriction that rule needs stated), the estimand is written out with its numerator and denominator, the index set of (q, m, a, n) is generated by a stated rule rather than left as a gestured list, and both the null calibration and the discriminating comparison are named. The contested presupposition is flagged and is the question's own variable rather than a smuggled premise. The point against: presupposition 3's regime is unverified in this record, so the clause "against the Hardy–Littlewood prediction transported to F_q[t]" is answerable only once that transport's validity range is stated — which the answer condition now requires in writing.

**Significance: 2/3.** The audience is real, named and currently active, the anchoring is to a live unsettled mechanism, and the fecundity is genuine on both the function-field and the order-theoretic side. Held to 2 rather than 3 because, by my own reasoning in presupposition 7 and not from any source, delta may be derivable from the gap distribution composed with the deterministic position-to-residue map, so a competent reader might get the leading behaviour in a page from existing F_q[t] correlation results — which would make the computation a confirmation of a short argument rather than a discovery. That is the first thing a Gate 4 triviality attack should test, and the search that would ground or refute it (for published order-dependent pair statistics in F_q[t]) was not run.

## Strongest remaining objection (for the judge and the adversaries)

Triviality by short derivation: with the order fixed, the class of a monic mod m is a deterministic function of its position, so delta is a composition of the irreducible gap distribution in that order with a known bijection — and a reader who already has the F_q[t] correlation asymptotics may be able to write the leading term down. This is my own reasoning about the problem's structure, not a source finding; it is also what makes the question well-posed, so an adversary must decide whether the derivation is a page or a paper, and should spend a query on order-dependent pair statistics over F_q[t] before ruling either way.
