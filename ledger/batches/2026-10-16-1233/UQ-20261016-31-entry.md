Domain(s): STEM — radio astronomy / spectrum engineering and satellite regulation; neighbouring: EMC and measurement-system metrology, RF emitter characterisation, optical satellite photometry
Question (canonical): For a cohort of individually identified LEO satellites (NORAD IDs) of one hardware model, observed with one instrument in fixed bands and a fixed beam/elevation configuration, with multiple passes per satellite per epoch and >=2 epochs >=6 months apart: (a) how does the variance of per-satellite band-limited radiated power (normalised to 1000 km) partition into pass-within-epoch, epoch-within-satellite, between-satellite-within-model and between-model components; (b) with model fixed, does epoch-level per-satellite emission change with on-orbit age; (c) what fraction of previously-detected satellites still in orbit fall below a stated, sensitivity-matched per-satellite upper limit at the later epoch? (Gate 1 reformulation; intake version asked whether UEMR is a fixed property of a hardware model or varies over a satellite's operational life, biasing snapshot-based aggregate forecasts.)
Alternative formulations: (1) Does a satellite's radio-noise leakage drift as it ages on orbit (thermal cycling, degradation, payload state, software updates), or is UEMR set per hardware generation at launch? (2) Are UEMR non-detections of previously-detected, still-in-orbit satellites explained by emission variability and duty cycle rather than by survey sensitivity?
Motivating gap: Anchor anomaly in Grigg et al. 2025 (arXiv:2506.02831): of 68 Starlink satellites detected by Di Vruno et al. 2023, "None of these 68 satellites were detected in our work even though 55 of them were still in orbit during this survey", with the emitting component stated to be unknown; the same null recurs for Starlink-3618 in Yang et al. 2026 (arXiv:2603.07631). Existing designs are snapshot-by-generation (Di Vruno 2023; Bassa et al. 2024), and the one within-satellite study (Dong et al. 2026, arXiv:2605.17150) explicitly declines the variance decomposition. NOTE (Gate 4, then corrected): the intake built this gap on a half-quote and never opened the anchor, which stands. But the stronger claim — that the anchor's own next sentences explain the non-redetections, so the gap does not exist — was checked against the primary papers and withdrawn. Grigg's two mechanisms are frequency non-overlap for the v1.0 narrowband lines and EDA2 sensitivity for the v1.5 broadband, assigned one per model class and covering 58 of the 68; they assert that the two campaigns are NOT COMPARABLE, which makes the non-redetection uninformative about emission change rather than evidence that the emission was unchanged; and no numerical upper limit is published for any of the 55 still-in-orbit non-redetections. The gap as written overstated the anomaly; what survives is that the episode is uninformative in both directions and that nobody has published the per-satellite limits that would make it informative.
Presuppositions: [established] individual emitters are trackable across epochs by NORAD ID (Bassa 2024 Tables B.1-B.3; Dong 2026 on the Grigg catalogue). [established] 55 of 68 were in orbit at the later epoch, so re-entry does not explain the nulls. [contested] per-epoch sensitivity can be made commensurable so a non-detection becomes a usable per-satellite upper limit. [contested] hardware is fixed on orbit, so within-satellite change is state, degradation or software (sub-model revisions inside a launch group would break the model-level control). [established] aggregate UEMR forecasts feed real decisions (IAU CPS/SKAO, ITU-R WP 7D, WRC-27 1.16/1.18); per-satellite limits are being proposed (arXiv:2608.11659, unopened). [contested] operator telemetry is needed only for attribution, not measurement — strained: Dong 2026 state operational state "is not directly observable from the ground". [flagged] the estimand is per-satellite radiated power, not the astronomer-facing aggregate; the two diverge if fleet composition changes concurrently (Mallama 2026). [contested — graded FAILED at Gate 4 on adversary 1's kill, re-graded after verification] that the 68 non-redetections are an unexplained anomaly, i.e. that variability versus sensitivity is an untested dichotomy. The dichotomy is indeed untested, but not because the anomaly is unexplained: Grigg offers two instrumental mechanisms that make the campaigns non-comparable, so the episode cannot discriminate between variability and sensitivity either way. The presupposition survives only in the weaker form that no published work bounds the non-detections, which adversary 2 re-derived independently.
What would count as an answer: A matched-sensitivity repeated-epoch campaign — one instrument, fixed bands and beam/elevation configuration, individually identified cohort, >=2 epochs months-to-years apart, multiple passes per satellite per epoch, publishing per-satellite flux densities and a stated upper limit for every non-detection — delivering (a) the four-level variance decomposition, (b) an age test with model fixed, (c) the upper-limited non-detection fraction. Either direction is informative: large epoch-within-satellite variance invalidates snapshot-derived forecasts and forces duty-cycle-aware monitoring and percentile-form limits; stability vindicates the snapshot design and per-satellite limits. A null at stated power is publishable.

Significance — consequence: If epoch-within-satellite variance is comparable to between-model variance, snapshot-based aggregate forecasts estimate the wrong quantity, generational contrasts are partly confounded with operating-state sampling, monitoring must characterise a distribution rather than a level, and per-satellite limits must be written as percentiles over operational states. If per-unit emission is stable, the snapshot design and per-satellite limits are vindicated. Discounted at Gate 4, then partly restored: Bassa 2024's "stability" is a population-distribution consistency remark about first-generation satellites as a group, demonstrated only above a bright floor (LBA S_nu < 10 Jy at 3 sigma against detections of 15 to 1300 Jy) in a paper that never links a single spacecraft across the two epochs and reports in the same breath that brightness varies from satellite to satellite — so the first-order version is not settled at version level, and the discount was too large. What replaces it is sharper and comes from adversary 2: "biased" is the wrong word, because aggregate band power is a sum and within-unit variance does not shift its mean; but the regulated functional under RA.1513-2 is an exceedance share of samples, not a mean, so per-satellite duty cycle and variance enter the regulated quantity directly. The practical stake is therefore real and sits in the monitoring specification — whether compliance can certify a unit from one pass or must sample its states to a percentile — rather than in restating aggregate forecasts.
