## Verification of the rejection ground at first hand (post-batch, 2026-10-21)

Two fresh-context readers, each handed one claim and its source and neither handed any reviewer's reasoning. The verdict moved: Rejected to Provisional.

**The ground falls.** The rejection rested on HSIB having put the candidate's own lay formulation — does one patient's delivered oxygen depend on the rest of the hospital's concurrent draw, and does it show up in their saturations — to a real incident. A reader with the full report (128,114 characters) found that it does not pose that question in any form. What the report contains is (i) a twice-stated, explicitly unresolved attribution question about whether one measured pressure drop caused two desaturations in one HDU, and (ii) five governance recommendations to NHSE/I and the CQC. The general quantity appears only twice and in neither case as a question: as asserted qualitative physics (a hosepipe/sprinkler analogy; "reduces the potential pressure available to oxygen outlets later in the system") and as A/2021/041, a Trust action installing estate-level flow meters. The term "diversity factor" appears nowhere in the report. An analogy is not a posed question, and an incident the report could not attribute is not the candidate's estimand asked before it.

**Three defects underneath the ground, each of a kind this ledger has catalogued.**

1. *The body is misattributed.* The report's own front matter says HSIB, the Healthcare Safety Investigation Branch. HSSIB is the successor body that hosts the page. The name was taken from the host rather than from the front matter, in this entry and in both adversary logs. Corrected to HSIB throughout this block; the citation in the prior list still reads HSSIB and is corrected here rather than by rewriting it.
2. *Both quoted fragments are severed at the hinge.* "unable to identify whether the pressure drop in the HDU was responsible" is verbatim but cut at a disjunction: the sentence could not identify whether the pressure drop was responsible OR whether other clinical factors were. The cut turns an even-handed non-determination into what reads like an unproven but live pipeline hypothesis. "unable to conclusively determine whether the desaturation" drops its entire predicate, stopping before all three branches — pressure loss, reduction in oxygen flow, other factors in their care. The two formulations are also not interchangeable: only the Executive Summary version mentions FLOW, while para 2.26 says pressure drop and CLINICAL factors, and neither carries a citation. This is the clipped-quotation defect that was the signature of the 2026-10-18 09:45 batch, found here on the sentence the verdict turned on.
3. *The figures were cited for the wrong measurand and circumstance.* 4.2 and 3.1 are pipeline pressure in bar in the HDU, recorded by the Trust during a deliberate Sunday pressure test after patients had been moved to redistribute flow. They are not flow, not L/min, not bedside, not from the original incident, and not compared against any prescribed setting. Presupposition P4 above is narrowed accordingly rather than withdrawn, because the comparison with the 3.45 bar calibration point is between like quantities.

**One near-miss recorded so it is not re-litigated.** The report notes that a red-dot plan was not working and that capacity calculations did not account for uneven demand. Those are absences in the TRUST'S PLANNING, not an acknowledged knowledge gap about delivered flow, and reading them as a flag of this candidate's gap would have routed a question nobody posed. Against the non-finding's weight, note also that the report states it "has not explored the clinical management of patients requiring oxygen therapy in this report" while resting its non-determination on being unable to exclude other clinical factors: the alternative it cannot rule out is one it did not examine.

**PMC8680427 is identified, and the nine-route failure was a routing failure, not a missing source.** It is a Clinical Communication on one hospital's medical-gas pipeline failure (volume 65, issue 11, pages 834-836 per Crossref-registered metadata; the publisher's served full-text page prints no volume, issue or pages, and the affiliations are unrecoverable from it). Its measurements are system-side throughout: bar at the wall outlets and at the pressure-reducing station, LPM of reducing-valve capacity, set against a headcount of patients concurrently drawing oxygen. So it is a close neighbour on the candidate's EXPOSURE and carries no evidence on the candidate's endpoint, delivered bedside flow against the prescribed flowmeter setting.
