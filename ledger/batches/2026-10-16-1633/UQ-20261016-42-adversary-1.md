## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take the published studies that certify a noninvasive or continuous blood-pressure device by comparing it against pressure read off a fluid-filled arterial catheter, under ISO 81060-3:2022 or whichever sibling standard governs that device class. Sort each study's reference lines into two bins — lines whose catheter-transducer system responds faithfully, and lines that ring or are over-damped — using a fast-flush test where one was recorded and a waveform-shape classifier otherwise, carrying the classifier's own error through. Then ask two things. (a) Separately for systolic, diastolic and mean pressure: how much of the total squared device-versus-reference disagreement sits in the bad-line bin, over and above the share of pairs that bin contributes? (b) For how many devices does the number the standard actually grades — bias and SD of paired differences against its threshold — cross that threshold if you throw the bad lines away?

In one line: the candidate proposes to audit the referee rather than the player, and to count how many certifications the audit overturns.

### The missed prior, stated precisely (quotes from the full text I opened)

Mikhailov & Fabián 2021 ask whether a fluid-filled catheter system is fit to serve as the **reference gauge** in clinical validation of automated noninvasive sphygmomanometers under ISO 81060-2:2018. Reported: a well-irrigated system at f_n = 38.8 Hz, ζ = 0.130 met the accuracy requirement with errors "in tenths of mmHg"; an air-bubble-affected system at f_n = 6.5 Hz, ζ = 0.281 deviated by "tens of mmHg"; repeated irrigation of the *same* system moved f_n "by tens of Hz." Their characterisation of the standard: "The reference measuring system is subject to requirements for a maximum permissible error of ±2 mmHg," and "the resonant frequency and damping coefficient must also be characterized." Conclusion: the fluid-filled system "can be a very promising reference pressure gauge," provided it is well irrigated; inverse filtering is not a universal fix "because the parameters change with every irrigation"; extension tubes "not recommended."

**Scope limits on my own use of this, flagged deliberately.** (i) The ±2 mmHg and characterisation requirements are *this paper's description of ISO 81060-2:2018*, not the standard's text, which I did not read; I quote the paper's line, not the clause. (ii) ISO 81060-2 governs **intermittent** automated sphygmomanometers. The candidate names ISO 81060-3:2022 for **continuous** devices, with "per-class analogues declared per dataset" — so 81060-2 is inside the candidate's frame for the intermittent class, but this finding does **not** transfer to the continuous class without the 81060-3 text. (iii) The paper has two catheter systems on a bench and **no device under test**: it measures the reference's own error, as the record's P2 does in silico. It performs no stratification of a validation dataset, no apportionment of pooled disagreement, and no pass/fail re-run.

### What ISO 81060-3:2022's published scope does and does not say (from the reseller reproduction)

It "specifies the requirements and methods for the clinical investigation of continuous automated non-invasive sphygmomanometers"; it is "not applicable to … invasive blood pressure monitoring equipment as given in IEC 60601-2-34"; and it "does not provide a method to assess the effect of artefacts during the clinical investigation." **Honest reading, against over-claiming in my own favour:** the IEC 60601-2-34 exclusion says what equipment the standard applies *to*, not what reference a trial may use, and I must not read it as a statement about the reference line. The artefacts sentence is the stronger point and is still not specifically about the reference. A scope that omits reference-system requirements is consistent with their absence but does not establish it. So: suggestive on the candidate's side for the continuous class, not proof.

## 4. Single strongest killing point

Mikhailov & Fabián 2021 (doi:10.14311/CTJ.2021.1.01, opened in full) is the record's own Alternative formulation 1 asked and partly answered — "can the fluid-filled catheter serve as the ISO 81060 reference gauge?" — in a clinical-engineering vocabulary that thirteen novelty queries missed, and it reports that ISO 81060-2:2018 already puts a ±2 mmHg maximum permissible error on the reference measuring system and already requires its resonant frequency and damping coefficient to be characterised. That simultaneously falsifies the Reason-for-silence claim that the standards put reference fidelity outside the analysis frame "by construction," proves the Gate 2 search record incomplete in the vocabulary nearest the question, and shows the committee already acts on this variable.
