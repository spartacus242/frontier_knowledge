Question (canonical):
V1 (intake, as supplied): How much of the shape and spread of published labour-progress curves, and of the diagnosed incidence of labour dystocia, is produced by measurement error in digitally assessed cervical dilatation rather than by variation in labour itself, and what do the curves and the dystocia thresholds become when that error is modelled explicitly?

V2 (Gate 1 reformulation — the one permitted, for well-posedness only; this is the canonical form): In a labour-curve dataset with per-examination dilatation records (Consortium on Safe Labor, WHO BOLD, or the Labour Progression Study), under (i) an error model for digital dilatation estimated from paired same-time-point measurements — replicate independent examiners as the PRIMARY identification route, an instrumented/ultrasound reference as secondary — comprising an error variance, examiner random effects and an explicit whole-centimetre rounding (coarsening) process, and (ii) a jointly fitted informative-observation-time model for the examination schedule: (a) what is the shift in the median and 95th-centile dilatation-versus-time curves and in the implied action line, stated relative to the published naive fit and to that fit's own confidence bands; (b) what proportion of recorded labour-dystocia / failure-to-progress diagnoses change classification under the error-corrected curve at the published threshold, applied mechanically as written; and (c) how do (a) and (b) decompose between the measurement-error component and the observation-timing component?

Why the reformulation: V1's "how much of the shape and spread" names no estimand, no dataset and no comparator, and its threshold limb ("what the dystocia thresholds become") is normative rather than estimable. V2 fixes the measurand, names the identification route that survives the contested gold-standard presupposition, makes the comparison against the published fit's own confidence bands (so a negative answer is a result), and makes limb (b) a mechanical reclassification count against the written threshold — which keeps clinical deviation from the threshold out of the estimand rather than assuming it away.

Measurand declaration (load-bearing, required by V2): true dilatation is defined procedurally — the maximal internal diameter of the cervical os that an examiner using a stated standardised technique and examination pressure would obtain. This is the metrological move that makes the error/true-variation decomposition clean despite the cervix being deformable: without it, replicate examiners partly measure different physical quantities and "error variance" silently absorbs real mechanical perturbation.

Alternative formulations:
1. If cervical dilatation were measured without examiner error, would the partogram's action line and the diagnosis of failure to progress move, and for how many women?
2. Is "slow labour" partly an artefact of how dilatation is measured rather than of how labour proceeds?

Motivating gap:
Source method: presupposition inversion. The settled claim: modern labour curves (Zhang et al.; the WHO Labour Care Guide) established that normal labour is slower and more variable than Friedman's partogram assumed, and labour dystocia / failure to progress — the commonest indication for intrapartum caesarean — is diagnosed against them. Every one of those curves is built from digital estimates of cervical dilatation. The untested presupposition is that digital assessment is a measurement of a physical quantity whose error is small relative to the between-woman variation the curves describe. The field has instrumented the measurement (intrapartum ultrasound validation; commercial cervicometry devices and patents) and documented that digital examination disagrees with it, and has published digital-examination error magnitudes since 1995 — but nothing found propagates an error model back into a labour-curve dataset. Two features make the error non-classical, so the inversion bites rather than merely adding noise: dilatation is recorded almost exclusively at whole centimetres (coarsening, not Gaussian noise), and examinations are performed more often when progress is doubted (informative observation times). Gate 2 also surfaced the live anomaly this would speak to: the partograph's alert and action lines have near-null diagnostic accuracy for severe adverse birth outcomes, and the field has no accepted explanation.

Presuppositions:
1. A reference measurement substantially more accurate than digital examination exists — CONTESTED. Instrumented/ultrasound dilatation is itself imperfect. V2 therefore declares the replicate-independent-examiner design as the primary identification route; replicate designs identify error variance with no gold standard, and the instrument enters only as a secondary cross-check. This presupposition is now load-bearing for the secondary route only.
2. The error distribution is estimable — i.e. either independent of true dilatation or with an estimable dependence — CONTESTED. Examiners may round toward decision-relevant values (9 cm, "fully"), making the error differential with respect to the very outcome being diagnosed. Partially addressed: a rounding process with true-dilatation-dependent thresholds is estimable from replicates plus the heaping pattern, but differential rounding toward a decision the examiner has already made is only weakly identified and must be declared as a bounded sensitivity analysis, not a fitted component.
3. Examination times are accurately recorded and the examination schedule is conditionally ignorable — CONTESTED and probably false. Treated not as an assumption but as the second, separable artefact, modelled explicitly; limb (c) exists precisely to keep it separable from limb (a).
4. Digit preference at whole-centimetre values is present and measurable in the source data — NOT VERIFIED by this Gate 2. Query 3 aimed squarely at it and returned only partogram teaching material; no published quantitative digit-preference analysis of dilatation records was found. Must be verified in the source data as step 0 of any attempt, not assumed. (Note the asymmetry: if recording is essentially 100% whole-centimetre, that is coarsening with no heaping signal to exploit, and the rounding process is identified only from replicates.)
5. Dystocia thresholds are applied as written, so reclassification counts mean something clinically — CONTESTED. The decision is also driven by fetal status, maternal exhaustion and unit culture. V2 narrows the estimand to a mechanical reclassification count against the written threshold, which is well defined regardless; the clinical meaning of that count is a separate, acknowledged interpretive step and must not be overclaimed.
6. "True dilatation" is well defined at a time point — defensible for a physical aperture, but the cervix is deformable. Handled by the procedural measurand declaration above; the declaration is a required part of the design, not a footnote.

What would count as an answer:
Two linked deliverables, with a pre-declared analysis plan.
(i) An error model for digital dilatation fitted to paired measurements at the same time point — replicate independent examiners (primary) or digital versus a reference instrument (secondary) — in labouring women, large enough to estimate error variance, examiner random effects and the whole-centimetre coarsening process, with the measurand stated and error variance reported as a function of true dilatation (error is unlikely to be homoscedastic across 1–10 cm).
(ii) Re-estimation of a published labour-curve dataset (CSL, WHO BOLD, or LaPS) under that error model AND under an informative-observation-time model, reporting: the shift in the median and 95th-centile dilatation-versus-time curves; the shift in the implied action line; the proportion of dystocia diagnoses reclassified at the published threshold; and the decomposition of each between the two artefacts.
A negative answer is a result and must be pre-committed as such: movement entirely within the published curves' own confidence bands answers the question negatively, retires the field's commonest standing objection to partogram-based dystocia diagnosis, and is as publishable as movement outside them.

Significance — consequence:
If YES (measurement error and informative timing account for a large share): the published curves' 95th centiles are inflated and their shape partly flattened by examiner noise and whole-centimetre coarsening, which means the WHO Labour Care Guide's centimetre-by-centimetre expected durations and any action line derived from them are calibrated partly to the instrument rather than to labour. The implied response is not a revised threshold — recalibrating a threshold to noisier data does not remove the noise — but either a different measurement or a decision rule whose operating characteristics are robust to ±1 cm. It would also mean that a share of caesareans for dystocia is attributable to examination error, a quantity no one has.
If NO (the corrected curves move within the published fit's own confidence bands): the commonest standing objection to partogram-based dystocia diagnosis — that dilatation cannot be measured well enough for the thresholds to mean anything — is quantitatively retired. That strengthens the Labour Care Guide, redirects attention to the real source of the alert/action lines' poor diagnostic accuracy, and substantially weakens the clinical case for instrumented cervicometry, whose argument currently rests on agreement statistics alone. Both directions change a decision; neither is a null result in the uninteresting sense.

Significance — audience:
1. WHO's intrapartum-care guideline group (Labour Care Guide) and the national bodies that set dystocia criteria against these curves (NICE; ACOG/SMFM "Safe prevention of the primary cesarean delivery") — they would learn whether their thresholds are calibrated to examiner noise, and the answer changes whether the right fix is a threshold, a measurement, or a robust rule.
2. The CSL, BOLD and LaPS analysis teams — they hold the data and already use interval-censored and threshold-regression machinery (P2 proves access and feasibility); a re-estimation is within their existing capability and would be reported as a sensitivity analysis on their own headline curves.
3. Manufacturers and regulators of instrumented cervicometry (Gate 2 returned multiple patents and a vendor white paper) — their clinical case is currently an agreement statistic. A demonstrated shift in curve or diagnosis is the evidence they lack; a demonstrated non-shift is the evidence against them. Either way they would act.
4. Measurement-error and informative-visit biostatisticians (the field that owns the method, per query 7) — a high-stakes applied instance combining non-classical coarsened error with an informative observation process on a real decision threshold.

Significance — fecundity:
Newly askable: what is the minimum dilatation measurement precision at which a given action line retains its stated operating characteristics — i.e. a performance specification for cervicometry devices, which currently have none and are validated against agreement rather than against decision quality; whether EXAMINER IDENTITY is an exposure in dystocia diagnosis and caesarean risk (a variation and equity question with a tractable design); whether part of the Friedman-versus-Zhang difference is a measurement-PRACTICE difference (examiner continuity, examination frequency, rounding convention) rather than a population or obstetric-practice difference, which would reopen a settled historical dispute on new grounds; and whether the other threshold-based intrapartum measurements built from the same digital examination — station, effacement, position — carry the same artefact into the decisions that rest on them.

Significance — anchoring:
Touches a live, unexplained anomaly surfaced by this Gate 2: the partograph's alert and action lines have near-null diagnostic accuracy for severe adverse birth outcomes, and cervical dilatation over time is a poor predictor of them (BOLD-era diagnostic-accuracy work, query 10) — with no accepted explanation. Measurement error is an obvious candidate explanation that nobody has quantified. It also anchors to the unexplained persistence of large inter-unit variation in caesarean-for-dystocia rates, and to P1's standing and unanswered claim that these regression methods "should not be routinely used to define labour progress", which left the field with a criticism and no replacement.

Reason for silence:
A disciplinary boundary with an incentive gap on top of it, not ignorance. The error magnitudes have been in the obstetric literature since 1995 and are routinely cited as a limitation; both P3 and Roper 2023 state the downstream implication explicitly and then decline to compute it. So the premise is common knowledge in the field and the silence is specifically about the join. Three communities hold the three pieces and none owns the junction: the error data sit with phantom-study authors, simulation-training groups and device validators; the curve datasets sit with large consortium epidemiology groups; and the technique required — a coarsened-data measurement-error model with examiner random effects, fitted jointly with an informative observation process — sits in biostatistics, where (query 7) it is a mature named family with no obstetric application. The incentive runs the wrong way as well: the curve datasets' value is their descriptive authority, and an error-corrected re-analysis can only widen or blur them, so the curve authors have no reason to run it; device vendors want an agreement statistic, not a demonstration that the agreement barely moves the decision; and guideline bodies have just finished rebuilding their thresholds on these curves. P2 is the diagnostic case: a statistician did reach CSL, did correct measurement error — on the time axis — and the work stayed in an unpublished dissertation.
