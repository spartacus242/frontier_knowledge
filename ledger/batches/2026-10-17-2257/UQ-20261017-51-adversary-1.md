## Restatement (my own words)

Hatton et al. (PNAS 2023) tallied every cell type in one reference human and found that, when you sort cells into doubling bins of per-cell mass, each bin holds about the same total mass (count falls as roughly mass^-1). The candidate asks: is that flat biomass-by-size-class profile a property of animal bodies generally, or just of the human tissue mix? Concretely (V2): census two or more other species spanning at least four orders of magnitude in body mass, fit each body's count-vs-mass slope with a size-spectrum estimator and a pre-declared "what is one cell" rule, test whether the slopes equal -1 and equal the human -0.97, test whether slope depends on body mass, see how much of the flatness survives removing muscle and fat, and check whether equal-mass bins are also equal-energy bins.
