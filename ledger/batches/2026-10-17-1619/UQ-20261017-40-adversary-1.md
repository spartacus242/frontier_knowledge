# UQ-20261017-40 — Adversary 1 log

## Restatement (my own words)

Chart-review instruments that count diagnostic errors judge an index encounter against what the record later says. If the later record is partly a copy of the index clinician's own label (inherited diagnosis / diagnostic momentum), the instrument will under-count, because the record agrees with itself. The candidate proposes: take one case set, adjudicate it twice under a pre-registered protocol — once with the full record, once with the index encounter's diagnostic assertions and every traceable downstream copy of them blacked out (but downstream independent findings kept) — and report the signed paired change in the classified error rate, a measured blinding-success index, inter-annotator agreement on the "traceable copy" tagging, and the same contrast on a subset with an index-independent truth (autopsy, external re-adjudication, independent registry) to fix the sign.

Generic form of the question: *does a reviewer's knowledge of the index interpretation suppress the error rate a retrospective review instrument measures, and by how much?*

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional.** No attack is Unresolved; attacks 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 are Partial. Novelty does not hold at Medium: the generic question (reviewer knowledge of the index interpretation suppresses the measured error rate) has been asked and meta-analytically quantified in radiology QA (Wu et al., Radiology 2014: unblinded 2.0% vs blinded 12.1% major discrepancies, across-study, five blinded studies, CI 4.4–29.4%), which the record's 12 queries missed; the candidate survives only as the chart-review-specific, contaminated-reference, paired variant. Promotion conditions: (1) add Wu 2014 and its five blinded primary studies as the nearest prior and restate the novelty claim narrowly; (2) address instrument compatibility of arm B (can Safer Dx-type process items be scored with the index assessment redacted?); (3) strike or restate anchoring (a), since the Safer Dx ~30% is a trigger-enriched sample; (4) read Zwaan & Singh 2015 (still COULD NOT READ on both sides); (5) re-verify Blume 2026 via a publisher route (doi.org failed for me).

Single strongest killing point: Wu et al., Radiology 2014 — the blinding-of-the-reference-reader effect on a measured error rate is already quantified in radiology peer review (2.0% unblinded vs 12.1% blinded), so the record's central negative ("nobody has estimated this bias on an error-measurement instrument") is false as stated and holds only for chart-review instruments with a record-derived reference.
