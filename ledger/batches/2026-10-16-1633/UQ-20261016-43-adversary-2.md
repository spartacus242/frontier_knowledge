## Restatement (my own words, before engaging the record's framing)

Every published "our catalyst held X mV for Y hours" is, formally, a lifetime observation that was cut off at a time the author picked. So: (1) if you collect the Y's across the oxygen-evolution literature, what does that distribution look like, and how much of it sits exactly on human round numbers; (2) if you declare one failure line in advance (overpotential has risen by a fixed Delta from that run's own start) and treat runs that never crossed it as right-censored, what survival curve and what Weibull/lognormal fit can the corpus actually sustain; (3) did authors stop *because* of what they were watching — does the slope at the end of the trace predict where the trace ends, once you condition on lab/year/journal/current density/cell type; and (4) given the fitted distribution, how many hours would a test have to run to tell two catalysts apart at 80% power.

In one line: **are published durability numbers measurements of catalysts, or measurements of when chemists stop watching — and can the corpus tell the difference?**
