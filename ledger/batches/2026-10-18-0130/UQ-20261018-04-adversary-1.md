## Restatement (my own words)

Hospitals that barcode/RFID-tag individual instruments now know, for each instrument, how many times it has been through decontamination and sterilisation and which operation it went into. The candidate asks: does an instrument's "mileage" (cumulative reprocessing cycles, and calendar age) at the moment it is used predict three different bad things — (a) that instrument breaking or being swapped out during the operation (instrument-level), (b) the tray or sterilizer load it was in failing a sterilisation check such as a biological indicator, wet pack or recall (load-level), and (c) the patient of that operation developing a surgical-site infection within 30/90 days (case-level) — after adjusting for who the surgeon was, what service line, how complex the case, and what tray type, with a negative-control exposure and a pre-stated minimum detectable effect for each limb. The candidate's premise is that nobody has ever used per-item cycle count as an exposure because attention went to flexible endoscopes and rigid-instrument life is governed by bench-validated cycle limits.

## Recommended verdict

**Rejected** — not on a Gate 4 Unresolved (none of attacks 1, 2, 4 reached Unresolved on the evidence I could source) but because the Gate 2 ground stands and is strengthened: the candidate's load-bearing premise that per-item cycle count from tracking logs has never been used as an exposure is false twice over (Yoshikawa 2019, verified on the publisher page; Homma 2023, newly found at Gate 4 and verified via DOAJ), its alternative formulation 1 is the title of P1, and novelty therefore remains Low, which blocks Validated regardless of the Gate 4 table. On Gate 4 alone the entry would cap at Provisional (Partials on 1–4, 6–8; nothing Unresolved).

Routing: the unoccupied residue — limbs (ii) load/tray-level sterilisation failure and (iii) NHSN SSI with item/tray history as exposure — found no prior in 20 queries across two gates and could be re-intaken as a standalone candidate, provided it drops the "never used as an exposure" premise and states a mechanism and a power calculation for limb (iii). Promotion condition for any re-intake: open and read BMC Surg 2024 10.1186/s12893-024-02407-1 (three routes failed; three listed above remain untried).

Single strongest killing point: the "newly measurable quantity" (per-item cumulative reprocessing/use count from an instrument-tracking log) has been the exposure in two field studies of in-use instruments in the candidate's own field — Yoshikawa 2019 (34,390 operations; OR 1.026 per use, R² 0.032; retirement thresholds 169/224 uses) and Homma 2023 (69 devices; cleanings 281 vs 261, P = 0.27; age 7 vs 7 y, P = 0.90) — and both found the exposure weak or null, which simultaneously falsifies the motivating gap and undercuts presupposition 2.
