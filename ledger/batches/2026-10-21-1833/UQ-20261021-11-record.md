(Gate 1 version V1, as intaken, recorded not graded: "Across k independently seeded pretraining runs of one fixed recipe (identical architecture, data, data order and hyperparameters, differing only in the initialisation/dropout RNG), is the IDENTITY of the residual-stream channels that carry massive activations — the specific dimension indices, not their count or layer profile — reproduced beyond a declared null, and is the per-channel magnitude ordering preserved?" Why the reformulation was needed (well-posedness only, no change of subject): V1 left three things underspecified that decide the answer rather than refine it. (i) "Per-channel magnitude ordering" is undefined when two seeds' top-m sets differ — a rank correlation needs a common support, so V2 pins the primary ordering statistic to the full d_model magnitude vector, which is well defined because the architecture and d_model are shared, and demotes the top-m-restricted version to a declared sensitivity arm on the union of the sets. (ii) Massive activations in the source literature are heavily concentrated on particular token positions, so a per-channel magnitude aggregated over all tokens mixes two regimes; V2 declares the token-position classes separately. (iii) V1's "a declared null" permits the free null; V2 names both nulls and makes the at-initialisation ranking the primary matched null, because if index identity is fixed at initialisation then the at-init ranking — not a uniform permutation — is the reference the claim has to beat. Nothing was added to the endpoint and nothing was weakened.

Alternative formulations:
(1) When you train the same model twice, do the same few "hot" dimensions light up, or different ones?
(2) Is a transformer's outlier-channel layout a predictable property of the recipe that a numeric format or kernel can be designed around in advance, or is it per-checkpoint symmetry breaking that must be re-measured for every artifact?
(3) Is the residual-stream index of a massive-activation channel a recipe-level constant, an initialisation-level constant, or a per-run lottery ticket?

Presuppositions:
P1 The residual-stream basis is privileged enough for channel-index identity to be a meaningful quantity (elementwise RMSNorm gains, elementwise activations, per-channel quantization scales all act in the standard basis). The elementwise-op argument is established by construction. The STRENGTH of the privilege is CONTESTED in interpretability; the relevant source, "Privileged Bases in the Transformer Residual Stream" (transformer-circuits.pub/2023/privileged-basis), was surfaced at Q1 and is NOT OPENED, so it is flagged as contested rather than resolved either way. Note the asymmetry that makes the candidate cheaper than its MoE and SAE analogues: because the basis is literally shared between seeds of one recipe, the candidate needs NO cross-run matching step, unlike expert matching in MoE or dictionary matching in SAEs.
P2 Massive-activation channels are individuable by a pre-declared threshold rule and the result is not an artifact of the threshold. CONTESTED and handled by construction: the figure is re-derived at >= 3 thresholds drawn from the source literature's own rules. Gate 2 sharpened this into a real hazard: the individuation rules in the literature are not commensurable. Puccetti et al. require magnitude PLUS a functional-damage criterion ("at least 5x more damaging effect on accuracy on a representative downstream task", their §2, with a magnitude gate of "within 2 standard deviations from the mean for RoBERTa, and within 3 for BERT"), whereas arXiv:2605.06611 uses a pure concentration ratio, "the ratio of the maximum absolute magnitude to the mean absolute magnitude", and arXiv:2603.05498 gives no numeric cutoff at all, only "These outliers, which often exceed typical activation scales by several orders of magnitude". So the threshold arm must span a damage-based rule and a magnitude-based rule, not three cut points of one rule.
P3 k >= 3 (preferably >= 5) runs of one recipe differing only in RNG are obtainable at a scale where the phenomenon reproduces. PARTLY ESTABLISHED and partly CONTESTED, now with a measured ceiling: PolyPythias (opened) supplies "9 new seeds across 5 model sizes, from 14M to 410M parameters", 10 seeds per size counting the original — so k >= 5 at >= 2 scales is obtainable off the shelf, but only up to 410M. Whether small-scale (100M-1B) channel behaviour transfers to the 7B+ checkpoints that quantization toolchains actually target remains CONTESTED and is not resolvable from any public seed suite, because none exists at 7B+. The question is therefore answerable at >= 2 scales, as required, while remaining silent about the regime that matters commercially — a limitation of the obtainable answer, not of the question.
P4 The stack can hold data order fixed while varying initialisation (separate RNG streams). FLAGGED, and Gate 2 turned the flag into a named confound in the only suite that exists: PolyPythias varies the seeds for "parameter initialisation and batch composition", and says it uses "batch composition" instead of "data order" because the codebase shuffles documents before packing them. So the off-the-shelf route confounds initialisation with data order and cannot answer V2 as written; V2 either requires purpose-run seeds with split RNG streams, or must be run on PolyPythias as a strictly weaker question (joint init-plus-order contingency) with the confound declared in advance.
P5 "Beyond chance" has a defensible null. CONTESTED and the verdict depends on which null is used; a uniform index-permutation null is nearly free to beat. V2 makes the at-initialisation per-channel magnitude ranking of the same recipe the primary matched null, which is constructible and is the stringent one if index identity is set at init; the uniform permutation is retained as the floor so the result is comparable to how the surrounding literature would report it.
P6 Quantization/kernel engineers would act on the answer. NOT EVIDENCED, and Gate 2 did not improve it. Current practice re-calibrates per checkpoint, which is consistent with either answer; the demand is inferred from the design of outlier-aware formats, not demonstrated. The two titles that would bear on it — "Coverage-Based Calibration for Post-Training Quantization via Weighted Set Cover over Outlier Channels" (arXiv:2604.24008) and "Outliers and Calibration Sets have Diminishing Effect on Quantization of Modern LLMs" (arXiv:2405.20835) — are NOT OPENED; the second's title points the other way and is a live threat to significance, not a support.

What would count as an answer:
(i) k >= 3 (preferably >= 5) seed replicas of one recipe at >= 2 scales, with data order held fixed by a separate RNG stream (or, if run on PolyPythias, with the init/order confound declared before any pull and the weaker joint question stated as the one being answered);
(ii) per-layer Jaccard overlap of the top-m channel index sets across all seed pairs with CIs, plus Spearman rho on the full per-channel magnitude vector (and on the union of the top-m sets as the declared sensitivity arm), each computed separately per token-position class, and each scored against BOTH the uniform-permutation null and the statistics-matched null (at-init ranking; different-corpus run), re-derived at >= 3 thresholds spanning a magnitude rule and a functional-damage rule;
(iii) a pre-declared decision rule: overlap indistinguishable from the matched null means index identity is seed-contingent, so any format, kernel or calibration that bakes in a channel layout must be re-derived per checkpoint and cannot be shared across a model family, and dimension-level interpretability claims are claims about one artifact; overlap at or near 1 means the layout is recipe-determined, and the next question is whether it is predictable from the recipe without training. A clean negative counts equally. A third reportable outcome is a reproducible core plus a seed-contingent remainder, localised by layer and token-position class.
NOT answers: within-model cross-layer persistence of a dominant dimension (already published); cross-architecture or cross-family index comparisons (indices are not commensurable); overlap reported only against a uniform permutation null; agreement across CHECKPOINTS of one run (Puccetti's "the same for all the checkpoints for this seed" is this, and it is a within-run statement).

Significance — consequence: If index identity is reproduced at or near 1, the outlier-channel layout is a property of the recipe rather than of the artifact, and three things currently done per checkpoint become amortisable in principle: a numeric format's or kernel's channel layout could be fixed once per recipe family rather than re-derived per release; calibration could be warm-started or skipped for a known recipe; and a dimension-level mechanistic claim would be a claim about the recipe. The immediately askable follow-on is whether the layout is predictable from the recipe without training at all. If overlap is indistinguishable from the matched null, the index is symmetry breaking: every proposal that bakes a channel layout into a format, kernel or shared calibration must re-derive it per artifact and cannot ship it across a family, and every published statement attributing a role to a specific residual dimension is a statement about one run — which would also mean the at-init null is the one that matters, i.e. the index is a lottery ticket drawn before any data is seen. The likeliest outcome, a reproducible core plus a seed-contingent remainder localised by layer and token-position class, is the number a layout-sharing decision actually needs and nobody has.
Significance — audience: (1) low-precision inference and numeric-format designers — per-channel scale schemes, microscaling block formats, rotation-based outlier removal, and outlier-channel selection methods of the arXiv:2604.24008 kind, who would learn whether their per-model calibration step is necessary or merely conventional; (2) interpretability groups that attribute function to specific residual-stream dimensions, and the privileged-basis line directly, since a cross-seed index-overlap figure is a measurement of how privileged the standard basis is that does not need a matching step; (3) pretraining groups that release seed suites (EleutherAI/PolyPythias, OLMo) who already hold the artifacts and would learn that a per-dimension analysis is worth shipping, and specifically that splitting the init and batch-composition RNG streams in future suites costs nothing and unlocks this question; (4) architecture designers trying to suppress or relocate massive activations (sink tokens, gated attention, norm placement), for whom a seed-contingent index means their interventions must be evaluated distributionally.
Held at 2/3, argued not asserted: the discount is P6. Current practice re-calibrates per checkpoint and is cheap, so the yes-branch saves little and the no-branch merely confirms what the toolchain already does; the strongest single threat is a title I did not open, arXiv:2405.20835 "Outliers and Calibration Sets have Diminishing Effect on Quantization of Modern LLMs", which if correct would shrink the engineering stake further. What keeps it at 2 rather than 1 is that the no-branch is a genuine negative result against a live design idea (shared/static layouts) and against dimension-level interpretability claims, and that the audience is identifiable, named, and already holds the runs.
Significance — fecundity: (a) if recipe-determined, is the index predictable before training from initialisation statistics, embedding geometry or the tokenizer's frequency profile — and can a channel be deliberately DESIGNATED as the outlier channel so layouts become shareable by construction? (b) if seed-contingent, at what point in training is the index fixed, and is it already determined at initialisation (the at-init null answers this as a by-product)? (c) what IS invariant across seeds — count, layer profile, magnitude distribution, token-position concentration — and does the invariant part suffice for format design? (d) does index reproducibility track the strength of the privileged basis, giving the privileged-basis debate a measurable quantity? (e) does a seed-contingent index predict anything about the run, e.g. quantization sensitivity or terminal loss?
Significance — anchoring: the privileged-basis question in interpretability; the attention-sink / massive-activation emergence line (arXiv:2410.10781, arXiv:2603.05498, arXiv:2605.06611), which explains WHY the channels exist and never asks WHICH; permutation symmetry and linear mode connectivity, where the residual stream is the one place the symmetry is already broken elementwise, making it the natural test bed; the quantization literature's unexamined convention of per-model outlier calibration; and the ledger's own UQ-20261017-06, whose MoE analogue needs expert matching where this needs none.

Reason for silence: a disciplinary boundary that runs exactly through this quantity, plus a narrow instrument window. The two communities that hold the phenomenon each have a structural reason not to ask. Quantization and kernel engineers only ever possess one artifact at a time — they receive a released checkpoint, calibrate on it and ship — so a cross-seed question is not expressible inside their workflow; and because per-checkpoint recalibration is cheap, the answer would not change what they do tomorrow, which removes the incentive to spend pretraining compute on it. Interpretability built the cross-run universality machinery (Prior 3, 2024) for objects where cross-run MATCHING is the headline difficulty — MLP neurons, SAE dictionaries — and the residual-stream index needs no matching at all, so inside that community the question looks too easy to be a paper while inside the other it looks too architectural to be theirs. The instrument window is also short: massive activations were named as a phenomenon in 2024 and the only public multi-seed suite at >= 2 scales appeared in March 2025 from a group whose stated interest was run-level stability, not activation geometry — and that suite ties initialisation to batch composition, so even the group that owns the runs cannot answer the clean version without new ones. The mechanism is therefore boundary-plus-instrument, not "no one thought of it": the mechanism-side papers in 2025-2026 ablated ten recipe factors on this exact phenomenon (arXiv:2410.10781) and ablated architecture configurations one seed at a time (arXiv:2606.20743, by its own stated limitation), which is what a field looks like when it treats the RNG as nuisance rather than as a factor.
