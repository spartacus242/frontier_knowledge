time for the sensor after insertion". The opening words of the clause it sits in SCOPE it to the
gap-accounting rule - it tells the analyst what does NOT count as a gap. Logical form: it is an
EXEMPTION from a completeness criterion, not an exclusion of readings. It RECORDS A MOVE AWAY from
treating warm-up as missing data; it does not ASSERT that warm-up readings are discarded as
artifact. Read as an artifact rule it would be a misreading of its modality and its direction.

## DEFECTS FOUND

1. Nothing in these four documents contradicts the entry's premise. On the terms swept, no
   artifact, compression, pressure, signal-quality or trace-exclusion rule for the SENSOR TRACE is
   pre-specified in any of the four. The entry's premise is TRUE OVER THE INSTANCES I OPENED.

2. The entry is nevertheless gradeable only as instanced, not as established. It is a universal
   over a kind; I opened four documents from three groups and name above the ones I did not open.
   Anyone who restates this as "CGM SAPs do not pre-specify artifact exclusion" has written an
   unenumerated universal, which this ledger has twice seen falsified on the one instance nobody
   opened.

3. A NEAR-NEIGHBOUR THAT WOULD MISLEAD IF COLLAPSED, with the overlap in both directions.
   D3's Section 6.2 rule is a genuine pre-specified POINT-LEVEL exclusion from a CGM analysis.
   NUMERATOR CLASS of that rule, quoted with its exclusions: paired data points whose "Flagged
   reference measurement as indicated in source documentation (e.g., expired membrane, expired
   calibration, dilute sample)", plus pairs carrying intake protocol deviations "(e.g. hematocrit
   out of window, visit out of window, recent use of tetracycline/sorbitol/mannitol, DKA or Severe
   hypoglycemia during the past 30 days)". DENOMINATOR: all paired sensor-reference measurements in
   the per-protocol effectiveness population, checked against the near neighbours it is not - it is
   not all sensor readings, and not the evaluable population, which is gated separately at "a
   minimum of three paired glucose readings" per participant.
   WHAT THAT CLASS MISSES relative to the claim's quantity: every artifact of the sensor trace
   itself - compression lows, pressure-induced drops, early-wear drift, dropout - none of which is
   named or flagged anywhere in the document.
   WHAT IT INCLUDES that the claim excludes: assay-side and eligibility-side defects (membrane,
   calibration and dilution of the YSI sample, hematocrit, drug interferents), which are not
   sensor-trace artifacts at all.
   So the rule is one word away from the claim's quantity and is a different quantity. It would be
   a defect to cite D3 as a counter-instance, and equally a defect to say point-level exclusion
   machinery is absent from this kind of document: it exists and it is pointed at the comparator.

4. A SECOND NEAR NEIGHBOUR. D4's useable-day machinery (80% of 288 points, no gap >= 2 hours,
   10 useable days, linear interpolation under 2 hours, MI for the primary) is a COMPLETENESS and
   MISSINGNESS system, not a quality system. The entry is right to keep these apart. But a
   candidate who asserted that CGM SAPs contain no pre-specified sensor-trace data-handling rules
   at all would be FALSE on D4. The gap the entry points at is narrower than that: it is
   specifically artifact and signal-validity, inside an otherwise detailed data-handling chapter.

5. LIMITS ON MY OWN ABSENCE FINDINGS, stated so they are not read as stronger than they are.
   (i) D2 got ONE multi-term sweep and no dedicated second sweep; its absence finding is the
   weakest of the four. (ii) The two Senseonics PDFs serve garbled ligatures - the D3 cover renders
   as "Sta?s?cal Analysis Plan" - so a literal sweep could in principle miss a term containing ti
   or fi. "artifact" and "compression" contain neither, so the central terms are safe; "calibration"
   does, and it was nonetheless found in D3 and reported absent in D2, which I flag rather than
   smooth over. (iii) D1 was read in windows, not paged end to end. (iv) D4 is the only one I can
   call effectively complete, and it is also the most on-regime, which is the best news in this
   note for the entry.

6. MY OWN REASONING, LABELLED: that an absent term implies an absent rule is an inference, not a
   quotation. A SAP could in principle delegate artifact handling to a device data-processing
   standard operating procedure or a vendor data-export specification without ever using these
   words. Nothing I opened does so, and I ran no search that could have settled it; I name the
   document kind that would in (e) below.

(e) WHAT KIND OF DOCUMENT WOULD SETTLE THIS DEFINITIVELY. The kind is: a CGM-endpoint trial's
FINAL, SIGNED SAP together with its CGM DATA-PROCESSING SPECIFICATION - the analyst-facing
derivation document (often an appendix, a data-handling or derived-variable specification, or the
core-lab/CGM-reading-center SOP) that states how raw sensor files become the analysed glucose
series. My reason: all four documents I opened locate glucose handling in a data-conventions
chapter, and D1 shows the rules can be delegated downstream ("a minimum amount of data as defined
in the SAP") - so the delegation target, not the protocol, is where an artifact rule would sit if
it exists anywhere.
IS ANYTHING I CAN NAME THAT KIND? Honestly: only partly. A CGM reading center's data-processing SOP
(for instance the kind a coordinating center such as Jaeb would hold) IS that kind, but I have not
opened one and do not know that any is public - naming it is A GUESS ABOUT AVAILABILITY, and I call
it one. A posted Prot_SAP bundle for a CGM-primary-endpoint trial IS that kind and IS reachable: the
search that found these four returned further such bundles, and two more from new groups would
discharge the promotion condition above. Journal supplementary appendices are the same kind as D4
and would add instances without changing the kind.
