## Restatement in my own words

Weld and component inspection archives contain, for some locations, calls from more than one physically different NDT method (film/digital radiography, manual UT, phased-array UT, eddy current), and nobody ever cut those locations open, so the true flaw state is unknown. The question asks: treat the true state at a location as a hidden binary variable ("a flaw at or above a declared size class is present here"), treat each method as an imperfect classifier of it, and ask whether the standard epidemiological trick — Hui–Walter: two or more imperfect tests applied to the same units across two or more subpopulations whose flaw prevalence differs but whose method accuracies are assumed equal — pins down each method's hit rate and false-alarm rate per size class, with the correlation structure between methods declared in advance rather than assumed away. Then, where those numbers come out identified, ask how they compare with whatever detection-performance basis the specific assessment route in use actually consumes — with the route and its basis documented first rather than assumed to be a POD curve. A clean "not identifiable in real archives, and here is the extra modality / stratum / small truth sample that would identify it" is accepted as an answer.

In one line: can inspection accuracy be read off disagreement among methods instead of off known cracks, and does the resulting number contradict the certified basis?
