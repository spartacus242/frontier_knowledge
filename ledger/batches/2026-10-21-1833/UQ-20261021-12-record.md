Question (canonical) — V2, the one Gate 1 reformulation, scored as canonical:
In reward models trained by the standard pairwise (Bradley-Terry-style) objective — whose likelihood depends only on reward DIFFERENCES within a comparison set, leaving the fitted reward identified only up to an additive function of the prompt — (i) what share of the variance in the scalar reward is that per-prompt offset, with CIs, for >= 3 released pairwise-trained reward models on >= 2 preference corpora; (ii) is the between-prompt component REPRODUCIBLE (stable across k re-fits with different seeds and permuted prompt order) or seed-contingent; and (iii) for each NAMED shipped practice that compares or thresholds reward ACROSS different prompts — absolute-cutoff preference-data filtering, cross-prompt best-of-n or routing budgets, reward-model report cards that average scores over prompts, and advantage computation in pipelines that do not centre per prompt — what is the decision-flip rate when the score is centred within prompt, computed under THE DECISION RULE THAT PRACTICE'S OWN SOURCE STATES (a stated numeric cutoff where the practice has one; the stated argmax / allocation rule where it has none), with a CI?

Motivating gap (source method: presupposition inversion):
The pairwise logistic likelihood is invariant to adding any function of the prompt to every response's reward in that prompt's comparison set; so the fitted reward is identified only up to a per-prompt additive term, while shipped practice reads the scalar as an absolute quality score across prompts. Two things verified first-hand in this run sharpen the gap rather than close it. (a) The identifiability fact is stated in the literature as a GLOBAL constant, not a per-prompt one: the method-owning field's own 2026 review says the BT model "is identifiable only up to an additive constant" and pins it with a single global constraint, 1^T u = 0, "provided that H_n(E) is connected" (Fang et al., arXiv:2601.14727v2) — but in RLHF the comparison graph is a disjoint union of per-prompt cliques, so connectedness fails and the normalisation is per-component, which the review does not discuss; and the candidate's own field restates the constant as item-independent (Sun, Shen & Ton, Section 2 Takeaway: "the BT reward model estimation converges to the real reward value up to an additive constant"; Appendix B.2 Proposition 24: "for a constant C does not depends on i" [reproduced exactly as served; the served text is grammatically damaged and I did not silently correct it] — explicitly independent of the item, i.e. the OPPOSITE of a per-prompt shift). (b) A shipped method that compares raw reward across prompts and never centres exists and was opened (AdaBoN, below).
Nearest prior located at intake: Sun, Shen & Ton, arXiv:2411.04991 — re-opened and re-read first-hand in this run (see priors); the intake characterisation holds, with one version discrepancy recorded.

What would count as an answer:
(i) A variance decomposition of the scalar reward into between-prompt and within-prompt components, with CIs, for >= 3 released pairwise-trained RMs on >= 2 preference corpora, with the panel declared (prompt set, responses per prompt, response-generating policy — the share is defined only relative to that panel; being a ratio it is invariant to any affine rescaling of a model's whole reward, which is what makes it comparable across models).
(ii) A reproducibility test: re-fit one architecture with k seeds and with prompt order permuted; report whether the between-prompt component is stable. A reproducible offset is a learned difficulty signal; a seed-contingent one is noise in an unidentified direction.
(iii) For each NAMED cross-prompt practice, the decision-flip rate under within-prompt centring, computed under that practice's own stated decision rule, with a CI.
A CLEAN NEGATIVE COUNTS: a small between-prompt component, or one that is reproducible and monotone in a declared difficulty proxy, with negligible flip rates, licenses current practice and converts the offset into a free difficulty estimator.
NOT answers: within-prompt pairwise accuracy (RewardBench-style); reward-hacking results; calibration of reward against human ratings; a theoretical restatement of BT identifiability with no decision-flip measurement.

Significance — consequence: If the between-prompt share is large AND seed-contingent, then every decision that compares raw reward across prompts is partly reading a quantity the objective never pinned: AdaBoN-style cross-prompt budget allocation, any absolute preference-data cutoff, any report card that averages reward over prompts, and any advantage computed without a per-prompt baseline. The fix is cheap and specific (centre per prompt, or pin the scale with cross-prompt anchor comparisons), which is what makes the measurement worth making. If the share is large but REPRODUCIBLE across seeds and monotone in a declared difficulty proxy, the finding inverts into a positive: the offset is a free, already-trained difficulty estimator, and current cross-prompt practice is licensed rather than condemned. If the share is small, or flip rates are negligible at the practices' own stated rules, the question closes with a licence — which is why a clean negative counts.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism — disciplinary boundary plus a design mismatch inherited with the model, not "no one thought of it"):
The identifiability fact travelled into RLHF attached to the wrong normalisation. In paired-comparison statistics the canonical application is ONE connected tournament, so the field states the indeterminacy as a single global constant and fixes it with a global constraint — verified verbatim in that field's own 2026 review, which pins 1^T u = 0 and requires the comparison graph to be connected. RLHF's comparison graph is a disjoint union of per-prompt cliques, so connectedness fails and the indeterminacy is per-component; but the candidate's own field restated the constant as item-independent (Prior 2's Proposition 24), so the per-prompt version was never written down in either literature. Two reinforcing mechanisms kept it unmeasured: the field's evaluation culture scores reward models by within-prompt pairwise accuracy, a design under which the offset is invisible by construction (and under which, as Prior 1 observes, selection "relies on relative ordering within prompt, not absolute calibration"); and the practices that the offset would damage are engineering decisions published without an identifiability audit — Prior 3 compares raw rewards across prompts in the same paper in which it writes that the precise values "are often meaningless". There is also a mild incentive gap: the measurement's only immediate effect is to constrain the measurer's own pipeline.
