Domain(s): Biology — single-cell genomics methodology and tissue-atlas construction. Adjacent: compositional data analysis; destructive-sampling / two-phase-nonresponse estimation. Non-clinical (atlas/composition claims).

Question (canonical) — V1 (as intake, retained per Gate 1 record-both rule):
In droplet single-cell RNA-seq of dissociated tissue, is the ambient-RNA ("soup") expression profile a quantitative record of the cell types destroyed during dissociation — i.e., does deconvolving the soup against a pre-declared reference recover the identity and relative mass of the lost populations well enough to correct that same run's cell-type composition estimate?

Question (canonical) — V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, well-posedness only; this is the version scored):
For a tissue block split across modalities, does the empty-droplet (soup) profile — deconvolved against a pre-declared signature matrix, then converted from RNA-mass fractions to CELL-COUNT fractions using independently measured per-type RNA content per cell and a separately estimated per-type degradation factor — yield a per-type missing-cell-count estimate whose calibration slope against the orthogonal (in-situ count minus droplet count) deficit is distinguishable both from 0 and from 1, with interval reported?
(V2 exists because V1 equivocates between "relative mass" of lost RNA and a "composition estimate", which is in cell counts. The mass-to-count conversion needs TWO nuisances — per-type RNA content per cell, and per-type degradation — and V1 names only the second. The reformulation fixes identifiability, not novelty, and does not narrow away any prior.)

Alternative formulations:
- Can the RNA in the empty droplets tell us which cells the dissociation killed, and in what proportion?
- Treating ambient RNA as a destructive-sampling byproduct rather than as contamination, what is the accuracy of a soup-based estimator of composition loss, benchmarked against matched single-nucleus or in-situ measurement of the same tissue block?

Motivating gap (as intake, with the Gate 2 correction recorded):
Intake claim: two facts are reported in nearly every droplet scRNA-seq paper and never joined — (1) every run carries ambient RNA from lysed cells and a removal toolchain exists (SoupX, GigaScience 2020; CellBender; DecontX), and (2) dissociation destroys cell types differentially, which is why dissociated composition disagrees with single-nucleus and spatial measurement of the same tissue. Intake asserted that SoupX models the soup as a global average of the profiled cells and that "nobody appears to have asked whether that assumption is true."
GATE 2 CORRECTION: that last assertion is false. Janssen et al. (Genome Biology 2023) deconvolve the empty-droplet profile against a cell-type reference, compare the result with the captured-cell composition, and report in the published text that "the contamination profiles do not always reflect the cell type proportions of the sample", with snRNA-seq showing "a clear shift in cell type composition with a decreased PT fraction"; they also conclude that "using the empty rather than the endogenous profile as a reference to model background noise is the better choice" for their data. The assumption has been tested and found to fail. The surviving un-asked residue is narrower than the intake question: not "is the soup different from the cells" (asked, answered, different) but "is the difference a calibrated estimator of lost cell counts against an external truth".

Presuppositions:
1. Ambient RNA originates predominantly from cells/nuclei lysed during or after dissociation rather than from already-extracellular tissue RNA or uniform-at-random lysis. CONTESTED, and partly undercut: the iScience 2023 contamination paper states ambient RNA arises because "cell death, lysis, and leakage results in ambient RNA deposited into the loading buffer", but for nuclei preparations it also states "substantial amounts of cytoplasmic RNA and ribosomes adhere to the surfaces of isolated nuclei" that "can easily shed into the loading buffer" — a lysis-independent source that the estimator cannot distinguish from destroyed cells.
2. The soup is a readout of WHICH cells were lost rather than of how much RNA each surviving type carries. CONTESTED and actively contradicted by prior: Caglayan et al. attribute the neuron-dominated ambient signature to abundance, not to differential destruction — "likely because of transcriptomic abundance of neurons compared to other cell types in brain tissue". Soup mass fraction is (cells lysed) x (RNA per cell) x (survival in suspension); the candidate's target is the first factor alone, and the other two are nuisances of the same order.
3. mRNA from lysed cells survives long enough and degrades in a way the design can model. CONTESTED — nuisance parameter, must be estimated separately or a null is unidentifiable from a biased soup.
4. A reference measures true composition more faithfully (snRNA-seq, in situ, stereology). CONTESTED — snRNA-seq has its own biases; the reference must be declared in advance with its own error characterised or the benchmark is circular.
5. The soup is well mixed across a run. Partly testable from existing data.
6. Published cell-type proportions are load-bearing for atlas composition and differential-abundance claims. Established.
7. Empty-droplet barcodes are retained in enough archived datasets for a retrospective limb to exist. CONTESTED and UNQUANTIFIED — see the dedicated fraction-limb query below; no published denominator was found.

What would count as an answer:
A pre-registered benchmark over K tissues: one block split and measured by (i) droplet scRNA-seq with empty-droplet profiles retained, (ii) snRNA-seq, (iii) an orthogonal in-situ/spatial count; the soup deconvolved against a pre-declared signature matrix. YES = soup-derived missing mass per type tracks the (iii)-minus-(i) deficit above a pre-declared threshold, with calibration slope and interval reported and type-dependent degradation modelled. NO = the soup is statistically indistinguishable from a weighted average of the recovered cells once degradation is accounted for. Either way the deliverable is the shift in published cell-type proportions after soup-based correction over a pre-declared set of atlas datasets, with the denominator (datasets retaining empty droplets) stated. Not an answer unless the type-dependent-degradation nuisance is separately estimated — and, per V2, unless per-type RNA content per cell is measured independently, since otherwise a mass-fraction mismatch is not a count deficit.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
vs P1 (the kill): P1 already performs the candidate's central operation — deconvolve the empty-droplet profile against a cell-type reference and compare the result with the captured-cell composition — and already publishes the finding the candidate's NO arm was written to test, that the contamination profile does not always match sample proportions (clear shift, decreased PT fraction, in snRNA-seq). Four attributes differ: ESTIMAND (P1: within-profile relative proportions; candidate: per-type MISSING COUNT and the calibration slope of soup-derived deficit on an external deficit); REFERENCE (P1: the run's own recovered cells, so unrecovered types are structurally unable to appear in the reference — the candidate's whole target is invisible to P1's design; candidate: a pre-declared signature matrix plus an orthogonal in-situ/snRNA count as external truth); UNIT (P1: pseudobulk profile per replicate; candidate: a split tissue block measured across three modalities); ENDPOINT (P1: correlation and fractions; candidate: calibration slope with interval, plus the induced shift in published proportions). P1 also does not model type-dependent degradation or per-type RNA content, and does not use the background to correct proportions. So the candidate's second limb genuinely survives P1 — but its first limb and its stated motivating gap do not, and the ledger's rule is that an on-point own-field paper doing the measurement forces a Low grade rather than a Medium.
vs P2: P2's estimand is enrichment of a gene set, its reference is a sorted dataset, and it explains the soup's neuronal bias by TRANSCRIPT ABUNDANCE rather than by differential destruction — which is not a weaker version of the candidate but an alternative explanation that the candidate's design must defeat. vs P3: P3 measures HOW MUCH soup there is and uses it as a QC score; it has no compositional estimand at all. It is the clearest statement that the field's objective function for the soup is minimization, and it supplies the contested nuclei-surface-adhesion source that breaks presupposition 1.

Significance — consequence: If YES, every atlas that archived unfiltered matrices gains an in-experiment, retrospective correction for its single largest known composition bias, and the ambient-removal toolchain's discarded quantity becomes a measurement instrument; differential-abundance tests between conditions acquire a loss term that is currently absent from every tool in query 9. If NO (soup indistinguishable from a weighted average of the recovered cells once degradation and RNA content are accounted for), SoupX's construction is vindicated for composition purposes and the field is told that no cheap in-run correction exists, pushing composition claims permanently onto orthogonal references. The honest discount: P1 has already made the crude version of the YES observation without anyone acting on it for three years, so the consequence is not that the mismatch is unknown but that its interpretation is unclaimed — a weaker consequence than intake implies.

Significance — audience: atlas consortia reporting proportions (HCA and organ-atlas groups); authors of the compositional-DA tools in query 9 (scCODA, DCATS, milo, voomCLR, scDC), none of which carries a loss term; authors of the ambient tools (SoupX, CellBender, DecontX, scAR), for whom a YES inverts the objective from removal to joint estimation; genomics core facilities, who would gain a per-run loss readout from data they already generate and delete. Real but narrow: the people who would act are the ~dozen method groups who already read P1 and did not act.

Significance — fecundity: per-type RNA-content-per-cell and per-type degradation priors become estimable quantities worth publishing in their own right; the soup becomes a candidate protocol-QC readout of which populations a dissociation protocol destroys, comparable across protocols; a reporting standard requiring deposition of unfiltered matrices acquires a concrete scientific justification and a measurable denominator (query 5 shows nobody has computed it); and the two-phase-nonresponse framing from query 11 opens the question of whether the soup is a first- or second-phase sample, i.e. whether its own capture is informative.

Significance — anchoring: the sc/sn and sc/in-situ composition discrepancy (P1's and Denisenko's territory); the reference-frame problem in compositional differential abundance; the masked-rare-cell-type problem (P2); the unquantified archival denominator for raw droplet data.

Reason for silence: a role boundary inside one subfield rather than between fields. The soup is owned by the QC-and-denoising literature whose entire objective function is its removal (P3 makes this explicit by scoring it as a defect), while composition bias is owned by the atlas and differential-abundance literature, which treats dissociation loss as a caveat in a Discussion rather than an estimand. P1 shows the two meeting in one paper and still not joining: having deconvolved the soup and found it compositionally different from the cells, it used that finding to pick a better background reference for denoising. Second mechanism: the inference the candidate needs is two-phase/double sampling of nonrespondents, a mature survey-statistics family (query 11) that is not in either subfield's toolkit, so the mismatch P1 reported had no estimator waiting for it. Third, weaker: the external reference needed to calibrate it (a split block measured three ways) is expensive and was not routinely available until paired sc/sn/spatial designs became common. Caveat for the adversary: the first mechanism is partly undercut by P1 itself — a field that has published the anomaly and declined to follow it may be silent because the follow-up is unidentifiable (two unestimated nuisances, per presupposition 2), not because of a boundary.
