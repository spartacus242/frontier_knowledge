## Restatement (my own words, before reading the record's framing as authoritative)

Published "more reasoning tokens → higher (or lower) accuracy" curves are mostly fitted on traces the model chose to emit. A model emits long traces on the problems it finds hard, so the pooled curve mixes two things: (dose) what extra thinking does to a fixed problem, and (selection) which problems get long traces. The candidate wants (a) a per-stratum signed number saying what fraction of the pooled slope is selection, obtained by comparing a pooled slope on natural lengths with a within-item slope under randomly assigned forced budgets on the same items; and (b) a formal equivalence test of whether a trace cut off at N tokens scores the same as a trace that ended at about N on its own. The canonical wording conditions all of this on "a deployed model's thinking-budget dial" being the exogenous instrument.

## Presupposition clauses (attack 4), written out and tested

Clauses the question needs true before it can be asked (literal to the canonical wording, then to the graded (a)/(b)):

1. A deployed model exposes a "thinking-budget dial" that sets a reasoning-token count N. — CONTESTED (sourced). Gemini's current documentation (F3) exposes `thinking_level` as four categorical levels and no numeric thinking budget; the only numeric control is `max_output_tokens`, a hard cutoff on the whole response that returns "truncated or empty output", i.e. it caps output, not reasoning at N with a scorable answer. The Interruptible paper (F2) states "the corresponding model APIs do not support mid-reasoning interruptions." Anthropic `budget_tokens` page returned, not opened — UNVERIFIED for that provider. So the dial-as-token-count clause is false for at least one major provider and unverified for another.
2. The dial is a cap (the model stops at N) rather than a target or a soft guidance. — UNVERIFIED (the page that would settle it for Anthropic was not opened; the Google forum threads titled on budgets being exceeded were not opened). Note the candidate declares exogeneity (P1) a measurand; cap-compliance is a DIFFERENT clause (compliance with assigned dose), not covered by that declaration.
3. Natural per-item trace length is observable on the same model. — TRUE (sourced): Wang 2026 (F1) uses per-response reasoning_token_length on open-weight LRMs; F2 obtains full traces per sample.
4. The same items can be rerun at different budgets with difficulty stable across repeats. — TRUE (sourced): F2 reruns each sample at nine cut points; the record's PRIOR 2 does so at forced budgets.
5. A terminal answer can be elicited when reasoning is cut, under at least two conventions. — TRUE (sourced): F2 uses two elicitation forms (end-thinking token; "<force-answer>" with a format directive); Wang 2026 names "a common procedure for eliciting a final answer".
6. An identifiable body of published claims rests on the OBSERVATIONAL association. — MEASURAND per the candidate's own text (P4); exemption declared, not graded.
7. For limb (b), a non-empty subset of items exists where the model naturally emits approximately N tokens, at each N of interest. — UNVERIFIED as stated (no source opened prints a per-item natural-length distribution at the needed resolution); plausible from F2's statement that "reasoning length varies substantially … across samples".

Does the question survive clause 1 being false? The graded (a)/(b) form drops the deployed dial and says "randomly assigned forced budgets", which open-weight budget forcing supplies (F1 proposal, F2, PRIOR 2). The canonical form does not: its closed-model, deployed-dial arm is not realisable as a token-count cap on Gemini as documented. So attack 4 is PARTIAL — a real, sourced defect in the canonical instrument clause, repaired by the graded reformulation, with cap-compliance still unverified.
