## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Published labour-progress curves (Zhang/CSL, WHO BOLD, LaPS) and the dystocia thresholds read off them are built entirely from a number a clinician produces with two fingers, writes down as a whole centimetre, and produces more often when they are worried about progress. The candidate asks: if you build an explicit error model for that number — error variance (possibly varying with dilatation), examiner random effects, and whole-centimetre coarsening, identified primarily from two examiners measuring the same woman at the same moment — and fit it jointly with a model in which examination times depend on the latent state, then (a) how far do the median and 95th-centile dilatation-versus-time curves and the implied action line move, judged against the published naive fit and that fit's own confidence bands; (b) what fraction of recorded failure-to-progress diagnoses flip when the written threshold is applied mechanically to the corrected curve; and (c) how does each split between the measurement artefact and the timing artefact. Short form: is the published shape of normal labour partly a picture of the instrument, and if so does the diagnosis move?
