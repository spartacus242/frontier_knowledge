## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

When a lab says a neuron's tuning changed between Monday and Friday — "representational drift" — the claim that it is the *same* neuron on both days is the output of a matching algorithm that is known to be wrong some of the time. The question asks for a number instead of a reassurance: take at least two published cross-day matchers at stated settings; score their mistakes (false matches, splits, merges) against identity labels that do **not** come from the neurons' tuning; compute how much apparent day-to-day tuning change those mistakes *alone* would manufacture if nothing had truly changed (the "matching-error drift floor"); then ask whether published drift magnitudes, in comparable brain areas over comparable intervals, sit above that floor. A clean negative — floor far below published drift — settles it the other way.

Put crudely: **how much of representational drift is a filing error about which cell is which?**

## New facts I established, with REGIME fields

**SOURCE A — bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.03.14.643204, "Long-term neuron tracking reveals balance of stability and plasticity in functional properties". READ LEVEL: full text.**
**REGIME:** monkey (two subjects, A and B); chronic multi-electrode array, per-channel mean-waveform matching (NOT high-density in the Neuropixels sense); unit of analysis = a tracked neuron / cross-session waveform group; operating condition = long-term chronic recording across sessions; instrument = mean-waveform similarity (Fisher-transformed Pearson r + log Euclidean distance); threshold = **0.554 (Monkey A) / 0.563 (Monkey B)**, the 95th percentile of a null distribution, i.e. a **nominal 5% false-positive rate**; category = functional stability/plasticity of spiking properties (ISI, firing rate, spike-field coherence, preferred-direction span).
Numbers and their printed locations: Methods, *Defining a threshold similarity score* — "**ensuring a false positive rate of 5%**". My reading of what that number is: a rate **set by construction** from a null distribution of waveform pairs *assumed* to come from different neurons; **not measured against any ground truth, and not validated in the paper**. Discussion — "**this approach does not explicitly control false negatives**." No split/merge rate.
Identity is assigned **without** functional response: "**as firing rates were not used in the tracking algorithm**." But: no independent ground-truth validation is presented, and the paper's own Discussion concedes partial circularity of its waveform-stability result ("given that waveform similarity was the primary criterion for tracking").
The confound, in the tracker-builders' own words, Discussion: "**Conversely, findings of instability may result from tracking inconsistencies rather than true neuronal plasticity.**" Clause direction: it **asserts the ambiguity as unresolved** — it does not claim the tracker is clean, does not quantify the share, and does not record a move away from the quantity. "Representational drift" does not appear.
*Internal consistency note:* the paper gives two conflicting surrogate counts for its PD-span null ("repeated 100,000 times" in Methods vs "2,000 surrogate PD spans" in Results). Flagged; I lean on neither.

**SOURCE B — Stevenson et al. 2011, J Neurophysiol 106:764–774. READ LEVEL: full text.**
**REGIME:** monkey M1 (four animals), 100-electrode intracortical array; unit of analysis = a single unit **within one session**; operating condition = blocks of 40/120/240 trials inside a single 30–40 min session, i.e. **minutes to hours, no cross-day matching of any kind**; instrument = bootstrap/adaptive-filter assessment of preferred-direction change; threshold = α = 0.05 Hotelling's T² (201 of 285 units kept); category = tuning-curve (preferred-direction) stability.
**NUMERATOR CLASS:** its only error-rate number is "**~4–5% of the stable neurons are mistakenly identified as fluctuating**" (Results, adaptive filtering) — a false-positive rate of a **change detector** on **simulated stable neurons**, denominator not printed numerically. **DENOMINATOR:** simulated stable neurons, not cross-day unit pairs.
**OVERLAP IN BOTH DIRECTIONS (the check that gets skipped):** what Stevenson's class **misses** that the candidate includes — identity error entirely; there is no matcher, no cross-day pair, no false-*match* rate. What it **includes** that the candidate excludes — within-session *estimation* noise in the tuning estimate, which the candidate's floor deliberately holds out (its floor is matching-error-only under a declared zero-drift null).
It nonetheless performs the **structurally identical operation** on a different error term, and prints the result: "The SD of PD changes was **39.0 ± 2.2°** for blocks of 40 trials and **22.9 ± 3.2°** for blocks of 120 trials" → after noise correction, "the corrected SD for blocks of 40 trials was **0.4 ± 1.1°** and for blocks of 120 trials, was **1.8 ± 2.5°**" (Results), and the corrected estimate is "compatible with absolutely stable PDs". These are the source's own point estimates **with its own stated spreads**, carried through unaltered; I derive nothing from them.

**SOURCE C — Sadeh & Clopath 2022, eLife 11:e77907. READ LEVEL: full text.**
**REGIME:** mouse, Allen Brain Observatory **Neuropixels**, V1 (plus higher visual areas and CA1); unit of analysis = unit (and population vector); operating condition = two stimulus blocks **within a single session, "separated by more than an hour"** — **no cross-day interval and no cross-session matching at all**; instrument = default quality criteria, no matcher; category = representational-drift index / representational similarity.
It **decomposes representational drift against a nuisance contributor (behavioural variability)** and reports no matcher error rate, no registration error rate, and **no shuffled-identity or zero-true-change floor for its drift measure** (its only shuffle is of activity against behaviour).

### Single strongest killing point

**The record's "young instrument" mechanism for silence is false, and the field had both the practice and the method for fifteen years.** Stevenson et al. 2011 (J Neurophysiol 106:764–774, read in full) refers in its Introduction to prior work "recording many trials from the **same neurons over the course of several days**", so cross-day same-unit tracking with functional comparison predates the 2023–2025 Neuropixels matchers by over a decade; and that same paper performs the structurally identical operation for a different error term, printing an apparent preferred-direction change of "39.0 ± 2.2°" (40-trial blocks) that falls to "0.4 ± 1.1°" after correction, "compatible with absolutely stable PDs". So the propagation template was in this literature's own hands in 2011. That leaves the silence explained only by the ownership/incentive boundary (Leg A), over a much longer window than the record claims — which under Gate 4's own rule ("if no credible reason for silence exists, treat the novelty finding itself as suspect and return to Gate 2") is a reason to re-run Gate 2 in the Utah-array/BMI vocabulary before promotion, not to pass the entry.
