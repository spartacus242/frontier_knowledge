## Question (canonical)

**V0 (as intaken):** What is the empirical distribution of real removal demands against a deployed pretraining corpus — their count, the size and structure of the targeted content, its token share of a named corpus and its entanglement with retained material — and how does that distribution compare with the forget sets the field's unlearning benchmarks stipulate?

**V1 (Gate 1 reformulation — the canonical form from here; one reformulation used, for well-posedness only):** Over a pre-specified window and a pre-registered set of enumerable public records of removal demands against LLM training data — Lumen-indexed DMCA notices naming dataset or model hosts, court-ordered and voluntary dataset withdrawals, documented consent withdrawals in research corpora, and Crossref/Retraction Watch retractions resolvable to DOIs — what is the joint distribution of (legal basis, demands per unit time, retrievable artifacts named per demand, and, for the subset resolvable to documents of a named open corpus such as Dolma or peS2o, document count, token share and co-document entanglement with retained material), with the unobserved private/confidential fraction explicitly bounded rather than assumed away; and do the published forget sets of TOFU, MUSE, WMDP and DUSK fall inside or outside the support of that distribution on those axes?

Why V0 failed well-posedness: V0 pooled four legally distinct demand types without stratification, left "entanglement" undefined, left the sampling frame unnamed (so a NO answer was unfalsifiable — any mismatch could be attributed to coverage), and bundled a second, separately-answerable empirical claim (method re-ranking) into the same sentence. V1 names the frame, the stratification, the corpus, the entanglement operationalisation (co-document count per targeted artifact), and the coverage obligation, and demotes re-ranking to a stipulated downstream step whose hinge status is now partly settled in the literature (see Novelty).

## Alternative formulations

- Who actually asks for what to be removed from a training corpus, and does the unlearning literature's benchmark design resemble those requests at all?
- What is the size and shape of the forget set in the wild, and is it the shape the methods were built for?

## Motivating gap

Silence mapping: a dense how-literature with no measurement of the demand it exists to serve. LLM unlearning has hundreds of methods and several benchmarks — TOFU's fictitious authors (arXiv:2401.06121), MUSE's books and news, WMDP's hazardous knowledge, DUSK's shared-knowledge setting (arXiv:2505.15209), RWKU's famous entities (arXiv:2406.10890) — each of which *stipulates* a forget set. Several demand-side sources are enumerable: Lumen DMCA notices, GDPR/CCPA erasure requests, consent withdrawals in research corpora, dataset takedowns (Books3), and retractions, which the Crossref-hosted Retraction Watch database (open since 2023) makes countable by DOI against open corpora such as peS2o. Gate 2 found no source that counts or characterises that demand stream against a training corpus.

## Presuppositions

1. Real removal demands against training corpora exist in recorded, enumerable form — **CONTESTED**. Many are private or settled confidentially; the measurable sources are a biased sample and the study must bound the unobserved part rather than treat the record as the population. V1 makes the bound a deliverable.
2. A demand can be mapped onto a specified corpus — **CONTESTED**. Mapping a legal demand onto documents is an inference with its own error rate. V1 restricts to demands naming retrievable identifiers (URL, DOI, ISBN, searchable string). Partly de-risked by evidence that the mapping step is itself consequential and non-unique: CleanSlate (arXiv:2608.14855) shows different curators map the *same* request to forget sets differing by more than an order of magnitude (2,432 to 67,323 windows at |W_f|=50).
3. Unlearning benchmarks' forget sets can be characterised on the same axes as real demands — **ESTABLISHED**. They are published artifacts with countable documents, token shares and topical structure.
4. A mismatch would matter, i.e. method performance depends on forget-set size and structure — **NOW LARGELY ESTABLISHED, no longer the candidate's own hinge**. CleanSlate compares SimNPO, UNDIAL and RMU across curators and reports that "retrieval-derived forget sets compose unpredictably with the downstream unlearner", with forgetting under BM25-pre at 98.2% (UNDIAL) vs 32.8% (RMU) vs 10.5% (SimNPO) and SimNPO and UNDIAL both at 100% under the evaluation-aware curator. The coreset-effect result (arXiv:2504/"Stronger-Than-Expected Coreset Effect in Current Benchmarks", found in search, NOT OPENED) points the same way. This upgrade helps the candidate's significance but removes the novelty of the re-ranking limb.
5. "Removal demand" is a coherent category across copyright, privacy, retraction and defamation — **CONTESTED**. Different remedies and different acceptable end-states; V1 stratifies by legal basis and forbids pooling.
6. Corpus membership of the targeted content is checkable for at least one widely used corpus — **PLAUSIBLE/ESTABLISHED for open corpora, impossible for closed ones, which bounds the frame**. Demonstrated feasible: CleanSlate resolves 4,596 of 4,663 works to at least one 5-gram match in the Dolmino midtraining mix (~10B tokens).

## What would count as an answer

A measured joint distribution over (source/legal basis of demand, demands per unit time, targeted artifact count per demand, token share and document count in a named open corpus, topical concentration, co-document entanglement with retained material), built from the pre-registered enumerable sources, with stated coverage and the unobserved fraction bounded (e.g. by capture–recapture across overlapping registries, or a stated sensitivity envelope). The same statistics computed for TOFU, MUSE, WMDP and DUSK forget sets and set beside it.

- **YES:** the real distribution concentrates where the benchmarks do not sample — e.g. thousands of single-document, identifier-level demands scattered across the corpus and split across four incompatible remedies, versus benchmarks' coherent topical blocks.
- **NO:** the distributions coincide on the stated axes within the stated coverage bound.
- The downstream re-ranking test (do method orderings change under a demand-matched draw?) is retained as a *consequence* step, not as part of the question's own novelty, because forget-set-construction sensitivity of method ordering is already demonstrated.

## Novelty — how this differs from each

- **vs P1 (CleanSlate):** P1 takes the request as given and studies the *downstream* mapping — its "requests" are a researcher-drawn sample of size 50 from the works a model can be shown to extract ("A suppression request is a model specific sample of size |W_f| = 50 drawn from the model-extractable works of θ"), framed hypothetically ("A requester may ask a model to stop reproducing a song, book, or other protected work"). It counts no demands, has no demand source, no arrival rate, no legal-basis stratification and no token share. The candidate's unit of analysis is the *demand* as recorded in a public register; its estimand is the demand distribution's shape and its coverage. P1 is nevertheless the closest thing in the literature and it removes two items from the candidate's original answer list: it measures corpus-footprint/entanglement for a stipulated target set, and it demonstrates that method ordering is curator-dependent (Table 3). So the candidate's conjunctive V0 was partly answered; V1's demand census is not.
- **vs P2 (Consent in Crisis):** same corpus-side method (token share of a named corpus), opposite direction in time and in legal mechanism. P2 measures publishers pre-emptively forbidding future collection, at domain granularity, with no identified remedy and no claimant; the candidate measures named claimants demanding removal of already-collected documents, at artifact granularity, stratified by remedy. P2 also shows the "fraction of a named corpus" quantity is computable and has been published — for restriction signals (5% / 45% of C4), not for removal demands. No unlearning connection in P2.
- **vs P3 (Cooper et al.):** P3 argues the mismatch; the candidate measures it. P3 supplies the normative reason the measurement matters and explicitly declines the empirical work ("conceptually... in principle"). Its four mismatches are derived from what unlearning *can* do against what policy *says* it wants, never from a measured population of what is actually demanded.
- **Flagged, not graded on:** Bertram et al. "Five Years of the Right to be Forgotten" (CCS 2020) is the nearest *method* precedent — a real removal-request census with requester types and topical concentration — but against a search index rather than a training corpus, and with no benchmark comparison. NOT OPENED; listed so a later pass can check it, and it is the first thing an adversary should open.

## Significance — consequence

If YES (the real demand distribution lies outside benchmark support): the field's ranking of unlearning methods is established on a region of forget-set space that occurs rarely or never in practice, and the practically dominant regime is something the benchmarks barely instantiate — a long stream of small, identifier-level, legally heterogeneous demands arriving over time, which is a *continual/sequential* unlearning problem with per-demand cost, not a one-shot topical-block problem. Because curator-induced ordering reversals are already demonstrated, "the rankings would probably change" stops being speculation and becomes a priced risk. It would also give regulators the first denominator for deletion-duty feasibility: how many demands per year, over how many documents, at what share of a corpus. If NO (distributions coincide within the coverage bound): the benchmarks are vindicated on the axes that matter, published rankings transfer, and the Cooper-style critique is relocated from "wrong forget sets" to "wrong end-state guarantees" — a narrower and more actionable complaint.

## Significance — audience

(1) Unlearning benchmark authors (TOFU, MUSE, WMDP, DUSK, RWKU, CleanSlate) — would re-draw forget sets by demand-weighted sampling and report per-stratum results instead of a single aggregate. (2) Model providers' data-governance and trust-and-safety teams — hold the real request queue and currently have no public reference distribution to size tooling or triage against. (3) Regulators and compliance drafters (EU AI Act training-data-summary duties; GDPR Art. 17 supervisory authorities; the EDPB's 2025 right-to-erasure enforcement action) — would learn whether deletion duties are being discharged against a demand stream of 10^2 or 10^5 per year and at what corpus share. (4) Open-corpus curators (AI2's Dolma/peS2o, EleutherAI) — would learn which fraction of their artifact is under live demand and whether identifier-level removal is even tractable at that volume. (5) Metascience/retraction infrastructure (Crossref, Retraction Watch) — a new downstream consumer of the open retraction corpus.

## Significance — fecundity

Opens: a demand-weighted unlearning benchmark; the arrival process of demands (is it heavy-tailed, bursty around litigation events?) and hence whether one-shot forgetting is the wrong problem formulation; a per-demand cost model (compute per removed token) and thus the economics of compliance; "what share of a corpus is under demand at steady state?" as a corpus-design statistic; whether the four legal bases require different *end-states* (suppression vs parameter removal vs provenance annotation) and so whether "unlearning" should be one research problem at all; and a coverage-bounding methodology transferable to other opaque governance streams.

## Reason for silence

A disciplinary boundary reinforced by an incentive gap, plus a genuine new-instrument component.

- **Boundary:** the demand record lives in legal-studies and metascience infrastructure (Lumen, court dockets, DPA case logs, Crossref/Retraction Watch), while unlearning is a methods community whose publication currency is a number on a stipulated benchmark. Nobody in the methods community is rewarded for spending a year counting notices, and the empirical-legal community that counts notices (Urban/Schofield/Karaganis; the RTBF censuses) has never needed to resolve a notice down to documents in a token corpus.
- **Incentive:** the only parties holding the complete demand stream are model providers, who settle confidentially and face a disclosure disincentive — which is also why the candidate must bound rather than ignore the unobserved fraction.
- **Instrument:** the countable part of this is recent. Retraction Watch went open on Crossref in 2023, Lumen's dataset/model-host notices are a post-2023 phenomenon, and corpus-membership tooling at the scale needed (infini-gram-style n-gram indices over Dolma-class corpora) is newer still — CleanSlate's 2026 use of exactly that tooling is evidence the second half of the measurement only just became cheap.

## Strongest remaining objection (for Gate 4 to press)

The candidate's surviving novelty is the demand census, and a census is only as interesting as its coverage: if the confidential fraction dominates — plausible, since the largest removal demands arrive as lawsuits and settle under seal — then the measured distribution is a distribution of *the publicly notifiable sliver*, and the benchmark comparison compares benchmarks against a known-biased sample. "Bound the unobserved fraction" is stated as a deliverable but no method for it is specified, and capture–recapture across registries that index the same public filings will not reach the sealed stratum at all. A Gate 4 adversary should also (a) open the final 7,188 characters of arXiv:2412.06966 to check §7 for a future-work call for exactly this measurement, and (b) open Bertram et al., CCS 2020, to test whether the demand-census *method* is simply being transplanted with no new problem at the interface.
