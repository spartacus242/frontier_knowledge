Question (canonical): [R1, adopted at Gate 1] In broth-microdilution AST read from per-well raw optical density, with a *skip* defined as a well whose OD stays below the growth threshold while the next-lower concentration and at least one higher concentration exceed it: (a) in a crossed design of N isolates x k>=5 replicate panels x >=2 panel lots x >=2 laboratories, what is the isolate-level intraclass correlation (95% CI) for skip occurrence (binary, per drug) and for skip position (ordinal, dilution steps below the MIC), with plate-lot, run, reader and laboratory entered as separate random effects; and (b) does an isolate's estimated skip propensity rank-correlate with the resistant-subpopulation frequency measured for the same isolate and drug by population analysis profiling (PAP)?

[V0, superseded at Gate 1, recorded per protocol: "In broth-microdilution antimicrobial susceptibility testing, is the occurrence and position of a 'skipped well' a reproducible property of the individual bacterial isolate once plate lot, run, reader and laboratory effects are accounted for, and does an isolate's skip propensity correlate with the resistant-subpopulation frequency measured for the same drug by population analysis profiling?" V0 left "skip" undefined (the single-vs-multiple distinction is load-bearing: EUCAST treats one skip and >1 skip under different rules), left "reproducible property" without an estimand, and did not say how skip *position* would be modelled. Reformulation was for well-posedness only; the subject matter is unchanged.]

Alternative formulations:
- Are skipped wells in MIC panels random technical error, as the reading rules assume, or a strain-level phenotype carrying information about subpopulation structure?
- Does an isolate's skip pattern replicate across independent runs, panel lots and laboratories, and does skip propensity track heteroresistance measured by PAP?

Motivating gap (source method: anomaly mining): Skipped wells are reported constantly and classified as error *by rule*. EUCAST's own reading guide, opened and quoted: "Occasionally a skip may be seen, i.e. a well showing no growth bordered by wells showing growth"; "When a single skipped well occurs, retest the isolate or read the highest MIC value"; "Do not report results for antimicrobial agents for which there is more than one skipped well" (Results invalid!). The convention is applied at the instant of observation, so occurrence and position are overwritten before they reach any record. The two papers that study skips frame them as method failure, in their titles: Bixby et al. 2024 ("Skipped wells and scientific error during fosfomycin agar dilution and broth microdilution lead to inconsistent minimal inhibitory concentrations...") and Elias et al. 2021 (colistin, "Impact of the 'Skipped Well' Phenomenon"); Elias et al. state outright that "The underlying cause of this phenomenon is unknown." Heteroresistance, the subpopulation phenomenon routine AST is known to miss, is detected only by PAP — described in the 2025 EBioMedicine ML paper as "considered the gold standard but it is labour-intensive and costly" — and never from skip patterns. The enabler is new: instrument and plate-imaging/AI readers retain per-well raw optical growth, so skip occurrence and position are a retrievable quantity at scale for the first time.

Presuppositions:
1. Skip events are recorded or recoverable at per-well level. ESTABLISHED for instrument and imaging systems; CONTESTED for manual reading, where the reading rule overwrites them. The design therefore specifies instrument/imaging readout, not visual reading.
2. Enough isolates can be tested repeatedly for an isolate-level variance component to be estimable. CONTESTED; power is the main practical risk, aggravated because skip occurrence is a rare binary outcome and binary ICCs with k=5 are poorly estimated. Partly mitigated by the observed base rates (11–13% of isolates per replicate for colistin; "up to 38.1%" of BMD final MICs for fosfomycin/E. coli).
3. PAP is an adequate heteroresistance reference. CONTESTED: PAP is itself poorly reproducible and heteroresistance definitions vary (the EBioMedicine labels required HR criteria to hold "in at least 2 of 3 replicates"). Arm (b) is therefore secondary to arm (a), and a null in (b) is uninformative about (a).
4. Skip propensity is not merely an inoculum-preparation or plate-manufacturing artefact. CONTESTED, and partly what is asked — which is why lot, run, reader and laboratory enter as separate random effects rather than being controlled away.
5. The published report of inconsistent fosfomycin skips is drug- and species-specific rather than general. CONTESTED; it is the leading evidence for the null. NOT CLEARED: see the honesty note under nearest priors — I could not read that paper's full text.

What would count as an answer: The replication design above, read from instrument raw OD so skips survive, spanning high-skip drugs (colistin, fosfomycin) and low-skip comparators, with a mixed model of skip occurrence and skip position yielding the isolate-level ICC and 95% CI.
- YES = ICC CI lower bound above 0.10 for at least one drug AND a pre-specified rank correlation between isolate skip propensity and PAP resistant-subpopulation frequency whose CI excludes zero.
- NO = ICC CI upper bound below 0.05 for every drug and no PAP association; this vindicates the reading rule.
- The 0.05–0.10 band is a declared indeterminate zone, not an oversight: it resolves by raising k or N, and must be pre-registered as a third verdict so the study cannot be read as supporting either side.
- Secondary: whether skip propensity adds predictive value beyond the reported MIC for in vitro regrowth under sustained drug exposure.
Each outcome is settled by data, and the community that would act is identifiable.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
1. Elias 2021 holds almost exactly the data the candidate needs and never asks the question. 141 K. pneumoniae colistin BMDs in triplicate; multiple skips in 15/19/19 isolates across replicates A/B/C and single skips in 5/5/4. It reports *no per-isolate reproducibility statistic*. Its closest datum is a replicate-level tally of the 26 undetermined isolates — 3 with multiple skips in all three replicates, 8 in two, 13 in one, 2 in none — which is precisely the signature the candidate asks to be estimated as a variance component, left uninterpreted. The paper says the cause "is unknown", floats plastic adherence, and explicitly does *not* link skips to subpopulations (its subpopulation observation, four isolates with two populations, comes from MIC test strips, not from skips). No PAP, no lot/laboratory decomposition, single site, single drug. The candidate asks the unasked question over this design and adds the PAP arm.
2. Bixby 2024 and the P-2116 companion quantify skip *frequency* and the MIC inconsistency it produces, under an explicit error frame. Bixby: 160 clinical K. pneumoniae, "No-growth wells at concentrations below the MIC occurred in up to 10.9% of wells at a given concentration", skips called "the most frequent scientific error". P-2116: 106 E. coli urine isolates, "performed in technical triplicate and in biological duplicate on separate days", "no-growth wells occurred up to 38.1% of the time". Neither reports whether skips recur within the same isolate across replicates, and neither proposes a cause; the unit of analysis is the method, not the isolate. HONESTY NOTE ON THE NULL: the intake flagged that Bixby 2024 reports skips as inconsistent on repeat testing, which would be partial evidence for the null in one drug-species pair. I could not verify that: PMC blocked the full text and the mirror carried only the abstract. The abstract-level claim is about inconsistent *MICs*, not about within-isolate skip inconsistency. Presupposition 5 therefore stands UNCLEARED, and this is the first thing Gate 4 or any follow-up must read.
3. The EBioMedicine ML paper is the closest thing to a published answer about predicting heteroresistance cheaply, and it predicts from a different substrate: "The model uses whole-genome sequence data", with features for resistance-gene counts, repeats, insertion sequences, plasmid copy number, against PAP labels. It "does not mention" skipped wells or non-monotonic growth across the dilution series. The DnD preprint likewise reaches PAP-grade subpopulation frequencies (99.3% agreement with PAP over 540 classifications) but by a purpose-built dilution-to-extinction plus delay-to-growth assay; it does not mention skipped wells and makes no isolate-level reproducibility claim about irregular growth. Both occupy the "cheaper route to subpopulation frequency" niche with new assays or new data; the candidate asks whether the signal is already sitting, discarded, inside the routine panel.

Significance — consequence: If YES, a quantity that standards explicitly instruct readers to discard or retest away carries strain-level information, and three things change. The reading rule becomes a known information-destroying step rather than a neutral hygiene step, so EUCAST's "retest the isolate or read the highest MIC value" needs a third branch (flag and record). Instrument and imaging vendors can emit a skip/HR-suspicion flag from per-well data they already capture, at no marginal assay cost, against a reference (PAP) their own field calls "labour-intensive and costly". And the method-evaluation literature has been mis-scoring: if skips are isolate-level, counting them as "scientific error" penalises the method for a property of the organism, which bears directly on the live question of whether BMD should be endorsed for fosfomycin. If NO — ICC upper bound below 0.05 across drugs, no PAP association — the rule is vindicated on evidence rather than convention for the first time, the colistin and fosfomycin anomaly literature is redirected to plate manufacture and inoculum preparation, and a speculative bridge between AST artefacts and heteroresistance is closed before people build on it. Both branches act; the yes branch's practical value scales with effect size, which is the honest weak point.
Significance — audience: (i) CLSI AST subcommittee and the EUCAST steering committee / development laboratory, who own the skip reading rule and are currently deciding the status of fosfomycin BMD — the non-endorsement is attributed in P-2116 to the "presence of no-growth wells before the minimal inhibitory concentration". (ii) AST instrument and reader manufacturers (bioMérieux Vitek, BD Phoenix, Beckman MicroScan, and image-based/AI reader vendors), who hold the per-well raw data and the regulatory channel for a new flag. (iii) Heteroresistance researchers, for whom any PAP surrogate is a bottleneck removed. (iv) The specific method groups already publishing on skips (Hirsch lab on fosfomycin, the colistin AST groups), who would be the natural executors.
Significance — fecundity: Does skip propensity map onto known HR mechanisms (gene amplification; mgrB/pmrB for colistin; uhpT/glpT loss for fosfomycin)? Does skip *position* encode the subpopulation's MIC, making the panel a crude two-population readout? Can skip flags be back-applied to vendor panel archives, giving the first population-scale heteroresistance surveillance from data already banked? Does the "read the highest value" branch of the rule bias MIC distributions and hence ECOFFs for high-skip drugs — a question that only becomes askable once skips are known to be non-random? Does skip propensity predict regrowth under sustained exposure, or clinical failure?
Significance — anchoring: The documented blind spot of routine AST for heteroresistance; the irreproducible-polymyxin-B-MIC anomaly; the explicitly unresolved cause of the skipped-well phenomenon ("The underlying cause of this phenomenon is unknown", Elias 2021); and the standing refusal of CLSI/EUCAST to endorse fosfomycin BMD.

Reason for silence: A measurement-rule artefact compounded by a disciplinary boundary, with a new enabler. The reading convention executes at the moment of observation and overwrites the variable: a single skip is resolved to a legal MIC, multiple skips void the result, and no AST database has a field for "skip occurred / at which dilution". The quantity has therefore never existed as data to be analysed, which is why no amount of retrospective database work would have raised the question. On top of that, ownership is split: skips belong to quality-control and method-evaluation workers whose frame is error rates against a reference method — the word "error" sits in the title of the main paper — while subpopulation structure belongs to population-biology researchers who work with PAP plates and have no reason to look at diagnostic panel wells. Neither community's unit of analysis is the isolate-level variance of an artefact. The enabler is recent: instrument and plate-imaging readers now retain per-well optical growth, so for the first time the discarded quantity is recoverable at scale.
