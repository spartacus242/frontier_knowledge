## Gate 0 — Intake

**Question (canonical, V1, as submitted):** In returned carbonaceous asteroid rock (Bennu and Ryugu fragments), does the internal microcrack population that depresses the rock's thermal conductivity carry the geometry and spatial statistics of volume-change (dehydration/shrinkage) cracking — near-isotropic orientation, spacing set by phyllosilicate-rich domain thickness, crack area density rising with local aqueous-alteration grade — or of impact-shock cracking (planar preferred orientations, density uncorrelated with alteration grade)?

**Alternative formulations (as submitted):**
- What broke the inside of Bennu's and Ryugu's rocks: parent-body dehydration of hydrous silicates, or shock?
- Does internal crack area density in returned carbonaceous asteroid material scale with the local degree of aqueous alteration, and with what slope and orientation anisotropy?

**Motivating gap.** Bennu's and Ryugu's boulders show thermal inertias far below meteorite analogues of similar composition. The intake named Rozitis et al. 2020 (Science Advances, Bennu's weak boulders) and Grott et al. 2019 (Ryugu) as the anomaly's sources at **knowledge level only, NOT opened in this run, grounding nothing**. ## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

**What would count as an answer (as submitted, retained):** a pre-declared 3D crack census on N≥20 fragments per body recording per crack its orientation, aperture, length and host domain, plus per-fragment crack area density and an alteration index. Decisive outputs: (a) orientation-tensor anisotropy against a pre-fixed threshold separating planar-preferred (shock) from near-isotropic (shrinkage); (b) the regression slope of crack area density on alteration index with a CI excluding zero; (c) measured crack spacing against the shrinkage prediction that spacing scales with phyllosilicate-domain thickness. The informative null is pre-stated: isotropic, no alteration slope, spacing unrelated to domain thickness → a third mechanism such as parent-body regolith jolting.

This is statable, so the question is well-posed in the ledger's sense. Three defects found, two of them from the paper I opened:

1. **The resolution and sample size in the design are not what the only existing dataset achieves.** The candidate presupposes 3D CT at "~1 µm" on "N≥20 fragments per body". Ryan et al. 2026 report XCT voxel sizes of **3.12 µm and 3.56 µm** for their high-resolution scans and approximately **6–10 µm/voxel** across datasets, and the segmented crack networks used for thermal modelling come from **7 particles** (4 hummocky, 168–592 mg; 3 angular). So the stated resolution is optimistic by about a factor of three and the stated N exceeds the existing segmented census by a factor of about six, on consumptive allocations of returned material.
2. **The alteration index is pre-declared and enumerated nowhere** — the 2026-10-23 well-posedness defect exactly. The candidate names "an alteration index" and "local aqueous-alteration grade" without listing its members or stating the rule and source that generate it. I did not list them and the candidate does not; nothing I opened supplies a continuous per-fragment index for Bennu material. Ryan et al. work with a **two-class** alteration contrast carried by morphology (angular vs hummocky), which is a granularity gap relative to a regression on a continuous index — reported as a granularity gap, neither as an absence nor as a known.
3. **The orientation-tensor threshold is pre-declared in form only.** The candidate's discriminant axis (near-isotropic = shrinkage vs planar-preferred = shock) has no sourced calibration in hydrated carbonaceous material in anything I opened. Ryan et al. contain **no occurrence of "orientation", "preferred orientation", "anisotrop", "foliation" or "shock"** (dedicated in-document term sweep, reported below). Lindgren et al. 2015 do compute orientation anisotropy in CM chondrites but on **chondrule long axes, explicitly not on cracks**. So the threshold that decides leg (a) would have to be set without a calibrated reference in this material class.

**Presuppositions, re-graded against what was opened:**
- *Fragments contain internal cracks resolvable in 3D in pieces large enough to carry orientation statistics.* **ESTABLISHED** (Ryan et al.: segmented crack networks from 7 particles, 168–592 mg, at 3.12/3.56 µm voxel) — but at ~3 µm, not ~1 µm, and at n = 7, not N ≥ 20.
- *Local alteration grade measurable on the same fragments at the same spatial scale.* **STILL CONTESTED, now with a granularity gap named.** Ryan et al. tie crack style to alteration *regime* qualitatively and state that angular particles "appear to have undergone multiple episodes of aqueous alteration"; no per-fragment alteration index is printed. The intake's own worry (Bennu possibly too uniformly altered) is partly relieved — there is a two-class contrast — and partly confirmed — the contrast is coarse and morphology-carried.
- *Shrinkage and shock cracking leave geometrically distinguishable signatures under a pre-declared discriminant.* **CONTESTED and now the weakest premise.** Ryan et al. assert a *different* discriminant axis: polygonal patterns ← volume-increasing (hydration), fractal/dendritic patterns ← volume-decreasing (desiccation), citing Okamoto and Shimizu 2015. That is a pattern-topology axis, not an orientation-anisotropy axis. The candidate's axis is uncalibrated in this material.
- *Cracks were not created by sampling, re-entry, curation or handling.* **PARTLY ESTABLISHED by a source, and still the principal nuisance** — see reconstruction (D) below, whose own opening clause concedes the origin is unknown. Ryan et al. also performed **controlled chisel splits** on OREX-800055-0 (591 mg) and OREX-800088-0 (329 mg), so a handling-induced-crack control exists in practice. Naming this artefact pathway is the measurand's principal nuisance, not a false premise — **measurand exemption declared, not merely considered.**
- *Thermal conductivity of the same fragments can be tied to the crack census.* **ESTABLISHED but model-mediated.** Ryan et al. measure thermal diffusivity by lock-in thermography on 1 hummocky (~49–50 mg, 9 sites) and 3 angular particles (~2–3 mg, 4 sites each), and compute the conductivity reduction **numerically** (steady-state, PuMA) on the segmented crack networks. Conductivity was **not** measured against confining pressure.

**One reformulation, used (V2, well-posedness only):**

*In returned Bennu and Ryugu fragments, does the internal microcrack population whose segmented networks reproduce the measured thermal diffusivity carry the orientation statistics of volume-change cracking (orientation tensor consistent with isotropy at a threshold fixed in advance from a laboratory volume-change-cracked analogue of the same phyllosilicate assemblage) or of impact cracking (planar preferred orientations), and does crack area density differ between the alteration classes resolvable on the same fragments at the achievable ~3 µm voxel scale?*

**What V2 itself presupposes, tested as hard as V1** (the 2026-10-22 18:33 repair defect): V2 (i) requires a laboratory volume-change-cracked analogue of the same phyllosilicate assemblage to exist and to have published an orientation tensor — **I did not open any such analogue, and I did not search for one; that premise is Unverified**; and (ii) replaces the continuous alteration index with "alteration classes resolvable on the same fragments", which inherits Ryan et al.'s two-class morphology-carried contrast rather than curing it, so V2 buys computability at the price of assuming morphology class is an alteration axis — which Ryan et al. only *suggest*. V2 is a genuine tightening of legs (a) and (b) and it does **not** change the Gate 2 outcome.

### PUBLISHED IS NOT PERFORMED — applied in both directions

**What Ryan et al. do NOT measure.** No orientation statistic, no anisotropy measure, no crack area density, no numerical crack spacing, no alteration index, no regression. Crack morphology is **qualitative**: "The angular particles generally feature fewer, longer, and straighter cracks"; hummocky particles are "more densely populated with shorter, jagged, and disjointed cracks". The one quantity printed near the candidate's object is a **porosity partition, not a density**: visible pore and crack volume "accounts for no more than 15% (often <2%) of the total internal porosity." The conductivity reduction is **modelled** (PuMA, steady-state) on segmented networks, not measured against confining pressure. So Ryan et al. **PROPOSE and PREDICT** the candidate's discriminant and **state** a hedged alteration control; they do not **MEASURE** any of the candidate's three estimands. **I cannot name a number, a table or a figure in Ryan et al. that prints the candidate's quantity, so no novelty-Low grade on the quantitative limb is available from this paper.**

**What Lindgren et al. do measure — and on what class.** Required numerator/denominator/overlap check before leaning on it:
- **(a) Numerator class:** chondrules, chondrule fragments and chondrule pseudomorphs whose **long axes** lie within 10° of the median azimuth. Stated exclusion: aspect ratios measured "excluding rims". **Cracks are explicitly not in the numerator** — "We also measured the orientations of open fractures, and veins of calcite, dolomite and gypsum", but those are plotted qualitatively as dashed lines against the chondrule median azimuth (Fig. 3) with **no statistic computed on them**.
- **(b) Denominator:** the measured chondrule objects in a given section or block face of a given meteorite (n = 133 for the XCT ellipsoid set); for the alteration relation, **8 meteorites × petrologic subtype**. Checked against near neighbours: this is *objects measured*, not *objects present*, and not *cracks*.
- **(c) Overlap, both directions.** What Lindgren's class **MISSES that the candidate includes**: cracks entirely — their orientation tensor, aperture, length and area density; returned asteroid material; crack spacing; any phyllosilicate-domain-thickness comparison. What it **INCLUDES that the candidate excludes**: chondrules, which Bennu's CI-like, largely chondrule-poor material does not supply in the same way (Ryan et al. speak of **clasts**, never chondrules, in the sentence that carries the alteration claim); and six Antarctic finds (EET 96029, LON 94101, MET 01072, QUE 93005, LAP 031166, SCO 06043) whose fracture populations carry terrestrial residence and weathering that the candidate's handling-controlled design is built to exclude.
- **Internal consistency:** the reported direction is coherent — the paper states fabric strength *increases* with degree of aqueous alteration while giving r² = 0.35 for petrofabric versus *petrologic subtype*, described as an anticorrelation, and CM subtype numbering decreases with increasing alteration, so the two statements agree. Murchison and MET 01072 are flagged by the authors as exceptions. Porosity is explicitly **not** related to alteration.

**Conclusion of this check:** Lindgren prints numbers, but on a **different numerator (chondrules, not cracks) and a different denominator (eight meteorites, not returned fragments)**. It therefore does **not** print the candidate's quantity either. **No source I opened prints the candidate's quantity.**

## Gate 3 — Significance (argued)

**Consequence.** If yes (near-isotropic orientation, positive alteration slope, spacing tracking phyllosilicate-domain thickness): the low thermal inertia of C-complex boulders is an inherited parent-body aqueous-alteration structure, and thermal inertia becomes a **remote proxy for alteration grade** readable from thermal infrared alone — a new observable for bodies no one will sample. If no (planar preferred orientations, no slope): the cracking is made on the current body by impacts and thermal fatigue, thermal inertia becomes a **surface-exposure-age proxy instead**, and Prior 1's hedged alteration-control sentence is withdrawn rather than strengthened. The pre-stated triple null (isotropic, no slope, spacing unrelated) is genuinely informative and points at parent-body regolith jolting — it is not a design that confirms itself.

**Audience.** Named and real, and visible in Prior 1's own citation set: small-body thermophysical modellers (Molaro, Delbo, Lucchetti, Rozitis, Cambioni — all cited in reconstruction (B) and the sentence after (A)); sample-return curation and XCT teams at JSC, JAXA and the Natural History Museum who allocate and scan the material; and strength modellers for deflection and mining who need to know whether weak C-type boulders are weak everywhere or only where altered. Deflated honestly: these communities already have Prior 1's qualitative answer, so what they would act on is the **orientation and slope numbers**, not the question.

**Fecundity.** Whether thermal inertia is a remote alteration proxy across the C complex; whether the crack networks predate or postdate parent-body disruption, which dates the alteration episode relative to the catastrophic event; whether CI/CM meteorite fracture populations are a **survivorship-filtered** version of the asteroid population, since only the strongest tail survives atmospheric entry — Prior 3's pre-existing-crack fragmentation work is the hinge there; and whether the "saturation spacing" scaling from soil and fracture mechanics (Q11) transfers to micron-scale phyllosilicate domains.

**Anchoring.** The Bennu/Ryugu thermal-inertia anomaly itself; Prior 1's own stated unknown; the Lindgren/Rubin fabric-versus-alteration correlation; and the published CM paradox between low shock stage and clear evidence for compaction, which is literally an unresolved question about whether CM fabrics are shock-made.

## Reason for silence

**Not applicable as a novelty-supporting mechanism — the question is not silent.** It is asked in print and labelled unknown in the paper the candidate was mined from, so the honest question is why it is **unanswered**, and there are four credible mechanisms: (i) the segmented-crack dataset is **seven particles from one body**, and returned material is consumptively allocated, so N is capped by curation policy rather than by interest; (ii) the shock-versus-volume-change discriminant has **no calibration in hydrated carbonaceous material** — the analogue literature Prior 1 reaches for (Okamoto and Shimizu 2015) is reaction-driven fracture in terrestrial rock at a different scale and assemblage; (iii) a **disciplinary boundary at exactly the method** — orientation-tensor petrofabric analysis belongs to structural geology and is where Prior 2's community lives, while the crack-census-for-thermophysics work belongs to planetary thermophysics, and the two communities measured **different objects in the same kind of scan** (chondrule axes vs crack networks); (iv) the discriminant's spacing leg lives in **soil and fracture mechanics** (Q11), a third field again. "No one thought of it" is not invoked and would be false here.
