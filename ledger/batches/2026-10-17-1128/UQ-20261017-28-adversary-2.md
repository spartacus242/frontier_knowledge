## Restatement in my own words

Real pretraining data loaders do not draw examples i.i.d. from the corpus; they read a handful of
shards into a bounded shuffle buffer and emit batches from it, so the documents in one batch are
correlated in source/language/length. Survey statistics has a name for what that does to a
variance-based estimate: the design effect, the ratio of the estimator's variance under the actual
(clustered) design to its variance under i.i.d. sampling at the same sample size, written
deff = 1 + (m-1)rho. The candidate asks: freeze a checkpoint, take N batches as the production
loader actually emits them and N batches resampled i.i.d.-with-replacement from the same corpus at
the same nominal size B, project both onto the full-corpus gradient direction, and report the
variance ratio. Then ask what that ratio does to the gradient-noise-scale number
B_simple = tr(Sigma)/|G|^2 and to the batch-size prescription people read off it, and whether the
correction is stable across checkpoints and buffer sizes. Pre-committed bands: <=1.1 null, 1.1-2
modest, >2 material. In short: "how many i.i.d. examples is a nominal batch of B worth, and does
anyone's batch-size number change once you know?"

## Finding A — the quantity has a name, in the field that owns the method

CorgiPile (Xu et al., SIGMOD 2022; VLDB Journal 2024, DOI 10.1007/s00778-024-00845-0, OPENED) is a
two-level shuffle — sample and shuffle whole blocks, then shuffle tuples inside a buffer of the
sampled blocks — i.e. exactly the shard-plus-buffer design the candidate wants to audit. It
defines, verbatim from the paper:

- "the factor \(h_D\) to characterize the upper bound of a block-wise data variance"
- "\(h_D\) is an essential parameter to measure the “cluster” effect within the original data blocks"
- "Therefore, the larger \(h_D\), the more “clustered” the data."
- h_D = 1 for fully shuffled blocks, h_D = b when samples within a block are identical; the bound is
  on the average squared deviation of a block's gradient from the full gradient, against the
  reference scale sigma^2/b.
- comparator named explicitly: vanilla SGD, where "the common assumption is that data is sampled
  uniformly and independently with replacement in each epoch"; and "Then CorgiPile reduces to full
  shuffle SGD algorithm" when the buffer holds all blocks.

Corgi^2 (arXiv:2309.01640, OPENED) extends this: h_D' = 1 + (1/n - 1/(nb))*h_D after an offline
block-shuffle pass, with the ratio h_D'/h_D = 1/h_D + (b-1)/(nb) and the stated condition under
which the two-level scheme reduces it; "h_D is a constant that characterizes the variability of this
block-wise gradient", "always holds for h_D=b", approaching 1 for well-shuffled data.

So: a cluster-effect factor on the SGD gradient estimator's variance, induced by block/shard-based
loading, bounded in [1, b], with the i.i.d.-with-replacement design as the named comparator, and a
published analysis of how changing the shuffle scheme changes it. That is the candidate's deff with
a different letter. The record's load-bearing negative — "Negative stated in the vocabulary of the
field that owns the METHOD: ... nothing applies it to a gradient estimator, a minibatch, or a
training run", and "nobody owns the join, and the join's natural vocabulary ... has essentially no
presence in ML training papers, so the question has no name in the field that would act on it" — is
false as a matter of record. The join is owned by data management, and it has a name.

What honestly survives for the candidate (from the same opened sources): CorgiPile reports
"Empirical measurement of gradient variance: None" — h_D is a worst-case bound constant inside a
convergence theorem, never measured; it is defined on block-mean gradients rather than on the
emitted minibatch; the work is on tabular/vision SGD in PostgreSQL and PyTorch, not LLM
pretraining; and neither paper mentions gradient noise scale, critical batch size, effective batch
size, intraclass correlation or design effect. So the candidate's *measurement* is un-done. But the
*question* — how much does block/shard-plus-buffer sampling inflate the SGD gradient estimator's
variance relative to i.i.d., and what loader design minimizes it — has been posed, named, and
answered to the level of a bound and an optimal scheme. By this ledger's own definitions that is an
open problem to route, not an un-asked question.

## Finding B — the record's correction is the wrong correction, and its sign may be inverted

From arXiv:1812.06162 (OPENED), App. A.1, verbatim: the method computes the two norms "by computing
the norm of gradient before and after averaging between devices", "In that case Bsmall is the
“local” batch size before averaging", "and Bbig is the “global” batch size after averaging". The
estimators' denominators appear verbatim as "|G|2 ≡ 1/(Bbig − Bsmall)" and
"S ≡ 1/(1/Bsmall − 1/Bbig)", and Eq. A.1 is the relation E|G_B|^2 = |G|^2 + tr(Sigma)/B (the fetch
rendered it in words: "the expected squared norm of the batch gradient equals the squared true
gradient norm plus the trace of the covariance divided by the batch size"). Footnote 6 verbatim:
"This is strictly true only when training examples are sampled independently from the same data
distribution." / "but our conclusions can be altered straightforwardly to account for correlated
samples."

MY OWN ARITHMETIC from those two equations (labelled as mine, not the paper's). Write
V(B) = deff(B)*T/B with T = tr(Sigma). Solving the paper's two equations as the paper does gives
    E[S_hat]   = (deff(Bs)*T/Bs - deff(Bb)*T/Bb) / (1/Bs - 1/Bb)
    E[|G|^2_hat] = |G|^2 + T*(deff(Bb) - deff(Bs)) / (Bb - Bs)
Two regimes, and they disagree with the record in different ways:

(i) Bs = the per-rank local batch, Bb = the global batch (McCandlish's own deployment). If
deff is roughly equal at the two sizes, E[S_hat] = deff*T and |G|^2_hat is unbiased, so the tracked
B_simple is ALREADY the realized design's noise scale, not an i.i.d. counterfactual. The record's
headline consequence — "every GNS number ... is a statement about whichever design its batches
happened to come from, and the run's realized optimizer noise is not the measured one" — is
inverted for in-run trackers: the measured number is the realized one, and multiplying by deff
would de-correct it toward a counterfactual nobody acts on. If instead deff differs between the
local and global batch (it should: one rank's batch draws from fewer shards), the bias is not a
single multiplicative factor at all and its sign is not fixed.

(ii) Bs = 1, Bb = 64, which is how the record says Merrill et al. estimated GNS, and is effectively
what a per-example-gradient tracker does. deff(1) = 1 identically (one draw has no within-batch
correlation, and both arms are marginally uniform over the corpus). Then, with
deff(Bb) = 1 + (Bb-1)*rho, my arithmetic gives E[S_hat] = T*(1 - deff(Bb)/Bb)/(1 - 1/Bb) ~ T for
large Bb, i.e. S_hat is essentially UNBIASED for the i.i.d. trace, while the whole bias lands in
the denominator: E[|G|^2_hat] = |G|^2 + rho*T. So
    B_simple_hat  ~  B_simple / (1 + rho * B_simple),
which is biased DOWNWARD and saturates at 1/rho however large the true value is.

Three consequences, all against the record as written. First, limb (b) as posed — "does multiplying
tr(Sigma) by deff move the gradient-noise-scale estimate" — is not the correction the estimators in
use require; for the Bs=1 estimator the correction is in |G|^2, not tr(Sigma). Second, the record's
stated sign ("deff > 1 raises the corrected noise scale, i.e. moves it toward the measured CBS") is
backwards in regime (ii): clustering makes the reported noise scale too small, which is the
direction of Merrill's observed underestimate, but the fix then has nothing to do with multiplying
the trace. Third, the arithmetic above is a page of algebra from a published estimator plus a
one-parameter cluster model, so the mechanism part of the candidate's payoff is derivable without
any measurement; only the value of rho is empirical. Scope caveats I am bound to state: this is my
derivation, it assumes the single-rho exchangeable-cluster model the candidate itself declares, it
takes Bs/Bb for P3 and P4 from the record rather than from those papers (I did not open them), and
I did not see Eq. A.2's leading coefficients, only its denominators.

Also recorded, not a kill: Yin et al. 1706.05699 (OPENED) defines gradient diversity
"Delta_S(w) := sum_i ||grad f_i(w)||^2 / ||sum_i grad f_i(w)||^2" and a batch-size bound
"B_S(w) := n * Delta_S(w)". MY ARITHMETIC: n*Delta_S = (tr(Sigma_1) + |G|^2)/|G|^2 = B_simple + 1,
so the AISTATS 2018 batch-size bound is the simple noise scale up to an additive 1 — a prior in the
candidate's own lineage that the record's P-list misses. It is no disguise, though: quoted, it
analyses only "where each index s_i is drawn uniformly at random from [n] with replacement" and
"does not analyze non-i.i.d., correlated, clustered, or without-replacement minibatch sampling."
