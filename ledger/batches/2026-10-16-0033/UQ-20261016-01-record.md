Question (canonical):
(V1, Gate 1 reformulation — the version assessed) In large-scale neural-network training runs, (a) within a defined population of instrumented runs, what is P(hardware-fault-coincident | loss spike or divergence not explained by batch replay or optimizer state), estimated with a confidence interval and with a false-attribution rate measured on fault-free control periods; and (b) can a *reference-free* weight-space statistic — one computable from a released checkpoint alone, with no access to a clean twin of that run — detect, above a stated detection floor, that an uncaught corruption event occurred during the run that produced it?

Alternative formulations:
- (V0, intake — recorded per Gate 1, superseded for well-posedness) In large-scale neural-network training runs, what fraction of undiagnosed loss spikes and run divergences is attributable to silent data corruption in accelerators or interconnect rather than to data or optimization, and do released checkpoints retain detectable weight-level traces of corruption events that were never caught?
- (lay) Are the loss spikes that frontier training runs routinely resolve by rewinding and skipping batches hardware faults rather than data problems, and can a shipped model's weights be audited for corruption that went undetected?
- (technical, alternative) What is the attributable share of silent arithmetic faults in the unexplained instabilities of large training runs, and what weight-space signature would such a fault leave in a final checkpoint?

Reason for the Gate 1 reformulation (well-posedness only): V0's "what fraction ... is attributable" leaves the reference class and the estimand's conditioning direction unspecified, and the SDC-in-LLM-training literature in fact reports the *forward* conditional (how many confirmed SDC incidents produced an observable training failure) rather than the reverse conditional the question wants. V0's "detectable weight-level traces" is also satisfiable trivially by differential methods that compare against a clean reference model, which a released checkpoint by definition does not have. V1 fixes the conditioning direction, names the control-period requirement, and makes reference-freeness explicit. The reformulation is not a novelty rescue: novelty below is scored against both versions, and V1 does not escape the pre-emption of limb (b).

Motivating gap:
**The gap as stated at intake is falsified and must be recorded as such.** Intake asserted that "two reported literatures never meet" — fleet-scale SDC work (Dixit et al., arXiv:2102.11245; Hochschild et al., HotOS 2021) and large-run instability reports (PaLM, arXiv:2204.02311; Llama 3's 419 interruptions in 54 days; the OPT-175B logbook). Gate 2 shows they met, repeatedly, in 2025-2026: an SDC-in-LLM-training subfield now exists with at least five independent entries, two of them OSDI '26 papers from production fleets (ByteDance/Tsinghua AEGIS over 3.5x10^7 GPU-hours; ByteDance/SJTU SDCHunter), one ACL 2025 paper on real unhealthy nodes (Ma et al.), one fault-injection study of GEMM corruption during pretraining (TU Berlin, 2026), plus an OCP/NVIDIA whitepaper and a Google test-escape paper on the supply side. The intake claim that the anomaly is "asked about only as an availability/checkpointing problem, never as an attribution question" is wrong as of 2026.
What survives the correction is narrower and different in kind. Every paper opened builds machinery to *catch* corruption in flight (checksums, xorsum fingerprints, cross-DP-rank outlier tests, deterministic replay, RMS-parameter-update detectors) and terminates at rollback-to-checkpoint. None reports the reverse-conditional attributable share for spikes whose cause was never determined; none measures a false-attribution rate on fault-free control periods; and none asks what the *shipped artifact* retains. Two of the papers opened state the residual explicitly without posing it as a question: AEGIS concedes it "cannot completely rule out undetected SDCs," and SDCHunter only *estimates the number of polluted checkpoints from an assumed SDC rate* rather than inspecting any checkpoint's weights. The one genuinely un-asked limb is therefore (b): a reference-free audit of a released checkpoint, which is structurally harder than everything in the literature because the existing weight-level tool (BitFlipScope) requires a clean twin and the existing nulls come from backdoor-detection work, not hardware corruption.

Presuppositions:
1. Large training runs experience loss spikes or divergences whose cause is never determined — **established** (PaLM arXiv:2204.02311 reports ~20 spikes mitigated by rewind-and-skip and that replaying the same batches from an earlier checkpoint produced no spike; the SDC literature opened repeats the point).
2. Silent data corruption occurs at a measurable rate on the accelerators used — **established as of 2026, no longer contested**: AEGIS reports 18 SDC incidents on 13 faulty GPUs over 3.5x10^7 GPU-hours; SDCHunter reports 23 confirmed SDC-defective GPUs (10 offline, 13 in training) and 40 found in production. Intake's "CONTESTED for datacenter GPUs" is out of date.
3. A silent fault can perturb weights in a way that survives subsequent optimizer steps — **now substantially established, against intake's "CONTESTED"**: Ma et al. observe that "model weights on unhealthy nodes incrementally drift away from those on the healthy node" and that "SDCs can make models converge to different optima with different weights"; the TU Berlin injection study reports "persistent parameter divergence." This presupposition's establishment *weakens* the candidate's novelty, since it was partly what the question asked.
4. Attribution is achievable by replay plus fault telemetry — **established for the forward direction, contested for the reverse**: SDCHunter does deterministic replay from the latest checkpoint with fixed seeds, deterministic kernels and locked communication order, so bitwise replay is achievable in a controlled fleet. But SDCHunter also reports that loss and gradient-norm metrics and hardware signals (ECC counts, clocks, thermals, machine-check exceptions) "did not correlate with SDCs and so cannot flag them," and AEGIS reports that "SDCs can be masked by normal loss fluctuations, making loss monitoring unreliable." Reverse-conditional attribution from a spike is therefore the contested limb, not replay feasibility. Intake's worry about batch-invariance failure is real but is not the binding constraint.
5. Released checkpoints are the artifact evaluated and deployed, so residual corruption in them matters — **established** (trivially true; no source needed).
6. Some *reference-free* weight-space statistic can distinguish a corruption trace from ordinary training noise — **CONTESTED, and the limb most likely to fail.** Two independent reasons found at Gate 2: (i) BitFlipScope's only quantified detector is differential, i.e. it needs a clean model; its self-referential variant localizes to *block* granularity by residual-scaling loss sweeps and quantifies no detection limit; (ii) a null distribution built on "verified fault-free runs" may be unconstructible, because AEGIS states it cannot rule out undetected SDCs in its own fleet — so the control population the question's design requires does not demonstrably exist.
7. **(New presupposition, not in intake, needed by limb (a))** Spikes recorded as unexplained in public reports are a *stable reference class* that can be re-identified in an instrumented run. Contested: the population of "undiagnosed" spikes is defined by what a given team's diagnosis pipeline failed to explain, so the estimand is pipeline-relative and not portable across labs.

What would count as an answer:
Three separable deliverables, of which only the third is un-asked.
(a) **Attribution (asked, open).** An instrumented run logging ECC/SDC telemetry plus per-step arithmetic checks (duplicated reduction on a shadow replica, or ABFT/checksum verification on matmuls) alongside the loss, classifying spikes as fault-coincident or not, reporting P(fault-coincident | unexplained spike) with a confidence interval *and* a false-attribution rate estimated on fault-free control periods. Note that Ma et al. already report ABFT "fails to reliably detect SDCs with high precision and recall" in bf16, so the checker in this design must be a fingerprint/replay scheme of the AEGIS or SDCHunter kind, and the deliverable's novelty reduces to the two statistics (reverse conditional + control-period false-attribution rate) rather than the instrument.
(b) **Calibration (largely pre-empted).** Fault injection of realistic SDC patterns at known rates into a reproducible mid-size run, yielding P(spike), P(silent absorption), and residual weight perturbation after N steps — a detectability curve. The TU Berlin study already injects faults into GPU matmul instructions across 60M/350M/1.3B LLaMA-style pretraining runs, sweeps injection frequency (eval loss 7.38 at every step, 4.77 at 1-in-10, 4.33 at 1-in-100, 4.30 at 1-in-1000), reports persistent parameter divergence, and builds a detector on max RMS parameter update with a warm-up baseline. It explicitly declines recall/F1 because "many SDC are masked during training and do not result in observable degradation" — so the *curve itself* is unreported, but the question is asked and the experiment exists. Only the explicit absorption probability remains.
(c) **Checkpoint audit (un-asked).** A pre-registered, *reference-free* weight statistic (per-tensor outlier counts against the tensor's own fitted tail; mantissa/exponent-pattern anomalies; per-tensor update-residual structure) with a null distribution established on checkpoints from runs verified fault-free by in-flight checking, then applied to public checkpoints with a stated detection limit. A negative result is equally informative: an upper bound on the attributable fraction and a stated detection floor for checkpoint traces. The hard part, now visible, is constructing the null: the audit must either (i) obtain checkpoints from a run that carried AEGIS/SDCHunter-class verification throughout, or (ii) derive the null synthetically from fault-free training dynamics and accept a weaker claim.

Significance — consequence:
If limb (a) returns a non-trivial share: the rewind-and-skip-batches practice that frontier runs use as a data remedy is partly mis-specified — a team skipping batches is discarding data to work around a faulty device that will corrupt the next window too, and the correct action (quarantine the device, re-verify, replay) is different in kind. It also retroactively reopens the interpretation of the PaLM-style observation that replaying the same batches from an earlier checkpoint produced no spike: under the data hypothesis this exonerates the batch, but under the hardware hypothesis it is exactly what a transient device fault predicts, so the single most-cited piece of evidence in these reports is diagnostically ambiguous and currently read in one direction only. If limb (a) returns a near-zero share with a tight interval and a low false-attribution rate, that is equally consequential in the other direction: it would license the optimizer-side account (Adam instability, low-precision dynamics) as the default explanation and tell reliability teams that spike monitoring is worthless as an SDC signal — which AEGIS and SDCHunter already assert qualitatively and would then have quantified.
If limb (b) returns yes: a released checkpoint becomes an auditable object with respect to the hardware history of the run that produced it. That creates a third-party capability that currently does not exist — every method found requires the lab's own telemetry, replicas, seeds or a clean twin. If it returns no, the deliverable is a stated detection floor, which is the more likely and still useful outcome: it would convert "we cannot rule out undetected SDCs" from an open-ended concession into a bounded one, and would tell evaluation and procurement that weight-level corruption below some magnitude is permanently unobservable from the artifact.

Significance — audience:
(i) **Frontier pretraining teams** (the groups behind the AEGIS and SDCHunter deployments and their counterparts) would change triage order: a quantified reverse conditional tells them whether a spike warrants device quarantine before batch-skipping, and the false-attribution rate tells them what the quarantine costs in wrongly-pulled healthy GPUs. These teams demonstrably act on such numbers already — they built and deployed detectors with measured overhead trade-offs (4.3% overhead for 5.65% coverage at communication boundaries versus 683.9% for kernel-level checks), so they are a community that prices reliability information precisely.
(ii) **Model evaluators, auditors and safety-evaluation teams**, who currently have no notion of hardware provenance for a checkpoint. A stated detection floor changes what an evaluation report can claim about the artifact it tested, and is the first thing that would let an external party say anything at all about corruption in a model they did not train.
(iii) **Accelerator vendors and fleet-reliability/test engineering** (the OCP and semiconductor-test community now publishing on test escapes and in-field screening), for whom the reverse conditional is the missing link between device-level DPPM figures and the workload-level cost they are trying to justify screening against.
(iv) **Secondary, weaker: model-release governance and provenance work**, which is currently about attestation and tamper-evidence (integrity against an adversary) and has no construct for accidental corruption. I flag this audience as the most speculative of the four; its interest is real but its willingness to act on a detection floor is unevidenced.

Significance — fecundity:
New questions that become askable: (1) whether the magnitude of a surviving perturbation predicts anything downstream — does an absorbed corruption event leave a measurable evaluation-level deficit, or does it only move the run to a different optimum of equal quality (Ma et al.'s "different optima with different weights" makes this immediately askable); (2) whether the audit statistic can be gamed or laundered — does a fine-tune, a merge, or a quantization pass erase the trace, which if yes makes every public checkpoint unauditable by construction and connects to the weight-provenance literature; (3) what the corruption-free control population actually is, i.e. whether any existing public checkpoint was produced under verification strong enough to serve as a null (the answer is probably none, which is itself a finding about the state of the artifact supply); (4) whether reproducibility claims about training runs are falsifiable at all given that bitwise replay requires locked kernels and communication order; (5) a decision-theoretic question the attribution number makes well-posed: the optimal spike-response policy given P(fault | spike), the cost of quarantine and the cost of discarded data.

Significance — anchoring:
Touches (i) the open problem of training instability in large models, where the hardware branch and the optimizer branch (Adam instability, low-precision dynamics, Flash-Attention numeric deviation) are currently argued separately with no shared attribution framework; (ii) the SDC-at-scale reliability problem, where fleet studies and the new LLM-training papers supply rates and detectors but no workload-attributable cost; (iii) the semiconductor test-escape problem, which needs exactly such a cost to justify in-field screening investment; (iv) model-provenance and artifact-integrity work, which is adversary-focused and has no accidental-corruption construct; (v) the reproducibility-of-training-runs question, since the attribution design presupposes a determinism level that production stacks only sometimes have.

Reason for silence:
For limbs (a) and (b), the correct finding is that **silence has ended** — these were asked in 2025-2026, and the mechanism that had kept them quiet is now visible as the mechanism that *broke*: a new instrument (fleet-scale per-kernel fingerprinting and cross-replica checksums at 10^7 GPU-hour scale) plus a new cost (a single corrupted frontier run) made the question answerable and expensive enough to ask. Recording this is more useful than defending the intake claim.
For limb (b), the checkpoint audit, three mechanisms remain and are of the acceptable kinds:
1. **Incentive / liability gap.** The operational remedy for corruption is rollback, which is cheap and fully available to the lab. Rollback restores the run but answers nothing about what already shipped. The party with the means to build the null — the lab, which alone holds both the verified-clean runs and the telemetry — gains nothing from an audit and bears a disclosure liability if the audit finds something in a flagship checkpoint. The party that would benefit — downstream evaluators, auditors, deployers — has the weights and nothing else. Means and motive sit on opposite sides of the artifact boundary, so the question has no natural asker.
2. **Disciplinary boundary at the checkpoint.** Reliability engineering owns the fleet and defines success as "the run completed and the loss recovered"; its papers end at rollback, and the one that counts polluted checkpoints does so by modelling a rate rather than looking at weights. ML evaluation owns the artifact and treats weights as a black box with no hardware provenance at all. The checkpoint is the handoff, and neither side's success criterion extends across it.
3. **Vocabulary capture.** Every weight-level forensic tool found was built for an adversary (bit-flip attacks, Rowhammer, trojans, backdoors, integrity attestation). Adversarial framing presupposes a clean reference or a known trigger, which is why the one weight-level localizer found is differential and needs a clean twin. Accidental, unattributed corruption has no adversary, no trigger and no reference, so it falls outside the toolkit that would otherwise be the obvious one to reach for — a conceptual-bridge gap rather than an oversight.

It loses a point for two real defects: presupposition 7 (the "undiagnosed spike" reference class is diagnosis-pipeline-relative, so the attributable fraction is not a portable quantity across labs), and the null-distribution problem in presupposition 6 (the design calls for checkpoints from "verified fault-free runs," and the strongest fleet study on record says such verification cannot be completed — so the control population may be unconstructible, which makes limb (b)'s detection-limit claim conditional on an assumption the question cannot itself discharge).
Significance 2/3: the audience is real, specific and demonstrably acts on numbers of this kind, and both the yes and the no outcomes change a practice. It is held to 2 rather than 3 because most of the decision-relevant value has already been delivered by the existing detectors — a lab that runs AEGIS or SDCHunter does not need the attributable fraction to act, since it catches the corruption directly — and because the surviving un-asked limb's audience (third-party auditors of released checkpoints) is the one whose willingness to act on a detection floor is least evidenced.
