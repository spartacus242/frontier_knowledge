Domain(s): Medicine — hospital pharmacy, injectable-drug compatibility and drug-information resources; physical chemistry of formulations; measurement-agreement methodology

Question (canonical):
V0 (intake): For intravenous drug pairs whose Y-site physical compatibility has been determined independently more than once under comparable conditions, how often do the published verdicts disagree, and is the disagreement explained by the detection method used (visual inspection versus turbidimetry, particle counting, pH or HPLC)?
R1 (the single Gate 1 reformulation, for well-posedness only — V0 left the unit of analysis, "comparable conditions" and the verdict mapping undefined, and did not separate primary determinations from compendium entries):
Over the published corpus of Y-site physical-compatibility determinations, restricted to drug pairs with >=2 INDEPENDENT PRIMARY determinations matched by a pre-specified comparability rule (same two drugs and salt forms; each drug's concentration within a factor of two; same diluent class; mixing ratio inside a stated band; overlapping observation window; same temperature band), with every determination's verdict mapped to {compatible, incompatible, conditional}: (a) what is verdict concordance across matched replicates (percent agreement plus a chance-corrected statistic, with a pre-specified collapse rule for "conditional"); (b) does discordance follow a pre-specified detection-sensitivity ordering (visual inspection < pH shift < turbidimetry < sub-visible particle counting or microscopy; HPLC for the chemical endpoint) — i.e. do less sensitive endpoints systematically return "compatible"; (c) how much discordance survives adjustment for concentration ratio, diluent, observation time and proximity to the pair's solubility / pH-precipitation boundary; and (d) for discordant pairs, do compendium entries (Trissel's Handbook on Injectable Drugs, King Guide, Stabilis, Micromedex IV Compatibility) track the majority verdict or the most sensitive determination?
R1 is the operative formulation for Gates 2-3.

Alternative formulations:
- What is the inter-study reliability of a Y-site compatibility verdict, and what would a compendium entry look like if it carried a confidence rather than a binary call?
- Are "compatible" and "incompatible" labels in injectable-drug compendia properties of the drug pair, or of the sensitivity of the assay that was used on it?

Motivating gap:
Source method: silence mapping. The literature is dense with how-answers (thousands of pairwise determinations; compendia and hospital wall charts that emit one binary per pair and concentration; ICU audits of how often incompatible pairs are co-infused) and thin on questions about the verdict itself: its reproducibility between independent determinations and its dependence on detection sensitivity, since visual inspection cannot see sub-visible particulate that light obscuration counts, so "compatible" may mean "nothing was looked for". Intake flagged, correctly, that the coverage/census limb ("what share of co-administered pairs has no data") is already the standard finding of ICU audits and was deliberately not made the question. Gate 2 result partly overturns the gap claim: the discordance limb HAS been asked and quantified in restricted form at the level of commercial databases (Neininger 2018: 17.1% outright contradiction on shared pairs; EJHP GRP-163: conflicts in 1.8% of references), and the method-heterogeneity explanation is stated in print as a conjecture, uninvestigated. What remains unasked is the measurement: concordance between independent primary determinations under matched conditions, and a test of whether discordance is method-ordered.

Presuppositions:
1. Enough pairs have >=2 independent primary determinations at matched concentrations, diluents, ratios and observation windows for a concordance estimate — TESTABLE, UNVERIFIED, and the question's load-bearing weakness. Partial positive evidence: Gostynska 2024 (Pharmaceutics 16:264) tables show the same drug carrying C in one study and I in another (aminophylline, ampicillin, alprostadil, fosphenytoin, furosemide, ceftazidime). Partial negative evidence from the same tables: those verdict differences coincide with different PN formulations, manufacturers' products, concentrations and ratios — so strict matching may leave very few qualifying replicates, in which case R1 degenerates into the coverage question the intake set out to avoid.
2. Reports state detection method, concentrations, diluent, ratio and observation window well enough to define "comparable conditions" — CONTESTED. The Medicina Intensiva review's own extraction shows what is reported (precipitate 100% of 27 original studies, colour 93%, gas 67%, pH 44%, HPLC 22%) and what is not (only 26% tested in triplicate; "none of the included studies followed all the methodological requirements"). Incomplete reporting will itself appear as discordance, which limb (c) must absorb rather than assume away.
3. A binary compatible/incompatible verdict is the field's operative output — ESTABLISHED for compendia, hospital charts and the alert/wall-chart logic built on them (GRP-163 turned 7,488 source look-ups into a colour-coded chart); primary studies sometimes report graded or multi-endpoint results, which is why R1 maps verdicts explicitly.
4. Sub-visible particulate matters clinically (catheter occlusion, embolic and inflammatory risk; USP particulate limits exist) — CONTESTED in magnitude. It is the reason method sensitivity is not merely academic; the candidate does not require a settled dose-response, only that the two endpoints are not interchangeable.
5. Discordance, if found, is attributable to method and conditions rather than to irreducible stochasticity of precipitation near a solubility boundary — EXPLICITLY NOT ASSUMED: boundary proximity enters limb (c) as a competing explanation, and nucleation near the metastable limit is stochastic, so a pair sitting on its boundary can legitimately return different verdicts to identical protocols.
6. Compendium entries are traceable to identifiable primary determinations — CONTESTED for commercial products (King Guide, Micromedex IV Compatibility and Trissel's are editorially curated and not auditable from outside); limb (d) is feasible mainly where citations are exposed (Stabilis, Trissel's reference lists), and the share of entries that cannot be traced is itself reportable.

What would count as an answer:
Systematic extraction of published Y-site determinations into a pair x condition x method x verdict table; application of the pre-specified matching rule to identify replicated pairs; percent agreement and a chance-corrected agreement statistic on the matched set; regression of discordance on detection endpoint, concentration ratio, diluent, observation time and boundary proximity; and a trace of compendium entries for discordant pairs. Pre-committable readings: "verdicts are assay properties" = discordance substantial and method-ordered (less sensitive endpoints systematically call compatible); "verdicts are pair properties" = high concordance across endpoints, which retires the worry and justifies the binary form; "not estimable" = fewer than a pre-specified minimum of pairs survive matching, reported as a coverage result about the literature rather than as reliability. Deliverable either way: a flagged list of pairs whose compendium verdict rests on a single low-sensitivity determination.

Significance — consequence:
If discordance is substantial and method-ordered, a compendium's "compatible" is partly a statement about the assay used, and three things change concretely: entries resting on a single visual-only determination are not evidence of compatibility at the sub-visible level and should be labelled as untested rather than cleared; the asymmetry is directional and therefore actionable (false reassurance, not false alarm, is the expected error); and compendium editorial rules would have to choose explicitly between majority verdict and most-sensitive verdict, a choice currently made silently. If concordance is high across endpoints, the binary form is vindicated, the "visual inspection is too insensitive" worry — repeated as a recommendation in P2 and in the QC literature — is retired for this application, and effort moves to the coverage problem the ICU audits already document. A third outcome is informative and cheap to reach: if almost no pairs survive strict matching, then the field has essentially no replication, and no verdict in the compendia has ever been independently reproduced under comparable conditions — which is a stronger statement about the evidence base than either of the first two.
Significance — audience:
(i) Compendium editors and database curators (Trissel's Handbook on Injectable Drugs, King Guide, Stabilis/Infostab, Micromedex IV Compatibility) — they set the entry format and would be the ones to add a confidence or method field, or a "single low-sensitivity determination" flag; (ii) hospital P&T committees, pharmacy informatics staff and the people who build local co-infusion wall charts and smart-pump/EHR compatibility rules from these sources (GRP-163 is a documented instance, including its own 1.8% conflict rate resolved by unstated rule); (iii) ICU and neonatal nursing protocol authors who choose which pairs may share a lumen; (iv) the methodologists and journal reviewers of compatibility studies (PTHP, EJHP, AJHP, JPPT) — a method-ordering result changes minimum-endpoint requirements for publishable determinations, which is the one lever that would alter future entries; (v) PN guideline groups (ASPEN/ESPEN) where Y-site drug/PN verdicts feed directly into practice statements.
Significance — fecundity:
Makes askable: what a probability-of-detection curve for an incompatibility looks like as a function of endpoint, by analogy with visible-particle inspection; whether the clinically relevant threshold is visible precipitate or a USP-type sub-visible count, which is a dose-response question nobody can currently pose for a specific pair; whether compendium disagreement predicts anything about downstream events (catheter occlusion, line-related complications), which turns the reliability estimate into an outcome study; how much of apparent discordance is nucleation stochasticity near a metastable boundary, which converts a bibliometric result into a physical-chemistry experiment with pre-specified borderline pairs; and whether pairs with a single visual-only determination are over-represented among the pairs clinicians actually co-infuse.
Significance — anchoring:
The "no data available" bucket of ICU co-infusion audits (the coverage anomaly the intake deliberately avoided); P1's untested method-heterogeneity conjecture; P2's finding that only 26% of original studies even ran triplicates; USP particulate-matter limits and the visible/sub-visible boundary; the probability-of-detection and inspector-agreement apparatus of pharmaceutical visual inspection (USP/PDA/ISPE), which exists as a ready-made method and has never been pointed at this question; alert/chart configuration practice, where a binary with no confidence attached is consumed as if it had one.

Reason for silence:
Not "no one thought of it" — P1 did think of it, in one sentence, and stopped. Three mechanisms, in order of weight. (1) Incentive and ownership gap: compatibility determinations are produced as single-pair service studies answering a local need, and are consumed through compendia that must emit one actionable binary per pair; the aggregate has no owner. The compendium publishers are commercial, do not expose their grading for audit, and have no incentive to publish a reliability estimate of their own product — while academic authors are rewarded for adding a new pair, not for re-measuring an old one, which is also why replication is scarce enough to make the study hard. (2) A practice rule that removes the need for the number: pharmacy resolves source disagreement conservatively ("if any source says incompatible, treat as incompatible"), so discordance is handled as an operational nuisance with a decision rule rather than treated as a quantity to estimate; once the rule exists, the rate stops being interesting to the people who meet it. (3) A vocabulary boundary: the apparatus for this question — chance-corrected agreement, limit of detection, probability of detection, measurement-system analysis — lives in clinical laboratory medicine and pharmaceutical QC, not in hospital pharmacy's stability-and-compatibility tradition, whose methodological literature is about how to do one study well (quality criteria for a single publication) rather than how a body of verdicts agrees. Supporting observation rather than assertion: query 9 found the agreement machinery fully developed one wall away, for human inspection of visible particles in parenterals, and query 7 found the same question form fully developed for DDI compendia — neither has crossed into Y-site compatibility.
