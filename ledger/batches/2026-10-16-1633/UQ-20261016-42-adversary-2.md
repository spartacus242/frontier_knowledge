## Restatement in my own words

Take the published studies that certify a blood-pressure device (a finger volume-clamp monitor, a cuff, a cuffless wearable) by comparing it beat-for-beat or cuff-for-cuff against a fluid-filled arterial line. The arterial line is treated as the truth. But a fluid-filled catheter–tubing–transducer assembly is a second-order mechanical system that rings or sags, and roughly a third of in-service radial lines in one 300-line single-centre series were underdamped. So: inside those validation datasets, if you label every reference line as acoustically clean or ringing (by fast-flush test where recorded, otherwise by a waveform rule), how much bigger is the device's apparent error in the ringing stratum — separately for systolic, diastolic and mean — expressed as the resonant stratum's share of total squared disagreement over and above its share of the records; and would any device that passed (or failed) its standard's bias/SD thresholds have come out the other way had only clean reference lines counted? The implicit charge is that part of what we call "device error" is reference artefact, and that the certification standard never looks at the reference's own fidelity.

That last clause is the premise I went after, and it is where the candidate breaks.

## The finding that decides this

ISO 81060-3:2022 — the standard the canonical v2 question names — clause **4.3.1 "Reference invasive blood pressure monitoring equipment"**, read in the sample PDF above, lists as a normative requirement, item c):

> "The natural frequency and damping coefficient of the reference invasive blood pressure monitoring equipment" — the clause uses **"shall"**, not "should", requires that these be **examined and optimized to meet dynamic requirements**, states this **"for each subject"**, and cites bibliography reference [1].

(Partial verbatim: the fetching reader declined a character-for-character transcription of the whole item on quotation grounds, but returned the quoted subject phrase and confirmed on a second, differently-prompted read that the modal verb is "shall" and that "for each subject" is in the text. Items a), b), d), e), f) were quoted directly: reference equipment "shall conform with the requirements of IEC 60601-2-34"; "shall be referenced to the level of the right atrium"; "Appropriate measures shall be taken to remove air bubbles and clots from the system"; equipment not outputting waveform data "may be modified to permit such data collection"; conformance is checked by inspection of the clinical investigation report. Note 1 points to guidance in Clause A.2, which the sample does not contain.)

**Scope check on my own claim, as the ledger requires.** This is not a textbook recollection applied out of regime. The clause sits in ISO 81060-3:2022, i.e. the exact document v2 names; it governs the exact device class v2 names (continuous automated measurement type); it is normative, not informative; and it is per-subject, not a one-off bench qualification. What I did **not** establish: any numerical limit on natural frequency or damping coefficient (the sample gives none in 4.3.1, and Clause A.2 and clause 4.3.4 were unreadable), nor whether published validation studies actually report compliance.

Three load-bearing items in the record are contradicted by this text:
1. Presupposition 6, "Device-validation studies do not currently check reference fidelity" — false as a statement about the governing requirement: the standard mandates the check, per subject.
2. The reason-for-silence mechanism, "the standards themselves make the question hard to formulate, because naming the intra-arterial signal 'the reference' puts its fidelity outside the analysis frame by construction" — inverted. The standard puts reference fidelity *inside* the frame, in its own words, and cites a dynamic-response reference for it.
3. The audience claim, "the IEC/ISO committee behind ISO 81060-3:2022, which must decide whether to specify reference-system dynamic response at the next revision" — they specified it in the 2022 edition.

And it damages limb (b) structurally: in a dataset conducted under the named standard, the resonant stratum is supposed to be empty by protocol, so "restrict the analysis to the non-resonant stratum and see whose verdict flips" is either a no-op or, if resonant records are found, a **compliance audit of 4.3.1(c)** — a different and already-askable question — rather than the error-apportionment question posed.
