### (g) Uncertainty on the 0.73%

**None.** 0.73% carries no confidence interval, no standard error, no p-value and no statement of sampling variability, in either of its two formulations. The Methods' statistics paragraph, in full (6 consecutive served fragments, assembled):

> "Data was entered into Microsoft Excel and analyzed using STATA version 15. Descriptive statistics, including frequencies, percentages, means, medians, and interquartile ranges (IQR), were used to summarize data. Paired comparisons between initial and repeat test results were performed using appropriate statistical tests based on data distribution. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant."

It is an unlabelled paragraph at the end of the Methods; there is no "Statistical analysis" subheading. Note what it commits to: descriptive statistics plus paired initial-vs-repeat comparisons. Interval estimation of the repeat rate is not in scope, so the absence of a CI is a design consequence, not an omission from a stated plan. The uncertainties the paper does carry belong to other quantities: "a mean reporting delay of 64 ± 41 minutes" (delay, not rate), and the p-values in Table 4 (initial vs repeat results, not the rate).

## REGIME

What this paper establishes, and where:

- **Population / setting:** one laboratory — the Clinical Biochemistry Laboratory of Dhulikhel Hospital, Kathmandu University Hospital, Nepal. A single tertiary-care university hospital in a low-income / resource-limited setting, explicitly framed as such ("particularly in resource-limited settings").
- **Window:** one month. Not a year, not multi-site, not multi-season.
- **Unit of analysis:** the individual biochemistry **test**, as logged by the analyser ("Biochemistry analyzer records were reviewed to identify repeated tests"). Not the patient, not the order, not the episode, not the laboratory.
- **Denominator:** all biochemical tests performed in that one department in that month — 95,757 in the text, 117 test types; the repeated set is 701 tests over 43 parameters.
- **Endpoints:** frequency of repeat per analyte; reasons for repetition; concordance between initial and repeat result; mean reporting delay; direct cost (NPR 153,188 total additional direct cost).
- **Comparator:** none. There is no intervention, no control laboratory, no before/after. Initial-vs-repeat concordance is the only paired comparison.
- **Estimand:** a descriptive point proportion for a census of one month at one site. Not an estimate of a population rate with sampling error, which is why no interval appears.

Where the regime is narrower than a reader might assume:

- 0.73% is **repeat testing as this one analyser's records recorded it**, driven mostly by critical-value confirmation and instrument flags. It is a share of ALL biochemistry tests in the department, not a share of critical-value results, not a share of orders, and not clinically-redundant duplicate ordering. Anyone using 0.73% as "the rate of unnecessary/duplicate test ordering" is outside the regime: the paper's own finding is that 44.37% of the repeats were critical-value confirmations, i.e. protocol-driven.
- The 0.5%–5% comparison range is attributed to **high-income settings** on the strength of a US trade-magazine column (2010) and a PLoS One single-laboratory study from China (2013). The paper's own framing — Nepal, resource-limited — sits outside the regime it names as the comparator, and ref 13's setting is not a high-income one on the usual classification. The comparison sentence is the softest thing in the paper and the only one that reaches outside its own data.
- The decomposition percentages are shares **of the 701 repeats**, not of 95,757 tests. 44.37% of repeats is 0.32% of all tests. If a ledger entry quotes "critical values 44.37%" next to "0.73% of tests", the two denominators must be kept apart.

## DEFECTS FOUND

1. **Not a review, and nobody claimed it was** — but worth stating plainly for the rejection: this is a single-site, one-month, original cross-sectional observational study, n = 95,757 tests / 701 repeats, with 13 references. It carries no pooled estimate and no systematic search. Anything the ledger rests on it for inherits a one-lab one-month regime.
2. **The paper's own denominator does not reconcile.** The text and Abstract say 95,757 total tests and 0.73%. **Table 3's total row prints "Total 93706 701 0.75"** — i.e. 93,706 total tests and a 0.75% frequency. 701/93,706 = 0.75%; 701/95,757 = 0.73%. The paper prints both figures, 2,051 tests apart, without reconciling them, and prints two different repeat rates in consequence. Re-derived at the paper's own arithmetic, both are internally correct against their own denominators. **A ledger entry that cites "0.73%" is citing the text's figure; the paper also prints 0.75% in a table.** This is the most substantive defect in the source.
3. **The decomposition is figure-backed, not table-backed, and three of its four counts are not in the text.** Only n=311 is printed. "n=311" for critical values is the paper's; 37.09%, 9.55% and 8.98% are printed as bare percentages against an unstated denominator. An earlier agent's record of the decomposition is accurate as to the four numbers but should not be described as a table, and the "shares of the 701" reading is a derivation (consistent: sums to 99.99, and 311/701 = 44.37%).
4. **Published-vs-performed, on uncertainty:** the paper MEASURES 0.73% and PRINTS it twice. It does not BOUND it. No CI, SE or variability statement anywhere, and no "Statistical analysis" subheading — the Methods' stated analysis plan is descriptive statistics plus paired initial/repeat comparisons, which does not call for one. Sub-claim (g) is confirmed: it carries none.
5. **Front-matter inconsistencies in the article itself:** two different publication dates across the two routes (PDF "Published 30 May 2026", landing page 2026-07-20); a "Copyright © by 2022" line in a 2026 issue; no article-type label above the Abstract (the label lives in the landing-page section and the p.19 running header); first author affiliated to Clinical Microbiology on a clinical-biochemistry paper. None of these touch the numbers; all of them bear on how carefully the issue was copy-edited.
6. **OCR damage, reproduced not corrected:** reference 10's title is served as ":jf:Yo aLdf sfo{qmd dfkm{t pknAw ;]jfx?sf] ljj/0f" with an incomplete URL — a Devanagari-font encoding failure. Not one of the references the claim depends on.
7. **The earlier agent's two stated limits were both avoidable.** It read both routes "through the summariser" and "never saw a rendered table": Tables 2 and 3 render as readable text through WebFetch, including Table 3's total row, which is where defect 2 lives. Figure 1 is the only thing that genuinely did not render.
8. **Unchecked within budget:** whether refs 9 and 13 actually support a 0.5%–5% range. This paper asserts the range and cites them; I read the citations' front matter only.
