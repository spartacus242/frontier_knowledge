## Restatement (my own words)

Take every pair of published phylogenetic papers that, once their tip names are reconciled to one taxonomy, share at least k taxa. Prune both published trees to the shared taxa. Count the clades that paper A labels at >=95% (bootstrap, UFBoot or posterior) which are incompatible with a clade that paper B labels at >=95%, and divide by the number of comparable clade pairs. Report that rate split by support type, by how much underlying data the two papers share, and by era; treat it as a lower bound on how often at least one of the two 95% labels was wrong; and compare it with what each method's 95% is supposed to mean, under a benchmark fixed in advance. In short: does the published phylogenetic record contradict itself at 95% more often than 95% permits?

C3. "incompatible, on the shared taxon set and under a declared bipartition-conflict test" — decidable procedure; the induced bipartition of a true clade on a subset is itself true, so incompatibility of the two restrictions does imply the two full clades cannot both be clades of one true tree. C4. "stratified by a declared study-to-study data-overlap measure" — presupposes locus identity is recoverable from what papers report, at corpus scale. Neither the record nor I opened an instance. Grade: UNVERIFIED. Kills nothing; blocks nothing by itself.
C5. "as a lower bound on the joint error of the two >=95% labels" — presupposes that two incompatible >=95% clades cannot both be CORRECT LABELS, i.e. that both labels address the same estimand (one true tree). Tested against F3. Under incomplete lineage sorting, "gene trees may differ in topology from each other and from species trees" (Degnan & Rosenberg 2006, abstract). A bootstrap proportion or posterior probability is computed on the characters of the alignment analysed; a 95% label on a single-locus (e.g. mitochondrial) tree is a statement about THAT gene tree's clade, and a 95% label on a different locus's tree is a statement about a different gene tree. When the two gene trees genuinely differ, both labels can be correct and the clades still incompatible. So for pairs of studies built on different loci — which is exactly the zero-overlap stratum V2 proposes to use as its cleanest "replication" stratum — mutual contradiction is NOT a lower bound on the joint error of the labels: it is the sum of label miscalibration AND genuine locus-history discordance AND model/sampling differences, with no term in V2 to separate them. The data-overlap stratifier does not do it (zero overlap covers both "two independent species-tree estimates" and "two different gene trees"), and the era stratifier is only a proxy. Grade: CONTESTED on a source I opened (F3 establishes the biological premise; the step "a support label's nominal meaning is scoped to the analysed data" is definitional for both bootstrap and posterior probability but I could NOT source it to an opened page within budget — Felsenstein 1985 / Hillis & Bull 1993 not opened — and I say so rather than dress it as sourced).
C6. "how does that rate compare with a per-method nominal benchmark declared in advance" — presupposes a non-circular nominal benchmark per method. The record itself declares this CONTESTED for bootstrap (Huang et al. 2020, read in full by the record) and "straightforward" for posterior probability. C5 undermines the "straightforward" half too: a posterior probability is conditional on the model and the alignment, so a cross-locus contradiction is not a posterior-calibration failure. Grade: CONTESTED (record's own source for bootstrap; my C5 argument for PP, with the same unsourced definitional step).
C7. Repair-introduced premise: V2's "declared data-overlap measure computable from what papers report" — same as C4; the record flags it UNVERIFIED and I did not close it.
