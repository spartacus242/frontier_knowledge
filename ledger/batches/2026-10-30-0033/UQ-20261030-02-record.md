Question (canonical):
V1 (as submitted, retained): Is the genomic site at which a LINE-1 element inserts determined in part by three-dimensional nuclear proximity to the donor locus that transcribed it — so that, conditional on endonuclease sequence preference, chromatin accessibility and replication timing, de-novo insertion density scales with measured contact frequency with the donor — or is insertion-site choice independent of where the element came from?

V2 (THE ONE PERMITTED GATE 1 REFORMULATION, for well-posedness only; this is the version scored):
In isogenic human cell lines each carrying one tagged, inducible, single-copy L1 donor at a mapped chromosomal locus, does the per-bin rate of de-novo L1 insertion depend on that bin's DISTANCE-NORMALISED 3D contact frequency with the donor locus (observed/expected at matched genomic separation), with the primary estimate taken on TRANS bins (chromosomes other than the donor's, where linear distance is undefined), conditional on endonuclease-consensus density, chromatin accessibility, replication timing and base composition — and does the coefficient move when the donor is moved to the opposite A/B compartment? Insertions must be recovered WITHOUT selection for expression of the tag.

Alternative formulations:
- Does a retrotransposon preferentially land in DNA that is spatially near the locus that made it, extending LINE-1's known cis-preference from its own RNA to its choice of target?
- Holding the one-dimensional determinants fixed, does the per-bin rate of new LINE-1 insertion depend on that bin's Hi-C/Micro-C contact frequency with the donor element, and does the dependence move when the donor is moved?

Motivating gap:
The settled claim being inverted is that L1 insertion-site selection is set by LOCAL, one-dimensional features: the ORF2p endonuclease nick-site consensus, accessibility, replication timing and base composition. Its unexamined presupposition is that the relevant geometry is one-dimensional — that the target is chosen without reference to where in the nucleus the element was made. Two things make the inversion live: L1's cis-preference (ORF2p acts on the RNA that encoded it) is a property of the machinery, and for retroviruses integration-site choice is known to be steered by nuclear architecture.

2. CONTESTED. The donor's 3D contact profile is measurable in the same cells. Contact maps are population averages while insertion happens in individual nuclei, and insertion is thought to occur in S/G2 while maps are usually cycle-mixed.

Significance — consequence:
If YES: insertion-site choice carries a memory of origin, which means the cytoplasmic RNP stage does not erase positional information — implying either a nuclear-retained RNP pool, nascent-chain capture at the donor, or re-entry biased toward the donor's neighbourhood. The practical consequence is that the expected de-novo insertion landscape stops being one genome-wide prior and becomes a function of WHICH source element is active and WHERE it sits; since hot L1 source elements differ between individuals and between tumours, predicted insertion risk becomes individual- and source-specific. If NO (coefficient indistinguishable from zero in both lines, compartment terms absorbing the signal): that establishes origin-amnesia as a mechanistic fact about the L1 RNP, which is a real constraint on where RNPs assemble and how they re-enter the nucleus, and it closes a question the field currently assumes rather than tests.

Significance — audience:
L1 and mobile-element biologists, who would add or exclude a donor-position term in insertion-landscape models; 3D genome groups, for whom this is a rare chance to test whether contact frequency is CAUSAL for a genome-altering event by moving the reference locus rather than by correlating across loci; cancer-genome analysts calling somatic retrotransposition, who currently model insertion preference without reference to the source element's position; gene-therapy and insertional-mutagenesis safety groups, who already model vector integration-site preference and would gain a donor-position covariate; germline mutation-rate modellers.

Significance — fecundity:
A yes immediately makes askable: whether the term scales with donor transcription rate; whether it is abolished by disrupting nuclear-pore or lamina tethering; whether it survives mitosis or is re-set each cycle; and — the cleanest follow-up — whether Alu and SVA, which are trans-mobilised by L1 ORF2p and therefore have a DIFFERENT donor locus from the protein source, show proximity to their own RNA's donor or to the L1 protein donor, which separates RNA-origin memory from protein-origin memory. A no makes askable where in the cytoplasm-to-nucleus cycle the positional information is lost.

Significance — anchoring:
Touches the cis-preference puzzle (why ORF2p acts preferentially on the RNA that encoded it, and whether that preference has a spatial component); the unresolved regional clustering of de-novo insertions seen in the engineered literature, where P1 and P2 point opposite ways at chromosome grain; the "where does target-primed reverse transcription physically happen" question; and the broader open problem of whether 3D contact is causal or merely correlated for genome-altering events, which the translocation-partner literature (P3) raised and did not settle because it could not move a locus.

Reason for silence:
Structural, and specific. The field's workhorse assay delivers the L1 donor on a TRANSFECTED PLASMID — P1's Methods say "transfected with 3 μg synL1_neo rescue vector", and P2 used a synthetic L1RP construct — so in the standard design the donor has no chromosomal coordinate and donor-proximity is not a quantity the experiment can express. The question is therefore unaskable in the design almost everyone uses, and nobody builds a single-copy mapped chromosomal donor in order to interrogate its own neighbourhood. Second component, and the presupposition being inverted: the cytoplasmic assembly of the L1 RNP is treated as settling the matter a priori — assembly outside the nucleus is taken to make origin memory impossible — which converts an empirical question into an assumed answer that nobody then measures. Third: the field that owns the METHOD (contact-frequency regression against a reference locus) owns it for double-strand-break joining, where both partners are broken chromosomal loci and "donor" means the other end of a rearrangement, not the source that manufactured a mobile element, so the transplant never happened in either direction.
