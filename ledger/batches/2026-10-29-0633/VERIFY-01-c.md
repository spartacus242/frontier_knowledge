### 3.5 Answer to check (2) — correlated or uncorrelated?

Nothing I opened reports crack density in shocked or impact-deformed hydrated carbonaceous material as **uncorrelated** with aqueous alteration. Everything I opened asserts or models a **positive** coupling between impact deformation and alteration, but **none of it measures crack density**:
- Rubin 2012 (FULL TEXT, LPSC abstract): measures particle alignment (r = −0.92 with subtype, n = 9) and MODELS fractures as the fluid pathway ("more water was retained in rocks with more fractures and greater porosity … These are the same rocks that became more aqueously altered"); fracture density not measured.
- Hanna et al. 2015 (ABSTRACT): alteration "post-dated or was contemporaneous with the deformation", with "serpentine veins parallel to the foliation fabric" and "multiple episodes of fracturing and mineralization" — fractures and alteration coupled texturally and in time, in one S1 Murchison stone; no density.
- Hanna et al. 2026 (ABSTRACT fragments): "impact-driven microporosity modification played a key role in controlling subsequent aqueous alteration"; porosity, not crack density; correlation words only.
- Lindgren et al. 2015 (full text by the batch's three readers; not re-opened by me): petrofabric strength increases with alteration, r² = 0.35; fractures presence-only.

So: **leg 2's clause ("shock-crack density uncorrelated with alteration grade") is non-identifying only as an inference** — by transfer from fabric-strength and porosity proxies and from stated models — and **not as an established measured fact**, because no opened source measures crack density against alteration in this material. That is exactly how adversary 2 (CONTESTED, "scope transfer, written out") and the entry (vulnerability paragraph) frame it. Two further points, MY OWN REASONING, labelled: (i) the published coupling runs in the direction opposite to the candidate's clause (positive, not zero), which is why the clause is contested rather than merely unverified; (ii) Hanna 2015's "serpentine veins parallel to the foliation" and Rubin's altered fracture walls mean impact fractures in CM material get mineralized, so the sign of an OPEN-crack-density-versus-alteration relation for impact cracks is not even guaranteed positive — infilling could make it negative. A read of Hanna et al. 2015 and 2026 full texts for an open-fracture volume fraction by alteration zone is the computation that would ground (ii); I did not do it.

Regime caveat on all of (2): every source is a CM **meteorite** (mostly Murchison) deformed at low shock stage (S1) by impact compaction; the candidate's "impact-shock cracking" in chondrule-poor CI-like returned fragments is outside each source's regime, as adversary 2 wrote.
