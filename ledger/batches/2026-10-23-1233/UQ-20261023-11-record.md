## Gate 0 — Intake

**Question (canonical, V1 as submitted):** In a declared seismic monitoring network with per-channel data-availability records, how much of the day-to-day variance in realized detection capability is produced by CORRELATED (common-cause) station downtime rather than independent downtime, and by how much does the measured correlation raise the probability of a multi-hour interval in which the network's realized detection threshold is worse than its published capability map by at least a declared margin ΔM?

**Question (canonical, V2 — the one Gate 1 reformulation, and the version scored):** For one declared network N over one declared window W, with (i) the station frame fixed in advance as the channels whose FDSN epoch covers ≥90% of W, (ii) the reference fixed in advance as a NAMED published capability or completeness product for N stated on a declared magnitude scale, and (iii) the parameter grid fixed in advance at clustering window w ∈ {15 min, 1 h, 6 h}, margin ΔM ∈ {0.2, 0.3, 0.5} magnitude units and duration T ∈ {1, 3, 6, 12} h — estimate, from per-channel availability records and a declared threshold model mapping the set of up channels at time t to a realized threshold field: (a) the share of downtime station-hours falling in multi-station coincident clusters in excess of the expectation under a per-station independent (Poisson or renewal) null, at each w, with a permutation interval; (b) the paired probabilities P(an interval of ≥T h in which the realized threshold exceeds the named reference by ≥ΔM), computed once under that independent null and once under the measured joint downtime process, reported as a ratio, over the (ΔM, T) grid; and (c) the share of the variance of the realized threshold attributable to correlated versus independent downtime under a declared variance decomposition.

**Why V2 (recorded per the one-reformulation rule):** V1 carried two well-posedness defects. First, ΔM, T and the clustering window were referred to as "declared" eleven times in the intake and enumerated nowhere — the exact defect the protocol's Gate 1 set-enumeration rule names. V2 **enumerates** the grid (it does not merely state a rule); the station frame is given by a **stated rule** (FDSN epoch coverage ≥90% of W), and I am saying which of the two I did for each. Second, "its published capability map" presupposed a unique reference object; V2 requires the reference to be a named document on a declared magnitude scale.

**WHAT THE REPAIR ITSELF PRESUPPOSES (tested as hard as the clause it replaced):**
- R1. That N has a published capability/completeness product expressible as a threshold FIELD on the **same magnitude scale** as the realized model. **CONTESTED, and this is the repair's weak point.** For the IMS the reference is a station-noise-based ≥3-station 90% detection-threshold map (commensurable). For most regional networks the published product is a catalogue-derived Mc map, which is a different estimand on a different scale from a noise-based threshold; subtracting one from the other is not defined. V2 therefore restricts the answerable instances to networks with a noise/signal-based published threshold product, which is a narrower population than V1 implied, and I am recording that narrowing rather than hiding it.
- R2. That the realized-threshold series exists at a resolution finer than T. Supported for the IMS primary network by prior A (10-second estimates), not established for any other network by anything I opened.
- R3. That "coincident cluster" at a declared w is identifiable from the availability record's own time stamps. Not established by anything I opened; see presupposition 1.

**Alternative formulations:**
- Do seismic stations go dark together, and if so how much does that inflate the chance of a detection blind window compared with the independent-availability picture behind published capability maps?
