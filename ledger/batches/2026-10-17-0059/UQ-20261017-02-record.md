Question (canonical, V0 as intaked): Across BIPM key comparisons, are a participating national metrology institute's deviations from the key-comparison reference value autocorrelated over successive comparisons of the same measurand — and correlated across different measurands within the same institute — to a degree that the current per-comparison treatment of degrees of equivalence leaves out of the uncertainty a calibration user inherits?

Question (canonical, R1 — ADOPTED at Gate 1; the single permitted reformulation, for well-posedness only): Over a frame of CIPM and RMO key comparisons declared before any data are pulled (comparison set, measurand set, inclusion rule for retrievable participant values, and the extraction rule, all fixed in advance, with the achieved extractable subset and its selection reported as part of the result), computing for each (institute, measurand, comparison, date) a LEAVE-ONE-OUT deviation against that comparison's own published reference-value recipe re-run with the assessed participant excluded, standardised by the participant's stated uncertainty, and coding documented method-generation changes as a covariate: (i) what share of standardised-deviation variance is attributable to an institute effect persistent across comparisons, (ii) what is the lag-1 autocorrelation of standardised leave-one-out deviations within (institute, measurand), (iii) what is the within-institute cross-measurand correlation of institute effects — each with an interval — and (iv) under a propagation rule stipulated in advance, what multiplicative inflation of a CMC-derived uncertainty follows if that persistent component were carried forward instead of each comparison outcome being treated as an independent draw?
(Why R1: V0's operative clause "to a degree that ... leaves out of the uncertainty a calibration user inherits" names no estimand, no comparator and no threshold; V0 also leaves the KCRV circularity unaddressed, which is fatal in small-N comparisons where the assessed participant partly determines the reference value. R1 fixes the comparator (leave-one-out), names four estimands, pre-commits the frame and extraction rule, and makes limb (iv) depend on a stipulated rule rather than an intuition. Both versions recorded as required.)

Alternative formulations: (1) Does a national metrology institute carry a persistent, institute-level measurement offset that successive key comparisons keep re-measuring rather than removing? (2) Is the independence that the per-comparison degrees-of-equivalence treatment assumes between one key comparison and the next actually present in the published comparison record? (3) Lay: if the same national laboratory comes out slightly high in one international comparison, is it likely to come out high in the next one too — and if so, is that extra uncertainty passed on to anyone who buys a calibration traceable to it?

Motivating gap: Presupposition inversion. The CIPM MRA publishes, per key comparison, each participant's degree of equivalence with respect to the comparison reference value, and the arrangement's logic is that each comparison establishes present equivalence on its own evidence. The inverted presupposition is independence across comparisons: the per-comparison deliverable carries no participant-level bias term forward, so a user who inherits traceability through one institute's CMC inherits an uncertainty built as if each comparison outcome were an independent draw. The record now makes the test possible — the same small set of institutes recurs across decades of comparisons of the same measurand, so a lag-1 autocorrelation and a within-institute cross-measurand correlation are estimable from public data. The intake's single motivating query returned only reference-value-choice methodology and individual comparison reports; that was one query and not a novelty finding, which is what Gate 2 below exists to settle. IMPORTANT CORRECTION FROM GATE 2: the strong form of the motivating presupposition is FALSE as stated. Cox & Shirono (Metrologia 2023, opened — see priors) model a common bias shared by one linking laboratory's values in two different comparisons and take the correlation between those two values as a model input. So the field does possess the concept of a participant-level effect shared across comparisons. What survives is the weak form: that correlation is supplied case by case from a named mechanism (a common reference standard or the same instrument) in the service of linking one comparison to another, and is nowhere estimated as a population quantity over the comparison record, nor propagated into delivered uncertainty. The candidate must be read against the weak form only.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
vs PRIOR 1 (Cox & Shirono 2023) — this is the one that matters and the reason the grade is not High. It establishes that a participant-level effect shared across two comparisons is already a modelled object in metrology, so the candidate's motivating claim that "nothing carries forward a participant-level bias term" is false in its strong form and must be narrowed to the weak form. The four attributes still differ on every axis. Unit: their unit is a laboratory's value-pair across one linked pair of comparisons; the candidate's is (institute, measurand, comparison, date) over a whole declared frame of comparisons spanning decades. Endpoint: theirs is the DoE of the RMO participants in one analysis; the candidate's are a variance share, a lag-1 autocorrelation, a cross-measurand correlation and an inherited-uncertainty inflation factor. Comparator: theirs is a single CIPM KCRV held fixed; the candidate's is each comparison's own reference-value recipe re-run leave-one-out with the assessed participant excluded. Estimand: theirs is a per-case DoE computed under an ASSUMED correlation supplied as input from a named physical mechanism; the candidate's is an ESTIMATED population-level persistence parameter with an interval, plus its consequence for delivered uncertainty. In short: Cox & Shirono use an assumed common bias as a nuisance input in order to link two comparisons; the candidate asks whether a persistent component exists across the record, how large it is, and what it does to the uncertainty a user inherits. Those are different questions over different objects, but anyone grading this must record that the concept is not foreign to the field.
vs PRIOR 2 (Hornikova & Guthrie, Survey) — it is the obvious "across all key comparisons" paper and it pools the right population for the wrong variable: unit is the comparison, endpoint is methodology, estimand is descriptive. It has no participant-level effect, no time ordering of a participant's successive results, no reference-value recomputation and no uncertainty propagation. It is evidence that the cross-comparison survey has been done at the level of method choice and not at the level of result structure.
vs PRIOR 3 (Hornikova & Guthrie, Troubleshooting) — same unit, same comparator, same absence of a quantitative estimand; "troubleshooting" means diagnosing heterogeneous practice in how comparisons are run, not detecting participants who are repeatedly off. Nothing in the abstract touches participant deviations or persistence.
vs PRIOR 4 (Thang & Nguyen) — explicitly single-comparison: b_i is a constant within the comparison and the paper is silent on whether it persists outside it. It is the clearest available documentation of what the standard DoE treatment does and does not contain, and it contains no cross-comparison term. It therefore supports the candidate's weak-form presupposition rather than defeating it.
vs PRIOR 5 (Analyst 2013) — one level down the traceability chain: a routine laboratory in a PT scheme, not an NMI at the top of it; the endpoint is how to assess and correct a laboratory's own bias, not the autocorrelation of standardised deviations from a comparison reference value, not a variance share attributable to a persistent institute effect, and not the inflation of an uncertainty inherited by downstream users. Significance — consequence: If YES (a material persistent component): three concrete changes follow, and they are not the same change. (a) The per-comparison degree of equivalence understates what a user inherits, so either the DoE acquires a persistence component or CMC review acquires one — the first is a change to the CC working groups' published deliverable, the second to RMO/JCRB review practice. (b) The KCRV itself is implicated: the weighted-mean and related estimators treat participants' errors as independent, so a shared persistent component among participants using a common method generation would bias the reference value, which is a problem one level more serious than the uncertainty understatement the question asks about. (c) The linking machinery's case-by-case correlation inputs (Prior 1) would have an empirical basis for becoming a default rather than an exception. If NO (null autocorrelation): the current treatment is positively vindicated with a number and an interval, which is at present assumed and not demonstrated anywhere I could find; and it simultaneously constrains Prior 1's common-bias model, which would be carrying a parameter the record does not support outside the cases with a named shared instrument. If the frame proves too thin: a documented coverage gap in the public comparison record, with a named recipient.

Reason for silence: Primary mechanism — an incentive/normative gap specific to the MRA. Within the arrangement a persistent participant deviation is a DEFECT TO BE INVESTIGATED AND REMOVED, not a parameter to be estimated: the remedy for a discrepant result is corrective action, a revised CMC, or a repeat comparison. Estimating the stable component across the record would be measuring the magnitude of something the system is constituted to drive to zero, and publishing it would amount to pricing in a bias that policy says should not persist — and it would do so with named institutes attached, since participants are identified in every final report. That is a reason to analyse per comparison and not across them, and it is a reason that acts on the question being asked at all rather than merely on its being answered. Reinforcing mechanisms, both weaker: (a) a unit-of-ownership mismatch — each comparison is owned by its own CC working group whose deliverable is one final report, the KCDB is organised as a per-comparison publication store rather than a participant-indexed longitudinal panel, and query 7 found that the KCDB-wide analytical documents that do exist are private CC/JC working documents; (b) data-form friction — participant values live in PDF final reports, so the panel only became assemblable as decades of reports accumulated behind a queryable database, which is a new-data mechanism rather than a conceptual one.
