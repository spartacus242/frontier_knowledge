## Restatement (my own words, before engaging the record's framing)

Five small bodies have now been touched by hardware (Itokawa, 67P, Ryugu, Bennu, Dimorphos). For each, go back to what was publishable *before* arrival — thermal inertia, spin-limit cohesion, radar polarisation ratio, boulder size-frequency, slope stability — freeze an estimator family, and ask: how wide an error bar on near-surface cohesion (and on penetration resistance at a stated depth) would an honest pre-encounter analyst have had to quote? Then check the arithmetic three ways. (a) How wide is that bar? (b) Did the post-encounter in-situ number land inside it, and if not, which way did it miss — with the post-encounter number labelled by how real it is (actual mechanical measurement / one-sided bound / a strength value backed out of an impact or slope model)? (c) Is the bar narrower than the step between the strength regimes that a sampler, a lander or a kinetic impactor is actually designed differently for — where those steps must be quoted from a real requirements document, not assumed to exist?

In short: stop saying "remote sensing worked" and compute the calibration. The pre-declared negative — "it covers, but the interval is too wide to be a design input" — is as publishable as the positive, and the question carries a forward limb (deposit sealed intervals for Hera at Didymos and for Apophis in 2029).

Two things I note before attacking. First, the question's *object* is not asteroid strength; it is the calibration of a class of inference. Second, limb (c) is the only limb that makes the headline ("narrow enough") meaningful, and it is the limb whose presupposition Gate 2 could not confirm.

### What the two reads actually say (quotes I rely on)

Groussin et al. 2019, on 67P — the one body where remote-derived and in-situ strengths both exist in quantity:
- Remote, from imaging/shape: tensile 3–15 Pa and compressive 30–150 Pa at 5–30 m scale (Groussin et al. 2015); cohesion 1–2 Pa at tens of metres (Vincent et al. 2017); minimum tensile 0.02–1.02 Pa for overhangs (Attree et al. 2018a).
- In-situ, from Philae: compressive 3.5–12 kPa (Möhlmann et al. 2018); "uniaxial compressive strength of about 10 kPa or less" (Biele et al. 2015); average 2 kPa rising 3 kPa per metre (Roll & Arnold 2016); "at least 2 MPa" at Abydos (MUPUS, Spohn et al. 2015); 18 kPa–9800 kPa from Young's modulus under an assumed E/σc ≈ 100–400 (Knapmeyer et al. 2018).
- Direct quotes: "the strength is a scale dependent parameter"; "The general trend is a decreasing of the strength with increasing scale"; the material dependence of the scaling law "makes the comparison between the strengths determined from observations, laboratory experiments and modelling difficult"; "Reconciling low bulk strengths and granular materials with stiff/brittle surface features is a difficult challenge"; "The properties of the top layer (the first meter) are not representative of that of the bulk nucleus"; "ground truth even from a single point would be a valuable constraint to improve the existing models".
- **My own arithmetic** from the quoted values, labelled as mine: the gap between the remote compressive range (30–150 Pa, at 5–30 m scale) and the Philae-derived compressive values (2 kPa to ≥2 MPa, at cm scale, and at two different sites) is roughly 1.1 to 4.8 decades depending on which pair is taken. I do *not* attribute an "orders of magnitude" headline to the paper: it states scale dependence and the reconciliation difficulty, and does not publish a prediction-error figure. The paper also does not compare pre-landing predictions with Philae's measurements.

Raducan et al. 2024, on Dimorphos: cohesion "less than a few pascals (Pa)" for the best-fit case, but "must not exceed ≈ 500 Pa" to match ejecta, and "likely lower than ≈ 50 Pa" for f = 0.4, φ0 = 45% — i.e. the post-encounter constraint moves across ~2 decades with the assumed friction/porosity. Mass and bulk density "were not directly measured"; surface properties "were also not directly measured"; "Multiple possible combinations of cohesion, coefficient of internal friction, and bulk density could result" in the observed deflection. It makes no comparison to pre-impact remote strength predictions (its only pre/post comparison is bulk density, pre-impact range 1500–3300 kg/m³), and it says nothing about predicting strength from remote data before arrival. This corroborates the record's prior 4 rather than overturning it, and it means the second of the two flagged papers moved the grade in neither direction.

**Promotion conditions:** (1) quote at least one numeric strength/bearing-capacity threshold from a real mission requirement document, or rewrite limb (c) to measure interval width against the published post-encounter constraint widths instead; (2) state how the measurand map's own uncertainty (scale dependence, depth, packing) is carried into the containment statement rather than only declared; (3) read s41467-024-50147-w and Walsh et al. 2022 Sci Adv and settle whether any body reaches tier A; (4) add Groussin et al. 2019 as prior 5 and distinguish from it explicitly.

**Single strongest killing point (does not kill, but is the one to beat):** at the only body where remote-derived and in-situ strengths both exist in quantity, the primary review states that strength "is a scale dependent parameter" whose material-dependent scaling law "makes the comparison between the strengths determined from observations, laboratory experiments and modelling difficult", and that "the properties of the top layer (the first meter) are not representative of that of the bulk nucleus" — so the gap between remote (30–150 Pa at 5–30 m) and in-situ (2 kPa to ≥2 MPa at cm scale) values, which by my own arithmetic from those quoted numbers is roughly 1.1–4.8 decades, is read by the field as signal rather than prediction error. A containment count therefore scores the declared measurand map at least as much as it scores the prediction, and until the map's own uncertainty is bounded, limb (b)'s signed errors are not attributable and limb (c) may be decided before any data are pulled.
