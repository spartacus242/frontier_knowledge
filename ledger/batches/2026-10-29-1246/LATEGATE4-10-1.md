## 2. Restatement, in my own words

Programs running on JIT-compiling VMs often never settle to a stable per-iteration speed even under the tightest controls (Barrett 2017). One suspect is the VM's own adaptive machinery: it watches the program, recompiles on what it sees, the new code changes what it sees, and so on. The question asks whether that self-referential loop is what keeps performance from settling. Operationally: take one VM and one program; in one run let the VM keep making compilation decisions throughout; in another let it decide only up to a chosen point and then forbid new decisions. Does the second run settle while the first does not? (The spent reformulation turns this into a between-arm difference in the share of executions a pre-declared changepoint rule calls non-convergent, with a pinned pilot plan and a sham-freeze arm.)

### Attack 2 — Disguise (8 new-vocabulary queries executed; all listed in section 5)

- §5.2: "The large number of non-warmup cases in our data led us to make the following hypothesis: H3 Non-warmup process executions are largely due to JIT compilation or GC events." Then: "The relatively few results we have with GC and JIT compilation events, and the lack of a clear message from them, means that we feel unable to validate or invalidate Hypothesis H3. Whilst some non-warmups are plausibly explained by GC or JIT compilation events, many are not, at least on HotSpot and PyPy. When there is no clear correlation, we have very little idea of a likely cause of the unexpected behaviour."
- The JIT disjunct was examined on its own, not only inside the disjunction. §1.1: "in an attempt to see if there are easy explanations for the odd effects we see, we take two VMs and separate out the time taken to perform Garbage Collection (GC) and JIT compilation. Fig. 7 is the JIT-only example; Fig. 6 the GC-only example.
- The authors also name the adaptive machinery as the suspected source of mid-run changes, §9.1.1: "VMs have many counters which, when they exceed a certain threshold, cause the VM to change behaviour (e.g. (re)compile code, change garbage collection strategy etc.) … we might observe VM effects at in-process iterations around this point", and: "if one is confident that the VM(s) do not cause unpredictable performance changes, then it may be possible to run many fewer in-process iterations".
Post-warmup "JIT compilation events" in an adaptive VM are the outputs of the profile-guided (re)compilation loop; the candidate's phrase names the mechanism that produces Barrett's events. The candidate's own intervention — forbid further compilation decisions — removes exactly those events, so operationally the frozen-versus-live contrast tests whether post-freeze compilation events cause the non-convergence: H3's JIT disjunct, interventionally. (One definitional step here is mine: a compilation decision is what produces a compilation event, so freezing decisions removes the events. Deoptimization is not a compilation event and continues under a freeze; that cuts toward attack 4, not toward the two questions differing.)
Which clause of the LEDGER's definitions applies: **"Open problem: asked but unanswered."** H3 is asked in print, its JIT disjunct separately examined, and declared unsettled by its authors; the vocabulary shift ("profile-guided recompilation feedback loop" for post-warmup "JIT compilation events") makes the candidate the **disguised form of that open problem**, i.e. "an asked … question in new vocabulary". Either way it is not for this ledger; as asked-but-unanswered it routes.
**Best response for the candidate.** (1) H3 is a disjunction, and an open disjunction does not pose each disjunct — but §1.1 and Fig. 7 show the JIT disjunct was separated and examined on its own. (2) H3 is about recorded events coinciding with changes, whereas the loop is a causal structure whose effects need not coincide with an event — but no document draws that distinction, and the candidate's own design (stop the decisions) collapses it: both are removed by freezing. (3) The sham-controlled freeze design and the between-arm estimand are not in Barrett, who propose only "obtaining similar data from other VMs" — true, but the LEDGER places "what would count as an answer" at Gate 1; a design for settling an asked question is not itself an un-asked question, and the reformulation was "for well-posedness only". ### Attack 4 — Presupposition failure (conditioning clauses numbered and graded)
- **C1. JIT-compiled programs fail to reach a steady state.** TRUE in Barrett's regime (abstract, opened: "at most [43.5]% of ⟨VM, benchmark⟩ pairs consistently reach a steady state of peak performance"; Antoch 2026 Table 1 tally 20/25 bad inconsistent, VERIFY-10-b). Magnitude depends on the reading: the strict "no steady state" class is a minority of executions — 8.7 / 9.6 / 2.8% of process executions and 4.3 / 6.5 / 0.0% of pairs consistently (Linux4790 / Linux1240v5 / OpenBSD4790), **cells as read by VERIFY-10-a from a third-party extraction with that extraction's class-key order; the LaTeX table is an `\input` I did not open** — while the 43.5% headline counts slowdowns (steady states at a worse level) and inconsistency. See attack 5.
- **C2. The VMs have a profile-guided recompilation loop that is LIVE during the non-converging phase.** CONTESTED, VM-dependent. HotSpot bounds it per method: PR #28966 (opened) "Default: 201 (derived from default PerMethodRecompilationCutoff = 400)", after which "the VM performs deoptimization but does not recompile the method anymore". HHVM is one-shot by design: Jump-Start §II-B (opened) "the JIT stops profiling new code and starts tier-2 compilation"; "JITing ceases and the system finally achieves its peak performance". Tracing JITs have no per-method plan (verification item 6). Barrett §9.1.1 (opened) shows threshold-driven VM changes do occur mid-run (JRuby+Truffle around iteration 1050). So the loop is live mid-run for part of the matrix and designed to halt for part of it.
- **C3. Compilation decisions can be frozen after a declared point WITHOUT changing the executed code.** Instrument half: CONTESTED/UNVERIFIED — an instrument of the right form exists for at least one matrix VM, LuaJIT (opened): "Turns the whole JIT compiler on (default) or off"; whether already-compiled traces survive the whole-engine `jit.off()` is NOT FOUND on the page, and the per-function form "disables JIT compilation for a Lua function and flushes any already compiled code from the code cache" — i.e. changes the code. PyPy's jit-hooks page, HotSpot's WhiteBox test API (CompilerWhiteBoxTest.java) and runtime compiler directives were returned and not opened. Code-preservation half: **FALSE for speculative JITs.** Schiavio §2.2 (opened): "the JVM performs deoptimization, invalidating the compiled code and falling back to interpretation." Deoptimization is a correctness mechanism that continues under any freeze of compilation; with recompilation forbidden, the method stays in the interpreter (my inference from that sentence: the only thing that would restore compiled code is a compilation decision). PR #28966 (opened) documents this very regime's pathology — deoptimization "but does not recompile the method anymore" → "thousands of deoptimizations per second" → "significant performance drop". So the frozen arm is "the same code until the first failed speculation, then slower code", and has its own mechanism for non-convergence. The record's reading rule — "no drop locates the cause outside the compiler" — is wrong in that direction: a frozen arm that fails to converge because of stranded deoptimized paths is the compiler's speculation plus the freeze, not an exogenous cause, and the sham arm shares the same fallback behaviour so does not control for it.
- **C4. Convergence is a property of the (VM, program) PAIR** ("the same pair with the loop live does not"). FALSE for about half the pairs: Barrett (opened) "in almost half of cases, the same benchmark on the same VM on the same machine has more than one performance characteristic" (bad inconsistent 45.7 / 43.5 / 70.0% of pairs, cells per VERIFY-10-a). The spent reformulation repairs this by comparing proportions of executions across arms — the candidate's valid response.
- **C5. Steady state is decidable by a pre-declared changepoint rule.** TRUE in kind (Barrett, opened: "we (somewhat arbitrarily) define that a process execution reaches a steady-state if all segments which cover the last 500 in-process iterations are considered equivalent to the final segment"); CONTESTED as to stability of the class under the horizon (§9.1.1 kinks).
- **C6. The phenomenon persists on current releases of matrix VMs that could run the freeze.** UNVERIFIED by me; Antoch is Java 11/Renaissance (VERIFY-10-b); Schiavio §8 (opened) cites it as known: "Even though benchmark performance can oscillate indefinitely [1]"; Traini unopened beyond its abstract by anyone.
- **C7. Freezing does not trivially produce convergence by removing all variation.** CONTESTED, as the record says (Georges: nondeterminism remains under replay).
**Best response for the candidate.** Define the frozen arm as "no new compilation decisions after the point" and read the contrast as a difference-making test in both directions: if the frozen arm converges and the live one does not, the loop's continued action is destabilising; if the frozen arm shows new non-convergence (stranded interpreter paths), the loop's recompile-after-deoptimization action is stabilising — informative either way, with recompile-after-deopt counted as a loop action. C4 is met by the reformulation's proportion estimand. C3's instrument half is open, not closed: LuaJIT's whole-engine switch exists and its trace-retention semantics are checkable.
**Status: PARTIAL.** The presupposition that a freeze isolates re-decision from code is false for speculative JITs and the record's interpretation rule inherits that error; but the question survives under a corrected, difference-making reading, so the best response is plausible rather than weak or circular. Load-bearing: documents I opened (Schiavio §2.2; PR #28966; LuaJIT doc; Barrett LaTeX) plus one inference of mine (stranding), labelled.

### Attack 5 — Vagueness
**Objection.** "Fail to reach a steady state" is ambiguous between three phenomena with different plausible relations to the loop: (i) Barrett's strict "no steady state" class (a minority of executions, cells above); (ii) "not a steady state of peak performance", the 56.5%+ headline complement, which counts slowdowns — steady states at a worse level — and inconsistency; (iii) cross-execution inconsistency, which Georges 2008 (record's read) shows is driven in part by plan variation across runs. The spent reformulation says "classified non-convergent by a changepoint rule declared before any execution is timed" without saying whether slowdown or inconsistency count, and "frozen" is under-specified (the record's own deduction (i)). Barrett (opened) also show the class is horizon-sensitive (§9.1.1).
**Best response.** A rule fixed before timing makes the estimand a number either way, which is fine for an effect-size question; Barrett's class definitions are explicit and can be adopted verbatim; pre-registration "count[s] neither way" per the LEDGER.
**Status: PARTIAL.** The pre-declared rule removes "answered no matter what", but which phenomenon the canonical wording targets is unrepaired, the reformulation is spent, and the answer plausibly differs by reading. Load-bearing: Barrett LaTeX (opened); cells via VERIFY-10-a.

### Attack 6 — Significance inflation
**Objection.** The methodology community has already decided to accommodate rather than diagnose (Antoch 2026, VERIFY-10-b: lack of steady state "should be considered an inherent property of these workloads rather than a fault"); VM implementers fix loop pathologies case by case and would not act on a population share.
**Best response.** The audience is demonstrably real and active in 2026: Antoch et al. (eight authors incl. Oracle Labs) extend Barrett; Schiavio et al. cite indefinite oscillation as known; HotSpot engineers are fixing a 17-year-old deoptimization-storm defect with a reported ~10x effect (PR #28966, opened); HHVM built Jump-Start partly for steady-state throughput (5.4% RPS, opened). A large loop-attributed share would challenge the "inherent property" stance; a small one would license it as published fact. Either outcome changes a stance someone holds.
**Status: RESOLVED** (audience real; consequence plausible), with the caveat that Antoch's stance lowers the appetite for a single-cause test.

### Attack 7 — Reason for silence
**Objection.** For the runtime-freeze version of the canonical question the instrument is trivial and old: LuaJIT's `jit.off()` (opened) and PyPy's JIT hooks (returned) are command-level switches in two matrix VMs the anchor authors know intimately, so "split instrument / disciplinary boundary" (the record's mechanism) does not explain why nobody flipped the switch after warmup and classified the result. Candidate mechanisms that do fit: (a) the experts see the confound of attack 4 (freeze strands deoptimized paths), so the experiment is judged uninformative as a cause test; (b) VM engineers meet loop pathologies as individual bugs with no incentive to measure a population share (PR #28966 is the type); (c) the measurement community's stance that non-steadiness is "inherent" (Antoch) discourages single-cause tests; (d) Barrett's own data already show "many" unexplained cases, so the expected share was small. (a) is "they thought of it and judged it confounded", which the LEDGER says makes the novelty finding suspect; (b)–(c) are incentive and disciplinary mechanisms of the accepted kind.
**Best response.** (b) and (c) are credible mechanisms of the accepted kind, and the sham-controlled, pre-declared-rule design is from experimental science, not systems benchmarking, where a flag flip is a speed comparison.
**Status: PARTIAL.** A credible mechanism exists for the Jikes-style design; for the runtime-freeze version the silence is better explained by the experiment being seen as confounded, which supports attacks 2 and 4 rather than the candidate. Load-bearing: LuaJIT doc, PR #28966 (opened); Antoch via VERIFY-10-b; the mechanism ranking is mine.

### Attack 8 — Dissolution
**Objection.** "Loop versus exogenous" is a false dichotomy: Antoch §1 (VERIFY-10-b) frames the loop as a propagator — "execution differences can also influence feedback-directed just-in-time compilation and therefore expand their impact to future workload repetitions" — and Antoch §3 attributes one change to "a combination of factors"; deoptimization blurs "loop on/off" (attack 4); the likely answer is "some pairs yes, some no, and in some the freeze itself creates non-convergence", which dissolves the canonical yes/no into a per-pair, per-mechanism accounting.
**Best response.** Under the interventionist reading a propagator that is a difference-maker is a cause, and the between-arm share remains a well-defined number whatever the mechanism mix.
**Status: PARTIAL.** The quantity survives; the canonical yes/no and its "inside/outside the compiler" gloss do not.
