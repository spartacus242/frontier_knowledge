## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take one genome-scale single-gene CRISPRi Perturb-seq matrix (~10⁴ perturbations × ~10⁴ measured genes). The published reading of that matrix goes down the perturbation column: *which knockdowns did anything?* (answer on record: 31.1%). The candidate turns the matrix sideways and reads the gene row: of the genes you measured well enough to say anything about at all, how many were never shifted by **any** of the ~10⁴ perturbations — and it insists on splitting that non-mover set in two, "measured with adequate power at a declared effect size and still did not move" versus "too thinly sampled to tell". Then: do the same genes stay unmoved in a second cell line, more often than chance given each line's class sizes? Everything hard about it is in the declaration — what counts as a response when you are taking the union over 10⁴ chances to be called, and what counts as "powered" when power differs per gene and per perturbation.

Said plainly: *how much of a cell's measured transcriptome is out of reach of switching off one gene at a time, as opposed to merely out of reach of the sequencer?*
