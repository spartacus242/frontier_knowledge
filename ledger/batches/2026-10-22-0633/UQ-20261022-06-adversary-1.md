## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

When a hospital laboratory re-measures an analyte on the SAME tube — because the first value was critical, failed a delta check against the patient's prior, needed diluting, or was checked on a second instrument — and the value that leaves the laboratory is the second measurement rather than the first, (a) what fraction of everything the laboratory reports is such a self-given second opinion, broken down by why it was taken; (b) how do the first-value and reported-value distributions differ; and (c) would reference intervals, the prevalence of results crossing a decision limit, and the serial-monitoring delta come out differently if computed on first values instead of reported ones, by more than a bias bound fixed in advance — plus, how many laboratories can even retrieve the discarded first values?
