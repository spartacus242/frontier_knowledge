# UQ-20261017-44 — Adversary #1 log

## Restatement (my own words)

Take every theorem in a human-written Lean/mathlib-style library or blueprint project that is explicitly tagged as "this is Theorem X of paper/book Y". Read Theorem X in the source, applying a fixed rule about which standing conventions ("throughout, rings are Noetherian") count as hypotheses. Compare the formal statement to it. Count how often the formal version proves MORE (fewer/weaker hypotheses, fewer axioms, generalized structures) and how often it proves LESS (extra hypotheses the formalizer had to add). Split the count into what tools can flag automatically (axiom footprint, binders never used, typeclass weaker than the paper's structure) and what two humans must judge, with their agreement reported. Then ask whether the "formal proves more" share depends on how old, how long, or in which subfield the source is. The candidate's claim is that nobody has produced such a signed, source-anchored rate for human formalizations of published mathematics.
