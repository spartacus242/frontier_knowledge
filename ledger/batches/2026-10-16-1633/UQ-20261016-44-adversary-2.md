## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take one hospital's pathology archive. Pick one cancer diagnosis. Pull blocks from at least three accession eras (say 1970s / 1990s / 2010s). Have a present-day pathologist re-read every era's slides against one current protocol and re-stain fresh sections from the block, so that "the same diagnosis" means the same thing in all three eras — never trusting the original report. Run one molecular panel, fixed in advance, on all the cases in a single run with batch order randomised. Then ask three things. (a) Do the tumours differ molecularly by era? (b) How much of any era difference is just the block being old — estimated from controls matched to each era: the patient's own non-tumour tissue from the same block, and same-era blocks of other diagnoses? (c) Is the era difference that remains, reported with an interval, larger than the molecular gap between cohorts that trials already accept when they borrow a historical or external control arm? "At the sample size an archive can supply, no era effect can be told apart from the artefact" counts in advance as a real answer.

In one line: *is the molecular exchangeability that historical-control borrowing silently assumes testable in archival tissue, and does it survive — with block-age artefact subtracted rather than assumed away?*

### Correction to the record, independent of the above
The motivating gap's claim that exchangeability "is argued entirely from clinical covariates" and rests on a **silent** molecular premise is overstated against the primary document I opened. The FDA externally-controlled-trials guidance names era comparability repeatedly and explicitly — changing diagnostic criteria, changing natural history, changing standard of care, and biomarker assay comparability. The premise is not silent; it is named and unquantified. The gap is narrower than the record states, which lowers rhetorical force while leaving the untested-at-the-molecular-level claim standing.
