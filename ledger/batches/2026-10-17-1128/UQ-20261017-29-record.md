Question (canonical — Gate 1 reformulated version, used from here on):
On a dated, pinned frame F of the public post-training data pool (an enumerable, snapshot-fixed set of public instruction-tuning and preference datasets, inclusion rule declared before any data pull), under a pre-declared normalization N (strip system prompts/templates/field names to a canonical (instruction, [context], response-or-preference-pair) form) and a pre-declared ladder of at least three near-duplicate thresholds t1<t2<t3: (a) what is the example-level duplication graph across datasets — pairwise cross-dataset overlap shares, the pool's de-duplicated example count |F|_dedup(t), and the distribution over distinct examples of the number of named datasets each appears in; (b) for at least three published mixtures, what are the realized distinct-example proportions of the components against the declared weights, under a pre-declared attribution rule for an example appearing in several components (fractional split / first-wins / set-of-origins), reporting component rank order before and after; and (c) what is the retrievability-adjusted version of (a), stating the unretrievable share of F at the snapshot date?

Question (Gate 0 original version, recorded as required):
Treating the public post-training data pool as one object, what is its example-level duplication and re-export structure — what fraction of examples in widely used instruction and preference mixtures are near-duplicates of examples in other datasets in the pool, what is the pool's de-duplicated size, and what do named mixture weights therefore actually weight?

Reformulation justification (Gate 1, one reformulation used, for well-posedness only): the original leaves three things undefined that decide the answer — the frame (which datasets, at what date), what "the same example" means across differing templates, and how an example present in k components is charged against k declared weights. Limb (b) is not evaluable without that last rule: declared weights sum to 1 over records, so without an attribution rule the "realized proportion of distinct examples" has no denominator. The reformulation adds F, N, the threshold ladder and the attribution rule, and changes nothing about what is asked.

Alternative formulations:
1. How many distinct post-training examples does the open ecosystem contain, as opposed to how many dataset records it distributes?
2. When a post-training recipe mixes k named datasets at declared proportions, how far are the realized proportions of distinct examples from the declared ones once cross-dataset re-export is removed?

Motivating gap:
New-instrument question. Hub-scale queryability of the public dataset ecosystem (automatic Parquet conversion plus SQL over the hub, standardized dataset metadata) together with cheap billion-scale near-duplicate detection makes the UNION of public instruction and preference datasets addressable as a single object; previously each dataset could practically be examined only alone. Post-training recipes are specified as weighted mixtures of NAMED datasets, while the field's deduplication effort is within-corpus (Lee et al. 2022) or against benchmarks (decontamination). Known re-export chains (Alpaca-derived sets; preference sets reappearing inside later mixtures) mean one example can enter a mixture through several named components, so a declared weight on a dataset is not a weight on distinct content. Two documents opened this run attest the phenomenon in passing and decline to quantify it:
- Data Provenance Initiative, §2: "Some collections have overlapping datasets and examples, but we choose not to deduplicate" (reason given: preserve original design choices — templates, formatting, filtering). No overlap quantity is reported.
- Tulu 3: "Since many publicly released datasets are compositions of other datasets, we have to manually track the provenance" — and the paper reports no cross-component dedup and no distinct-example counts.

Presuppositions (status after Gate 2):
1. The public post-training datasets are enumerable and retrievable as a frame at hub scale. CONTESTED IN DEGREE — gated, relicensed and deleted datasets exist, so the frame must be pinned to a dated snapshot and the unretrievable share reported (limb (c)). Ledger neighbour UQ-20261016-46 is this ledger's own warning about manifest-based frames.
2. "The same example" is definable across datasets whose formatting differs. CONTESTED; the main design obligation. Pre-declared normalization plus a threshold ladder with sensitivity reporting, not a single number. Supported indirectly by Tulu 3's own account of why it avoided embedding matching: it "found it difficult to distinguish mere distributional similarity from actual paraphrasing" — i.e. the threshold choice is a known hard point in this exact literature, which is a reason to report a curve rather than a scalar.
3. Mixture weights are intended as weights on distinct content rather than on dataset records. CONTESTED — a curator may have intended record weights. The question must report the discrepancy and let the community say which it wanted. Tulu 3's Table 15 is per-source record counts summed to totals, which is consistent with record-weighting as the de facto convention; so the question is a bookkeeping discrepancy report, not an imputation of intent.
4. Duplication at the measured level changes training outcomes enough to matter. NOT ASSUMED — it is the stake, not a premise. Only existing evidence that repetition matters is within-corpus dedup work, whose unit and endpoint differ. Limbs (a)–(c) are bookkeeping and stand whatever the training effect is.
5. The Data Provenance Initiative's lineage audit did not already compute example-level cross-dataset overlap. NOW VERIFIED by reading the paper (ar5iv full text): unit of analysis is the dataset (1,858 datasets in 44 collections), endpoint is licence/creator/source/task/language/format descriptors, and the paper states explicitly that it does not deduplicate. Presupposition holds.
6. Near-duplicate detection at pool scale is computationally feasible at the declared thresholds. Plausible, unbenchmarked here; the method field (data management) routinely runs repository-scale redundancy detection (R2D2, arXiv:2312.13427 — SURFACED, NOT OPENED), which is weak support, not a benchmark.

What would count as an answer:
On the pinned frame F at date T: (a) the duplication graph at normalization N and thresholds t1,t2,t3 — pairwise cross-dataset overlap shares, |F|_dedup(t) as a curve in t, and the multiplicity distribution (how many named datasets each distinct example appears in); (b) for ≥3 published mixtures (e.g. a Tulu-class SFT mix, a preference mix, a large aggregated collection), realized distinct-example proportions vs declared weights under the declared attribution rule, with component rank order before and after; (c) the retrievability-adjusted (a) with the unretrievable share of F stated. Either direction answers: a pool nearly duplicate-free at every threshold licenses current mixture practice and retires a worry voiced in passing by DPI and Tulu 3; a pool that is largely re-exports tells curators their declared weights are not the weights they apply.

Significance — consequence:
If the pool is largely re-exports: declared mixture weights are weights on records, not on distinct content, so (i) every published mixture-ablation conclusion of the form "component X helps" is partly a conclusion about multiplicity rather than content, because the compared mixtures differ in realized distinct-content composition in a way nobody reported; (ii) "N million examples" scaling claims for post-training deflate by a measured factor, and the pool's de-duplicated size becomes the honest denominator for data-scarcity arguments about post-training data; (iii) curators gain a concrete action — add a cross-dataset dedup step and publish distinct-content weights beside declared ones. If the pool is nearly duplicate-free at every threshold: current practice is licensed, the worry that P1 recorded and set aside and that P3 handled by manual provenance tracking is retired with a number, and redundancy research is correctly confined to within-dataset selection. Note the consequence does not depend on the untested training-effect premise: limbs (a)–(c) are bookkeeping, and the bookkeeping discrepancy is itself the first-order result.
Significance — audience:
Mixture curators who publish weighted recipes and would change what they publish and what they dedup (AI2 Tulu/OLMo-class post-training teams; BAAI Infinity-Instruct; HF SmolTalk-class collections); preference-data and reward-model builders, for whom duplicate prompts across sources inflate apparent agreement; data-provenance auditors, who have an explicit open methodological choice here (P1's "we choose not to deduplicate") that a measured overlap share would let them revisit; and hub/dataset-infrastructure teams, who would be the ones to expose pool-level duplicate structure as metadata. P3 shows the action is live: its authors are already tracking composition-of-other-datasets manually.

Opens: does cross-dataset multiplicity act on training like epoch repetition does (joining the multiplicity measurement to the repetition/multi-epoch scaling literature)? Reason for silence:
Four mechanisms, none of them "no one thought of it". (1) New instrument: the union was not addressable until hub-scale Parquet auto-conversion plus SQL-over-the-hub made thousands of post-training datasets queryable without downloading each, and cheap MinHash/LSH made billion-scale near-duplicate detection routine; before that, the practical unit of work was one dataset loaded alone, which is exactly the unit every prior above uses. (2) Unit mismatch inherited from pretraining engineering: deduplication in this field is owned by corpus-cleaning-before-a-run, where the deliverable is one clean corpus (P2 measures per-corpus prevalence with exact hashing for precisely this reason), so the pool was never an object anyone was responsible for. (3) Incentive and ownership gap: no actor owns the union. Each curator is accountable for their own release, and quantifying re-export would deflate the record and download counts datasets are credited by; P1's "we choose not to deduplicate … to preserve the original design choices" is the locally rational choice, documented in the paper, and it leaves the pool-level number unowned. (4) Disciplinary boundary: repository-scale redundancy is a named problem family in data management (data-lake redundancy, overlap/containment estimation) and in information science (overlap and unique-coverage analysis of bibliographic databases), whose vocabulary ML data curation does not share — so the ML field's searches for "deduplication" return within-corpus work and never the overlap-analysis design.
