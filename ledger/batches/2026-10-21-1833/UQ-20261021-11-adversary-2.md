## 1. Restatement in my own words

Fix one training recipe. Run it several times, changing nothing but the random-number stream that draws the initial weights (and dropout masks); keep the data and even the data order identical. In each finished model, look at the residual stream and find the handful of channels whose activations are enormous ("massive activations"), counting magnitudes separately for BOS-type tokens, delimiters and ordinary content tokens.

Question: do the *same dimension numbers* come out hot in every run? Not how many hot channels there are, not which layers they live in, not how big they get — the actual addresses. Measured as per-layer Jaccard overlap of the top-m index sets with CIs, at three or more thresholds taken from the source literature's own individuation rules, against (a) a uniform index-permutation null and (b) a statistics-matched null; plus, as a second limb, whether the whole d_model-length per-channel magnitude ranking is preserved (Spearman rho).

Stake as the record frames it: if the addresses recur, an outlier-channel layout is a property of the recipe and a numeric format, kernel or calibration could in principle be fixed once per recipe family; if they do not, the address is symmetry breaking and every layout must be re-derived per artifact, and every "dimension 2533 does X" claim is a claim about one run.

## 3. The source my kill rests on

**arXiv:2606.31963 — John Sweeney (Sideplane AI, single author, taken from the front matter of the PDF, not from a short form), "Signed-Permutation Coordinate Transport for RMSNorm Transformers".**
**READ LEVEL:** summariser-mediated targeted read of the PDF (front matter, experimental setup, cross-seed statements, metric definitions). Not a full read.
**REGIME:** population = Qwen2.5-1.5B same-base fine-tune pairs (18 pairs: 9 cross-seed, 9 cross-dataset), plus Llama-2-7B / TinyLlama-1.1B / Qwen2.5 gauge-invariance, steering, SAE and LoRA transfer checks, plus one custom 10M-parameter 4-layer d=256 RMSNorm transformer trained from scratch on identical data with different seeds; unit of analysis = a residual coordinate (within a model pair); operating condition = mostly a *known, randomly sampled gauge* applied to one model and then recovered by an alignment method, plus the one genuinely independent from-scratch seed pair; instrument = alignment-recovery accuracy, per-coordinate **sign** agreement, and a **defined** top-k overlap metric (k = 10, ranked by activation magnitude); threshold = none for massive activations; category = completeness of coordinate alignment under RMSNorm gauge symmetry.
**NUMBER, and where it is printed:** the only cross-seed number is "about 50% sign mismatch", reported in §5 and the appendix for the independently seeded 10M RMSNorm pair; the quantity is per-coordinate sign agreement with coordinates (d = 256) as the denominator. **The paper's top-k activation-magnitude overlap metric is DEFINED and never measured across independently trained models** — no Jaccard, agreement rate or overlap number for hot-coordinate identity appears. So this source **DEFINES and BOUNDS** the candidate's quantity; it does not publish it. My kill is therefore a *triviality* kill, not an *already-published* kill, and I say so explicitly.
**Verbatim fragments (each returned whole, under the summariser's quotation cap; not a reconstruction):**
- "Thus permutation-only alignment is symmetry-incomplete for RMSNorm models."
- "coordinate names are reproducible only relative to an explicit gauge."
- "two RMSNorm transformers trained from different seeds develop about 50% sign mismatch without an imposed gauge"

**Caples & Neuhaus (with rrenaud), "Adam Optimizer Causes Privileged Basis in Transformer LM Residual Stream", LessWrong, 6 Sep 2024.** READ LEVEL: full post, summariser-mediated.
**REGIME:** population = one 12M-parameter transformer LM on TinyStories; unit = residual-stream channel magnitudes (excess kurtosis over training); operating condition = Adam vs SGD-with-momentum, **one run each, seed not varied**; instrument = excess kurtosis; threshold = none; category = optimizer cause of a privileged basis. **NUMBERS:** Adam excess kurtosis "over 100" by end of training; SGD-momentum "the flat green line at 0". Verbatim: "We conclusively show that Adam causes outlier channels / privileged basis within the transformer residual stream." It reports **nothing** about whether the same indices recur across runs — I checked for exactly that and the post does not address it.

**My own derivation, labelled as mine (not any source's conclusion).** A standard decoder-only transformer is *equivariant* under a permutation of residual-stream coordinates with the weights conjugated: every op touching the residual basis is either a linear map that absorbs the permutation (embedding, Q/K/V, out-proj, MLP in/out, unembedding) or an elementwise op whose per-channel parameter vector permutes with it (LayerNorm/RMSNorm gain and bias, residual addition); the MLP's elementwise nonlinearity lives in the MLP hidden basis, and rotary acts inside head dimensions. Standard initialisations draw entries i.i.d., so the init measure is permutation-invariant; AdamW is elementwise, so it commutes with a coordinate permutation; the loss depends only on logits. Hence the law of a trained run is invariant under the group action, the top-m index set at a given layer is an **exchangeable** random set, and two runs with independent RNG are independent draws — so the expected cross-seed Jaccard overlap equals the candidate's own uniform-permutation null, and the expected cross-seed Spearman rho on the full d_model magnitude vector is zero. Note the scope: this needs only *permutation* equivariance, which holds for LayerNorm as well as RMSNorm (LayerNorm's mean subtraction breaks only the *sign* part of the gauge), so it does not depend on the RMSNorm-specific strengthening in arXiv:2606.31963. It is exact only in exact arithmetic: floating-point reduction order, GEMM tiling and tensor-parallel sharding do make a coordinate index physically realised, and that is the one channel through which a non-chance answer could arise. I found no source claiming such an effect, and I am not asserting it cannot exist.

Consistency check against the record's own index lists, so the derivation is not inverted: Puccetti prints bert-base-uncased (308, 381) against multiberts-seed-1 (218, 674), and arXiv:2606.20743 prints 828 / 550 / 22 across its configurations — every printed cross-artifact pair in the record is non-overlapping, which is what exchangeability predicts. Nothing I opened or surfaced claims that massive-activation indices coincide across independently initialised models.

## 5. Which leg the verdict stands on

The verdict stands on **attack 1 alone**: the permutation-equivariance / exchangeability derivation, which pins the candidate's primary endpoint to the candidate's own primary null. If that leg fell — if someone showed that implementation-level asymmetry or some non-equivariant component of real recipes produces a shared index preference — attacks 2 through 8 would leave the candidate at Provisional, not Validated, because Disguise, Presupposition failure and Vagueness are all only Partial and the 2605.06611 "Multi-run Consistency" check is Unverified.

The two supporting legs would **not** carry the verdict on their own and are not asked to: arXiv:2606.31963's "about 50% sign mismatch" is per-coordinate sign agreement in a 10M d=256 model, not hot-index overlap at ≥410M; and the literature's non-overlapping printed index pairs (308,381 vs 218,674; 828/550/22) are single pairs across *different* recipes, with no CI, no null and no threshold sweep.

Unverified (unrun, and recorded as evidence in neither direction): arXiv:2605.06611v1 §5.2 "Multi-run Consistency" and Appendix B.2 (two routes truncated); arXiv:2305.19268 *Intriguing Properties of Quantization at Scale*, which ablates optimization choices against outlier-dimension emergence and is the best remaining candidate for a disguised prior; arXiv:2307.12941 *On Privileged and Convergent Bases*; arXiv:2110.06296 (the classical permutation-invariance result, which I lean on only as background — the RMSNorm-specific gauge statement is quoted from a source I opened, and the derivation is labelled as my own so it can be checked).
