# C3 — Adversary 2 (second, harsher pass)

## Restatement (my own words)

The candidate asks: if you take the small weekday-minus-weekend drop in a city's near-surface urban–rural temperature difference, estimate it for dozens of cities from filtered Netatmo-type stations, and line those amplitudes up against each city's net inbound-commuter share, does the commuter share explain the amplitude once you strip out size, density and climate? Stripped of the PWS instrument, it is: *does the weekly canopy-UHI amplitude scale across cities with a measure of weekday daytime-population surplus?*

## Recommended verdict

**Rejected.** Unresolved on attack 2 (Disguise) and attack 4 (Presupposition failure); also Unresolved on 7 (Reason for silence), which Gate 4 says should send the novelty finding back to Gate 2. The decisive find is Fujibe 2010 (Theor. Appl. Climatol.), which the record never surfaced: a national-network, multi-decade analysis of the weekday–weekend drop in urban-minus-rural air temperature, stratified by population density, showing the amplitude scales with urban size/density. The candidate's increment over that (commuter share instead of density; PWS instead of AMeDAS; multi-country) is a covariate and instrument swap, and its P6 admits the new covariate is collinear with the old. The Chinese New Year (31 cities) and COVID lockdown (>300 cities) GRL papers already run the "natural experiment" the record proposes as confirmation.

If salvaged, the salvageable residue is narrower: *can CrowdQC-filtered PWS detect a weekly canopy-UHI cycle at all in a single megacity where Fujibe-type official data show one (Tokyo, Osaka, Melbourne), and does the PWS-measured sign match?* That is a methods question for the crowdsourced-meteorology community, not the cross-city causal question posed.

Caveats: Wiley abstracts for Zhan et al. 2022 and Liu et al. 2022 could not be fetched (403); their cross-city dose–response content is inferred from AGU/press summaries, which report no per-city migration regression. Lee 2025 GRL abstract was not retrievable.
