## Question (canonical)

**Gate 0 version (as intaken):** Using quantitative wastewater lineage deconvolution as a severity-independent denominator, do respiratory-virus lineages differ systematically in clinical ascertainment rate, and if so how much of the published relative growth advantage of designated variants is ascertainment artefact rather than transmission?

**Gate 1 reformulation (one reformulation used, for well-posedness only — both versions recorded):** For a designated variant's invasion window in a given sewershed, is the per-lineage sewage-to-clinical frequency odds ratio *constant in time*, and does its measured time-drift displace the fitted logistic growth advantage by enough to change a designation or strain-selection decision?

Reason for reformulating: the Gate 0 version asks for the total ascertainment contribution to growth advantage. That quantity is not free — a constant multiplicative per-lineage ascertainment ratio provably cancels out of the logistic *slope* and is absorbed by the midpoint (Dreifuss et al., below). So the Gate 0 version is partly answered by algebra, and partly ill-posed in that it treats an invariant quantity as if it were estimable. Only the time-varying component of the ratio can bias the slope. The reformulation isolates that component.

## Alternative formulations

- Does a lineage's sewage-to-clinical frequency ratio vary by lineage, and does correcting for it change which variants would have been designated variants of concern?
- Are variant growth-rate estimates from clinical sequencing biased by lineage-dependent probability of being tested, and can paired wastewater lineage frequencies measure that bias directly?

## Motivating gap

Relative growth advantage from clinical sequence frequencies drives variant designation and feeds vaccine-composition decisions. It is a frequency ratio measured among people who sought testing, so lineage-dependent differences in severity, illness duration, age distribution or care-seeking could in principle enter as if they were transmissibility. The claimed new instrument is multi-year, city-level quantitative lineage deconvolution from wastewater (Freyja and successors), offering a severity-agnostic denominator.

**Gate 2 revision of the gap claim:** the intake's gap statement — "no use of sewage as an ascertainment denominator propagated into growth-advantage estimates" — is **falsified**. Dreifuss et al. perform exactly that propagation analytically, state the conclusion for differential clinical testing explicitly, and compare wastewater-derived against clinical-derived logistic growth rates for Omicron BA.1 across six Swiss sewersheds (they agree closely). The surviving gap is narrower and is about the *time-varying* residual, which that work explicitly does not cover.

## Presuppositions

1. **Wastewater lineage frequencies are unbiased with respect to clinical severity** — CONTESTED and central. Partly *refuted* as stated: variant-specific wastewater shedding rates do differ (Prasek et al. 2023: highest in the Delta period, lowest in Omicron). Mitigating finding: Dreifuss et al. show a *constant* shedding ratio does not bias the growth-rate estimate, so the presupposition can be weakened to "the sewage-to-clinical ratio's shedding component is time-constant over the invasion window," which is itself untested.
2. **Deconvolution of mixed lineages from wastewater reads is accurate enough at the relevant frequencies with quantified uncertainty** — partly established; benchmark/gold-standard datasets and method comparisons exist; the error model is limiting at low frequencies.
3. **Clinical and sewage samples can be matched on catchment and time window** — established where sewershed boundaries and residence-level case data align; a data-availability constraint, demonstrated feasible in the Swiss and Québec settings.
4. **Published growth advantage is interpreted and used as transmissibility** — established in practice, caveated in the papers themselves.
5. **Variant designation and strain-selection committees would respond to a correction** — plausible; WHO TAG-VE and strain-composition processes cite growth advantage explicitly. Not verified against committee minutes in this gate.
6. **NEW, surfaced at Gate 2 — the shedding bias and the ascertainment bias are separately identifiable from paired series** — CONTESTED and probably the binding defect. The sewage-to-clinical odds ratio is a single composite number; attributing its drift to clinical ascertainment rather than to lineage-specific shedding kinetics requires external within-host shedding data. Without that, the question's answer is not identified.

## What would count as an answer

For a set of lineage pairs with multi-month paired series in roughly ten or more sewersheds: an estimated per-lineage, **time-resolved** sewage-to-clinical odds ratio with an explicit shedding-model sensitivity analysis, plus a refit of published logistic growth-advantage estimates carrying that ratio as a time-varying offset. The answer is the magnitude and sign of the shift in the slope (not the midpoint, which is known to absorb the constant part), with a statement of whether any actual designation or strain-selection decision would have flipped. A demonstration that presupposition 1 fails by a comparable amount answers the question in the negative and is itself actionable for the same communities. A demonstration that the shedding and ascertainment components are not separately identifiable would *dissolve* rather than answer the question.

## Novelty — how this differs from each

**vs Prior 1 (Dreifuss et al.) — overlap is large; this is the near-duplicate.** Prior 1 asks the mirror-image question (is the *wastewater* growth-advantage estimate biased by differential shedding?) and in answering it hands over the candidate's question as a corollary. Three specific collisions:
   - It derives that a constant quantification/shedding ratio *c* leaves the logistic growth rate *a* untouched: "the logit-transformed estimates of wastewater-based prevalences are shifted by the constant," with the midpoint carrying "an additive bias of −log(c)/a."
   - It extends that explicitly to the candidate's estimand: "differential clinical testing between variants (for example due to differential asymptomatic infection rates) should, following the same reasoning, introduce a bias in the quantification of a variant that will not impact variant growth advantage estimates from clinical data."
   - It empirically compares wastewater-derived and clinical-sequencing-derived logistic growth rates for Omicron BA.1 across six Swiss sewersheds and finds them very similar — which is a direct, if coarse, test of the candidate's hypothesis, with a null result.
   Genuine differences that survive: (i) prior 1's analysis is for a *constant* factor only — the Re simulations use a fixed 50% incidence scaling, and the discussion defers "changes in mean generation time or shifts in shedding load distributions" as things that "should be rigorously studied"; (ii) prior 1 never *measures* a per-lineage sewage-to-clinical odds ratio as an empirical quantity; (iii) prior 1's quoted extension to clinical testing is, by its own framing, an extrapolation of the authors' reasoning and not a result they tested with data; (iv) prior 1 does not ask whether any designation or strain-selection decision would flip. The candidate therefore differs only in the time-varying residual and the decision-reversal question — a real but narrow remainder on top of a published negative answer.

**vs Prior 2 (Québec).** This is a concordance and lead-time study, exactly the genre the intake described. It compares lineage frequencies in wastewater against semi-random clinical and outbreak samples in the same cities in two-week windows, reporting adjusted R² = 0.63 (semi-random) and 0.34 (outbreak), 41.7% / 85.7% call concordance at a 7-day lag, and detection-order counts (37 lineages earlier in semi-random clinical, 7 earlier in outbreak, 3 first in wastewater). It never forms a per-lineage sewage-to-clinical ratio, never treats the discrepancy as clinical ascertainment, and attributes discrepancies to sampling effort (about 10× more clinical and 5× more outbreak samples than wastewater) and prioritised outbreak sampling. It states the weaker clinical concordance "is not explained by the identity of the lineage in question, but rather by discrepancies in the timing of detection" — which is weak evidence *against* the candidate's lineage-dependence hypothesis. The candidate differs by making the ratio the estimand, resolving it in time, and propagating it into a growth-advantage refit.

**vs Prior 3 (Prasek et al.).** Measures population-level wastewater shedding rates by variant period (early SARS-CoV-2, Delta, Omicron), finding rates highest in the Delta period and lowest during Omicron, and most consistent across the six communities during Omicron. It therefore supplies the strongest available evidence *against* the candidate's central presupposition 1, but it does not compare lineage frequencies, does not touch ascertainment, and does not touch growth advantage. The candidate differs by using lineage *frequency* ratios rather than absolute shedding rates, and by propagating into designation decisions — but it also inherits this paper's finding as a live threat.

## Significance — consequence

If yes (the time-varying component materially shifts slopes): a correctable, signed bias sits in the parameter that drives variant designation and feeds strain-composition advice, and paired wastewater deconvolution becomes a required co-input to growth-advantage fitting rather than a confirmatory sidecar. If no: it converts prior 1's analytic invariance claim plus one six-sewershed comparison into an empirically bounded result across lineages and settings, which is decision-relevant in the opposite direction — it licenses *not* building expensive paired sewershed-matched infrastructure for this purpose, and lets committees keep citing clinical growth advantage without the caveat. Both branches are informative, which is a point in the question's favour. The deflating consideration: the expected magnitude is now known to be second-order, since the first-order term provably cancels, so the most likely finding is "small," and "small" changes no decision.

## Significance — audience

WHO TAG-VE (variant designation) and TAG-CO-VAC (vaccine strain composition), which cite growth advantage explicitly; ECDC and UKHSA variant risk assessment teams, which publish growth-rate estimates as standing products; CDC's variant-proportion nowcasting; the groups that maintain the estimators themselves (CoV-Spectrum / Stadler group, and the authors of prior 1); and the wastewater genomics community (Freyja developers, Eawag). Concrete action on a yes: add a time-varying offset term or publish a sensitivity bound alongside each growth-advantage estimate. Concrete action on a no: cite the bound and drop the caveat. The audience is real and identifiable — but note that prior 1's authors overlap heavily with the estimator-maintaining community, meaning the audience has already considered the question and acted by reasoning rather than by measurement.

## Significance — fecundity

Opens: whether the same offset logic applies to variant-specific Re rather than to the logistic slope (prior 1 finds the Re bias transient but nonzero during growth, scaling with undershedding and with the growth advantage); whether the approach transfers to influenza clade selection for vaccine composition, where ascertainment differs sharply by age and the strain-selection stakes are annual; whether environmental surveillance for polio and measles has the same denominator structure; and the methodological question of whether the shedding and ascertainment components of a sewage-to-clinical odds ratio are identifiable at all without external within-host shedding data.

## Significance — anchoring

Touches the standing open problem of case-ascertainment estimation in wastewater-based epidemiology (aggregate versions exist; per-lineage versions do not); the generation-time confound in growth-advantage estimation, which prior 1 flags as needing rigorous study and which is entangled with this one because a lineage's generation time and its care-seeking profile both shift during invasion; and the unresolved lineage-specific shedding question that prior 3 opens.

## Reason for silence

Candidate mechanism: **closure by theorem — an analytic invariance result removed the incentive to measure the residual, across a disciplinary seam.** The constant-ratio cancellation (a shift in the logit intercept, with growth rate untouched) is clean, easy to state, and was published by exactly the group that maintains the growth-advantage estimators. Once "differential clinical testing will not impact variant growth advantage estimates" is in a discussion section, the empirical question looks settled, and the time-varying residual becomes an acknowledged footnote nobody owns. The seam compounds it: the groups holding multi-year sewershed-matched deconvolution series are environmental-microbiology and water-engineering groups whose publication incentive is detection, concordance and lead time; the groups fitting growth advantages are phylodynamics groups whose incentive is the estimator, not its denominator. Neither side's reward function pays for a null-expected-effect robustness check on the other's parameter.

This mechanism is admissible under the ledger's list (a disciplinary boundary plus an incentive gap). But it is also an argument *against* the candidate's novelty: the question is not silent — it was asked, answered by reasoning, and the remainder was explicitly deferred. Per Gate 4 attack 7's converse, a reason for silence that reduces to "already dismissed in print" means the Gate 2 finding should be read as near-duplication, which is how it is recorded above.
