# VERIFY — Sheldon et al., pipotiazine palmitate market withdrawal

Checked 2026-10-22. Fresh-context check of ONE source against ONE claim. Budget used: 3 WebSearch, 5 WebFetch attempts (plus 1 proxy-rejected before contact, 1 curl).

## CLAIM

That Sheldon RJG et al., "Clinical outcomes following switching antipsychotic treatment due to market withdrawal ... pipotiazine palmitate", *Therapeutic Advances in Psychopharmacology* 2022;12, may be closer prior art than the ledger entry allows, "because it appears to measure clinical outcomes after a product left the market — which is the candidate's own shape."

Six sub-claims were put to the source: (a) front matter; (b) patients / period / jurisdiction; (c) outcomes measured after withdrawal and the numbers printed, each with its denominator; (d) comparator; (e) whether the exposure is a *medicine* withdrawal rather than a manufacturer exit leaving an *implanted device* unsupported; (f) whether it enumerates everyone affected by the withdrawal or only its own cohort.

**(b) Patients, period, jurisdiction** (reported, not quoted):

**(c) Outcomes and numbers** (reported, not quoted; note the hedges, which are the summariser's):

- Use of acute mental health care within 12 months: **19%** ("needed acute mental health care").
- **Denominators are not stated in what was served to me.** The natural reading is 31.5% and 19% of the 137 switchers, and 31.5% of 137 is 43.2 — not an integer, so either the denominator is not 137 or the figure is rounded from a different base. I flag this as unresolved rather than assert a denominator. Per the "published is not performed" rule, I can name these two numbers but I **cannot say where they are printed** — table, figure or sentence — because I never saw the text.
- Associational findings, all reported without effect sizes or intervals in what was served: drug class switched to (typical depot / atypical long-acting injection / atypical oral) was **not** significantly associated with discontinuation; switching to an atypical LAI was associated with **more** acute care than switching to a typical depot; schizophrenia was associated with **less** discontinuation and **less** acute care than schizoaffective disorder; women were **more** likely than men to discontinue. **No hazard ratios, odds ratios, confidence intervals or p-values reached me.** Rule (8) — re-derive at the author's own threshold, carrying stated uncertainty — is therefore **not satisfiable from my read level**. Anyone who wants to cite a number from this paper must open the paper.

**(d) Comparator** (reported, not quoted): the comparisons I can see are **internal to the switched cohort** — between the three classes of drug switched to, with typical depot appearing to serve as the reference, plus diagnosis and sex as covariates. **No comparator group of patients on a still-marketed product is described**, and **no pre-withdrawal period in the same patients is described**, in what was served. I will not convert that into "there is none": an abstract's silence is not the paper's silence, and a 12-month pre/post design is exactly the kind of thing a naturalistic cohort paper states in Methods and omits from a crowded abstract. **(d) is UNRESOLVED at this read level.**

**(e) What the exposure actually is.** The exposure is the **withdrawal of a medicine from a national market**: pipotiazine palmitate injection (Piportil Depot), a long-acting injectable antipsychotic, withdrawn in the UK in 2015. The paper's own design presupposes substitution — its unit of interest is literally "medication to which patients switched", and its three exposure arms are three classes of replacement drug. So this is **a withdrawal of a substitutable medicine, not a manufacturer exit leaving an implanted device unsupported.** The 68 patients in the 205 who did not switch are not described in what I read.

**(f) Enumeration of those affected.** What was served enumerates **its own source population only**: 205 patients on pipotiazine in one UK trust's records in October 2014, 137 of whom switched. That is a local denominator, not a count of everyone affected by the UK withdrawal. Whether the full text cites a national figure, I **cannot say** — I did not read the full text.

**Source type.** A **primary retrospective observational study** — the title calls it a "retrospective naturalistic cohort study". Not a review, protocol, guideline or editorial. The findings are its own; nothing in what I read attributes a finding to a third party. Two SLaM CRIS project pages on the same discontinuation ("The discontinuation of pipotiazine palmitate (piportil) and its discontents"; "Examining the effect of pipotiazine palmitate discontinuation on patients with psychosis after 12 months") and a MetaTOC entry for "Switching away from pipotiazine palmitate: a naturalistic study" indicate **separate work on the same withdrawal event by other groups** — relevant to prior-art breadth, and not checked here.

## REGIME

The source establishes what it establishes in this regime:

- **Population:** adults in the care of one UK mental health service (Sussex) receiving pipotiazine palmitate depot injection in October 2014; psychosis-spectrum diagnoses (schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder named).
- **Unit of analysis:** the individual patient who switched (n = 137), not the injection, the prescription, or the service.
- **Exposure:** compelled switch of a long-acting injectable antipsychotic following national market withdrawal, with a substitute available in every case.
- **Endpoints:** discontinuation of the switched-to antipsychotic, and acute mental health care use, each over 12 months post-switch.
- **Comparator:** class of substitute received (within-cohort), reference apparently typical depot. Not a still-marketed-product control, not an unexposed control.
- **Estimand:** association between substitute class (and diagnosis, and sex) and the two endpoints, in a naturalistic uncontrolled cohort. Observational and unadjusted-for-confounding by indication, by construction.

**Does the ledger claim sit inside that regime?** Partly, and the mismatch is the load-bearing part. The claim handed to me is a claim about *shape*: "measures clinical outcomes after a product left the market." On that description, **yes** — the source sits squarely inside it, and does so on a real patient cohort with named endpoints. But the candidate's regime, as the claim itself states it, turns on **non-substitutability**: "a discontinued injectable can be substituted; an implanted device cannot, which is the candidate's whole point." Sheldon et al. are proof of the opposite condition. Their entire estimand is *which substitute was chosen and how patients fared on it*. A result about choice among three available substitutes is **silent** about a population with no substitute at all — not supportive, not contradictory. The method transfers; the exposure does not.

## BEARING ON THE LEDGER ENTRY

**The claim holds in its weak form and fails in the form that would matter.**

It holds that this paper runs something very like the candidate's design: a real cohort, a market withdrawal as the exposure, hard clinical endpoints (discontinuation, acute care use) measured over a defined post-withdrawal window, in a named jurisdiction. As a **method neighbour it should be reported as one**, and the ledger entry should name it rather than leave it out. The appeal to novelty should not rest on "nobody has measured clinical outcomes after a product left the market," because this paper did, on 137 patients, in 2022.

It fails as the thing that would sink the candidate. The candidate's distinguishing premise — a manufacturer exit leaving an **implanted device** with no substitute — is outside this paper's regime entirely. Sheldon et al. measure *which of three available substitutes a patient got and how they fared*. That estimand cannot be evaluated in a population where substitution is impossible, and a result proved under substitution is silent, not supportive, outside it. So this is **prior art on method, not on exposure.** Whether the candidate's novelty survives depends on whether the ledger entry's novelty claim is pitched at the method or at the non-substitutable exposure; if it is pitched at the method, this paper weakens it materially.

**Two things must not be recorded as settled on my read:**

1. **Anything numerical.** I did not open the paper. The figures here are abstract-level, denominator-uncertain and interval-free. If the ledger entry needs a number from Sheldon et al. — a rate, a ratio, a count of those affected — someone must read the full text, and a successor should start with **api.figshare.com/v2/articles/23488739 through WebFetch** (curl was refused tool-scopedly, which proves nothing about the host) to recover the figshare `ndownloader` link to the 287.82 kB PDF.
2. **"No comparator."** Unresolved, and distinguishable in Methods only.

And one correction to the handoff that framed this check: the earlier read was recorded as "abstract only after figshare 403 and PMC blocked." Mine is abstract only after **SAGE failed as well** — so the brief's confident "this journal is open access at journals.sagepub.com; try that directly" should be amended in the routing notes. It did not serve this reader.
