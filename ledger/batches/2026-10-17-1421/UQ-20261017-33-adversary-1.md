## Restatement (my words)

Take biology papers (genomics, phylogenetics, single-cell, imaging) whose analysis can actually be re-run from the archived code and data. Plant one realistic bug at a time — the kind an analyst really makes — and re-run. For each planted bug ask two things: did anything the pipeline itself prints (QC tables, logs, diagnostic plots) look wrong to a blinded reader, and did the paper's main number move by more than the paper itself treats as meaningful? The question is what fraction of planted bugs are BOTH invisible and result-changing, and whether that fraction is a property of the kind of analysis rather than of how carefully the code was engineered. A second arm checks whether pairs of bugs behave as single-bug results would predict.
