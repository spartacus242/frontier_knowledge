## Restatement in my own words

If a national metrology institute comes out a little high (or low) against the reference value in one BIPM key comparison, does it tend to come out the same way in the next comparison of the same quantity, and in comparisons of other quantities it takes part in? If it does, is that stickiness missing from the uncertainty that a calibration customer downstream of that institute ends up with? Concretely (R1): over a pre-declared frame of comparisons, compute leave-one-out standardised deviations per (institute, measurand, comparison, date), fit a variance-component model, and report (i) the persistent-institute variance share, (ii) lag-1 autocorrelation within (institute, measurand), (iii) cross-measurand correlation of institute effects, (iv) the CMC-uncertainty inflation implied under a stipulated propagation rule.
