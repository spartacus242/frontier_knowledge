Question (canonical, V2 after the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation):
For macromolecular complexes whose ligand- or nucleotide-bound states are resolvable and countable in situ by cryo-electron tomography, and whose state is a reversible binding equilibrium on the timescale of the measurement (driven, energy-consuming cycles admitted only with a declared steady-state rather than equilibrium null), does the measured in-cell occupancy of each bound state match the occupancy predicted from in vitro dissociation constants and the measured or bounded intracellular FREE ligand concentration, and what is the sign and magnitude of the signed log-ratio discrepancy across complexes?

Question (V1, as intaked — recorded per Gate 1 rule):
For macromolecular complexes now classifiable in situ by cryo-electron tomography, does the measured in-cell occupancy of ligand- or nucleotide-bound states match the occupancy predicted by mass action from in vitro affinities and measured intracellular free-ligand concentrations, and what is the sign and magnitude of the discrepancy across complexes?

Reason for the reformulation (well-posedness only): V1's own motivating exemplar — the ribosome elongation-state census — is a GTP/ATP-consuming cycle whose stationary state distribution is not an equilibrium distribution, so an equilibrium mass-action null is the wrong null for it. V1 carried this as a scope restriction in its presuppositions while simultaneously resting its feasibility on that very census. V2 resolves the collision by requiring a per-complex declared null (equilibrium mass action for reversible binding; flux-balance steady-state occupancy for driven cycles) and by making the near-equilibrium restriction part of the question rather than a footnote. No change of subject: the estimand is still the signed observed-minus-predicted log-ratio in occupancy.

Alternative formulations:
- Do in-cell structural state censuses agree with the occupancies predicted by in vitro dissociation constants and cellular free-ligand concentrations?
- How wrong is the biochemical prediction of binding-state occupancy inside a living cell, when occupancy is counted structurally rather than inferred from an assay?

Motivating gap:
Subtomogram classification has turned "the fraction of molecules of complex X in state S inside an intact cell" into a directly counted quantity. The quantity is now published: Xue et al. classify 101,696 ribosomes from 356 cellular tomograms of Mycoplasma pneumoniae, report that "the ten classes within the elongation phase account for 70% of all localized ribosomes", that 72% / 67.5% / 59.5% of 70S are trapped in a single state under chloramphenicol / spectinomycin / a puromycin-like drug, and that 26.2% of 70S are in polysomes. Separately, in-cell NMR, crowding studies, cellular thermal-shift/thermal-proteome-profiling and in vivo PET occupancy work all report that in-cell affinities and engagement depart from dilute-buffer values. Yet cell-scale kinetic models are parameterised almost entirely from in vitro dissociation constants acting on a single free cytoplasmic pool, and no source found here uses in-situ state censuses as a quantitative test of that parameter transfer. The reading of Xue et al. in this task confirms the narrowing the intake predicted but does not close the question: that paper's comparisons to in vitro work are qualitative concordance statements ("in agreement with previous single-molecule fluorescence studies showing rapid release of the E-site tRNA"; the absence of three-tRNA classes set against "a previous single-molecule study showing that ribosomes are rarely occupied (1.7%) by three tRNAs"), it quotes in vitro affinities for the antibiotics (Cm Kd 2-6 uM, Spt ~0.1 mM, PUM IC50 0.1 uM) and then states that "the exact concentrations of antibiotics inside cells cannot be determined", it reports no classification error rate, and it builds a "putative energy landscape" from class populations without testing those populations against any biochemically predicted occupancy. So the three terms the comparison needs — free ligand, classification error, vitrification fidelity — are each named in the literature and none is assembled into a prediction-versus-measurement test.

Presuppositions (established / contested / flagged):
1. In-situ classification can assign state labels with a quantifiable error rate and without template-induced prior. CONTESTED, and now evidenced as contested: Evans et al. 2025 (opened) caution that populations estimated by counting particles per class "are highly susceptible to noise" and should not be treated as quantitative evidence of thermodynamics; Xue et al. report template matching retrieving ">90%" of 70S and "~50%" of free 50S with "no discrimination for different 70S classes", and no misclassification rate.
2. Intracellular FREE (not total) ligand concentration is measurable or boundable in the same cells and state. CONTESTED and usually the binding constraint; Xue et al. give total copy-number densities (EF-G ~10,000 um^-3, EF-Tu ~40,000 um^-3, total tRNA ~14,500 um^-3) but declare intracellular drug concentration undeterminable.
3. Vitrification preserves the state distribution. CONTESTED but partly argued in the literature: Xue et al. state "the vitrification time (0.1 - 0.2 microsecond) is much faster than any known translation steps (at least at millisecond scale)" while conceding ~3 s of room-temperature blotting exposure on a plunger "without a temperature controller".
4. In vitro dissociation constants exist for the same complex and ligand under stated, comparable conditions. Established for some complex/ligand pairs, unproven as a set of three.
5. The declared null (mass action with a single free pool, or a steady-state flux model) is the appropriate reference. Declared AS a null to be tested.
6. Particle counts per state suffice for occupancy confidence intervals at the needed precision. FLAGGED feasibility; counting is ample for ribosomes (10^4-10^5 particles) and unknown for other complexes.
7. Cells are in a declared, reproducible physiological state at freezing and that state is not the dominant variance source. FLAGGED; Xue et al. describe "small cell-to-cell variability" only qualitatively.
8. Complexes set by irreversible or energy-consuming cycles are out of scope unless a steady-state null is declared in advance. SCOPE RESTRICTION, now moved into the canonical question (see reformulation).

What would count as an answer:
For >= 3 complexes each having (a) an in-situ state census with counting statistics, (b) an in vitro dissociation constant under stated conditions, and (c) a measured or bounded intracellular free-ligand concentration: a table of signed log-ratios log(observed occupancy / predicted occupancy) with fully propagated uncertainty; a test of whether discrepancies share sign and magnitude (a systematic, crowding-like offset) or are complex-specific; and an explicit error budget separating classification error (including the population-estimation bias of Evans et al. 2025), free-concentration error, and affinity-transfer error. Discrepancies inside the error budget vindicate in vitro parameter transfer into cells; a shared-sign offset yields modellers a correction factor and makes in-cell occupancy a calibration target for structural methods; complex-specific discrepancies say no transferable correction exists.

Significance — consequence:
If the signed log-ratios sit inside the error budget, the single commonest parameter-transfer move in cell-scale modelling — plug in vitro Kd, assume one free pool, assume mass action — is vindicated by a structural measurement for the first time, and in-cell occupancy becomes a validation set rather than a worry. If they share a sign and magnitude, modellers get a numerical correction factor with an uncertainty, and in-cell occupancy becomes a calibration target that structural pipelines can be benchmarked against. If they are complex-specific, the result says no transferable correction exists and that in-cell occupancy must be measured per complex — which would redirect effort in both communities and would make the crowding/quinary-interaction debate decidable one complex at a time rather than in the aggregate.
Significance — audience:
(i) Whole-cell and cell-scale kinetic modellers, who would change how Kd tables are entered and how parameter uncertainty is propagated; (ii) in-cell structural biologists (the groups producing censuses of ribosomes, proteasomes and other complexes), who would have a reason to report free-ligand context, counting statistics and a classification error rate alongside class percentages — none of which P1 reports; (iii) cryo-EM methodologists already working on population-estimation bias (Evans/Cossio and the ensemble-reweighting line), who would gain an in-cell benchmark with an external prediction instead of a simulated ground truth; (iv) in-cell biophysics (crowding, quinary interactions) and (v) target-engagement pharmacology, for whom a structural occupancy would be an orthogonal check on an assay-inferred one.
Significance — fecundity:
Makes askable: does the offset scale with complex size, charge, or local crowding; is in-cell classification a Boltzmann-faithful estimator in situ; can free-ligand concentration be inferred FROM occupancy once an affinity is trusted (inverting the comparison into a measurement of the cytoplasmic free pool); does the offset differ between compartments or between growth states; and what the minimum particle count is for a decision-grade occupancy interval.
Significance — anchoring:
Touches the long-running crowding/quinary-interaction question of whether and by how much in-cell affinities differ; the open methodological problem of population estimation from class counts (P2, and the 3dem thread "correspondance between protein populations in solution and on the grid?"); the free-versus-total metabolite concentration problem in metabolomics and in-cell NMR; and the parameter-provenance weakness of whole-cell models.

Reason for silence:
Primarily a disciplinary boundary on top of a new instrument. The censuses are only a few years old, and the three terms the test needs are owned by three different communities: the census by in-cell structural biology, the free-ligand concentration by metabolomics / in-cell NMR, the affinity by solution biochemistry. The reward structure of the field that produces the data is mechanistic — the paper's result is the structure and the pathway, and P1 shows the characteristic move of converting populations into a qualitative energy landscape rather than testing them against a quantitative external prediction. The field that would use the number does not make tomograms and treats Kd tables as given inputs. There is also an incentive gap specific to the outcome: the most likely headline is a null or a modest offset, which has no home as a structure paper and is not a model-building result either, while the author would have to own three error terms from three literatures and publicly quantify a classification error rate the field currently does not report. Pharmacology, which does run this comparison in its own idiom (predicted versus measured occupancy from free concentration and Ki), has no stake in intracellular macromolecular complexes and no access to structural state labels, so the idiom never crossed over.
