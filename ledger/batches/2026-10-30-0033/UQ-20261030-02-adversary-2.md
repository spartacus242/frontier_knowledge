## 1. Restatement in my own words

When a LINE-1 element is copied out of one spot in a chromosome and pasted into another, does the landing spot depend on where in the nucleus the element was MADE? The candidate says: build human cell lines that each carry one switchable, tagged L1 at a known chromosomal address; turn it on; collect new insertions without using a drug-resistance screen that would only let you see insertions in open chromatin; measure the 3D contact map of the same cells; then ask whether a 100-kb chunk of some OTHER chromosome receives more insertions when it touches the donor's address more often than chance predicts, after holding fixed the four things already known to steer L1 (ORF2p nick-site consensus density, accessibility, replication timing, base composition). Then physically move the donor into the opposite A/B compartment: a true memory-of-origin effect should travel with the donor, while a plain compartment preference should sit still. Headline number: the regression coefficient on log distance-normalised donor-contact frequency, with a CI, in each line. A coefficient of zero in both lines is a real answer — it would say the L1 particle forgets where it came from.
