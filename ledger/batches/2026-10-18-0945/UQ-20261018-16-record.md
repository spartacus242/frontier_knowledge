Question (canonical):
V2 (Gate 1 reformulation — the one permitted, adopted as canonical; V1 below). Over a frame of co-held microbial accessions declared before any sequencing — strains linked as "equivalent" across two or more independent collections (ATCC, DSMZ, NCTC, CIP, JCM, NCIMB ...) by catalogue cross-reference or StrainInfo identifier matching — sequence the material *as distributed* by each collection (freshly ordered ampoules, primary arm; public assemblies whose BioSample provenance names the distributing collection, secondary arm) and report: (a) the distribution of pairwise genomic divergence between collections' holdings of one accession, partitioned into SNV / indel / IS-element / plasmid / prophage classes; (b) the share of accession pairs whose divergence exceeds a comparator MEASURED INSIDE THE SAME STUDY — the divergence between two independently revived ampoules of one lot from one collection (within-accession replicate baseline), not a stipulated threshold; and (c) the dependence of divergence on recorded transfers-since-deposit and years-since-deposit, with a pre-declared rule separating post-deposit drift from cross-referencing error.
(V1, as intaked, superseded at Gate 1: "Across the enumerable set of microbial strains distributed under cross-referenced 'equivalent' accessions by two or more independent public culture collections, what is the distribution of genomic divergence between the collections' own distributed material, and in what share of cases does it exceed a pre-declared within-strain comparator?" Superseded because "a pre-declared within-strain comparator" is undefined — the intake record concedes no within-strain standard exists — so limb (b) was answerable by stipulation. V2 replaces it with a measured internal baseline (replicate ampoules of one lot), names the two material arms separately, and makes drift-versus-mis-cross-reference a declared rule rather than an assumption.)

Alternative formulations:
- (lay) Is "the same strain" one organism across the world's culture collections, or has each collection's freezer grown its own version?
- (metrology / reference-material words, the field that owns the method) Treating each co-held accession as a reference material issued by two or more producers, what is the between-producer inhomogeneity of the genomic property value, and how does it compare with each producer's within-lot inhomogeneity?
- (systematics words) For strains co-deposited under cross-referenced accessions, is administrative equivalence also genomic equivalence, and does the answer scale with each deposit's transfer history?

Motivating gap: Divergence between nominally identical holdings of one strain is reported case by case and never aggregated as a population quantity. One case is now read in full and is on point: Desroches et al. 2018 sequenced the ancestral Escherich E. coli strain as held by NCTC, CIP, ATCC and DSMZ and found 164/803/878 SNPs and 127/227/230 indels (CIP61.11, ATCC 4157, DSM301 against the NCTC86_Dunne reference), i.e. four collections' holdings of one strain are four lineages — and the paper makes no call for a broader survey. The population question is therefore visibly available and visibly unasked in that paper. The catalogue side is also now verified: StrainInfo matches 717,337 identifiers / 476,193 deposits onto 314,035 distinct strains (20,065 type strains), verifying matches "by comparing sequence accessions" and curating taxonomic conflicts by hand — i.e. equivalence is asserted by identifier linkage, with no genomic equivalence check. NCIMB publishes its cross-collection equivalence search as a cataloguing convenience (catalogue page, title/record only). Presuppositions (status after Gate 1):
P1 At least K co-held accessions exist to estimate a distribution — NOW SUPPORTED, not merely assumed: StrainInfo's 476,193 deposits mapping to 314,035 distinct strains implies a large multi-deposit set, and its identifier graph is the enumerable frame V2 names. The exact count of accessions co-held by >=2 *independent* collections is still unpublished (StrainInfo says only that "many strains are deposited in more than one collection"; its Figure 1 shows exchanges without numbers), so K is bounded below by nothing I opened and must be extracted from the frame itself as step 0.
P2 Each collection's distributed material is obtainable for sequencing — ESTABLISHED for the prospective arm (ampoules are sold); the retrospective arm stays CONTESTED and is now demoted to secondary, because public genome metadata names a strain, not a distributor. Yarmosh et al. matched RefSeq assemblies to ATCC accessions by strain *designation*, not by distributor provenance, which is precisely the weakness V2 avoids by buying material.
P3 Divergence between two stocks is well defined under a reference-free variant/SV protocol applied symmetrically — ESTABLISHED for bacteria (Desroches et al. report SNP/indel/cgMLST counts for exactly this comparison); CONTESTED for fungi and protists with large repetitive genomes, which V2 should exclude or stratify in advance.
P4 A comparator exists for "exceeds within-strain divergence" — the intake's weakest link, REPAIRED by V2: no external standard is claimed; the comparator is the within-lot replicate-ampoule divergence measured in the same study with the same protocol. Residual risk: that baseline is itself a sample and may be near zero with a long tail, so (b) needs an interval, not a point.
P5 Deposit histories (transfers, preservation method, dates) are retrievable for enough strains to test the transfer-count limb — CONTESTED, nothing opened establishes it. Desroches et al. could only say the strain "has undergone numerous subcultures" and that "Shipment to other collections represents additional subcultures" — qualitative, uncounted, for the most historically documented strain in the world. Limb (c) is the limb most likely to fail on data availability and must be declared as secondary.
P6 Divergence found between two holdings was acquired after deposit rather than reflecting two different original isolates wrongly cross-referenced — CONTESTED and load-bearing, as the intake says. Partly tractable: a drift pair is a small clonal variant set nested within one lineage, a mis-cross-referenced pair is phylogenetically displaced (and Desroches et al.'s own numbers, 446-572 cgMLST alleles, sit in the awkward middle). V2 requires the separation rule to be declared before data, with deposit records for a verified subset, and the mis-cross-reference rate reported as a result rather than assumed away.
P7 One ampoule stands for the accession — CONTESTED where collections hold multiple lots; the replicate-ampoule baseline in V2 measures part of this directly, but lot-to-lot (as opposed to ampoule-to-ampoule) variance needs its own arm to be representative of "what they ship over time".
P8 NEW, flagged at Gate 1: the question presupposes that a cross-reference *means* "same physical lineage" rather than "same nomenclatural type" or "same original isolate". If collections only ever claim the latter, a large divergence distribution refutes nothing the collections assert, and the question's significance shifts from error-finding to measuring an unmeasured property. Not resolved by anything opened; NCIMB's page and the StrainInfo paper were read only as far as recorded above.

What would count as an answer: (i) the declared frame of co-held accessions, fixed before any data pull, with K reported; (ii) per accession, assemblies of each collection's distributed material (prospective arm primary), plus the within-lot replicate-ampoule pairs that constitute the comparator; (iii) one pre-declared reference-free variant+SV protocol applied symmetrically; (iv) the reported pairwise divergence distribution with class partition (SNV / indel / IS / plasmid / prophage); (v) the share of pairs exceeding the measured within-lot baseline, with an interval, under pre-committed bands so a null is informative (CONCENTRATED-NEAR-ZERO / MODEST-DRIFT / LINEAGE-DIVERGENT); (vi) regressions on transfers-since-deposit and years-since-deposit for the subset with retrievable histories, reported with its own coverage fraction; (vii) an explicit estimate of the mis-cross-reference rate under the declared separation rule. A distribution tightly concentrated near zero is an equally publishable answer and retires a standing worry.

Significance — consequence: If divergence is concentrated near zero, administrative equivalence is validated as genomic equivalence: cross-reference tables become usable as scientific statements, methods sections citing either accession are interchangeable, and a standing worry retires with a number attached. If a material share of co-held accessions exceeds the within-lot baseline — and (A)'s 878 SNPs / 230 indels for a single strain shows the upper tail is not hypothetical — then "the strain" is a collection-specific lineage, and four load-bearing practices lose a premise at once: the ICSP type-strain principle (one nomenclatural type realised in several non-identical holdings), QC-strain-based assay standardisation (CLSI/EUCAST QC organisms sourced from whichever collection is local), probiotic and industrial strain-identity claims tied to a deposit number, and the reproducibility reading of thousands of methods sections. The direction also decides a live design question: whether a genome sequence can stand as the nomenclatural type for a taxon, or must be tied to one named holding.
Significance — audience: concrete and organised. (i) The collections themselves and their federations (WFCC/WDCM, ECCO, and StrainInfo as the body maintaining the equivalence graph) — an action as specific as flagging or withdrawing a cross-reference, or publishing per-accession diffs, follows from the answer; ATCC has already built the capability (B) and the public argument ("serial culturing comes with risk"), so the actor exists and is instrumented. (ii) ICSP and the IJSEM/SeqCode taxonomy community, deciding what a type is. (iii) Standards bodies that specify QC strains by accession (CLSI, EUCAST, ISO/EURL reference-material producers, who already run between-unit homogeneity studies for every other property). (iv) Reusing laboratories deciding whether to sequence on arrival. (v) Regulators of probiotic and biocontrol products whose identity claims are accession-based. Counter-argument recorded: (B) is already pressing collections toward authenticated reference genomes without the cross-collection axis, so part of the practical consequence is pre-committed — the candidate changes which cross-references get flagged and how the type concept is argued, not whether sequencing-on-deposit is being urged.
Significance — fecundity: a measured drift rate per transfer or per year makes passage budgets computable for collections (how often may a lot be re-amplified before it is a new lineage); it makes "how much divergence does a QC strain tolerate before MICs shift" an answerable follow-on; it opens whether drift is random or convergent (mutator alleles as in (A), IS activation, prophage loss — the same loci repeatedly across collections would be laboratory domestication, not noise); it makes a cross-collection coalescent datable, i.e. the accession's own phylogeny as an archival record; and it exports directly to the cell-line, antibody, mouse-line and plasmid analogues, one of which (plasmids) is already a ledger entry.
Significance — anchoring: the type-strain-as-physical-object problem in systematics (and the genome-as-type debate); (A)'s unexplained mutator acquisition in collection passage; (B)'s discordance between authenticated and crowd-sourced assemblies; the pre-genomic "strain degeneration on subculture" literature (query 8), which posed the worry qualitatively for decades and was never converted into a population estimate; and reference-material metrology's between-unit homogeneity framework (query 10), which is the method this question imports.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanisms, strongest first):
1. A data/instrument obstacle that is conceptual, not merely practical: public genome metadata names a strain, not a distributor, so the question cannot be asked retrospectively from the archives at all — the only constructible version requires buying the same accession twice and sequencing both, which no archive-mining project can do. (B) is the first collection-scale set of distributor-provenanced assemblies, and one collection gives one arm; a second arm requires a second collection to do the same, which none has published.
2. A boundary inside the only institutions that could answer it: the equivalence graph is maintained by collection informatics (StrainInfo/WDCM catalogues), while sequencing sits in each collection's own QC, and the comparison that matters is between institutions that are each other's peers and sister depositories. No single actor owns the pair, and the one who does the work publishes about its own holdings.
3. An incentive and reputational gap on top of that: authenticity is the product that collections sell. A study whose possible result is "your equivalent accession is not equivalent" has a diffuse scientific payoff and a concentrated institutional cost, and it would most naturally be run by one collection on another's material.
4. A norm in systematics that makes the variation read as a curation error rather than as a distribution: "the type strain" is treated as a nomenclatural object with one identity, so a discrepancy between two deposits is something to fix case by case (an emended description, a withdrawn cross-reference) rather than a population quantity to estimate. (A) is exactly this: a fascinating single case, framed as one strain's odyssey, with no survey proposed.
Not offered: "no one thought of it" — (A) demonstrates the thought, and stops at n = 1.
