## Restatement (my own words)

Labour-progress curves (Zhang/CSL, WHO BOLD, LaPS) and the dystocia thresholds drawn off them are
built entirely from a number a clinician produces with two fingers and records as a whole
centimetre. The candidate asks: if you write down an explicit model of how that number goes wrong
— random examiner error, examiner-specific bias, and the fact that the record is coarsened to whole
centimetres — and at the same time model the fact that women get examined more often when someone
suspects slow progress, then refit a published labour-curve dataset, (a) how far do the median and
95th-centile curves and the implied action line move, measured against the published fit's own
confidence bands; (b) how many recorded "failure to progress" diagnoses flip when the written
threshold is reapplied mechanically; (c) how much of (a) and (b) is the measurement and how much is
the examination schedule. Non-trivial because the error is not Gaussian noise (it is coarsening),
because the sampling process depends on the latent state, and because the whole thing lands on a
threshold that sends women to theatre. A null answer — everything moves inside the published bands —
is pre-committed as a result.

Ain Shams sonopartogram-vs-partogram, 40 women, digital VE as reference; dilatation VE vs transperineal US r = 0.8601 (95% CI 0.7493-0.9240, r2 = 0.7396); conclusion "agreement was good for cervical dilatation, head rotation and caput but less so for head descent". **No dilatation-versus-time curve, no dystocia reclassification count.**
- https://arts.units.it/retrieve/5ac54115-7354-44a3-ade6-602f35e4d171/Acta%20Obstet%20Gynecol%20Scand%20-%202024%20-%20Hanidu%20-%20Intrapartum%20ultrasound%20for%20cervical%20dilatation%20Inter%E2%80%90%20and%20intra%E2%80%90observer.pdf — **READ**. London, May 2021-Nov 2022, nulliparous women in active labour; 110 participants, **147 paired DVE/TPUS observations** (median gap 27.2 min; pairs >60 min excluded). TPUS vs DVE: 0 cm difference 34%, 1 cm 46.3%, within 1 cm 80.3% (118/147); "a mean difference of -0.925 cm (95% LOA = -0.777 to 2.628 cm)"; ICC 0.85 (95% CI 0.75-0.90). Inter-observer (offline re-measurement of stored cine-loops, 30 participants / 50 measurements): mean difference 0.07 cm, LOA -0.96 to 1.10 cm, ICC 0.939; second scan 0.09 cm, ICC 0.969. Intra-observer: mean difference 0.01 cm, LOA -0.29 to 0.30 cm, ICC 0.995. The paper states DVE "is not standardized in respect of anteroposterior or transverse measurements", declines to treat TPUS as a gold standard, and **does not propagate the error into a curve or a diagnosis**, mentioning only that imprecise cervical assessment "can contribute to variable clinical decisions". Repeat in-labour assessments were done in 33.6% of observations and were sometimes prevented by maternal fatigue or inadequate analgesia.
  - Caveat carried through, not laundered: the TPUS-DVE limits of agreement as printed (-0.777 to 2.628) are not symmetric about the stated mean (-0.925), so that interval is internally inconsistent in the source. I use the percentage-agreement figures and the ICC, and do not rely on the LOA. The high TPUS reproducibility is **reproducibility, not accuracy** — the paper measures no bias of TPUS against a true aperture, so I do not claim the instrument is accurate, only that its repeat-measure spread is small relative to the DVE-TPUS discrepancy.
