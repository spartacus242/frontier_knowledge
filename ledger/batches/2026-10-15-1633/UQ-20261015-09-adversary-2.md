# UQ-20261015-09: Gate 4, adversary #2

## Restatement (my words)
Take Lean blueprint projects whose informal text was written by people. Break each into nodes: one informal lemma with its proof, plus the Lean declarations linked to it. For each node, compute log(compressed Lean size / compressed informal size). Then ask whether nodes have a smaller ratio when more of the formal results they depend on were already in mathlib, rather than proved inside the project, at the commit where the node was first formalised. The comparison holds project, field, depth and Lean 3 versus Lean 4 fixed. The record says a "no" would vindicate de Bruijn's constant loss factor at lemma level. It says a "yes" would give project leads a coverage elasticity for estimating cost.

## Spot-check of cited priors (WebFetch, opened)

- Wiedijk, cs.ru.nl/~freek/factor: real. De Bruijn's "constancy of the loss factor... does not increase if we go further in the book". Ten factors, from 0.44 to 8.8. No library dimension. Matches.
- arXiv:2510.15924 (Kontorovich): real. Sec. 9.2 says "Lines of code are no longer a meaningful proxy for human effort." Sec. 9.3 says "depends primarily on ... comprehensive libraries." Correction: sec. 9.2 puts the time-based factor at "perhaps 10, perhaps 100", not the "4-10" the record reports. Sec. 9.3's library condition concerns *stating* theorems with "minimal foundational work". It does not pose a measurement question. Matches in substance.

| 4 | Presupposition failure | **R1 conditions the library effect away by construction.** Missing prerequisites that the project must prove itself are blueprint nodes.
