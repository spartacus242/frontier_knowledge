Question (canonical): For a pre-declared set of shipped open-weight LLM releases, a pre-declared prompt set, and a detector-defined set U of under-trained vocabulary items (reported SEPARATELY from the set R of tokenizer-unreachable items, since the two sets and their hazard meanings differ), under free self-conditioned generation: (i) what is the mean and upper-tail step-wise pre-truncation probability mass on U and on R; (ii) at what share of generation steps does at least one member of U (resp. R) remain inside the sampling support after the release's own declared default truncation rule is applied — with the arm defined per artifact from `generation_config.json`/model card, and an explicitly declared substitute arm recorded as such for releases that ship none; and (iii) what is the realised emission rate of members of U and of R per 10^6 generated tokens, against a null of a vocabulary subset matched on unigram frequency profile?
Reformulation note: this is Gate 1 version B, the single permitted reformulation, used for well-posedness only and widening no scope. Version A (recorded, superseded) fused U with R, left the shipped-default arm undefined for releases that ship none, and stated limb (iii) with no null. Both adversaries restated version B independently and both restatements match it.

Alternative formulations: (1) Are glitch tokens a live inference-side hazard or only an input-side one — how often do shipped default sampling configurations actually let an under-trained token through into output? (2) What is the measured leakage rate of the untrained region of an LLM's output vocabulary under the decoding settings the model is actually served with? (3) Version A as intaken: "In ordinary, non-adversarial generation from shipped open-weight LLMs, how much step-wise probability mass does the model place on its own under-trained or unreachable vocabulary items, what share of that mass survives the truncation sampler at the release's own default decoding settings, and what is the resulting emission rate per million generated tokens?"

Motivating gap: the detection literature establishes that shipped tokenizers carry items the model was effectively never trained on, and tests them by FEEDING THEM IN. The symmetric output-side arm — whether the model puts mass on them unprompted, and whether the shipped truncation rule already removes it — is measured by none of the nine sources opened at first hand across the record and the two adversaries, and the one 2026 paper that builds exactly the per-step support-membership instrument it needs points it at the opposite end of the distribution (legitimate human words being cut off). Intake's gap claim was title-level only; Gate 2 re-established it from full-text reads and narrowed it.

- vs PRIOR 1. Prior 1's numerator = word-context pairs all of whose constituent tokens survive the filter; denominator = 1,000 word-context pairs. Candidate's numerator = generation steps at which >=1 member of U or R lies inside the post-truncation support, and emitted members of U per 10^6 GENERATED tokens. Prior 1's 100-item word list is drawn from a 10k-40k frequency band of human words and by construction contains no under-trained, unreachable or special-token artifact; it never measures mass, by its own disclaimer; and its steps are prefix-forced, so no self-conditioned emission rate can be read off it. Significance — consequence: if post-truncation support membership and emission are non-negligible for some release/default pair, the fix is a logit mask over U union R in the serving stack — machinery that already exists — and the deliverable is a shippable list per tokenizer release rather than a research programme; it would also reclassify the glitch-token hazard from input-only to bidirectional, which matters because every mitigation proposed in that literature is training-side or activation-side (Li et al. Sec. 8.3 offers dataset curation, architecture changes, post-training evaluation and feedback loops; GlitchProber patches MLP activations) and none is serving-side. If the answer is at or below round-off across models and settings, the output-side arm closes, the detection literature's input-side scoping is vindicated, and the on-device vocabulary-pruning line gains a direct argument that the under-trained region is free to drop. A negative is informative, which is what keeps this a question rather than an agenda.
Significance — audience: (a) serving-engine maintainers (vLLM/SGLang/TGI/HF generate), who own the truncation step and already expose the mask; (b) model release teams writing generation_config.json, whose declared defaults Awad et al. show are already being harvested by third parties; (c) the SE glitch-token line (Li et al. Discounted honestly: (a) and (b) act only if the number is non-trivial, so for them a null is a licence rather than a change, and leg (d) was tested by neither adversary. Significance — fecundity: does emission concentrate after special tokens, in code, in non-Latin scripts, at high temperature? Is emission rate a cheap behavioural detector of under-training, giving the disagreement between weights-only and behavioural detectors an external referee? Does instruction tuning suppress it, as Prior 1's 0.740 -> 0.728 base-to-instruct shift makes immediately askable? Does speculative decoding change the hazard, since the draft model's U differs from the target's? Does quantization inflate mass on near-zero-logit rows? Should a shipped tokenizer carry a declared emission-safe mask as part of its release contract?
Significance — anchoring: the detection literature's untested symmetric arm; the non-canonical-mass problem, where R-emission is a subset nobody isolates and the figure is only shaded in a plot; the sampler-adapter literature's open question of what truncation actually removes; the on-device vocabulary-pruning line, which prunes by log-mass and would be told whether the under-trained region costs anything; Prior 1's own stated limitation that it audits membership and not soft probabilistic erosion; and, newly, the RL invalid-action-masking literature as a structural precedent for the same question shape on a different population.

Reason for silence: an instrument and discipline asymmetry, evidenced at first hand rather than asserted, and the strongest-evidenced field in this entry. The owning field's apparatus deletes the sampler by design and puts the token in the input: GlitchProber does both ("we set the temperature to 0 to eliminate randomness", token inside a repetition template, no truncation rule anywhere, mitigation at the MLP); GlitchMiner likewise prefills the token in the assistant turn and, per its summariser, contains no sentence on any sampler; Land & Bartolo App. B and Li et al. both set temperature to zero for the same reason. From inside that apparatus the candidate's object is not hard to measure, it is invisible — there is no sampling step to audit.
