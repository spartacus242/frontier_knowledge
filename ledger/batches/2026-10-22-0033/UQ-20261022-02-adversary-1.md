## Restatement, in my own words (not the record's framing)
When a biochemist reports a turnover number, they divide a maximum rate by "how much enzyme is in the tube", where the amount of enzyme comes from a bulk protein assay that counts every protein molecule whether or not it can catalyse anything. Question (a): if you pick a list of enzymes in advance, purify each by the textbook route, and then COUNT the working molecules by a stoichiometric method (titrant burst or single-turnover amplitude), what fraction of the counted protein actually works — and is that fraction a stable property of the purification route or a lottery per batch? Question (b): published turnover numbers for one and the same enzyme disagree across papers; of that disagreement, how much could a batch-to-batch spread in working fraction, of the size just measured, account for at most — the rest being assay conditions? Limb (b) is explicitly a ceiling on a counterfactual, not a decomposition of the actual history, because the old preparations are gone.
Two things I note before attacking. First, the question is a CONJUNCTION: a bench survey plus a corpus-level variance budget, and the two limbs can be attacked separately. Second, limb (b) is a RATIO, so it has a denominator, and the denominator is the between-REPORT (same enzyme, different papers) variance of log10 kcat — not the famous between-ENZYME spread. That distinction is where this record turns out to be wrong.

## The decisive finding: limb (b)'s denominator, verified first-hand
SOURCE: Dan Davidi, Ron Milo, "Lessons on enzyme kinetics from quantitative proteomics", Curr Opin Biotechnol 2017;46:81-89, accepted-manuscript PDF at the Weizmann institutional repository (URL above). READ LEVEL: targeted full-text read of the dispersion passages, summariser-mediated.
REGIME: population = E. coli (and yeast/Arabidopsis/human) enzyme-reaction pairs and the in vitro kcat compilations they are compared against; unit of analysis = the enzyme-reaction pair, one value per enzyme; operating condition = cells in defined growth conditions, fluxomics plus absolute proteomics versus literature in vitro kcat; instrument = quantitative proteomics and flux inference; threshold = none; category = dispersion of reported kcat values and agreement between in vivo and in vitro rates.
THE NUMBER AND WHERE IT IS PRINTED: "the ≈130 values reported in E. coli span a range well over a thousand fold with a standard deviation of 1.4 on a log10 scale" — Introduction, one occurrence only (verbatim fragment as served by the summariser).
WHAT IT IS COMPUTED ACROSS: the ≈130 values reported in E. coli, i.e. one value per enzyme across ~130 different enzymes/reactions; the authors then use that distribution to assign kcat values to UNMEASURED enzymes by random sampling, a use that only makes sense for an across-enzyme distribution. On my targeted read the paper contains NO sentence on variation between different publications' kcat values for the SAME enzyme; the nearest statements are "some kcat values vary by orders of magnitude between organisms" and "Measured kcat values span over six orders of magnitude".
WHY THIS KILLS SOMETHING: limb (b) is a SHARE, and its denominator is the between-REPORT log10 kcat variance for one enzyme. The record cites this 1.4 as "a printed bound on the total spread (log10 SD 1.4) that limb (b)'s budget would have to live inside" (vs PRIOR 2) and lists it under anchoring as the figure "every predictor benchmark is scored against". That is a scope transfer across units: between-enzyme dispersion of a biological quantity is not between-report dispersion of repeated measurements of one quantity, and the first is not a bound on the second in either direction. Consequence: on this record limb (b)'s denominator has NO published value at all. The record's own MANDATORY-query section already says Prior 2 "BOUNDS the total spread" — my read shows it bounds a different spread, so even that concession overstates what is in print.

## My own arithmetic/derivation, labelled as mine
From the definition the record itself takes as P1 (kcat computed as Vmax over total protein): if a fraction f of the counted protein works and the inactive protein is kinetically inert, then kcat_obs = Vmax/[E]total = f · kcat_true, so log10 kcat_obs = log10 kcat_true + log10 f. For a variance budget, Var(log10 kcat_obs) = Var(log10 kcat_true) + Var(log10 f) + 2Cov(·,·). TWO consequences, both mine, not any source's.
(i) Given limb (a)'s Var(log10 f) and a between-report denominator, limb (b)'s central number is the one-line ratio Var(log10 f)/Var(log10 kcat_obs). So limb (b) is near-trivial GIVEN its inputs; its difficulty is entirely in getting them, and one of them (the denominator) is, per Leg 1, unmeasured.
(ii) A MAXIMUM share is not determined by the measured distribution alone: with the covariance free, log10 f can even cancel variance (negative covariance), and with positive covariance the share generated can exceed Var(log10 f)/Var(total). So limb (b)'s ceiling needs a declared assumption on the covariance between active fraction and both true kcat and assay conditions. The record declares exchangeability (P8) and does not declare this one.
SCOPE CHECK on my own derivation, as required: the multiplicative relation holds where inactive molecules contribute zero rate and do not otherwise perturb the kinetics. If misfolded or apo protein still binds substrate, sequesters it, or inhibits, then kcat_obs is not f · kcat_true and Km moves too — which is an UNSTATED presupposition of both limbs (call it P9), absent from the record's P1-P8 list. I have no source measuring substrate binding by the inactive population in the panel's families, so I press this as an unstated-presupposition objection, not as a sourced refutation.
Uncertainty carried: Prior 2 states 1.4 as a point value with no interval, so nothing I say above attaches an uncertainty to it; I do not treat it as a conclusion of mine beyond "it is the across-enzyme dispersion".
