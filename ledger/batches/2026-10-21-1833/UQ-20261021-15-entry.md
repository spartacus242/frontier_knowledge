Question (canonical): Over an enumerated frame of published studies that applied a since-retired commercial model snapshot and released per-item outputs plus a recoverable configuration: (a) what is the per-item agreement between the archived predecessor outputs and freshly collected outputs from the vendor-designated successor named in dated vendor documentation, under the recorded configuration, stratified by task class, with a declared distance per output type, read against the predecessor's own archived run-to-run band; and (b) under a pre-declared rule mapping each paper's own stated claim and threshold onto a re-derivable decision on the same items, with inter-rater reliability on the mapping, what share of those studies' conclusions reverse, with a confidence interval — the excluded share and exclusion reasons being part of the answer?
Reformulation note: this is R1, the single Gate 1 reformulation; the intake V0 (which named neither comparator nor unit) is in the record.
Alternative formulations: When a vendor retires a model and points you at its replacement, is the replacement a substitute for the purposes of the papers that used the original? / At what rate does carrying a result forward from a retired snapshot to its designated successor fail?
Motivating gap: Silence mapping. Retirement is dense with how-answers (deprecation calendars, successor tables, migration guides) and the exposure has been censused (arXiv:2609.04699: 42% of 8,931 paper-model mentions exposed, median 538 days to retirement), while the intake claimed nobody asks whether the prescribed migration preserves the finding. Gate 2 narrowed that gap itself: arXiv:2510.25506 re-executes five studies' artifacts on later snapshots and names model deprecation as the chief cause of reproduction failure, so the question is in print in the candidate's own field; only the candidate's estimand was absent.
Presuppositions: P1 a non-trivial set of studies released per-item outputs (binding feasibility risk; only neighbouring number found is executability, 5 of 69 OpenAI-based ICSE/ASE articles, arXiv:2510.25506 Sec. 3.1/4.1/4.2 — not per-item output release). P2 a vendor-designated successor is identifiable from dated vendor text (flagged; the OpenAI deprecations page was never opened, in the record or by either adversary). P3 configuration is recoverable (contested; failed in practice for the studies 2510.25506 attempted). P4 "conclusion reverses" is operationalisable by a pre-declared claim-mapping rule with IRR (contested; no such rule exists anywhere in the record or the adversary sweeps). P5 successor-equivalence is the question worth answering rather than output archiving or weight escrow (weakened: 2510.25506 Sec. 5.1 treats deprecated-model use as "an issue exclusive to commercial models"). P6 reproducibility-policy bodies and auditors would act. P7 predecessor run-to-run variation must be separable via a negative control (flagged in the record as unsupplied — defeated, see below).
What would count as an answer: (i) the enumerated frame with excluded share and reasons; (ii) per-item predecessor-vs-successor agreement by task class with CIs and a declared distance per output type; (iii) a conclusion-level re-derivation under the pre-declared mapping with IRR, reporting a reversal rate with a CI; (iv) the predecessor-repeat negative control. Not answers: vendor release-note aggregates, an exposure census, within-snapshot drift studies, a single case study.
