## Restatement (my own words, before reading the record's framing back)

If you take a biopsy today from the healthy-looking lining of the large bowel of, say, a 35-year-old, a 55-year-old and a 75-year-old, isolate individual crypts, sequence each crypt's genome, count only the mutations that bear the two catalogued fingerprints of the bacterial genotoxin colibactin (SBS88 substitutions, ID18 indels), and keep only the ones laid down early in that clone's history, does the count per crypt go **up** the later the donor was born? The trick being used is that if colibactin only mutates the gut in childhood, then an old person's crypts are a frozen record of the 1950s and a young person's of the 1990s, so one day's sampling reads a half-century of changing exposure — and that is the exposure change the early-onset bowel-cancer rise in people under 50 would need.
