## Question (canonical)

**Gate 1 reformulation (the version to carry forward; one reformulation used, for well-posedness only):**
For WHO International Standards now on their third or later generation whose collaborative-study reports are publicly retrievable, (a) what is the product across replacements of the discrepancy between each study's estimated potency ratio to the outgoing standard and the unitage actually assigned to the incoming one (the convention-and-rounding residual), (b) what is the convolution across replacements of the published per-study dispersions that the continuity convention sets to zero, and (c) do (a) and (b) together exceed the tolerance of the named IU-denominated clinical cut-offs and pharmacopoeial potency limits for those analytes, and the routine reproducibility (CV) of the assays used to apply them?

**Gate 0 original (recorded, superseded):**
For the WHO International Standards with the longest replacement chains, how far has the realised value of the International Unit drifted from its original definition once the bridging uncertainty that each replacement discards by convention is propagated, and does the accumulated uncertainty exceed the margins on which IU-denominated clinical cut-offs and pharmacopoeial specifications rest?

Why the reformulation was needed (and it is the single most important thing in this record): under the continuity convention the point estimate of drift is **identically zero by construction**. A replacement standard is assigned the unitage that makes its potency equal the collaborative-study estimate, so the bridging ratio is absorbed into the assigned value and the product of ratios is 1.000 by definition. The original wording therefore named an estimand that cannot be non-zero. What *can* be non-zero and is never aggregated is (i) the residual left when assigned unitage is rounded to a convenient figure (standards are routinely assigned round values — 500 IU/ampoule, 10 IU/ampoule), (ii) the discarded dispersion, and (iii) re-anchoring and chain-splitting events. The reformulation makes those the estimand.

## Alternative formulations

1. Is the International Unit of a biological substance metrologically continuous across standard generations, or is today's IU an undocumented random walk away from the first standard?
2. What is the total uncertainty of "1 IU" today, when every replacement's calibration uncertainty is carried forward instead of being set to zero to preserve continuity of the unit?
3. (Added at Gate 1, closest to the reformulation) Across the WHO International Standard portfolio, is the per-replacement continuity evaluation that WHO already mandates sufficient to bound the *cumulative* deviation of the realised IU, or does the per-standard scope of that evaluation make a portfolio-level deviation unobservable in principle?

## Motivating gap

Source method: presupposition inversion. A replacement WHO International Standard is value-assigned against the outgoing standard in an international collaborative study; the assigned unitage is then treated as exact so the unit stays "continuous", and the study's own dispersion is not propagated into the new standard's stated value. Standards for hormones, coagulation factors, heparin, vaccines and therapeutic antibodies are on their second to sixth generation, while every clinical threshold written in IU (anti-HBs 10 IU/L, FVIII potency labelling, hCG, anti-D) behaves as if the unit were fixed. The field has a per-replacement continuity check; it has no portfolio-level or cumulative account.

Gate 2 confirmed that the convention and the concern are both real and documented *inside the field*, which changes the gap from "nobody noticed" to "nobody aggregated". See Novelty below.

## Presuppositions (status after Gate 1/2)

- **P1 — each replacement is calibrated against its predecessor and the collaborative-study report publishes a usable ratio estimate with dispersion.** PARTLY CONFIRMED. Gray 2017 (NIBSC) shows value assignment against the predecessor is the recommended route and that results are reported as geometric means with geometric CVs (e.g. 1st IS FXI plasma: GM 0.86 U/ampoule, GCV 7.2%). CONTESTED for the oldest generations and where replacement "[is] not always possible, products change over time" (Gray 2017).
- **P2 — assigned unitage is conventionally stated without uncertainty.** NOW RESOLVED AS HISTORICALLY TRUE, CURRENTLY FALSE AS POLICY, hence the question must be read generation-by-generation. Bristow (NIBSC, BIPM/JCTLM working document ID-909) records the 1997 FSH standard as "Arbitrary assignment (without formal metrological traceability)" with "No uncertainty assigned", and records that the WHO recommendations (revised 2004) now call for "a formal consideration of uncertainty, including a statement of the uncertainty". So the convention is documented, and WHO has moved against it — per replacement, not cumulatively.
- **P3 — the chain is linear and documented.** CONTESTED AND PARTLY FALSIFIED, as anticipated. Poller et al. 2005 recommend bridging a replacement to *three* predecessors (rTF/95, RBT/90, OBT/79) to minimise route-dependent bias — i.e. multi-parent bridging is already practice in thromboplastin. Bristow documents re-anchoring: the 2nd growth-hormone standard was value-assigned against the 1st by HPLC (lab RSD 2.69%), and the 1988 FSH standard's replacement split into separate immunoassay and bioassay standards after the 1988 outcome Bristow labels "chaos". Re-anchoring and splitting break the random-walk model; the reformulation treats them as part of the answer.
- **P4 — clinical decision thresholds and pharmacopoeial limits are genuinely denominated in IU and would move with the unit.** SUPPORTED for named cases. Poller 2005 converts a between-IRP ISI shift into an INR bias and compares it to a 10% clinical-relevance threshold, which is exactly this presupposition in use. An EMA draft guideline on potency labelling of insulin-analogue products "with particular reference to use of International Units or Units" appeared in search, and a USP statement on heparin potency unit assignment and harmonisation with the IU exists (URL captured; **fetch failed, contents not verified — do not cite its numbers**).
- **P5 — "drift" is meaningful only if the artefact's own activity is stable in storage; degradation is confounded with bridging error.** CONFIRMED AS LIVE AND ACKNOWLEDGED IN THE FIELD, and the reformulation partly routes around it. Poller 2005, finding rTF/95 at mean ISI 0.99 vs 0.94 previously, states that "a change in the physical or chemical properties of rTF/95 or RBT/90 cannot be excluded" alongside centre/technician/sample effects. Van den Besselaar & Bertina 1993 infer *stability* of OBT/79 and CRM 149R from their near-identical mean ISIs — i.e. the field's standard move is to read agreement as evidence of no drift, which cannot distinguish compensating errors. The rounding-residual component of the reformulated question is documentary, not measured, so it is immune to this confound; the dispersion-convolution component is too. Only the "realised value" reading remains confounded.
- **P6 — collaborative-study dispersion estimates bridging error rather than inter-laboratory method variance alone.** CONTESTED AND NOT RESOLVED BY SEARCH. Gray 2017 states explicitly that deviation from the mean reflects "assay imprecision and method bias", and that NIBSC's multi-method approach aims at an estimate that is "accurate" but not "precise" — so the published GCV is, by the field's own account, partly method variance. The estimand must state which component it propagates; the honest version propagates the standard error of the consensus potency, not the GCV.

## What would count as an answer

A per-analyte chain reconstruction from the public collaborative-study record, for the subset where the record is retrievable: the product of successive assigned-versus-estimated residuals as a point estimate of accumulated systematic deviation; the convolution of the successive consensus-potency standard errors as cumulative uncertainty; each compared against (a) the clinical tolerance of every IU-denominated decision threshold for that analyte and (b) the routine reproducibility of the assays used to apply it. Three distinguishable and all-informative outcomes: cumulative deviation and uncertainty are small relative to assay CV for every reconstructable chain (the convention is metrologically harmless and can be stated as such, with numbers, for the first time); they are large for named standards (which should carry a generation tag or be re-anchored to an SI-traceable or physicochemical method); or the record is too incomplete for the oldest chains (a documented coverage gap addressed to the bodies holding the unpublished reports — itself reportable).

## Novelty — how this differs from each

1. **vs Poller 2005.** This is the candidate's comparison executed for exactly one step: it measures a shift between two WHO IRPs (mean ISI 0.99 vs 0.94 previously, over ~6 years), converts it into an INR bias (~6% at INR 3.0) and tests it against a 10% clinical-relevance threshold — drift-versus-clinical-tolerance, in the field's own words, published. It differs from the candidate in three ways: it is one replacement interval, not a chain, so nothing accumulates; it propagates no dispersion (the 0.94→0.99 shift is reported as "small but significant", not convolved with anything); and it cannot separate artefact change from calibration error, which it says outright. The candidate's increment is *aggregation plus propagation across generations*, not the single-step comparison. This paper is also strong evidence that the single-step question is routine, which weakens any claim that the field has not thought about unit stability.
2. **vs van den Besselaar & Bertina 1993.** Tests calibration precision and route-dependence against two reference preparations, and reads the near-identical mean ISIs of OBT/79 and CRM 149R as showing both "have remained stable since their original calibrations". It differs by being a cross-sectional agreement check rather than a chain propagation; by treating agreement as proof of no drift (the inferential move the candidate questions, since compensating errors are indistinguishable from stability under it); and by making no pharmacopoeial or threshold comparison. Its relevance is as the field's default method, which the candidate's framing says is underpowered by construction.
3. **vs Gray 2017.** The closest thing found to the candidate's empirical core, and the reason novelty is not graded High: it reports heparin standards from 1941 to 1973 each compared against the 5th IS (2008), with the 3rd IS reading about 173 IU/mg against about 130 IU/mg — a retrospective all-generations-against-current check that exhibits exactly the generation-level non-continuity the candidate predicts. It differs by being one analyte; by reporting point potencies rather than bridging ratios with propagated dispersion; by drawing no comparison to clinical decision thresholds or pharmacopoeial limits; and by being a conference slide deck rather than an analysis (the comparison is shown, not interpreted as cumulative drift). It is nonetheless proof that the question is live inside NIBSC, so the candidate's claim must be "never aggregated or propagated", not "never asked".
4. **vs Bristow ID-909.** Establishes that the convention the candidate inverts is real and documented ("No uncertainty assigned", "arbitrary assignment (without formal metrological traceability)" for 1997 FSH) and that WHO's revised recommendations now require both "a formal consideration of uncertainty, including a statement of the uncertainty" and an evaluation of "extent to which continuity of the IU has been maintained" at each replacement. It differs by being per-replacement policy, not a cumulative estimate: it asks whether continuity was maintained at this step, which is precisely the scoping that makes a portfolio-level deviation unobservable. The candidate is the question that this document's own framework cannot pose.

## Significance — consequence

If **no** (cumulative deviation and propagated uncertainty are small relative to routine assay CV for every reconstructable chain): the continuity convention is vindicated with numbers for the first time, and the per-replacement continuity evaluation WHO already mandates can be defended as sufficient rather than merely conventional. That is not a null result in context — Gray 2017 asks, of coagulation standards, "A single IU for all products – for how much longer?", so a bounded answer settles a recurring argument in biosimilar and biologic potency labelling instead of leaving it to assertion.

If **yes** for named standards: it identifies specific chains where the IU-denominated threshold should carry a generation tag (reporting "anti-HBs 10 IU/L against the Nth IS" rather than bare "10 IU/L"), or where the standard should be re-anchored to an SI-traceable or physicochemical method. Precedent for consequential unit moves exists in the field — growth hormone was re-anchored by HPLC, insulin-analogue potency labelling in IU is the subject of an EMA draft guideline, and a USP statement on reconciling the heparin unit with the IU exists (unopened; cited as a pointer only). Re-anchoring also silently breaks long clinical time series, which is itself actionable for anyone pooling IU-denominated measurements across decades.

If **the record is too incomplete**: a documented coverage gap, addressed to WHO ECBS and the custodian laboratories that hold the unpublished collaborative-study reports. This is a reportable result with a named recipient and a concrete remedy (publish the historical bridging statistics).

## Significance — audience

WHO Expert Committee on Biological Standardization and the custodian laboratories that run collaborative studies (NIBSC, CBER, PEI) — who would add a propagated-uncertainty line to replacement reports, or state why they decline to. The JCTLM / BIPM working group on metrology in laboratory medicine — already the owner of this problem's framing, as the Bristow document and the 2022 JCTLM traceability Q&A show, and currently without a cumulative-deviation estimate for the conventional-calibrator level of the ISO 17511 hierarchy. Pharmacopoeias carrying IU-denominated limits (Ph. Eur. BRP programme, USP), who would learn whether a limit's numeric value is generation-specific. Guideline committees owning named IU cut-offs (anti-HBs 10 IU/L for vaccine response, FVIII potency labelling, anti-D prophylaxis dosing), whose action is narrow but concrete: tag the generation, or restate the threshold.

## Significance — fecundity

Newly askable: (i) are the per-replacement continuity evaluations WHO mandates statistically powered to detect a shift of the size that is clinically relevant for that analyte — a power question nobody appears to pose, and the one that decides whether per-step checks can substitute for a cumulative account; (ii) which IU-denominated clinical thresholds in current guidelines are generation-tagged and which are bare numbers, a surveyable question with an immediately publishable answer; (iii) did documented re-anchoring events (HPLC-assigned growth hormone, the FSH immunoassay/bioassay split, IU-to-mass transitions) introduce step discontinuities into long clinical or epidemiological time series denominated in IU; (iv) do the remedies the index-number literature developed for chain drift — multilateral rather than chained comparison, i.e. bridging every new standard to several retained predecessors rather than one — transfer as a *design rule* for standard replacement? Poller 2005 independently recommends exactly that for thromboplastin (bridge to rTF/95, RBT/90 and OBT/79 together), which suggests the transfer is live and unnamed.

## Significance — anchoring

Touches: the SI-traceability problem for biological measurands (JCTLM/BIPM; the ISO 17511 hierarchy, where biologicals terminate at an international conventional calibrator with no SI route — the acknowledged open problem this candidate is a quantitative instance of); the FVIII one-stage versus chromogenic potency discrepancy and biosimilar potency-labelling disputes; the IPK artefact-drift-versus-comparison-error confound, which is the same inferential problem solved in mass metrology; and the chain-drift problem in index-number economics (IMF CPI manual ch. 6), the exact formal analogue with a worked remedy and no contact with this literature.

## Reason for silence

A conceptual-boundary mechanism reinforced by institutional scope and an apparent in-principle obstacle.

The convention is not an oversight; it is the definitional act that makes the IU usable. Zeroing the bridging uncertainty is what keeps "1 IU" meaning one thing across decades, so from inside biological standardisation, propagating uncertainty into the unit reads as a category error rather than an unasked question: you cannot have an uncertainty on the definition of your own unit, and the realised value of the unit *is* whatever the current standard says it is. Physical metrology dissolved this exact tension only recently and only by abandoning artefacts, and the people who hold the machinery for chain propagation and for separating artefact drift from comparison error treat biologicals as outside their traceability framework precisely because there is no SI route — the Bristow document is a NIBSC scientist presenting the problem *to* that community, which is the shape of a disciplinary boundary rather than a shared research programme.

Institutional scope does the rest. WHO ECBS and the custodian laboratories organise work per standard and per replacement; the deliverable is a per-standard report, the mandated continuity check is a per-replacement check, and no body owns the portfolio across generations. A question whose unit of analysis is the chain has, structurally, no author. Gray 2017's heparin comparison is what it looks like when someone does the chain-level comparison anyway: it appears as an illustrative slide, not a paper, because no venue or mandate calls for it.

Finally, the confound (P5) makes the question look unanswerable, which deters formulation more effectively than lack of interest would: since artefact degradation and bridging error are entangled by exactly the convention that discards the bridging statistics, the question presents as ill-posed on first inspection. Working out that the rounding residual and the dispersion convolution are documentary quantities, immune to that confound, takes one step past the point where the question is usually abandoned.

## Scores

**Well-posedness: 2/3.** The reformulated question names a retrievable estimand (assigned-versus-estimated residual per replacement; convolution of consensus-potency standard errors) and named comparators (clinical thresholds, assay CV), and the Gate 1 work removed the fatal defect in the original wording, whose "drift from original definition" is zero by construction. Two deductions remain. P6 is unresolved: the published dispersion is, by NIBSC's own account, a mixture of assay imprecision and method bias, so the estimand must specify which component it propagates, and the field's reported statistic (GCV) is not the one the question wants. And the "realised value" reading of the question stays confounded with artefact degradation (P5), so a non-zero cumulative figure would not be cleanly attributable; the question is well-posed for the convention's arithmetic, only partly well-posed for the physical claim. Not 3/3.

**Significance: 2/3.** Consequence, audience and action are real and named, and the fecundity items are genuine (the power question in particular is a well-formed question that does not currently exist). Two deductions. The outcome space is asymmetric: the likeliest result — cumulative deviation small relative to assay CV — changes no practice, and its value is documentary ("the convention is harmless, here is the number"), which is worth having but is not a decision change. And the audience already acts on the single-step version of this concern under WHO mandate, so the marginal behaviour change is an added line in a report and a generation tag on some thresholds, not a reorganisation. Not 3/3; clearly above 1/3 because the "large for named standards" branch has a specific, consequential remedy with precedent in the field.
