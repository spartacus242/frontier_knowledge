Domain(s): Biology — microbiology / microbial systematics and biological resource centres; secondary comparative genomics, research-reagent reproducibility. Method-owning neighbour: reference-material metrology (between-unit homogeneity / stability, ISO Guide 35).

Question (canonical): Over a frame of co-held microbial accessions declared before any sequencing — strains linked as "equivalent" across two or more independent collections (ATCC, DSMZ, NCTC, CIP, JCM, NCIMB) by catalogue cross-reference or StrainInfo identifier matching — sequence the material as distributed by each collection (freshly ordered ampoules, primary arm; distributor-provenanced public assemblies, secondary arm) and report (a) the pairwise cross-collection divergence distribution partitioned into SNV / indel / IS / plasmid / prophage classes; (b) the share of accession pairs exceeding a comparator measured inside the same study — the divergence between two independently revived ampoules of one lot from one collection — not a stipulated threshold; (c) the dependence of divergence on transfers-since-deposit and years-since-deposit, under a pre-declared rule separating post-deposit drift from cross-referencing error.
(This is V2, the one Gate 1 reformulation, adopted as canonical. V1, superseded at Gate 1: same question with "a pre-declared within-strain comparator", which was undefined and so answerable by stipulation.)

Alternative formulations:
- (lay) Is "the same strain" one organism across the world's culture collections, or has each collection's freezer grown its own version?
- (metrology) Treating each co-held accession as a reference material issued by two or more producers, what is the between-producer inhomogeneity of the genomic property value versus each producer's within-lot inhomogeneity?

Motivating gap: Divergence between nominally identical holdings of one strain is reported case by case and never aggregated as a population quantity. Desroches et al. 2018 sequenced the ancestral Escherich E. coli strain as held by NCTC, CIP, ATCC and DSMZ — 164/803/878 SNPs and 127/227/230 indels against NCTC86_Dunne, i.e. four collections' holdings are four lineages — and calls for no broader survey. StrainInfo welds 717,337 identifiers / 476,193 deposits onto 314,035 strains, verifying matches by comparing sequence *accessions*, never sequence content. READ LEVEL caveats from the record: prior (B) read as preprint; four PMC-hosted leads unread at Gate 2.

PROMOTION ITEM (i) IS DISCHARGED. Salvà-Serra et al., "Beware of False 'Type Strain' Genome Sequences", Microbiology Resource Announcements 8(22):e00369-19 (2019), doi 10.1128/MRA.00369-19, is about MISLABELLING: five strains in five Genome Announcements papers reported as type strains while not descending from the designated nomenclatural types under ICNP rule 18a, with one, Lelliottia nimipressuralis SGAir0187, additionally misclassified at species level. It makes no claim anywhere that two AUTHENTIC holdings of one type strain differ from each other, and its Table 1 ANI values compare a mislabelled strain against the authentic type — different organisms, not divergent holdings — so they must not be cited as divergence figures. It gives a count of five with NO denominator, no rate and no share. Borgers et al., BMC Genomics 20:561 (2019), doi 10.1186/s12864-019-5909-5, is framed as the specific history of one vaccine and generalises no further than TB vaccines; asked un-primed, it does not pose divergence between institutions' holdings in general as open or future work. So the existing "type strain" literature is about identification error rather than material divergence, which is the gap this candidate occupies.
