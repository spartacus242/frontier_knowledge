Domain(s): Medicine — clinical microbiology; hospital epidemiology and infection prevention; antimicrobial stewardship. Adjacent/method-owning: measurement reliability and QC, reliability engineering (common-cause failure in a 2-out-of-2 channel), diagnostic biostatistics (conditional dependence between repeated applications of one test).
Question (canonical): Is contamination of the two blood-culture sets drawn in one episode independent between sets, and given the measured within-episode correlation what is the misclassification rate of the standard interpretive convention that one positive set of two indicates contamination while both positive indicates true bacteraemia?
Alternative formulations:
- How much of what is counted as true bacteraemia on the strength of a commensal growing in both of two sets is correlated contamination from a shared draw, operator, skin-prep failure or antiseptic lot rather than bloodstream infection?
- Does the two-set rule for separating contaminants from pathogens rest on an independence assumption that the way blood is actually drawn violates, and by how much?
- (Gate 1 reformulation, retained) In episodes with exactly two sets and retrievable draw-level metadata, with commensal status fixed by a pre-specified organism list and concordance required to be species- (ideally strain-) matched: (a) the ratio of observed both-sets-concordant commensal proportion to the proportion predicted by between-set independence from the same cohort's per-set rate, with a CI and null ratio = 1; (b) variance components of that excess by operator, draw structure, prep protocol and antiseptic lot; (c) the RELATIVE reclassification fraction of the 1-of-2 / 2-of-2 rule against a pre-specified blinded adjudication standard, reported as an interval under stated assumptions rather than as an absolute error rate.
Motivating gap: The convention's stated logic is probabilistic — two contamination events are unlikely to coincide — so it needs between-set independence, while contamination is a clustered process attached to operator, prep and technique, and the two sets are routinely drawn by one person minutes apart. The intake claim that the within-episode correlation is "never reported ... at episode level" is FALSE and the correction is load-bearing: Borad et al., ASHE 5(1), Sept 2025 (opened in full) takes the pair as the unit, reports how often both sets grew commensals, and stratifies that proportion by shared vs separated draw (28.1% single-time-stamp vs 18.5% multiple-time-stamp, P = .11; per-pair commensal recovery 6.3% vs 7.3%) — the candidate's mechanism test in weaker form, in its own field and unit, eight weeks before intake.
Presuppositions: P1 the 1-of-2/2-of-2 multiplicity rule really drives interpretation — UNVERIFIED at Gate 2 (no CLSI/ASM document opened, fetch cap), then EVIDENCED at Gate 4 from a primary source: Yu et al. 2020's own contamination criterion A classifies isolates as "potentially contaminant if they grew in three or fewer of the six bottles"; Beekmann 2005's best algorithm requires ">=2 blood cultures positive for CoNS within 5 days", which is multiplicity across 5 days, not two sets in one episode. P2 within-episode contamination events share causes — PARTLY EVIDENCED (Borad 2025, direction as predicted, underpowered). P3 correlated contamination separable from true bacteraemia retrospectively — CONTESTED. P4 LIS carries linkable draw-level metadata — time stamps VERIFIED (Borad), operator identity and antiseptic lot UNVERIFIED as retrievable at scale; Borad concedes the time-stamp proxy "may not always hold". P5 a reference standard independent of the rule under test — CONTESTED and conceded; every adjudication standard found is unblinded to multiplicity, Yu 2020's being a bottle-count rule outright. P6 downstream costs material — plausible, no source opened. P7 per-set rates estimable in-cohort for the independence benchmark — VERIFIED (Borad publishes both).
What would count as an answer: An episode-level analysis giving (i) within-episode commensal concordance in excess of the independence prediction from the same cohort's per-set rate, with a CI; (ii) variance components for that excess by operator, prep and draw context; (iii) the 2-of-2 rule's PPV and the 1-of-2 rule's NPV under the measured correlation against a pre-specified standard. Strengthened by a prospective arm in which the two sets are deliberately drawn by different operators with independent prep, making the correlation experimentally manipulable. A null would be an informative first defence of a decades-old convention.
