## Restatement (my own words, not the record's)

Nurses change a norepinephrine drip only at the moments they look at the patient, and how often they look is set by unit policy (hourly, two-hourly), not by the patient. So the bedside is a feedback loop whose sampling interval is an administrative number. The candidate asks three things of that loop, from pump logs plus continuous arterial pressure: (a) does the commanded-dose series carry a spectral line at the policy interval, with dose-to-pressure coherence and a phase consistent with closed negative feedback, above a surrogate floor and a control band; (b) does that line's centre frequency shift when the policy interval shifts for reasons unrelated to the patient; (c) does the size of that line predict organ dysfunction once mean dose, severity and phenotype are held fixed. In one sentence: is some of the dose churn in septic shock made by the watching schedule rather than by the patient, and does the churn hurt.
