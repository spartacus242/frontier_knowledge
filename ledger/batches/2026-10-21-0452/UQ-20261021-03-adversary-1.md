## Restatement in my own words

A hospital order passes through several checks that are each supposed to catch a medication error — a CPOE alert, a pharmacist's verification, a bedside barcode scan. Hospitals talk as if these checks fail independently, so that stacking them multiplies down the chance an error reaches the patient. The candidate asks: using the per-order electronic record of what each check actually did, how much more likely is check k+1 to miss an error *given that* check k already missed it, compared with its ordinary miss rate; what common-cause coupling (beta or alpha factors) does that imply; by what factor does the realised all-checks-missed rate exceed the product of the cohort's own per-check miss rates; how do you bound the errors nobody ever noticed; and does the field, in its own published analyses, ever actually write such a product down.
