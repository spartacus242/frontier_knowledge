# UQ-20261016-30 — Adversary #1 log

## Restatement (my own words)
When a big pretraining run crashes and resumes, the dataloader may not pick up exactly where it left off. The candidate asks, for runs whose restart log and data order can actually be audited (open runs, or a replication with injected faults): (a) how far does the stream of tokens the final model actually consumed differ from the stream the run *claims* to have consumed — measured as tokens dropped, tokens fed more than once, and documents shifted out of position — and (b) do documents sitting next to a restart boundary get memorized, or exert influence, differently from matched documents far from any boundary, under a pre-registered test with a stated floor?

## Evidence (quoted from pages I opened)
A. PaLM (arXiv:2204.02311) §5.1: "observed spikes in the loss roughly 20 times during training"; "We re-started training from a checkpoint roughly 100 steps before the spike started"; "skipped roughly 200–500 data batches, which cover the batches that were seen before and during the spike"; "With this mitigation, the loss did not spike again at the same point"; trained "on those same data batches starting from a different, earlier checkpoint" and "did not see a spike". Schedule: "batch size 512 (1M tokens) until step 50k, then double it to 1024 (2M tokens) until step 115k"; "double again it to 2048 (4M tokens) until training is complete at step 255k"; corpus "780 billion tokens".
   My own arithmetic, carrying the paper's "roughly": ~20 x (200–500) = ~4,000–10,000 batches deliberately never consumed, out of 255k steps = ~1.6%–3.9% of steps. In tokens: if the spikes fell in the 4M-token phase (not stated), 16–40B of 780B = ~2%–5%; lower bound ~1.6% if spread evenly. This is a *published, computable* claimed-vs-realized multiset divergence for a frontier run, of the "never consumed" kind, and PaLM also ran the candidate's limb-(b)-style control (same batches, different model state -> no spike). The record's queries 3 and 9 concluded "no source reports tokens or documents skipped or re-consumed per restart, as a fraction or otherwise" — that is false for PaLM, with the caveat that PaLM's skip is an intentional training decision rather than an unintended dataloader-recovery residual.
B. Pythia (arXiv:2304.01373): "All models were trained on the same data in the same order"; "intermediate checkpoints can be linked with the exact data seen up to that checkpoint"; "a script that can be used to reproduce the exact dataloader used by our models during training ... so that the contents of each batch at each training step can be read out"; "resume training of the model such that it sees the exact same data until the end of training". The paper does not discuss restarts/interruptions.
C. MosaicML Streaming Fast Resumption: "immediate and deterministic resumption in the middle of an epoch by being stateful"; example: after saving at batch 4 the resumed loader "will start from batch 5 now". No statement about world-size change, num_canonical_nodes, skips or repeats — an asserted guarantee, as the record says.
D. Hernandez et al. (arXiv:2205.10487) abstract: "performance of an 800M parameter model can be degraded to that of a 2x smaller model (400M params)" "by repeating 0.1% of the data 100 times, despite the other 90% of the training tokens remaining unique."
E. Krasheninnikov et al. (arXiv:2509.14223, ICLR 2026), title only (not opened): training-order recency is linearly encoded in LM activations — a published mechanism by which position-in-stream leaves a readable trace, directly relevant to limb (b) and to the record's fecundity claim about reading restart history out of weights.

## Recommended verdict
Provisional — nothing Unresolved, but attacks 1, 2 and 4 are only Partial: PaLM already publishes the numbers to compute a deliberate-skip fraction for a frontier run (~1.6–5% of steps/tokens by my arithmetic, carrying the paper's "roughly"), and V1 conflates rollback reprocessing with duplication, so for the step-indexed loaders of the open runs it scopes to, the fault-recovery divergence is zero by construction. Promote only after (i) redefining "realized sequence" on the final-weights lineage, (ii) restating the novelty claim to exclude deliberate skips (PaLM/OPT) and (iii) narrowing limb (a) to non-deterministic-loader and resharding cases, with limb (b) anchored to arXiv:2509.14223 for its power analysis.
