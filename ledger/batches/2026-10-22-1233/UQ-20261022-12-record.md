Question (canonical) — V0 (Gate 0 intake):
Restricted to standard test methods for which two or more independent interlaboratory studies on comparable materials exist, is the published reproducibility limit R itself reproducible — what is the distribution of the ratio R2/R1 across such method-material pairs, and does that distribution lie inside the dispersion that the two studies' own laboratory, material and replicate counts imply?

Question (canonical) — R1 (the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation, adopted; forced by the document-kind check below, which showed the design fields the V0 test needs are not reliably in a precision statement):
Over a frame F of (standard test method, material class) pairs declared before any data pull and admitted only when TWO conditions hold — (a) two or more independent interlaboratory precision studies of the SAME designated method exist on materials matching a declared rule (same material class, same property level, overlapping concentration decade), and (b) for each study the participating-laboratory count p, material count m and replicate structure n are recoverable from a public or purchasable document (precision clause, archived research report, or published collaborative-study paper) — what is the distribution of log(R2/R1), and does its realized spread exceed the pair-specific spread implied by the chi-square sampling distribution of each study's own s_R at its own (p, m, n)? Pairs failing (b) are enumerated and reported as a coverage gap rather than dropped silently.

Alternative formulations:
(1) Is the reproducibility limit a property of the test method, or of the laboratories, materials and decade that happened to be in its interlaboratory study?
(2) If the interlaboratory study behind a currently active standard's precision statement were re-run today, by how much would the published repeatability and reproducibility limits change, and in which direction?

Motivating gap:
A precision and bias statement is mandatory in an ASTM test method, and the published R is then used far outside the study that produced it: accreditation bodies and laboratories derive measurement uncertainty from it, specification writers set acceptance limits from it, and proficiency-test providers set scoring limits from it. The study behind it is run once, with a handful of laboratories, on materials and instruments of its era. INTAKE READ LEVEL WAS: NO SOURCE OPENED, gap statement UNVERIFIED. So "is the published R still the R you get?" is an institutionalised, standardised, per-method conformity check in petroleum testing. Significance — consequence:
If R2/R1 lies inside the design-implied interval, current practice is licensed: R behaves as a method property at the precision standards claim, the worry retires, and accreditation guidance that derives uncertainty from published R keeps its warrant. If it lies outside, then uncertainty derived from a published R is a biased procedure with a measurable inflation factor, and the excess decomposes into laboratory-population, material and era components — which tells committees whether the fix is re-running studies (era/population) or narrowing the material scope of a precision statement (material). Prior 2's existence raises the stakes rather than lowering them: a whole industry already runs the per-method check, so a measured aggregate inflation factor would feed directly into machinery that exists.

Significance — audience:
(i) Accreditation bodies and the laboratories they assess, who use method-performance data as an uncertainty budget component (Nordtest/Eurachem-style); a measured inflation factor changes the budget. (ii) Proficiency-test providers, who set scoring standard deviations from published precision — prior 1 shows them already comparing their own spreads to a trend, and prior 2 standardises the comparison for petroleum. (iii) Standards committees (ASTM E11/D02, ISO TC28/TC69, TAPPI, AOAC), who decide when a precision statement needs re-study and currently have no cross-method evidence on how fast one decays; T 1200's "Suggested information" clause is exactly the lever such evidence would move. (iv) Specification writers and contract-dispute arbitration, where ASTM D6708-style between-method agreement arguments rest on published r and R being the real dispersion.

Significance — fecundity:
Once the ratio distribution exists: does R drift systematically downward with calendar time (instrument improvement) or upward (laboratory-population widening)? Is the drift predictable from the method's technology class, so an age-based discount factor could be published? What laboratory count p makes a precision statement stable to a stated tolerance — turning prior 3's simulation into a design rule with realized data behind it? Does the same instability affect r, which is within-laboratory and should be more stable? And the normative question: should a precision statement carry an expiry date or a confidence interval on R rather than a point limit?

Significance — anchoring:
Touches the metrological-traceability-of-uncertainty problem (uncertainty estimated from method performance data), the known chi-square sampling behaviour of variance components (prior 3), the Horwitz-function literature and its scatter (prior 1), the petroleum precision-monitoring machinery (prior 2), and the reproducibility-crisis literature at its measurement-science end — where the object is usually a result's reproducibility and almost never a published dispersion estimate's reproducibility.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism, argued):
A disciplinary-ownership and access gap, not a conceptual one. The per-method question has an owner and a published procedure (prior 2), so no one experiences an unasked question; the cross-method aggregate has NO owner, because the inputs are held one committee at a time, as archived research reports behind standards-body paywalls, with the design fields (p, m, n, date) only "suggested" in the precision statement itself — which is what my two opened instances show. Anyone inside a committee can answer it for one method and has no access to the other methods' reports; anyone outside has access to neither. Added to that is a normative-artifact effect: a published R is a committee-adopted number, so measuring its dispersion across studies reads as auditing consensus documents rather than measuring a quantity, and no community's incentives reward that. The era/instrument angle is additionally a new-data question: PT schemes large enough to re-estimate s_R at scale (prior 1's 907 rounds with median 41 participants) are a recent artefact of PT-provider digitisation.
