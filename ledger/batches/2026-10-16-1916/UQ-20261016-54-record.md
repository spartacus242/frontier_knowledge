Question (canonical) [R1, adopted at Gate 1]: For a pre-specified set of open-weight releases R, over the population A(R) of publicly available derivative artifacts whose lineage includes R — partitioned in advance into (i) format-only requantizations, (ii) fine-tunes, (iii) merges, (iv) behaviour-modification derivatives (abliterated/"uncensored"), with a declared rule for artifacts in more than one class and for merges with parents outside R — and with the PRIMARY weight w_a being each artifact's cumulative hub download count at a pinned census date (per-file where the hub exposes it, per-repo with a stated within-repo allocation rule otherwise): how large is D = E_w[m(a)] − m(ref), where m is each of a pre-specified capability suite and a pre-specified refusal/jailbreak-resistance measure, m(ref) is measured on the pinned reference weights run through the SAME inference engine and precision path as the artifact class being compared (format-matched control, so D is not confounded by engine), each component of D is judged against a pre-declared decision margin for that metric, and D is reported with CIs, a stated census coverage error, sensitivity across at least three alternative weightings, and a variance decomposition into between-class and within-class (uploader) components?

Alternative formulations:
- [V0, as submitted] For a given open-weight release, what is the download-weighted distribution of decision-relevant metrics (a fixed capability suite and a fixed refusal/jailbreak-resistance measure) over the population of publicly available derivative artifacts actually obtained, and how far does that population expectation sit from the value measured on the reference weights that releasers and independent evaluators report?
- What is the gap between the model that is evaluated and the models that are run?
- If every published number for an open-weight model were weighted by how often each derivative artifact is actually downloaded, what would the model's score be?

Gate 1 note — what the one permitted reformulation fixed (V0 -> R1): V0 left four things unsettled that decide whether any number answers it. (1) E_population was indexed by an unnamed weighting, so the estimand was a family, not a quantity: R1 names a primary weight and demotes the others to sensitivity. (2) The derivative population was not a partition and lineage is a DAG — a quantization of a fine-tune, and a merge with a parent outside R, had no class: R1 requires the rule in advance. (3) "Pinned harness" is not achievable across formats, because a GGUF artifact and a safetensors reference cannot share an engine; V0's m(ref) was therefore engine-confounded, which would have let the whole gap be an artefact of llama.cpp-vs-vLLM. R1 requires a format-matched reference control. (4) "Decision-relevant" and "exceeding the margins" were undefined; R1 requires a declared margin per metric before measurement. R1 also fixes the unit-of-analysis/weight-granularity mismatch V0 hid: the artifact is a weight file, the hub's weight instrument is often per-repo, and one GGUF repo holds Q2..Q8 — so the allocation rule must be stated, not assumed.

Motivating gap: New instrument. The derivative graph of an open-weight release became simultaneously ENUMERABLE and WEIGHTABLE only recently — per-repo (and partly per-file) download counts, base_model lineage metadata with weight-similarity recovery of missing edges, machine-readable quantization metadata, and inference cheap enough to evaluate hundreds of artifacts on one pinned stack. That makes a POPULATION quantity estimable where the literature has only ever reported properties of the reference weights or of individual author-produced quantizations. Release decisions, open-weight safety cases and the policy arguments built on them are all made about the reference weights; essentially all use is of derivatives. The silence is visible in the instrument's own first users: the ATOM Report (2026) uses hub download data to measure adoption and explicitly EXCLUDES GGUF/MLX re-uploads from derivative counts and never mentions merges — i.e. the population this question is about is the population the closest existing instrument scopes out. In parallel, a TMLR 2026 problem-statement paper asserts that "thousands of open-weight text models have been specifically fine-tuned to disable safeguards" with, in its own text, no measurement and no citation.

Presuppositions:
- P1 Downloads proxy use. CONTESTED, and the main weakness. Counts include mirrors, CI and bots; one download may serve many or zero inferences. R1 defuses the WELL-POSEDNESS half by defining the estimand ON downloads (a download-weighted expectation is a fact about downloads, not a claim about use); the residue is a SIGNIFICANCE risk, handled by sensitivity across >=3 weightings (likes, inference-provider catalogue presence, published router traffic shares, package pulls) and reported as such. Supporting evidence that the conflation is live and recognised: a public dispute over Qwen's "3 billion downloads" being read as users (search 3).
- P2 The derivative population is enumerable with bounded coverage error. Plausible via lineage metadata plus weight-similarity recovery; coverage error must be MEASURED (e.g. by held-out known-derivative recall), not assumed. Unknown-unknown residue: private re-uploads and non-hub mirrors are outside any frame and must be declared out of scope rather than estimated.
- P3 Hundreds of derivatives can be evaluated comparably. Requires pinned revisions, pinned engines per format, pinned hardware. Feasible but costly; the format-matched control in R1 is what makes it comparable rather than merely repeated.
- P4 The reference weights are a fixed artifact. CONTESTED — this ledger's UQ-20261016-12 finds non-weight files in release repos mutating after release. R1 pins revisions and thereby INHERITS the hazard rather than restudying it; the inheritance must be stated in any answer.
- P5 Within-class, between-uploader spread exists at nominally identical quantization settings (e.g. from uploader-chosen imatrix calibration corpora). PARTLY POSED ALREADY, and now partly MEASURED outside academia — llama.cpp issues #13736 and #5856 pose it, and practitioner benchmarking (localbench's KL-divergence comparison of unsloth / bartowski / lmstudio-community / ggml-org GGUFs of one model; kaitchup's "Lessons from GGUF Evaluations") measures it. Subordinate and flagged; never the question's core. It enters R1 only as the within-class term of the variance decomposition.
- P6 A population-expectation gap would be decision-relevant to someone. Supported, not assumed: see Significance — audience, where the relevant communities are already using this vocabulary in print.
- P7 The capability suite's metrics are meaningful on every artifact in A(R). CONTESTED and newly flagged at Gate 1: a fine-tune narrowed to one task, or a merge with a foreign parent, may be out of the suite's domain, so E_w[m(a)] can mix a degradation signal with a domain-mismatch signal. The class partition plus a pre-declared in-domain rule is the only defence; without it the capability limb is weaker than the refusal limb.

What would count as an answer: For the pre-specified releases — (a) a census of A(R) with download weights and a measured coverage error; (b) a pinned evaluation of a sample stratified by derivative class and download weight, each artifact run in its format's engine with the format-matched reference control; (c) D = E_w[m] − m(ref) per metric with CIs; (d) sensitivity across >=3 weighting schemes; (e) a variance decomposition into between-class (requantization / fine-tune / merge / behaviour-modification) and within-class (uploader) components. YES: some component of D exceeds its pre-declared decision margin and the mass is concentrated in a nameable class. NO: every component of D falls inside the reference measurement's own interval and the between-class term is negligible — which vindicates reference-weight evaluation as a population proxy and retires a live governance worry cheaply. Both branches close the question; neither is unfalsifiable.

Significance — consequence: If YES (some component of D exceeds its margin, concentrated in a nameable class): the object of open-weight safety cases and comparative capability claims is shown to be unrepresentative of what is obtained, with a measured size — which converts a rhetorical objection that currently circulates without a number (TMLR 2026 makes exactly this claim with no measurement) into a quotable quantity. Three concrete downstream changes: release-time safety cases acquire a population-conformity line item, by direct analogy with in-service conformity in vehicle regulation, where the type-approval-vs-in-use gap became a regulated measured quantity once it was measured; marginal-risk arguments about open releases get a measured baseline shift instead of an assumed one; and head-to-head "open model X is N points behind" claims become claims about an artifact a minority of downloads obtain. If NO (D inside the reference interval, between-class term negligible): reference-weight evaluation is vindicated as a population proxy — a load-bearing negative, because it is currently assumed rather than demonstrated, and it would retire a live governance worry cheaply and let evaluators keep a much cheaper measurement regime with justification. The NO branch being genuinely informative is what separates this from questions whose only interesting answer is yes.
Significance — audience: (i) Open-weight releasers who write safety cases and tampering evaluations — the TMLR survey found tampering evaluations in exactly one of 15 models' documentation, so the practice is nascent and shapeable rather than entrenched; a measured population gap is the argument that would move it. (ii) National safety institutes: UK AISI publishes on open-weight risk management and on how far open-weight models trail the frontier, and both lines of work currently evaluate reference weights. (iii) Evaluation-infrastructure maintainers (leaderboards, harness authors, Artificial Analysis-style index operators) — the question implies a different sampling unit for their pipelines. (iv) Procurement and on-prem deployment teams, who must pick one quantized artifact and today have only practitioner KL-divergence blog posts (localbench, kaitchup) to choose on. (v) Policy analysts arguing marginal risk. This audience is evidenced from opened sources using this vocabulary in print, not asserted.
Significance — fecundity: The estimand, once it exists, makes a family of questions askable that cannot be posed today: which derivative class carries the download mass (does the ecosystem concentrate on faithful requantizations or on behaviour-modified fine-tunes?); whether D widens with time since release, i.e. whether a release's population drifts away from its reference as derivatives accumulate — a release-decay curve, and the natural follow-on study; whether uploader identity predicts within-class deviation better than the nominal quantization setting does (the imatrix-calibration limb, already live in llama.cpp #13736/#5856 and now measured by practitioners); whether a releaser can shrink D by shipping first-party reference quantizations — a testable intervention with an obvious owner; and whether population-conformity reporting could be required at release as in-service conformity is for vehicles.
Significance — anchoring: the open-weight marginal-risk debate; the tamper-resistance literature (fine-tuning removes safeguards); quantization-activated backdoors (Egashira et al., NeurIPS 2024 — named in the intake, not opened here); leaderboard-validity and contamination work; ATOM's own stated exclusion of re-uploads, which this question would turn from a scoping footnote into a measured object; and inside this ledger, UQ-20261016-12 (post-release mutation) as the temporal sibling of a cross-sectional question and UQ-20261015-08 as the dependence-correction sibling.

Reason for silence: Primarily a unit-of-analysis norm reinforced by a disciplinary boundary, not absence of imagination. ML evaluation's unit is the model-as-trained-artifact: benchmarks, leaderboards, model cards, safety cases and release decisions are all keyed to a release, and the field has no convention — no sampling frame, no weights, no "in-service conformity" concept — for an estimand defined over a POPULATION of artifacts. The method for such an estimand is owned elsewhere: by survey sampling (unequal-probability estimation of a superpopulation mean), by fleet metrology and vehicle in-service conformity (type-approval value vs in-use value, a mature regulated quantity per query 5), and by software-supply-chain registry measurement, where download-weighted prevalence over a registry population is routine (query 10). Nobody owns both the method and the data. Two reinforcing mechanisms: an incentive gap — releasers have no reason to publish a number that can only be worse than their headline, third-party uploaders are unaccountable and unfunded for evaluation, and a census-plus-weighted-mean reads to reviewers as engineering rather than contribution; and a genuine new-instrument lag — per-repo and partly per-file download counts, base_model lineage tags and machine-readable quant metadata only recently made the population enumerable and weightable at once. The clearest evidence that this is the live mechanism rather than a story: ATOM, the first work to use the weights at ecosystem scale, wrote the candidate's population out of scope by rule ("exclude local-inference re-uploads (e.g., GGUF, MLX)"), and the field's own problem-statement paper asserts the harm in prose with no measurement and no citation.
