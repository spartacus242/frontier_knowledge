## 1. Restatement (my words)

Take archived N2/Ar isotherms that can be tied to the paper that measured them, where that paper prints a BET area computed from that same curve. Recompute each BET area with one frozen, pre-registered implementation of the Rouquerol-type consistency criteria. Let Δ = (printed area − frozen-criteria area)/(frozen-criteria area). Is Δ positive on average, and does it grow as the *recomputed* area approaches the then-current record for the material class, after controlling for material class, publication year and isotherm quality? Informally: when the BET-window choice is discretionary, is it exercised upward, and more so where a record is in reach?

## 3. What the opened sources say (quotes as returned by the fetch tool; paraphrases marked)

Ambroz et al., Small Methods 2018 (opened):
- Verbatim: "This can lead to obtaining higher or overestimated surface areas" (p. 4, on range selection/pore filling).
- Paraphrase, p. 6: for IRMOFs with pores below 7 Å the consistency-criteria ranges, which sit at lower P/P0, are expected to give HIGHER areas than the standard 0.05–0.30 range; the standard-range results for IRMOF-9 and -13 are flagged as "likely too low".
- Verbatim, p. 9 (mesoporous NU-1103/NU-1104): "Hence the monolayer loading is overestimated resulting in excessive surface area"; pore filling that begins before monolayer completion inflates the monolayer loading.
- Paraphrase: no clean micro-vs-meso rule; effect is material-dependent with both over- and underestimation cases; BET area "may vary widely" with range (p. 12); ~10% tolerance needed for NU-110x to satisfy all criteria (p. 9); UiO-66 cannot satisfy criterion 4 (p. 11); NU-110 meets all four criteria yet disagrees ~33% with its accessible area (p. 11). The paper recomputes several literature-derived values but has no corpus, no sign statistic, and no incentive or record discussion (terms "arbitrary/subjective/cherry-picking/inflated" absent).

AIChE 2019 631b, Datar/Sinha/Jeong/Deng/Chung/Lin (opened): simulated snapshots of hundreds of MOFs/CNTs; "the predicted area is highly dependent on the region of the isotherm that is chosen"; ESW proposed "particularly in cases where the BET method overestimates the surface area significantly". No literature values recomputed. Establishes that a second automated criteria/region-selection lineage (SESAMI) predates BETSI.

My own arithmetic (labelled as mine, no source): for a saturated Type I plateau n = n_sat, the BET transform is y = x/[n_sat(1−x)]; the tangent-line fit at x0 gives slope 1/[n_sat(1−x0)²], intercept −x0²/[n_sat(1−x0)²], hence n_m = 1/(slope+intercept) = n_sat(1−x0)/(1+x0). At x0 = 0.15 this is ≈0.74·n_sat. So moving the window UP within the plateau LOWERS the area — consistent with Ambroz p. 6 and contrary to the unconditional form of the record's presupposition 6.
