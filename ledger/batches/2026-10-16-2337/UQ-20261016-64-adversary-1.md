## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take outpatient visits where you hold both (a) what was actually said, as a verbatim transcript, and (b) the note the clinician signed. Build one predictor from the transcript and one from the note, each trying to predict a diagnosis that gets confirmed later by someone else. Measure how much worse the note-fed predictor does than the transcript-fed one (AUROC, calibration, net benefit), after controlling for the fact that transcripts are longer, that speech-to-text itself makes errors, and that a predictor could be reading speech style rather than content. Then check whether the note's deficit is larger for patients who used an interpreter, have limited proficiency in the clinic's language, have speech or cognitive impairment, or speak with an accent that ASR handles badly. Finally, redo it on old-fashioned human-typed notes from recorded visits, to show the deficit is about note-writing, not about AI scribes.

Two limbs are claimed to be never asked: (1) the transcript-vs-note *predictive gap* on an externally established outcome; (2) the *patient-attribute interaction* of that gap.
