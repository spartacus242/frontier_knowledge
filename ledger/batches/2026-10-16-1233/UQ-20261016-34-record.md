## Question (canonical)

In a dense whole-brain connectome whose two hemispheres were reconstructed from a single image volume, how large must a difference in a cell-type-to-cell-type connection weight be before it exceeds the reconstruction-and-proofreading noise floor — that is, what is the detection limit for any connectome-to-connectome comparison, measured by independent re-reconstruction of the same volume?

**Gate 1 reformulation (one permitted; both recorded).** The intake version bundles two things: a quantity (the detection limit) and a specific method of obtaining it (independent re-reconstruction). That makes it unfalsifiable-by-substitution — a modelled floor would both answer and not answer it. Tightened version, used for Gates 2–3:

> For a dense EM connectome in which both hemispheres derive from one image volume and one pipeline, what is the smallest difference in a cell-type-to-cell-type connection weight (in synapse count, or in log weight ratio) that is distinguishable at a stated confidence from the pipeline's own segmentation-plus-synapse-detection-plus-proofreading error, and how does that threshold vary with edge weight, cell type, neurite length and proofreading effort?

The "independent re-reconstruction" clause is demoted from the question to one candidate estimator of the answer (the empirical one, as against a forward noise model). Recording both versions matters here, because the literature turns out to have answered the tightened version by the modelled route.

## Alternative formulations

- What is the smallest circuit difference a connectome can resolve?
- Of the left–right difference in connection weights inside a single fly brain, how much is the reconstruction pipeline and how much is the animal?

## Motivating gap (as stated at intake — found to be factually false, see Gate 2)

Intake claim: whole-brain dense connectomes are being compared across hemispheres, individuals and sexes, and "the field publishes those differences without an error model that says which of them are resolvable"; within-brain left–right discrepancies are reported as 'variability' with no separation of pipeline error from developmental variation; "the detection limit has not been stated as a number."

Gate 2 finding: each clause is contradicted by the flagship papers of the very instrument named. The error model exists, the decomposition exists, the number exists (±30% in edge weight), and the number is already being applied to filter between-connectome biological claims.

## What would count as an answer (intake criteria, scored against what exists)

- (i) Independent re-reconstruction of a defined sub-volume, with edge-weight agreement as a function of weight, cell type, neurite length and proofreading effort — **partly met, by the wrong route.** Agreement *as a function of weight* is published ("edges of >10 synapses can be reproducibly found (>90%)"; "edges constituting >1.1% of the target cell type's total inputs have a greater than 90% chance of persisting"; technical noise "approached 100% for weaker synapses"). Not stratified by cell type, neurite length or effort. Obtained from a forward noise model, not an independent re-reconstruction.
- (ii) Decomposition of the within-animal left–right discrepancy into pipeline and residual terms — **met.** 65% technical / residual "establishing a lower bound for likely biological variability"; within-brain edge-weight correlation R = 0.96; edge persistence 57% left→right, 59% right→left.
- (iii) A stated minimum resolvable difference, an actual number, at a stated confidence, plus the biology-dominated regime — **met.** "differences in edge weights of +/-30% or less may be entirely due to technical noise and should not be over-interpreted"; distributional form given ("25% of all 30-synapse edges on the left will consist of 15 synapses or less on the right, and 5% of only 1–7 synapses" — i.e. a 5–95% envelope); strong-edge regime defined as ">40 synapses or 3% edge weight".
- Significance-settling step ("apply the floor to claims already in print") — **met and already executed** by the 2025 male-CNS paper, including a worked within-paper example in the 2024 paper (LHCENT3→LHAV3g2, 30 synapses left vs 23 right, inside the 5–95% technical-noise range).

Two of three criteria met, plus the application step. Only the *estimator* in criterion (i) is unmet.

## Novelty — nearest prior formulations (with the four attributes)

### Prior 1 — the near-duplicate (OPENED, twice)

**Schlegel P, Yin Y, Bates AS, et al. "Whole-brain annotation and multi-connectome cell typing of *Drosophila*." Nature 634:139–152 (2024), doi 10.1038/s41586-024-07968-y.** Preprint: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.06.27.546055 ("...quantifies circuit stereotypy in *Drosophila*"). Accepted manuscript opened at https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10521685

- **Unit of analysis:** the individual and cell-type-to-cell-type edge within the FlyWire/FAFB volume, left hemisphere against right hemisphere of the *same brain* (and, separately, FlyWire against hemibrain across brains).
- **Endpoint:** edge weight in synapse count — its left–right difference and its persistence; and the fraction of that observed difference lying inside a technical-noise envelope.
- **Reference/comparator:** a forward model of technical noise — a fictive ground-truth connectome subsampled according to postsynaptic completion rate and then perturbed by published synapse-detection false-positive and false-negative rates. (Not an independent re-reconstruction.)
- **Estimand:** the share of within-brain left–right edge-weight variability attributable to the pipeline, the implied interpretability threshold on an edge-weight difference, and a lower bound on biological variability.

### Prior 2 — the stake, already discharged (OPENED)

**"Sexual dimorphism in the complete connectome of the *Drosophila* male central nervous system" (2025).** https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.09.680999 ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12636603/ (author list not confirmed from the pages opened — recorded as unverified)

- **Unit of analysis:** cell-type-to-cell-type edges compared across connectomes (male CNS vs female FAFB/FlyWire), and across hemispheres within the male volume.
- **Endpoint:** whether an apparent male–female connectivity difference is a genuine dimorphism; extent of dimorphism.
- **Reference/comparator:** an explicit per-dataset noise threshold on edge weight — "we therefore defined a noise threshold for each dataset and ignored weak edges below these weights (<8 for male CNS, <11 for FAFB/FlyWire" — plus a "30% difference in connection weights" envelope in Fig. 8d, FDR correction across the many tests, and expert re-curation of candidate differences.
- **Estimand:** the set and extent of true sexual dimorphism, explicitly a *lower bound*: "most apparent differences in male-female connectivity were explained by segmentation issues"; "we found that expert curation was required to separate biological signal from cases of technical noise"; "Our account therefore represents a lower bound on the extent of dimorphism in the brain"; neuron-level error rate estimated "at <1% for neurons in each hemisphere". It also records the regime the candidate asked about from the other side: "some connections in the male were too variable across hemispheres to be distinguished from zero synapses".

## Novelty — how the candidate differs from each

- **From Prior 1:** only in the estimator. The candidate demands the floor be measured by independent re-reconstruction of a sub-volume; Prior 1 derives it from a forward noise model calibrated on completion rates and detector FP/FN rates. The candidate additionally asks for stratification by cell type, neurite length and proofreading effort, where Prior 1 stratifies only by weight. Both are refinements of an answered question, not a new question: the canonical formulation's own headline quantity — "how large must a difference be" — is answered as ±30%, with a 5–95% envelope and a >10-synapse / >1.1%-input reproducibility threshold. This is precisely the failure mode the Do-block warned about: the on-point paper sits in the candidate's own literature, and it was missed at intake because the intake query used the phrase "reconstruction error vs biological variability" where the field says **"technical noise."** One word of vocabulary separated an UNVERIFIED grade from a Low one.
- **From Prior 2:** the candidate frames the application of the floor as the *future* work that would settle significance ("Significance is then settled empirically by applying the floor to the between-connectome claims already in print and reporting how many survive it"). Prior 2 has already done this, for the single highest-profile between-connectome biological claim in the field (sex differences), and reports the survivors as a lower bound. The candidate's stake-presupposition (#6) is therefore false in its strong form: the claims in print are already qualified, not unqualified.
- **From Prior 3:** cannot be stated — not opened.
- **Genuine residual, honestly stated:** no opened source performs a *blinded, independently-proofread second reconstruction* of the same sub-volume and reports edge-weight agreement against it. Queries 10 and 3 found only benchmark-against-ground-truth evaluations (AxonEM and kin), which measure an algorithm against an annotation rather than one full pipeline against another. In the method-owning vocabularies — metrology's gauge R&R (query 9) and psychometrics' generalizability theory (query 8) — the EM-connectome application appears genuinely absent. But that residual is a *validation study of an existing published number*, and a method transplant whose machinery is off-the-shelf in both neighbouring fields and in network science's measurement-error estimators (query 7). It is not an un-asked question.

**Novelty confidence: Low.** A near-duplicate exists in the candidate's own field, was opened in two versions, and contains the requested number.

## Significance (Gate 3 — argued)

- **Consequence:** Weak, because the counterfactual is not "no floor" but "a modelled floor of ±30%." If an empirical re-reconstruction returned ~±30%, nothing changes except confidence. If it returned materially worse — say ±60% — then the dimorphism and stereotypy claims currently filtered at 30% would need re-filtering, and Prior 2's "lower bound" language would have to loosen further; that is a real but bounded consequence. If materially better, the field gains resolution it is currently leaving on the table. The asymmetry the intake claimed ("a floor far below published effect sizes is as useful as one above") holds, but both branches now operate on an existing number rather than filling a void.
- **Audience:** Real and identifiable — the FlyWire/Princeton–Cambridge consortium, Janelia FlyEM, and the mouse-scale groups (MICrONS, Allen) budgeting proofreading effort. But this audience is the *author* of Prior 1, not a community awaiting the result; they built the noise model, published the threshold, and scoped it themselves ("this model was made specifically for the two hemispheres of FlyWire"). Being told to validate their own model empirically is a reviewer's comment, not a new research question.
- **Fecundity:** Modest. Downstream questions (does the floor transfer to mouse volumes? does it differ by weight unit — synapse count vs synaptic area vs vesicle count, presupposition 7? how should proofreading effort be allocated to minimise it?) are askable now, from Prior 1's number, without waiting for the empirical version.
- **Anchoring:** Touches a real anomaly — the published completion-rate asymmetry (mushroom body 46% FlyWire-right vs 77% hemibrain) means error magnitude is coupled to region and cell type, exactly as the intake argued, and Prior 1 concedes it "does not correct for other potential confounds such as differences in the synaptic completion rates." That concession is the sharpest surviving thread in the whole candidate. It also anchors to network science's measurement-error problem family, where heterogeneous, covariate-dependent edge error is the mature case.

Significance of the question **as posed**: 2/3. Significance of what remains after Prior 1: closer to 1/3.

## Reason for silence

**There is no silence to explain, and that is the finding.** The question was asked by the instrument's own builders, in the flagship paper describing the instrument, and answered there with a number; it was then applied as a filter by the next connectome released. The apparent silence at intake was an artefact of vocabulary: the field calls this **"technical noise"** and expresses the answer as a percentage envelope on edge weight, not as a "noise floor," "detection limit" or "reconstruction error versus biological variability." Query 1 reproduced the intake run's empty result using the candidate's words; query 2, using the field's own object (FlyWire left vs right, cell type, edge weight) in paper-title form, hit the answer immediately.

For completeness, the mechanism that *would* have been offered had the candidate survived — new instrument plus a disciplinary boundary between EM pipeline engineering and circuit neuroscience — is specifically refuted: the pipeline engineers and the circuit neuroscientists are the same authors on the same paper, which is why the error model shipped with the connectome instead of lagging it.

The narrow residual (no blinded independent re-reconstruction has been done) *does* have a credible reason for silence — interactive proofreading is expensive, so a second independent pass means paying twice for a volume already reconstructed, and no funder rewards it — and that is a cost argument, i.e. an open-but-unattractive validation task, not an unposed question.
