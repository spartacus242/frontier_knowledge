## Restatement in my own words

Take one *E. coli* cell, hold it in a microfluidic trap, and hit it over and over with the *same* dilution of the medium — each hit weak enough that one of them alone would not burst the cell. Does the chance of bursting on the n-th hit depend on how many hits the cell has already taken, once you have accounted for the biggest hit it ever received? And if it does: (a) does the median number of hits to bursting fall off as a straight line in log–log against hit size, over at least four hit sizes, the way an engineer's S–N curve does; (b) is there a hit size so small that the cell never bursts inside however many hits you can actually deliver; (c) if you give it n1 hits of one size and then hits of another, does it burst when the fractions of each size's budget add to one, as Palmgren–Miner says metal does — all of it repeated in cells that are building wall and in cells whose wall-building is blocked. In plainer terms still: is bursting a *strength* problem (set by the biggest hit) or a *wear-out* problem (set by the count), in a structure that repairs itself while you are wearing it out.
