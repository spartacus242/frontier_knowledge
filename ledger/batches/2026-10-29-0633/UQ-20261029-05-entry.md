Question (canonical): For ice grown from the vapour in one chamber at matched supersaturation, by how many kelvin does each of the three plate/column habit transition temperatures move between H2O and D2O, and does the measured set of shifts agree with the shift computed from D2O's own premelting driving force (the distance from its sublimation line to the metastable prolongation of its condensation line, a fixed-Δp threshold) or with the near-zero shift a step-generation kinetics mechanism with its own activation energies would require? (This is V2, the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation, scored as canonical; V1 fixed the premelting prediction at D2O's +3.8 K melting-point offset, a scaling the record's own premelting source denies.)

Alternative formulations:
1. Does heavy-water ice have the same Nakaya habit diagram as ordinary ice, shifted in temperature or not?
2. Is the plate/column habit transition a surface-melting or a step-kinetics phenomenon, as decided by isotopic substitution of the vapour? — flagged by the record itself: stripped of the isotopic method this is asked and unanswered in print, so it is not the un-asked content.

Motivating gap: Snow-crystal morphology diagrams have been measured in temperature and supersaturation since Nakaya and per-face growth rates are tabulated, but the mechanism setting the habit boundaries is openly disputed and no habit diagram for a second isotopologue surfaced. Three on-point papers were opened at Gate 2 and four more by the adversaries; dedicated sweeps found no occurrence of D2O / heavy water / deuter / isotope in any of the seven.

Significance — consequence: If the shifts match the computed fixed-Δp threshold at all three transitions, premelting pinning of the habit boundaries is established by a route that does not require resolving the film optically, against Sazaki's objection. If the shifts are under 1 K, premelting pinning of any kind is falsified and Prior 1's reported agreement with the Nakaya transitions becomes a coincidence of one force field. Non-uniform shifts would mean the three transitions do not share a mechanism, which no current model entertains. The record's stronger claim — that a match would displace the attachment-kinetics account — does not survive adversary 1's source: the leading kinetics model is itself premelting-pinned.

Water-model developers: TIP4P/Ice and relatives carry no H/D distinction, so a measured shift is a benchmark they cannot currently produce — plausible but unevidenced, since neither adversary opened a source on how water models handle isotopic substitution and the three path-integral papers returned at query 3 went unopened. Strongest remaining objection: The discriminant is not a discriminant. Libbrecht's SDAK step-nucleation model — the field's leading kinetics account — pins its own transition structure to premelting: "the position of the dip is determined by the onset of surface premelting on the relevant facet surface" (arXiv:1910.09067, opened by A1, dedicated isotope sweep clean). So a shift matching the computed premelting threshold would be consistent with both named mechanisms and would adjudicate nothing, and no opened source predicts the near-zero shift the question attributes to kinetics. This objection has two legs and the verdict stands on the first: the quoted premelting dependence inside the kinetics model, which is a sourced, located quotation from a document A1 read in full. The second leg — that nothing in print predicts a near-zero isotope shift — is weaker and is where this objection is itself vulnerable: it is a claim about what NOBODY HAS MEASURED OR MODELLED, an absence established by 18 queries plus 1910.09067's own admission that "it is not yet possible to calculate step energies at any temperature on any ice surface", and A1 states in terms that its nomination of a settling document is "a kind, not a named source" because it knows of none. If the first leg fell, the second would survive only as an unenumerated universal; if the second fell (someone has modelled isotope-independent step kinetics for ice habit), the first would still stand and would still make the match non-diagnostic. Related and to be kept distinct: the record's +3.3 K prefactor figure is an ALGEBRAIC consequence of Clausius–Clapeyron on D2O constants recalled from background knowledge, labelled by the record as its own arithmetic, and A1's "5% in pressure ≈ 0.5 K in temperature" is likewise its own algebra on d ln p/dT — neither is an empirical or independently recomputed result, and A1's proxy for Δp at −20 °C uses the H2O e_w/e_i ratio from background knowledge.
