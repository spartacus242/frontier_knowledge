## Restatement in my own words

Hospitals have two ways of getting a ward patient's vital signs: a nurse walks round every few hours and writes down a set, or a wearable streams values every minute. NEWS2 was built on the first kind of data and its trigger (usually 5) was set there. The question has three limbs.

1. **Operating-point transport.** Take the same patients and hours with both streams, keep only the parameters both streams measure, pick a rule for collapsing the stream into one score per window (declared in advance: worst value per hour, with 5/15/60/240-minute and median/time-above-threshold variants), and report, for each stream, with the published thresholds untouched: calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, discrimination, alerts per patient-day, PPV, and lead time.
2. **Recalibration map.** What intercept/slope/threshold change puts the continuous stream back on the intermittent stream's operating point.
3. **Timing informativeness.** Inside the intermittent stream alone, does *how often and when* someone chose to take observations predict deterioration over and above the values they wrote down — i.e. is part of NEWS2's apparent prognostic power actually credit for the nurse's decision to go and measure, which a machine streaming every minute cannot supply?

Slogan version: is an early-warning score a measurement of the patient, or partly a measurement of how often somebody decided to measure the patient?
