## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

During one hospital stay for acute pain, does the patient's private yardstick for "0 to 10" slide over the hours and days of the stay, so that part of the recorded fall in the pain number is the ruler changing rather than the pain easing? If so, how much of the fall is ruler, and how often does the ruler alone push a patient across the "stop/step down the opioid" line (NRS <= 4) or across the 2-point/30% "meaningful improvement" line? The generator proposes to measure this with a within-stay then-test whose timing is randomised, plus a longitudinal invariance model that uses opioid consumption, quantitative sensory testing and an ambulation milestone as the fixed reference.

| 1 | Triviality | Psychophysics already shows the 0-10 pain rating is relative to the recent stimulus context (Watkinson 2013: same pressure rated higher when it ranks highest; ratings higher when most recent stimuli were intense).
