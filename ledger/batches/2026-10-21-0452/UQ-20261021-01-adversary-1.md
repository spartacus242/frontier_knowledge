## Restatement (my own words)

How much of a drug's apparent efficacy, as measured at scheduled clinic visits on a biomarker that re-equilibrates within days (BP, LDL, HIV RNA, IOP, INR), is produced by patients dosing more faithfully in the days just before the visit — relative to what the same total number of doses, spread evenly over the inter-visit interval, would have produced — and does the manufactured component differ between randomised arms? The record's novelty claim is that this consequence has only ever been owned as a patient trait ("white-coat adherence") that contaminates the adherence estimate, never as a property of the visit calendar that biases the efficacy estimate.

## My own arithmetic (labelled as such)

Using the Tousset et al. 2006 abstract's point estimates (E at 100% exposure = 24 mmHg, EC50 = 51%) and ASSUMING a Hill coefficient of 1 (the abstract does not report it), I back out Emax = 24 x 1.51 = 36.2 mmHg and compute the SBP effect at the two exposure levels Poleon et al. report around a visit (85.5% in Pre14-4 and 88.5% in Pre3-1, overall group): E(0.885) - E(0.855) = 22.97 - 22.67 = 0.30 mmHg. Carrying the abstract's stated CIs through: EC50 at 26% gives 0.18 mmHg, at 76% gives 0.36 mmHg; scaling E(100%) across 11-37 mmHg gives roughly 0.08-0.56 mmHg. Against a typical-patient effect of about 23 mmHg this is about 1.3% (range about 0.4-2.5%) of the PER-ARM effect, BEFORE any between-arm cancellation. Caveats I own: the 3 pp surge is transplanted from glaucoma eyedrops into hypertension; Tousset's exposure variable is itself a 10-day pre-visit window; the Hill coefficient is assumed; this is an order-of-magnitude bound, not a result. Its use here is only to test whether the record's own evidence makes a material effect plausible a priori.
