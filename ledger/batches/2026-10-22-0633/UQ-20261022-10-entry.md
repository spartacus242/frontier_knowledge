Question (canonical): For a declared jurisdiction and window, under a support-withdrawal event definition fixed and published before any data pull (minimum per-class constituents of "support": programming/interrogation, firmware and security updates, consumable and external-component supply, replacement-part supply, remote monitoring, a named clinical point of contact): (i) what is the enumerated list of support-withdrawal events and, per event, the estimated number of living recipients with source and stated uncertainty; and (ii) for events whose recipients are followable, what is the incidence of therapy loss, revision, explant and newly acquired imaging or treatment contraindication in the post-exit period versus the same recipients' pre-exit period and versus matched recipients of still-supported same-class devices — estimand declared per class, null permitted for passive classes, and the share of events whose exposed population could not be counted and the share of classes with no possible outcome ascertainment reported as results rather than caveats.
Reformulation note: this is the version after the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation and is the version graded; the intake version ("how many people are living with an implanted device whose manufacturer has exited... and what are their measured clinical outcomes") is recorded in the record file.

Alternative formulations: (lay) what happens to patients when the company that made their implant goes away, how many are there, and what measurably happens to them; (technical) treating manufacturer exit or support withdrawal as a time-varying exposure on implant cohorts, what is the event incidence per class and the hazard of therapy loss, revision or explant; (method-field) what is the patient-level effect of product market withdrawal when the product is a permanent implant rather than a dispensed drug, i.e. when withdrawal cannot be answered by switching.

Motivating gap: silence mapping. The implantable-device literature is dense with approval, surveillance and registry method and near-empty on manufacturer exit as a patient-level exposure; the "what happens when the maker leaves" discussion sits in op-eds, trade press and bioethics. Adversary 2 narrowed the gap: the field did convene on the exposure (Okun 2024, below) and produced a definition without any count, prevalence or outcome rate. Adversary 3 narrowed it further on one flank: the analysis shape has been performed and published once, for a passive class under a product-defect construct (PIP).
