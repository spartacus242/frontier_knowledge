Opening them does not hand the entry its quantity, in two different shapes of no.** **Pageler 2016 is the published-is-not-performed case, and the cleanest instance of it on this ledger.** It is the on-topic paper — its whole subject is validating the completeness and accuracy of migrated data — it NAMES a non-migration event ("a failure to migrate some clinical notes") and prints no number for it at all. What it does is BOUND the migration error rate by a sampling plan and report zero errors found inside that bound. A bound is not a measurement and "some clinical notes" is not a fraction. This is the strongest available demonstration that the field ran the analysis and never printed the quantity, and it is an abstract-level finding, so no absence in the body text is recorded on the checker's authority. **Brinkman 2026 (OFID) is the regime case.** It prints ten figures at a real cutover and the tempting one, **36.5%**, is the wrong estimand three times over: patients rather than entries, discrepancies in both directions rather than losses, and a cause the authors decline to assign, having themselves pointed at "an incomplete legacy system list". Its loss-direction figure is a bare count of three patients with no printed denominator. Used as a per-fact-class non-migration fraction it would repeat exactly the defect that moved a verdict in the previous batch. If 36.5% is cited anywhere it must carry its denominator, its both-directions definition and the authors' own legacy-list caveat, or it should not be cited. Neither source measures a discontinuity in an incidence, exposure or comorbidity ESTIMATE at a cutover date: Pageler measures no epidemiological quantity at all, and Brinkman measures cross-system record disagreement, which is adjacent but is not a step change in a rate.

**3. N1 (Black et al. 2023) says most of what the entry attributes to it, but not the part that sets the cap.** Confirmed: the 36.4% patient linkage rate, the procedure counts (0 before 2012, then 17,171 and 12,867) and the antibiotic drop (1,601 to 475 per year at similar encounter volumes). But it produces **no per-fact-class non-migration fraction**, states no materiality bound, pre-registers nothing and performs no test, and its unmatched patients are linkage failures under a four-attribute probabilistic rule, **not records demonstrably never received** — so the entry must stop describing the 10,166 unmatched patients as unmigrated records. It therefore does not cap a candidate whose unit is the fraction of the pre-cutover record absent per fact class, nor one supplying a materiality threshold or an auditable reconciliation against the source system. It **does** cap any candidate whose novelty is merely that EMR migrations damage longitudinal research data and provenance should be checked first, which is this paper's own stated lesson. Repairs: rewrite the suitability claim to quote the paper's own sentence, strike the two sub-claims the checker could not support, and correct the front matter to the printed names and full title.

**4. The "already numbered" verdict must be split by limb.** On "does a cutover produce a measurable discontinuity in operational measures or model performance, and by how much" the answer is yes, asked and numbered twice over: Colicchio 2018 at 40 (98%) of 41 operational measures changing significantly in at least one region, with the under-quoted caveat that 20 (49%) of the between-group differences were produced by the **control** arm moving; and Nestor 2019 at a 0.29 AUROC drop (mortality, random forest, raw features), printed in abstract, introduction, conclusion and Table 1. Both numbers survive checking. On "what fraction of a record fails to migrate" **neither source asks it and neither answers it, and Nestor in particular cannot be used for it** — its discontinuity occurs with BOTH systems' records retained and read, a feature-namespace break rather than a data-loss measurement, and Colicchio does not touch migration at all. **So the "already numbered" verdict is withdrawn for limb (i) specifically.** Repairs regardless: restate the 20 (49%) as an additional count over 41 measures rather than a share of the 40; restore the "attributable to the intervention" and "intervention vs control sites" qualifiers on the 40 (98%); replace "segmented regression" with the paper's own "interrupted time-series analysis (ITSA) with an ordinary least squares model (OLS)"; soften "prospective" to routinely collected operational measures analysed over Feb 2013 to Jul 2017; and attach to the 0.29 that it is measured with no data loss.

**5. N3 (Le et al. 2025) holds at abstract level and is half-settled where it matters.** All nine numbers are printed in the abstract and the arithmetic is right: 425 + 36 = 461, and 425/461 = 92.2% on a denominator of **461 reviewed charts**, re-derived at the author's own rounding. The regime favours the entry's concern and is unambiguous: the sample was drawn **after** migration, from the 13,000 records that **had been** migrated, so 92.2% is silent on any record not migrated. But the abstract states **no pre-cutover total and no migration selection criteria**, so the entry must not assert that records were excluded from migration, nor that 13,000 was the whole pre-cutover pool; settling that needs the full-text Methods, which **could not be read** — a COULD NOT READ on the decisive question, not a NOT FOUND. Two qualifiers belong on any use of 92.2%: the unexplained 39-chart gap between 500 selected and 461 reviewed, making it a rate among *reviewable* charts, and the absent uncertainty, the source printing a bare proportion. Two of the entry's labels are paraphrase ("incorrect results" for "incorrect genetic testing results entered"; "extra" for "additional information transferred"), and the dropped word "entered" is the one that would say whether the 9 errors were made by the migration or merely carried through it. **It is also one genetics family-history tool swap, not an EHR replacement** — "a family history database", "from one commercial software solution to another" — so it must not be pooled with Pageler's whole-system transition: different units (data type vs patient chart), different endpoints (post-go-live null vs chart concordance), different estimands, and only Le prints a rate. This entry's quantitative content rests on Le's abstract alone.

**6. Schreiber & Garber grounds no grade in either direction, for a reason the entry had not established.** The source is real, correctly cited apart from a truncated title, open access, and a review-plus-case-studies hybrid rather than a primary measurement study. Three contents are confirmed at abstract level: nine consequence domains, a conclusion calling for prospective research, two new cases added to six already reported. The abstract prints no fraction — only counts of reports and of domains — **but the body is where a fraction would live and the body did not open**, so this source supports neither closure nor confirmation of the gap. The declared-absence sentence the entry quotes is **unverified as wording** and should not be quoted from this source until someone has read it, and "sparse" should not be substituted for it. Restate its status from "could not read at any route" to **full text blocked by publisher anti-crawler protection; abstract-level content obtained via Crossref; CC BY 4.0 and readable by a human in a browser** — a successor with a browser-capable route, or simply with fetch budget to finish the Crossref abstract and retry Thieme, can likely settle this outright. This is the remaining decisive unread source and the entry's cap now rests on it alone, not on three.
