## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Automated video measurement of animal behaviour — pose estimators (DeepLabCut, SLEAP) and
behaviour classifiers (supervised ethogram models, MoSeq-class unsupervised models) — is sold
and validated as one pooled accuracy number: mean keypoint pixel error, or per-behaviour F1 on
a test set drawn across all animals. But the instrument is a function of the animal's *picture*,
and the manipulations it is used to study change the picture: a sedated or ataxic mouse holds a
different posture, an obese or aged mouse a different silhouette, an injured mouse a different
gait, a mutant line sometimes a different coat colour or body size. So the question is whether
the machine makes *different* mistakes on treated animals than on controls — and if it does, how
much of a published behavioural effect is the measuring device changing its behaviour rather than
the animal changing its behaviour. Operationally: estimate sensitivity and specificity per group
(discrete behaviour calls) or the per-keypoint error distribution per group (pose) against blinded
multi-rater human labels; test equality across groups; and where it fails, correct the effect
estimate for differential misclassification and compare the corrected estimate against the
published confidence interval and the assay's usual effect size.

Short version: the field retired the biased human observer and may have hired a biased machine
observer, and nobody has weighed the machine's bias in the units the field's claims are made in.

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional** — nothing Unresolved (attacks 4 and 5 Partial, the rest Resolved), but novelty is
now thinner than the record's Medium: MARS realizes the exact residual risk the record named, so
presupposition 3 must be reworded from "no study reports group-stratified classifier error" to "no
study estimates per-group error for a *biological treatment* grouping and propagates it to the
effect estimate", and limb (b) must be re-specified for outcome-dependent error. Promotion to
Validated requires: (1) MARS cited as a partial prior and presupposition 3 reworded; (2) limb (b)
re-specified with a bout-structure-conditional error model or an explicit bias-bounding estimand;
(3) eLife reviewed preprint 95709 and eNeuro ENEURO.0500-23.2024 read.

**Single strongest killing point:** limb (b) is specified with the wrong error model, and the
field's own canonical pipeline paper is the proof. MARS reports that classifier precision and
recall rise with bout length, and its one stratified comparison (cabled vs uncabled) is null only
"when controlling for mean bout length" — so the error depends on the realized behaviour, not just
on the group. Per-group sensitivity and specificity, which is what limb (a) delivers, therefore do
not identify the bias that limb (b) promises to compute by "standard differential-misclassification
correction"; and because sedation, ataxia and injury change bout structure directly, this holds in
precisely the regime the candidate occupies (discrete behaviour calls and bout counts), though not
in the same form for the per-keypoint pose limb.
