Domain(s): Biology — bacterial cell biophysics (cell-envelope mechanics, osmotic-stress physiology, peptidoglycan remodelling). Method home: mechanical/reliability engineering (S-N stress-life, endurance limit, Palmgren-Miner linear damage summation).

Question (canonical): For single *Escherichia coli* cells held in a microfluidic trap and subjected to repeated hypo-osmotic downshocks of fixed amplitude DPi (each below the single-shock lytic threshold) at a fixed recovery interval, does the per-cell hazard of lysis depend on the cycle index n after conditioning on the largest amplitude the cell has experienced — and if so, (a) is median cycles-to-lysis N(DPi) describable by a stress-life power law log N = a - b log DPi across >= 4 amplitudes, (b) is there an amplitude below which no lysis occurs within the attainable cycle budget, and (c) in a two-block mixed-amplitude sequence, does SUM(n_i / N_i) = 1 at failure as Palmgren-Miner predicts — each tested in a growing arm and in a wall-synthesis-inhibited arm?

Alternative formulations:
- (lay) Do bacteria have a fatigue limit — an osmotic shock amplitude they survive indefinitely but not ten thousand times?
- (sharpest single limb) Is envelope failure a strength problem set by peak turgor, or a damage-accumulation problem set by cycle count?
- (method field's words) For the component "peptidoglycan sacculus under cyclic tensile loading", what is the stress-life relation, is there an endurance limit, and does linear damage summation hold — for a component repaired in service?

Motivating gap: Envelope mechanics is posed as a strength / peak-stress problem, so the cycle-count axis has not been traversed in a bacterial wall. The intake's universal was already narrowed at Gate 2 and is narrowed further here: the cycle-count axis exists in a single living cell (Qiang 2019, human RBC, ~900 electrodeformation cycles, Wohler-like curve in unopened SI), in a walled microbe at population level (Palou 1998, oscillatory HHP), in *E. coli* itself at population level (Sleight 2006, 28 freeze-thaw cycles), and under periodic osmotic forcing of single walled cells with per-cell death (Duveau 2024, yeast). What remained unoccupied is the intersection: per-cell lysis hazard versus osmotic cycle number in a bacterium whose wall is resynthesised during the test.

Significance — consequence: If cycle index carries information after conditioning on peak amplitude, the sacculus acquires a state variable current models lack, "sub-lethal" becomes a conditional category for any process that cycles osmolarity (bioprocess release, hurdle processing, soil wet-dry cycling, host niches), and the 28-year-old oscillatory-processing regularity gets a mechanism. If not, the finding is a quantitative statement about resynthesis outrunning damage accumulation, and marks the boundary of a transplant that succeeded in a non-repairing cell.
Significance — audience: single-cell osmotic-shock biophysics groups (Pilizota, Phillips/Chure, Huang) who own the instrument and would add one arm; oscillatory/hurdle food-process engineers who have acted on cycle count since 1998; cell-wall-active antibiotic pharmacodynamics (argued from mechanism, sourced to nothing opened — the record concedes this); the RBC-fatigue methodologists. Significance — fecundity: does repair rate SET the endurance limit, making it physiological rather than material; is damage localised at the division site; does cycling sensitise cells to beta-lactams; are mechanosensitive channels fatigue mitigation rather than emergency valves; is there a load-order effect; does the stress-life exponent differ between Gram-positive and Gram-negative envelopes.
