Domain(s): Biology — systematics/taxonomy and biodiversity informatics (target field). Method transplanted from forecast verification / calibration of verbal probability expressions and from diagnostic-test predictive value.

Question (canonical): For specimen and occurrence records bearing an open-nomenclature qualifier ("cf.", "aff.", "sp.", "?"), drawn only from sources in which qualifier survival from original label to delivered record has been verified end-to-end, and under a resolution rule declared before any data pull: what is the per-qualifier agreement rate with an independent later determination at a declared rank (reported separately for a revision-based arm and a sequence-backed arm), does that rate order the qualifiers in the sequence their published usage guidance implies, and does the between-stratum spread of that rate across determiner, institution, higher taxon and decade stay inside a pre-declared tolerance — the tolerance being the criterion for whether an aggregator may read the mark as a transferable probability?

Alternative formulations:
- Do taxonomy's hedging marks carry a reproducible probability — does "cf." mean the same chance of being right in two collections, two taxa or two decades?
- Treating each qualified determination as a probabilistic forecast and each later revision or barcode-backed determination as its resolution, how well calibrated are open-nomenclature qualifiers, and how is the miscalibration distributed across determiners and higher taxa?
(V1, as intaken, and the Gate 1 reformulation rationale are in batches/2026-10-18-0945/UQ-20261018-18-record.md; V2 is scored as canonical.)

Motivating gap: Open nomenclature is a formalised convention with published usage guidance and an asserted ordinal confidence axis (Sigovini et al. 2016, read: "the degree of confidence essentially increases from the top down"), which itself records that authors rank "aff." and "cf." in opposite orders and defers numbers to a prospective [0,1] field. The marks propagate at scale into aggregated data, where consumers drop or keep qualified records by convention and the backbone silently lifts unmatched qualified names (Waller 2022, read). The resolution machinery already exists and has been used for the unconditional quantity (Goodwin et al. 2015, read: "at least 58%" wrong names; "29.1%" inter-herbarium disagreement) without conditioning on a qualifier. Three decades of listserv-level complaint that workers use the marks differently, never measured.

Presuppositions: (1) qualifiers retrievable at scale with the mark preserved — CONTESTED, evidenced contested by Waller 2022; (2) independent later determinations exist for enough qualified records — CONTESTED; (3) later determination as ground truth — CONTESTED and conceded (revision arm yields agreement, not predictive value; arms never pooled); (4) resolution not selected on prior doubt — CONTESTED, principal validity threat, requires wholesale-revision or whole-drawer-barcoding resolutions; (5) "confirmed" declared per rank — ESTABLISHED as a declaration; (6) determiner identifiers present for the by-determiner limb — CONTESTED, else reported unestimable; (7) determiners used the marks with documented meanings — NOT ASSUMED, this is the object; (8) that a probabilistic reading is the right reading — CONTESTED, flagged as dissolution risk, V2 phrased so the answer bears on whether users' reading is licensed.

What would count as an answer: a pre-declared frame of qualified determinations with qualifier survival verified end-to-end; a pre-declared resolution rule naming which determinations count and at which rank, revision and sequence arms separate; then (a) per-qualifier agreement rates with intervals, (b) a reliability curve against the qualifiers ordered as Sigovini 2016 Fig. 1 implies, (c) a variance decomposition across determiner, institution, higher taxon and decade against the declared tolerance, (d) a sensitivity analysis using only selection-free resolutions. Correct ordering at stratum-specific levels licenses a recalibration layer; no reproducible ordering establishes that the flag carries no transferable information.
