Question (canonical) — GATE 0 AS SUBMITTED:
Among completed multicentre randomised trials whose intervention or primary outcome is mediated by a
software-containing measurement device, in what fraction can a manufacturer firmware or
calibration-algorithm revision during the enrolment window be established from manufacturer, regulatory
or recall records, and where it can, does the estimated treatment effect differ between pre- and
post-revision device eras by more than the trial's own pre-specified minimum clinically important
difference?

Question (canonical) — GATE 1 REFORMULATION (the one reformulation permitted; well-posedness only,
no change of subject). Both versions are on the record; the reformulation fixes three defects named
under Gate 1 below (unspecified denominator, unspecified effect scale, unavailable MCID benchmark):
For a pre-specified sampling frame of completed multicentre RCTs published in a fixed window whose
primary outcome is generated or titrated by a software-containing measurement device AND whose report
or protocol names the device model (the frame; the share of device-mediated trials excluded for not
naming a model is itself reported as denominator attrition):
 (i) RETRIEVAL LIMB — for what fraction can a firmware or calibration-algorithm revision dated inside
     that trial's enrolment window be established, with the retrieval route recorded per trial from a
     pre-specified, ordered route list (manufacturer release notes / service bulletins; FDA recall and
     MAUDE records; EU field-safety notices; direct manufacturer enquiry; the trial's own report), the
     result being explicitly a LOWER BOUND on occurrence and an upper bound on nothing; and
 (ii) INTERACTION LIMB — in the subset with both a datable revision and recoverable IPD (or a trial
     group willing to re-run), what is the treatment-effect-by-device-era interaction on the trial's
     own pre-specified effect scale, with the interaction test pre-specified, benchmarked against the
     trial's own pre-specified MCID where one exists and otherwise against the half-width of the
     trial's own primary 95% CI (a stated fallback, not a substitute), and reported alongside the
     degree of collinearity between device era and calendar time (so trials where the interaction is
     not estimable are counted as not estimable rather than as null)?

Alternative formulations:
 - Do randomised trials silently pool incommensurable measurements because the devices generating their
   endpoints were re-calibrated by their manufacturers mid-trial?
 - For device-mediated RCTs, is "firmware era" an unmeasured effect modifier large enough to change what
   the trial concluded?

Presuppositions (established / contested, as carried into Gate 1):
 P1. Enrolment window and device model are retrievable from publication or protocol — PARTLY TRUE, and
     the reformulation makes the non-naming fraction reported denominator attrition rather than a hidden
     assumption.
 P2. CONTESTED AND LOAD-BEARING: manufacturer firmware/calibration revision dates are retrievable for
     NON-recall revisions. Recall/MAUDE/EU-FSN databases capture only a subset; routine change-control
     records are proprietary. The BOOST-II case is an existence proof that such a date can be obtained
     (Masimo cooperated with the trialists) and simultaneously evidence of the bias: it was obtainable
     because the trialists asked, which is not available retrospectively to third parties. The census is
     therefore a LOWER BOUND, and the reformulation requires it to be reported as one.
 P3. CONTESTED: IPD are available to re-estimate by era for a non-trivial subset (Vivli / YODA-type
     platforms, or trial groups willing to re-run). Unquantified; the recoverable subset may be small.
 P4. A device software revision can change the RECORDED value of the endpoint, not merely internal
     device behaviour — ESTABLISHED for the oximeter case (Johnston 2011, quoted above), not general.
 P5. Trials pre-specified an MCID or target effect size to benchmark an era difference — PARTLY TRUE;
     the reformulation supplies an explicit fallback (half-width of the primary 95% CI) and requires the
     fallback to be labelled wherever used.
 P6. Firmware era is not fully collinear with calendar time within a trial — MUST BE CHECKED PER TRIAL.
     Supported in one case (BOOST-II had 1187 of 2448 infants on the revised algorithm and could
     estimate the interaction), not assumable. The reformulation counts non-estimable trials as
     non-estimable rather than null.
 NOT PRESUPPOSED: that any such revision materially changed any trial's conclusion anywhere other than
     the index case. The census and the interaction magnitude are the question.

What would count as an answer:
A pre-registered census over a pre-specified sample of device-mediated RCTs (fixed window, device model
named) giving (i) per trial, whether a mid-enrolment firmware/calibration revision can be established,
with the retrieval route recorded for each and the result declared a lower bound, and (ii) for the subset
with recoverable IPD and a datable revision, era-stratified treatment-effect estimates with a
pre-specified interaction test, a collinearity diagnostic, and comparison against that trial's own MCID
(or stated fallback). A null — no datable revisions outside the index case, or interactions below the MCID
in every recoverable trial — is an equally complete answer and would retire the concern, which currently
circulates as a cautionary tale of unknown generality. A positive finding in even one further influential
trial implies a concrete change to SPIRIT/CONSORT device reporting and to DSMB practice.
NOT an answer: expert commentary that this "could" happen (the Johnston 2011 warning already is that);
a retelling of the oximeter case alone (BOOST-II 2013 already is that).

   candidate asks a meta-research question about a CORPUS of trials: how often such a revision is even
   establishable retrospectively, by what route, and whether the era interaction exceeds each trial's
   own MCID. N1 supplies the candidate's existence proof for presupposition P4 and nothing on either limb.
 vs N2: N2 performs, for one trial family, exactly the candidate's limb (ii) — era stratification plus a

   This is the closest prior and it is CLOSE: for the index case the interaction question is asked AND

   benchmark against an MCID, and explicitly does not generalise. The candidate's novelty rests on the

Significance — consequence:
 If YES (revisions are establishable in a non-trivial fraction and the era interaction exceeds the MCID
 in more than the index trial): three concrete changes follow, each with an existing mechanism to carry
 it. (1) A SPIRIT/CONSORT item requiring device model and software/firmware version with dates of any
 change during enrolment — the principle is already conceded for one device class, since CONSORT-AI and
 DECIDE-AI require model version, so the argument becomes why the requirement stops at AI rather than
 whether versioning matters. (2) DSMB practice: a device-change notification channel and era as a
 pre-specified interaction, where today a mid-trial recalibration reaches the DSMB only by accident.
 (3) IPD meta-analysis practice: NeOProM-style pooling across trials would need a device-era covariate,
 which is not currently collected. If NO (no datable revisions beyond the index case, or interactions
 below MCID everywhere recoverable): the oximeter episode is reclassified from a general threat to
 trial validity into a singular event, which is worth establishing precisely because it is currently
 cited as a cautionary tale with no known base rate. A lower-bound null is weaker than a null but still
 bounds how much of the device-trial replication gap this mechanism can explain.

Significance — audience:
 Reporting-guideline groups (SPIRIT/CONSORT steering groups, EQUATOR) — would add or decline a device-
 version item on evidence of base rate, not on anecdote. Trial statisticians and DSMB chairs in
 device-mediated fields (neonatology, critical care, diabetes/CGM, sleep medicine, cardiology) — would
 pre-specify era interactions and ask vendors for change-control dates at protocol stage. Device
 regulators (FDA CDRH, EU MDCG) — the census's retrieval-route table is a direct measurement of whether
 their own databases can answer a question trialists need answered, which bears on whether change-control
 disclosure to ongoing investigations should be mandated. IPD-sharing platforms (Vivli, YODA) — whether
 to require device metadata in deposited datasets. Manufacturers' regulatory-affairs functions — whether
 to notify ongoing trials of calibration revisions, a decision currently made ad hoc.

Significance — fecundity:
 Opens: should manufacturers be obliged to notify ongoing trials of calibration changes, and what would
 that obligation cost; does the DIRECTION of a revision (artefact removal versus accuracy tuning) predict
 the direction of the era interaction, which would make the mechanism causal rather than merely
 correlational; do multi-vendor trials buy robustness against firmware era that single-vendor trials
 lack, which is a designable property; how much of the poor replication record of device-mediated trials
 is attributable to measurement-era shifts rather than to population or practice differences; and whether
 the AI/ML solution already in force (predetermined change control plans, locked versus adaptive
 algorithms) should be extended to conventional measurement devices used as endpoint instruments.

Significance — anchoring:
 Touches (a) the unresolved NeOProM oxygen-saturation-target controversy, where the algorithm-era
 heterogeneity sits at the centre of what the trials are taken to have shown; (b) the pulse-oximeter
 accuracy/bias literature (including occult-hypoxaemia and skin-pigmentation calibration work), the same
 device class and the same dependence on a vendor calibration curve; (c) measurement invariance and
 break-in-series methodology in survey research and laboratory metrology, which have mature treatments of
 exactly this threat and no bridge to trials; (d) the regulatory debate over adaptive versus locked
 device software, which solved the versioning problem for AI/ML devices and left it open for every other
 software-containing device.

Reason for silence:
 A disciplinary boundary reinforced by a records asymmetry, plus an incentive gap — not "nobody thought
 of it". The people who hold firmware revision dates (manufacturers' change-control and regulatory-affairs
 functions, device engineers) do not estimate treatment effects; the people who estimate treatment effects
 do not read service bulletins or board-firmware release notes and have no standing route to them. The one
 documented place the two met is the anchor: Stenson's group were simultaneously trialists and
 investigators of the device, with vendor cooperation, and so could date the revision and stratify by it —
 and even there the result was treated as a property of those trials rather than as an instance of a
 class. The incentive gap closes the loop: establishing a revision date for someone else's trial requires
 a manufacturer to disclose proprietary change-control records with no benefit to itself and an obvious
 liability risk, so the census looks infeasible from the outside, and questions that look unanswerable do
 not get asked. A fourth, self-sealing mechanism: because no reporting standard ever required the device
 version, the retrospective corpus is sparse by construction, which makes the question appear
 unanswerable in exactly the way that keeps the reporting standard from changing.
