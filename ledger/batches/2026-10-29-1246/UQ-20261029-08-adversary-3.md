## Restatement (my own words)

Run a program on a managed runtime and record, for every heap object, the byte-count at which it was allocated and the byte-count at which it became unreachable. Tabulate deaths by (age at death, byte-time of death), with the objects alive and at risk in each cell as exposure, and fit log-linear rate models with factor effects for age, death time ("period") and birth time ("cohort" = death time minus age). Because the three indices are exactly collinear on one clock, only second differences along each axis are identifiable. The question asks: are the second differences along the death-time axis and the birth-time axis non-zero (with intervals); does a likelihood-ratio test reject the model in which the death rate depends on age alone; and how much deviance do the death-time and birth-time axes remove beyond age, under every order of adding the axes? In one sentence: does a heap object's instantaneous death rate depend on WHEN in the program it is dying and WHEN it was born, over and above HOW OLD it is — and by how much?

Note on the record's own repair: V2's period axis is "death time p" on the bytes clock, i.e. program progress, NOT a phase partition. The record's presupposition 2 ("a phase rule declared before any trace") is inherited from V1's wording and is not a presupposition of V2 as written; V2 needs only a pre-declared bin width on the bytes clock. The record's prose ("program-phase (period)", "phase-keyed") keeps saying "phase" where V2 says "death time"; the judge should align the prose to V2 rather than the other way round.

## Attack 4 clause list (V2 as it stands, repair included), each graded on something I opened

1. A managed-runtime program can be traced to exact object death times on a bytes-allocated clock. **TRUE (sourced, B):** "A perfect trace ... includes the birth and death time of all objects, measured in bytes allocated."; death time = "the time interval immediately following the one when it was last reachable." Qualifier carried: "exact" means exact to the allocation at which the clock ticks, and Merlin "assumes that there are no pointer stores involving an unreachable object." V2's binned cells sit inside that resolution.
2. The at-risk set of every (age, death-time) cell is recoverable from birth and death times. **TRUE (arithmetic; my own reasoning, not a source finding):** an object born at b and dead at d is at risk in cell (p − b, p) for every p with b ≤ p < d.
3. Age, period and cohort lie on ONE clock so c = p − a exactly. **TRUE (sourced, B):** birth and death are both "measured in bytes allocated", so age in bytes is their difference and the dependence is exact.
4. Under that exact dependence only second differences (and certain slope combinations) are identifiable without an arbitrary constraint. **UNVERIFIED (by me and by the record):** neither of us opened an APC identifiability source; the arXiv titles the record returned (2112.08299, 2312.09698) are title-level only. I believe this to be the textbook result but it is not sourced in this record. Kills nothing; blocks a 3/3 well-posedness claim until opened.
5. A Poisson GLM over cells with exposure "the objects at risk in that cell" is a valid hazard model. **PARTIAL — a specification wrinkle, my own reasoning, no source opened:** with exact death times the exposure should be object-bytes at risk within the cell (or a binomial/complementary-log-log discrete-time hazard), because in the nursery a cell can lose a large share of its entrants and "count of objects at risk" × cell width overstates exposure; and with binned age and period, c = p − a spans two cohort bins unless Lexis triangles are used (the "unequal intervals" issue named in the TITLE of arXiv:2112.08299, unopened). Both are repairable by declaration and leave the identification structure unchanged; neither makes the question unanswerable. The search that would ground this (piecewise-exponential–Poisson equivalence) was not run.
6.
