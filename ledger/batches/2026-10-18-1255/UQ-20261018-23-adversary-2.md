## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

When a hospital or health system rips out one vendor's EHR and puts in another's,
only some of the old record actually crosses over into the new system in a form a
query can reach; the rest sits in a read-only viewer or as scanned images. The
candidate asks three things. (i) Per category of record — structured results, notes,
waveforms, images' metadata, orders, medication administration, scanned documents,
device feeds — what share of what existed in the old system on go-live day can
afterwards be pulled from the new system's own research query surface (its warehouse
or OMOP/i2b2 extract), counting a read-only legacy viewer as *not* surviving.
(ii) Inside at least two systems, does an element-density time series with the
departmental go-live as the breakpoint (plus one class expected to be untouched and
one known to have been dropped) reproduce those shares. (iii) For one already-published
EHR study and one live risk model whose look-back reaches across that date, which way
and how much do feature availability and actual calibration move.

In short: treat an EHR replacement as an archival migration, measure its fidelity by
data class, and then show what that fidelity does to evidence and to deployed models.

migration date — is the one part of the candidate that the review explicitly does not cover
