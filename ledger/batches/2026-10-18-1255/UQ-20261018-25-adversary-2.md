## Restatement (my own words)

When a randomised trial of a long-term drug ends, what happens to the people who were in it over the following year? Specifically: (a) what share of them are still on the trial drug, back on whatever they took before, on some third drug, or on nothing, at 3, 6 and 12 months after their own last study visit, measured against what they were dispensed in the year before randomisation; and (b) does the trial's own outcome measure (or a routinely recorded stand-in) step or bend at that last-visit date, compared with a declared non-trial control series — and does that step differ depending on which arm they were randomised to.

Put plainly: is there a measurable clinical discontinuity at the moment a trial stops looking after its participants, and is it arm-dependent?
