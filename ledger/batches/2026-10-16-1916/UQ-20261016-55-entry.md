Verification (post-batch, fresh-context checker, log UQ-20261016-55-verification.md): both priors resolve, and neither does what the kill said.
arXiv:2508.17076 is “Towards a Real-World Aligned Benchmark for Unlearning in Recommender Systems” (Lubitzsch, Ovcharenko, Chen, de Rijke, Schelter; v1 2025-08-23, v2 2025-09-18), a position and benchmark-design paper. It argues that existing recsys unlearning benchmarks — CURE4Rec at 5% or more, NeurIPS 2023 at 2% — “simulate unrealistically large unlearning requests” and that the setup should “reflect the sequential, time-sensitive nature of real-world deletion requests”. It ASSERTS that; it does not measure it and does not pose it as a question. Its own experiment uses a 0.1% forget set on Instacart (about 1,000 users for alcohol, about 650 for meat), with Recall, nDCG, PHR, KL divergence against retraining from scratch.
arXiv:2601.21682 is “FIT to Forget: Robust Continual Unlearning for Large Language Models” (Xu et al.; v1 2026-01-29, v2 2026-05-07). It stipulates a sequential request stream over 600 GPT-4o-generated samples (200 each personal, copyright, harmful; 0 to 300 requests in steps of 60) and its Limitations state that the streams are predefined and that real requests may differ.
Neither measures a real, enumerable stream of removal demands — counts, legal basis, size per demand — against any corpus. So the two papers support this candidate's gap claim rather than closing it: a sibling field believes the mismatch exists and has not measured it. What they do establish is that the FRAMING is published outside this corpus, which is why attack 2 lands at Partial rather than Resolved.
Not verified: the claim that GitHub's DMCA archive holds only single-digit notices naming machine-learning training datasets could not be checked by the checker's one web search (it returned mirrors of individual notices). Strongest remaining objection (restated after a late strict adversary, 12 executed Disguise queries, every Unresolved claim sourced): a defect in the sampling frame itself. V1 pre-registers retractions as one of four “enumerable public records of removal demands”, but COPE's retraction guidelines make a retraction close to the opposite of a removal demand: “the online version of the article should be retained with a clear notice of retraction”, because “Retaining the original work ensures transparency of the published record”, with removal reserved for “extremely limited cases” (defamation, privacy violation, court order, serious health risk) and even then “the metadata (title and authors) should be retained”. So a quarter of the frame contributes demands no claimant made — and the record's new-instrument reason for silence leans on exactly that stratum, Retraction Watch having been open on Crossref since 2023. The defect is severable by dropping the stratum, which is why it caps rather than kills.
Second, on support, carried over from adversary #3 and still standing: the benchmark side is partly degenerate by construction — TOFU's forget set is drawn from “200 diverse synthetic author profiles” (arXiv:2401.06121, opened), so its corpus footprint is zero and its position outside any real-demand distribution is settled without a census; WMDP is a hazardous-knowledge safety proxy per its own README; and MUSE already spans request sizes, its own abstract naming “scalability with respect to the size of removal requests” and “sustainability over sequential unlearning requests”, with scal and sust configs. On the demand side, 6 counted answer-searches of the github/dmca archive (fifteen years of notices) returned notices naming machine-learning training datasets in the single digits — one wikiHow notice in 2025 being the only one targeting a scraped-content training dataset, with LAION and “Common Crawl” returning zero — and the largest in-frame real demand, the Bartz v. Anthropic order, is an order to destroy torrented files rather than an unlearning request. That is one host and a code-search index that may be incomplete, so it bounds rather than settles the stratum; but unless a census of Lumen or of developers' own disclosures finds a non-trivial stratum, the joint distribution this question asks for has too little support to estimate.
Third, on significance: xAI's mandated CCPA disclosure already publishes real removal-demand counts for a frontier model developer — for 1 January to 31 December 2025, 65 requests to delete received, 65 completed, 0 denied — so the “first denominator for deletion-duty feasibility” partly exists already, as a recurring legal disclosure rather than as research.
Why this is not a rejection: no source opened measures, counts or stratifies a population of real removal demands against a named training corpus, and the obvious own-field position paper that could have posed it (arXiv:2410.02879, opened) argues forget/retain dependency and target ambiguity instead. Attack 2 is Partial, not Unresolved: the framing is in print in a sibling field as an assertion, not as a measurement.
