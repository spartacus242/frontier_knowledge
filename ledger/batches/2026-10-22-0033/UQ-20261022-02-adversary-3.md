## Restatement (my words, before engaging the record's framing)

Pick a list of enzymes in advance, each prepared by a standard published purification route. (a) For every preparation, count the protein molecules that actually have a working catalytic site — by stoichiometric active-site titration or by a single-turnover burst amplitude, with the counting endpoint fixed in advance — and divide that count by the total protein in the same preparation as measured by whatever bulk protein assay the original kcat papers used. Report how that ratio is distributed, split into a part that depends on which enzyme it is and a part that depends on which purification route was used. (b) Then ask an accounting question about the literature: if preparations vary in that ratio by as much as you just measured, what is the largest fraction of the report-to-report scatter in published log10 kcat for those same enzymes that such variation could possibly produce — treating your preparations as interchangeable with the long-gone ones behind the published numbers — with the leftover scatter blamed on assay conditions after correcting for whatever temperature, pH, buffer, ionic strength and substrate the papers declared.

Two things I notice in the restatement that the record's own wording hides. First, limb (b) is an accounting identity, not an experiment: once (a) gives a spread and the corpus gives a total spread, the "maximum share" is one division. Second, limb (a)'s denominator is not a free choice — it is pinned to a historical fact about each source paper ("the same assay the source papers used"), so the question presupposes that this fact is recoverable. The record never lists that presupposition. It is the one I attacked, and it is where the candidate dies.

### My own derivation, labelled as mine and sourced to nothing
Write a reported turnover number as log10 kcat_reported = log10 kcat_true - log10 f + e, with f the active fraction and e the assay-condition-and-error term. If f is independent of the other terms, var(log10 kcat_reported) = var(log10 f) + var(e) + const, so the share limb (b) asks for is var(log10 f) / var(log10 kcat_between-report) — one division of a number limb (a) measures by a number the corpus supplies. If f is NOT independent of e (a laboratory whose preparations are poor may also run poorer assays, and the sign of that covariance is unknown), the cross term reappears and the attributable share is not bounded by that ratio at all; with adversarial correlation it approaches 1. This is elementary variance algebra, it is my own, I cite no paper for it, and I flag the dependence on independence explicitly — because that independence IS the record's P8 exchangeability declaration. Consequence, and it is the record's own conceded objection made precise: limb (b) is either one line of arithmetic (under the declaration) or undefined (without it). No attack I mark Unresolved rests on this derivation; it supports only the two Partials at attacks 1 and 5.
