Question (canonical) — V2, the one Gate 1 reformulation, scored as canonical:
For specimen and occurrence records bearing an open-nomenclature qualifier ("cf.", "aff.", "sp.", "?"), drawn only from sources in which qualifier survival from original label to delivered record has been verified end-to-end, and under a resolution rule declared before any data pull: what is the per-qualifier AGREEMENT RATE with an independent later determination at a declared rank (reported separately for a revision-based arm and a sequence-backed arm), does that rate order the qualifiers in the sequence their published usage guidance implies, and does the between-stratum spread of that rate across determiner, institution, higher taxon and decade stay inside a pre-declared tolerance — the tolerance being the criterion for whether an aggregator may read the mark as a transferable probability?

Question (V1, as intaken):
When a specimen or occurrence record carries an open-nomenclature qualifier ("cf.", "aff.", "sp.", "?"), what is the realized probability that the qualified identification is confirmed by an independent determination, and is that probability stable enough across taxa, determiners, institutions and decades for the qualifier to be read as a calibrated probability statement?

Why V1 was reformulated (well-posedness only, per Gate 1's one permitted reformulation):
(i) V1's "realized probability that the identification is confirmed" presupposes a reference standard. A later revision is another expert judgement, so what is estimable is an AGREEMENT (concordance) rate against a declared resolution rule, not a predictive value against truth; V2 renames the estimand accordingly and keeps "probability correct" only for the sequence-backed arm, where it remains conditional on reference-library error. This is the same discipline that forbids computing PPV without a reference standard, and it is the fix the diagnostic-test transplant itself demands.
(ii) V1's "stable enough ... to be read as a calibrated probability" could be answered either way by taste. V2 makes the stability criterion a pre-declared tolerance on between-stratum spread, tied to the decision it would license, so the answer cannot float.
(iii) V2 moves qualifier-survival verification and per-rank declaration out of the presuppositions and into the frame definition, since the candidate itself flags both as contested.

Alternative formulations:
- Do taxonomy's hedging marks carry a reproducible probability — does "cf." mean the same chance of being right in two collections, two taxa or two decades?
- Treating each qualified determination as a probabilistic forecast and each later revision or barcode-backed determination as its resolution, how well calibrated are open-nomenclature qualifiers, and how is the miscalibration distributed across determiners and higher taxa?

Motivating gap:
Open nomenclature is a formalised convention with published usage guidance. Sigovini, Keppel & Tagliapietra (Methods in Ecology and Evolution 2016, OPENED AND READ this pass) reviews the marks, proposes conventions and a flow chart, and arranges them on an ordinal confidence axis — its Fig. 1 caption states that "the degree of confidence essentially increases from the top down" — while itself recording that different authors rank "aff." and "cf." in opposite orders. It assigns no probability to any mark anywhere in the paper, and its only gesture at quantification is prospective: "the degree of confidence in data could be quantified, for instance by assigning them a value in the [0,1] interval" (citing Huber & Klump 2009). So the field has an ordinal structure, a published complaint that the ordering is not shared between authors, and an explicit note that numeric confidence COULD be attached in future — and no measurement of what the existing marks have actually delivered.
Meanwhile the marks propagate at scale into aggregated biodiversity data, where downstream users drop qualified records or keep them wholesale by convention. Waller (BISS 2022, OPENED AND READ) shows the aggregator side of this is live and unmeasured: GBIF's own guidance is "do not put identification qualifiers in the dwc:scientificName field but rather use the dwc:identificationQualifier field," and names with qualifiers or remarks are among those the backbone cannot match and silently lifts to a higher rank — but that abstract reports no count of qualifier-bearing records and no test of whether qualifiers survive name matching.
The ground-truth machinery the calibration question needs already exists and has already been used for the UNCONDITIONAL quantity: Goodwin et al. (Current Biology 2015, OPENED AND READ, via Oxford ORA) report that "at least 58% of the specimens had the wrong name prior to a recent taxonomic study" in ~4,500 Aframomum specimens from 40 herbaria against a 2015 monograph, and that "29.1%" of duplicate dipterocarp collections "had different names in different herbaria." Its categories are Correct / Genus or Family / Synonyms / Other; it does not condition on, or report any rate for, a qualifier being present. So revisions and duplicate comparison routinely generate the resolutions this question would consume, and the conditional quantity has simply not been computed.

Presuppositions (each marked established or contested):
1. Qualified determinations are retrievable at scale with the qualifier preserved. CONTESTED, and now partly evidenced as contested: Waller 2022 (read) documents that qualifier-bearing name strings are among those GBIF's backbone cannot match and that publishers are advised to move qualifiers into a separate field, which is exactly the normalisation step that can strip or hide them. Establishing qualifier survival end-to-end is the answer's first step, not an assumption, and V2 writes it into the frame.
2. An independent later determination exists for enough qualified records to estimate a rate (determination/annotation histories, or a sequence from the same specimen). CONTESTED. A Darwin Core identification-history extension is listed at rs.gbif.org (dwc/identification_history_2025-07-10.xml, URL seen in search results, NOT opened), which suggests the plumbing is newly standardised; coverage is unknown and must be measured in the frame step.
3. The later determination can serve as ground truth. CONTESTED and conceded: a revision is another expert judgement, not a reference standard. V2 therefore reports an agreement rate for the revision arm and reserves probability-correct language for the sequence-backed arm, which still inherits reference-library error. Arms reported separately, never pooled.
4. Resolution is not selected on prior doubt. CONTESTED and the principal threat to validity: specimens get revised BECAUSE someone doubted them, and a qualifier is a published invitation to doubt, so the naive agreement rate is biased by a mechanism that is correlated with the predictor itself. The design must break the dependence — resolutions generated by wholesale revision of an entire collection or clade, or by a barcoding campaign that sequenced everything in a drawer, not by targeted re-examination.
5. "Confirmed" is definable at a declared rank: a species-rank "cf." can be wrong at species and right at genus, so the estimand is declared per rank. ESTABLISHED as a declaration, not an assumption.
6. Enough qualified records carry a determiner identifier for the by-determiner variance limb. CONTESTED; if not, that limb is reported as unestimable in the frame rather than estimated badly.
7. Determiners applied qualifiers with their documented meanings rather than idiosyncratically. NOT ASSUMED — this is the question's object. Sigovini 2016 (read) already records disagreement between authors over the aff./cf. order, so the presupposition is known to be false in the strong form, which is why the variance decomposition is the point rather than a nuisance.
8. That a probabilistic reading is the right reading. CONTESTED and flagged as the dissolution risk: the mark may be a speech act about the state of the evidence rather than a bet. Either way the quantity is the same and the users are the same — V2 is phrased so that the answer bears on whether USERS' probabilistic reading is licensed, which does not require that determiners intended one.

What would count as an answer:
A pre-declared frame of qualified determinations drawn only from sources where qualifier survival has been verified end-to-end (publisher label -> delivered record, per pipeline version); a pre-declared resolution rule naming which independent determinations count and at which rank, with the revision-based and sequence-backed arms reported separately; then (a) the per-qualifier agreement rate with intervals, (b) a reliability curve against the qualifiers ordered by their documented strength as Sigovini 2016's Fig. 1 implies, (c) a variance decomposition of that rate across determiner, institution, higher taxon and decade against the pre-declared tolerance, and (d) a declared sensitivity analysis against revision-driven selection, using only resolutions generated by wholesale revision rather than by prior doubt. Qualifiers that order correctly but sit at institution-specific levels license a recalibration layer in aggregated data; no reproducible ordering establishes that a flag on a large number of records carries no transferable information. Both outcomes are decisions the aggregators and their users would act on.

Significance — consequence:
If the rates order the qualifiers as the published guidance implies AND the between-stratum spread falls inside the declared tolerance, the marks are a transferable signal and aggregated biodiversity data acquire a principled per-record weight: a qualified record stops being either discarded or silently promoted to a certain one, and the misclassification-aware models (P3's family) get the informative Omega priors they currently invent. If the rates order correctly but sit at institution- or clade-specific levels, the licensed artefact is a recalibration layer keyed to those strata — the aggregator publishes a calibrated probability rather than the mark. If the rates do not order at all, that is the finding with the largest operational consequence: a flag carried on a large and unknown share of records is established as carrying no transferable information, which retires an entire class of informal filtering rule and tells standards bodies that the field they are advising publishers to populate cannot be read quantitatively by consumers. All three outcomes are decisions, and the null is as actionable as the positive, which is the mark of a question worth asking rather than a hypothesis wearing a question mark.

Significance — audience:
(1) Aggregator data-quality teams (GBIF, OBIS, iDigBio, ALA, PBDB) — they already issue publisher guidance about where qualifiers go (Waller 2022, read) and already lift unmatched qualified names to higher ranks, with no evidence about what the marks mean; a measured rate changes the flag's documented semantics and the default filter. (2) TDWG / Darwin Core maintainers — dwc:identificationQualifier is defined by example, not by meaning; the newly standardised identification-history extension is the plumbing that would let consumers compute this, and the result bears directly on what the term's documentation should say. (3) Modellers who use occurrence data at scale — SDM and macroecology practitioners currently drop or keep qualified records by convention, a choice made in a methods sentence and never justified; and the misclassification/false-positive occupancy community needs exactly this quantity as a prior. (4) Collection managers and monographers — a by-determiner and by-institution decomposition is a feedback instrument on determination practice, and the between-institution limb is the inter-herbarium disagreement Goodwin et al. measured without the qualifier conditioning. (5) Regulatory and biosecurity identification programmes, where a hedged determination triggers or withholds action and the hedge's predictive value has never been reported the way a diagnostic test's would be.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: whether qualifier USE is itself an informative-missingness signal (does the mark predict which records later move, independent of whether it was right?); whether machine identification confidence (platform CV scores, barcode assignment posteriors) is better or worse calibrated than the human marks on the same specimens, which is a head-to-head nobody can currently run; whether a recalibration layer can be fitted and validated out of sample; whether determiner-level calibration can be estimated and fed back, i.e. whether taxonomic determination admits the calibration training that improved judgmental forecasting; whether the qualifier's information content has drifted across the barcoding transition (a decade limb with a mechanism behind it); and, in palaeontology, whether marks applied to fragmentary material behave differently from those applied to whole specimens.

Significance — anchoring:
Touches (a) the misidentification-rate literature's open question of what to DO about a >50% error prevalence (Goodwin et al. 2015, read, ends in a call for taxonomic effort, not in a weighting scheme); (b) the identifiability problem in misclassification-aware distribution models, which currently manufactures its classification probabilities internally (Adjei et al., read); (c) the GBIF backbone name-matching problem, where qualified strings are a documented failure mode (Waller 2022, read); (d) the long-standing listserv-level complaint, visible in Taxacom 1993 and Paleonet 2020 threads, that workers use "cf." and "aff." differently — an anomaly reported for three decades and never measured.

Reason for silence:
Three mechanisms, none of them "no one thought of it".
(1) Disciplinary boundary with a normative/evaluative split. Open nomenclature belongs to the normative side of systematics — how a determination should be LABELLED — a domain governed by codes, conventions and published guidance, where the correct output of a paper is a rule (which is exactly what Sigovini et al. 2016 produces). Calibration belongs to an evaluative tradition (forecast verification, psychometrics, diagnostic-test evaluation) whose unit is the realized outcome of a judgement. Nobody in systematics treats a determination as a forecast because the professional self-understanding is that a determination is a revisable statement about the current state of evidence and the authority of a reviser, not a bet that can be scored; and the evaluative fields have no reason to know the marks exist.
(2) Incentive gap on the consumer side. The aggregators and modellers who would act on the answer face a binary housekeeping choice (drop or keep) that is cheap to make by policy and expensive to audit; nobody is rewarded for auditing a filter that nobody contests, and a published rate would create an obligation to recalibrate.
(3) New instrument, recently. Computing this needs determination histories machine-readable at scale with determiner identity and dates preserved, plus a verifiable path showing the qualifier survived publication and name matching. That is precisely what the Darwin Core identification-history extension and current annotation infrastructure have only lately standardised (rs.gbif.org lists a dwc identification_history extension dated 2025-07-10; URL seen in search results, not opened), and what barcoding campaigns over whole drawers only lately made available as a second, selection-free resolution arm. Before that the question was askable but not answerable, which is a sufficient reason for a field to not have formed the habit of asking it.

Strongest remaining objection (carried forward to Gate 4):
Feasibility collapse of the informative limbs. Every presupposition that makes this more than a one-number curiosity is contested at once: qualifier survival (P-1), coverage of independent later determinations (P-2), determiner identifiers (P-6) and selection-free resolutions (P-4). It is entirely possible that after the frame step the only estimable quantity is a single pooled agreement rate from a handful of monographic revisions, with the determiner, institution and decade decomposition unestimable and the selection-free arm too small to carry the sensitivity analysis — in which case the answer is a thin number plus a coverage report, and the decision it was supposed to license cannot be made. The design's answer is that the coverage report is itself the deliverable the standards bodies need, but that is a weaker claim than the question advertises, and a Gate 4 adversary should test whether the frame step can be shown to clear a stated minimum before the rest is worth running.
