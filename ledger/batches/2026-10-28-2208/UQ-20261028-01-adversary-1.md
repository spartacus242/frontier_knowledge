## Restatement in my own words

Take the monic irreducible polynomials of one degree n over a prime field F_q (q = 2, 3 or 5), line them up by reading their coefficient string as a base-q integer with the constant term as the least significant digit, and look at each pair of neighbours in that line. Reduce both neighbours modulo a fixed monic m of degree 1 to 3. Do neighbours land in the SAME residue class less often than the uniform-independent expectation N_pairs/phi(m)^2, as Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan report consecutive primes do in Z? How does that deficit move as n grows at fixed q? And if the same irreducibles are re-lined-up with the digit significance reversed (leading coefficient least significant), does the deficit's sign or size change?

Grade: **TRUE by my own reasoning** — f mod m = (t^n + sum_{i>=d} a_i t^i) mod m + sum_{i<d} a_i t^i, and the second summand runs over all residues as the low digits run over F_q^d. ## Triviality derivation (my own arithmetic, labelled as such, no source)

Take a Cramér-type model with no local correlations: each monic of degree n coprime to m is irreducible independently with probability d ≈ (q/(q-1))·(1/n) for m = t (or the corresponding density for general m), and the forward order cycles through the phi(m) coprime classes on each block (C3). The next candidate in the SAME class as f is the phi(m)-th candidate position ahead, then the 2·phi(m)-th, etc. Then P(same class) = d·r^{phi(m)-1}·sum_{j>=0} r^{j·phi(m)}, r = 1 - d, = d·r^{phi(m)-1}/(1 - r^{phi(m)}) ≈ (1/phi(m))·(1 - (phi(m)-1)·d/2) to first order in d. Hence delta_Cramér ≈ -(phi(m)-1)·d/2 < 0 whenever phi(m) > 1. For m = t this is about -0.75/n at q = 3 and about -1.9/n at q = 5 (zero at q = 2 where phi(t) = 1 and delta is identically 0 because there is one class). This is a one-page argument and it predicts the SIGN and the leading 1/n ORDER of delta in the forward order (and, for m coprime to t, identically in the reversed order) with no correlation input at all. What it does NOT predict is any excess over this term — which in Z is where LO-S's log log x / log x secondary term lives — nor whether such an excess changes under re-ordering. I did not read LO-S past the abstract and attribute to it no statement about a naive model.
