## Findings
1. Identity. Johan Lundin (first name from the Lund catalogue record and the SBUF PDF summary; both name him). Title "Safety in Case of Fire - The Effect of Changing Regulations". Year 2005, defended 15 Dec 2005. Type: DOCTORAL thesis (not licentiate), 446 pages, English. Report 1032, Dept of Fire Safety Engineering and Systems Safety, Lund; ISRN LUTVDG/TVBB--1032--SE; ISSN 1102-8246; ISBN 91-628-6679-6. Supervisor Sven Erik Magnusson; opponent Ben Ale. SBUF 11011 final report: its running header carries the identical title and the text refers to itself as "this thesis", and its summary text matches the Lund one. But the SBUF title page, SBUF number and publisher were not seen, and the Lund record does not mention SBUF. So "same document" is STRONGLY INDICATED, NOT CONFIRMED from front matter.
3. Material. Lund abstract (read): "reviewed fire protection documentation from 46 projects, analysed the Swedish building regulations in detail, and carried out an extensive risk analysis of one building class" (paraphrased by summariser, not verbatim). Assembly halls are the class: SBUF PDF section 1.7 says evacuation safety in assembly halls is studied as examples. CONFIRMED (46 projects + one building class), with the class identified as assembly halls (strongly indicated).
4. Endpoint. NOT CONFIRMED. CFAST is certainly used: Appendix B (CFAST vs FDS comparison), Appendix D (CFAST input file for base case), Appendix E (response surface replacement of CFAST), and a cited paper on error and uncertainty in CFAST 2.0 temperature predictions. Section 4.3.3 "Measures of risk" exists but was not read, so I cannot say the endpoint is "occupant fatality risk" or give its unit (per person, per building, per year). The abstract speaks of "evacuation safety" and risk analysis; the word fatality was not seen in that context. Dispersion: see 7.
5. Central finding. FOUND, Summary of the thesis (as returned by the summariser): "One dilemma identified is that no unambiguous acceptance criteria have been stated in the building regulations." The string is under 125 characters so likely verbatim, but it came through a summariser and was seen once; treat as probable verbatim. It was not tied to a specific page. The Lund abstract in addition says little regulation or guidance exists on how to perform verification, leading to arbitrary design decisions.
6. Realized fire outcomes. Partly read. Not found in the risk calculation: risk figures come from calculations for a modelled base case (CFAST). Section 2.1.1 (context chapter) uses Swedish Rescue Services Agency statistics: "Each year, about 100 people die in Sweden as the result of fires in buildings" (national, per year, all building fires), average yearly fatalities by accident type 1991-2000 (Fig. 3), accidents with five or more fatalities in the 1970s and 1990s (Fig. 4). The 1998 Goteborg dance hall fire and 2001 WTC are mentioned as examples. So: realized statistics appear as background; NOT seen used as an endpoint or validation of design-method risk. Chapters after 2.1.1 unread, so absence is not established.
7. Variance by design method. Qualitative statement FOUND (Summary): prescriptive design "leads to great variation in risk level within a class of buildings"; and in some buildings "The actual risk ... is estimated to be so high that its acceptability can be questioned" (elided in the summariser's quote). Numerical figures: COULD NOT READ. They are presumably in section 5.4.1 "Quantitative analysis of the variation of risk within a class of buildings" (p.125) and Chapter 10 (pp.295-302), both unread. Note the qualitative statement concerns variation within a class under PRESCRIPTIVE design, not a comparison of variance across design methods; no such comparison was seen.
