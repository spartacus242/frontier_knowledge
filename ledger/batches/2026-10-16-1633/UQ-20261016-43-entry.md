Domain(s): STEM — electrocatalysis / energy materials (OER durability reporting). Method fields: biostatistics (censored survival analysis), reliability engineering (life data with suspensions, competing risks). Also metascience.

Question (canonical): Treating each published electrocatalyst durability run as a right-censored lifetime observation whose censoring time is the author's chosen test duration: what lifetime distribution does the published corpus actually support, and is the censoring informative — does the stopping time depend on the degradation the experimenter was watching? (Gate 1 reformulation, the graded version, adds a pre-specified frame (OER, pH class, current-density band, cell type), one pre-fixed failure definition (overpotential at fixed current exceeds its run-initial value by a pre-fixed Delta), and four limbs: (i) stopping-time distribution and mass on convention values (10/12/24/100/500/1000 h); (ii) Kaplan-Meier plus Weibull/lognormal fit with pre-registered sensitivity over the comparability restriction and a stated publication-selection model; (iii) does end-of-run degradation rate predict stopping time given design covariates, which under non-informative censoring it should not; (iv) test duration needed to separate two catalysts at 80% power, two-sided alpha 0.05. Both versions are in the record.)

Alternative formulations:
1. Are headline claims such as "stable for 100 h at 10 mA cm-2" statements about catalysts, or about the conventional durations at which this field stops testing?
2. Read as censored life data, how many reported "stable" results carry lifetime estimates distinguishable from one another, and what duration would separate two candidates at stated power?

Motivating gap:
Reported durations cluster on human-convenient values; the field's documented response has been metric invention and shorter surrogates (S-number, accelerated stress tests) rather than treating its own stopping times as a statistical object with a censoring distribution. The transplanted machinery (right censoring with suspensions, competing risks, tests for informative censoring) is mature, and the interface friction is real: here the censoring time is set by the person watching the outcome, the case independent-censoring assumptions exclude.

Presuppositions:
P1 durations and "degrading at stop" recoverable at scale from reports — CONTESTED (figure reading; extraction error must be measured on a hand-coded subsample). P2 stops are experimenter-chosen, not imposed — CONTESTED (equipment/shift/protocol-fixed stops are non-informative by construction; censoring-reason taxonomy required). P3 one comparable failure definition can be pre-specified — CONTESTED (frame may shrink severely; shrinkage is a reportable result). P4 publication selection acts on which tests are reported — CONTESTED, weakly identifiable. P5 "lifetime" is a meaningful latent quantity for a material-plus-conditions pair — CONTESTED (non-monotone/recoverable degradation forces a competing-risks fallback). P6 (Gate 1) the failure definition is reached often enough for a non-degenerate estimate — CONTESTED (near-degenerate KM is an admissible answer).

What would count as an answer:
A pre-registered extraction over a defined frame yielding: the stopping-time distribution with heaping quantified; a Kaplan-Meier plus parametric fit under the stated failure definition with sensitivity bands; a direct test of informative censoring conditioned on design covariates; and the power-based duration number. Plus a measured extraction-error rate and a censoring-reason taxonomy with its missingness rate. A null on (iii) with a degenerate (ii) is an admissible, pre-declared answer.

Significance — consequence:
If censoring is informative, every literature-derived durability ranking is biased with an estimable sign, benchmark tables need a censoring indicator, and literature-trained stability predictors are fitting censored times as if they were outcomes. If uninformative, the field gains a legitimate pooled lifetime estimate conditional on the publication filter and a design number ("N hours to separate A from B at 80% power") that currently no committee can produce. The pre-declared negative — the corpus supports no lifetime statement beyond the modal stopping time — would itself mean a decade of durability claims measures community convention.

Significance — audience:
(1) Electrocatalysis groups: a censoring-reason field, a "degrading at stop" flag, run-to-failure for headline claims. (2) Journals and referees setting stability-reporting requirements: an auditable checklist item in place of "test longer"; such bodies demonstrably convene on this axis (ISOS consensus statement in the sibling field; PEM testing-procedure reviews; active OER work on shortening stress tests). (3) Electrolyzer developers and funders setting 40,000-80,000 h targets — discounted: the field's own specialists find aqueous model-cell stability off by orders of magnitude from MEA operation, so extrapolation value is limited whatever the censoring structure. (4) Materials-informatics groups whose literature-mined stability labels exist only as free text ("excellent stability of 100 h", "more than 250 h").

Significance — fecundity:
A competing-risks formulation separating dissolution, reconstruction, substrate corrosion and bubble-induced loss; a censoring-reason taxonomy as a new covariate class; informative-censoring-corrected rankings; whether accelerated protocols shift the censoring distribution rather than the lifetime distribution; heaping structure by journal, year or lab as measurable sociology; direct transfer to perovskite T80, membrane fouling and thermal-catalysis time-on-stream.

The verdict stands at Provisional and the novelty finding strengthens materially. The access failure in the record was not a paywall: the full text is CC-BY and was found on the first search.
(i) ACCESS: the prior is open. Ligorati et al. 2026 is freely readable at https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/files/128353/128353.pdf (OPUS Augsburg docId 128353), CC-BY 4.0. doi.org, linkinghub and ScienceDirect were never needed. The DOI, journal, authors and year all check out.
(ii) THE PRIOR'S ENDPOINT IS MATERIALLY MISCHARACTERISED IN THE RECORD, AND THE CORRECTION CUTS BOTH WAYS. DegRate is not "a rate observed during the test, not a time to failure". The paper defines it by a failure threshold — "we define the DegRate as a capacity loss leading to 80 % of the initial capacity" — and states "Therefore, DegRate is an end-of-life benchmark, not a local slope at a specific cycle number". It is 20 percentage points divided by cycles to that threshold, i.e. a reciprocal transform of a failure time. So the prior is CLOSER to the candidate on endpoint than the record allowed, and further from it on method, which is the distinction that matters.
(iii) AND THE PRIOR CONTAINS THE CANDIDATE'S OWN ANOMALY, UNNAMED. By its own account, "Approximately 55% (344 out of 627) of DegRates in the regression dataset are estimated using partial cycling data because the 80% end-of-life threshold was not reached", computed by "dividing the observed capacity fade by the number of cycles up to the last reported cycle" under an acknowledged linear extrapolation. That is 54.9% of the estimation sample consisting of right-censored failure times, handled by extrapolation, under a name that never mentions censoring. The paper bounds it only with a 90%/80% ratio sensitivity check it calls "conservative" and "worst-case", never states a direction of bias, and never asks whether cells that failed to reach 80% differ systematically from those that did. One number is worth carrying: its upper range of 8 %/cycle implies an end-of-life of 2.5 cycles, which is not a physically meaningful cell lifetime and suggests the right tail is dominated by short-window extrapolations rather than observed threshold crossings. The paper does not comment on this; it is an inference from its own figures.
(iv) THE DECISIVE ABSENCE IS CONFIRMED AND UNDERSTATED. Every censoring and survival term is absent from the full text — censor in any form, right-censored, survival, time-to-failure, Weibull, Kaplan-Meier, hazard, informative censoring, competing risks — with no censoring indicator, no statement of how long any test ran, and no statement of why any test stopped. And the paper does not do the weaker thing either: test duration is not even a covariate, having been dropped for missing data ("We excluded moderators with substantial missing numerical data, such as experimental duration, battery mass, and electrolyte composition."). It raises publication bias and runs no test of it, by its own admission, and contemplates selection on reported results but never on stopping time.
(v) Three counting corrections. The 917 effect sizes across 146 studies are the full sample; the meta-regression that the record's comparator and estimand describe runs on 627 observations across 88 studies, a listwise-deletion loss of 290 and 58. The record's covariate "groups" are not the paper's: it names five categories in section 2.2 and four in its conclusion, chemistry is not a group but sits under "battery differences", and cut-off voltage and temperature are individual moderators. The paper also contradicts itself on moderators — "67 moderator variables" in five categories in section 2.2 against "46 moderators ... categorized into four groups" in the conclusion — so any citation of a moderator count must say which section it came from. And "pooled" is the checker's word, not the paper's; "conditional mean" is the paper's own and is confirmed.
(vi) THE CENTRAL NEGATIVE HOLDS, WITH A TELLING COMPARATOR. Fourteen targeted queries found nothing treating a corpus of published durability tests as right-censored lifetime data whose censoring time is the experimenter's chosen stop, nor any test of whether that censoring is informative. The three adjacent things separate cleanly: ordinary reliability life-data analysis of engineering test data, where censoring is designed and the analyst owns the stopping rule, is abundant and textbook; meta-analysis of published degradation rates exists, including this prior, an Augsburg photovoltaics sibling, NREL's PV compendium and a Nature Communications perovskite database analysis; and treating the literature's own stopping decisions as a possibly-informative censoring mechanism was NOT FOUND. The sharpest evidence for the gap is that perovskite paper: it analyses 7,419 literature devices with a true time-to-failure endpoint (TS80), uses no censoring methods at all, extrapolates by exponential decay instead, and never mentions that the experimenters chose the test durations.
(vii) Method limitation on the absence findings: the proxy refused both raw curl and urllib to the PDF host, so the keyword absences are the fetch tool's reader scanning the full extracted text, corroborated across two independent passes, rather than a grep the checker ran itself.
