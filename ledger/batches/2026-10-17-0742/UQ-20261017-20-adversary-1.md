## Restatement (my own words)

When a clinical laboratory refuses a blood or urine specimen because of a pre-analytical defect (haemolysis, clot, short draw, label problem), what happens to the patient behind it? Three parts: (i) how often is the test simply never redone; (ii) how much later does that patient get a usable result than a comparable patient whose specimen was accepted; (iii) does the rejection itself, isolated from how sick the patient was by using who drew the sample or by what route as an instrument, change anything clinically important (time to a critical result being recognised, escalation, ICU transfer, length of stay, extra needle sticks)? The candidate claims nobody has asked this. The Gate 2 record found limbs (i) and (ii) asked and answered in the field's own words (CAP Q-Probes "Clinical consequences of specimen rejection"; Jacobsz 2011 Cape Town) and limb (iii) asked but open. I treat that as a prior finding and re-run all eight attacks.
