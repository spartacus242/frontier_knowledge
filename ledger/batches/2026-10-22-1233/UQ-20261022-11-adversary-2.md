## Restatement in my own words

Take every publicly documented thing that carries a time-scale offset — interface specifications, broadcast message formats, data formats, and open-source implementations — put them in a declared list, and for each one write down (a) how it carries the offset (a number of stated width / a table or file / a bare flag with no number), (b) the biggest offset it can hold and still pass on correctly, and (c) what it does past that (saturate, wrap, reject, undefined). Then report what fraction of the list cannot cope once UTC is allowed to run up to an hour away from UT1, and name which ones give out first. Representability is scored over the whole list; propagation only over the members you can actually run.

## Attack 4 run as instructed — conditioning clauses as a numbered list, then tested

Clauses the question needs TRUE before it can be asked at all, read literally off the V2 canonical wording, as distinct from anything a study would measure:

**C1.** There is an adopted maximum |UT1−UTC| of 3 600 s, from CGPM Resolution 5 (2026), effective 20 May 2027, to serve as the primary threshold. -> **TRUE (sourced, S1)**, corroborated independently by my French (macg.co, Oct 2026) and Chinese (stdaily.com, 2026-10-15/16) returns. Caveat recorded: the BIPM page carries no explicit adoption label and I did not obtain its operative paragraphs verbatim, so "adopted" rests on the page's operative verb plus press corroboration, not on a quoted adoption clause.

**C2.** Every frame member — the frame being defined as artifacts that "carry UTC−TAI **or** UT1−UTC" — has a "largest |UT1−UTC| it can represent and correctly propagate", and that figure is the right thing to compare against 3 600 s. -> **FALSE (sourced, S2 + S1).** For the UTC−TAI class there is no such figure, in either direction. `current-utc-offset` is declared `int16` and described as "The offset between TAI and UTC"; it never carries UT1−UTC, and under Resolution 5 the quantity it does carry becomes "continuous without any future adjustment", i.e. it stops changing and never approaches 3 600 s. The record's own worked cell is the proof of the defect: it computes `int16` -> 32 767 s and concludes "3 600 s is representable with ~9.1 h of headroom", which scores a TAI−UTC field's range against a UT1−UTC threshold. The arithmetic is right; the quantity is wrong. And this is not a stray example — all four instances the record opened to test its document-kind premise (ietf-ptp, linuxptp `currentUtcOffset`, RTKLIB `utc_gps[4]`/`leaps`, NTP's leap indicator) are UTC−TAI carriers, so 4/4 of the cells that established the premise are cells to which the threshold does not apply.

**C3.** The frame is enumerable from public specifications and open-source code. -> **UNVERIFIED by me.** Nothing I opened bears on the completeness of such a frame. Kills nothing; the record's own demotion to a "public-artifact frame" is reasonable.

**C4.** The carrying mechanism (scalar of stated width / table or file / bare flag) is identifiable per member. -> **NOT A PRESUPPOSITION.** V2 converts this into a reported classifier and the record reports the failing instance (NTP, no scalar field) as a primary output. Attack 4 does not reach it, and I say so rather than grading around it. This is the one place the record did the right thing.

**C5.** For members that genuinely carry UT1−UTC, the largest representable value is not already printed in the governing specification. -> **FALSE for the radio time-signal class (sourced, S3).** TF.460-6 Annex 2 fixes the DUT1 code at "n ... from 1 to 8 inclusive" in steps of "(n × 0.1) s", and Annex 1 D.1.1 states "The magnitude of DUT1 should not exceed 0.8 s" against the D.1.2 tolerance "± 0.9 s". So for every emission conforming to TF.460 the representable maximum is 0.8 s, by declared design, and the "earliest-breaking artifact named" limb has a published answer before any census is run.

**C6.** A single "cumulative share of the frame non-conformant at 3 600 s" is an interpretable quantity over this frame. -> **FALSE, as a consequence of C2 (sourced).** The denominator mixes artifacts carrying a frozen integer (TAI−UTC) with artifacts carrying the growing quantity (UT1−UTC). PTP, NTP, linuxptp and RTKLIB would all score conformant — not because they are ready, but because the threshold is inapplicable to them — so the headline share is driven by the frame's composition between two incommensurable offset kinds rather than by the world.

**Grade: attack 4 Unresolved.** C2 is a clause the question cannot survive without, and it comes back FALSE on two sources I opened. The best response available is to re-read the question as "which artifacts lack a UT1 mechanism at all" — a coherent and interesting question, but a different one, not expressible in the record's scalar/table/flag taxonomy (which classifies the mechanism's shape and never which offset it carries), and reaching it needs a V3 when Gate 1's one permitted reformulation is already spent on V2.

## Recommended verdict

**Rejected** — Unresolved on attack 4 (and on 5 and 8): the frame pools UTC−TAI with UT1−UTC under a |UT1−UTC| threshold, so for the class holding all four of the record's own opened instances the measurand does not exist, and the headline non-conformant share is uninterpretable.

**Single strongest killing point, and the leg the verdict stands on if the others fall:** attack 4 / clause C2 alone. `ietf-ptp@2019-05-07.yang` declares `leaf current-utc-offset { ... type int16; }` and describes it as "The offset between TAI and UTC"; BIPM Resolution 5 (2026) makes UTC "continuous without any future adjustment" with "the maximum value for the difference |UT1-UTC| will be 3 600 seconds (1 hour)". A TAI−UTC field therefore has no largest representable |UT1−UTC| and can never be stressed by the 3 600 s threshold — yet the record's one worked cell scores exactly that field against exactly that threshold and reports "~9.1 h of headroom". If attacks 1, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 8 all fell, this leg would still stand on those two opened sources.
