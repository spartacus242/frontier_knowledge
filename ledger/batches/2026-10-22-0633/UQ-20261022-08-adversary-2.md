## Restatement in my own words
A hospital or health system turns off EHR vendor A and turns on vendor B on date T, and keeps the old system's data in some kind of archive. Two questions. (i) Counting question: take everything the OLD system held as structured data in the years just before T — each diagnosis on the problem list, each medication order, each allergy, each numeric lab result with its units, each immunisation, each procedure, each note — and ask what share of those individual facts you can still find, still query, and still read with the same value inside the NEW system's machine-readable record. Decide in advance how a fact in A's model maps to B's model, and when A holds something B's model has no slot for at all, count that as its own bucket instead of quietly discarding it. (ii) Bias question: compute three named longitudinal numbers (incident diabetes, current ACE-inhibitor use, Charlson prevalence) from the new system's ordinary research extract and see whether the series jumps or bends at T by more than a margin you fixed beforehand; then show the jump is a measurement artefact rather than real clinical change by recomputing the same number for the same patients out of the old archive, and by checking an equal-length window with no cutover in it (and a non-converting peer site if one is available).
So: how much of the computable past survives a vendor swap, per kind of fact, and does the part that does not survive bend the statistics people draw across the swap.

## Conditioning-clause test (attack 4), run against something I opened
The canonical wording conditions on "a queryable legacy archive", from which the pre-cutover denominator is supposed to come. Document-kind test, one instance opened (one is not three, and I say so): https://www.harmonyhit.com/lp-it claims the archive "Accurately stores structured and unstructured extracted data from any healthcare application, maintaining complete data relationships and context", delivered through "a secure, user-friendly browser-based platform" with SSO and EHR integration, under "a customized archiving strategy". On that page there is no mention of SQL, reports, analytics, or data release for analysis, and no statement that the source system's tables or fields are preserved in queryable form.
So the one instance I opened supports the clause's weak half (structured data is retained) and does NOT establish its strong half (that the retained structured data is queryable as a denominator), and it adds a circularity the record does not name: the archive is itself an *extract* built under a per-client scoping decision, so a fraction computed against the archive is a retention rate relative to the archive's scope, not to the source system's structured tables. That is a granularity/instrument gap in the denominator, not an absence of the phenomenon. I did not open a second or third instance and I did not test the alternative substrate (a dormant read-only instance of the source EHR, or a full database backup), which is the candidate's plausible escape; that escape is unevidenced here, which under the standard of proof makes this Partial rather than Unresolved.
