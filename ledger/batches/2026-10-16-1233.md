- **UQ-31's kill is confirmed on the narrow point and fails on the inference.** Grigg et al. 2025 does attribute the non-redetections to frequency non-overlap and EDA2 sensitivity and never proposes that the emission changed. But the printed model breakdown is 12 v1.0 plus 46 v1.5 — 58 of 68, leaving 10 with no stated class and so no stated mechanism; no upper limit in Jy is reported for any of the 55 still-in-orbit non-redetections, the only Jy figure being the ~1 Jy *expected* v1.5 level; and decisively, **both mechanisms assert that the two campaigns are not comparable, which makes the non-redetection uninformative about emission change rather than evidence that the emission was unchanged.** Bassa et al. 2024's "stability" is a population-distribution consistency remark about first-generation satellites as a group, above a bright floor (LBA below 10 Jy at 3σ against detections of 15 to 1300 Jy), with no spacecraft linked across epochs and satellite-to-satellite variation reported in the same breath.
- **UQ-32's Gate 2 ground is refuted on its decisive leg.** Verbeke 2022's numbers are exact — "T-stage assignment differed in 142 (45%) cases between the simple and comprehensive approach", 10 mm, 36% — but what it varies is the *examination protocol*, one plane against two planes with microscopic corroboration, on specimens already fixed, and it holds timing constant by design ("As specimen grossing is usually undertaken following fixation"). It is therefore on the far side of the distinction the record itself had added as presupposition 8, and it reports no near-threshold fraction at all. - **UQ-33's algebra survives and its reading of safeguards does not.** A concealed removal does satisfy every term of the Gate 1 identity, so the declaration-only balance has no minimum detectable rate. But a safeguards material-balance test is not the balance standing alone: Burr, Bonner, Michalak & Norman (JSM proceedings 2019, opened) state that the paired data "are declarations usually based on measurements by the operator", that falsification "would cause a mean shift between the operator declaration and the corresponding inspector measurement", and that the D statistic rests on "estimates of operator and inspector measurement error RSDs". ## The rejection rate and what it is allowed to mean

The batch as its agents returned it was four rejections and one Provisional, 80%, inside the band this ledger has run in for most of its life. After verification and the late Gate 4 work it is **nought**. That is the first 0% batch in twelve, and `LEDGER.md`'s instruction is to treat a low rate as a warning rather than as good news, so:

**It is not a statement that these five candidates are better than the last fifty.** Not one is Validated, which makes twelve consecutive batches with no Validated entry. Three of the five are held below Validated by well-posedness defects their own adversaries sourced — an underived consequence clause (31), an unspecified window quantile (32), an estimand with no stated replication design (34) — and the two at 3/3 are held there by close neighbours: a safeguards structure that redirects the question to a branch the candidate never names (33), and a sibling-mechanism paper that already published the governance half of the payload and moved nothing (35). On the ledger's own criteria these are five middling candidates, not five good ones.

**What the number is a statement about is the reviewing.** Four of five rejection grounds were wrong in the same way: a real paper, quoted accurately, aimed at a different object — the examination protocol instead of the specimen's physical state, a forward noise model instead of an independent re-reconstruction, a retained archive instead of a mandated deletion, two non-comparable campaigns instead of a stability measurement. That is precisely the failure mode written into `LEDGER.md` Gate 2 step 4 earlier today, and mirrored into the batch script's research prompt, and in force for this batch. It did not prevent the error, because in all four cases the reviewer *did* state what the prior was about. It stated it wrongly, and only opening the paper caught that.

So the operative lesson is narrower and more expensive than a prompt change: **the check that works is not asking an agent to characterise the prior, it is opening the prior in a fresh context that has not seen the characterisation.** Every reversal in the last four batches has come from that one move, and it costs a separate pass with its own budget.

Two honest limits on this batch's verdicts. First, **three of the five now rest on a single adversary** rather than two, because the late Gate 4 runs were one agent each; under the ledger's own rule that alone keeps them below Validated, and a second adversary could restore a fatal objection on any of them. Each entry names what would do it. Second, several load-bearing documents remain unread behind access controls rather than absent, and each entry says so: `arXiv:2608.11659`, `2512.07341` and `2412.14483` for UQ-31; PMID 29580861 and Kain et al. 2025 for UQ-32; Epstein/Gratch/McNulty/Grunwald 2011 for UQ-33; Schneider-Mizell et al. 2016 and Pedigo et al. for UQ-34. Any one of them could turn a Provisional into a Rejection, and the melanoma and eLife items are the two most likely to.
