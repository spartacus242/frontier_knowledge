## 1. Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Pick, in advance, a set of finished placebo-controlled drug trials that share one property: during the years they were enrolling, the drug being tested was also gettable by ordinary means outside the trial (prescription elsewhere, over the counter, compounded, telehealth). For those trials, go back to the control arm's frozen specimens and run one chemical test for the drug itself. Three numbers come out. (a) Of the control-arm visits where a specimen exists, at what share does the test say the drug is there — reported alongside how little the test can see, how often it cries wolf, and how far back in time a positive result reaches compared with the gap between visits. (b) When the test says yes, does that participant's own trial paperwork also say they were taking the drug. (c) Take the trial's published headline result, then recompute it under one differently-defined target quantity that accounts for control-arm drug use, fixed in writing before anyone sees an assay result, and report the difference with its sign. Plus a fourth, bookkeeping number: of the trials you declared in your set, what share of them did ethics or biobank governance refuse to release specimens for, so the denominator is auditable.

Plainly: the active arm's pill-taking is checked in blood; the control arm's pill-taking is checked on a form. Measure the control arm in blood too, and see whether the trial's answer moves.

## 3. The source my kill rests on

**S1. Parish S, Buck G, Aung T, Mafham M, Clark S, Hill M, Collins R, Bowman L, Armitage J, on behalf of the ASCEND Study Collaborative Group. "Effect of low-dose aspirin on urinary 11-dehydro-thromboxane B2 in the ASCEND (A Study of Cardiovascular Events iN Diabetes) randomized controlled trial." *Trials* 2023;24:article 166. DOI 10.1186/s13063-023-07198-z. CC BY 4.0. Peer-reviewed journal article, not a preprint.** Authors taken from the opened PDF's front matter and the ORA record, not from a short form. READ LEVEL: **FULL TEXT**.

**REGIME (mandatory field).** Population: 15,480 adults with diabetes and no occlusive arterial disease, randomised in the 2x2 factorial ASCEND trial to "aspirin 100 mg once daily versus placebo tablets" for 6-10 years (ISRCTN60635500, registered 1 Sept 2005). Unit of analysis: one participant's mailed spot urine at a single follow-up sampling, "a mean of 2 years after randomization", in a randomly selected sample of 152 participants "(76 aspirin arm, 74 placebo arm)" plus 198 purposively selected adherent participants "(93 aspirin arm, 105 placebo arm)" — **not** the participant-visit, and not all scheduled visits. Operating condition: spot urines "collected locally in general practice surgeries and mailed to the central laboratory", assayed from "previously frozen aliquots … in duplicate", with time since the last tablet recorded. Instrument: competitive ELISA for urinary 11-dehydro-thromboxane B2 — a **pharmacodynamic marker of platelet COX-1 inhibition, not a detection assay for the study class**. Threshold: "U-TXM <1500 pg/mg creatinine by this assay taken as indicating effective suppression", justified in the paper only by a reference to an FDA 510(k) decision summary for a commercial kit. Benefit category: adherence/suppression verification in a trial of a drug freely available over the counter throughout its conduct.

**THE NUMBER, and where it is printed.** Results text: "82% allocated aspirin versus 7% allocated placebo achieved effective suppression of U-TXM (Table 1)" and "Only 3% of participants adherent to placebo had effective suppression of U-TXM at follow-up." Table 1, placebo column, "effective suppression" row, as printed: random sample, all — "5/74 (7%)"; random sample, adherent — "1/63 (2%)"; adherent sample, adherent — "4/105 (4%)"; either sample, adherent — "5/168 (3%)"; random sample, **non-adherent — "4/9 (44%)"**. Units: share of placebo-allocated participants with urinary 11-dehydro-TXB2 below 1500 pg/mg creatinine. Denominators as above.

**What S1 does NOT publish, checked explicitly (published is not performed).** It does not print the concordance count: the Methods define non-adherence to include "or known to be taking non-study aspirin" and Figure 1's legend carries the category "Placebo aspirin, non-adherent (took non-study aspirin)", but **no count is printed for that category** — the paper DEFINES and CLASSIFIES the candidate's limb-2 quantity and never measures it. It does not re-estimate ASCEND's treatment effect for placebo-arm aspirin use, and contains no sentence on dilution or attenuation. It reports no LOD for aspirin exposure, no false-positive rate, and no detection window stated against a visit interval. There is no governance-release question because the sampling was inside the protocol.

## 6. Verdict

**Recommended verdict: Provisional** — Unresolved on attack 7 only (reason for silence); attacks 1, 2 and 4 are Partial, not Unresolved, so no fatal status is reached. Promotion to Validated requires (a) a reason-for-silence field rewritten around the mechanism the evidence supports — the control-arm figure is a by-product of adherence verification, reported and never counted or carried — with the disciplinary-boundary, incentive-gap and new-regime mechanisms withdrawn or restricted to the retrospective-release case; (b) the surviving novelty sentence reworded to "no source prints the control-arm fraction **by an assay that detects the study class**", with Parish et al. 2023 added as a fourth prior and the novelty grade re-argued at Medium; (c) the unopened nutrition-contamination cluster in §5 swept; and (d) two or three actual consent forms / biobank release policies opened, which neither the record nor I have done.

**Single strongest killing point.** Parish et al., *Trials* 2023;24:166 — read in full — is one opened instance in which an academic trial-methodology group, in a placebo-controlled trial of a class obtainable over the counter throughout its conduct, pre-specified a control-arm biochemical comparison, assayed frozen control-arm specimens, and printed placebo-arm positivity with denominators ("82% allocated aspirin versus 7% allocated placebo achieved effective suppression of U-TXM (Table 1)"; Table 1 placebo cells 5/74, 5/168, 4/9). That instance contradicts three of the record's four stated reasons for silence and dates the testable regime two decades earlier than the record does.

**Multi-leg discipline.** This kill has three legs: (a) a documented sweep gap in the pharmacodynamic-adherence-verification vocabulary; (b) limb 1 published with denominators; (c) the collapse of three reason-for-silence mechanisms. Legs (a) and (b) can be argued down — the instrument is a pharmacodynamic proxy, not a detection assay for the study class, so limb 1 as worded is still unpublished. **The leg my verdict stands on if the other two fall is (c)**, which needs only the existence and provenance of the paper, not the match of its instrument: a trial-methodology group did pre-specify and publish a control-arm biochemical-exposure comparison, with the specimens frozen and the drug over-the-counter, long before the regime the record says made the question askable.
