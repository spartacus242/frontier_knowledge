## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Pick a fixed list of genes and a fixed rule for when two long reads count as "the same isoform" (same chain of splice junctions, ends allowed to differ within a stated tolerance). Take two or more deep long-read libraries from the same tissue and pretend each one is a biologist's net sweep of a pond: the isoform classes you see are the species you caught, and the ones you caught exactly once are the suspicious ones. Now ask three things. (a) Using unseen-species machinery (coverage-based rarefaction/extrapolation, Chao/Good–Turing) but with an extra term that says "a known share of what you caught once was never alive" — a contamination rate measured per read class from synthetic spike-ins and technical replicates — how many *genuine* isoforms of each gene did you fail to catch, with error bars? (b) Does that machinery correctly predict how many brand-new isoform classes one further independent library will turn up, checked against a library held out for the purpose? (c) Does the per-gene accumulation curve actually flatten to a finite number that stays put as you sequence deeper, and does it do so for every contamination rate the spike-ins allow — or does no allowed contamination rate make it flatten, in which case "this gene has N isoforms" is a statement about how hard you sequenced, not about the gene, and annotation resources are publishing a measurement of their own budget.

In short: is "isoforms per gene" a population quantity you can estimate with a completeness statistic, or an instrument reading?

## New prior this pass found that Gate 2 did not have

**bioRxiv 2024.10.09.617406, "RNA sequencing depth guidelines for the study of alternative splicing."** Unit of analysis: alternative-splicing events / alternative junctions, and genes-with-AS. Endpoint: number of AS events and junctions detected as a function of library depth, plus an extrapolated count of events *missed* at typical consortium depths. Comparator: deeper in-house sequencing, against GTEx and TCGA depth distributions. Estimand: expected loss of detectable AS events at a given depth — explicitly a sample-level sensitivity quantity, obtained from linear/quadratic fits, with no unobserved class, no contamination term, no asymptote, and no predictive held-out validation.

Why this matters to the grades, in both directions. It does **not** duplicate the candidate: different unit (junctions/events, not per-gene isoform classes under a declared individuation rule), different estimand (sensitivity loss at depth, not population richness), crude method the authors flag themselves, and short-read data where the candidate's artefact mechanisms (intrapriming, RT template switching, degraded 5′ ends) barely apply. But it does erode two of the record's claims. First, the record's Gate 2 conclusion that "NO published per-gene (or global) unobserved-isoform extrapolation" exists is too strong: a published extrapolated count of *undetected splicing features* exists — "up to 7489 genes with AS/114717 alternative junctions" lost at GTEx mean depth (the authors' own figure, and note they write "up to", so this is a bound, not a point estimate). Second, it weakens the reason-for-silence story: the field does ask "how much are we missing at depth D?" and does publish numbers for it. What it does not do is ask it per gene, with an unobserved class, with a modelled artefact rate, or as a question about whether the quantity is an estimand at all.

## Single strongest killing point

The estimand's notion of "real" cannot do the work the question gives it. Monzó, Frankish & Conesa 2025 (Genome Research 35:583, opened) say their spike-in artefact figure "only represents a possible upper limit" *and* that "many validated novel transcripts were lowly expressed and found in single samples" — so in the single-observation class that limb (a) exists to partition, the artefact share is only bounded, validated real isoforms are concentrated in exactly the non-reproducible stratum that the record's operational definition of "real" (reproducible across independent libraries and donors) would discard, and that definition cannot be evaluated at all on the never-observed isoforms being counted. Unless "real" is redefined as the real component of a calibrated mixture — which the record does not do — limb (a)'s intervals are liable to span both branches of limb (c), and the candidate's headline disjunction resolves by construction rather than by evidence.
