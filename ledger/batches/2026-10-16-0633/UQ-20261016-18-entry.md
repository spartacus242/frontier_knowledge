Domain(s): Medicine — critical care, patient safety, clinical informatics. Transplanted from human-operator control theory / aerospace human factors (describing-function analysis, pilot-induced oscillation); neighbours: process control, human intermittent-control motor neuroscience.

Question (canonical): In adult ICU patients on continuous norepinephrine, with device-integrated pump-rate logs and timestamped pressure at >=4x the hypothesised cadence frequency: (a) does the pump-rate series carry excess band-limited power, and the dose-to-MAP cross-spectrum coherence with loop-consistent phase, in a narrow band centred on the unit's documented assessment/charting cadence (1 h => 2.8e-4 Hz; 2 h => 1.4e-4 Hz), against a pre-specified surrogate noise floor (phase-randomised, block-shuffled) and control band; (b) does that centre frequency move with cadence across units or exogenous protocol/staffing changes; (c) does cadence-band dose-oscillation amplitude predict organ dysfunction (delta SOFA components, new RRT) net of time-weighted mean dose, severity, phenotype and procedure-driven periodicity? [V2, the single permitted Gate 1 reformulation; V1 said "limit-cycle oscillation tracking charting cadence" — see record.]

Alternative formulations: (1) Is bedside vasopressor titration a stable controller, or is there a clinician-induced oscillation — the ICU analogue of pilot-induced oscillation — whose frequency is set by how often the nurse looks? (2) Does the dose-pressure loop show a spectral peak at the charting interval rather than a physiological frequency, and does that peak carry prognostic information? (3) Identify the describing function of the human titrator from clinical logs: gain, effective lag, remnant, phase margin at the sampling frequency.

Motivating gap: Cross-field transplant. Aerospace and process control routinely identify the human operator as a controller and name its failure modes (PIO, hunting, deadband cycling). Critical care is building closed-loop MAP/norepinephrine control (NCT03515161, NCT04106791, NCT04586218, COMAT) on the premise that manual titration performs worse, while comparing devices to manual care only on outcome summaries (time in target, hypotension burden, dose totals) and never identifying the incumbent manual loop's dynamics.

Presuppositions: P1 log resolution finer than the hypothesised period (established with device-integrated pumps + arterial line; made an inclusion criterion in V2). P2 CONTESTED and load-bearing: cadence is exogenous to patient state, so controller and plant are partially separable. P3 CONTESTED: oscillation, if present, is harmful (could be benign or adaptive probing for the weaning point). P4 largely established: physiological rhythms separate in frequency — true for Mayer waves, not for slow drift or sedation/turning/suctioning periodicity. P5 CONTESTED: charted pressure reflects what the clinician saw (charting-time bias corrupts phase). P6 CONTESTED: a prognostic association is not reverse causation from instability. P7 (Gate 1): pump logs record commanded, not delivered, dose; line dead-volume and carrier flow can generate cycling independently. P8 (surfaced by the adversary, never listed, FALSE as used): that a loop sampled every T can express an oscillation of period T.

What would count as an answer: Spectral and describing-function analysis of paired dose and pressure series from >=2 institutions with different documented cadences; pre-registered band, surrogate floor, control band, covariates and endpoint. Positive = cadence-band excess power above floor and control band, coherence with loop-consistent phase, centre frequency that moves when cadence moves exogenously, share of patient-hours in the oscillatory regime, estimated gain and lag, plus an outcome model identified off the cadence contrast. A clean negative settles it equally and retires one implicit justification for closed-loop devices.

**Judge's post-Gate-4 corrections to attacks 4 and 5 (2026-10-16), each verified at source:**

- **A1's fatal argument is refuted, not merely unproven.** A1 rejected the candidate on the claim that a controller sampling every T cannot host an oscillation of period T, so the pre-registered band at 1/T measures only the actuation step-train comb. All three legs fail. (i) The intermittent-control literature the candidate transplants from derives a limit cycle of period *exactly* one intermittent interval — Gawthrop, arXiv:1705.08228 §3.3, λ_crit = +1: "there is a limit-cycle with period Δ_crit"; 2Δ is the λ = −1 case, not a floor. No source states a 2T minimum. (ii) The Nyquist-style 2T bound is a property of a once-per-T observation *record*, not of the loop: a period-T physical oscillation is permitted and is merely aliased to DC when sampled once per interval. (iii) The step-train-comb leg is inverted for the stationary case: the zero-order-hold envelope |H(f)| = |sin(πfT)/(πf)| has its exact *nulls* at f = k/T, so a staircase of hold steps carries a spectral **minimum** at period T, not a peak (verified numerically: comb/background 0.099 at k = 1 for stationary held levels). Sampling in the human intermittent-control models is also aperiodic (Loram et al., IEEE TBME 2022), so there is no single T to collide with.
- **But one half of that leg survives in a form that matters, and is logged as a confound rather than a kill.** If the held level drifts monotonically — which is exactly what steady weaning is — the hold error is a sawtooth of period T whose Fourier series puts lines at exactly k/T with 1/k amplitudes; a simple zero of the sinc cannot cancel a ramp's dipole image (verified: comb/background 6.8e9 at k = 1 for a drifting staircase, against 0.099 stationary). So f = 1/T cannot be used as a built-in negative control: a successor analysis must detrend or high-pass the commanded-dose series before leaning on the null, and must treat k/T as potentially contaminated. A1's intuition was right for weaning trajectories and wrong in general, and its stated mechanism was wrong either way.
- **Attack 5's surrogate objection is locally valid and does not generalise, so attack 4 and attack 5 both stand at Partial, not Unresolved.** A Fourier-phase-randomised surrogate reproduces the periodogram by construction (Theiler et al. 1992 §§2.2.3 and 2.4.1, read in full; verified to 4.5e−13 relative error), so the band-power limb has literally zero power against *that* null — and that floor was the headline methodological addition V2 was credited with, which is a genuine defect. It reaches no further. The **block-shuffled** surrogate V2 also names preserves only the marginal distribution and the total power, and the spectral shape only as a ~1/L-smoothed, O(1/L)-biased version (Künsch 1989; Lahiri 1999; Politis & Romano 1994; Zhang et al. 2006/2007 on bucket permutations); measured, a narrowband line 25× above 1/L retained only 0.074 of its bin power, so band-limited power *does* have power against the block floor. And **magnitude-squared coherence** — the other half of V2's primary test — is normalised by both auto-spectra, so its no-coupling density depends only on the degrees of freedom, p(C²) ∝ (1−C²)^(K−2) (Hannan 1970; Thomson & Chave 1991; Carter 1987); independently randomising each series' phases randomises precisely the phase differences that *are* the cross-spectrum (Prichard & Theiler 1994, Eq. 4), driving coherence to that null while leaving both amplitude spectra exact. Faes et al., IEEE TBME 2004;51(7):1156–1166 is the dedicated primary source and *recommends* spectrum-preserving surrogates as the coherence floor. Measured: data MSC 0.985 versus independent-FT-surrogate mean 0.040 (≈ 1/K) and 95th percentile 0.727. Naming both floors and both statistics is therefore a well-founded design that mis-assigns one pairing, not a self-defeating one.
- **A2, which had none of the above and reached its own view, independently found the FT-surrogate defect at source and also graded it Partial**, on the same reasoning: phase randomisation is the correct null for the coherence-and-phase criterion, while the block surrogate and the control band carry the band-power limb. It also answered A1's and its own staircase objection the way the methods check does — a stepped monotone ramp and a cycling loop differ in the dose–MAP cross-spectrum, not in dose-series band power.
- **A2's new objection is now the strongest one on the record** and is stated below; it is not a signal-processing error but a confounding structure, and it is why this entry is Provisional rather than higher.

Strongest remaining objection: **The exposure and its one frequency-matched confound are collinear by construction, and limb (b) does not break them apart.** ICU nursing care is bundled onto the same hourly or two-hourly rhythm as charting, so procedure-driven dose change and cadence-driven controller cycling occupy the same band; van der Eijk et al. (Acta Paediatrica 2012;101:E97–E104) evidences that procedures dominate large manual titration steps in the closest analogous loop (126 of 136 adjustments of 15% or more were procedure-associated). Entering procedure periodicity as a covariate cannot fix a covariate sitting at the exposure's own frequency, and limb (b)'s cadence contrast does not rescue it, because a unit that changes its documented assessment interval moves its care-round rhythm with it — shifting the hypothesised cause and the confound together. The one real separation left is the *sign* of the dose–MAP phase relation (disturbance rejection has pressure leading dose; controller cycling has dose leading pressure), and that rests on a phase estimate which P5 and P7 independently threaten, charting-time bias on one side and line dead-volume and carrier-flow lag on the other. Secondary, and the reason well-posedness stays at 2/3: band width, block length, segment length, detrending and the numeric meaning of "loop-consistent phase" are named as categories but not as numbers, while each of them determines whether a 1/T peak is resolvable or removed.
