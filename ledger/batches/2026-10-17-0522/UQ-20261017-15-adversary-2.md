## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Every method we use to find out which proteins stick to which other proteins only works over some
range of "how tightly they stick" (Kd) and "how much of them is in the cell" (copies per cell, or
concentration). The candidate asks us to take those ranges off the published protocols rather than
off the interactome field's own hit-rate calibrations, draw each method's reachable patch on a
(Kd × concentration) map, overlay all five method families, and look at what is left over — the
patch no method in use can reach. Then it asks how big that leftover patch is as a share of
interactions, under two stated denominators, and asks what a headline number like "≈8% of the human
interactome is mapped" turns into once you say explicitly that the denominator is only the reachable
patch. In one line: *is the field's completeness figure a statement about the interactome, or about
the part of it the instruments can physically reach, and how big is the difference?* The claimed
novelty is not the idea that methods have limits (Hein 2015 says so for one method) but computing
the limit in the cell's own units, taking the union across method families, and re-expressing a
published coverage number against it.
