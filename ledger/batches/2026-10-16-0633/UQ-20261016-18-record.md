Question (canonical) — V1 (intake, as received):
In ICU vasopressor titration reconstructed from infusion-pump and charting logs, does the clinician-plus-patient loop exhibit measurable limit-cycle oscillation whose period tracks the institution's documented assessment/charting cadence rather than any physiological rhythm, and does oscillation amplitude predict organ-dysfunction outcomes independently of mean dose and illness severity?

Question (canonical) — V2 (Gate 1 reformulation; the single permitted reformulation, for well-posedness only; THIS is the version carried forward):
In adult ICU patients on continuous norepinephrine, restricted to encounters with device-integrated infusion-pump rate logs and timestamped blood pressure at a sampling resolution at least 4x the hypothesised cadence frequency: (a) does the pump-rate series carry excess band-limited power — and the dose-to-MAP cross-spectrum carry coherence with a phase lag consistent with a closed negative-feedback loop — in a narrow band centred on the unit's documented assessment/charting cadence (1 h => 2.8e-4 Hz; 2 h => 1.4e-4 Hz), measured against a pre-specified surrogate noise floor (phase-randomised and block-shuffled matched segments) and against a pre-specified non-cadence control band; (b) does the centre frequency of that excess power move with cadence across units or across documented protocol/staffing changes whose timing is exogenous to patient state; and (c) within that cadence contrast, does cadence-band dose-oscillation amplitude predict a pre-specified organ-dysfunction endpoint (delta SOFA renal/hepatic/cardiovascular components, new renal replacement therapy) after conditioning on time-weighted mean dose, severity, sepsis phenotype, and sedation/procedure-driven periodicity?
(Reformulation added: the explicit noise floor and control band, the coherence/phase requirement that distinguishes a closed loop from coincident band power, the resolution inclusion criterion that forecloses the P1 aliasing failure, and the cadence-contrast as the identifying variation for limb (c). Nothing was added to the substance of the question; V1's "limit-cycle oscillation" was weakened to the measurable claim of cadence-band excess power with loop-consistent phase, because "limit cycle" strictly implies a nonlinear autonomous attractor that cannot be established from observational series alone.)

Alternative formulations:
1. Is bedside vasopressor titration a stable controller, or is there a clinician-induced oscillation — the critical-care analogue of pilot-induced oscillation — whose frequency is set by how often the nurse looks?
2. Does the dose-pressure loop at the bedside show a spectral peak at the charting interval rather than at a physiological frequency, and does that peak carry prognostic information?
3. (Engineering register) Identify the describing function of the human titrator from clinical logs: what are the gain, effective lag and remnant of the nurse-plus-protocol controller acting on a septic circulation, and is the loop's phase margin small enough at the sampling frequency to sustain cycling?

Motivating gap:
Cross-field transplant. Aerospace and process-control human factors routinely characterise the human operator as a controller (gain, lag, describing function, remnant) and have named failure modes where operator lag meets plant dynamics: pilot-induced oscillation; "hunting" and deadband cycling in process control. Critical care is now building the automation that would replace the manual vasopressor titrator (closed-loop MAP control: NCT03515161, NCT04106791, NCT04586218, the COMAT RCT) on the premise that manual titration performs worse, without having identified the manual loop's dynamics at all. Gate 2 confirmed the asymmetry: the closed-loop literature compares devices to manual care on outcome-level summaries (time in MAP target, hypotension burden, dose totals), never on loop identification of the incumbent controller. The transplant has genuine friction rather than being a pastiche: the plant (a septic circulation) is non-stationary and time-varying; the controller is a protocol-constrained human with a documented, institution-set sampling interval; and that interval is the identification strategy as well as the discriminator, since a cadence-locked peak near 2.8e-4 Hz cannot be confused with Mayer waves near 0.1 Hz or with circadian variation near 1.2e-5 Hz.

Presuppositions:
- P1 (resolution): pump and pressure logs are retrievable finer than the hypothesised period. ESTABLISHED where pumps are device-integrated and an arterial line is in place; FALSE where only hourly charted doses exist — that case aliases the signal of interest exactly, and V2 makes adequate resolution an inclusion criterion rather than an assumption.
- P2 (CONTESTED — the identification assumption): controller and plant are partially separable because assessment cadence is exogenous and documented. Defensible only on a contrast across units or protocol/staffing changes whose timing is unrelated to patient state. This is the load-bearing contested presupposition; if cadence tracks acuity, limb (c) is unidentified and limb (b) is confounded.
- P3 (CONTESTED, to be tested not assumed): oscillation, if present, is harmful. Dose cycling could be benign, or adaptive (probing for the weaning point is deliberate practice, and "wean when able" protocols instruct it).
- P4 (largely established): physiological rhythms separate in frequency from charting cadence — true by 2-3 orders of magnitude for Mayer waves, but NOT for slow drift and NOT for sedation-bolus, turning, suctioning or procedure-driven periodicity, which must enter as covariates or be excluded by segment censoring.
- P5 (CONTESTED): charted blood pressure reflects what the clinician actually saw and acted on, not a retrospectively entered value. Charting-time bias corrupts the phase estimate (and the known prevalence of hourly-vitals errors, omissions and outliers in ICU records bears directly on this); V2 mitigates by requiring device-sourced pressure for the phase limb and treating charted values only as the cadence marker.
- P6 (CONTESTED): a prognostic association, if found, is not reverse causation from patient instability. Severity- and phenotype-matched comparison plus the exogenous cadence contrast are required; this is the strongest standing objection to limb (c).
- P7 (newly surfaced at Gate 1): the pump rate log records commanded dose, not delivered drug; line dead-volume and carrier-flow changes add their own lag and can themselves generate cycling independent of the human operator. Must be handled as a competing explanation, not ignored.

What would count as an answer:
Spectral and describing-function analysis of paired dose and pressure series from at least two institutions with different documented assessment cadences. A positive answer is: excess dose-series power in the cadence band above the pre-specified surrogate noise floor and above the control band; dose-MAP coherence in that band with loop-consistent phase; a centre frequency that moves when cadence moves for exogenous reasons; a reported share of patient-hours spent in the oscillatory regime; estimated controller gain and effective lag; and an outcome model with cadence-band oscillation amplitude as the exposure, identified off the cadence contrast rather than off between-patient comparison. A clean negative — no cadence-locked excess distinguishable from the noise floor at either institution — settles the question as decisively as a positive, and would retire one implicit justification for closed-loop devices. Pre-registration of the band, floor, control band, covariate set and endpoint is required for either direction to be credible.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
vs 1 (Sarraf 2024): the drug-titration paradox is a population-level, cross-sectional dose-vs-effect correlation artefact; the candidate is a within-patient, time-domain, frequency-resolved property of the operator loop, with a named predicted frequency set by an institutional parameter, a surrogate noise floor, and a phase requirement. Sarraf asserts clinicians work to fixed goal ranges; the candidate asks what that behaviour does to the loop's dynamics and whether the consequence is measurable and prognostic. Domain differs (anaesthesia vs ICU vasopressors) and the method differs (narrative control-engineering commentary vs identification from pump and pressure logs).
vs 2 (St-Arnaud 2013): that study measures the SETPOINT half of the loop (what target was prescribed, and that targets change) and explicitly leaves the controlled variable, the actuation and the dynamics unmeasured — the paper itself names "did achieved pressure match the target" as future work. The candidate measures the actuation and pressure series and their cross-spectrum, and uses cadence rather than target as the exogenous variation. It is the complement of this paper, not a variant of it.
vs 3 (PMID 30513357, contents unverified): on its title alone it is an observational description of nurse practice in managing noradrenaline — the kind of study that would report how many dose changes occur and under what protocol authority. The candidate differs in asking whether those changes are PERIODIC at the institution's assessment cadence, whether the period MOVES when cadence moves exogenously, and whether amplitude carries prognostic information net of mean dose and severity. Descriptive counts of titration events, even if present there, do not answer any of the three limbs. This differentiation is provisional until the paper is read, and reading it is the first thing Gate 4 should do.
vs the fourth (intermittent control): that literature establishes the mechanism in continuous psychomotor tasks at 1-10 Hz with the operator as the sole intelligence in the loop. The candidate transplants it to a loop three to four orders of magnitude slower, where the sampling interval is not a neural refractory property but an institutional policy that is written down, audited and occasionally changed — which is precisely what makes the question identifiable in observational data and makes it a question about health-system design rather than about motor control.

Significance — consequence:
If YES (cadence-locked peak present, moves with cadence, amplitude prognostic): the dominant premise of the closed-loop vasopressor programme stops being an assumption and becomes a measured quantity with a mechanism — and, more consequentially, assessment cadence becomes a modifiable determinant of haemodynamic stability. That is a candidate intervention that requires no device, no capital purchase and no regulatory pathway: change the sampling interval, the titration step size, or add a damping rule to the protocol, and test it. It also reframes a class of observed dose variability as controller-generated rather than patient-generated, which changes how the prognostic variability literature should be interpreted.
If NO (clean negative at both institutions): one implicit justification for automation is retired, and the case for closed-loop devices must rest on workload, protocol adherence and night-shift reliability rather than on superior loop dynamics — a sharper and more honest basis for device trials and for regulators assessing their claims. A negative also tells the haemodynamic-variability field that within-patient dose variability is plant-driven, strengthening its causal interpretation.
If PARTIAL (peak present but benign, P3 fails): the most instructive outcome — it would establish that deliberate dose probing is a feature of competent manual control, which is a direct warning to closed-loop designers whose cost functions penalise exactly that behaviour.

Significance — audience:
(i) Closed-loop/automated-haemodynamic-management developers and trialists (the groups behind NCT03515161, NCT04106791, NCT04586218, COMAT) — would gain, and would be held to, a mechanistic comparator for the manual arm and a defensible cost function; (ii) ICU nursing leadership and quality committees, who set assessment and charting cadence and nurse-patient ratios, and who currently set them on workload and documentation grounds with no evidence that the number is haemodynamically consequential; (iii) critical-care human-factors and patient-safety researchers, who imported checklists, CRM and alarm fatigue from aviation but not the operator-as-controller method, and for whom this is a template; (iv) ICU data-science and EHR data-quality groups, for whom cadence periodicity is currently a nuisance to be filtered and would become a measurable clinical signal; (v) regulators and payers assessing automated titration claims.
Honest discount: audience (i) is small and would likely proceed with device development regardless; audience (ii) is large but acts on staffing economics, so a measured effect would have to be sizeable to move cadence policy.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: is cadence-locked cycling present in the other manual ICU loops (insulin/glucose, sedation depth, FiO2/PEEP, CRRT fluid balance, inotropes), and does its amplitude scale with the same cadence parameter? Does identified controller gain vary with nurse-patient ratio, shift, seniority, or protocol authority (nurse-titrated vs physician-ordered)? Can a per-unit "loop quality" metric be computed from routinely archived logs and used as a quality indicator that no current measure captures? Does controller-generated oscillation mediate part of the long-standing, mechanism-free association between nurse staffing levels and patient outcomes? Is a stepped-wedge randomisation of assessment cadence ethically and practically admissible, and what does the existence of a measurable loop imply for what a closed-loop device should be trained to imitate versus to avoid? Does the same identification strategy work in ward-level loops (oxygen titration, insulin on general wards) where cadence is far coarser?

Significance — anchoring:
Touches: the drug titration paradox (Sarraf 2024) as an unexplained anomaly in dose-effect data; MAP-target trials (SEPSISPAM, the 65 trial) whose estimands are defined on mean pressure while the dynamics around the target are unmeasured and plausibly outcome-relevant; the closed-loop-device justification gap documented at query 5; the nurse-staffing-to-outcomes literature's missing mechanism; the ICU vital-sign data-quality literature (hourly-vitals errors, omissions and outliers; charting-time bias) which this question converts from a limitation section into a measurement target; and the haemodynamic/dose-variability prognostic literature (dose-trajectory vs AKI), to which it proposes a frequency-resolved and causally interpretable exposure.

Reason for silence:
Three mechanisms, none of them "nobody thought of it".
(i) DISCIPLINARY BOUNDARY WITH ASYMMETRIC IMPORT: critical care imported aviation's social and procedural technologies (checklists, CRM, briefings, alarm-fatigue work) because they transfer without mathematics; it did not import the operator-as-transfer-function method, which requires time-series identification and lives in control-engineering journals that intensivists do not read and that reward in-silico controller design over measurement of incumbent human performance. Queries 4, 8 and 9 show the method sitting fully developed one field away with no clinical citation.
(ii) INSTRUMENT TIMING: the question needs minute-resolution commanded-dose logs linked to continuous pressure, which requires smart-pump/EHR interoperability plus waveform archiving. That combination became routinely retrievable only in the last several years, and the available public substrates mostly store hourly charted doses — which aliases the signal exactly, so the data most researchers hold cannot see the effect and quietly teaches them there is nothing to see.
(iii) ANALYTIC HABIT AS A FILTER (the sharpest mechanism): the hypothesised peak sits near 1e-4 Hz, inside the band that physiology-trained analysts routinely detrend, high-pass or discard as drift, artefact or documentation noise before analysis. The very feature at issue is removed as a preprocessing step. Add the incentive structure: the field's funded question is "can a device do it better", and demonstrating that the incumbent human controller has characterisable dynamics is neither a device endpoint nor an outcome endpoint, so no programme pays for it.
