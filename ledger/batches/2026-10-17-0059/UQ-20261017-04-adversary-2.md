## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take the deposited coordinates of an experimental inorganic crystal structure exactly as the database holds them — no energy minimisation — and compute, with a universal machine-learned potential, an effective-harmonic phonon spectrum at the temperature the depositor says the measurement was made at. If any mode comes out imaginary (beyond a declared artefact screen), call the entry "flagged". Now ask three numbers: (i) how often does that flag fire on entries that crystallographers themselves later declared wrong — corrected, retracted, redetermined in a lower space group, or shown to be a space/time average of a real superstructure — versus on matched entries that someone independently re-measured and confirmed at the same symmetry; (ii) how often does the flag fire for reasons internal to the calculation rather than the structure (checked by DFT on a subsample); (iii) does the flag tell a curator anything that the automated validation report (checkCIF-style alerts) does not already tell them. In one line: *is "this deposited model is dynamically unstable" a usable diagnostic of "this deposited model is wrong", and does it beat the checks already in production?*

Two things the framing is doing that I had to notice: the question's whole claim to distinctness from its nearest ancestor rests on the word **unrelaxed**, and its claim to significance rests on an unread list of what existing validation actually checks (the record's own P6).
