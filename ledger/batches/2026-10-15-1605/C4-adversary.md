# C4 adversary log

## Restatement
Using citation-addition events in English Wikipedia, does the hazard that a cited URL stops resolving vary by article topic once addition year, domain type and domain popularity are held fixed (with editor replacement treated as censoring)? Which topics are worst? A second outcome is "dead and no usable archive snapshot".

## Extra queries run
1. "Wikimedia Research Fund 'Improving the Persistence of External References on Wikipedia'". The meta page URL was returned, but WebFetch failed twice (url_not_found, including the action=raw form). Its contents are unverified. Search snippets gave no abstract.
2. "InternetArchiveBot dead links by Wikipedia category WikiProject statistics analysis". Returned IMC'22, Community Tech pages and the IA 2026 blog. The IA blog (fetched) has no topic or domain-type breakdown. It reports only Pew sample rates, link-age rates and the 30M+ links repaired.
3. "survival analysis hazard of URL death Wikipedia references subject area news sources domain covariates". Only Hennessey & Ge (discipline tables) and generic survival-analysis pages.
4. "Wikipedia citation sustainability 'link rot' topics biography sports science history ... 2025" (extended). Only Wikipedia's Link rot page, IMC'22 and a Harvard Perma post. No topic study.
5. Arquivo.pt Wikipedia references preservation by topic. Returned the Arquivo.pt preservation project posts. These are archiving operations with no topic analysis in the snippets.
6. "'link rot' Wikipedia 'by topic' OR 'by category' ..." Returned generic explainers, IMC'22, and IA posts.
Disguise vocabularies tried:
- Information retrieval and digital-library vocabulary: "URL decay", "citation half-life", "Kaplan-Meier lifetime of URLs", "persistence of references".
- Epidemiology and survival vocabulary: "hazard of URL death", "survival analysis ... covariates". This was also cross-read against the record's scientometrics queries on "obsolescence" and "decay by discipline".
Cited priors spot-check: IMC'22 (real, confirmed via ACM link and USC PDF), the BMC Bioinformatics Hennessey & Ge tables (real, returned in results), the IA 2026 blog (real, fetched). The CJR NYT study and Pew 2024 are well known and consistent with what I remember. The record's quoted figures are plausible. I did not independently re-verify the 18%, 11% and 54% numbers or the NYT section percentages.

| 4 | Presupposition failure | "Hazard of becoming unreachable" is contaminated by soft-404s, bot blocking and paywalls (IMC'22 mis-tagging). Collinearity is an empirical question and either result is informative. Soft-404 noise is a measurement problem, not a falsification of the question. | Resolved |
| 5 | Vagueness | "Practically large" (HR >= 1.3) is arbitrary, and with millions of citations any topic coefficient is significant. So "yes" is nearly guaranteed. | The threshold is stated and the question is effect-size based. A "no" (topic effects vanish) remains a possible outcome. Partial because the threshold is arbitrary. | Partial |
| 6 | Significance inflation | The named audience (IABot and WMF engineers) already archives every link on edit and rescues dead ones regardless of topic. A finding of "topic matters via source type" changes little. | Fecundity extends beyond tooling: coverage bias in what remains verifiable, and provenance for LLM training. Still, the decision impact is modest and the audience is only plausibly real. | Partial |
| 7 | Reason for silence | "Remediation crowded out diagnosis" is plausible but unevidenced. ## Recommended verdict
Provisional: no Unresolved on attacks 1, 2 or 4, but attack 2 (unread Wikimedia Research Fund page, possible internal analyses), plus partials on 3, 5, 6 and 7, cap it. It is a low-surprise empirical transplant of the CJR design; if the grant page is later read and shows a topic breakdown, downgrade to Rejected.
