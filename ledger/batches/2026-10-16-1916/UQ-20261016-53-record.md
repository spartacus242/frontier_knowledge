Question (canonical — Gate 1 REFORMULATION, the version that stands):
Over a pre-registered frame of published pretraining-recipe and architecture ablation studies with sufficient configuration disclosure, reconstruct each headline comparison as a design matrix over a fixed factor list (learning rate and schedule, batch size, token budget, sequence length, warmup, data mixture, tokenizer, numeric precision, parallelism/sharding, parameter count, depth/width) under an explicitly stated model including two-factor interactions: (i) in what share of headline claims is the claimed factor's main-effect column not estimable — exactly or near-collinear (above a pre-registered variance-inflation / alias-coefficient threshold) with another factor's column or with an interaction column — rather than separated by the reported design, with inter-coder agreement reported and the undecidable (insufficient-disclosure) share reported separately; and (ii) for a sample of such claims whose artifacts permit it, after adding the missing design points to break the alias, how large is the de-aliased effect relative to the originally reported effect, judged against a pre-registered equivalence band estimated from a same-configuration seed-variance study rather than against intervals the original papers mostly do not report?

Question (ORIGINAL intake version, recorded as required):
Treating each published pretraining-recipe or architecture ablation as a designed experiment, what is the distribution of design resolution across a defined frame of such studies — in what fraction is the headline factor's main effect aliased with another factor that moved with it (learning rate, batch size, token budget, sequence length, warmup, data mixture, tokenizer, numeric precision, parallelism) rather than separated by the design — and, for the subset whose artifacts permit it, how large is the aliased contribution relative to the reported effect once the missing design points are run?

Why reformulated (well-posedness only, one reformulation used):
Two defects in the intake wording, both fixed in one pass. (a) "Design resolution" is defined for REGULAR 2^(k-p) designs; ML ablations are irregular, usually rank-deficient one-factor-at-a-time sets, for which resolution is undefined but the alias matrix A = (X1'X1)^-1 X1'X2 and column estimability/collinearity under a stated model are well defined. The reformulation asks for estimability and alias coefficients, not a resolution number, which is what the candidate's own Presupposition 2 flagged as the technical core. (b) The intake's NO criterion ("de-aliased estimates stay inside the originally reported intervals") presupposes reported intervals; pretraining ablations are predominantly single-seed with no error bars, so the criterion could never be evaluated. Replaced with a pre-registered equivalence band from a seed-variance study. No change to the question's subject matter or its YES/NO direction.

Alternative formulations:
1. Are the field's pretraining "best practices" identified in the experimental-design sense, or are their main effects confounded by construction with whatever was moved to hold a budget fixed?
2. What is the alias structure of the deep-learning ablation, and what do the recipe literature's conclusions look like after de-aliasing?
3. (lay) When a pretraining paper says "this change helped," how often did something else have to change with it, and does the claim survive changing them one at a time?

Motivating gap:
A reported anomaly with an un-asked diagnosis. Architecture and recipe modifications reported as improvements routinely fail to transfer across implementations and tasks (Narang et al. 2021, "Do Transformer Modifications Transfer Across Implementations and Applications?"; Melis et al. 2018 on LM architectures under equal tuning — both NAMED BY THE CANDIDATE AND NOT OPENED BY THIS AGENT, fetch budget exhausted; see COULD NOT READ below). The field's standing diagnosis is "the baseline was under-tuned." The design-of-experiments diagnosis — that ML ablations are COMPUTE-CONSTRAINED designs in which factors are deliberately moved together to hold a budget fixed (raise sequence length and cut batch size to hold tokens/step; change architecture and change depth to hold parameter count), exactly the constrained / split-plot situation for which DoE has an alias calculus and ML has none — has never been stated as a measurable property of the literature, let alone counted. In DoE and agronomy "what is this design's resolution and which effects are aliased" is asked of every experiment as a matter of course; in ML recipe papers, of none.

Presuppositions:
1. Published ablations report enough configuration detail to reconstruct which factors varied. CONTESTED — reporting is uneven; the frame must be restricted to studies with sufficient disclosure and the exclusion rate is itself a reportable number. Partly mitigated by the open-weights era's recipe disclosure (this is also the enabling condition, see Reason for silence).
2. A design-separation criterion can be defined for the irregular, mostly one-factor-at-a-time designs ML actually runs. RESOLVED BY THE REFORMULATION, not by assertion: the alias matrix and column estimability of a non-regular design under a stated model are standard (confirmed against DoE sources opened at query Q2/Q3: alias/confounding is defined by the design's column structure, and nonregular designs have alias coefficients rather than a resolution integer). The resolution INTEGER was the undefined object; estimability is not.
3. The co-moved factors have non-negligible effects at the scales studied. ESTABLISHED for learning rate and batch size (Choi et al. 2019, opened: expanding the tuned search space can reverse or collapse optimizer rankings). CONTESTED for tokenizer and numeric precision.
4. The audited conclusions are consequential — they propagate into frontier recipes. PLAUSIBLE, evidenced by citation into model reports; must be shown by the study, not asserted. Not verified by this agent.
5. Re-running de-aliased design points is feasible for a non-trivial subset. CONTESTED — most frontier-scale ablations cannot be re-run; limb (ii) is restricted to small-scale studies with released code and may not generalise upward. This is the candidate's weakest limb and it is flagged, not hidden.
6. NEW, added at Gate 1: "moved with it" is separable from "legitimately re-tuned for it." A learning rate re-tuned per arm is a design choice that can either break an alias (if tuned on a grid at every arm, approximating a nested/split-plot design) or create one (if tuned only for the treatment arm). CONTESTED and load-bearing: the coding rule must distinguish these, and it is the single place where the answer could be manufactured by the protocol. The candidate's intake did not state this.

What would count as an answer:
(i) A coded frame under a pre-registered inclusion rule (e.g. all pretraining-recipe and architecture ablations in a defined venue-year set with released configs, plus a named set of open model/technical reports) in which each headline comparison is reconstructed as a factor matrix with an explicitly stated model, yielding (a) the share of headline claims whose claimed main effect is not estimable given the reported design points, (b) the alias-coefficient distribution for the estimable-but-correlated remainder, (c) inter-coder agreement (two independent coders, kappa on the estimability verdict), (d) the undecidable / insufficient-disclosure share reported separately as its own number.
(ii) For a sample of non-estimable claims with available artifacts, a de-aliased re-run adding the missing design points, reporting the effect with and without de-aliasing against a pre-registered equivalence band from a same-configuration seed-variance study.
YES: a large non-estimable share and de-aliased effects that shrink below the band or reverse sign. NO: most designs separate their factor, or de-aliased estimates stay inside the equivalence band.
Either outcome is publishable and neither is guaranteed by the protocol — the question is not answerable-regardless.

Significance — consequence:
If YES (large non-estimable share, de-aliased effects shrinking below the equivalence band or reversing): the recipe literature's standing diagnosis for non-transfer ("the baseline was under-tuned") is at least partly replaced by a structural one ("the design could not have separated the factor"), which is actionable in a way the tuning diagnosis is not — under-tuning is a quality failure you exhort people out of, non-estimability is a design property you can compute before spending compute and fix with named extra runs. It supplies a review artifact that does not exist: an alias statement per ablation table, computable from the configs an author already has. It would also put a number on how much of the folklore propagated into frontier recipes is unidentified, which bears on how much credence to give recipe transfer at new scales.
If NO (most designs separate their factor, or de-aliased estimates stay inside the band): that is a substantive defence of current practice against a standing suspicion, and it relocates the non-transfer anomaly away from design confounding toward implementation variance, seed/data-order variance, or scale-dependence of effects — narrowing the anomaly rather than leaving it diffuse. The undecidable share is informative either way: a large insufficient-disclosure fraction is itself a reportable reporting-standards finding with an obvious remedy (publish the full factor matrix, not the delta).

Significance — audience:
(a) Pretraining teams at labs that run ablation suites: would act by computing the alias structure of a planned suite before spending the compute and by adding the cheapest alias-breaking points, which is a change to how compute is allocated, not a change in exhortation. (b) Conference reviewers and checklist maintainers (NeurIPS/ICLR reproducibility checklists, ACL responsible-NLP checklist): a concrete checklist item — "state which factors moved with the treatment and whether the main effect is estimable" — is the kind of thing these bodies do adopt, and unlike "tune your baselines" it is verifiable from the submitted configs. (c) The ML metascience/reproducibility community (the P3 and P4 communities): a new coded endpoint for a genre they already code. (d) The AutoML/HPO and hyperparameter-transfer community: de-aliasing is their problem restated as a design problem, and limb (ii) gives them targets.
Honest discount: the field already has a competing diagnosis it acts on, and prevalence findings in ML metascience have a poor record of changing behaviour (Troubling Trends, Q11, is highly cited and widely ignored). The audience is real; the action is plausible but not assured.

Significance — fecundity:
Once design resolution is a computable property of an ablation suite, these become askable and were not: what is the compute-optimal de-aliased design for a pretraining ablation under a fixed FLOP budget (a constrained D-optimal / split-plot design problem with hard-to-change whole-plot factors like parallelism and tokenizer); what is the cheapest augmentation that breaks a specific named alias (and is it cheaper than re-tuning the baseline, the current remedy); do scaling-law fits inherit the alias bias of the ablations that set their fixed factors; can an alias-structure extractor be run automatically over released configs, making the audit continuous rather than a one-off; and does the non-estimable share predict which published claims later fail to transfer — which turns the audit into a falsifiable forecaster rather than a census.

Significance — anchoring:
Touches the non-transfer anomaly the candidate is built on (Narang et al. 2021; Melis et al. 2018 — candidate's citations, not opened here); the optimizer-comparison instability that P1 demonstrates and P1's own caution that results depend on tuning protocol, workloads and budgets; the ML reproducibility literature's open question of why reported gains evaporate; and the hyperparameter-transfer line of work (muP and successors), which is an engineering answer to exactly the co-movement problem the candidate wants to measure and whose existence argues the problem is recognized while its prevalence is not.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism — disciplinary boundary plus incentive, with a new-data enabling condition):
1. Disciplinary boundary, in the vocabulary rather than the ideas. ML's experimental vocabulary descends from software benchmarking and from neuroscience lesion studies — "ablation" is a borrowed lesion term, and a lesion study is one-factor-at-a-time by construction. It does not descend from agricultural and industrial DoE, so no ML curriculum teaches the alias calculus and no ML paper template has a place to put a resolution or an alias matrix. Symmetrically, the people who own the alias calculus (industrial statisticians) do not read pretraining papers and have no access to the configs; Q2/Q3/Q7 show both halves of this: the calculus is textbook, and it has never been pointed at this target.
2. Incentive gap, and it is asymmetric. A paper's headline claim is the unit of credit, and computing your own design's resolution can only weaken your claim — there is no version of the result that helps the author. Auditing other people's designs is high-labour, low-prestige metascience with no natural venue in ML, which is also why P4 exists as an unexecuted student thesis announcement rather than as a paper.
3. A framing effect specific to compute constraints, which may be the real mechanism. Co-movement in ML is experienced as a physical necessity ("we had to hold tokens/step fixed, so batch size had to come down"), not as a design choice. Something experienced as unavoidable does not get measured, because measuring it seems to yield no decision — the DoE insight that a constrained design still has a choosable alias structure, and that you can choose WHICH effects to alias, is exactly the piece that does not travel without the calculus.
4. Enabling condition (new data): the coded frame only became constructible recently. Reconstructing a factor matrix requires released configs and detailed recipe disclosure at a density that the open-weights/technical-report era supplied in roughly the last three to five years; before that, Presupposition 1 would have excluded nearly everything and the exclusion rate would have been the only finding.

Strongest remaining objection (for Gate 4 to press):
The coding rule for Presupposition 6 — "co-moved with" versus "legitimately re-tuned for" — is where the answer can be manufactured. Code a per-arm re-tuned learning rate as a co-moved factor and the non-estimable share goes to nearly 1 by construction, which is the vacuous answer; code it as alias-breaking and the share may collapse toward 0, which is the other vacuous answer. Nothing in the candidate as stated pins this down, and the DoE analogue (is per-arm tuning a nested/split-plot structure or a confounder?) has a real answer that depends on whether the tuning was done on the same grid at every arm and whether that grid is disclosed — which is exactly the detail papers omit. Until the rule is pre-registered with a worked example on both sides, limb (i)'s headline number is protocol-determined rather than measured.
