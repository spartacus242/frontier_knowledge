## 0. Restatement in my own words (before attacking)

Do national metrology institutes have measurement *personalities* — a stable, institute-specific offset that
reappears every time they are compared internationally? Operationally: take the public record of CIPM and RMO
key comparisons; for each participant in each comparison, recompute how far it sat from the comparison's
reference value *with its own value removed from that reference* (because with four or six participants the
assessed laboratory partly is the reference), divide that miss by the uncertainty the participant itself
claimed, and then ask three statistical questions of the resulting panel: (i) how much of the spread in those
standardised misses is explained by *which institute it is* rather than *which comparison it was*; (ii) how
strongly an institute's miss on a quantity in one round predicts its miss on the same quantity in the next
round; (iii) whether an institute that runs high on one quantity also runs high on others. Then a fourth,
different kind of question: if that persistent slice were passed down the traceability chain instead of being
forgotten at the end of each comparison, by how much would the uncertainty on a calibration bought from that
institute have to grow — under a propagation convention the asker fixes in advance.

Three things I note about this restatement before searching. First, limbs (i)-(iii) are estimates with
intervals and a null is a real answer; limb (iv) is not a measurement at all, it is a consequence computed
under a chosen convention. Second, the divisor is the participant's *own* claimed uncertainty, which is itself
one of the things under suspicion. Third, "institute" is doing work the data may not support: the chain spans
decades of staff, instruments and methods.

**RECOMMENDED VERDICT: Provisional** — promotion requires (1) the gravimetry and radiocarbon leads in §2(a)-(b)
opened and found negative, (2) P1 verified by actually extracting one CC's chain from the final reports, and

**Single strongest killing point:** CIPM MRA-G-13 §8.3 and §9, which I opened and quoted, already provide that a
"significant unresolved deviation from a comparison result with respect to a CMC" triggers "a reduced range or
an increase of the measurement uncertainty", corrective action within six months, or grey-out — so the
candidate's target is not "the persistent component" but only the *sub-threshold* persistent component that a
per-comparison screen cannot see, and limb (iv)'s inflation factor must be computed on that residue alone. The
question survives in that narrowed form, but its motivating gap has now been narrowed twice (Gate 2 killed the
strong form of "nothing is carried forward"; this kills the unqualified form of "it is left out of the
uncertainty a user inherits"), and a gap that shrinks each time a primary document is opened is a gap whose
remaining size is not yet established.
