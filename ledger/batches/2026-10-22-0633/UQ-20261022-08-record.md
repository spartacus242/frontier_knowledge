Question (canonical — Gate 1 reformulation, one reformulation used):
For a health system that replaced its EHR, with a queryable legacy archive and a declared look-back window W before the cutover date T: (i) per pre-declared fact class (problem-list entries, medication orders, allergies, quantitative lab results with units, immunisations, procedures, notes), what fraction of the facts present in the SOURCE system's structured tables within W is present, queryable and value-identical in the TARGET system's computable record, under a correspondence rule fixed before any data pull, with "no admissible correspondent in the target model" counted as its own category rather than dropped; and (ii) on at least three named longitudinal estimands (e.g. incident-diabetes rate, prevalent ACE-inhibitor exposure, Charlson comorbidity prevalence) computed from the target system's standard research extract, is there a level or slope discontinuity at T that exceeds a materiality bound declared in advance, where "artefactual" is established by re-deriving the same estimand for the same patients from the legacy archive and by a pre-declared no-cutover control window of equal length (and, where available, a contemporaneous non-converting control site)?

Alternative formulations:
- (V0, intake) When a health system replaces its electronic health record, what fraction of the pre-cutover clinical record is never brought into the new system's computable record at all — measured per pre-declared fact class — and does that out-of-scope remainder produce artefactual discontinuities at the cutover date in the incidence, exposure and comorbidity measures that EHR-derived research and quality reporting compute?
- (lay) After a hospital switches EHR vendors, how much of its patients' past survives as a usable number rather than a scanned image or a decommissioned archive, and what does the missing part do to the statistics drawn from that record?
- (method field's words) Applying digital-preservation concepts — significant properties, migration fidelity, fixity — to EHR conversions: what are the per-fact-class retention rates in the target system, and what is the measured discontinuity they induce in named longitudinal estimands at the cutover boundary?

Gate 1 note — what the reformulation changed and why (well-posedness only): V0 left three things unsettled. (a) The DENOMINATOR: "the pre-cutover clinical record" is not a countable object; the reformulation fixes it as facts in the source system's structured tables within a declared window W, which makes the fraction computable and makes image-only/paper-origin content a declared sensitivity arm rather than an ambiguity. (b) ATTRIBUTION: V0's word "artefactual" asserted a cause. Cutover coincides with real documentation and workflow change, and prior N2 below shows that in 20 of 41 monitored measures the intervention-vs-control difference was driven by changes at CONTROL sites, so a bare before/after shift at T cannot be attributed to non-migrated data. The reformulation requires the legacy-archive re-derivation (the archive makes the counterfactual computable) plus a no-cutover control window, which is what converts limb (ii) from an observation into a test. (c) MATERIALITY: V0 said "artefactual discontinuities" with no bound; the reformulation requires the bound pre-declared, so a null is reportable. Both versions recorded; V0 is retained as the lay/intake phrasing.

Presuppositions (with status):
P1. The health system retains an accessible, QUERYABLE legacy archive at cutover, so the pre-cutover denominator is knowable. Commonly true during a contractual retention period; CONTESTED after decommissioning, which is a datable event; and "retained" is not "queryable" (tape/read-only viewer vs archive-as-a-service). The answer spec therefore requires a stated count of candidate sites that could not supply a denominator at all.
P2. A fact-class-level correspondence between source and target data models can be defined. CONTESTED — this is the transplant's interface problem (coding-system change, units, result-status semantics, problem-list vs encounter-diagnosis). Handled by pre-declaring the rule and counting "no admissible correspondent" as its own category.
P3. Research and quality extracts are drawn from the target system's computable tables, so out-of-scope facts are absent from them. True where extracts are vendor-standard; verify per site. NOT universally true as an institutional matter: the UDS instrument opened in this gate instructs the opposite (merge both systems and de-duplicate), so P3 must be verified per extract pipeline, not assumed.
P4. Cutover dates are identifiable in the extract. Usually true at a site; NOT generally true in multi-site network data, where cutovers are typically unlabelled — which is itself part of the significance.
P5. Any discontinuity is not already neutralised by analysts' standard practice. CONTESTED and only ONE-INSTANCE CHECKED this gate. Document-kind premise test: the premise "programmes/networks deliberately truncate observation periods at cutover" was tested against one actual instance of the document kind — UDS "Guidance for Health Centers that Switched EHRs" (updated August 2025) — and the premise did NOT hold in that instance: it instructs health centers to submit one unduplicated report for the full calendar year, recommends merging data from both systems with de-duplication, and where merging fails says to report "to the best of the health center's ability with the available data" with gaps explained in a Table Comment, warning that incomplete data "may result in a questionable rating". It gives NO numeric data-loss estimate and does NOT acknowledge that post-switch measures are non-comparable with prior years. One instance is not three: the OHDSI/PCORnet observation-period convention was NOT opened, so P5 remains contested with one instrument read.
P6. Scanned or read-only retention counts as loss for computable purposes. A declared definitional choice, with a sensitivity arm treating image-only retention as partial.
P7 (added at Gate 1). The legacy archive can serve as a re-derivation substrate for the same estimand on the same patients. Needed for limb (ii)'s attribution; follows from P1 where the archive is queryable, fails where it is a viewer only.

What would count as an answer: Per fact class, a measured retention fraction (present, queryable and value-identical in the target computable record) at two or more systems with DIFFERENT source and target vendor pairs, under a correspondence rule fixed before any data pull, with correspondence failures reported as their own category; plus a pre-registered discontinuity test on at least three named estimands at T, judged against a materiality bound declared in advance, with the legacy-archive re-derivation and the no-cutover control window as the attribution arms; plus a stated count of candidate sites that could not supply a legacy denominator at all. Near-complete retention per class with no discontinuity beyond the bound is a clean NEGATIVE answer and is informative: it would license the current, unevidenced practice of pooling research and quality data across cutover boundaries.

Significance — consequence: YES answer (a material out-of-scope remainder with a discontinuity beyond the bound): every EHR-derived incidence, exposure and comorbidity estimate that spans a cutover carries a datable, vendor-change-shaped artefact that is currently unlabelled; analysts would need cutover-aware observation periods, a per-fact-class loss profile to decide which estimands survive, and the cutover disclosed as a provenance fact in RWE submissions; migration scope stops being purely an IT cost decision and becomes an evidence-quality decision with a measured price. NO answer (near-complete retention, no discontinuity beyond the bound): a clean negative that licenses the current, unevidenced practice of pooling across cutovers and tells health systems that migration scope can be cut without downstream evidential cost — which is the direction the UDS guidance opened here already implicitly assumes without evidence.
Significance — audience: (1) health-system informatics and HIM teams specifying migration scope, who price this decision at every conversion and today have no loss profile to price it against; (2) research-network data-curation groups (OHDSI data-quality/observation-period conventions, Sentinel curation, PCORnet data characterization), who would gain a cutover-indexed check class — none of their materials returned at query 4 or 6 is cutover-indexed; (3) RWE regulatory scientists assessing data accrual and provenance; (4) quality-reporting programmes, concretely and verifiably: the UDS "Guidance for Health Centers that Switched EHRs" (updated August 2025) instructs merging both systems with de-duplication and reporting "to the best of the health center's ability with the available data" where that fails, warning of a "questionable rating", while nowhere acknowledging non-comparability with prior years — a live instruction whose premises this question tests; (5) secondary-use researchers, for whom N1's authors are the existence proof of the audience, having hit this and dropped a planned analysis.
Significance — fecundity: is the loss profile predictable from the source/target vendor pair, so a site could forecast its own artefact before converting? Is there a dose-response between per-class retention and measured estimand bias, which would turn retention into a usable data-quality index? Can the legacy archive serve as a recovery instrument (reverse-ETL of out-of-scope classes) and does recovery move the estimand — the recoverability question that UQ-20261016-05 asks for statistical missingness, here asked for scope-missingness? How many unlabelled cutovers sit inside existing multi-site network data, and should networks maintain a public cutover register? Does image-only retention behave as partial loss or total loss for named estimands? Which fact classes are systematically sacrificed, and is that choice clinical or contractual?
Significance — anchoring: EHR data-quality frameworks (Kahn et al. conformance/completeness/plausibility, including temporal plausibility, which is the check family a cutover artefact should trip and apparently does not); OHDSI observation-period construction and ETL conventions; N1's provenance finding; GAO's finding that VA did not ensure migrated-data quality (returned, not opened); the digital-preservation significant-properties literature and NIST's long-term-EHR-preservation programme; and the general anomaly that migration validation in medicine is defined on the migrated set, so its success criterion cannot see its own complement.

Reason for silence: a disciplinary and incentive boundary that runs exactly through the complement. The people who hold the fact are health-system IT, HIM and migration vendors, whose success criterion is contractual go-live plus validation of what was migrated — and both opened migration-audit papers (N3, Pageler) define their denominator as the migrated set, so the quantity is outside the measurement by construction, not by oversight. The people who would suffer from its absence are downstream researchers and quality analysts who see only the target system's tables and therefore cannot see the denominator; N1 is the mechanism caught in the act, a research team discovering the artefact ad hoc, reporting it as a lesson learned about one database, and abandoning an analysis rather than measuring the quantity. Three further reinforcers: measuring the complement requires querying an archive whose retention is a cost centre and whose decommissioning is often the project's financial justification, so the measurement competes with the business case; publishing a per-class loss profile is an institutional-risk disclosure about one's own record; and the method vocabulary for the complement (significant properties, migration fidelity, fixity) sits in archival science, whose literature clinical informaticians do not read and whose practitioners have no access to live EHR conversions.
