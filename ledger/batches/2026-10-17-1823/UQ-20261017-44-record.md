Question (canonical): [V2, the one Gate 1 reformulation, adopted] Over a pre-declared frame of theorem-level declarations in human-authored machine-checked libraries and blueprint projects that carry an attribution to a specific published theorem (library source attributions, docstring references, blueprint-to-paper node maps), and comparing each against the published statement read under a declared blanket-hypothesis rule (standing conventions such as "all rings here are commutative Noetherian" count as stated hypotheses of the published theorem): what is the signed rate of logical-strength divergence — the share formalized strictly weaker (hypotheses dropped or generalized, or a strictly weaker axiom set) against the share formalized strictly stronger (the formalization had to assume more) — decomposed into mechanically detected divergences (axiom set via `#print axioms`, declared-but-unused binders, typeclass weakening relative to the published hypothesis) and hand-adjudicated substantive ones on a random sample with inter-adjudicator agreement; and does the weaker-side rate vary with the source's publication age, length or subfield?

(V1, as intaken, superseded: "Among theorems formalized in a large machine-checked library from identifiable published sources, what fraction were formalized with strictly weaker hypotheses or a strictly weaker axiom set than the published statement, and does the rate of such over-hypothesization vary with the source's age, length or subfield?" Reformulated for well-posedness on three defects, not for novelty: (a) "the published statement" is not a well-defined object until standing/blanket hypotheses are ruled on, and that ruling plausibly dominates the measured rate; (b) a bare "fraction ... weaker" is one-sided and can be read as confirmed by any positive count, so the symmetric strengthening arm is moved from the answer criteria into the question itself; (c) "over-hypothesization" names a defect in the paper and prejudges attribution, so V2 asks for signed divergence and leaves the paper-defect reading to interpretation. Note the residual estimand gap V2 does not close and no reformulation can: a weakening in the library shows that SOME proof needs less, not that the PUBLISHED proof needed less.)

Alternative formulations:
1. How often does formalization reveal that a published theorem's stated assumptions were unnecessary — and how often the reverse?
2. Now that axiom use and hypothesis use are mechanically checkable at library scale, what is the measured logical slack between published mathematics and what its proofs actually require?

Motivating gap: The enabling quantities are mechanical and recent — for any declaration in a large formal library one can now ask which axioms its proof actually invokes (including whether choice or classical logic is used) and whether each declared binder is used at all, neither ascertainable at scale for the informal literature. Formalizers report generalization-on-formalization constantly as anecdote (weakening typeclass assumptions because the proof does not need them), and the formalization community's public case for its own value rests partly on such anecdotes (commentary of the Buzzard/Avigad kind; Avigad, "Mathematics and the formal turn", arXiv:2311.00007 — ABSTRACT ONLY, the full text was not read, and the abstract shows no bearing on this question, so it is cited as the genre of claim and not as evidence). What Gate 2 changes about this gap: the mechanical limb's instrument and its library-scale counts ARE published (Best, FMM 2021 — a Lean metaprogram that finds over-strong typeclass hypotheses, 2,877 hits on one ~80,000-declaration mathlib pass), and a SIGNED formal-vs-source fidelity rate IS published for adjacent corpora (arXiv:2606.14000, Faithful/Stronger/Different percentages with a "Stronger" class that explicitly includes dropped redundant hypotheses). Neither compares human library formalizations against PUBLISHED PAPER statements, and neither carries CIs, covariates or a frame-selectivity audit. The gap survives, narrowed, in the join rather than in either limb.

Presuppositions:
P1. A frame of formalized theorems with identifiable published sources can be constructed from library attributions, docstring references or blueprint projects. CONTESTED: attribution is patchy, and a frame built where attribution is good over-represents well-curated areas. Partly supported: arXiv:2606.14000 constructs source-linked frames of 175 / 4,636 / 6,960 entities, but from textbooks, and for one corpus the natural-language side is machine-generated.
P2. "Strictly weaker hypotheses" is adjudicable by a declared protocol — mechanical for typeclass weakening and unused binders, hand-adjudicated otherwise. LARGELY ESTABLISHED for the mechanical part (Best 2021 implements it, with admitted false positives); CONTESTED for the hand part.
P3. Unused-hypothesis detection is sound at scale. CONTESTED: a binder may be present to make a definition type-check, to match a downstream interface, or to support a stated corollary the formalization omits — none of which is over-hypothesization by the paper.
P4. Formal statements are faithful renderings rather than convenient variants of a different theorem. CONTESTED, and now quantified in the adjacent case: arXiv:2606.14000 classes 18-42% of statements "Different" from their source against 1-4% "Stronger", i.e. in that frame drift swamps discovered weakening. Statement drift is the standard criticism of formalization-based claims about the literature and it cuts both ways.
P5. The library's selection of what to formalize is not so purposive that the rate says nothing about the literature. CONTESTED: a library formalizes what is useful downstream, so the estimand may have to be restricted to blueprint projects that formalize a single paper end to end.
P6. NEW at Gate 1, load-bearing: the published statement's hypothesis set is determinate once standing/blanket conventions are ruled on. CONTESTED and probably the dominant error source: papers routinely carry blanket hypotheses in prose far from the theorem, so the same theorem can be scored weaker-in-Lean or faithful depending on the reading rule. V2 makes the rule explicit rather than resolving it.
P7. Over-hypothesization is distinguishable from deliberate expositional choice by authors who knew the hypothesis was removable. CONTESTED and not mechanically decidable; it is also the attribution step that makes the "paper defect" reading licit, and V2 declines to make it.
P8. Someone acts on the rate: formalization funders and journals weighing whether formalization pays for itself, and mathematicians who cite results at their stated rather than their actual strength. Partly supported at Gate 3; see the autoformalization-scoring audience, which is the firmest of them.

What would count as an answer: A signed rate with confidence intervals over a pre-declared, pre-registered frame, decomposed into (i) mechanically detected divergences — axiom set, declared-but-unused binders, typeclass generalization against the published hypothesis — and (ii) hand-adjudicated substantive divergences on a random sample with inter-adjudicator agreement reported; both arms reported in both directions, so that the pre-declared symmetric null (weakening rate indistinguishable from strengthening rate, which dissolves the "slack" reading) can actually fire; plus the covariate analysis by source publication age, length and subfield with pre-declared strata; plus a sensitivity analysis over the blanket-hypothesis reading rule (P6) and an audit of the frame's selectivity (P1, P5). The frame and the reading rule, not the counting, are where this question can go wrong. An admissible settlement in either direction: a weaker-side rate that is material and concentrated in older or longer sources; or a rate within a pre-registered equivalence band of the strengthening rate, or one that collapses under the blanket-hypothesis sensitivity analysis.

Significance — consequence: If the weaker-side rate is material and separates from the strengthening rate and from the blanket-hypothesis artefact, formalization acquires a measured, citable by-product beyond verification — a generalization yield per formalized paper — where today it has anecdotes and a tool that emits 2,877 unaudited hits; and ordinary citation practice is shown to under-use the literature at a quantified rate, which is a claim about mathematical writing that can be acted on. If the rate is indistinguishable from the strengthening rate, or dissolves under the P6 sensitivity analysis, then the generalization-on-formalization anecdote is deflated to noise, one of the formalization community's public arguments for its own value is withdrawn with evidence rather than left standing on vividness, and PRIOR 2's asymmetry (drift 18-42% vs strengthening 1-4%) is confirmed as the dominant signal in formal-versus-source comparisons. Both arms change something, which is what the gate asks; against this, the magnitude is modest and the second arm's audience may simply stop citing the anecdote rather than act.
Significance — audience: (i) autoformalization and benchmark evaluators are the firmest, and the least obvious: PRIOR 2 shows they already assign a "Stronger" class and must decide whether it is a fidelity failure, and a measured literature-slack base rate tells them whether a stronger-than-source formalization is drift to penalize or discovery to credit — a scoring-rule change they would make. (ii) mathlib maintainers and blueprint leads, who would record source attributions and weakening provenance (they already run and debate generalization tooling, so the marginal action is attribution policy, not detection). (iii) formalization funders and journal editors weighing whether formalization pays for itself, who currently see anecdote; diffuse, and the weakest link in the chain. (iv) reverse-mathematics and proof-mining researchers, who have a hand-built per-theorem catalogue and no population rate, and for whom a corpus rate would be a sampling frame for case selection rather than a result in their own idiom.
Significance — fecundity: the weakening rate as a function of time since publication (does slack get found or does it get written out?); whether mathlib proofs of classical theorems invoke choice/classical logic less often than the literature assumes, i.e. a corpus-scale reverse-mathematics-lite with the per-theorem programme as its validation set; whether over-hypothesization predicts citation under-use; whether a model can predict removable hypotheses from paper text alone, with this rate as the base rate; and whether blanket-hypothesis conventions turn out to be the dominant cause, which converts the finding from one about mathematical content into one about mathematical prose.
Significance — anchoring: the "what is formalization for" debate (Avigad's formal turn; Buzzard-genre advocacy); reverse mathematics and proof mining as the two mature programmes asking the per-theorem version; mathlib's own generalization-linter practice (PRIOR 1); autoformalization fidelity evaluation (PRIOR 2), where the same comparison is already being scored for a different purpose; specification-coverage/vacuity detection in deductive verification; and in this ledger, UQ-20261015-09, whose cost side this question's yield side would pair with.

Reason for silence: A disciplinary boundary with an incentive asymmetry on each side, plus genuine instrument recency for the specific join. The field that owns the METHOD (metamathematics — reverse mathematics, proof mining; and, for the detector itself, deductive program verification) asks "is this hypothesis necessary?" as a per-theorem logical analysis, where a population rate over a corpus is not a result its subfield rewards: a rate is not a theorem. The community that owns the DATA (formalizers) has the instrument and fires it routinely, but reads each hit as a local engineering fact and a pull-request title, not as an observation about the literature — and its public argument is better served by vivid individual anecdotes than by a rate that might come back small or be swamped by blanket-hypothesis artefacts. There is also a politeness/incentive gap: a formalizer who finds a colleague's stated hypothesis unnecessary has reason not to frame it as a defect in that paper, which produces anecdotes while suppressing the systematic tally. And the enabling join — source-attributed human formalization at blueprint scale, plus mechanical axiom tracking — is only about three years old at usable scale, though the unused-dependency half of the instrument is 15 years old in the Mizar lineage (PRIOR 3), which is the part of this mechanism that is weakest and that an adversary should attack first.
