Domain(s): Biology — evolutionary genomics / cytogenetics / phylogenetic comparative methods (chromosome-number evolution). Method-owning neighbours: metrology (agreement between two imperfect measurement systems), statistics (measurement error in a discrete variable).
Question (canonical): Over the frame of species holding both (a) a chromosome-level assembly in a public archive, pseudo-chromosome count extracted under a pre-declared rule, and (b) a published haploid chromosome number in a compiled karyotype resource (CCDB, coleoguy/karyotypes), matched by accepted name against one backbone: what is the rate, signed direction and taxonomic structure of disagreement between the two counts; and when one chromosome-number evolution model is refitted on one fixed phylogeny per clade using assembly-derived counts in place of cytogenetic counts, by how much do estimated polyploidization, demi-polyploidization, fission and fusion rates move, relative to the movement produced by perturbing cytogenetic counts at the measured error rate and direction alone?
Alternative formulations: (1) How often does a chromosome-level genome assembly contradict the karyotype of record for the same species, and in which direction does it err? (2) Is the chromosome-number data underlying chromosome-evolution models measurement-error-free, and what does realistic count error do to inferred rates of polyploidy and rearrangement?
Motivating gap: EBP/DToL/VGP output makes a pseudo-chromosome count a second, high-throughput realization of haploid number for species already carrying a 20th-century karyotype. ChromEvol-class models treat each compiled count as observed without error; the only literature interrogating them tests model adequacy, not data error (Rice & Mayrose 2021). Hjelmen 2024 states the alignment of scaffold number with haploid count as an expectation in print and then uses the count only as a scaling denominator; Rice & Mayrose name count-collection error in their Discussion as an unexamined candidate cause of adequacy failure.
Presuppositions: (1) the pseudo-chromosome count measures haploid number — CONTESTED, and on adversarial evidence contested in practice, not just in principle (see attack 4); (2) the cytogenetic count of record is retrievable and attributable to a matching species identification — CONTESTED (synonymy, cryptic species, karyotype races); (3) the intersection is large enough, with taxonomic spread, to estimate a clade-wise rate — SUPPORTED at least for Lepidoptera (452 chromosome-level assemblies, >half with light-microscopy counts: Lukhtanov & Pazhenkova 2025); (4) ChromEvol-class inference is sensitive to count error at realistic magnitudes — CONTESTED, untested; (5) neither measurement is a gold standard — CONTESTED by field practice, which treats the karyotype as the validator.
What would count as an answer: (i) a discordance table with explicit denominators by clade, signed direction, and a pre-specified subset adjudicated by a third method (FISH, optical mapping, Hi-C contact-map inspection, fresh meiotic counts); (ii) refits of one model on one fixed phylogeny under cytogenetic versus assembly-derived counts, reporting rate displacement with uncertainty, plus a perturbation simulation at the measured error rate and direction as the internal comparator. A null — discordance rare and rates unmoved — settles it the other way and would license pooling.

Verification (post-batch, fresh-context checker, log UQ-20261016-56-verification.md): the kill's source is real and says what was claimed, on a weaker footing than the batch implied.
DOI 10.18699/vjgb-25-26 resolves to Lukhtanov and Pazhenkova, "Cytogenetics of insects in the era of chromosome-level genome assemblies", Vavilov Journal of Genetics and Breeding 29(2):230-237 (2025). Verified verbatim: "information on chromosome assemblies, including determination of the haploid number of chromosomes, is available for 452 species"; "For more than half of them, there are data on chromosome numbers obtained using light microscopy methods"; "Having compared these data, we found complete agreement in the haploid chromosome number calculations made using the bioinformatics approach and light microscopy methods"; and the conclusion "Examples of such validation reinforce the conclusion that chromosome level genome assemblies are a reliable source of information on karyotypes".
But it is a NARRATIVE REVIEW, and the comparison is a single summary sentence. The compared N is given only as "more than half" of 452, so about 226 or more, with no exact figure. There is no per-species table, no listed exceptions, no statistic, no confidence interval, and no model: the paper fits nothing and estimates no rate of fission, fusion or polyploidization, saying only qualitatively that karyotype change proceeds by simple fusions and fissions in the most parsimonious way. It also says classical cytogenetics is still needed to validate assemblies.
What that does and does not settle. It settles the candidate's premise: in the clade with the densest data, the two instruments have been compared and the reported discordance is zero, so a large discordance rate is not waiting to be found there, and limb (ii) — how far likelihood rate estimates move when assembly counts replace cytogenetic ones — has no signal to propagate in that clade. That is why the rejection stands: what is left is a corpus extension plus a conditional sensitivity analysis. What it does NOT settle is a measured rate with a denominator and CIs, which this review does not contain. One route could revive the line: a per-species audit of the comparison behind that sentence, published as a table, showing whether "complete agreement" conceals mismatches. The kill rests on a review's assertion, not on a table, and this entry says so rather than claiming more.
