# UQ-20261017-25 — Adversary #1 log

## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take old, dated, published design numbers — the 50-year wind speed a 1980s/90s ASCE 7 map gave for a station, the 1%-chance discharge a 1980s FEMA Flood Insurance Study gave at a gauge, a 100-year significant wave height in a published coastal design report. For each, count how many years since publication the observed annual maximum beat the published number. Pool across many sites, correcting for the fact that one storm or flood beats many sites at once. Compare the pooled realized frequency with the nominal 1/T. Then split any gap into: (a) what the old estimator got wrong on the data it had (re-run the old method and a modern method on the old record), (b) sampling noise on the old record, (c) real change since. The candidate says nobody has computed this pooled realized-vs-nominal rate for issued environmental design values and that the field only ever re-estimates.

## Documents opened (4 fetches)

- https://www.nist.gov/publications/hurricane-marilyn-caribbean-measured-wind-speeds-and-design-wind-speeds-compared-nistir — Marshall & Schroeder, NISTIR 5987 (1997). Single-event magnitude comparison: peak over-water sustained speeds (St Croix ~40, St Thomas ~46, Culebra ~43 m/s) against "a 50-year design event ... about 50 m/s". One site's maximum assigned "a mean recurrence interval of about 30 years, or an annual probability of 0.033". No pooled count, no rate. The record's unopened exposure item is cleared: it is not a prior for the realized-rate statistic.
- https://doi.org/10.3133/sir20095108 — USGS SIR 2009-5108 (April 2007 flood, western Connecticut, 28 gaging stations). "Flood-frequency estimates made after the April 2007 flood were compared to" FEMA FIS values; "The estimates in this report update and supersede previously published flood-frequency estimates"; at 0.01 AEP the new estimates are SMALLER than FEMA's (median about -13%). This is the revise-by-re-estimation genre: a quantile-scale comparison including the new event, not an exceedance count. Confirms the gap the record describes rather than filling it. (Fetch did not return title/authors; identified by DOI only.)
- https://gfzpublic.gfz-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_1724897_4/component/file_1727888/1724897.pdf — Mak, S. & Schorlemmer, D. (2016), "A Comparison between the Forecast by the United States National Seismic Hazard Maps with Recent Ground-Motion Records," BSSA 106(4):1817-1831. Tests four dated issued map vintages (1996, 2002, 2008, 2014 NSHM) at six nominal return periods (10-400 yr) against ~15 years of station records; statistic = number of sites with at least one exceedance vs a Poisson-binomial forecast with a 5-95% interval: "If the observed hazard falls outside this interval, the forecast is judged as inconsistent with the observation." Dependence handled explicitly: "Because the sites are spatially clustered (Fig. 2a,c), they are not independent" ... "When they are not, the actual variance will be larger than the computed one"; solution is declustering by 25/50 km minimum separation, 400 random subsets, a choice the authors call "fundamentally arbitrary". Power stated: at the 400-yr return period ~15 years of data detect a discrepancy only at roughly 3x the forecast; 30 years lowers that to ~2.2x. Attribution to model vs data is NOT settled; they list site-effect corrections, exclusion of small events and induced seismicity as confounders.
- https://iris.unive.it/retrieve/740b91b1-1d9b-4e63-a8a8-4c4ab4452d86/KjeldsenProsdocimi2023JoH.pdf — spot-check of the record's Prior 3. Confirmed: Kjeldsen & Prosdocimi, J. Hydrol. 626 (2023) 130235; 939/842/767 gauges; chi-squared at 10% finds "negative binomial and geometrical distributions provide a better fit to the annual exceedance count than the Poisson distribution"; thresholds "set so that there is an average of one event per year"; no out-of-sample comparison with issued design values. The record's description is accurate. (One caveat: the record's quoted line "the paper contains no such analysis... it never checks how often issued or fitted design floods were actually exceeded" reads like a fetch-tool summary, not paper text; the substance is correct but it should not be presented as a quotation.)

Prior-existence spot checks: Prior 2 (EGU26-18045) returned independently by my query 6 — exists. Prior 3 — opened and confirmed. Prior 1 (Simiu et al. 2003) — not re-opened by me (budget); the NIST landing page the record cites is a real NIST publication record, and I have no reason to doubt it, but I did not verify it this run.

## Recommended verdict

Provisional. Nothing Unresolved on attacks 1, 2 or 4; Partial on 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8. Promotion conditions: (i) rewrite the novelty section to name the seismic hazard-map-testing literature (Mak & Schorlemmer 2016 BSSA and predecessors) as the nearest prior formulation and re-argue the difference; (ii) open the FEMA-floodplain-vs-claims literature (Blessing/Sebastian/Brody-type studies surfaced by my query 2, not opened) to confirm it is a spatial-delineation check and not a realized-rate statistic; (iii) demonstrate on a handful of sites that an issue-era estimator can be reproduced from the issue-era record, since presupposition 5 remains the load-bearing contested assumption.

Strongest killing point (one line): the identical statistic — observed exceedance counts of dated, issued hazard-map values at many stations against a Poisson-binomial expectation, across successive map vintages, with inter-site dependence handled and a power analysis — has been published for seismic hazard maps since at least Mak & Schorlemmer (2016, BSSA 106(4):1817) and a 2006-onward literature, so the candidate is a known hazard-map-verification question renamed into hydrology/wind vocabulary, its "obvious next frame" is already done, and its disciplinary-boundary silence mechanism fails in the neighbouring field.
