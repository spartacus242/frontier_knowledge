## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (canonical, V0 as intaken): For a pre-declared set of analytes and accredited clinical laboratories, what is the signed difference between a laboratory's error rate on declared external-quality-assessment samples and its error rate on identical material submitted blind through the routine patient workflow, and how much of any difference is attributable to declared-sample handling (senior-staff assignment, batching, repeat runs before reporting)?

Alternative formulations:
- Do clinical laboratories grade themselves on an exam they know they are sitting — what is the declared-versus-blind performance gap?
- Transplanting forensic science's blind quality-control programmes into clinical pathology: what would a blind EQA scheme have found that the declared scheme did not?

Motivating gap (as intaken): the method's home field is forensic QA and federally regulated workplace drug testing, where blind proficiency specimens are submitted through routine intake precisely because declared samples are known to be handled differently; clinical accreditation and PT use declared samples, and the standing prohibition on referring PT specimens to another laboratory is itself an admission that declared samples are treated as special.

**Correction to the motivating gap, established at Gate 2:** the premise that the method is foreign to clinical laboratory medicine is false. Blind proficiency surveys were *run by the CDC* on US clinical laboratories c. 1979–1982 and reported in the American Journal of Public Health (Boone et al. 1982), and a paired open-versus-blind PT experiment on 42 certified clinical laboratories was published in Clinical Chemistry (Parsons et al. 2001). This is not a transplant; it is a dormant own-field design.

Presuppositions (as intaken, with Gate 1 disposition):
1. Material commutable with patient specimens and indistinguishable at intake can be produced and submitted — CONTESTED, and now partly *settled in the candidate's favour*: Parsons et al. did exactly this for whole-blood lead pools, and Boone et al. report blind samples "sent through normal patient sample acquisition routes". Feasibility is demonstrated for at least two analyte classes; generality across a routine chemistry menu is not.
2. Blind submission is ethically and legally feasible without a fictitious patient identity — CONTESTED, and left unresolved by the candidate (it defers the choice of consenting-donor versus governance-approved residual-material route). My read of Parsons did not return the requisition route, patient-identity handling or ethics arrangement: the summariser stated those Methods specifics were not in what it was served. So the candidate's hardest presupposition is the one the nearest prior's accessible text does not settle either way.
3. Error defined against a reference value independent of participants, not peer consensus — design requirement, declared. Note the nearest prior does NOT do this: Parsons scored against CLIA '88 blood-lead acceptability criteria, i.e. a regulatory tolerance band, and I could not confirm at my read level whether the target was reference-method assigned or pool-assigned.
4. Whether laboratories actually handle declared samples differently is the MEASURAND, not an assumption — **MEASURAND EXEMPTION DECLARED AND APPLIED.** I do not grade this clause as a presupposition failure; an attack that the question presupposes special handling would be attacking it for asking its own primary quantity.
5. Blind specimens can be run without alerting staff through volume, container type or ordering pattern, and the study must audit concealment — CONTESTED and UNRESOLVED by either opened prior: neither Parsons (at my read level) nor Boone reports the share of blind specimens recognised.
6. Middleware and audit logs retain operator, repeat-run count and time to report — CONTESTED across vendors and retention policies. **Document-kind rule: NOT CHECKED.** I opened zero actual laboratory middleware configurations or vendor audit-trail specifications; the candidate's own intake says this should be verified on two or three real configurations before the design is fixed, and it was not verified here. ## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

What would count as an answer (as intaken, accepted): paired error rates plus bias and imprecision on the SAME material in the SAME laboratories over the same period, declared versus blind, per analyte with confidence intervals; and the handling mechanism measured from middleware logs (operator seniority, repeat runs before release, time to report) rather than inferred.

V0 is answerable in principle and states its settling evidence, so it clears Gate 1 in structure. Three under-specifications were fatal to grading it against priors, so **one Gate 1 reformulation (R1) is taken and recorded**:

R1 (the one permitted reformulation): *Among currently accredited general clinical laboratories running automated analysers with retained middleware audit trails (>=2 vendors), over a set of routine quantitative analytes declared before any submission and restricted to those with documented commutability of the study material, what is the signed per-analyte difference in the rate of results falling outside a pre-declared acceptability limit around a reference-method-assigned target between (a) declared EQA samples and (b) the same material submitted blind through the routine patient workflow by a single governance-approved route named in advance; with (c) the share of blind specimens recognised by staff reported as a primary result, and (d) the difference decomposed against middleware-logged operator grade, repeat runs before release and time to report?*

**What R1 presupposes that V0 did not — tested as hard as the clause it replaced:**
- That >=2 accredited laboratories will consent to covert submission into their own patient workflow under institutional governance. Parsons achieved this with two state public-health laboratories supplying material; whether a general hospital laboratory would consent to being the *subject* is untested by anything I opened.
- That retained middleware audit trails carry operator grade at all. Operator *identity* is widely logged; operator *seniority/grade* is an HR attribute that must be joined to it. This is my own inference about what such logs contain, NOT a source finding — I opened no middleware specification, and the search that would have grounded it was not run.
- That a single submission route can be fixed in advance for a multi-analyte menu. A residual-material route is plausible for chemistry and haematology and implausible for analytes needing a dedicated tube or immediate processing, so R1 may be forced back to a narrower analyte set than it promises.
R1 is therefore not a free repair: it trades V0's vagueness for two new load-bearing feasibility premises, one of which rests on my own reasoning.

It is a question, not a thesis: both signs of the answer are informative, and the candidate says so.

## Gate 3 — Significance

- **Consequence.** Real but substantially pre-empted. If the gap is large and general, accreditation bodies, EQA providers and payers cannot read declared-EQA performance as an estimate of patient-result quality, and the mechanism limb would say which handling practice to interdict. If null, it would retire the suspicion — but the intake's claim that a null would retire a suspicion with "no number attached to it anywhere in clinical medicine" is FALSE: the number is +13.2 percentage points for blood lead, printed in Clin Chem 2001;47:322. What a null would actually do is bound the generalisability of that number, which is a smaller consequence than claimed.
- **Audience.** Real and identifiable: CMS/CLIA and the CLIAC advisory committee (which has agenda material on PT), CAP and ISO 15189 accreditors, EQA/PT providers (CAP, Noklus, SKML, UK NEQAS), and the forensic-QA community that already runs blind programmes. Boone et al. show the audience existed and acted on the design; Parsons et al. show it can be published in the field's flagship journal. Audience is not inflated.
- **Fecundity.** Moderate. An answer makes askable: what share of accreditation findings would change under blind submission; whether autoverification rules differ in effect between declared and blind material; whether the CLIA PT-referral prohibition has measurable effects on handling.
- **Anchoring.** Touches the commutability literature, the EQA-versus-patient-sample validity debate, and the ledger's own UQ-20261022-06 (repeat runs before release, from middleware logs) — the mechanism limb here is a declared-versus-blind stratification of that entry's instrument.

**Score: Significance 2/3.** Loses a point because its consequence was argued from a false premise about the absence of any number, and because the genuinely novel residue (mechanism from logs) rests on an unverified premise about what middleware retains.

## Reason for silence

Not silence — **dormancy, with a governance/incentive mechanism.** (a) Disciplinary boundary: the two own-field precedents sit in *public-health laboratory surveillance* (AJPH 1982) and *blood-lead toxicology* (Clin Chem 2001), literatures that routine clinical chemistry and accreditation science do not routinely read, which is exactly why the design looks like a foreign transplant from inside the candidate's field — the intake's own framing is the mechanism's evidence. (b) Incentive and conflict-of-interest gap: the parties able to run a blind scheme (EQA providers, accreditors) are the parties whose product's validity it would test. (c) Legal chill around PT handling specifically: the CLIA prohibition on PT referral makes laboratories legally exposed on anything to do with proficiency specimens, which discourages experiments that deliberately blur PT and patient material. (d) Identity and billing integrity: submitting non-patient material under a patient-route requisition collides with specimen-labelling and billing rules, which is the practical obstacle Boone et al. already flagged in recommending blind surveys only "where logistics allow". These are mechanisms, not "no one thought of it" — and they explain a 25-year gap after a published positive result better than they explain an absence of asking.
