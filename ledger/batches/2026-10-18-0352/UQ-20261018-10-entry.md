Question (canonical): For one declared national installed base of static residential electricity meters, taking a probability sample stratified by meter model, firmware revision and build year from a distribution utility's asset register, and applying a pre-declared library of residential load mixes synthesised from a published appliance current-waveform set against a reference instrument whose immunity to those same stimuli is demonstrated rather than assumed: what is the population-weighted distribution of relative registration error per mix (mean with CI, plus lower and upper tail quantiles), and, under a declared national load-composition model, what fraction of billed residential energy does that distribution imply is misregistered? (v2; the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation of v1, which asked the same thing without a sampling frame, a declared mix library, a demonstrated-immune reference, or the conditional status of the billed-energy limb.)
Alternative formulations:
1. Do the waveforms used in meter type approval and periodic sample testing represent the loads meters actually see, and what does the installed base register under realistic household load mixes?
2. Is there any population-level estimate of static-meter over- and under-registration, as opposed to laboratory demonstrations that large errors can occur?
Motivating gap: The field is dense with how-to-answer apparatus (IEC 62052/62053, EN 50470, utility periodic sample testing, verification procedures) and appears empty of the population value.
