## Restatement (my own words)

Take people using a commercial closed-loop insulin system that tunes itself. Every so often a human — the patient or the clinic — reaches in and edits the system's settings (basal profile, correction factor, carb ratio, glucose target, activity mode). Meanwhile the algorithm is also changing what it does on its own. The question asks: using the vendor's cloud logs, how much does the human edit buy, *net of* what the algorithm would have done anyway over the same window, measured as the same person's own time-in-range (70–180) and time-below-54 at 2, 4 and 12 weeks after the edit versus comparable person-time with no edit — and which setting classes repay editing. The whole thing hinges on one arithmetic image: subtract the controller's own adaptation from the observed post-edit improvement. So the question needs three things from the logs: the edit, with a timestamp; who made it; and a *quantity* standing for the controller's concurrent adaptation. The third is the only thing that distinguishes it from a question already published.

## Document-kind check (the one the Gate 0–3 record declared NOT RUN)

The candidate conditions on a kind of document: a cloud log that timestamps controller-side adaptation and human settings edits, with edit authorship. Per the standing rule I opened three actual instances of that kind. **Zero of the three supply what the question conditions on**, and they fail in three different ways.

**Instance 1 — Tidepool `pumpSettings` data type. READ LEVEL: full page.**
REGIME: document kind = open third-party aggregator's device-data model, ingesting Medtronic, Tandem and Insulet pumps; unit of analysis = one `pumpSettings` object per upload; instrument = pump upload via vendor cloud (CareLink for Medtronic); operating condition = routine ambulatory upload; threshold/category = data availability, not a glycaemic endpoint.
Reconstruction from 9 ordered consecutive fragments returned by the summariser (assembled only from consecutive returns; no gap filled by inference):
> "Most insulin delivery devices do not, unfortunately, keep a historical record of all insulin pump settings" / "whenever a settings change is made."
> "When ingesting data from Medtronic insulin pumps through the CareLink cloud service," / "Tidepool has been able to build the full pump settings history from a combination of:" / "current pump settings records (at time of upload to CareLink); and records of changes to particular settings."
> "For Medtronic devices, the time on a pump settings object represents the time at which the settings" / "became effective." / "For all other devices, the time on each pump settings object represents the time when the settings were" / "read by the insulin pump."
Full-page read also establishes: the object is a snapshot "representing insulin pump settings at a given point in time," that time "usually the time of a data upload from the device"; and **no field anywhere on the page records who made a change, or whether a change was human or algorithmic.**
What this does to the question: for every non-Medtronic pump — which includes Omnipod 5, one of the two profile-adapting systems the record itself says the question is only meaningful on — the export carries **no edit timestamp at all**, only an upload-time snapshot. P1 is therefore not merely unverified, it is **false for part of the eligible population**, and the failing clause is a statement about the *devices* ("most insulin delivery devices do not … keep a historical record"), not an artefact of Tidepool's pipeline: if the device never recorded the change, no vendor cloud downstream can recover its time either.

**Instance 2 — Tidepool `basal` / `automated` data type. READ LEVEL: full page.**
REGIME: same document kind and instrument; unit = one interval of automated delivery; condition = closed-loop operation; category = delivered insulin, units/hour.
The type records "intervals of basal insulin delivery triggered by the pump itself," with `rate` = "the amount of insulin delivered in units per hour," a duration, an optional expected duration, and a `suppressed` scheduled-basal object. It records **delivery**, not the controller's adapted *parameters*. Nothing on the page exposes a learned basal profile, an adapted total-daily-dose term, or any other parameter trajectory.
What this does to the question: the candidate's distinguishing limb — "over and above the adaptation the system performed during the same period" — needs the controller's adaptation as a quantity. In the one openable data model, the adaptation is **not exported as a quantity in any form**; only its delivery consequence is. This is the ledger's published-is-not-performed failure one step earlier: the quantity is not even *defined* in the instrument, let alone measured.

**Instance 3 — Glooko Web 26.2 / Mobile 6.16 release (vendor-adjacent cloud, newest surface). READ LEVEL: full page.**
REGIME: document kind = commercial diabetes-data platform release describing what it transmits to an EHR; unit = the patient's current pump configuration; condition = routine clinic workflow; category = configuration fields, no endpoint.
"Pump profile settings can be transmitted into EHR flowsheets as structured information rather than static PDFs" — basal schedules, carb ratios, sensitivity factors, target ranges, closed-loop status, active insulin time, explicitly replacing "device portals, PDFs, or screenshots." **No change history, no attribution of who changed a setting, no record of algorithmic adaptation.** The flagship 2026 product for getting settings out of the cloud ships *current configuration only* — which is also the regime of the record's prior 2 (settings in force at user level, 176,405 users), and is exactly not the candidate's regime.

Net: three instances, three failure modes — no change history on most devices (1), no adaptation quantity anywhere (2), current-configuration-only as the state of the commercial art (3). P2 (authorship) finds support in none of the three. P6 (logged settings are the settings in force) is undercut by instance 1's own distinction between "became effective" (Medtronic) and "read by the pump" (everyone else).

## Recommended verdict

**Rejected** — Unresolved on attack 4 (presupposition failure): the question's conditioning clause requires the controller's own adaptation as a logged quantity, and on three opened instances of the document kind no export represents it at all, while human-edit timestamps exist only for Medtronic-sourced data and edit authorship exists nowhere; strip that clause and the question is the one already published at the candidate's unit and endpoint.
