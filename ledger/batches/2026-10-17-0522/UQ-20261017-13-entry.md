Alternative formulations: Do spatial-transcriptomics conclusions survive a change of support, in the sense geostatistics means it? / What is the modifiable-areal-unit sensitivity of published spatial-omics analyses, and can a support-stability criterion be defined for them?

Verification (post-batch, fresh-context checker, log UQ-20261017-13-verification-comber.md): the rejection stands and the routing row holds, with one claim softened — the "two-rung comparison" is a single illustrative example inside a methods paper, not a study of support-dependence.
**The posing sentence is verbatim.** Comber, Zormpas, Queen & Cockell (2024), "Lessons from spatial transcriptomics and computational geography in mapping the transcriptome" (AGILE-GISS 5, 21), contains the string exactly, in the PDF body at Section 3, Example 1 — not on the abstract page, which matters for anyone re-checking it: "The choice of aggregation unit will influence process understanding through scale (MAUP) driven distortions but this is completely unknown and ignored." The preceding sentence fixes what "this" refers to: "Sub-cellular observations are aggregated in different ways (grid, image-segmentation etc) to at least cell level." Section 6 adds the general form: "Despite the MAUP being a core consideration in all analyses of spatial data, its effects are rarely tested for". It is a short position piece: it runs no analysis, tests nothing, and proposes testing as future work. That is precisely the asked-and-still-open shape the routing file takes.
**The second mention is confirmed and also untested.** STExplorer (bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.01.17.633539) repeats the MAUP concern in its introduction — "high-resolution platforms, like Stereo-seq or Visium-HD, may introduce artificial ecotones by binning data" — and runs no test of support-dependence: its analyses use Visium spots only, Visium HD and Stereo-seq appear in background, and the only varied parameter is the GWPCA/GWR bandwidth, with no sensitivity analysis reported.
**The third claim is softened.** arXiv:2602.02825 (Su, Fung, Wang, Yang, Knowles, Rabadan, "On the consistent and scalable detection of spatial patterns") does compare 8 um against 16 um Visium HD — "comprising 164,800 bins at 16 um resolution, and 656,788 bins at 8 um" — but the comparison appears only in the full HTML text, not the abstract, and is one qualitative example: per-gene spatial-variability Z-scores at the two supports plotted against the identity line, with outliers picked out, including that "the 'Swiss roll' pattern introduced during sample preparation was preferentially detected at 8 um resolution" and attributed to "signal dampening at the 16 um Nyquist limit". The entry's "has already run a two-rung 8-vs-16 um comparison" overstates it: it is a cross-resolution sanity check inside a paper whose purpose is a consistent, scalable spatial-pattern test, with no noise floor. The rejection does not depend on the stronger reading — the ground is that the question is posed in print and unanswered, not that it is answered.
**The gap the row claims is intact.** No published systematic measurement of how spatially-variable-gene calls or spatial-domain partitions change across a ladder of aggregation supports, with a within-support resampling noise floor, was found. That negative is bounded and recorded as such: 7 searches, 6 fetches, no SVG-method benchmark opened, and PMC, Europe PMC and PubMed all unavailable.
