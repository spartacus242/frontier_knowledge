## Gate 0 — Intake (as received)

Question (canonical, V1 as intaken): In users of commercial automated insulin delivery systems whose cloud logs record both the controller's own adaptation and every human change to its settings, what is the measured within-person effect of a clinician- or user-initiated settings change on subsequent glycaemic outcomes, over and above the adaptation the system performed during the same period?

Alternative formulations:
- When the algorithm is already retuning itself, how much does a human changing its settings add — and for which settings?
- Using automated-insulin-delivery device logs as the data source and one pre-declared design (target-trial emulation, or a regression discontinuity at visit dates), what is the effect of a settings-change event on time in range at 2, 4 and 12 weeks relative to matched no-change person-time, conditional on the controller's concurrent adaptation?

Motivating gap (as intaken, and **now refuted in its strong form** — see Gate 2): the intake asserted that "the outcome literature compares AID against non-AID therapy, or compares follow-up intensity as an undifferentiated bundle," and that the separable quantity "became measurable only once controllers began logging their own adaptation alongside timestamped human edits for very large user populations." The intake itself flagged that NO web query had been run for this candidate and that the gap statement was "a Gate-0 conjecture, not a checked absence." Gate 2 checked it and found the headline quantity measured and published at the candidate's own unit and endpoint in 2021 (O'Malley et al., below), including a per-settings-class breakdown. The conjecture was wrong.

REGIME of the candidate (as intaken): unit of analysis = one settings-change event within one person; population = commercial AID users with consented cloud logs; instrument = vendor device logs plus CGM; outcome category = consensus CGM metrics; condition = routine ambulatory care, not a trial protocol; threshold = TIR 70–180 mg/dL and TBR <54 mg/dL at pre-declared horizons (2/4/12 weeks).

Presuppositions (as intaken, with Gate 1 adjudication):
- P1. Vendor logs distinguish controller adaptation from human edits, with timestamps. **CONTESTED and UNVERIFIED — see the document-kind check below. This presupposition is a claim about a kind of document and I opened ZERO instances of that kind.**
- P2. The author of an edit (clinician vs user) is recorded or inferable. CONTESTED; the unattributable share must be reported, not assumed away. Not checked (no instance opened).
- P3. Settings changes are not wholly explained by concurrent events that independently move glucose (illness, pregnancy, device or insulin change, school/work transitions). CONTESTED — confounding by indication is the central identification threat.
- P4. Consensus CGM metrics (TIR 70–180, TBR <54) are adequate endpoints at the stated horizons. Established by published consensus (not re-verified here; it is not load-bearing for the verdict).
- P5. Sufficient matched no-change person-time exists within the same individuals. Likely; unverified.
- P6. Logged settings are the settings in force (no silent vendor-side defaults or firmware-driven changes). CONTESTED; firmware updates must be modelled as exposure, not noise.

**Document-kind check — NOT RUN, and it bounds the whole question.** P1/P2/P6 are conditional on vendor cloud exports behaving a certain way (a log that separately timestamps controller adaptation and human edits, with edit authorship). Per the standing rule, such a premise is not established until two or three ACTUAL instances of the document kind have been opened. I opened none: the fetch budget (5) was spent on the prior-art reads. So the vendor-by-vendor feasibility audit that the intake correctly calls "step zero" remains entirely unperformed here, and nothing in this record should be read as having established that any vendor's log supports the netting the question requires. I note one substantive reason for doubt that does not need a fetch: the system in the killing prior (Control-IQ) does not retune its own personal profile at all — it modulates delivery against a user-set profile — so for that system the "adaptation the system performed" term is close to vacuous, and the candidate's distinguishing limb is only meaningful on profile-adapting controllers (MiniMed 780G auto-basal, Omnipod 5 adaptive basal). That makes the candidate's eligible population much narrower than "commercial AID users" and makes the feasibility audit decisive rather than preliminary.

## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

V1 states what would count as an answer but leaves the load-bearing phrase "over and above the adaptation the system performed during the same period" without an estimand: it names no quantification of the controller's adaptation, no role for that quantity in the model (covariate? mediator? competing exposure?), and no identification strategy for confounding by indication. One reformulation is permitted; both versions are recorded.

Question (V2, the single permitted Gate 1 reformulation, for well-posedness only):
> In adult and paediatric users of a **profile-adapting** commercial AID system (MiniMed 780G-class auto-basal or Omnipod 5-class adaptive basal), in routine ambulatory care, using vendor cloud logs that timestamp both controller-side adaptation and human settings edits with recorded or inferable authorship: under one design and one adaptation metric pre-declared before any data pull (target-trial emulation with matched no-change person-time, or regression discontinuity at visit dates), what is the within-person effect on TIR 70–180 mg/dL and TBR <54 mg/dL at 2, 4 and 12 weeks of a human settings-change event of each class (basal rate/weight, correction factor, glucose target, carbohydrate ratio, activity mode), estimated with the controller's concurrent adaptation over the same window entered as a pre-declared covariate, with at least one negative-control outcome, and reported alongside (a) the fraction of change events whose author could not be identified and (b) the fraction of the eligible population whose logs cannot separate adaptation from edits?

V2 is the version scored. It is well-posed in the weak sense (an answer is describable) but not in the strong sense: the estimand is specifiable only once "the controller's concurrent adaptation" is given a metric, and no source I opened supplies one, so a pre-declaration remains to be written rather than merely executed. Confounding by indication is named and a design is offered, but neither design is shown to identify the effect here. It is a question, not a thesis: the intake states explicitly that a null is a publishable answer, and a null is in fact what the nearest prior reports.

Well-posedness: **2/3**.

## Gate 3 — Significance

- **Consequence.** If a human edit buys a measurable increment on top of an adapting controller, settings review is the active ingredient of a visit and its frequency and content become optimisable; if it buys nothing within a pre-declared equivalence band, the routine titration visit is being paid for as a therapeutic act while functioning as a monitoring act, and visit cadence, remote-monitoring programmes and reimbursement codes for titration all come under question. That consequence is real — but it is **substantially discounted by prior 1**, which already reports the overall null in its own regime, so the decision-relevant news would be confined to the adaptive-controller/routine-care regime and to the subgroups O'Malley's own hedge points at (higher HbA1c; following user-directed boluses).
- **Audience.** Diabetes-technology clinicians and paediatric endocrinology services designing follow-up; AID manufacturers deciding how much profile authority to leave to users; payers pricing titration visits; ADA/ISPAD/ATTD consensus writers. The audience is specific and would act — this is not a hypothetical constituency, and the existence of an industry podcast episode and a vendor clinician guide on exactly "settings and time in range" (returned at queries 13 and 2) shows the question is already commercially live.
- **Fecundity.** Which settings classes repay editing; whether edit value declines as controllers adapt better; whether remote-monitoring-driven edits differ from in-visit edits; how to define a controller-adaptation metric that is comparable across vendors (a prerequisite this record shows nobody has supplied).
- **Anchoring.** Touches O'Malley's unresolved subgroup hedge; the open design question of how to do causal inference on irregularly observed device logs; and the policy question of what a titration visit is for.
- **Significance: 2/3** — consequential and with a real audience that would act, but the marginal informational value is reduced by the published null, and the whole question is bounded by an unperformed vendor feasibility audit.

## Reason for silence

For the headline question there is **no silence to explain** — it was asked and answered in 2021, which is itself the finding of this Gate 2 and the reason the intake's gap conjecture failed. For the residual refinement, a credible mechanism exists and is instrumental plus disciplinary: profile-adapting controllers that log their own adaptation alongside timestamped human edits reached large populations only recently (780G-class auto-basal from 2020, Omnipod 5-class adaptive basal from 2022), the logs sit in vendor clouds behind commercial access rather than in investigator-held trial databases, and the method that would own the estimand (target-trial emulation with irregular, informative observation times) belongs to a different discipline from the clinicians who hold the data. That mechanism explains a two-to-four-year lag in the refined version; it does not explain, and is not offered as explaining, an absence in the headline version, because there is none.
