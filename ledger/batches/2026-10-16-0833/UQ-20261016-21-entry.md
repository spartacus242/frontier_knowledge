Domain(s): Medicine — randomised-trial methodology; medical-device regulatory science; measurement/metrology in clinical research

Question (canonical): For a pre-specified frame of completed multicentre RCTs whose primary outcome is generated or titrated by a software-containing measurement device named by model: (i) in what fraction can a firmware or calibration-algorithm revision dated inside the enrolment window be established from a pre-specified ordered route list (manufacturer release notes/service bulletins, FDA recall/MAUDE, EU field-safety notices, direct manufacturer enquiry, the trial report), reported explicitly as a lower bound; and (ii) in the subset with a datable revision and recoverable IPD, what is the treatment-effect-by-device-era interaction on the trial's own effect scale, benchmarked against the trial's own pre-specified MCID (fallback: half-width of its primary 95% CI), with era/calendar-time collinearity reported so non-estimable trials are counted as non-estimable rather than null?
Gate 1 reformulation note: Gate 0 as-submitted version and the single permitted Gate 1 reformulation are both on the record; the above is the reformulation. Full text: batches/2026-10-16-0833/UQ-20261016-21-record.md

Alternative formulations:
 - Do randomised trials silently pool incommensurable measurements because the devices generating their endpoints were re-calibrated by their manufacturers mid-trial?
 - For device-mediated RCTs, is "firmware era" an unmeasured effect modifier large enough to change what the trial concluded?

Motivating gap:
Anchor (both primaries opened at Gate 2): Johnston et al., Arch Dis Child Fetal Neonatal Ed 96(6):F429 (2011) found a Masimo SET calibration-curve artefact ("Values above 87% were elevated by up to 2%"), removed by "board firmware V4.8 or higher", warning it "could affect interpretation of oxygen-targeting trials"; BOOST II, NEJM 2013 (10.1056/NEJMoa1302298): "Halfway through the trials, the oximeter-calibration algorithm was revised", mortality 23.1% vs 15.9% in the revised-algorithm cohort, heterogeneity by algorithm era P = 0.006. The record's gap claim was that no one had asked how often such a revision is establishable across device-mediated RCTs, nor whether the era interaction exceeds an MCID "anywhere other than in the trial that happened to notice". Gate 4 falsified the second limb of that claim and narrowed the first (see adversarial log).

Presuppositions:
 P1 device model and enrolment window retrievable — partly true; non-naming share declared as denominator attrition (measured at 6/22 in wearables by Khattak 2024).
 P2 LOAD-BEARING, CONTESTED: non-recall firmware/calibration revision dates retrievable by third parties; BOOST-II obtained its date because the trialists were also the device investigators, which is not available retrospectively.
 P3 CONTESTED: IPD recoverable for a non-trivial subset — unquantified.
 P4 a software revision changes the RECORDED endpoint value — Gate 4 showed this is mis-stated for the anchor: BOOST-II's endpoint was death/disability, and the revision changed the titration variable, not the recorded endpoint.
 P5 an MCID exists to benchmark against — partly true; CI-half-width fallback declared.
 P6 device era not fully collinear with calendar time — must be checked per trial; NeOProM's software strata are confounded with trial identity (SUPPORT and BOOST NZ wholly original-algorithm).
 UNDECLARED AND NEWLY NAMED BY TWO ADVERSARIES: that a release date identifies a per-patient device era. It does not — fleet roll-out is asynchronous; NeOProM needed trial-internal logs and a 74-infant "mixed exposure" category.
 Not presupposed: that any revision changed any trial's conclusion outside the index case.

What would count as an answer:
A pre-registered census giving per-trial establishability with retrieval route recorded and declared a lower bound, plus era-stratified effect estimates with a pre-specified interaction test, collinearity diagnostic and MCID (or declared fallback) comparison in the recoverable subset. A null would be an equally complete answer. Not an answer: commentary that it "could" happen (Johnston 2011 already is that); a retelling of the oximeter case (BOOST-II 2013 and NeOProM 2018 already are that).

Strongest remaining objection:
 **Limb (ii) presupposes a mechanism that cannot operate for the commonest outcome type, and its benchmark is statistically unsound as written.** A4 worked the cases against a primary source and labelled its own arithmetic. Where the device GENERATES a continuous endpoint and the revision is an additive shift, the between-arm difference is identical in both eras — Nab et al. 2019 §2.1.2, "the difference between the two treatment groups is unbiased" — so the interaction is exactly zero and pooling eras is harmless; under a gain change the interaction is (b₁−b₀)·Δ*, which needs a near-100% gain change to reach an MCID. The index case is not an endpoint-generating device at all: BOOST II's revision changed the intervention delivered ("clearer separation between the two target groups"), i.e. a TITRATION device, a different causal structure the question conflates with endpoint generation. So the "incommensurable pooled measurements" framing must be dropped for continuous endpoints, and the mechanism survives only for titration devices and threshold-defined endpoints. Second, within a trial era is a step function of calendar time ("used for all infants who were subsequently enrolled"), so treatment × era is unidentified from treatment × period and the proposed collinearity diagnostic cannot repair it — NeOProM's own authors remain in "considerable debate" over whether the software change caused their result. Third, benchmarking an interaction POINT ESTIMATE against the MCID, when an interaction's standard error is about twice the main effect's (Brookes 2004 inflation factor 4), yields by A4's arithmetic roughly 16% false exceedances under the null at 80% main-effect power, and roughly 33% under the proposed half-CI-width fallback. Compounding all of it, limb (ii) is already published for the index device class (Askie 2018), so the candidate stands or falls on limb (i).
