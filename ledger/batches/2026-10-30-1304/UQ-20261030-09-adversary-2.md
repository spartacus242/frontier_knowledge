## Restatement in my own words

Take a human pancreatic cancer that was cut out after the patient had been given gemcitabine-based chemotherapy. Some tumour cells in that block are still alive; others have been killed and replaced by treatment-effect tissue. Bacteria also live inside such blocks, in patches. The question: if you put both channels on the same section and measure distances at the 10–100 µm scale, do the still-living tumour cells sit nearer to bacteria-occupied patches than chance allows, and does the local amount of bacteria grade the local odds that a tumour cell survived — after you account for how much necrosis, how hypoxic, and how far from a vessel that spot is? The mechanistic reason to expect yes is that bacteria carrying a long-isoform cytidine deaminase chew gemcitabine into an inactive metabolite, so a bacterial patch would be a local drug shadow.
