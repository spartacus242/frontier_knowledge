Question (canonical): Within a single pathology archive, for one diagnosis held fixed by re-review of every era's slides under one contemporary protocol and by re-staining from the block, and on one pre-specified molecular panel assayed in a single batch-randomised run: (a) what is the accession-era effect on the tumour's molecular profile across >=3 eras, (b) what is the block-age/fixation-era artefact estimated from era-matched internal controls (paired non-neoplastic tissue from the same blocks; same-era blocks of other diagnoses), and (c) does the era effect in (a), net of (b) and reported with its interval, exceed the between-cohort molecular distance that external- and historical-control borrowing already tolerates? "No era effect distinguishable from artefact at achievable sample size" is a pre-specified admissible answer. (R1, the one Gate 1 reformulation, adopted.)
Alternative formulations: (V0, as intaken) Now that decades-old FFPE blocks are profilable, is the molecular phenotype of a histologically and immunophenotypically identical diagnosis constant across accession eras, or has it drifted enough that historical patients are not biologically exchangeable with contemporary ones? (lay) Is a 1975 cancer the same disease, on the inside, as a 2020 cancer a pathologist would give the same name? (trial-methods) Is the exchangeability assumption behind historical and external control arms testable at the molecular level in archival tissue, and does it survive the test?
Motivating gap: Historical/external controls and Bayesian dynamic-borrowing priors justify exchangeability from clinical covariates; the molecular layer went unchecked because archival material was treated as unreadable, a constraint now removed (Osaki 2026 decade-resolved FFPE proteomics 1972-2022; Krieger 2018 CNV back to the 1980s). Known secular findings (stage/grade migration, Will Rogers, subtype-mix shifts) are all between-class; this asks whether the inside of a fixed class changed. (i) THE FATAL GROUND IS CONFIRMED. No published tolerance threshold for historical or external control borrowing is expressed as a MOLECULAR or biomarker DISTANCE — NOT FOUND. Published tolerances are denominated in other units entirely: calendar recency, outcome-parameter drift, and covariate balance. So limb (c)'s comparator is a construct the study would have to invent, which is what attack 4 says.
(ii) BUT THE ADVERSARY'S SUPPORTING FIGURE IS STRUCK. There is no published 2-to-7-year recency limit — NOT FOUND. The only numeric recency rule of thumb located in a primary source is hedged and indication-specific: "more than say 10 years old for docetaxel in this indication should be avoided". Two further precision points belong with it. The per-year drift figures in Hatswell et al. (0.3% ORR, 0.5% PFS, 0.9% OS) and the "5% better than historical controls" figure are OBSERVED MAGNITUDES, not tolerances anyone proposed as a bound, and treating them as thresholds would overstate the literature. And Pocock's recency criterion is qualitative ("recent", "roughly the same time period"), with no numeric cutoff on covariate balance at all — no standardised mean difference, no propensity-overlap bound — so any claim that borrowing has an established quantitative covariate-balance tolerance is unsupported.
(iii) TWO OF THE THREE "CLOSEST OWN-FIELD PAPERS" ARE NOT PRIORS. Chaturvedi et al. is identified exactly as "Human Papillomavirus and Rising Oropharyngeal Cancer Incidence in the United States", J Clin Oncol 29(32):4294-4301 (2011), and it is the excluded prevalence-trend case: its estimand is the trend in HPV prevalence across four calendar periods and the annual percent change in incidence by HPV status, with the marker defining the subtype whose frequency is tracked, and no era-matched or same-era ageing control in the readable text. Its settling sentence: "Trends in HPV prevalence across four calendar periods were estimated by using logistic regression." The "Western Poland" item is Majewski et al. 2026, Front Oncol 16:1651941, read IN FULL, and it is not the candidate's design either.
(iv) AND MAJEWSKI CARRIES THE CANDIDATE'S OWN ARTEFACT, UNRAISED. It is misdescribed by the shorthand "two-decade comparison": it compares two five-year windows, 1998-2002 and 2016-2020, about eighteen years apart. Its older cohort's blocks were roughly 24-28 years old at analysis against 6-10 years for the newer, and antigen decay in older blocks biases Ki-67 downward in the 1998-2002 arm — the same direction as its headline result, Ki-67 higher in 2016-2020 at p = 0.02. That is precisely the artefact this candidate's question is about, present in a published comparison that never raises it. Ninety-two patients across the two periods, confirmed.
