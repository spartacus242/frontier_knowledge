Question (canonical — Gate 0 intake version, recorded, not used for grading):
For training corpora distributed as URL manifests rather than as content (LAION-2B/5B and Re-LAION, DataComp CommonPool, YFCC100M, OBELICS), is the subset still retrievable at a later date a *selective* rather than random thinning of the original manifest — selective on covariates the manifest itself records (host/domain, language, caption length, licence field, aesthetic and NSFW scores, pool shard) — and, at fixed training compute, does a model trained on the retrievable-today subset differ from one trained on a size-matched random subsample of the original manifest by more than the seed-to-seed difference on the dataset's own benchmark suite?

Question (canonical — Gate 1 reformulation, ONE permitted, this is it; both versions recorded):
Fix a corpus C from {LAION-2B-en, Re-LAION-2B-en, DataComp CommonPool-small/medium, YFCC100M, OBELICS}, pinned to a named archived manifest revision (HF repo commit hash) with publication date t0, and a resolution date T. Define RETRIEVABLE(row, T) operationally as: HTTP 200 within 3 retries over 14 days, Content-Type an image (or HTML parseable to the recorded media for OBELICS), bytes decodable, and not matching a pre-registered perceptual-hash blacklist of parked-domain / placeholder / "image removed" sentinels — with the soft-404 residual bounded by human audit of a 2,000-row subsample and reported as a measurement-error interval on every retention figure, not hidden inside it.
(i) SELECTIVITY: on a pre-registered simple random sample of N = 200,000 manifest rows per corpus, estimate retention ratios with 95% CIs for each manifest-recorded covariate (registrable-domain, TLD, language field, caption length, licence field, aesthetic score decile, NSFW/p_unsafe decile, width/height, shard index), and report the out-of-fold discrimination (AUC) and calibration of a retention model built on those covariates alone, against a pre-registered null band for AUC. MNAR on unobservables is out of reach by construction and is declared, not finessed.
(ii) MATERIALITY: on a corpus for which a byte-level archive of the t0 content exists (so that both arms are trainable — see P7), at a fixed-compute track, train k >= 5 seeds on the retrievable-at-T subset and k >= 5 seeds on a size-matched simple random subsample of the SAME archived manifest, and compare the task-suite average against an equivalence band fixed in advance at +/- 2 SD of the within-arm seed-to-seed spread. Outside the band = material; inside = immaterial, band reported.

Alternative formulations:
1. Has a dataset cited by name stopped naming a fixed training distribution — do two labs that each "train on LAION-2B" years apart train on measurably different data and get measurably different models?
2. Is link attrition in URL-distributed corpora ignorable given what the manifest observes, and how much of the gap between an original published result and its later reproduction does the attrition account for?
3. (equivalent, in the neighbour's words) Do URL-distributed training corpora suffer *persistence bias* in Elmas's sense — is retrospective collection a biased re-sample — and does the bias propagate to a trained model rather than only to a descriptive statistic?

Motivating gap (CORRECTED at Gate 2; the intake version overstated the silence):
These corpora are published as lists of URLs, so the artifact a lab obtains is whatever the open web still serves on download day, while papers, model cards and licences cite the dataset as a fixed object of a fixed size. Attrition is large, dated and documented: DataComp users reported ~91% download success at CommonPool-small in May 2023 and ~88.3% for DataComp-1B in July 2023 (mlfoundations/datacomp issues #3, #41), and Hong et al. measured 21.4% of 12.8M CommonPool image URLs failing to download in April 2025. Manifest edits are dated too: LAION withdrew LAION-5B in late December 2023 after the Stanford Internet Observatory report of 2023-12-19 and republished Re-LAION-5B on 2024-08-30, removing 2,236 hash-matched links and, in the released variants, 1.121% (65M of 5.8B) or 3.044% (176M of 5.8B) of samples. So the attrition magnitude limb is NOT unmeasured (presupposition P3 below is now SUPPORTED, roughly 9% at ~0 months to 21.4% at ~2 years for CommonPool), and the selectivity question has been asked once, in passing: Hong et al. compared 1,000 failed against 1,000 successful CommonPool URLs on size, face count and CLIP similarity and called the differences "slight". What remains unasked is the question as a question: selectivity as an estimand over the manifest's own covariates with CIs and discrimination, across the corpus family, and — found nowhere in any vocabulary tried — whether the attrition reaches a trained model at fixed compute against a seed-to-seed equivalence band.

Presuppositions (status after Gate 1 and Gate 2):
P1. The original manifests are archived and retrievable in as-published form. SUPPORTED for LAION-2B/Re-LAION/DataComp/YFCC100M/OBELICS as HF-hosted parquet with revision history; the Gate 1 reformulation pins a commit hash so the claim is checkable per corpus. Caveat recorded: LAION-5B's original manifest was withdrawn in Dec 2023, so the as-published LAION-5B artefact may now be obtainable only from third-party mirrors — unverified in this session.
P2. Retention is measurable from HTTP status alone. CONTESTED, and conceded: soft-404s, parked domains, hotlink-blocking and placeholder images make "retrievable" a judgement call. The reformulation repairs this by defining retrievability content-side and carrying an audited measurement-error interval; Hong et al.'s own failure taxonomy (35.4% broken/dead, 19.0% permission-denied) shows the categories are separable in practice.
P3. Attrition is large enough for the selectivity question to be non-trivial. Was UNMEASURED at intake; now SUPPORTED — 21.4% of CommonPool URLs failed in April 2025 (published), ~9-12% in 2023 (user reports). Limb (ii) is not dead on arrival on magnitude grounds.
P4. A fixed-compute small/medium track probes materiality validly. CONTESTED and left contested: an effect could be absent at small scale and present at frontier scale or vice versa. The answer is scale-indexed, which the reformulation states rather than hides. Prior art is adverse here: in UQ-20261016-38's kill paper an 8% compliance-driven token loss produced no detectable general-benchmark gap at 1.5B/100B tokens, so a null at small scale should be expected and the band must be reported for the result to mean anything.
P5. The dataset's own benchmark suite registers a compositional shift of this size. CONTESTED. DataComp's 38-task suite was built to rank filtering strategies, i.e. to be sensitive to composition, which is mild support; it has no published seed-to-seed variance table, which is why the band must be measured in-experiment rather than assumed.
P6. Manifest covariates are accurate enough to regress retention on. PARTIALLY CONTESTED, unchanged: aesthetic and NSFW scores are themselves model outputs (and the LAION-Aesthetics predictor has itself been audited, arXiv:2601.09896, found in search 12), so a retention ratio across aesthetic deciles is a statement about a scorer's deciles, not about beauty. Domain, TLD, shard and caption length are not model outputs and carry the limb if the scores are dropped.
P7. (ADDED AT GATE 1 — the substantive well-posedness defect in the intake version.) Limb (ii) as intaken is not executable: you cannot train on the arm that is defined by being unretrievable. A size-matched random subsample of the ORIGINAL manifest contains rows whose bytes no longer exist, so the comparison requires a byte-level archive of the t0 content, not merely of the manifest. This is true of YFCC100M (a full content mirror is widely believed to exist — NOT VERIFIED in this session) and false by design of LAION, which distributes links precisely so as not to host content. CONTESTED and load-bearing: if no corpus in the family has both a rich manifest and an archived content copy, limb (ii) is unanswerable as posed and the question collapses to limb (i) plus a simulation. The reformulation makes the archive an explicit precondition rather than an assumption.

What would count as an answer:
(i) Per-covariate retention ratios with CIs on a pre-registered N = 200k sample per corpus, plus out-of-fold AUC and a calibration curve for a retention model on manifest covariates. A calibrated, well-discriminating model establishes selectivity on observables; an AUC inside the pre-registered null band establishes the opposite. MNAR on unobservables declared out of reach.
(ii) k >= 5 seeds per arm at a fixed-compute track, retrievable-at-T subset vs size-matched random subsample of the same archived manifest, compared on the dataset's standard suite against a band set at +/- 2 SD of the within-arm seed spread. Outside the band = yes; inside = no, with the band stated. A result that cannot separate the two is a failed experiment, not an answer.

Significance — consequence:
If YES (selective and material): "trained on LAION-2B" stops denoting a distribution and starts denoting a distribution-indexed-by-date, which breaks three things at once — reproduction of published results (a failed replication becomes uninterpretable without the download date), the DataComp-style leaderboard premise that entrants compete on filtering over a fixed pool (they are in fact competing over different pools, with later entrants holding thinner and differently-shaped ones, which is exactly the fairness worry raised in datacomp issue #3 and never studied), and the model-card/licence practice of naming a dataset as a fixed object. It would also make the download date a required reporting field, and would motivate publishers to ship content-hash manifests plus archived mirrors rather than bare URLs.
If NO (retention near-random on observables, or selective but immaterial at the tested compute): the result is worth as much. It licenses URL-manifest distribution as a reproducibility-preserving format at the scales tested, converts attrition from a validity threat into a sample-size cost, and gives dataset documentation a number to cite instead of a hedge. The equivalence band itself becomes a reusable instrument: nobody has published the seed-to-seed spread of the DataComp suite at a fixed-compute track, and every future claim of the form "filtering strategy X beats Y by 0.4 points" needs it.
The honest discount: prior A found "slight" differences and prior art in the neighbouring ledger entry (UQ-20261016-38's kill paper) found an 8% composition change producing no detectable general-benchmark gap at 1.5B/100B tokens. The modal outcome is a measurable selectivity on observables (domain concentration alone almost guarantees some) with an immaterial model effect at small scale — which is a publishable answer, but a quiet one.

(a) Dataset publishers who distribute manifests — LAION e.V., the DataComp/mlfoundations consortium, HuggingFace dataset maintainers, the Spawning/PD12M group (whose own HF discussion thread on link rot surfaced in search 4) — who would act by shipping content hashes, dated retention telemetry, and archived mirrors, and by versioning pools by resolution date. (b) Benchmark organisers running fixed-pool competitions, who would act by freezing a distributed content snapshot or by reporting per-entrant pool size and composition. (c) Reproduction and audit teams (the replication literature, model-card reviewers, and the AI-evaluation community) who currently treat "we trained on LAION-2B" as a sufficient data description. (d) Information scientists and digital-preservation researchers, who own the method and would gain their highest-stakes application. The weakness in this list: none of (a)-(c) is obviously willing to pay for limb (ii)'s compute, and (a) has a disincentive to publish a finding that its artefact is unstable.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: the retention-weighted corpus (can IPW on manifest covariates reconstitute the t0 distribution from what still resolves, and does reweighting close the model gap if there is one?); attrition as an adversarial surface (a host that knows its images are in a manifest can decide which ones stay up, making manifest-distributed corpora steerable after publication — a data-poisoning channel that requires no poisoning); the same analysis for text (Common Crawl WARC-pointer corpora, OBELICS's interleaved documents) and for video-ID corpora; the differential-decay-by-licence question (do permissively licensed images outlive restrictively licensed ones, inverting the composition that licence filters were meant to produce?); whether attrition and robots.txt exclusion (UQ-20261016-38) compound or offset on the same domains; and a measurement question for the field — what IS the seed-to-seed spread of the standard multimodal suites, which nobody has published.
Anchoring:
Touches the reproducibility-of-ML-results literature (Cherti et al.'s reproducible scaling laws assume the data are reproducible); the data-exhaustion/scaling debate (if corpora shrink as fast as they grow, the usable-web estimate is a flow, not a stock); the provenance and consent programme (Data Provenance Initiative, Consent in Crisis, arXiv:2511.08637 on consent mechanisms in VLM datasets); the long-standing absence of a sampling frame for the web in corpus linguistics; and the Re-LAION episode itself, which is a documented, dated, deliberate manifest edit sitting unexamined as a natural experiment.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanisms, argued, in rough order of strength):
1. Incentive and ownership gap, with a legal edge. The measurement would be run by whoever holds the manifest, and for them a positive result indicts their own artefact's stability; meanwhile the strongest version of the experiment requires RE-DOWNLOADING content from a corpus (LAION-5B) that was withdrawn in Dec 2023 over CSAM links, which creates real legal exposure for exactly the academic groups best placed to do it. This is the rare case where the deterrent is not indifference but liability, and it is specific to this corpus family and dated to Dec 2023.
2. The question is pre-classified as engineering. The attrition number lives in issue trackers under titles like "the normal success rate and downloading speed?" and is answered as a throughput problem; datacomp issue #3 states the fairness consequence out loud ("the person with more candidate samples can benefit more") and the thread's framing is still how to cope, not what it biases. A quantity that has a folk answer in a tracker does not get asked as a research question.
3. Disciplinary boundary in the direction that matters. The method belongs to information science and survey methodology; the name for the phenomenon in ML ("link rot") belongs to a literature about citations. B shows the conceptual move was made in social-media research in 2018-2023 and it has not crossed into multimodal pretraining — and note that B's field had a reason to make it (their datasets are ID-distributed by platform policy, so attrition is unavoidable and visible to everyone), while ML's manifest distribution is a choice whose cost is borne later and by someone else.
4. Instrument timing, weakest of the four but real. The question needs an as-published manifest with rich covariates (LAION-400M/5B 2021-22, DataComp 2023) AND enough elapsed time for attrition to bite (A's 21.4% is at ~2 years) AND an archived content copy for limb (ii) (P7). The first two only coincided in about 2025; the third may still not hold for any corpus in the family, which would make the silence on limb (ii) partly a silence of impossibility rather than of neglect.
