The Brown case is the sharpest, because nothing in it is misquoted at all. "Cell death by phagocytosis represents the most common form of cell death in the body" is the document's own sentence, word for word. It is also its **unhedged, uncited** formulation; two sentences later the same subsection says "appears to be the most abundant form of cell death in mammals", hedged, scoped to mammals, and citing reference 22 — the author's own earlier review. The abstract gives a third scope. Quoting the strongest of three formulations is not a misquotation and is not honest either.

**Rule: when a source states the same claim more than once, the entry quotes the formulation the source hedged and cited, not the one it did not.**

The figure carries no literature citation (the only parenthetical is "(Figure 3B)", a pointer to the review's own micrograph), is hedged "Imaging of fixed testis samples suggests that", and is stated with no n and no interval. ## The quotation cap is a brief defect, not an environment limit

Two readers in this batch reached opposite conclusions about the same ~125-character cap. One declared full-sentence verbatim reproduction impossible. The other, on Newbold, recovered a **complete six-sentence abstract** by requesting ordered segments and reassembling only from consecutive returns — the junction verified by an overlap ("the nu|mber of apoptotic"), with the sentence's final eight words independently re-requested as a check. Both the key strings arrived **un-primed**, in a neutral sentence-by-sentence transcription, before any fetch had been spent confirming them.

The discipline that makes it sound, and that the next brief should state:

1. Request **ordered consecutive segments**, each short enough to return whole.
2. Assemble only from consecutive returns; **never complete a cut string** from memory or inference.
3. Verify completeness independently — an overlap at the junction, or a word count of the reconstruction against the summariser's own.
4. **Label the result a reconstruction**, and say how many fragments it came from.

A separate technique from the Bursch reader is worth the same treatment: a narrow request for short exact fragments **succeeded where a sentence-by-sentence request was refused**. That is a different, smaller ask, not a rework of a refusal — and the reader was right to say so explicitly rather than quietly re-issuing.
