## Restatement (my own words)

Pick a time window and a set of airspace blocks where GNSS jamming/spoofing is known to have happened, where "known" is established *without* using any aircraft's own GNSS output (regulator bulletins, NOTAMs, spectrum monitoring). Pick matched airspace where it did not happen. Now go into the NWP centres' per-report observation-feedback archives and pull every aircraft-derived wind and temperature report (AMDAR plus the Mode-S EHS / ADS-B surveillance-derived stream) from both sets, and ask three separate empirical questions. (1) Are the observation-minus-background bias and spread worse in the jammed blocks than in the matched clean blocks, once you split aircraft by what navigation kit they carry? (2) How often does a report arrive attached to a grossly wrong position — detectable because consecutive reports from the same airframe cannot be joined by any physically possible flight path? (3) Of the corrupted reports, what share gets through the operational quality control into the analysis, and what increments do they put into it? And the null is a real answer: if the two sets' departure statistics sit inside a band declared in advance and the rejection rate does not rise, interference does not reach the analysis and the matter is closed.

So: an exposure-contrast measurement on an existing archive, not a new instrument and not a modelling study. Three limbs that can be settled separately.
