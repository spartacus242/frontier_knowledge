## 7. Check (4) — the caveat on the estimand

The caveat (adversary 2's attack 4): δ_sl has no scalar referent for a faceted particle without a declared dividing-surface convention and facet weighting. My assessment, with sources and my own reasoning marked:
- Sourced: orientation anisotropy of δ — "There is significant anisotropy in δ among the three crystal orientations, and little temperature dependence." (Cheng & Ceriotti, Results; verbatim per adversary 2, VERIFY-02-a and VERIFY-02-b at full text; I did not re-read it.) Sourced: sign conventions differ between groups in print (Entropy 2019 §3.4(viii), per VERIFY-02-b). Sourced: δ is DEFINED in Prior 1 as h^V − h^σ for a planar interface (Eq. 1, per VERIFY-02-b), i.e. relative to a named pair of dividing surfaces.
- MY OWN REASONING, labelled: for a one-component system the equimolar surface and the surface of tension are each uniquely defined, so δ per orientation is well defined once that standard pair is named — the "convention" cost is smaller than the caveat implies for a pure metal, and reduces to stating the pair and the sign of R.
