## 7. Point (5): the sign reading
VERIFIED VERBATIM by two routes, 2 consecutive segments: **"The negative value of δ suggest that the equimolar dividing surface is closer to the bulk solid than the surface of tension, and that the interfacial energy of a curved equimolar surface γ^V(R) has a positive curvature dependence."** (The verb "suggest" is in the source.) Definition sentence (F7, 2 segments plus Eq. 1): "Furthermore, the Tolman length is just the difference between the location of the surface of tension and of the equimolar dividing surface in the planar limit, i.e." [Eq. (1)] [Tolman 1948].
Fit to the entry's words: the paper's sentence does read the sign positionally, so the entry's gloss "position of the equimolar surface inside the interfacial profile" is a fair paraphrase of "closer to the bulk solid than the surface of tension" — but the paper's words are "bulk solid" and "surface of tension", not "interfacial profile" or "interfacial width". The candidate's third sub-clause asks for "sign and magnitude relative to the interfacial width". The paper answers the SIGN and gives the positional reading; it prints **no magnitude** and **no interfacial width** (a sweep for "width"/"thickness"/"profile" (F7) returned only "band widths", a slab "thickness ⟨h⟩", and "nucleation free energy profile" — nothing about interfacial width). The entry's summary line that this reading "is V1's third sub-clause" therefore holds for the sign half and overstates on the magnitude-relative-to-width half; the entry's own number check concedes the magnitude point ("prints no scalar"), so this is a compression in the summary, not a contradiction of the paper. Sign-convention consistency (my own reasoning): with the candidate's form γ(R) = γ_inf(1 − 2δ/R) and δ < 0, γ(R) > γ_inf, i.e. γ rises as the crystal shrinks, matching the paper's "positive curvature dependence" for the equimolar surface.
Also verified (F7, 2 segments): "Unsurprisingly, the value, temperature dependence, and anisotropy among the principal orientations of γ vary considerably with the choice of the Gibbs dividing surface." — about γ, not δ, as adversary 2 correctly noted.
