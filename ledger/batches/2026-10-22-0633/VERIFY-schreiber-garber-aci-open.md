## REGIME

What the abstract establishes, it establishes in this regime: **EHR-to-EHR transitions** as the
setting; the **institution-level migration episode** as the unit of analysis — two of them, one per
case study, with contrasting migration methods (manual abstraction and data entry versus several
electronic tools); and **domains of potential consequence** as the reported category. Potential
consequences, in the abstract's framing, are a **qualitative taxonomy**, not an endpoint with a
comparator or an estimand. There is no stated population of patients or records, no threshold, no
instrument and no measured quantity in the regime the abstract describes.

Bearing of that on the claim handed to me: a paper whose abstract-level output is **a taxonomy of
nine consequence domains plus two narrative cases** is in a regime that is **silent on a per-fact-class
migration-completeness fraction**. Silence outside a regime is not support in either direction. So
even if the full text turned out to print no fraction, the honest reading is that this paper did not
set out to measure one — which is a different and weaker statement than "the field has no such number".

One scope caution for whoever uses sub-claim (b): a declaration that little empiric information
exists is a statement about **the peer-reviewed literature as of early 2020**, made by these authors
about **data migrations during EHR-to-EHR transitions**. It is not a statement about completeness
fractions specifically, and should not be stretched into one without the sentence in hand.
