Presuppositions:
  P1. The genealogy is recoverable: Hugging Face base_model:finetune / base_model:merge tags, plus inference from weights or outputs (MoTHer, Horwitz et al. 2024; PhyloLM). PARTLY ESTABLISHED. Tamura et al. 2025 built lineage graphs from these tags. CONTESTED: Jin et al. 2025 (App. B, fn. 3) report that the leaderboard "base model" column is often itself a fine-tune and needed a four-layer inference; Hardy et al. 2026 call the metadata "of unknown quality". MoTHer's accuracy drops on deeper trees (0.79 on its 121-model tree).
  P2. The genealogy is tree-like enough for phylogenetic regression. CONTESTED. Merges create reticulation. Handling options: (i) exclude merges, (ii) attach each merge to its primary parent, (iii) use a network Brownian-motion covariance (weighted parental covariances). Sensitivity across (i)–(iii) is required.
  P3. Ancestors are in the sample (base models are leaderboard entries). ESTABLISHED as a feature of the data; PhyloLM's Discussion flags it ("common ancestors are present among the studied models"). It is handled as sampled ancestors (zero-length terminal branches). This is not standard in PCM software and is flagged.
  P4. A trait-evolution covariance model (Brownian motion, Ornstein–Uhlenbeck, Pagel's lambda transform) describes score change under fine-tuning adequately. CONTESTED. Fine-tuning is directed optimisation, often aimed at the leaderboard itself (selection, not drift). Lambda is used as a descriptive covariance-fit statistic, which only partly sidesteps this. OU is a sensitivity check.
  P5. Branch lengths are meaningful. CONTESTED. There is no natural "time" for a fine-tuning step. Schemes: unit length per derivation step, Grafen-type, and output-similarity distances (PhyloLM-style). Lambda and PGLS are sensitive to this choice.
  P6. Scores come from one consistent harness. ESTABLISHED within Open LLM Leaderboard v1 and within v2; versions are not mixed.
  P7. The target analyses pool many related models. ESTABLISHED for leaderboard-based studies: Ilić & Gignac (3,862 → 591 models), Jin et al. (3,360 with a known base), Hardy et al. (4,281), Kearns 2026 (4,395). FALSE or weak for Burnell 2023 (29 HELM models) and Ruan 2024 (base models only), hence their removal in R1.
  P8. The evolutionary-correlation estimand is the one capability-structure claims should target. CONTESTED. Those papers make population-level psychometric claims, and some authors would reject the corrected estimand as irrelevant. R1 states it as the target and leaves the descriptive estimand to the uncorrected fits.
  P9. The leaderboard sample is not dominated by survivorship or selection effects that a tree covariance cannot capture. CONTESTED (submissions are self-selected and deleted models drop out; Ilić & Gignac lost 264 models to deletion). Flagged, not resolved.

What would count as an answer:
  Build the genealogy: metadata, cross-checked by output-similarity inference on a subsample, giving tree (merges excluded), tree (primary parent) and network versions. On it:
  (a) Estimate lambda and K, with 95% CIs, for each of the six v2 benchmarks (or the v1 set), raw and after residualising on log parameters.
  (b) Refit, for each representative published analysis:
    - the inter-benchmark correlation matrix;
    - the one-factor / bifactor general-factor variance share (Ilić & Gignac; Hardy et al.);
    - the base-model-invariant subspace and causal ordering (Jin et al.).
    Do this under four covariance structures: independence, contributor random effect, base-model stratification, phylogenetic (PGLS / phylogenetic factor analysis with estimated lambda).
  Decision rule (robust only if it holds in ≥2 of 3 merge treatments AND ≥2 of 3 branch-length schemes):
    "No material pseudo-replication bias": the upper CI of lambda is < 0.2 for most benchmarks, OR the phylogenetic estimates fall inside the 95% CIs of both the independence and the contributor-RE fits.
    "Yes": the lower CI of lambda is > 0.5 for most benchmarks AND at least one headline estimate (general-factor share, a key inter-benchmark correlation, or the Jin et al. causal ordering) falls outside the 95% CI of the best existing coarse correction.
    "Signal present but harmless": lambda is high but the estimates are unchanged. This is a legitimate answer, read as correlated evolution and not as an artefact of shared ancestry.
    "Model-dependent": the result flips across merge or branch-length schemes. This is also an answer: the genealogy cannot settle the matter at current metadata quality.
  The outcome space is closed and every cell is informative, so this is a question, not a thesis.

Significance — consequence:
  Yes (high lambda, and headline estimates move beyond the coarse-corrected CIs):
    - Leaderboard-based claims of a strong LLM "positive manifold" and general factor (Ilić & Gignac; Hardy et al.) are partly artefacts of family over-representation. So are causal capability orderings (Jin et al.).
    - Benchmark-redundancy judgements built on pooled inter-benchmark correlations are overstated. These feed decisions to drop or subsample benchmarks (cf. the "You Don't Need to Run Every Eval" / matrix-completion line, 2606.24020, seen in Q8 results but not opened).
    - Cross-model meta-science would need lineage covariance as standard practice, in the way comparative biology adopted it after 1985.
  No (lambda ≈ 0, or estimates unchanged versus the coarse corrections): the existing crude fixes are vindicated, the costly genealogy step can be skipped, and published structure claims gain robustness evidence they currently lack.
  Either way: per-benchmark lambda is a new descriptive quantity, the "heritability" of a capability under fine-tuning. It bears on how far post-training can move each benchmark from its base.
  Neither branch is trivial, but the "no" branch is largely confirmatory of practice.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism):
  Mainly a disciplinary boundary. ML evaluation took its cross-model statistics from psychometrics, where examinees are exchangeable and non-independence is handled by clusters or multilevel models (hence contributor RE, base-model strata, de-duplication). Comparative biology, where non-independence is a tree, was not a source field. The groups that do build LLM phylogenies (PhyloLM, LLM DNA, Gallagher et al., MoTHer, Laufer et al.) use trees as the object of interest, for prediction, provenance or ecosystem description, not as a nuisance covariance for other groups' estimates.
  Secondary: a new-instrument mechanism. Usable lineage data is recent: Hugging Face base_model tags and model trees (2024), and lineage-inference methods (2024–2025).
  Caveat for Gate 4: the silence is only partial. The coarse question has been asked (see Asked-but-open), so the mechanism explains why the tree-structured version was not tried, not why the concern went unasked.
