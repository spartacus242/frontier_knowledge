## 1. Restatement (in my own words, before engaging the record's framing)

Take the fouling-resistance number a designer copied out of a standard's table and treat it as a **forecast** of the fouling that exchanger will actually suffer in service. Then, for one named service class and a defined set of in-service exchangers for which you can obtain **both** the design datasheet and the operating history:

- **Limb 1 (forecast scoring).** How wrong is the forecast on average (bias in the ratio realized ÷ tabulated), and how variable is the error (spread) — carrying measurement error through, and stating the floor below which fouling resistance cannot be distinguished from instrument and maldistribution noise at all?
- **Limb 2 (cost of the error, signed).** Restricting to exchangers whose heat-transfer area was actually set by the fouling allowance and not by something else, how much area did the forecast error buy or fail to buy — surplus area where the allowance over-predicted, deficit where it under-predicted?

So: a verification-and-calibration study of a table, with a sizing-consequence limb attached. The claim under test is that nobody has asked this.

## 5. Verdict

**Rejected** — Unresolved on attack 2 (Disguise), corroborated by Unresolved on attack 7 (Reason for silence); nothing turns on an unrun check.

### The single strongest killing point, and the leg it stands on

The record's distinguishing claim is that the fouling allowance is treated as a margin and never as a quantity to be verified, and that the method vocabulary which would own the verification has no heat-exchanger application. **Both halves are false in the candidate's own field.** *Risk-Based Design Margin Selection for Heat Exchangers* (Shilling, Rudy & Rudy, Heat Exchanger Fouling & Cleaning VIII, 2009, paper 48, **full text**) is design-margin theory applied to heat exchangers, authored at Heat Transfer Research, Inc. — the very consortium the record names as the audience — and it treats the fouling allowance explicitly as one of three probability-rated margin components, with area and fouling resistance as **interchangeable** ways of carrying the same risk (*"using zero fouling resistance with an additional 5 percent area"*). Together with King (BP, AIChE 2007 paper 93c, **abstract only**), which asserts limb 2's direction in print — *"The traditional method of sizing heat exchangers often leads to unnecessarily large design margins"* that *"increase capital cost, footprint & weight"* — this closes the one gap the record left genuinely open and declined to route: **limb 2 is asked, in a vocabulary the record searched once and recorded as empty.**

**Multi-leg disclosure.** This kill has two legs, sourced separately. If King 2007 fell (abstract only, no data, assertion rather than finding), **the verdict still stands on Shilling, Rudy & Rudy 2009 alone**, which I read in full and which alone defeats "the two literatures have not met", the reason-for-silence mechanism, and the fecundity claim that treating the allowance probabilistically is newly askable. If *that* leg fell too, the fallback is the Gate 2 leg (priors 1 and 2), which I did not re-verify and therefore do not rest on. Neither of my legs answers either limb — I claim only that the question is asked, which is what attack 2 tests.

**Routing note (unchanged in direction, strengthened in content):** this is an open problem, not an un-asked question. Route the distributional limb as asked-but-open, and — against the record's instruction — **limb 2 can now be routed too**, citing King 2007 (direction asserted, unquantified) and Shilling et al. 2009 (prescriptive margins, examples "not based on actual cases") as the sources showing it posed and unanswered.
