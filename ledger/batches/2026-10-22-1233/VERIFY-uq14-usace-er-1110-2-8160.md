## WHAT THE SOURCE SAYS (quotations as served)

### (a) Read level and route

Answered above: FULL TEXT of paragraphs 1-6 of ER 1110-2-8160, two agreeing routes, one of

### (b) Does it require project grades to be referenced to a nationwide vertical datum, and which one?

**Yes, unambiguously, and the datum for the inland case is NAVD88 as the realisation of the
NSRS.** Paragraph 4, assembled from six consecutive served segments (S4-S9) - labelled a
RECONSTRUCTION from 6 fragments, junctions verified at "maintained"/"elevation",
"nationwide"/"framework", "National"/"Water Level", "National"/"Oceanic", and
"datum"/"within":

> "4. Policy. It is the policy of the USACE that the designed, constructed, and maintained
> elevation grades of projects shall be reliably and accurately referenced to a consistent
> nationwide framework, or vertical datum--i.e., the National Spatial Reference System (NSRS)
> or the National Water Level Observation Network (NWLON) maintained by the U.S. Department of
> Commerce, National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA). The current orthometric
> vertical reference datum within the NSRS in CONUS is the North American Vertical Datum of
> 1988 (NAVD88)."

Single served segments, quoted as served and not joined:

> S9/S10: "The current NWLON National Tidal Datum Epoch (NTDE) is 1983-2001. (Other NSRS/NWLON
> defined datums are applicable in the Great Lakes and in isolated OCONUS regions)."

> S11-S13, RECONSTRUCTION from 3 fragments: "The relationships among existing, constructed, or
> maintained project grades that are referenced to local or superseded datums (e.g., NGVD29,
> MSL), the current NSRS, and/or hydraulic/tidal datums, shall be established as outlined
> below."

The reservoir-relevant sub-paragraph is 4c, and it is worth quoting at its full length because
the entry's population is reservoir operators. RECONSTRUCTION from 5 consecutive fragments
(S20-S24), junctions verified at "floodwalls,"/"multipurpose", "Designed or"/"constructed",
"water surface"/"profiles", "shall be"/"accurately":

> "c. Inland Flood Risk Management, Navigation, and Water Control Systems (to include levees,
> floodwalls, multipurpose hydropower projects, locks and dams, and non-tidal inland navigation
> systems). Designed or constructed flood protection or navigation clearance grades, hydraulic
> or hydrodynamic water surface profiles, river or pool stages, and stream gages in inland
> flood risk and water control systems shall be accurately referenced to the NSRS (e.g.,
> NAVD88)."

So: pool stages are in scope by name, and the required target is "the NSRS (e.g., NAVD88)."
The coastal sub-paragraphs point at tidal datums instead - 4a at "NWLON tidal gages and
coastal hydrodynamic tidal models," 4b at "a local Mean Lower Low Water (MLLW) datum modeled
on the latest NTDE as defined by NOAA for the project area" - and 4d at "a current NSRS, MLLW,
or MHW datum, as appropriate to local, state, and federal requirements."

Note for the ledger what kind of statement this is. **The regulation PRESCRIBES; it measures
and prints nothing.** There is no number in this document that could be a datum offset, a
conversion value, or a count of operators. There is no table and no figure. The only numerals
in the body are the NTDE epoch "1983-2001" and the review intervals "5 years" and "20 years."
Nothing here is a measurement, so nothing here can be mistaken for one.

### (c) Conversion to a geopotential surface or a geoid model? THE DECISIVE QUESTION

**ABSENT. Flatly, on both routes, with no near miss except one adjective.**

I asked both routes, independently, for every occurrence of: geoid, geopotential, orthometric,
ellipsoid, GEOID03, GEOID09, EGM, GPS. Both returned the same answer. The words **"geoid,"
"geopotential," "GEOID," "EGM," "ellipsoid," and "GPS" do not appear anywhere in the
document.** Route 2, on the cited publications.usace.army.mil host, confirms it in those
terms: "'geoid,' 'geopotential,' 'GEOID,' 'EGM,' and 'ellipsoid' do not appear anywhere in the
text."

The one adjacent word that does appear is **"orthometric," twice, and in both places it is an
adjective describing a levelling datum, not a requirement to convert to anything**:

> Paragraph 4: "The current orthometric vertical reference datum within the NSRS in CONUS is
> the North American Vertical Datum of 1988 (NAVD88)."

> Paragraph 6, segment S36: "The relationship between these local (legacy) orthometric or
> hydraulic reference datums and the current..."

Read the grammar on both sides of those cuts. In the first, "orthometric" qualifies which
existing NSRS datum is meant - it distinguishes NAVD88 from the tidal datums in the same
sentence - and the verb is "is," not "shall be converted to." In the second, "orthometric"
qualifies the *legacy* datums whose relationship to the current framework must be modelled;
again no conversion target beyond "the current nationwide frameworks maintained by the U.S.
Department of Commerce." Neither sentence names a geoid model, a geopotential number, a
gravity-based surface, or a dynamic height. Quoting "orthometric" as though it carried a geoid
requirement would be exactly the verbatim-but-mis-cut misquotation this ledger keeps making.

The forward-looking hook in the regulation is a *mechanism without a named destination*.
Paragraph 5 requires that projects be "periodically monitored after construction to account
for subsidence, settlement, **NOAA reference datum redefinitions and readjustments**, sea level
change, and other factors," and that "NSRS/NWLON reference datum updates and readjustments
must also be continuously monitored." That is the clause under which a future
geopotential-based datum would arrive at a USACE project. The regulation does not name one,
because on 1 March 2009 there was not one to name.

### (d) Does it say anything about PUBLISHING the conversion, as against holding it internally?

**It says the opposite of publishing: the conversion is to be documented in internal project
manuals.** This is the most load-bearing finding in the note.

Paragraph 6's operative sentence, as served by Route 2 in three segments and verified at the
junctions "current"/"nationwide" and "modeled,"/"documented" - RECONSTRUCTION from 3 fragments:

> "The relationship between these local (legacy) orthometric or hydraulic reference datums and
> the current nationwide frameworks maintained by the U.S. Department of Commerce must be
> accurately modeled, documented in O&M manuals, and be kept current; especially in high
> subsidence areas."

Followed, as served, by a single segment:

> "Long-term effort should be programmed to transition from legacy reference datum grades to
> the NSRS."

Note the hedging asymmetry, and quote it the way the regulation does: the *documentation* of
the relationship is "must," while the *transition away from legacy datums* is only "should be
programmed." The entry should carry the hedged form for the transition and the hard form for
the documentation, not the reverse.

The destination named is "O&M manuals." The surrounding sentence, S33-S35, lists where legacy
datums already live: "the baseline reference cited in FEMA flood insurance rate maps, water
control manuals, emergency operation & maintenance manuals, flood profile models, stream/pool
gages, inundation models, or as-built drawings." FEMA FIRMs are public; water control manuals
and O&M manuals and as-built drawings are project documents, releasable but not published as a
matter of this policy.

I asked both routes for every occurrence of publication language. **"publish," "published,"
"publication of elevations," "web," "website," "disseminate," "share," "report," "coordinate
with," "benchmark," "bench mark" do not appear in the document.** The single occurrence of
"public" is paragraph 3, and it is about the regulation itself, not about any elevation:

> "3. Distribution. This regulation is approved for public release; distribution is unlimited."

So the ER imposes a duty to *hold an accurate, current, modelled conversion* and a duty to
*write it into O&M manuals*. It imposes no duty to publish it anywhere, and it describes no
channel through which it would be published.

### (e) Does it name any geoid model by name?

**No. Not one.** The datums and frameworks named anywhere in the document are: NSRS, NWLON,
NAVD88, NTDE 1983-2001, MLLW, MHW, and - as superseded datums in paragraph 6 - "NGVD29, MSL,
SLD 1929, MSL 1912, USGS, Cairo Datum, Mean Low Gulf, etc." No GEOID03, GEOID09, GEOID12A,
GEOID18, no EGM96, no EGM2008, no NAPGD2022, no GRAV-D. The word "geoid" is not in the
document in any form.

refute, and it does not settle.** Reasoning in the BEARING section below.

## REGIME

**The regulation's own statement of its population and unit of analysis, quoted, because the
mismatch with the entry's population is the main defect I found.**

Population, paragraph 2, RECONSTRUCTION from 5 consecutive fragments (T8-T12), junctions
verified at "for the"/"planning", "flood risk"/"management", "water"/"supply/control":

> "2. Applicability. This regulation applies to all USACE commands having responsibility for
> the planning, engineering, design, construction, operation, maintenance, and regulation of
> flood risk management and coastal storm damage reduction, hurricane protection,
> multi-purpose water supply/control and hydropower, ecosystem restoration, and navigation
> projects. This regulation also applies to regulatory permitting activities."

- **Population:** USACE commands. Not operators generally.
- **Unit of analysis:** the project, and within it the "designed, constructed, and maintained
  elevation grades" - plus, in 4c, "river or pool stages, and stream gages."
- **Operating condition:** the whole project life cycle. Paragraph 5: assessment "during the
  Feasibility and Preconstruction, Engineering, and Design (PED) phases, during construction,
  and periodically monitored after construction."
- **Instrument:** not a measuring instrument. Paragraph 5's verification clause, RECONSTRUCTION
  from 3 fragments (T27-T29): "Technical reviews and periodic inspections shall appropriately
  confirm that adequate field surveys, gage observations, and/or other verification methods
  were utilized to maintain datum relationships consistent with the current NSRS/NWLON
  networks, as applicable."
- **Threshold:** a review interval, not an accuracy tolerance. T23-T24: "The frequency of
  required periodic assessments of project datums is project site dependent--ranging from 5
  years in high subsidence areas to 20 years in stable, non-tidal project locations." There is
  **no stated vertical accuracy threshold anywhere in the document** - no centimetres, no
  tolerance, no error budget.
- **Category:** policy. Paragraph 1 says what it is: "This regulation establishes U.S. Army
  Corps of Engineers (USACE) policies for referencing project elevation grades to nationwide
  vertical datums established and maintained by the U.S. Department of Commerce."

**Does the entry's claim sit inside this regime? Partly, and the part that falls outside is
the part the entry most needs.**

Inside: USACE-operated reservoirs with pool stages and water control systems are squarely in
paragraph 4c, named there.

Outside, in two directions:

1. **Denominator mismatch.** The entry's population is "USACE-class reservoir operators." The
   ER's population is "all USACE commands." Operators that are *of the same class* but not
   USACE - Reclamation, TVA, Bonneville, state agencies, investor-owned utilities, foreign
   operators - are not governed by this document at all, and its silence says nothing about
   them. The near neighbours the ER's denominator is not: it is not "federal reservoir
   operators," not "FERC licensees," not "operators of dams above some storage threshold." It
   is one agency's own commands.
2. **Numerator-class mismatch, and this is the sharper one.** The ER's numerator class is
   *what the policy requires*. The entry's leg (b) is a claim about *what operators in fact
   publish*. A requirements floor is silent about practice above the floor in both directions:
   it misses any conversion an operator publishes voluntarily or under some other authority
   (a state dam-safety filing, a FERC exhibit, a NOAA or USGS cooperative gauge datum sheet, a
   project's own hydrologic data portal), and it includes, as satisfied, an operator who holds
   an accurate NAVD88 relationship entirely inside an O&M manual and publishes nothing. A
   policy that does not require X is one word away from a population in which no one does X,
   and it is a different quantity.
3. **Epoch mismatch.** The document is dated 1 March 2009. It therefore cannot speak to
   practice after the 2022-onward NSRS modernisation, and cannot be read as declining to
   reference a geopotential datum that did not yet exist as a replacement framework.

## DEFECTS FOUND

1. **The entry's title phrase is wrong: "project evaluation grades" should be "project
   elevation grades."** The printed title is "POLICIES FOR REFERENCING PROJECT ELEVATION
   GRADES TO NATIONWIDE VERTICAL DATUMS." The error is traceable to a mirror's filename and to
   the search-result title derived from it, which means the entry's citation was almost
   certainly assembled from a search listing rather than from the document's front matter.
   Take author and title from the front matter.
2. **The entry carries no date.** 1 March 2009. The omission hides the epoch mismatch in the
   REGIME section, which is doing real work: a 2009 policy cannot be evidence about the reach
   of a datum chain in the NAPGD2022 era.
3. **The entry over-promises what this document can settle.** The entry nominates it as "the
   single unopened source most likely to settle legs (b) and (c)." It is two pages of policy
   with no appendix, no numbers, no survey of practice and no enumeration of operators. It
   cannot settle a population claim about what operators publish. It can constrain what they
   are *required* to publish, which is a different and much weaker proposition. The entry's own
   admission that "the kill there partly presupposes the answer" is not cured by this document.
4. **Leg (b)'s form does not change.** It remains an argument from absence. What this document
   adds is a *reason* for the absence - no policy requires publication - which makes the
   absence less surprising but does not make it observed. An argument from absence plus an
   explanation of the absence is still an argument from absence.
5. **A trap for the next reader: "orthometric" is in this document and "geoid" is not.** Anyone
   skimming for geopotential language will find "orthometric vertical reference datum" and may
   quote it as though the ER reached a geoid model. It does not. Both occurrences are adjectives
   on levelling datums, with the verb "is" in one and "relationship ... must be accurately
   modeled" in the other. See (c) for the grammar on both sides of each cut.
6. **EM 1110-2-6056 was not located and must not be scored as checked.** Two searches, neither
   returning any link to it. I make no claim that it does not exist or that its number is
   wrong; I claim only that I did not reach it. Its silence is not evidence in either direction.
7. **Minor, but record it:** I could not obtain the signature block on either route, so I

## BEARING ON THE LEDGER ENTRY

**Verdict: NARROW leg (b), and CONVERT the part of it that this document touches into a
promotion condition. Do not let this document kill leg (b), and do not let it rescue it.**

What the document does establish, and it is worth having:

- The controlling USACE policy for inland water-control projects, pool stages included by
  name, requires referencing to "the NSRS (e.g., NAVD88)" and to tidal datums. Nothing more.
- It names no geoid model, no geopotential surface, no EGM, no NAPGD2022. The words are absent
  on both routes, including the host the entry cites.
- It routes the legacy-to-current conversion into **"O&M manuals"** - internal project
  documentation - and contains no publication duty and no publication channel. The only
  occurrence of "public" concerns the release of the regulation itself.

So the entry may now say something it could not say before, in these terms and no stronger:
*the governing USACE policy creates no requirement that would produce an operator-published
conversion reaching a geopotential surface; it requires an internally documented conversion
reaching NAVD88.* That is a statement about the requirement, sourced to a quotable sentence.

What the document does **not** establish, and the entry must not be allowed to drift into:

- It does not show that no operator publishes such a conversion. It is a requirements floor,
  not a survey. It enumerates no operators and prints no counts. An operator may publish more
  than the ER demands, under some other authority or on its own initiative, and the ER would
  be silent.
- It does not cover non-USACE operators of USACE-class reservoirs at all. Its applicability is
  "all USACE commands."
- Being dated 1 March 2009, it cannot speak to the reach of any datum chain in the
  post-modernisation era. Its only forward hook is the duty to monitor "NOAA reference datum
  redefinitions and readjustments," with no destination named.

**Concretely, for leg (b):** strike the universal quantifier. "No operator publishes a
conversion reaching EGM2008" is not supported and is not refuted here. Replace it with the
narrower, sourced proposition above, and attach the promotion condition that would actually
close it: enumerate a defined population of operators - say, the USACE districts' published
water control manuals and gauge datum sheets for a named set of projects, plus the non-USACE
operators the entry wants to include - and check each for a published conversion and its
terminus. Until someone does that enumeration, leg (b) stays an argument from absence with a
plausible explanation attached, which is not a kill.

**For leg (c):** this document is genuinely informative and the answer is clean. The policy
mentions no geopotential surface and no geoid model by name, at all. If leg (c) is the claim
that the policy layer stops at NAVD88, this supports it on a direct quotation. If leg (c) is
the claim that *practice* stops at NAVD88, the same regime limits apply as for (b).

One sentence to carry forward verbatim, because it is the single most useful thing in the
document for this entry, quoted as Route 2 served it in three segments:

> "The relationship between these local (legacy) orthometric or hydraulic reference datums and
> the current nationwide frameworks maintained by the U.S. Department of Commerce must be
> accurately modeled, documented in O&M manuals, and be kept current; especially in high
> subsidence areas."

Modelled and documented in O&M manuals. Not published. That is the distinction the entry was
reaching for, and this is where the regulation prints it.
