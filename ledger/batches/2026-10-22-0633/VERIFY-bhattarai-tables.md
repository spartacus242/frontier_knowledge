### (d) THREE SEPARATE ANSWERS, after reading the full table list and figure list above

(i) A recomputed REFERENCE INTERVAL, on initial or final results: NO. Absent. Table 1 is
    "Different parameters and methods" - analytical methods, not reference intervals - and no
    table or figure derives a reference range from these data. This is a real absence, not an
    unread section: the article has exactly four tables and one figure and no supplement.

(ii) A PREVALENCE OF THRESHOLD-CROSSING ABNORMALITY: NO. Nothing reports what share of results
     fall outside a reference range or cross a decision threshold. The nearest printed figure is
     "Critical results accounted for 311 repeats (44.37%); of which 185 repeats for high value
     and 126 repeats for low value." That is a share OF THE 701 REPEATS, i.e. a composition of
     repeat REASONS, not a prevalence of critical results in the test corpus. Reading 44.37% as
     an abnormality prevalence would be precisely the share-of-users-as-share-of-utilisation
     error: its denominator is repeats, not tests.

(iii) A WITHIN-PERSON MONITORING DELTA: NO. Table 4's unit of analysis is a PAIR OF ANALYSES OF
      THE SAME SPECIMEN, not two timepoints in one patient. The paper's own definition of a
      repeat: "a re-analysis of the same analyte from the original sample aliquot", and the
      Methods exclude "Tests repeated on clinician's request after initial reporting or on a new
      sample". So the initial-vs-final comparison is analytical replication on one aliquot. It is
      silent on biological change over time within a patient.

## REGIME

The regime in which this paper establishes what it establishes, and which of the sub-claims
sit inside it:

- POPULATION / SITE: one month of testing in ONE clinical biochemistry department, Dhulikhel
  Hospital, a university hospital in Nepal. Single-centre, single-month. No multi-site or
  multi-period claim is supported.
- CORPUS: 95,757 biochemical tests across 117 TEST TYPES (text). Tables 2 and 3 cover only the
  43 ANALYTES THAT HAD AT LEAST ONE REPEAT. Table 2's "43 analytes" is therefore a SUBSET
  SELECTED ON THE OUTCOME, not the department's analyte menu. Any statement of the form
  "the lab runs 43 analytes" is outside this regime; and a per-analyte repeat share computed
  only over analytes that had repeats cannot be averaged into a lab-wide rate.
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS, Tables 2 and 3: the TEST (an ordered-and-performed assay), with the repeat
  as the event. Not the patient, not the sample, not the episode.
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS, Table 4: the REPEAT PAIR on ONE SAMPLE ALIQUOT - initial run vs final run of
  the same specimen. This is the scope finding that matters most here. Table 4 is an ANALYTICAL
  REPRODUCIBILITY comparison. It is silent about biological change, about serial monitoring,
  about new-sample confirmation, and about whether a clinician's decision would have altered.
- ENDPOINT / ESTIMAND, Table 4: a between-run difference in group-level central tendency
  (median with IQR, or for TAG mean with SD) across the set of repeat pairs, tested for
  statistical difference at p<0.05. It is NOT a paired per-specimen difference distribution,
  NOT a bias estimate, NOT a limits-of-agreement analysis. No n per row, no effect size, no
  confidence interval on any difference.
- COMPARATOR: initial result vs final result of the same aliquot, with analytical-error repeats
  lacking an initial result removed.
- THRESHOLD: p<0.05, statistical only. No clinical or analytical threshold anywhere.
- DENOMINATOR INSTABILITY IS PART OF THE REGIME: urine albumin's 50% is 4 of 8 tests; AFP's
  7.69% is 1 of 13; cortisol's 3.23% is 1 of 31; drain amylase's 7.14% is 1 of 14. These are
  single-event rates on single- and double-digit denominators. The paper itself frames them as
  such - "Higher repeats occurred in low-volume tests" - so quoting "urine albumin 50%" without
  n=8 takes the figure outside the regime in which the paper states it.

Where the sub-claims sit: (a) and (c)'s structural assertions and (b)'s two printed figures all
sit INSIDE the regime - they are statements about what the tables print, and the tables print
them. The claim that would sit OUTSIDE the regime is any use of Table 4 as evidence about
within-person monitoring or about clinical decision impact, and any use of 44.37% as an
abnormality prevalence.

## DEFECTS FOUND

1. NOT A DEFECT IN THE EARLIER READING, A CONFIRMATION: all three load-bearing table claims
   check out as printed. Table 2 does give per-analyte repeat shares for 43 analytes and all 11
   spot-checked values are exact. Table 3 does print 0.75% on 93,706. Table 4 does compare
   Initial vs Final for 18 analytes with mixed median(IQR)/mean(SD) and p-values, and the 8
   significant analytes are exactly AST, HbA1c, PPBS, Serum Albumin, Serum Sodium, Urea,
   B-HCG and TAG.

2. DEFECT IN THE SOURCE, UNDERSTATED BY THE EARLIER READING: the inconsistency is three-way,
   not two-way. Table 3's nine category rows sum to 88,706, and Table 2's 43 total-tests cells
   sum to 88,706, but Table 3's printed Total Tests cell reads 93,706 - a 5,000 discrepancy
   against its own rows, in the very cell that is the denominator of the paper's 0.75%. The
   repeats column of the same Total row sums correctly to 701. The real gap between the text's
   95,757 and the tables' row-level corpus is 7,051 tests, not 2,051.

3. DEFECT IN THE SOURCE: the paper prints two different repeat rates for the same numerator -
   0.73% (Abstract, on 95,757) and 0.75% (Table 3, on 93,706) - and reconciles neither. The
   rate implied by its own table rows, 0.79%, is never printed.

4. DEFECT IN THE SOURCE: no reconciling sentence exists anywhere. A partial explanation is
   available (117 test types in the text vs 43 repeated analytes in the tables) but the paper
   never offers it, and it does not account for 93,706, which matches neither corpus.

5. DEFECT IN THE SOURCE, UNDEFINED CONSTRUCT: the paper's headline conclusion uses "clinically
   significant" twice and defines it nowhere. No RCV, no biological variation, no total
   allowable error, no decision threshold. The only threshold is p<0.05, so "no clinically
   significant difference" is being carried by statistical non-significance - a different
   construct. And it is asserted while 8 of the paper's own 18 Table 4 analytes reach p<0.05;
   the hedge that carries this is only "For most of the analytes", with no threshold behind
   "most".

6. DEFECT IN THE SOURCE, PUBLISHED-IS-NOT-PERFORMED: Table 4 STATES an exclusion
   ("Repeat tests done due to analytical error showing no initial test results are excluded")
   and never QUANTIFIES it. No n is printed for Table 4 as a whole or for any row. Since
   analyser flags/errors are 37.09% of repeats, roughly a third of the corpus is removed by an
   unquantified exclusion, and the reader cannot recover the denominator of the paper's central
   comparison from anything the paper prints.

7. DEFECT TO GUARD AGAINST IN DOWNSTREAM USE, not in the source: 44.37% is a share of the 701
   REPEATS, not a prevalence of critical or abnormal results among tests. The paper states it
   correctly; it would be misread as an abnormality prevalence.

8. ARITHMETIC OWNERSHIP: 88,706, 0.79%, 7,051 and the approximately 260 analytical-error
   repeats are MY arithmetic on the source's printed digits. The source prints none of them.
   0.73%, 0.75%, 95,757, 93,706, 701, 311, 44.37%, 37.09%, 9.55%, 8.98%, 64 +/- 41 minutes and
   NPR 153,188 are the source's own printed figures.

9. MY OWN READ'S LIMITATION, stated rather than buried: table bodies came through the
   summariser, not from my own eyes on the PDF. Two independently-phrased passes agreed
   digit-for-digit on every load-bearing cell, and the 88,706 sum was reached twice by
   different means, which is why I report FULL TEXT rather than COULD NOT READ. A reader with a
   working PDF route should still spot-check Table 3's Total cell, because a 5,000 error is
   exactly the shape a transcription slip would take. One inconsistency between my own passes,
   recorded for honesty: an early pass placed "clinically significant" in the Discussion only;
   the targeted phrase search found it in both the Abstract Results and the Discussion, and I
   report the targeted search's result.

## BEARING ON THE LEDGER ENTRY

The paper is OPEN AND FULLY READABLE at the route the ledger itself names, and its tables are
not a summariser's paraphrase any more - all four are reproduced above. The previous reader's
caveat that they had seen the tables only as described can be retired.

For the rejection that rests on these three table claims: the claims HOLD. Table 2 is a
per-analyte repeat-share table over 43 analytes with all 11 spot-checked values exact; Table 3
prints 0.75% on 93,706 and the paper never reconciles that against the 95,757 in its own
Abstract and Results; Table 4 is an 18-analyte Initial-vs-Final comparison with mixed
median(IQR)/mean(SD) and p-values, with the 8 significant analytes exactly as listed. Nothing
in the source weakens the rejection on these points.

Two things should be CORRECTED in the entry, and both strengthen rather than weaken it. First,
the denominator inconsistency is worse than recorded: Table 3's printed Total of 93,706 does not

the paper carries three mutually inconsistent corpus totals and the true text-vs-table gap is

clinically significant difference - so that headline rests on p>0.05 in 10 of 18 analytes, on an
unprinted denominator, on no n per row, and on no RCV or allowable-error threshold.

Two scope limits that any use of this paper must carry. Table 4 measures REPEAT ANALYSES OF ONE
SAMPLE ALIQUOT, so it is silent about within-person change over time and about clinical decision
impact; and 44.37% is a share of the 701 repeats, not an abnormality prevalence in the corpus.
