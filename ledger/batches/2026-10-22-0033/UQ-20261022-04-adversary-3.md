## Restatement (my own words, not the record's)

Translation puts the wrong amino acid in about once per thousand residues. Everything we know
about that number is an average: it comes from chopping proteins into tryptic peptides and
comparing peak intensities, so it is a ratio over a population of molecules, not a property of
any molecule. The candidate asks: if you could read each individual protein chain end to end,
how many mistakes would each one carry? Specifically — in a normal, undrugged cell, write down
the count of misincorporations on each full-length molecule, build the histogram, and compare it
to the histogram you would get if every site erred independently at its own measured rate
(Poisson-binomial, not single-lambda Poisson). Does the real histogram have a fatter tail than
that — are the errors piled onto a minority of unlucky molecules rather than spread thin — and
what share of molecules carry two or more? Named instrument: single-molecule protein sequencing.

Two things the restatement makes visible that the record's framing softens. First, the question
is *conditioned on an instrument* in its own canonical wording, so the instrument's capability is
not background, it is part of the question. Second, the interesting limb is not "is there any
departure" (a histogram's tail versus a null's tail at large N) but "how much", and the record
concedes it bounds no effect size.
