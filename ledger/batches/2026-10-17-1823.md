## And one citation that is real but about something else

UQ-43's other supporting negative was "van Wieringen & de Mast 2008", used as having "answered the generic no-reference binary-inspection question negatively". The real paper is *Measurement system analysis for binary data*, Technometrics 50(4):468-478 — about the **repeatability and reproducibility** of binary measurement systems with no reference values, modelled with a latent class model, with maximum-likelihood and method-of-moments estimators and sample-size guidelines. Its abstract states no impossibility and no non-identifiability result; if anything it treats estimation as possible under the model.

The accuracy-versus-latent-class paper is a different one with a different co-author — van Wieringen & van den Heuvel, *Quality Engineering* 17(4):495-507 (2005) — which says "We argue that a latent class model is the most promising candidate", so it **endorses** the approach the entry used it to refute. Both the objection and the routing paragraph are corrected.

## Both kills survive anyway, and neither needed a late Gate 4

**UQ-43** is killed by attack 4, a presupposition failure about the estimand, reached independently by all three adversaries: the pre-declared flaw-size-class stratification needs measured sizes for locations that by construction no method called, so every size class loses its all-negative cell and the per-class false-call rate has no denominator. That is a reasoning argument, not a citation, and verification did not touch it. What verification added:

- The nearest potential duplicate, which **every agent in the batch had left unread**, is now identified and is not a duplicate: Amaya-Gómez et al., *RESS* 223 (2022) 108520 is "Matching of corroded defects in onshore pipelines based on In-Line Inspections and Voronoi partitions" — two consecutive passes of the same tool type separated in time, matched geometrically, with POD and false alarms as a secondary output. Not several different methods on the same locations at one time. Read at abstract level only, so its internals remain unknown and the re-submission prerequisite is only partly discharged.
- The identifiability argument is **unsupported and unrefuted in print**. The standard conditions (three conditionally independent tests for one population; 2L+V parameters with V strata; degrees of freedom at least the parameter count, with Jones et al. 2010 warning that the count alone does not guarantee identifiability) mean the **binary** four-method version clears the minimum — which is why the kill correctly aims at the size-stratified version. But nothing published treats a polytomous latent state whose size indicator is missing precisely for the class of interest. The kill stands as argument, and the entry now says so rather than implying literature support.
- A dedicated search found **no application of a latent-class or no-gold-standard model to NDE, weld inspection, POD or industrial inspection anywhere**. The transplant genuinely is unoccupied. That is why this stays a presupposition-failure rejection and not a disguised-known one.

**UQ-45** is killed as a disguised known on one paper, and that paper came back confirmed on its core: Asdrubali, Grazieschi & Gandola 2025, *Energies* 18(5):1308 exists, its corpus is as described, and all five quoted test statistics are in its running text. The adversary that found it had genuinely read it. Three corrections narrow the kill:

- **No variance decomposition.** Nothing in the body splits the declared-GWP spread into a methodological and a real-performance component — no share of variance, no eta-squared, only significance tests and one single-predictor R². It answers "is this factor significant?", not "how much of the difference is attributable to it?", which is an estimand difference and not merely an estimator one.
- **Two of the candidate's four methodological factors are untested.** Product category rule version is not a factor (EN 15804 +A1 and +A2 are treated as substantially equivalent), allocation is not examined, and declared-unit conversion appears only as a harmonisation step the authors perform.
- **Its headline does not hold for concrete, and it does not say so.** For ready-mix concrete the programme operator (F = 6.84, p = 0.0002) and the country (F = 4.42, p = 0.0008) **are** significant; the paper explains this by an untested overlap between programme operators and geography, calls the result "substantial equivalence" in the same breath, and its Discussion still concludes that the programme operator and background database "did not play a very relevant role in determining embodied climate change impacts of all products considered", with no carve-out.

The verdict holds — limb (i) is asked and answered in print, and an estimand upgrade plus two extra factors is the covariate-and-estimator swap this ledger rejects. But the entry's "belongs back in this ledger as a narrower candidate" now has content, written down so a later batch need not rediscover it.

## What verification changed in the three survivors

None of them moved, and all three had load-bearing descriptions corrected.

**UQ-41.** The record said arXiv 2603.25835 "never states how the data separate" unexpected from planned deorbits. It does state it, and the method is a **time cutoff** — the first 1,000 days as a baseline of "true in-orbit failures", with planned deorbits said to "begin to dominate only after day 1,395" — so no individual deorbit is classified by cause. That is a sharper criticism than the one the record made, and it replaces it. Confirmed: the 0.0128% mean daily failure probability is over the first 1,000 days, its n is stated nowhere in the section, and there is no stratification by launch, group, batch, shell or generation, no random effect or frailty, and no mention of space weather inside the survival section. The "likely reflecting different satellite batches or generations" conjecture is verbatim and untested. Corrected against the entry: 97.05% is the **TOTAL row** of arXiv 2307.02923's Table 1 over 35 launches, not the efficiency of 34 preceding ones, and that paper contains none of the words batch, production, manufactur or design flaw — so it is per-launch descriptive prior art, not prior art on a lot effect. The February 2024 deorbit of about 100 working first-generation satellites after SpaceX "identified a common issue" is confirmed as an event, but **neither report says whether they came from one launch, one batch or several**, so the anti-selection mechanism stands while its batch-level reading does not.

**UQ-42.** Both priors confirmed, and the candidate's increment survives intact: neither compares the properties of failed against retained systems, and neither recomputes any downstream statistic. MC3D's 38,739 and 85.5% are exact, and "The vast majority of errors come from the failure to converge to the required precision" is verbatim — but its covariate breakdown exists **only as unreadable figure bars**, so no rate may be quoted off it, and its abstract works in a different frame again (72,589 unique structures, 32,013 final after removing 1,129 duplicates, so a naive 44% is not a success rate). One new fact favours the candidate: MC3D **excluded** lanthanides and actinides because their pseudopotentials "often lead to numerical instabilities and a significant failure rate", with no figure attached — the candidate's own premise appearing as a design decision in its nearest prior. The correction that matters: Hilgers does **not** recover failures by escalating settings. Its limits are fixed and it states as an assumption that "Calculation parameters do not change from batch to batch"; recovery comes from ML-predicted starting inputs. So the entry's claim that it demonstrates presupposition 3 is struck, and the promotion item's "ladder-recovered stratum" has no published precedent — it is the candidate's instrument to justify, not an established one to borrow.

**UQ-44.** The entry's strongest objection rests on a quote nobody has read. "Ground False" is an **ICML 2026 workshop poster**, not a main-track paper; the OpenReview forum is behind a browser check, the PDF fetch failed, and no arXiv version exists, so only the abstract and project README were reachable. Confirmed from the abstract: 367 ProofNet formalizations, 104 (28%) false plus 100 (27%) unfaithful-but-not-false giving 204 (56%), and "provability, logical strength, and root cause" verbatim. Corrected: the 4.8–60.0% range is across the **six other** benchmarks and excludes ProofNet's 56%. **Unverified:** the sentence "absolute quality is dominated by curatorial process" is in neither the abstract nor the README, and all three uses in the entry are now flagged. Opening that body is added as promotion condition (iv), with the explicit consequence that a signed formal-versus-published-source strength rate inside it would make this a disguised known.

Best (FMM 2021) came back exact on every figure, with its comparator decisively the declaration's own proof term — two cautions recorded: 2,877 counts *results* not declarations, so 2877/80000 is not a rate, and 183/475 is a single-file subset. Meek et al. needed four corrections, all moving it **away** from the candidate: the triples are in Table 2 (Table 1 is sorries and axioms); the counts 175, 4,636 and 6,960 are a textbook statement count and two Table 3 proof counts, not this analysis's units (the real n are about 95, 1,864 and 1,438, derived by summing cells); for its largest corpus the comparator is largely the system's own generated docstring, making 73% a self-consistency figure; and **"Stronger" does not include dropping a redundant hypothesis** — the rubric says "a sound generalization", and the one hypothesis-dropping case was labelled *Different*. ## What to carry forward

1. **Two unlocatable citations in two batches.** Both were load-bearing, both had a plausible author and year, and both took a dedicated fetch to expose. Treat an uncheckable citation as unsupported, not as weak support.
2. **A real paper used for the wrong claim is the commoner failure.** Three cases this batch: a repeatability paper read as an accuracy result, a Table 1 read for Table 2's figures, and a rubric category read as including the one thing it excludes.
3. **"Already answered" deserves the same scrutiny as "never asked".** UQ-45's kill was right that the question is asked, and wrong about what the answer contains — no variance decomposition, two of four factors untested, and a significant result in the largest product class that the paper's own conclusion overrides.
4. **A denominator is part of a figure.** 38,739 against 72,589; 97.05% over 35 launches against 34; 2,877 results against 80,000 declarations; 175 statements against 1,864 entities. Four in one batch.
5. **An evidence base cited for a presupposition may not contain it.** Hilgers was the entry's proof that failed calculations are recoverable by escalation, and it escalates nothing.
