## 1. Restatement in my own words

Pick an asset class whose individual units are listed in a register that lets you date each unit's entry into service and its exit (either from a recorded exit date or by reconstructing exits to a stated resolution, e.g. by differencing annual files), and that also tells you — or lets you reconstruct — *why* each unit left. Name the cohort before you touch the data. Then produce three things. (i) How long units actually stay in service: a survival estimate (median, quartiles, and the probability of surviving to the design working life the code declared for them), with left truncation and right censoring handled and the denominator stated. (ii) Why they leave: having first written down a *rule* that decides class membership — the "structural" class is exactly those recorded reasons that more design resistance would have prevented, everything else is non-structural, and whatever the rule cannot decide goes into an explicit unclassifiable bucket — report the share in each class, plus an agreement statistic from a sub-sample two adjudicators scored, plus the unclassifiable share. (iii) What that does to code calibration: take the code's target reliability, which is stated over a nominal reference period, and re-express the lifetime failure probability over the *realized* service-life distribution instead; report the change in implied reliability index — and report the same figure again under the rival reading in which the reference period is merely the normalising window of the load statistics and not a duration anyone is exposed to, where the answer should be "no change".

Plain version: assets are designed as though they will be used for 50, 75 or 100 years. Do they actually leave service at that age, and do they leave because they ran out of strength or because somebody wanted them wider, straighter, or gone? And if the answer is the second, does the reliability arithmetic the codes are built on change?

Two things I note before attacking, because the record asks me to read the wording literally rather than generously:
- **Clause C3 is a disjunction, not a single instrument.** The canonical wording is "per-unit commissioning and removal dates **(or removal dates reconstructible to a stated resolution)**". The record's own Gate 2 graded presupposition 1 "CONTRADICTED-IN-PART" because NBI has no removal-date field and exits are recovered by differencing annual submittals. That is *reconstruction to annual resolution*, i.e. the second disjunct, i.e. the clause **satisfied**. Gate 2 attacked a premise ("prints a removal date per unit") that is not in its own canonical question.
- **The measurand exemption is DECLARED, not merely noted.** V2 reports (a) the unclassifiable share, (b) an agreement statistic, and (c) the limb-(iii) figure *under both readings of the reference period, with the convention arm's expected answer pre-stated*. Those three are reported quantities, not assumed ones, so attack 4 cannot reach them as false premises. This matters below, because my best-sourced finding lands exactly on the clause the question already converts into a measurand.
