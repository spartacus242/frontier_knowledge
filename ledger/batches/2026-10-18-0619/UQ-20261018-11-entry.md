Domain(s): AI architecture — sparse mixture-of-experts design and MoE inference systems (secondary: multilingual evaluation).
Question (canonical): For released sparse-MoE checkpoints run at inference under a declared drop rule (R1 arrival-order overflow; R2 lowest-gating-score drop) at C>=3 capacity factors on M>=2 checkpoints, with strata pre-declared (language/script, document domain, token-frequency decile, position bucket): (a) does the joint distribution of drop events over strata depart from the marginal input distribution over the same strata under a pre-declared exchangeability/permutation test with per-stratum drop-rate intervals; and (b) in a paired contrast against a capacity-free run on identical inputs, weights and decoding, is each stratum's loss/accuracy delta a positive function of that stratum's drop rate — a slope, against an in-harness evaluation-and-seed noise floor, that lets an operator forecast a capacity factor's incidence before shipping it? (V2, the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation; V1 left the drop rule, the noise floor and the frequency proxy unspecified — see record.)
Alternative formulations: When an MoE discards tokens to meet an expert-capacity limit, who pays — is the reported aggregate degradation spread evenly or is it a tax with an identifiable incidence? / Is MoE token dropping distributionally neutral, or can the per-stratum accuracy penalty be read off the per-stratum drop rate?

What would count as an answer: A measured joint distribution of drop events over the pre-declared strata vs the marginal input distribution, with a pre-declared permutation test and per-stratum intervals; plus a paired per-stratum loss/accuracy delta (capacity-limited vs capacity-free, same inputs/weights/decoding) regressed on that stratum's drop rate, crossed over M>=2 checkpoints x C>=3 capacity factors x R=2 rules, the slope judged against an in-harness noise floor. "No" = flat drop rates within intervals and no slope above the floor. "Yes" = a named stratum ordering plus a slope with a usable forecast interval.
