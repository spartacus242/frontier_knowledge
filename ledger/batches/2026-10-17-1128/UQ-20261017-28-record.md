Question (canonical) — V1, the single permitted Gate 1 reformulation, and the assessed version:
At a fixed checkpoint theta of a real pretraining run, and with the scalarization declared in advance as (i) PRIMARY: the variance of the scalar projection of the minibatch gradient onto the unit vector along the full-corpus (or large-reference-batch) gradient direction at theta, and (ii) SECONDARY: the trace ratio tr(Sigma_design)/tr(Sigma_iid) — what is the design effect deff(theta, B) of the stack's actual batch formation, defined as that variance under the loader's shipped sampling scheme (shard assignment plus shuffle buffer plus rank sharding, at its production buffer size and seed) divided by the same variance over batches resampled i.i.d. with replacement from the same corpus at the identical nominal batch size B, with the two arms differing in nothing but the sampling design; what gradient-space intraclass correlation rho does it imply under deff = 1 + (m-1)rho for a declared within-batch cluster size m; does multiplying tr(Sigma) by deff move the gradient-noise-scale estimate B_simple = tr(Sigma)/|G|^2 and the critical-batch-size prescription read off it by more than the pre-registered band; and is the correction's sign and size stable across checkpoints and across shuffle-buffer sizes? Pre-committed bands: NULL (deff <= 1.1 at stated power, which licenses the i.i.d. assumption the field makes), MODEST (1.1-2), MATERIAL (> 2). Required on >= 2 open pretraining stacks whose loader is inspectable and >= 1 corpus, with intervals on every ratio.

Question (canonical) — V0, as submitted at intake (recorded, superseded):
"In deployed pretraining data loaders, what is the design effect of real batch formation — the ratio of the gradient estimator's variance under the loader's actual clustered sampling to its variance under i.i.d. sampling from the same corpus at the same batch size — and does correcting for it move the gradient-noise-scale estimate and the critical-batch-size prescription derived from it?"
Why reformulated (well-posedness only): V0 left the vector-valued estimator unscalarized (the intake's own Gate 1 obligation), did not fix the i.i.d. comparator's replacement convention or the point of evaluation (fixed theta vs along a trajectory), named no cluster size m against which rho is defined, and set no band separating a null from a finding.

Alternative formulations:
1. Pretraining batches come from shard-and-shuffle-buffer schemes rather than i.i.d. draws: how much does that clustering inflate gradient variance, and does the inflation bias the batch-size rules the field reads off measured gradient noise?
2. What is the design-corrected effective batch size of a large pretraining run, as against its nominal one — i.e. how many i.i.d. examples is a nominal batch of B worth?
3. (own-field, instrumentation words) When a GNS tracker reports B_simple, is it reporting the noise of the run's actual sampling design or of a counterfactual i.i.d. one, and how far apart are they?

Motivating gap:
Survey methodology has a century-old correction for exactly this situation: when units arrive in clusters rather than independently, every variance-based quantity carries deff = 1 + (m-1)rho, and an uncorrected variance both mis-states precision and mis-sets any design chosen from it. Pretraining has the structure and not the correction. Verified at first hand this run, not asserted: McCandlish et al., "An Empirical Model of Large-Batch Training" (arXiv:1812.06162, OPENED), defines B_simple = tr(Sigma)/|G|^2 (Eq. 2.9) and B_noise = tr(H Sigma)/(G^T H G) (Eq. 2.8), and its footnote 6 states that the batch-variance relation Sigma/B "is strictly true only when training examples are sampled independently from the same data distribution", adds that the conclusions "can be altered straightforwardly to account for correlated samples", and does not carry that out; the paper discusses no data loader, no shuffling, no sharding and no within-batch correlation. So the assumption is explicit, load-bearing and self-flagged in the foundational source, and the correction was declined there. Meanwhile real loaders read shards into a bounded shuffle buffer, so documents within one batch are correlated in source, language, length and crawl segment; the error does not cancel, because the same clustered variance enters both the estimate and the prescription read off it. Two further live facts found this run sharpen the gap rather than close it: (a) Merrill et al., "Critical Batch Size Revisited" (arXiv:2505.23971, AI2, NeurIPS 2025, OPENED) measures CBS directly by branched training and concludes "the gradient noise scale underestimates the CBS by several orders of magnitude" and "the noise scale cannot be used reliably as a proxy for the CBS", while never discussing i.i.d. sampling, shuffling, shards, ordering or batch formation, and estimating the noise scale on "offline (i.e., unseen) data" batches whose formation it does not specify — so the published discrepancy is between a GNS measured under an unstated sampling design and a CBS measured under the run's real one, which is the candidate's quantity sitting unnamed inside the field's headline anomaly; (b) Gray et al., "Normalization Layer Per-Example Gradients are Sufficient to Predict Gradient Noise Scale in Transformers" (arXiv:2411.00999, Cerebras, OPENED) ships a LayerNorm CUDA kernel that tracks GNS in production and drives a batch-size schedule (18% wall-time gain), does not state or check the independence assumption behind its Eq. 1, and records the unexplained caveat "Changes in the batch size do not have the predicted effect".

Presuppositions (each established, flagged or contested, with what settled it):
1. Real loaders cluster-sample — shard plus shuffle-buffer schemes leave within-batch correlation in document attributes. FLAGGED, checkable per stack from loader code and corpus layout, NOT verified here: this run's search budget went to the novelty check, and no loader source was read. Fully global-shuffle pipelines exist (Ray Data documents a full global shuffle option; Mosaic/webdataset expose buffer-size knobs), so clustering must be measured per stack rather than assumed, and a stack with a true global shuffle is a legitimate deff = 1 arm rather than a counterexample to the design.
2. Within-batch correlation in document attributes implies within-batch correlation in per-example gradients. CONTESTED and the technical core: attribute ICC is not gradient ICC, and V1 therefore measures the gradient-space quantity directly rather than inferring it. Nothing opened this run measures a within-batch gradient ICC; Faghri et al. (arXiv:2007.04532, OPENED) assumes it away in the opposite direction — "the covariance between gradients of any two samples is zero" — while separately exploiting gradient-space clustering to build strata, which is evidence the gradient-space structure is real without being a measurement of rho under a shipped loader.
3. The noise-scale framework's variance term is estimable and its i.i.d. assumption is load-bearing for the critical-batch-size claim. ESTABLISHED from the primary source this run (1812.06162 footnote 6, Eqs. 2.8-2.9, App. A.1's two-batch-size estimator with EWMA smoothing, quoted above). The one qualification the paper itself adds is finite-population: footnote 6 notes without-replacement sampling scales like 1/B - 1/D and the authors restrict to B << D or with-replacement, which is the (1 - B/D) correction and not the clustering one.
4. A design effect is well defined for a vector-valued estimator. DISCHARGED at Gate 1 by declaring the scalarization (projection along the reference gradient direction, primary; trace ratio, secondary). Not an open question.
5. Batch-size decisions are actually taken from measured noise scale in practice. ESTABLISHED for a named, non-hypothetical audience: Cerebras built per-example-gradient GNS instrumentation into the backward pass and used the tracked GNS to set a batch-size schedule (arXiv:2411.00999); Merrill et al. record that the noise scale "appears to have been adopted in practice", citing the GPT-3 technical report. PARTLY CONTESTED in the other direction and this matters: Merrill et al.'s own conclusion tells practitioners not to rely on it, so the audience is a real but contracting one, and the question must address realized optimizer noise, not only the prescription.
6. The i.i.d. ideal is the right comparator. CONTESTED, and V1 keeps the framing explicit: one could hold that the clustered design is the intended estimator and the i.i.d. variance an irrelevant counterfactual. The question survives either way, and on a stronger footing than intake claimed: the prescription was derived under the i.i.d. form (footnote 6), and Merrill et al. estimate GNS on offline data while the run's realized noise comes from the production loader, so the two designs are already being silently mixed inside a published comparison.

What would count as an answer:
On >= 2 open pretraining stacks and >= 1 corpus, at >= 3 checkpoints each: (a) deff and the implied gradient-space rho under the declared scalarization, from two arms differing only in the sampling design — the stack's real batches versus i.i.d.-with-replacement resamples of the same corpus at identical B, at the same theta, with bootstrap intervals; (b) the design-corrected B_simple against the uncorrected one, with intervals, and the share of Merrill et al.'s GNS-to-CBS gap that deff closes (sign included: deff > 1 raises the corrected noise scale, i.e. moves it toward the measured CBS, so the arithmetic of how much of "several orders of magnitude" a plausible deff can account for is part of the answer); (c) the implied change in prescribed batch size, equivalently the design-corrected effective batch size B/deff in i.i.d.-equivalent units; (d) stability of the correction's sign and size across checkpoints and across shuffle-buffer sizes, which tells implementers whether a bigger buffer is a sufficient fix or whether shard granularity dominates. deff indistinguishable from 1 at stated power is a publishable null: it licenses the i.i.d. assumption the field already makes, and it would also establish that the GNS-CBS discrepancy is not a sampling-design artefact. No retraining is required — the measurement is two resampling arms at frozen theta — which is what makes the question answerable outside a frontier lab.

Significance — consequence:
If deff is materially above 1: every GNS number in the literature and in production trackers (P4's kernel; the torch-foresight GNS module; the GPT-3-lineage practice P3 cites) is a statement about whichever design its batches happened to come from, and the run's realized optimizer noise is not the measured one; nominal batch size stops being the right unit and B/deff (i.i.d.-equivalent) replaces it; the sign of the correction points toward P3's documented underestimate, so part of a published orders-of-magnitude anomaly acquires a measurable, non-speculative candidate contributor; and shuffle-buffer size becomes an optimization hyperparameter with a variance price rather than a pure throughput/memory knob. If deff is indistinguishable from 1 at stated power: the i.i.d. assumption the field makes without stating it is licensed explicitly, footnote 6's un-taken extension is retired as unnecessary, P3's discrepancy is established as not a sampling-design artefact (which narrows the search for what it is), and loader engineers get a defensible answer to "how much shuffle is enough" in variance terms. Honest discount: because P3 has already told practitioners not to read CBS off the noise scale, limb (c)'s prescriptive payoff is smaller than it would have been in 2024; the durable consequence is about realized gradient noise and about the interpretation of any GNS measurement, not about rescuing the prescription.

Reason for silence:
A disciplinary and artifact boundary with a vocabulary gap on top of it, and one incentive effect. The noise-scale estimator belongs to an optimization/scaling-laws literature whose unit is "a batch" and whose independence condition is discharged in a footnote (P2, fn. 6) rather than in an experiment; batch formation belongs to data-infrastructure code owned by systems engineers whose success criteria are throughput, determinism and resumability, and whose own name for the relevant property — "shuffle quality" — is a set-overlap or entropy heuristic that was never connected to an estimator's variance; nobody owns the join, and the join's natural vocabulary (design effect, intraclass correlation, effective sample size under clustering) lives in complex-survey statistics, which has essentially no presence in ML training papers, so the question has no name in the field that would act on it. Two reinforcing factors: measuring a gradient-space ICC needs per-example gradients at pretraining scale, which only became cheap with 2024-era instrumentation (P4's LayerNorm kernel); and once P3 declared the noise scale unreliable in 2025, the incentive shifted to replacing the estimator outright rather than auditing its inputs, which makes the un-taken correction less likely to be taken now than it was before. Competing mechanism an adversary should press, and which this record does not resolve: the silence may be the absence of a problem — if production loaders at frontier batch sizes draw from buffers large enough, across enough shards and enough data-parallel ranks, that per-batch cluster size m is small relative to B, then deff is 1 + epsilon by construction and the footnote was right to call the correction unnecessary. That is exactly what limb (a) measures, so the mechanism is testable rather than rhetorical, but presupposition 1 is unverified in this record and no loader source was read.

Scores: Well-posedness 3/3 · Significance 2/3
Well-posedness 3/3: after the Gate 1 reformulation the estimand is a ratio between two explicitly specified sampling arms at a frozen checkpoint, the vector-valued estimator is scalarized in a declared way, the comparator's replacement convention and B are fixed, rho is defined against a declared m, intervals and pre-committed bands are stated, and a null is a stated result rather than a failure. It is answerable without retraining and without frontier-lab access, on open stacks, with an instrument that already exists (P4). The one residual is that deff will be reported per (stack, buffer size, checkpoint) and the question does not say how those are aggregated into a headline number; that is a reporting choice, not an identification problem.
Significance 2/3, not 3/3: the consequence for the headline prescription is discounted by P3 having already rejected the noise scale as a CBS proxy by direct measurement, and by the arithmetic that a modest deff explains little of an orders-of-magnitude gap; what carries the score is that the quantity governs realized optimizer noise and the interpretation of every GNS measurement, that a named audience currently acts on tracked GNS (P4), that loader engineers have no variance account of a knob they tune, and that the null is genuinely publishable.

Suggested first step toward an answer (pointer only — not taken): at one frozen OLMo (or torchtitan/litgpt) checkpoint whose loader is inspectable, emit N real batches from the production loader and N batches of the same nominal size resampled i.i.d. with replacement from the same corpus shard set, compute per-example gradients with the LayerNorm-only estimator of arXiv:2411.00999 to keep the cost independent of sequence length, and report the two arms' projected-gradient variance ratio with a bootstrap interval before touching buffer size or additional checkpoints. Verify presupposition 1 first by reading the loader's shard assignment and buffer code, since a stack with a true global shuffle belongs in the design as a deff = 1 control arm.
