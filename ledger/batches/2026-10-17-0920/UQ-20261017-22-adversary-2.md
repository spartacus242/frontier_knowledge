## Restatement (my words, not the generator's)

Battery labs cycle dozens of nominally identical cells to death, one cell per cycler channel, all channels in one climate chamber, and call the resulting spread in cycle life "cell-to-cell variability" — a property of manufacturing. But each cell lives in exactly one channel for its whole life, so "which cell" and "which channel" are the same label: any difference the channel makes (its current/voltage calibration, its airflow and self-heating position in the chamber) is silently charged to the cell. The candidate asks two things. (a) If you deliberately move cells between channels on a schedule fixed in advance — with sham moves to control for the handling itself, and real per-channel calibration — how much of the variance in degradation *rate* belongs to the cell, how much to the channel, and how much to their interaction? (b) In the archived fast-charge datasets (Severson 2019, Attia 2020), where the same physical channels hosted different protocols in different rounds, how big is the channel-level variance when you put channel in the model as a random effect, and does the published list of "best protocols" survive that correction?

Put bluntly: is some of what the field calls cell variability actually rig variability, and were some of the winning fast-charge protocols just winning channels?

## My own arithmetic (labelled as mine, not a source's conclusion)

Taking a textbook-typical ageing activation energy Ea ≈ 50 kJ/mol, d ln(rate)/dT = Ea/(RT²) = 50000 / (8.314 × 298²) ≈ 0.068 K⁻¹, so a 0.5 °C position-to-position offset moves degradation rate by ~3.5% and a 2 °C offset by ~14%. This is my calculation on an assumed Ea (I did not open a primary source for Ea or for the chamber gradient), so it is an order-of-magnitude plausibility check only. It is offered against my own attack, not for it: it shows the magnitudes the candidate needs are not absurd, and the Attia SI's *"cell temperature increase during cycling is significant"* in a *"single temperature chamber for all channels"* is primary-source evidence that cell temperature in the flagship dataset is set by self-heating against position-dependent convection. I therefore could not build a sourced magnitude kill, and I do not assert one.

## Single strongest killing point

Attia et al.'s own Supplementary Information says *"We disallowed repeated runs of the same charging protocol within a given round for inducing more exploration"*, and its Table 5 and Table 2 give a *"48-channel cycler"* with 46/46/48/48 cells per round — so in the flagship closed-loop dataset there are only ~4 cells per channel and nearly as many protocol levels as cells, and any channel variance component there is identified through the protocol-similarity prior rather than from data. The candidate's headline archival promise, "does the top-k protocol set change under channel-shrunken estimates", is therefore answerable in Attia only as a function of a model V2 never pre-specifies; the limb survives only in Severson, where ~52 cells' worth of within-policy between-channel contrast remains.
