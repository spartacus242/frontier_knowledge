## Restatement in my own words

Pick alloy systems for which two or more groups have each independently published a CALPHAD Gibbs-energy
description. Build one digitised experimental corpus for each system in which every data point is tagged
with the literature study it came from. Score each published description on that corpus twice under one
pre-declared metric: once on the whole corpus (the "apparent" residual, recomputed by the evaluator because
the papers themselves print no fit number), and once by a leave-one-study-out procedure (the "predictive"
residual). Then report (a) the Kendall tau between the two resulting orderings of the competing
descriptions, (b) the distribution of predictive-minus-apparent residual, in metric units and as a ratio,
and (c) how many systems, and which, change their top-ranked description. It is the free-R idea from
macromolecular crystallography, and the apparent-vs-externally-validated idea from clinical prediction
modelling, pointed at published thermodynamic assessments.

## Measurand exemption — DECLARED AND APPLIED, out loud, before grading

Two clauses in R1 are REPORTED QUANTITIES, not assumptions, so attack 4 does not reach them and I do not
grade around them:

- The metric/weighting clause ("one pre-declared common metric" over heterogeneous property types). The
  record's answer item (4) mandates "a weighting-sensitivity panel showing the tau under at least two
  pre-declared weighting schemes". The worry that weighting decides the ordering is therefore a measurand.
- The null. P6 pre-declares that "a finding that the orderings agree (tau indistinguishable from 1, small
  gap) is an ANSWER to this question, not a refutation of it". I do not attack the candidate for the
  possibility that apparent fit turns out to be a sufficient selector.

Where P6 does NOT reach, and this is the crux of my strongest attack (MY OWN REASONING ABOUT THE RECORD'S
LOGIC, NOT A SOURCE FINDING): P6 protects an EMPIRICAL finding that apparent fit happens to rank the same
way as a genuine out-of-sample score. It does not protect an ANALYTIC IDENTITY under which the two
residuals are the same quantity by construction, because then tau, the gap distribution, the bootstrap
interval over folds, the weighting panel and the per-system table in "what would count as an answer" are
all known to be 1, 0, 0, 0 and 0 before any corpus is digitised. An exemption for a reported quantity is
not an exemption for a quantity fixed by arithmetic.

## C6 in full — the strongest attack, with each step labelled

**Step 1 (DOCUMENT FINDING about the record, established first-hand by the verification pass and relied on
without re-fetching).** The answer protocol contains no re-estimation step: *refit, re-optimise, adjust,
tune, calibrate* and *train* return zero occurrences. It also contains no statement that the parameter set
is fixed or frozen — the record's only "fixed" is about the metric, "whose full parameter sets are
retrievable" asserts retrievability and not invariance, and R1 (ii) says only "complete enough to
evaluate". It declares no fold aggregation, while its uncertainty device is "a bootstrap interval over
systems and over hold-out folds".

**Step 2 (MY OWN REASONING ABOUT THE CLAIM'S LOGIC).** The objects ordered are "independently published
CALPHAD assessments ... whose full parameter sets are retrievable". The published parameter values are
what makes an assessment that assessment and are the thing the frame selects on. So the protocol has two
readings and only two:

*Horn A, parameters at their published values.* Removing study k from the corpus changes nothing about the
model, so the model's deviation at every datum of study k is identical whether or not study k is in the
corpus. Withholding has no effect on anything measured.

*Horn B, refit on the K−1 retained studies.* Then what is ordered is no longer the published assessments
but re-optimised variants produced by one evaluator under one weighting; the published parameter values —
the frame's own selection criterion — are discarded, and limb (b) becomes an optimism estimate for the
evaluator's refitting pipeline rather than a property of anybody's published description.

**Step 3 (ARITHMETIC — mine, and independently the verification checker's, labelled as such).** Under
Horn A with pooled, datum-equal aggregation, each datum appears in exactly one held-out fold, so the
leave-one-study-out residual equals the apparent residual datum for datum. Then limb (a) tau = 1 exactly,
limb (b) gap = 0 exactly, limb (c) count = 0, for every system and every corpus — answerable in a page
with no CALPHAD knowledge and no digitisation. **I do not assert this for every admissible aggregation**,
because it is false for one: under mean-over-folds aggregation the gap equals
−(1/n)·Σ_k (n_k − n/K)(r_k − r̄), the negative size–residual covariance over studies, which takes either
sign and can reorder (verified worked counter-example: two studies of 100 and 10 data, apparent 1.8182 vs
2.0000 with A ahead, fold-mean 5.5000 vs 2.0000 with B ahead, rank correlation −1; Arlot & Celisse define
every cross-validation estimator in the fold-mean form at their eq. (9)).

**Step 4 (MY OWN REASONING, and the step a verifier should test hardest).** That escape does not rescue
limb (b), because the fold-mean gap is a function only of study sizes and per-study residuals. It is
exactly the change from datum-equal to study-equal weighting of the *same* per-datum deviations, and
carries no out-of-sample content whatsoever. The record's own answer item (4) mandates a weighting panel
"since R1 presupposes the metric choice is not what decides the ordering" — so under the one aggregation
that makes limb (b) non-zero, the entire reported gap is the artefact that item (4) exists to control.

**What would have grounded Step 4 in a source, and was not run on my budget:** a methodological source on
fold-aggregation choice in cross-validation estimators — Arlot & Celisse's survey itself, read past the
eq. (9) definition the verification pass already carried, or a crystallographic statement that R_free is
defined only against a model refined on the working set. I spent my four fetches on novelty threats and
the field's own methodology page instead, and I say so rather than recording the leg as sourced.

**The weakest sourced support I do have.** Thermo-Calc's own data-optimization methodology page (READ
LEVEL: full page, help.thermocalc.com/2025b/content/data_optimization/data_opt_summary.htm) states "Data
optimization is about adjusting the model parameters so that calculated equilibria fit well with
experimental data." and "During the optimization, the values of these variables are varied in order to
find a fit between the calculated equilibria and the experimental data" — the second sentence was served
TRUNCATED and the summariser reports it continues "that you base the optimization on."; I reproduce the
truncation and flag it rather than completing a cut string. OPENING WORDS SCOPE: both sentences are
scoped to *data optimization*, the parameter-adjustment step, and both ASSERT that this is where
experimental data act. The page mentions no withholding, held-out, validation, cross-validation or test
data anywhere, and does not mention choosing between competing published assessments. Honest limit: the
summariser states in terms that the page "never states that data are used *only* there", so this page
supports Step 2's framing and does not close it.

**Why P6 does not cover this.** See the measurand section: a pre-declared null covers an empirical
coincidence of two orderings, not an identity that fixes both orderings before the corpus exists.

**One reading that would save the question, which the record does not take.** Score each assessment on the
studies ITS OWN AUTHORS excluded from their optimisation. That is a genuine refit-free out-of-sample
residual, and the record has the ingredient for it — P2 quotes "filled for data included in the parameter
calibration, open symbols for data shown only for comparison". But that is not "leave-one-study-out",
which is a symmetric, assessment-independent device. Nor is it what P4's defence names: the record's P4
says verbatim "the informative structures are cross-PROPERTY ... and cross-COMPOSITION-REGION", and per
record line 67 P4 "is handled by requiring the protocol to declare its hold-out structure rather than by
settling the point". So the record's own defence of its hold-out against triviality names two structures,
neither of which is the one device the canonical question asks for, and defers the choice. Testing GATE
1'S REFORMULATION as hard as the clause it replaced (rule iv): V0 left the evaluation procedure
unspecified; R1 pinned it to a recomputation protocol in which the withholding step provably does no work
