Question (canonical): On a building frame declared before any data pull in one EGMS-covered region, with the damage cut fixed in advance at Burland D>=2 (D>=3 as a pre-specified sensitivity arm) and the predictor declared as EGMS-derived within-footprint differential vertical velocity over the service observation window rather than as cumulative angular distortion since construction: (a) what are the sensitivity, specificity, ROC/AUC with intervals and — on a probabilistically drawn, independently verified sub-sample that fixes damage prevalence — the positive predictive value of exceeding each criterion's own limiting value, evaluated only for those criteria whose measurand the service supplies (Skempton–MacDonald delta/L; Burland–Wroth Delta/L under a declared inflection-point rule), with Boscardin–Cording reported as not evaluable for want of horizontal strain; and (b) how much of any shortfall is attributable to the rate-for-cumulative substitution, estimated on the subset also carrying bed-joint levelling? (This is V2, the one Gate 1 reformulation, scored as canonical; V1 asked more loosely whether the Skempton–MacDonald / Burland–Wroth / Boscardin–Cording family discriminates damaged from undamaged buildings at population scale.)
Alternative formulations: Do the settlement-damage criteria embedded in geotechnical practice work when applied to every building in a region rather than to the few dozen case histories they were fitted on? / Over a declared building population, how well does satellite-measured differential settlement rate separate buildings with recorded damage from those without, at the thresholds practice actually uses?
Motivating gap: The criteria in daily use were fitted to small 1950s–1980s case-history sets because population-scale differential settlement was unmeasurable; a free continent-scale persistent-scatterer service makes the criterion evaluable as a screening test. Intake added that the InSAR-and-damage literature only draws risk maps, presupposing the discrimination. Gate 2 falsified that last clause, and it was the clause the candidate rested on.
Presuppositions: (1) EGMS supplies scatterer density sufficient for a within-footprint differential on a usable share of buildings — contested; Drougkas et al. IDW-interpolated 10 m grids to fill scatterer gaps, and the achieved frame is instrument-defined, not a sample of the stock. (2) The service's windowed roof-level vertical velocity maps onto cumulative foundation-level distortion — strongly contested; Prosperi et al. Sec. 5.2 attributes to Peduto et al. that satellite SRI values "may be lower" over a fixed 5-year window. (3) An independent, joinable building-level damage record exists at population scale — contested and unevidenced; all three priors used bespoke façade/levelling campaigns. (4) Damage has other causes, so this measures discrimination not attribution — established. (5) Each threshold has one stated definition — refuted for Boscardin–Cording, whose horizontal-strain input the product does not supply; Peduto et al. already computed Burland–Wroth at the original inflection-point definition. (6) The building, not the party-wall-joined terrace, is the right unit — contested and unresolved; Peduto et al. 2017 returned different damage ranges at the two units over the same area. (7) Damage and motion records share a period — flagged; V2's rate predictor declares the window.
What would count as an answer: Per-criterion sensitivity, specificity and ROC/AUC with intervals, plus PPV on a verification sub-sample that fixes prevalence, on a frame and measurand mapping declared before any data pull; the rate-for-cumulative substitution as a pre-specified sensitivity analysis against the levelling subset; Boscardin–Cording recorded as not evaluable rather than evaluated on a surrogate; a demonstrated inability to estimate reported as such. For the levelling instrument this answer substantially exists; for the EGMS rate predictor and for PPV it does not.

Significance — consequence: If the thresholds discriminate near chance on EGMS rates, code committees learn a daily-use criterion has no population-scale validity under that instrument; if they discriminate well, population screening is licensed and municipalities and insurers would act.
