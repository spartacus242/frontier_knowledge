Domain(s): Medicine — health systems, insurance coverage, access to care. Target field: US health-policy measurement (CMS, payers, KFF/trade analysts already ranking payers on the metric; specialty societies). Method-owning neighbours swept: queueing/OR (unobserved balking, silent abandonment), public-economics administrative burden and ordeal/take-up, survey methodology (coverage error, endogenous denominators), fair-lending discouraged-borrower econometrics, disability-insurance allowance-rate stringency.
Question (canonical): For a pre-declared set of payer-by-service cells drawn from the first years of CMS-0057-F public prior-authorization reporting, what fraction of prior-authorization-requiring clinical decisions terminate on the provider side before any request reaches the payer, and does cross-payer variation in the published denial rate predict that pre-submission attrition rate with a negative sign — in what share of cells does adding attrition reverse the ranking of payers by obstruction, against a flip threshold fixed before any data pull? (V2; the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation. V1 and the two factual corrections that forced it are in batches/2026-10-21-0452/UQ-20261021-04-record.md.)
Alternative formulations: (a) Does a low published prior-authorization denial rate mean a payer approves more care, or that fewer requests were ever filed? (b) Treating the new public prior-authorization metrics as an instrument with an endogenous denominator, estimate pre-submission attrition and the bias it induces in the metric as a measure of access to care.
Motivating gap: CMS-0057-F requires payers to publish, from CY2025, percentages approved/denied/overturned on denominators of requests the payer received or adjudicated; response time is measured from payer receipt, not provider send. Every published figure is conditioned on arrival, so care never requested cannot appear in it, while analysts (KFF, 13 Aug 2026; Becker's) are already ranking named payers on those percentages. The field has named denominator-side confounds it can see from payer files (gold carding, case mix, service mix, no counts) and none on the provider side.
Presuppositions: P1 arrival-conditioned denominator — ESTABLISHED, but not uniform ("CMS currently provides suggested denominators", standardisation only proposed in a 2026 rule; DHCS prints "adjudicated" against a narrative "received"). P2 pre-submission terminations identifiable from provider records — CONTESTED, lower bound only. P3 requirement lists matchable to orders — PARTLY ESTABLISHED, needs a sensitivity arm. P4 payers gain from submission friction — CONTESTED, carried as the hypothesis under test. P5 substitution vs abandonment separable — CONTESTED (already executed for drugs: 46.2%/53.8%, Brot-Goldberg et al.). P6 comparable cross-payer/service reporting — WEAKENED (percentages only, no counts, no service breakdown nationally, drugs excluded, inconsistent methods). P7 "public for the first time in 2026" — FALSE as stated; true only as "first nationally, on a uniform federal specification", and even that is qualified by the suggested-denominator finding.
What would count as an answer: Paired cell-level estimates of (i) the printed denial rate and (ii) a provider-side pre-submission attrition rate over PA-requiring decisions identified by a pre-declared matching rule, with the sign and magnitude of their cross-cell association under one primary estimator plus declared sensitivity arms (matching error, substitution-vs-abandonment, received-vs-adjudicated denominator), the post-submission auto-denial share reported separately because it loads the same metric with the opposite sign, and the share of cells whose obstruction ranking flips against a pre-fixed threshold. A null answers it; a negative association of stated size makes it a respecification question. Gate 5 note: on the present protocol the null is NOT yet interpretable (see strongest remaining objection).

Significance — consequence: If attrition is substantial and negatively associated with the printed rate, the metric is partly a gauge of submission friction and a published payer ranking is partly inverted; if small and uncorrelated, the metric survives its sharpest available criticism on the record while the trade press is already ranking payers on it. Either way, what a reader may conclude from an annually published number changes.
Significance — audience: Demonstrated, not assumed: a seven-author KFF issue brief on exactly these files (13 Aug 2026) that says it is "not possible to identify what is driving the differences"; a Becker's cross-payer ranking; same-week trade and advocacy pickup; a Senate HSGAC letter to Humana (14 Jul 2026); a live 2026 CMS proposed rule that "would standardize the denominators". Discounted: the stated lever is misdescribed — payers cannot count requests they never received, and the rulemaking standardises which determinations count, not non-submission, so a provider-side metric would need a different vehicle.
Significance — fecundity: Whether submission friction responds to publication (CY2025 is a clean pre-publication baseline for a Goodhart test); whether the auto-denial-on-non-response category should be reclassified and how much of the reported denial range it is; whether the appeal-side analogue (7.3% appealed) has the same structure; whether an exposure denominator can be built from gold-carding rosters plus requirement lists; whether unobserved-balking estimators transfer to a regulatory metric at all.
Significance — anchoring: The administrative-burden/ordeal claim that denials are produced by burden rather than eligibility (NBER w31239, still unread by anyone in this record; w30878, read); inference under unobserved balking and silent abandonment; the sentinel/gatekeeper-effect literature in drugs and imaging; measured abandonment in pharmacy and oncology (levels, never the metric's invariance); and this ledger's "a CMS measure may not measure what it is read as measuring" family (UQ-20261017-18, now a fifth member — family-level redundancy flag to the curator).
Reason for silence: Measurement-boundary split on an eight-month-old national instrument, in the narrowed form that survived attack. The metric is specified, computed and published entirely from payer holdings; the complementary term exists only in provider EHR/ePA logs, which are only now accumulating under FHIR-based ePA. The analyst community working from payer files reached for exactly the confounds those files show (gold carding, case mix, service mix, counts, format), which is the pattern the mechanism predicts. STRICKEN: the record's claim that the provider side "has no standing to ask" and that neither side owns both terms — health economists asked the deterrence question from outside and estimated it (w30878), provider-organisation UR request data have been studied academically (Kapur, Gresenz & Studdert 2003, ~half a million requests, ABSTRACT ONLY), and ePA intermediaries and integrated payer-providers hold both terms. Why that one party never published remains unexplained.

Strongest remaining objection: As protocolled, no outcome of the sign test is diagnostic — a negative association is already observed in the field and already attributed to gold-carding exposure, the stringency model predicts a positive one, and the two attrition channels (pre-submission, which lowers the printed rate; auto-denial after non-response, which raises it) are oppositely signed; compounded by a left-hand variable that is not one quantity in year 1 (percentages only, no counts, denominators merely "suggested"), so denominator heterogeneity is confounded with the very submission-side friction the design wants to detect and the cell-level flip threshold has no sampling interpretation.
Promotion requires: pre-declaring all three sign outcomes with an explicit stringency-vs-friction decomposition and a gold-carding exposure control so some outcome is diagnostic; fixing "obstruction" among its four meanings and either resolving or dropping the cross-service limb, to reach well-posedness 3/3; and completing Gate 2 — cite w30878 and read Secs. 6–7, open Kahan et al. 2006 in full by a non-Wiley route, open the services-side RBM/sentinel sources (ACR, Radiology Business, RSNA 2009 abstract 8003072) and NBER w31239, and confirm none pairs deterrence with cross-payer published denial rates.
