Question (canonical, V2 — the one Gate 1 reformulation, taken for well-posedness only):
For a declared, version-pinned high-throughput DFT attempt frame — every structure submitted, with per-task terminal states retained (a workflow whose task records carry success/failure states, as emmet-core's `TaskState` does: `SUCCESS = "successful"` / `FAILED = "unsuccessful"`), or a frame re-run from scratch — and a pre-registered recovery protocol applied to a random sample of terminally failed tasks (escalated settings: mixing/preconditioner, smearing, k-grid, initial magnetic configuration, symmetry handling, step limits) plus cross-code recovery where a second code or database converged the same structure: (a) conditional on composition and structure under a pre-declared featurization, is the distribution of formation energy, band gap, magnetic moment/ordering and dynamical stability among recovered-failed entries the same as among retained entries (the ignorability null), and what is the hand-adjudicated split of failures into numerics-driven and physics-driven (the latter recorded as a declared outcome category — "no local minimum / imaginary modes at the escalated endpoint" — not as a missing value); and (b) when the recovered set is reinstated, by how much does at least one published downstream statistic move — a screening hit rate, the count of entries on or within X meV/atom of the convex hull, or the held-out error of a universal MLIP retrained with the recovered entries included?

Question (V1, as intaken; recorded per Gate 1):
In high-throughput DFT databases, is failure-to-converge independent of the target property conditional on composition and structure, or does the discarded set differ systematically in formation energy, band gap, magnetic ordering and dynamical stability, such that published screening hit-rates and machine-learned potentials trained on database entries are conditioned on numerics in a way nobody has quantified?

Reason for reformulation: V1 states the estimand but leaves the frame, the recovery mechanism, the handling of physics-driven failures and the downstream statistic implicit, so "does the discarded set differ" has no measurement definition. V2 pins all four and makes the null explicit. Scope is unchanged.

Alternative formulations:
1. Are the materials a high-throughput workflow fails to converge a random subset of the ones it attempts, with respect to the quantities the database is used to predict?
2. How much do the property distributions, convex hulls and MLIP training sets everyone uses change when the non-converged tail is recovered — by heroic re-runs, or by cross-database recovery where a second code converged the same material?

Motivating gap:
The inverted presupposition is never stated: that a database's entries are a representative sample of what was attempted, so only the CHEMISTRY selection (which systems anyone bothered to try) needs discussing. The chemistry-selection half is published and quantified (Trezza & Chiavazzo 2023 measure "anthropogenic" cross-domain database bias with a classifier AUC); the numerics-selection half is not. Workflows are built to make failures disappear, and this is now source-verified rather than from memory: Custodian's error handlers retry and then abandon — `max_errors` / `max_errors_per_handler`, raising `MaxCorrectionsError` / `MaxCorrectionsPerHandlerError` "if max_errors is reached" (github.com/materialsproject/custodian, docs/custodian.custodian.md) — and the task schema carries a terminal FAILED state that the consumer-facing materials documents do not surface (materialsproject/emmet, emmet-core/emmet/core/qchem/task.py). Practitioners treat exclusion as hygiene, not as a missingness mechanism ("How to exclude poorly converged calculations?", matsci.org/t/.../2523 — returned by search, NOT OPENED). The adjacent published work is about the quality and reproducibility of the calculations that DID converge (arXiv:2508.07204, abstract opened: band-gap reproducibility across workflows; arXiv:1710.06028 on DFPT phonon pitfalls, title only), which is a different estimand: quality of the retained, not informativeness of the discarded. A September 2026 review of the whole database landscape (arXiv:2609.19833, abstract opened) frames coverage entirely as property families with no database ("blank zones") and does not raise failed, non-converged or discarded calculations at all. The stake is current: screening hit-rates and counts of "newly predicted stable compounds" are computed on retained entries, and universal MLIPs are trained on database relaxations, so a non-ignorable convergence filter propagates into both.

Presuppositions:
1. Attempted-but-failed calculations are enumerable for at least one database or workflow — via workflow states, logs, or the complement of a declared attempt frame. CONTESTED but PARTLY DISCHARGED: the schema-level state exists (emmet-core `TaskState.FAILED`), and a published study enumerated its own 6660-system attempt frame with a measured success rate (Hilgers et al. 2023), so a self-run frame is demonstrably feasible; whether a public database exposes its failed inventory is a separate, unverified matter.
2. "Failure to converge" can be defined by a declared, version-pinned criterion rather than inherited from a vendor default. Established in principle; version pinning does the work.
3. A recovery mechanism gives near-truth for a sample of the dropped set — escalated settings, or cross-code/cross-database recovery. Supported by precedent: Hilgers et al. moved a frame's success rate from 64.8% to 94.3% by improving input quantities, i.e. recovered ~84% of the previously failed tasks within the same code.
4. The target properties are defined at all for dropped entries. CONTESTED: some failures occur because the structure is physically unstable. V2 handles this by declaring an outcome category rather than a missing value — which is the hypothesis (failure is informative in the strong sense), not a nuisance removed by fiat.
5. Numerics-driven and physics-driven failure are distinguishable on a hand-adjudicated sample. CONTESTED — the identification crux of the whole question.
6. Downstream consumers are traceable: specific published screening statistics and specific MLIP training sets map to affected entry sets. Plausible for MLIPs (training sets are named database snapshots); unverified for individual screening papers.
7. Maintainers and MLIP builders would act on a measured bias (re-running tails, publishing failure inventories, reweighting training sets). Unverified; the schema field's existence makes publication cheap.

What would count as an answer:
For a declared attempt frame with a pre-registered recovery protocol: (a) the measured difference in target-property distributions between recovered-dropped and retained entries, with the adjudicated split between numerics-driven and physics-driven failure; (b) the implied change in at least one published downstream statistic when the recovered set is reinstated — a screening hit rate, a count of predicted stable compounds, or convex-hull membership (or an MLIP's held-out error after retraining); and (c) an explicit null that counts as an answer: no detectable difference after conditioning on composition and structure, i.e. convergence is ignorable for these properties, which would license current practice for the first time. An answer that cannot enumerate the attempt frame is not an answer to this question but a report that the frame is unobtainable.

Significance — consequence:
If the filter is non-ignorable: every published screening hit-rate and every "N newly predicted stable compounds" count is a statistic on a numerics-conditioned subpopulation, and the direction is not neutral — failures cluster where convergence is hard (open-shell, magnetic, strongly correlated, near-metallic, soft-mode systems), which is where metastability and interesting physics live, so hull counts and hit rates are plausibly biased against exactly the chemistries screens are run to find. Universal MLIPs inherit the same filter in their training data and, worse, in their held-out splits, so reported MLIP errors are optimistic precisely on the structures where database coverage fails. If it is ignorable (the null): current practice is licensed for the first time — the hygiene framing becomes a defended position rather than an unexamined one, and the "databases are biased by numerics" worry can be retired with a number attached.
Significance — audience:
Database maintainers (Materials Project/atomate2/emmet, OQMD, AFLOW, Materials Cloud/AiiDA, Alexandria) — the action is cheap and already half-built: the FAILED terminal state exists in the schema, so publishing a failed-task inventory with inputs is a release decision, not an engineering project. MLIP builders (MACE-MP, CHGNet, M3GNet, GNoME-style pipelines) — the action is reweighting or stratifying training and held-out sets on a failure-propensity score, and re-running the tail where recovery is cheap. Benchmark designers (Matbench Discovery and successors) — the action is reporting the attempt denominator alongside the hit numerator, the computational analogue of a CONSORT flow diagram. Screening authors — the action is a sensitivity analysis bounding the hit rate over the unrecovered tail.
Significance — fecundity:
A measured failure-propensity model becomes a reusable covariate (for weighting, for uncertainty, for active-learning acquisition). The numerics-vs-physics adjudication produces a labelled dataset for the inverse question — can convergence behaviour serve as a cheap instability detector, i.e. is abandonment a measurement? Manski-style bounds on hull membership under worst-case tails become computable. Attempt-frame reporting becomes askable as a norm for computational screening. And the same design transfers to every other simulate-filter-publish pipeline (molecular docking, CFD campaigns, phonon/DFPT databases).
Significance — anchoring:
Touches the HT-DFT reproducibility programme (P1; DFPT phonon pitfalls 1710.06028, unread), the database-bias literature that has quantified only the chemistry half (P3), the 2026 coverage roadmap's "blank zones" (2609.19833), the wet-lab dark-data/failed-experiment programme that named the analogous problem a decade ago and never crossed to computation (Raccuglia et al.), and the live distribution-shift complaint about MLIP benchmarks.

Reason for silence:
A disciplinary-boundary plus incentive mechanism, with a specific structural cause: the missingness is manufactured one layer below the layer that publishes. Error handling lives in workflow infrastructure whose success metric is throughput and whose explicit design goal is to make failures disappear (Custodian retries, then raises MaxCorrectionsError and abandons); the analysis unit that screening papers and MLIP trainers consume is the materials/entry document, which is downstream of a best-of-task selection and carries no attempt denominator, so the failed tasks are not merely unexamined but structurally absent from the object of analysis. The vocabulary that would name the problem — MNAR, ignorability, selection on unobservables — belongs to biostatistics and survey methodology, one boundary away (query 4: zero materials hits). And the incentive runs the wrong way: a failed-calculation inventory reads as a quality defect in one's own database, so there is no venue and no credit for publishing it — the same gap the wet-lab side closed in 2016 with failed-experiment ML and the calculation side never did. "No one thought of it" is not needed: the people who could see it are not the people who publish on it.
