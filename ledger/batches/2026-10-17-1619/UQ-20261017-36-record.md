Domain(s): Medicine — respiratory medicine / clinical pharmacology of inhaled drugs. Adjacent: regulatory science (orally inhaled drug product bioequivalence); aerosol science; epidemiological measurement-error methodology.

Question (canonical):
V1 (as intaken): Is the reported flatness of the inhaled-corticosteroid dose–response curve in asthma above low nominal doses partly an artefact of randomising *label* dose while per-patient *delivered* lung dose varies severalfold, and how much steeper is the dose–response when exposure is defined as individually estimated delivered dose rather than prescribed dose?

V2 (Gate 1 reformulation — the version scored; one reformulation used, for well-posedness, because V1 names the wrong error model):
In an inhaled-corticosteroid dose-ranging design in which nominal dose is randomised and per-patient delivered lung dose is independently estimated (recorded inhalation profiles plus device-specific in-vitro flow-to-dose characterisation, with actuation counts for adherence), how much do the fitted dose–response curve's maximum slope and ED50 differ when the exposure variable is estimated delivered dose rather than assigned nominal dose; and of the gap between the two curves, what share is attributable to (a) dispersion of the nominal→delivered multiplier, (b) pharmacodynamic saturation, and (c) between-patient heterogeneity in corticosteroid responsiveness — with a stated test of whether the delivery multiplier is independent of baseline severity (non-differential) or not?

Alternative formulations:
- Does the ICS therapeutic ceiling reflect receptor/pathway saturation, or dispersion in the exposure variable that every inhaled dose-ranging trial has used?
- If published inhaled dose-ranging trials were re-analysed on estimated delivered dose instead of prescribed dose, how far would the apparent plateau move, and what factor separates the two slopes?

Motivating gap:
Two literatures sit side by side and are never joined. (i) The ICS dose–response curve is reported as flat over a wide nominal range — VERIFIED this run: Powell & Gibson, Med J Aust 2003;178(5):223-225, "The dose–response curve for ICS in asthma is relatively flat, with little difference seen between doses of 100 μg and 1000 μg of fluticasone per day", and "most (>90%) of the benefit ... is achieved at relatively low doses, equivalent to 250 μg of fluticasone per day"; Masoli, Holt, Weatherall & Beasley, Curr Allergy Asthma Rep 2004;4(2):144-148 reports ~200 μg/d fluticasone achieving >=90% of maximal efficacy. (ii) Delivered lung dose depends strongly on inspiratory flow, device and technique. The flatness is the stated reason clinical-endpoint bioequivalence studies for generic inhalers are large and insensitive. Errors/dispersion in the exposure variable predict the observed flattening, yet the respiratory literature attributes the residual to individual *responsiveness*, not to exposure dispersion. VERIFIED this run that the two candidate "owners" do not make the argument: Powell & Gibson "does not discuss deposited or delivered dose variability, inhaler technique, or measurement error in the dose variable" and uses nominal prescribed doses from Cochrane meta-analyses; Masoli et al.'s abstract and reference annotations attribute variation to "considerable individual variability in the response to inhaled corticosteroids", with no mention of delivered dose, technique, measurement error, or receptor saturation. The named regulatory duplicate risk was also tested and does not hold (see Gate 2, query 4). Flow-sensing inhalers plus device-specific in-vitro flow-to-dose curves make a per-patient delivered-dose estimate newly possible, so the apportionment is newly computable.

Presuppositions:
1. A flat dose–response above low nominal doses is a real, replicated finding. ESTABLISHED this run from two independent sources read (Powell & Gibson 2003; Masoli et al. 2004).
2. Per-patient delivered lung dose can be estimated from recorded inhalation profiles plus device-specific in-vitro flow–dose curves. CONTESTED: in-vitro/in-vivo correlation for inhaled products is imperfect and is itself a live regulatory dispute (FDA's own OINDP bioequivalence presentation, read this run, does not treat in-vitro data as a route to an individual delivered-dose estimate).
3. Between-patient variance in delivered dose is large relative to the nominal dose steps used in dose-ranging trials. PLAUSIBLE, UNQUANTIFIED — not verified this run; no deposition-variance figure was opened, so the magnitude limb is unsupported.
4. The exposure error is predominantly non-differential with respect to outcome. CONTESTED: technique may be worse in sicker or older patients, which makes the error differential and can change the sign of the correction. Retained as an explicit limb of V2 rather than assumed.
5. The error structure is classical measurement error, so regression dilution applies. **FALSE AS STATED — this is why V1 was reformulated.** When nominal dose is *randomised*, nominal dose is not mismeasured; the true exposure varies around the assigned value. That is Berkson-type, not classical, structure, and Berkson error does not attenuate a linear slope. The flattening mechanism available here is different: a mixture of individually log-dose-shifted sigmoids smears a group-mean sigmoid. V2 states the mechanism as mixture-induced flattening rather than regression dilution. The candidate survives, but only because the mechanism was re-specified; an analysis run on the classical-error assumption would be wrong.
6. Receptor/pathway saturation does not fully account for the plateau — i.e. there is something left to apportion. CONTESTED; this is the rival hypothesis to be weighed, not assumed.
7. **Third rival, absent from the intake and added here:** between-patient heterogeneity in corticosteroid responsiveness flattens a group-mean curve on its own, with no exposure dispersion at all, and is the explanation the field already gives (Masoli et al. 2004; and Szefler et al., "Significant variability in response to inhaled corticosteroids for persistent asthma", J Allergy Clin Immunol 2002 — title and venue seen in search results, NOT opened). The apportionment is therefore three-way, not two-way. V2 carries all three terms.
8. Nominal dose was dispensed and taken as assigned, so adherence is a separate term from delivery efficiency. CONTESTED; requires actuation-level adherence data, which V2 requires.

What would count as an answer:
Either (i) an analysis — prospective, or a re-analysis of an inhaled dose-ranging trial with recorded inhalation profiles — reporting the clinical endpoint's dose–response curve fitted on estimated delivered dose with a confidence interval, alongside the curve fitted on nominal dose, the maximum-slope and ED50 difference, and the three-way apportionment of the gap between delivery-multiplier dispersion, pharmacodynamic saturation and responsiveness heterogeneity, each with uncertainty; a materially steeper delivered-dose curve whose steepening is not absorbed by the responsiveness term answers yes. Or (ii) a quantitative bound showing the measured dispersion of the nominal→delivered multiplier is too small to generate the observed flattening, which answers no and strengthens the saturation/responsiveness account. Either way the answer must report whether the delivery multiplier is independent of baseline severity, because a severity-dependent multiplier breaks the simple reading, and must state how (a) and (c) were separated, since without an independent measure of delivery they are not separately identifiable from endpoint data alone.

Significance — consequence:
If yes (delivered-dose curve materially steeper, with the steepening not absorbed by the responsiveness term): the "therapeutic ceiling" becomes partly an artefact of the exposure variable, which changes two decisions. First, generic-inhaler bioequivalence: the stated reason clinical-endpoint studies are insensitive becomes a fixable property of the exposure definition rather than a property of the drug, and a delivered-dose-conditioned or delivered-dose-stratified endpoint becomes a candidate design — directly relevant to the cost of OINDP BE programmes. Second, clinical escalation: "higher doses add risk without efficacy" would be re-read as "higher *label* doses add risk without efficacy in patients whose delivery is already good", making delivery optimisation (device fit, technique, flow training) a quantified alternative to dose escalation rather than an exhortation. If no (measured dispersion of the delivery multiplier too small to generate the observed flattening): the saturation/responsiveness account is strengthened with a number for the first time, which supports current step-down guidance and strengthens the regulatory case for abandoning clinical endpoints for OINDP BE in favour of in-vitro plus PK surrogates. Both branches change a decision.

Significance — audience:
(i) Regulatory inhalation scientists at FDA OGD and EMA, who currently run or require clinical-endpoint BE studies justified by the flatness and are on record (query 4) looking for "the most sensitive dose-response approach"; (ii) respiratory guideline committees (GINA and national equivalents) whose dose-step tables encode the plateau; (iii) inhaler-device and smart-inhaler manufacturers, for whom a quantified attenuation factor is the first efficacy-denominated argument for flow-sensing hardware; (iv) pharmacometricians doing model-informed drug development for inhaled products, who would gain a validated exposure variable.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens the same apportionment for inhaled bronchodilators, inhaled antibiotics and inhaled insulin-type products; opens "what is the within-patient, between-actuation reproducibility of the delivery multiplier, and does it set a floor on the power any inhaled trial can reach?"; opens the design question of randomising *delivered* dose (titrating label dose to a target delivered dose) rather than label dose, which no inhaled trial has done; and opens the symmetric question for the side-effect curve, since the systemic limb runs through a different (swallowed plus absorbed) route whose multiplier need not covary with the pulmonary one.

Significance — anchoring:
The ICS therapeutic-ceiling anomaly; the regulatory problem of insensitive clinical endpoints for orally inhaled products; the unresolved in-vitro/in-vivo correlation dispute for inhaled products (presupposition 2 is the same dispute, which is why a "yes" would itself be contested); and the broader methodological question of what randomisation does and does not protect when the randomised variable is a proxy for the exposure of interest.

Reason for silence:
Primarily a disciplinary boundary with three sides rather than two. The dose–response meta-analytic literature is clinical respiratory medicine and evidence-based medicine; it works on trial-arm nominal doses because that is the only exposure published trials report (verified: Powell & Gibson's analysis "uses nominal prescribed doses from the Cochrane meta-analyses"). Delivered-dose characterisation lives in aerosol science and device engineering, which measures deposition variance but has no patient-level clinical endpoints to regress it against (query 6). The correction machinery lives in epidemiological measurement-error methodology, which has never been pointed at inhaled dosing (query 9, run in the method field's own vocabulary). No single community holds all three, and none of the three is incentivised to produce a result whose main consequence falls on the other two. Secondary mechanism — new instrument: a per-patient delivered-dose estimate requires recorded inhalation profiles, which only became routinely obtainable with flow-sensing inhalers (query 8); before that the apportionment was not computable, so the silence has a date. Third, an incentive asymmetry: the flatness is load-bearing for a convenient conclusion (do not escalate dose) and for a costly one (clinical-endpoint BE studies), and neither the guideline writers nor the generic sponsors benefit from reopening it — the sponsors would face a harder exposure-characterisation burden, and the originators' devices would become the thing under test.
