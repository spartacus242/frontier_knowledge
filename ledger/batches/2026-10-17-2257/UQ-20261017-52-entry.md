Domain(s): Biology — functional genomics / cell biology (gene essentiality, targeted protein degradation). Method-owning neighbours: chemical-biology target validation; developmental genetics (transcriptional adaptation); systems biology (rate-dependent response).

Question (canonical): Is gene essentiality a property of the gene or of the rate at which its product is removed — for a matched panel of >= 100 genes in one human cell line assayed both by acute degron depletion and by CRISPR knockout, what fraction of pre-declared essentiality calls disagree (V2: conditional on measured residual target protein at the fitness readout, with an RNA-less knockout arm alongside PTC alleles), and does the residual discordance load on measured protein half-life or on the transcriptional-adaptation signature?

Alternative formulations:
- Does the standard essentiality call depend on removal kinetics, so that genes scored non-essential under gradual loss are essential under abrupt depletion, or the reverse?
- How much of a published essentialome is a statement about the dilution timescale of the perturbation rather than about the gene's function?

Motivating gap: Inverts the presupposition behind every essentiality map — that cutting a gene and reading fitness days later measures the gene's requirement. Knockout removes protein by dilution over divisions; degrons remove it in minutes to hours and are now library-scale. At intake the bookkeeping question looked unposed; Gate 2 found it posed and answered at proteome scale in budding yeast (2025).

Presuppositions: (1) degron and knockout depletion can be made comparable in depth — CONTESTED, partly falsified (both yeast papers name incomplete degradation first); V2 measures depth instead of assuming it. (2) degron tagging does not itself perturb function/stability — CONTESTED and now evidenced as a real confound: Nat Commun 13:5495, unread at Gate 2 and opened by both adversaries via doi.org, reports AID2 "led to a small but consistent increase in basal protein levels", poorly expressed fusions, and that "the kinetics of degradation and recovery are highly variable, unpredictable". (3) essentiality as a continuous fitness score with a pre-declared threshold — supported, and load-bearing (yeast answer swings 69% -> 4% with threshold). (4) panel protein half-lives available — plausible but collinear with depletion depth. (5) compensation detectable in window — mis-specified in V1: the demonstrated trigger is mutant-mRNA decay, not slow protein loss (P3). (6) endogenous tagging at scale plus the non-viability of essential-gene knockout clones — stands; the real human-cell barrier. (7) not settled by knockdown-vs-knockout literature — the risk landed from a different direction (degron-vs-deletion in yeast). (8) cell-autonomous fitness in culture acceptable — stands.

What would count as an answer: >= 100 genes in one cell line, each inducibly degradable and separately knocked out, threshold declared before data collection, yielding (i) discordance rate with CIs, (ii) its direction, (iii) regression of discordance on measured half-life and on measured compensatory transcript/protein change after conditioning on measured residual-protein depth, and (iv) a projection of how many public essentiality-map calls the rate would reclassify.

- CORRECTION on P2's figures: "recovered 69% of those originally defined essential genes" is real at a relative-growth threshold of < 0.5, but it CANNOT be reconstructed from the paper, whose Methods give two different YKO essential-gene denominators (560 in one place, 650 in another) and no counts. "Newly essential" overstates it.
- Both papers' comparator is the YKO gene-deletion collection (Winzeler 1999, Giaever 2002, with Despres 2020), not a CRISPR screen. The candidate's CRISPR limb is therefore met by a different knockout technology of the same perturbation class, which is why the Disguise ground holds on substance rather than on wording.
- ROUTING ROW NARROWED, not withdrawn, and the reasoning is worth following because it went both ways.
