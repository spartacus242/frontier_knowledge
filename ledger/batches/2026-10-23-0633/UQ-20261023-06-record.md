Question (canonical) — V2, the single permitted Gate 1 reformulation (V1 below; V2 is the version scored):
For a frame declared before any data pull — completed randomised trials or registry cohorts that (i) report a consensus CGM metric as a primary or key secondary endpoint, (ii) used a sensor model for which a published pressure-induced-sensor-attenuation (PISA) detector with a stated operating characteristic exists, and (iii) hold retrievable per-reading traces with timestamps, the excluded share reported as denominator attrition — what is the change, in percentage points, of nocturnal percent time <70 mg/dL and percent time <54 mg/dL, and of level-2 hypoglycaemic event count per 14 days, when readings flagged at a pre-declared PISA detector operating point are handled under a pre-declared removal rule (flag-as-missing with the imputation strategy fixed in advance); what is the detector's own false-positive contribution to that change, bounded from simultaneous dual-sensor-wear data at the same operating point; and does the recomputed between-arm difference, with confidence interval, cross the trial's own pre-specified margin (fallback: half-width of its primary 95% CI) or change sign, across a pre-declared sensitivity band of operating points?

V1 (Gate 0 as submitted, recorded and superseded):
In trials and registries whose endpoints are the consensus CGM metrics computed from raw sensor traces, what share of nocturnal time-below-range (<70 and <54 mg/dL) and of level-2 hypoglycaemic event counts is attributable to pressure-induced sensor attenuation rather than to plasma glucose, and does removing it change the magnitude or direction of the between-arm endpoint difference?

Why V1 was reformulated (well-posedness only): V1's "attributable to ... rather than to plasma glucose" asserts a counterfactual true-plasma-glucose comparison, while the intake's own estimand explicitly disclaims needing the true nadir. That is an internal contradiction in the canonical sentence, not a presupposition dispute, so it is the kind of defect Gate 1 may repair. V2 makes the estimand what the intake said it was: the change in the REPORTED metric under a declared handling rule, with the detector's false-positive contribution bounded separately rather than assumed away.

WHAT THE REPAIR ITSELF NOW PRESUPPOSES (tested as hard as V1, per the standing rule):
 R1 A published PISA detector with a stated operating characteristic exists for the sensor models in the frame. Established for ONE model generation only (Dexcom G6, Idi et al. 2024, read in full). V2's clause (ii) therefore shrinks the frame to that generation and must report how many candidate trials it excludes. If no trial in the frame used a covered model, the satisfying set is empty and V2 is unanswerable as written.
 R2 Simultaneous dual-sensor-wear data exist AT THE SAME OPERATING POINT to bound the detector's false-positive contribution. This is a requirement V1 did not carry. It is satisfied in Idi 2024's own population (36 adults wearing two CGMs, drawn from the G6 pivotal trial) and is NOT established for any trial in the frame. Importing the bound from Idi's population into a trial population is a cross-population transfer and must be declared as one, not reported as the trial's own false-positive rate.
 R3 A pre-specified margin exists on the hypoglycaemia endpoint. CGM trials pre-specify margins on TIR more often than on TBR; the CI-half-width fallback is declared for that reason.
 R4 Both arms' traces are retrievable, not just the active arm's. The direction-change limb is estimable only on two-arm trace access; one-arm access answers the burden limb alone.
 R5 UNRESOLVED PREMISE ABOUT A DOCUMENT KIND, DECLARED AS UNVERIFIED: whether CGM-endpoint trial SAPs already pre-specify an artifact-exclusion rule (which would make the removal rule a reanalysis of a declared choice rather than a new one). Significance — consequence:
 If yes (removal moves the metric materially and the contrast moves): hypoglycaemia endpoints in CGM trials are measured with a ruler that is partly instrument artifact, and the asymmetry matters wherever arms differ in anything that changes sleep posture, alarm-driven waking or sensor site — which is exactly what automated-insulin-delivery arms differ in. The consensus metric definitions would then need an artifact-handling clause, which they currently do not have: the quality gate in use is data sufficiency (percent of readings captured), and a compression artifact passes it, because the readings are present. Magnitude is not negligible a priori against the targets the metrics are scored on: N1's 0.33 faults per monitoring day against guideline targets of <4% of time below 70 mg/dL and <1% below 54 mg/dL means a sub-percentage-point artifact share is a large fraction of the level-2 target. (That comparison is MY OWN REASONING from two printed numbers with different denominators — faults per day against percent of time — and it is not a source finding; converting it into percentage points requires a per-fault duration that nothing I opened prints.)
 If no (both arms shift together, contrast inside a pre-declared delta): the artifact is a nuisance on absolute burden rather than a bias on inference. That is still an answer with an audience, because absolute burden is what target attainment is reported against and what device labels claim.

Significance — fecundity:

Reason for silence:
 Instrumental plus disciplinary, in four parts. (a) A pre-declarable removal rule needs a detector with a stated operating characteristic, and the unsupervised retrospective detector set only appeared in 2024 (N1); before that, artifact flagging meant manual visual labelling by multiple operators, which is not a pre-specifiable analysis step. (b) The metric-definition community's only data-quality gate is completeness, and an artifact is not missingness — there is no reporting slot in which an "artifact share" would be entered, so nobody's template asks for the number. (c) A disciplinary split: the field that owns the method (endpoint measurement error, quantitative bias analysis, inferential validity) does not hold per-reading traces, and the field that holds the traces (diabetes technology, and the manufacturers) treats compression as a user-education and product-differentiation matter — which is exactly the register the one 2026 document on nocturnal compression lows is written in. (d) An incentive gap at the point where the two would meet: a manufacturer has little reason to publish what share of its device's reported hypoglycaemia is its own artifact, and a sponsor whose safety endpoint is TBR has little reason to re-open a settled endpoint. None of these is "nobody thought of it": two sources I opened state the worry explicitly and neither is positioned to measure it.
