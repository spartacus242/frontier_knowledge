## 2. Restatement in my own words

Take a stochastic source whose per-symbol Shannon entropy rate H can be computed from its own definition (a small hidden Markov model; a probabilistic context-free grammar). Train sequence models from two architecture families (attention transformers; gated linear recurrences / state-space models) on unlimited fresh samples, over a ladder of model sizes N and token counts D at matched FLOPs. Fit L(N,D) = E + A/N^alpha + B/D^beta and read off E. Is E - H zero to within +/-0.01 nats per symbol, or positive; and if positive, does it differ between the two families on the same source (so that the excess is attributable to the architecture rather than to the source)? Headline quantity: Delta_a(S) = lim_{N,D->inf} L_a(N,D;S) - H(S), for architecture a and source S.

## 6. The eight attacks

### 1. Triviality — PARTIAL

Objection: two of the question's three parts are answerable in a page from opened sources plus one textbook theorem. (i) Representational floor: Hu, Liu & Jin prove "an L-layer finite precision Transformer is able to fit any HMMs of at least 2^L sequence length" and "there exists a log T-precision Transformer approximating the belief state sequence of length T" (exact fragments, F1), so the infimum loss over the transformer family at context T is the T-step conditional entropy H_T, and H_T decreases to H for every stationary process (Cover & Thomas, Elements of Information Theory, Thm 4.2.1 — textbook, cited from memory, exact mathematics, not opened). Hence the inf-over-family Delta on the HMM limb is zero. (ii) The dichotomy "architecture rather than data": Makkuva et al. (F4) print "L(theta*) = H(x_{n+1}|x_n), the entropy rate of the Markov chain" as the global minimum and, under weight tying, "it first reaches a plateau at a loss value corresponding to H(pi)", with "Increasing the depth, however, solves the issue." The plateau height H(pi) - H(x_{n+1}|x_n) = I(x_n; x_{n+1}) is set by the chain, exists only under an architectural constraint, and disappears with depth. MY OWN EXACT ARITHMETIC from the paper's printed p = 0.2, q = 0.3: pi = (0.6, 0.4), H(pi) = 0.6730 nats, H = 0.5446 nats, Delta_tied = 0.1284 nats/symbol (the paper prints no gap). So the dichotomy is answered "neither — joint, and vanishing with depth" in a paragraph. (iii) Whether SGD-trained models attain the representational floor as N, D -> inf on an HMM or PCFG is NOT page-answerable: P2's stall is scoped "within the allotted training budget" (VERIFY-07-b), Hu, Liu & Jin attribute a failure to "some optimization issues" (F1), and no opened source settles persistence in the limit.
Best response for the candidate: Version B's "asymptote of achieved cross-entropy" makes (iii) the question, and (iii) cannot be derived.
Status: PARTIAL. Load-bearing: F1 and F4 (opened), one textbook theorem from memory, my exact arithmetic.

### 5. Vagueness — PARTIAL

Objection: the two branches of the question are not symmetrically decidable. A null — trained loss within 0.01 nats of H at some finite (N, D) — is finitely verifiable without any fit, because L >= H for every model. A positive floor is NOT finitely verifiable: over any finite ladder it is indistinguishable from slow convergence, which is exactly what P2's own wording "within the allotted training budget" and "extremely slow optimization" records (VERIFY-07-b), so "H + Delta" can only ever be asserted through the parametric form, and C4 shows that form returning negative floors on PCFG data. A second vagueness: Version B declares no context length T, yet at fixed T even the perfect predictor has floor H_T - H > 0 on any HMM with memory, so "Delta" conflates a computable context floor with any architecture floor — and the SSM arm, being recurrent, has no such floor, which biases the cross-family comparison by construction.
Best response: the pre-declared estimator and equivalence band make the question operationally decidable; a wide bootstrap interval is a pre-declared "indeterminate"; T can be set far beyond the HMM's mixing time so H_T - H is negligible.
Status: PARTIAL. Load-bearing step is MY OWN REASONING (the asymmetry argument and the H_T point), supported by P2's budget scope (verified) and Pandey (opened); the H_T - H monotonicity is textbook (Cover & Thomas Thm 4.2.1, from memory).

## 9. Closing paragraph

The single strongest surviving objection is that the candidate is Makkuva et al.'s and Hu, Liu & Jin's question — does a trained transformer's loss reach the source's entropy rate or plateau above it, and is the plateau the architecture's doing or something recurrent architectures escape — restated in scaling-law vocabulary with a measurement protocol attached, and that the protocol's one published run on the candidate's own PCFG family (Pandey 2024) returned negative irreducible losses. The candidate's best answer is that nobody has estimated the limit with an interval or run the SSM arm; that is true of every document anyone in this ledger opened, and it describes an unperformed experiment on an asked question rather than an un-asked question. What would settle the objection one way: a document posing Version B's asymptotic, architecture-indexed Delta in so many words — then the candidate is a disguised known outright; the two unopened titles likeliest to do so are arXiv:2307.07843 ("Transformers are Universal Predictors") and Debowski's arXiv:2302.09049. What would settle the question itself, rather than the objection: a trained loss within 0.01 nats of a printed H at finite scale for both families on a declared source — the only branch of the question that finite evidence can close — and if that is observed, the record's Delta is zero without any fit; if it is not, no ladder can tell a floor from slow convergence, and Pandey's Table 2 is what the fit then prints.
