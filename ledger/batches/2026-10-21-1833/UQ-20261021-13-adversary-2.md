## 1. Restatement in my own words (before engaging the framing)

Preference corpora are sold and cited as "N preference pairs" and everyone downstream — reward-model
sample-size reasoning, binomial confidence intervals on reward-model accuracy, leaderboard intervals —
treats those N labels as N independent observations. But every label is produced by one human, each
human produces many labels, and the humans are themselves grouped by vendor, shift and guideline
revision. So the labels are clustered, twice over.

The candidate asks: on a set of corpora declared in advance that actually carry annotator identifiers,
fit a Bradley-Terry / logistic model with crossed random effects for annotator, annotator-cluster and
item; print the variance components and the implied intraclass correlations on both the latent-logistic
and the linear-probability scale, with intervals; convert them into a Kish design effect and an
effective sample size using the unequal cluster sizes that are really there rather than an equal-cluster
formula; and then check by experiment, at total labels held fixed while the number of distinct
annotators varies, whether reward-model accuracy follows N_eff or N.

Compressed: **how many independent human judgments is a million-label preference corpus actually worth,
and does that number predict anything downstream?** A clean null (rho ~ 0, Deff ~ 1, flat subsampling
arm) is declared an answer.

## 5. Single strongest killing point

**The feasible frame and the significant regime do not overlap.** rho is estimable only where stable
annotator ids exist, and there per-annotator cluster sizes are 6 (PRISM, after the n_j ≥ 6 filter) to
150 (PluriHarms) — where, by my own arithmetic on the record's own illustrative rho = 0.01, Deff runs
from 1.05 (inside the pre-registered NULL band) to 2.49 (MATERIAL by cluster size alone, not by rho).
Every consequence the significance case rests on — a million pairs worth fifty thousand, procurement
inverting from depth to breadth, an N_eff beside the N on a dataset card — lives at n ~ 2,000, which no
corpus in F exhibits, and limb (b)'s own instruction to use "the clustering ACTUALLY present" forbids
the extrapolation that would get there. The candidate can produce an honest number for F or the number
that matters, and not both.
**This kill stands on one leg and I name it as such:** the mismatch between F's cluster sizes and the
production cluster sizes the consequence argument uses, which is sourced to the record's own established
P1/P5 facts plus S1's printed illustration. It does **not** additionally rest on the unsourced
Kish-estimand-specificity argument in attacks 4 and 8; if that argument were wrong, this kill is
unaffected.
