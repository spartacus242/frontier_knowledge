Domain(s): Biology — comparative physiology and cell biology (cell-size and metabolic allometry); fitting method borrowed from aquatic size-spectrum ecology

Question (canonical): Is the near-equal partition of total cell biomass across logarithmic cell-size classes reported for the human body a general feature of animal bodies, and does the slope of that within-body cell-size spectrum shift with species body mass? (Scored as canonical: V1, because the Gate 2 ground attaches to V1's own words. The single permitted Gate 1 reformulation, V2 — S >= 3 species over >= 10^4-fold body mass, a pre-declared "one cell" rule for erythrocytes/myofibres/adipocytes/neurons, a declared volume protocol, fitted slope with CI, slope-equality tests against -1 and against the human -0.97, a dominant-tissue hold-out, and an equal-energy check — is in the record and does not escape that ground.)

Alternative formulations:
- Do animals other than humans show roughly equal total biomass per logarithmic cell-size class, and is the exponent conserved across a wide range of body mass?
- Is the within-organism distribution of cell biomass over cell size a conserved allometric property, or an incidental consequence of the particular tissue composition of the human body?

Motivating gap:
At intake: Hatton et al. (PNAS 2023) report a whole-body human census in which cell count falls as roughly cell mass^-1, so each logarithmic size class holds similar total biomass; the pattern is numerically reminiscent of the Sheldon spectrum but has no predation cascade inside a body, and was taken to exist for one species with no comparative measurement and no mechanism. READ LEVEL at intake: title/abstract only. Corrected on opening the paper at the publisher: two of three intake claims fail — the comparative question is posed in print, and a cross-mammal cell-size dataset plus fitted cross-species exponents already exist. What survives: no non-human whole-body census by size class exists, the paper makes no size-spectrum comparison, and it states no mechanism and runs no per-cell metabolic analysis.

Presuppositions:
- The human near-invariance is real, not an artefact of pooling literature estimates. CONTESTED; the source concedes >1,500 sources for ~400 cell types, mass "often inferred ... rather than measured directly", up to 4-fold spread in size and 20-fold in count.
- Two further species can be censused to comparable accuracy. CONTESTED and binding: the human number rests on a century of reference-man morphometry with no second-species substrate; adversary sweeps found only organ-level DNA-based rodent totals, self-described as "very approximate".
- "Cell size class" is usable at both geometric tails. CONTESTED; the source itself flags the myonuclear domain as unresolved. V2 exists only to discharge this.
- A whole-body spectrum is more than a statement about three tissues. CONTESTED and partly falsified: biomass is "dominated by muscle and fat", myocytes ~half of cell biomass, and the two visible departures from flatness sit at the dominant-tissue boundaries.
- Spectrum theory yields a body-mass prediction that survives removing the predation cascade. FALSE as stated: the -1 expectation comes from balanced growth with a lower size bound, with no body-mass term, so V1's "predicted direction" has no referent.
- Per-cell metabolic rate is measurable or boundable against cell size. Supported as boundable only (Savage et al. 2007 infer it indirectly).

What would count as an answer:
A cell census by size class for >= 2 further species spanning >= 10^4-fold body mass, under a counting rule and volume protocol declared before collection with propagated uncertainty, giving (i) a fitted slope with CI per species; (ii) slope-equality tests across species and against -1 and -0.97; (iii) a hold-out decomposition excluding each body's two or three dominant tissues; (iv) a test of whether equal-biomass classes are equal-energy classes under bounded per-cell metabolic rates. A clean negative settles it as well as a positive. Gate 1: statable and falsifiable under V2; but V1's predicted-direction clause is empty, and the answer condition names an artefact whose only instance took >1,500 sources with no route offered to a second — well-posed and inert.
