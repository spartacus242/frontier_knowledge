## Restatement (my own words)

When an LLM is trained with its weights stored in BF16 (or with 8-bit block-quantized Adam state), many of the per-step updates the optimizer computes are smaller than half the spacing of representable BF16 numbers around the weight, so the stored weight does not change at all. The candidate asks: in realistic training (pretraining, SFT, RL), what share of individual weight-update attempts is swallowed this way (or lands materially wrong relative to an FP32-master replay); which weights it hits (rare-token embedding rows, norm gains, small weights, attention vs MLP); and does the share rise as the learning rate decays? Secondary: does it matter for held-out loss when compared against Kahan/stochastic-rounding/FP32-master arms?

So the unit is (scalar parameter, optimizer step); the endpoint is bitwise equality of the stored BF16 value before/after the step (plus a relative-delta distortion endpoint); the comparator is an FP32-master replay; the estimand is a rate per (configuration, parameter group, schedule-phase bin).

F2. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11456 (AuroraRL) — READ. RL post-training system over geo-distributed GPUs that ships each step as a lossless sparse delta of BF16 checkpoints. Defines a per-step "nonzero ratio" over dW = W(t+1)-W(t) on BF16 checkpoints (Eq. 1), which is the complement of the candidate's bitwise-unchanged fraction. Reported nonzero ratios (Figure 3): Qwen3-4B 1.12%, Qwen3-8B 0.96%, Qwen3-14B 1.06%, Qwen3-32B 1.12%, Qwen2.5-72B 1.85%, Llama3-8B 2.56%, GLM4-9B 1.99%; Table 4 by RL algorithm (GRPO 0.96%, RLOO 0.93%, OPO 1.06%); Figure 4: below 1% and stable over 800 steps at fixed LR 1e-6. Appendix A gives the mechanism as ULP absorption with ULP ~ |W| * 2^-7 and r_i = |dW_i|/ULP_i; Table 10: 82.56% of one-step FP32 updates lie in (1e-7, 1e-6], 14.94% exactly zero; "only 1.27% of parameters with nonzero FP32 updates remain nonzero after BF16 differencing"; conclusion: BF16 "substantially sparsifies checkpoint-to-checkpoint updates". No per-layer breakdown (states sparsity is "uniformly distributed across all weight tensors"); no LR sweep (fixed 1e-6; argues post-training LR ~1e-6 is two orders below pretraining ~1e-4). Does NOT cite arXiv 2602.03839 — an independent measurement of the same estimand.
F3. https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.06192 (Revisiting BFloat16 Training, Zamirai et al. 2020) — READ. Abstract: "nearest rounding for model weight updates often cancels small updates, which degrades the convergence and model accuracy." Section 3.1 defines cancellation (update smaller than half the gap to the neighbouring representable value) and states it "often emerges in the late training stage when the magnitude of gradient becomes small or learning rate decays small." Does NOT measure the fraction of cancelled updates in a real run. BERT NLI: 16-bit training shows "16% and 7% lower training and validation accuracies than 32-bit training." Matched arms: stochastic rounding on the weight update matches 32-bit for "five out of seven applications"; Kahan summation "closes remaining gaps on all the models we consider" at the cost of doubled weight memory; control arm with 32-bit weights and everything else in 16-bit matches 32-bit accuracy, attributing the loss to weight-update rounding.
F4. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06060 — READ. "What is New in Stochastic Rounding: a Survey on Theory, Hardware, and Applications" (El Arar, Fasi, Filip, Mikaitis). Survey; no measured update-loss fraction; qualitative only ("helps prevent stagnation in low precision", citing Xia et al.; SR benefits "diminish once the gradients become too small relative to the quantisation noise/variance", citing Chmiel et al.). Confirms the numerics field frames the object as "stagnation" and delivers bounds, not incidence. Not on point for incidence.
