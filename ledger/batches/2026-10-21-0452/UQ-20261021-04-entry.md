Question (canonical): For a pre-declared set of payer-by-service cells drawn from the first years of CMS-0057-F public prior-authorization reporting, what fraction of prior-authorization-requiring clinical decisions terminate on the provider side before any request reaches the payer, and does cross-payer variation in the published denial rate predict that pre-submission attrition rate with a negative sign — in what share of cells does adding attrition reverse the ranking of payers by obstruction, against a flip threshold fixed before any data pull? (V2; the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation. V1 and the two factual corrections that forced it are in batches/2026-10-21-0452/UQ-20261021-04-record.md.)
Alternative formulations: (a) Does a low published prior-authorization denial rate mean a payer approves more care, or that fewer requests were ever filed? (b) Treating the new public prior-authorization metrics as an instrument with an endogenous denominator, estimate pre-submission attrition and the bias it induces in the metric as a measure of access to care.
Motivating gap: CMS-0057-F requires payers to publish, from CY2025, percentages approved/denied/overturned on denominators of requests the payer received or adjudicated; response time is measured from payer receipt, not provider send. Every published figure is conditioned on arrival, so care never requested cannot appear in it, while analysts (KFF, 13 Aug 2026; Becker's) are already ranking named payers on those percentages. The field has named denominator-side confounds it can see from payer files (gold carding, case mix, service mix, no counts) and none on the provider side.
Presuppositions: P1 arrival-conditioned denominator — ESTABLISHED, but not uniform ("CMS currently provides suggested denominators", standardisation only proposed in a 2026 rule; DHCS prints "adjudicated" against a narrative "received"). P2 pre-submission terminations identifiable from provider records — CONTESTED, lower bound only. P3 requirement lists matchable to orders — PARTLY ESTABLISHED, needs a sensitivity arm. P4 payers gain from submission friction — CONTESTED, carried as the hypothesis under test. P5 substitution vs abandonment separable — CONTESTED (already executed for drugs: 46.2%/53.8%, Brot-Goldberg et al.). P6 comparable cross-payer/service reporting — WEAKENED (percentages only, no counts, no service breakdown nationally, drugs excluded, inconsistent methods). P7 "public for the first time in 2026" — FALSE as stated; true only as "first nationally, on a uniform federal specification", and even that is qualified by the suggested-denominator finding.
What would count as an answer: Paired cell-level estimates of (i) the printed denial rate and (ii) a provider-side pre-submission attrition rate over PA-requiring decisions identified by a pre-declared matching rule, with the sign and magnitude of their cross-cell association under one primary estimator plus declared sensitivity arms (matching error, substitution-vs-abandonment, received-vs-adjudicated denominator), the post-submission auto-denial share reported separately because it loads the same metric with the opposite sign, and the share of cells whose obstruction ranking flips against a pre-fixed threshold. A null answers it; a negative association of stated size makes it a respecification question. ## Verification of the instrument and the close neighbour at first hand (post-batch, 2026-10-21)

Two fresh-context readers, each handed one claim and its source and neither handed any reviewer's reasoning. The verdict does not move: Provisional, with Validated barred by well-posedness 2/3 and by Medium novelty with a close neighbour. The deciding regulatory claim holds and comes back stronger; several framings around it need correcting.

**The drug carve-out holds, and it is the load-bearing fact.** It is express, repeated three times, and sits inside the reporting paragraph itself as "excluding data on drugs as defined in Sec. 422.119(b)(1)(v),". So the published metrics genuinely cannot see drugs, which is what keeps Brot-Goldberg et al. from occupying this candidate's limb even though it answers the framing question for drugs. One open edge: that cross-reference supplies the definition of "drugs" for all three exclusions and was not read, and the words "pharmacy benefit", "medical benefit" and "pharmacy" are all absent from 422.122, so whether the carve-out tracks the pharmacy/medical benefit split is OPEN and is the single highest-value unread string in this check.

**Two corrections to what the rule requires.** There is NO appeal rate and NO appeal-overturn rate in it: paragraph (c)(4) is "The percentage of standard prior authorization requests that were approved after appeal", which runs against standard requests rather than against appeals or denials, so calling it an overturn rate silently swaps the denominator, and it drops expedited requests entirely. And the metrics are published as percentages aggregated to the MA contract across all items and services, with no volume denominator, which is a sharper version of the entry's own objection and is corrected into it above. Taken with a timeliness clock that starts "between the submission of a request", the published metrics are structurally blind to abandoned requests, to care never requested, to per-service denial patterns, and to everything about drugs — which is the cleanest statement of why this candidate's gap sits where it does.

**Read-level limits on the regulatory check, stated so they are not mistaken for findings.** The rule's own amendatory text is COULD NOT READ: the Federal Register document page yields only 143,134 extractable characters and ends mid-sentence inside preamble section II.B, while the published rule runs 89 FR 8758-8988. The operative requirements above come from the CURRENT codified text at eCFR 42 CFR 422.122, not from the as-published text, which is a routing deviation forced by that truncation. The CMS display copy is the PRE-PUBLICATION version and its dates are placeholders, so no date should be cited from it. The first data year CY2025 is derived from two clauses rather than confirmed against CMS's compliance-date table, which is in the unread preamble. Only one of the rule's parallel payer sections was read; 42 CFR 457.732 and 45 CFR 156.223 are both titled "Prior authorization requirements." and were not opened, so identical wording across payer types is an expectation and not a finding. And the decision timeframes that the auto-denial limb turns on, 42 CFR 422.568(b)(1) and 422.572(a)(1), were not read, so what happens on payer non-response is unresolved and must not be filled in from memory.

**The close neighbour's gap is absent in a strong form, and the entry must not credit the paper with noticing it.** Brot-Goldberg et al. have no request-level data at all, so neither provider-side pre-submission abandonment nor plan-level approval and denial rates could be measured even in principle from their design: plans are compared only on how often they restrict a drug, which is the treatment variable, and the outcome is always a realised fill in claims rather than the fate of a request. That is why this candidate's limb sits outside it. But the paper's section 5 limitation sentences are declarations of its own data's limits for a paperwork-cost calculation, framed from the payer and claims side, not a flag that provider-side attrition is unmeasured — a source noting the absence of a different thing is not a flag of this gap, and the entry is corrected not to claim otherwise. One overstatement also trimmed: the paper does carry medical utilisation as an outcome in its health analysis, so the exact claim is that prior authorization ON medical services or procedures is never studied, not that medical services are absent. Figures recorded second-hand: the table locators in this block come from the paper's own cross-references rather than from tables the reader saw, no uncertainty could be attached to the 26.8%, and no published version was found, though the authors circulate a full PDF that the reader's fetch cap kept it from opening.
