## Question (canonical)

**Gate 1 reformulation adopted (one allowed; both recorded).**

*Version A (intake, as received):* In pretraining that uses block-scaled sub-8-bit numeric formats (MXFP8, MXFP4/NVFP4 on Blackwell-class hardware), is the loss penalty relative to a BF16 control uniform across the data distribution, or does it grow with rarity — rare tokens, low-frequency entities, low-resource languages — as predicted by the mechanism that a scaling block's shared exponent is set by its largest entry, so small-magnitude gradient components co-located with large ones are flushed to zero?

*Version B (canonical, Gate 1):* For matched pretraining runs differing only in numeric format (BF16 control vs MXFP8 vs MXFP4/NVFP4; identical data, order, seed and token count), **what is the slope of the per-stratum held-out loss gap to the BF16 control against log training-corpus frequency** — the primary estimand being the token-level slope beta over pre-registered log-frequency bins — and does that slope (i) differ from zero against a seed-variance baseline, and (ii) track the per-block flush-to-zero rate of gradient entries resolved by the same strata?

Reason for reformulation: well-posedness only. Version A names one estimand ("uniform or not") but three candidate stratifications (tokens, entities, language tiers) without saying which decides, and states the mechanism inside the question so that it reads partly as a thesis. Version B fixes a single primary estimand (a scalar slope on token log-frequency), demotes entity/fact-level and language-tier stratification to pre-registered secondary estimands, and moves the mechanism from the premise into limb (ii) as a check that can fail independently of limb (i). No scope was widened or narrowed.

## Alternative formulations

1. Does training in 4-bit block formats cost the same everywhere in the corpus, or does it quietly delete the long tail?
2. Is the published aggregate penalty of low-precision training an average that hides a frequency-dependent gradient, where the rarer the phenomenon the larger the loss?
3. (Neighbour's words) Is the format-induced loss gap of block-scaled training a *disparate impact* on under-represented strata of the training distribution, or is aggregate validation loss a sufficient statistic for format acceptance?

## Motivating gap

Block-scaled sub-8-bit training moved from research to production hardware in 2025-26, so for the first time the question can be put to the *training* process rather than to post-hoc compression of a finished model, and the decision it bears on — which format to run a nine-figure pretraining run in — is live and is currently being made on aggregate loss alone. The mechanism is specific to the training format and not shared with post-training quantization: a shared per-block scale means the representable floor inside a block is set by its largest element, and gradient contributions from rare phenomena are both small and sparse, so their flush-to-zero rate is a function of what else is in the block.

Two things found in this Gate 2 sharpen the gap rather than close it. First, NVIDIA's own 10T-token NVFP4 report (arXiv 2509.25149) contains exactly one visible non-uniformity — the Code category falls 59.52 to 56.67, HumanEval+ 59.93 to 57.43, MBPP+ 59.11 to 55.91 — and disposes of it with "We suspect the difference could be due to noisy evaluations." Winogrande also drops (80.58 to 78.77) without discussion. An anomaly is reported and not asked about. Second, the one opened paper that stratifies a *quantization* penalty by language finds the gap running the *opposite* way to the candidate's predicted direction (naive FP8 casting of Llama-3 degrades English +18% vs Brazilian Portuguese +3.9%, attributed to larger English activation outliers). That makes Version B a genuine question with a non-obvious sign, not an agenda: if outlier magnitude itself tracks dominant high-frequency structure, the shared-scale floor could be *highest* in blocks belonging to frequent patterns.

## Presuppositions

1. **Established.** Block-scaled sub-8-bit formats are in production or imminent use for pretraining. Verified this run: arXiv 2509.25149 trains a 12B hybrid Mamba-Transformer for 10T tokens in NVFP4; a scaling study of NVFP4 recipes to 8B dense / 30B-A3B MoE at up to 1T tokens is at ICML 2026 (icml.cc/virtual/2026/75179); NVIDIA publishes a production NVFP4 training recipe.
2. **Established for the mechanism's instrumentability.** The per-block flush-to-zero rate is measurable in a real training stack: arXiv 2602.02047 already defines FTZ as "the ratio of underflow-to-zero events where scaled values collapse to exactly zero," computes it per block against the effective encode scale, and reports it alongside a BF16 loss gap. This presupposition is no longer a feasibility guess.
3. **Established as a method.** Frequency-stratified per-token loss is a working instrument in this literature: arXiv 2410.11985 bins tokens by log frequency (bin i holds tokens with frequency in [3^(i-1), 3^i)) and fits disparate depreciation of low-frequency tokens against an intervention.
4. **Requires open corpora.** A frequency stratification needs access to the training corpus to compute token and entity frequencies — true for open corpora, impossible for closed models. This bounds who can run the study and should be stated as a scope limit, not a defect.
5. **CONTESTED.** A BF16 control at matched data, order, seed and token count is affordable at a scale where the effect is detectable. The effect may be scale-dependent in either direction; a null at small scale would not settle the frontier case. Note that arXiv 2602.02047's own BF16-referenced loss gaps are at 1.3B / 60B tokens, and the ICML 2026 study finds "selective high-precision layers are necessary for recovering accuracy at scale" — i.e. format penalties are known to move with scale, so the ≥2-scale requirement in the answer criterion is load-bearing, not decorative.
6. **CONTESTED, must be established before any claim.** Tail degradation, if present, exceeds seed-to-seed noise on the stratified metrics. The NVFP4 report's own dismissal of its Code gap as "noisy evaluations" is precisely the ambiguity a seed-variance baseline has to remove.
7. **CONTESTED, needs its own error analysis.** Rare-stratum loss is measurable without the measurement being noise-dominated: rare strata have few evaluation tokens by definition, so the stratified estimator's variance grows exactly where the hypothesised effect lives. This is the single most serious well-posedness hazard and is why the primary estimand is a slope fit across all bins rather than a tail-vs-head contrast.
8. **Flagged, newly contested by evidence.** That the mechanism predicts *tail* harm specifically. Prior 4 below supplies a counter-direction. The question's sign is open; limb (ii) and the intervention check are what distinguish the mechanism from a generic "low precision is noisy" account.

## What would count as an answer

A slope, with a mechanism check and an intervention check.

- **Design.** Matched pretraining runs at ≥2 scales — identical data, order, seed, token count — in BF16, MXFP8 and MXFP4/NVFP4.
- **Stratification, pre-registered before any run is scored.** Primary: training-corpus token log-frequency bins. Secondary: named-entity / fact frequency deciles; language resource tier.
- **Primary readout.** Per-stratum held-out loss gap to the BF16 control, fitted against log frequency; report beta with a CI.
- **Decision rule, stated in advance.** "Tail-differential" = beta significantly different from zero against a seed-variance baseline built from ≥3 BF16 seeds at matched tokens. "Uniform" = beta ≈ 0 with a CI tight enough to exclude a practically relevant magnitude fixed in advance (e.g. a per-stratum gap exceeding the aggregate gap by more than a pre-stated factor).
- **Estimator error analysis.** Required, not optional: the per-bin standard error must be reported for the rarest bins and the slope fit must be weighted accordingly.
- **Mechanism check.** The fraction of gradient entries flushed to zero per scaling block, resolved by stratum, should track beta. The FTZ instrument of arXiv 2602.02047 is reusable here; the novel step is conditioning it on data strata rather than on tensors.
- **Intervention check.** Changing block size, applying stochastic rounding, or permuting block layout so that small and large magnitudes are not co-located should move beta if the block-scale mechanism is the cause, and should leave it unmoved if it is not.

A negative result — a flat, tightly bounded slope — is a publishable answer that licenses current practice, which is part of why the question is well-posed.

## Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3 primary + 1 supporting, all opened)

**Prior 1 — Pretraining Large Language Models with NVFP4.** arXiv:2509.25149 (NVIDIA, 2025). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25149
- Unit of analysis: the whole training run (12B hybrid Mamba-Transformer, 10T tokens), plus the model as a single object at evaluation.
- Endpoint: aggregate validation loss (one curve; relative error <1% in stable training, rising to ~1.5% during LR decay) and category-grouped downstream accuracy (General, Math, Multilingual, Code, Commonsense).
- Reference/comparator: FP8 training (with BF16 as the reference at smaller scale).
- Estimand: the average quality gap of the format — "can a 4-bit block-scaled format reach parity?"
- Mechanism language present but data-blind: Section 2 motivates NVFP4 as "minimizing small magnitudes being lost to zero," framed in terms of tensor value ranges and outliers, never linked to rare tokens, rare facts or low-resource languages. No frequency- or tail-stratified analysis anywhere.

**Prior 2 — Dissecting Outlier Dynamics in LLM NVFP4 Pretraining.** arXiv:2602.02047 (2026). https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02047v1
- Unit of analysis: tensors, 16x16 blocks, channels, layers/operators (q/k/v/o, gate, MLP up/gate/down). Explicitly *not* tokens or data slices.
- Endpoint: excess kurtosis, top-k magnitude, **flush-to-zero (FTZ) ratio** per block, quantization MSE; performance endpoint is final training loss and the relative loss gap to BF16 (0.939% NVFP4 vs 0.588% for their CHON variant, GLA-1.3B at 60B tokens); secondary zero-shot accuracy (ARC, HellaSwag, PIQA, SciQ).
- Reference/comparator: BF16 for the loss gap; FP8 secondary; the NVIDIA NVFP4 recipe as the baseline recipe.
- Estimand: the average NVFP4-vs-BF16 loss gap, plus descriptive correlations between architecture, outlier statistics and quantization sensitivity. The authors disclaim causality: "our conclusions are largely based on empirical correlations observed during training."
- It computes exactly the mechanism quantity the candidate needs — "a higher FTZ indicates greater underflow risk and irreversible loss of small-but-nonzero signals" — on a RedPajama subset, and never conditions it on a data stratum. No per-stratum or frequency-conditioned loss gap.

**Prior 3 — The Fair Language Model Paradox.** arXiv:2410.11985 (2024). https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11985v1
- Unit of analysis: individual tokens, grouped into log-frequency bins (bin i = frequency in [3^(i-1), 3^i)); low-frequency also defined as frequency in [3^0, 3^1) and as below the 95th percentile.
- Endpoint: per-token cross-entropy loss, token-balanced loss, per-token accuracy/perplexity, per-token learning speed — set explicitly against the conventional *average* training loss.
- Reference/comparator: weight decay lambda = 0 baseline; and high-frequency tokens as the within-run comparator.
- Estimand: the change in per-token loss/accuracy/learning speed for low-frequency tokens as weight decay increases. Finding: "as weight decay increases, low-frequency tokens are disproportionately depreciated."
- Intervention studied: **weight decay**. The optimizer, LR and schedule are fixed. Numeric precision appears only as a setup detail ("mixed precision training (fp16) was employed to optimize GPU memory usage..."); the paper does not study precision or quantization as a factor and does not test whether its findings interact with precision.

**Prior 4 (supporting) — Lost in Quantization: Activation Outliers Explain Language-Specific FP8 Sensitivity in Llama-3.** PROPOR 2026. https://preview.aclanthology.org/new-sigs/2026.propor-1.108/ (abstract page read; full PDF not fetched)
- Unit of analysis: one finished model (Meta-Llama-3-8B) across two language sets, plus activation values; not formally defined on the page.
- Endpoint: perplexity change (+18% English, +3.9% PT-BR under naive FP8 casting).
- Reference/comparator: INT8-with-outlier-handling and naive unscaled FP8 (E4M3); no clearly named unquantized baseline on the page.
- Estimand: how differently a quantization format affects performance across languages — informally stated.
- Post-hoc, by the page's own description ("a naive casting stress test (no calibration/scaling)"), applied to a finished model; stratified by *language identity*, not resource tier or corpus frequency; no slope fit against log frequency. Mechanism named is **activation outliers and E4M3 saturation**, explicitly not block-scaled shared exponents.

## Novelty — how the candidate differs from each

**vs Prior 1 (NVFP4 pretraining).** Same intervention and comparator family, different unit and estimand. Prior 1's unit is the run and its estimand is an average parity claim; the candidate's unit is the *data stratum within* such a run and its estimand is the derivative of the gap with respect to log corpus frequency. The candidate is in part a question *about* Prior 1's method of acceptance: it asks whether the aggregate loss on which Prior 1 declares parity is a sufficient statistic. Prior 1 also contains the live anomaly (Code −2.85 points, dismissed as evaluation noise) that the candidate would resolve with a seed-variance baseline instead of a suspicion. Prior 1 therefore does not answer the question; it is the reason the question is now askable and is the thing that would be revised.

**vs Prior 2 (Dissecting Outlier Dynamics).** This is the closest work and the reason novelty is not graded High. It shares the candidate's intervention (NVFP4 pretraining), its comparator (BF16 loss gap) *and* its mechanism instrument (per-block FTZ ratio). It differs on the axis the candidate is about: its conditioning variable is architectural/tensorial (which operator, which channel, which block, softmax vs linear attention), never distributional. It explains *where in the network* small values are destroyed; the candidate asks *whose data* those values belonged to. Concretely, Prior 2 would be satisfied by a format that destroys the same fraction of small gradients everywhere, while the candidate's limb (i) could still be answered either way; and Prior 2's FTZ is reported as a tensor statistic, not resolved by stratum, which is the exact operation limb (ii) requires. The candidate's mechanism check is thus a one-line change of index on an instrument that already exists — which strengthens feasibility and weakens any claim that the candidate needs new apparatus.

**vs Prior 3 (Fair Language Model Paradox).** Identical method, different intervention, and a different causal mechanism. Prior 3 establishes that a frequency-binned per-token loss analysis can expose disparate depreciation hidden by an average, and does so for weight decay — a regularizer acting on parameter norms. The candidate transplants that readout onto the numeric *format*, whose hypothesised mechanism (a shared block scale whose floor is set by the block's largest entry, so harm depends on magnitude co-location inside a 16- or 32-element block) is not a regularization effect and makes different predictions: it should respond to block size, stochastic rounding and block-layout permutation, none of which touch weight decay. The candidate's intervention check is precisely what separates the two accounts. Note also that Prior 3's existence is what makes the candidate's *silence* informative: the method was published in 2024 in the same field and has not been pointed at the format question.

**vs Prior 4 (Lost in Quantization) and the post-hoc compression family generally.** Different object (a finished model's inference arithmetic vs the training process's arithmetic), different mechanism (activation outliers under per-tensor/unscaled casting vs shared-block-exponent underflow of *gradient* entries during optimization), different stratification (language identity vs corpus frequency), and no slope. The substantive difference is not merely temporal: in post-hoc quantization the damage is applied once to a fixed function, whereas in low-precision training the flush-to-zero acts on the update signal every step, so a stratum's rare gradients can fail to be learned at all rather than being perturbed after learning. Prior 4 is also evidence *against* the candidate's assumed direction (the high-resource language suffered more), which converts the candidate from a thesis into an open question with two live hypotheses.

## Significance — consequence

**If yes (slope > 0, tail-differential).** The acceptance criterion now used to choose a numeric format for a nine-figure pretraining run — aggregate validation loss parity plus throughput — is shown to be the wrong statistic, because it integrates over exactly the strata that carry the penalty. That bears directly on live decisions: Prior 1 recommends NVFP4 for 10T-token production training on the strength of an aggregate gap under 1.5%. Format choice would acquire a distributional column, and the mitigations are already in the vendors' hands (smaller blocks, stochastic rounding, selective high-precision tensors, block-layout permutation) — the intervention check tells you which one to buy. It would also mean the compute savings of 4-bit training are partly financed by users of rare languages and rare facts, which is a different kind of claim than "4-bit is slightly worse."
**If no (flat slope, tight CI).** A genuine and useful licence: aggregate loss is a sufficient statistic for format acceptance, the published parity claims transfer to the tail, and the post-hoc-compression intuition (that compression falls hardest on the under-represented) does *not* carry over to training formats — which is itself a substantive negative result about where that intuition applies, and would redirect the compression-fairness literature away from a wrong analogy.
**If the mechanism check fails while the slope is real** (beta ≠ 0 but unrelated to per-stratum FTZ), the result survives as a phenomenon and the block-scale story is falsified, pointing at other candidates (gradient-noise interaction with rare-token updates, loss-scaling dynamics, optimizer-state precision).

## Significance — audience

1. **Pretraining leads at frontier labs and open consortia** choosing a format for the next run. They would add a pre-registered stratified acceptance test to the format-qualification process they already run, and would have a reason to pick MXFP8 over MXFP4 for a multilingual run even at equal aggregate loss. This is a decision made on a timescale of months with nine-figure stakes.
2. **Hardware vendors and recipe authors** (the authors of Prior 1 and of the ICML 2026 recipe study). Their published artifact is a recipe plus an evaluation table; a yes answer changes the table they must report, and gives them a format-design target (block size, scale encoding, de-biased estimators) with a measurable objective rather than aggregate loss alone. A no answer is a defence of their current claim, which they also want.
3. **Multilingual and low-resource NLP community.** They currently inherit frontier models without any instrument for whether a systems-level decision taxed them. A slope gives them a quantity to demand in model cards.
4. **Compression-fairness researchers** (the "what do compressed models forget" / disparate-impact line). The candidate tests whether their central finding generalizes from post-hoc compression to training arithmetic — a boundary condition on their own claim.
5. **Evaluation designers.** A yes answer makes aggregate validation loss insufficient for format decisions and creates demand for stratified loss reporting as a standard artifact.

## Significance — fecundity

New questions that become askable: per-stratum *precision scaling laws* (does the slope shrink with model scale, or steepen, as the format's noise floor interacts with the diminishing per-example gradient?); whether the slope and post-hoc compression's tail harm share a mechanism or are independent taxes that compound in a model that is both FP4-trained and FP4-served; whether block-layout permutation or magnitude-aware block assignment is a cheap mitigation with no throughput cost; whether "precision-aware data mixing" is coherent — up-weighting rare strata to compensate a known slope; whether the Code-category regressions already visible in published FP4 runs are instances of the same phenomenon (code has a heavy tail of rare identifiers, which is a testable prediction); whether FTZ-by-stratum is a usable online monitor for tail health during a run; and whether optimizer-state precision, not GEMM precision, carries the effect.

## Significance — anchoring

Touches: (a) the unexplained Code and Winogrande regressions in arXiv 2509.25149, reported and attributed to evaluation noise; (b) the aggregate form of precision scaling laws, which predict a scalar penalty and would need a stratum index if the slope is real [the specific Kumar et al. 2024 result remains recalled and unverified here]; (c) the compression-identified-exemplars / "what do compressed models forget" family, by asking whether it extends from inference to training arithmetic; (d) the low-resource double bind in MT [recalled, unverified]; (e) the sign anomaly in PROPOR 2026, where the high-resource language took the larger quantization hit — an unexplained observation that this design would either reproduce or dissolve; (f) Prior 2's open question of what the FTZ'd "small-but-nonzero signals" were actually carrying.

## Reason for silence

Three mechanisms compound, none of them "no one thought of it."

1. **New instrument, roughly twelve months old.** Block-scaled sub-8-bit *training* at a scale where anyone would care only became producible on Blackwell-class hardware in 2025-26; Prior 1 is dated 2025 and the recipe-scaling study is ICML 2026. Before that the question's object did not exist, and the available object (post-hoc quantization) has a different mechanism, which is why the existing tail literature grew up on the inference side.
2. **Disciplinary boundary, in the expensive direction.** The people who can run matched BF16-vs-MXFP4 pretraining runs are systems and kernel engineers at hardware vendors and frontier labs, whose success criterion is throughput at aggregate loss parity; the people who own the frequency-stratified disparate-impact method (Prior 3's community, and the compression-fairness family) work post-hoc on released checkpoints precisely because they cannot buy a matched control run. The method and the apparatus sit on opposite sides of a cost barrier, and the control run is the expensive half.
3. **Incentive asymmetry in who publishes format evaluations.** Most published evaluations of a new training format are authored by the format's vendor or by groups demonstrating that it works. Such an author has no reason to stratify an evaluation looking for a slice where their format loses, and when one appeared it was attributed to evaluation noise in a single sentence. There is no adversarial reviewer with a matched control run of their own.
4. (Aggravating) **Data access.** The stratification requires corpus frequencies, so the labs best placed to run the experiment are often the least able to publish the stratification, and the open-corpus groups that can compute frequencies cannot afford the matched runs.

The one serious hazard — the rare-stratum estimator being noise-dominated — is explicit in presupposition 7 and is answered structurally by fitting a weighted slope across all bins rather than contrasting head against tail. Scale-dependence is flagged as contested rather than assumed away.

Significance: **3/3.** A live, dated, expensive decision (which format for the next pretraining run); a named community that would change a concrete artifact (the format-qualification test and the evaluation table); informative in both directions; and it anchors on a specific published anomaly rather than on a general worry.

The defence is that the join has a non-obvious answer with a disputed sign — Prior 4 found the *high*-resource language hurt more, and the shared-scale argument cuts both ways depending on whether outlier magnitude tracks frequent or rare structure — and that nothing in either prior predicts which. But that defence is an argument, not evidence, and an adversary is entitled to press it: if the slope's sign is genuinely unpredictable from theory, the candidate must still show that *either* answer changes a decision, which the significance section argues but does not demonstrate.
