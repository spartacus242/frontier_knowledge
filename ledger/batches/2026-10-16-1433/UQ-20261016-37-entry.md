Question (canonical): For terminally sterilized products certified at a PNSU/SAL of 10^-6, over a fixed window with an enumerated released-unit denominator: (i) what one-sided lower bound on the realized probability of intrinsic non-sterility per released unit does the ascertained record (release sterility-test failures, non-sterility recalls, confirmed intrinsic-contamination outbreaks) support, under an explicit attribution rule and a stated ascertainment interval; and (ii) if that bound exceeds 10^-6, is the excess reproduced in direction and order of magnitude by recomputing PNSU for the same cycles under published non-log-linear (shoulder/tailing, Weibull) parameters? (V1, Gate 1 reformulation of the intake V0 frequency-claim version; both in the record.)
Alternative formulations: Does the realized non-sterility rate of terminally sterilized product match the one-in-a-million that licensed its release, and has anyone done that arithmetic? / What interval on the true probability of a non-sterile unit does the observed population of sterility failures support, versus the extrapolated SAL used at release?

The verdict stands on Gate 4. Two of the Gate 2 record's three characterisations of P1 are wrong, and the one the record called decisive is wrong in its central assertion.
(i) CLAIM REFUTED, and it was the record's load-bearing reading of P1. P1 does NOT pre-emptively dismiss, as impracticable, the comparison of the claimed SAL against the field record of sterile products. The sentence citing Winckels & Dorpema [85] says it is practically impossible to find a difference in the RATE OF INFECTION between products sterilized to an SAL of 10^-6 and products from a procedure ensuring only a 10^-3 contamination likelihood. That is a between-TIER discrimination argument, and P1 deploys it to justify RELAXING the required SAL, not to close off the realized-record comparison. The record read an argument for lowering the bar as a refusal to measure against it.
(ii) P1 does not POSE the provability question; it ASSERTS the answer. A request for an interrogative sentence about provability returned nothing on the page. The only question P1 poses is its section heading, "Do the current theoretical sterility assurance requirements reflect the actual safety requirements?" The assertion itself is confirmed and is stronger than the record states, with three limbs rather than two: the SAL is "neither evidence based nor can it be accurately ascertained in experiments, or attained practically".
(iii) The attribution to tailing kinetics is over-stated. P1 gives TWO independent grounds for unprovability: a generic limit of detection of about "10-2 test organisms per test object", which holds whatever the kinetics, and non-linear or tailing survivor curves, which it argues specifically for non-thermal and low-temperature processes rather than universally — the authors conclude that only steam and dry heat are properly validated. The 10^-2 figure is an expected-count limit of detection per test object, not a bare probability, and should not be quoted without its units. P1 also proposes a FOURTH, non-numeric tier the record omits: "microbiologically safe for the designated use".
(iv) The record's own adjacent-field claim needed four corrections. The canning figures are real but: 1.3 x 10^12 cans of low-acid food were CONSUMED, not produced, and the figure is not restricted to the United States; 3.8 x 10^-12 is a probability of botulism per can consumed, which the task force presents as a risk estimate ("one chance in every 260 billion cans of foods consumed") rather than self-describing it as a realized rate, though it is numerically the observed incidents-per-can ratio; the arithmetic checks (5 / 1.3e12 = 3.85e-12); and the Odlaug & Pflug 10^-7 to 10^-10 range is the likelihood of POST-PROCESSING CONTAMINATION with C. botulinum, not a spoilage or survival probability — the commensurable onward estimate is 10^-9 to 10^-12, inside which 3.8e-12 sits, while it falls about 26-fold BELOW 10^-10 and so outside the range the record set it beside. "Has been set beside" is itself unsupported: Podolak 2010 presents both figures in sequence in one Introduction paragraph and never states that they agree, differ or reconcile. All of it was verified only in that secondary source (Food Protection Trends, p. 161/167); the primary NFPA/CMI paper (J Food Prot 47:801-816) was never opened, and Podolak's own reference for it is internally inconsistent on year.
