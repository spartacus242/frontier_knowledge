Grading from the established facts and the literature answers supplied. I ran no new searches. Prior grades are void and were not inherited.

## Classification
The candidate is neither a disguised known nor a bare method transfer. The nearest priors are:
- Melamud & Moult 2009: simulates library sampling and estimates error rates, but gives no unobserved-class count and names no richness estimator.
- Pickrell 2010: poses saturation qualitatively and answers it with a subsampling curve only. No model, no endpoint, no interval.
- Wang & Lindsay 2005: estimates unseen genes, with splicing only as a bias source.
- Chiu & Chao 2016: a singleton-correction richness estimator, applied to viral contig spectra. It never mentions transcripts or isoforms.
- Schertzer 2025: observed D0 and perplexity only.
- Monzo 2025: qualitative.

So a depth-dependence limb was posed and qualitatively answered (Pickrell). The quantified question was not posed: a count of unobserved real isoforms per gene with intervals, held-out-library prediction, and the estimand status of the asymptote across admissible contamination models. A method never applied is not enough on its own. Here the question it answers also differs from the one the priors posed. Their question was "is discovery saturated". This one is "is the asymptote an estimand, and with what interval". That gives an open problem that is also un-asked, with one overlapping limb.

## Attacks
1. Triviality: Resolved. A one-page answer from existing knowledge cannot give per-gene numbers, intervals or a held-out test. The qualitative direction of (c) is known from Pickrell, but it is only one limb.
2. Disguise: Partial. Four named priors were read first-hand and none applies an estimator to isoforms. Pickrell's non-saturation statement does pose the qualitative limb of (c), and that limb is answered graphically. The distinction is real but not fully evidenced. The literature searches were shallow (abstract-level), and Kim et al. 2004 (Nat Genet 36:915), a per-gene splice-variant estimate from ESTs, and Garcia-Ortega & Martinez 2015 were seen only through a summariser. The reads do not support Unresolved.
3. Combinatorial novelty: Resolved. The interface problem is real. Singleton-driven estimators are known to be sensitive to artefact singletons (Chiu & Chao, Willis), and isoform libraries have RT artefacts (Houseley & Tollervey) and noisy splicing (Pickrell). Calibrating the contamination term by read class is a genuine statistical problem.
4. Presupposition failure: Partial. The "artefacts reproduce across donors" leg is unsupported. Houseley & Tollervey tested reproducibility only on the same in vitro substrates, so it neither supports nor refutes this leg, and the adversary labelled it as its own inference. Under the rule it can be Partial at most. The "reality is depth-indexed" leg is a possible outcome of branch (c), not a hidden premise. The question states the reality criterion (presupposition 5) and the dissolution branch explicitly. Presupposition 2 (spike-in transferability) is contested. The "all admissible contamination models" clause and the technical-replicate calibration are the safeguards, but they are not evidenced to work. Not Unresolved.
5. Vagueness: Resolved. The rule is pre-declared, (b) is a falsifiable held-out test, and (c) has both outcomes defined. One soft point: "admissible" must be fixed before the data are seen.
6. Significance inflation: Partial. Annotation and long-read consortia would care about knowing whether a finite catalogue target exists. The record scores 2/3, and presupposition 6 concedes the estimators are lower bounds. Monzo et al. support the audience only qualitatively.
7. Reason for silence: Partial. A plausible mechanism exists: replicate deep long-read libraries became available only recently (ENCODE4, GTEx long-read), the richness-estimator literature sits in ecology and microbiome work, and the EST-era work counted genes. This is inference. The searches were limited, with 7 per reader.
8. Dissolution: Resolved. Dissolution (isoforms per gene as a depth-indexed quantity) is one pre-specified answer to (c) rather than a way the question disappears. With the declared individuation rule the target is well defined, so the question gets an answer either way.

## Verdict
Provisional. No Unresolved on attacks 1, 2 or 4. The attacks left Partial are 2, 4, 6 and 7. The highest-risk one, Disguise, should be re-checked against Kim et al. 2004 and Garcia-Ortega & Martinez 2015 at full-text level.
