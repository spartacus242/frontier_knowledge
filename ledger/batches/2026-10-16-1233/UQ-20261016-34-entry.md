Question (canonical): In a dense whole-brain connectome whose two hemispheres were reconstructed from a single image volume, how large must a difference in a cell-type-to-cell-type connection weight be before it exceeds the reconstruction-and-proofreading noise floor — i.e. what is the detection limit for any connectome-to-connectome comparison, measured by independent re-reconstruction of the same volume? (Gate 1 reformulation used for Gates 2–3 demotes "independent re-reconstruction" from the question to one candidate estimator, and adds stratification by edge weight, cell type, neurite length and proofreading effort; both versions in the record.)
Alternative formulations: What is the smallest circuit difference a connectome can resolve? · Of the left–right difference in connection weights inside a single fly brain, how much is the pipeline and how much is the animal?
Motivating gap: Claimed at intake that whole-brain connectomes are compared across hemispheres, individuals and sexes with no error model saying which differences are resolvable, and that "the detection limit has not been stated as a number." Gate 2 found every clause contradicted by the flagship papers of the named instrument: the error model, the pipeline/biology decomposition and the number (±30% in edge weight) all exist and are already used to filter between-connectome biological claims. Verification and the late Gate 4 both narrow that: the number exists and is in use, but it is a do-not-interpret-below floor derived from a forward uncertainty budget — not a detection limit at a stated confidence, and not measured by any independent re-reconstruction. On the adversary's arithmetic over two of the paper's own sentences, a 30% band would pass roughly a quarter of pure-noise comparisons at a 30-synapse edge. So the clause that falls is "no error model exists"; the clause that survives is that the limit in use is undocumented, borrowed across volumes, and patched by hand curation.
Presuppositions: (1) two hemispheres from one volume/pipeline, cell-type matchable — established; (2) genuinely independent re-reconstruction achievable, "independent" needs operationalising — contested, unmet by any opened source; (3) true left–right biological variation neither zero nor swamping — established with a number (65% of observed left–right edge-weight variability inside the technical-noise range); (4) per-edge proofreading status/effort retrievable — partly (completion rate is the published proxy, region-specific: mushroom body 46% FlyWire-right vs 77% hemibrain; per-neuron effort unpublished); (5) floor transfers to mouse scale — contested and explicitly denied by the source ("made specifically for the two hemispheres of FlyWire"); (6) THE STAKE: between-connectome differences are used to support biological claims that would need qualifying if below the floor — FALSE as stated, and restated at Gate 4: the 2025 male-CNS connectome already filters dimorphism claims by per-dataset noise thresholds and reports a lower bound, so the stake is not "claims are unfiltered" but "the filters in use differ by about 1.4x between datasets with no stated derivation, and expert curation was required to separate biological signal from cases of technical noise"; (7) synapse count is the right weight unit — flagged, untouched by any opened source; (8) [NEW at the late Gate 4, load-bearing and adverse] that a second pass over the same voxels can measure the dominant error term — CONTESTED and probably false: reconstruction errors are made at image defects (folds, lost sections, poor staining), so any re-reconstruction of the same volume inherits them and fails in the same places, and the male-CNS paper states that technical noise "is more commonly correlated across both sides of one brain" and that "most apparent differences in male-female connectivity were explained by segmentation issues". The candidate's estimator therefore captures the operator-plus-algorithm component and silently sets the common-mode term to zero.
