Domain(s): Other STEM — hydrology, meteorology, coastal and wind engineering (extreme-value statistics as practised); engineering standards and design-value issuance. Method imported from forecast verification / coverage-test backtesting.
Question (canonical): Over a pre-declared frame of (site, issued value, issue date) triples from dated standards and published quantile maps — 50-year wind speeds, 100-year flood discharges, 100-year significant wave heights — restricted to issued values carrying an explicit nominal annual exceedance probability p, what is the pooled realized exceedance rate in the post-issue site-years, estimated under a declared spatial-dependence model and reported as a ratio to nominal p; and under a pre-declared decomposition, how much of any departure is attributable to (a) issue-era estimator bias (issue-era estimator vs modern estimator on the identical issue-era record), (b) parameter and record-length uncertainty at issue, and (c) post-issue trend?
Alternative formulations: Have the 100-year values we published actually been exceeded about once a century? / Treating each issued design value as a probabilistic forecast with a stated exceedance rate, what are its out-of-sample verification statistics, and does the miscalibration sit in the estimator or in the climate?
Motivating gap: Silence mapping. EVA practice is dense with how-answers (estimator choice, regionalisation, nonstationary GEV) and standards are revised by re-estimation, yet the issued product — a dated number for a named site with a stated nominal rate — is never scored against what followed. Gate 2 added two gap exhibits: the Colorado State 100y/24h monitor flags exceedances daily and accumulates no counts, expected counts or rate; Kjeldsen & Prosdocimi (2023) run the field's own annual-exceedance-count machinery on 842 UK gauges but in-sample only, against thresholds fitted on the same record. Intake arrived with NOVELTY CHECK NOT RUN; Gate 2 was the first search.
Presuppositions: (1) issued values retrievable at site level with issue dates — bounds scope; (2) enough post-issue site-years for power — CONTESTED, forces a declared spatial-dependence model (0.4 expected exceedances/site over 40 yr at p=0.01); (3) measurement system stable, or rating-curve / anemometer-height / averaging-time changes enter as covariates — CONTESTED; (4) nominal rate readable as a frequency — CONTESTED, converted by V2 into a reported classification count on the way into the frame; (5) estimator bias at issue and post-issue nonstationarity separable — CONTESTED and load-bearing; (6) record quality adequate in the exceedance tail specifically — CONTESTED, and biases the realized count downward, i.e. toward the reassuring null.
What would count as an answer: A pooled verification over the pre-declared frame reporting the realized exceedance rate with a dependence-aware interval as a ratio to nominal, plus the three-way decomposition, limb (a) recovered by re-running the issue-era estimator on the issue-era record. A clean null is informative, so the question is not answerable identically regardless of evidence.
