## UQ-53: a lone third adversary, overturned by two cold judges

The question treats a long-read transcriptome as an incomplete sample of a gene's distinct
spliced-isoform classes and asks what a false-positive-aware richness estimator gives for
the number of unobserved *real* isoforms per gene, whether its prediction survives a
held-out library, and whether the per-gene asymptote is finite and depth-stable at all.

Adversary 3 graded Disguise and Presupposition failure Unresolved — independently fatal.
Each ground had two legs, and verification broke one of each:

- **Wang, Lindsay et al. 2005 estimates unobserved GENES, not isoforms.** Its real title is
  "Gene capture prediction and overlap estimation in EST sequencing from one or multiple
  libraries"; the zero class is the number of genes with no EST in the library; the
  "35%–40%" bias and the 12030-vs-9155 figures are estimates of gene number; and the "ISO"
  in "ISO error correction" is *insufficient overlap*, a clustering error, not isoform.
- **Houseley & Tollervey 2010 does not support the donor leg.** Every quoted phrase is in
  it, but reproducibility was tested only across repeated RT reactions, enzymes,
  temperatures and priming on the *same in vitro purified substrates* — never across
  independent RNA preparations, donors or individuals. Adversary 3 had labelled that
  extension as its own inference, and it is unsourced, so the ground is Partial at best.

What stood: Melamud & Moult and Pickrell do pose the depth-dependence limb, and Pickrell
says so in as many words ("We see no sign that our identification of isoforms is near
saturation"). But Pickrell's depth analysis is a **subsampling graph with no model fitted**,
neither paper names a richness estimator, and four fresh sweeps found no per-gene
unobserved-isoform estimate at any unit, with or without a false-positive term, and no
paper posing whether per-gene isoform number has a finite asymptote.

A late Gate 4 ran separately and cold — four neutral literature questions, then two judges
handed the Gate 0–3 record, the established facts, every answer, and the prior grade
declared void. **Both returned Provisional independently with no Unresolved attack.**
Judge 2 recorded its key kill as "None". Validated is barred by well-posedness 2/3 and by
two close neighbours read only through a summariser, not by any surviving fatal objection.

A citation error turned up inside the attack and is flagged in the entry: **Chiu & Chao
2016 was cited under Willis's title.** PeerJ 4:e1634 is "Estimating and comparing microbial
diversity in the presence of sequencing errors"; "Species richness estimation with high
diversity but spurious singletons" is Willis, arXiv:1604.02598. Both papers are real, and
Chiu & Chao's worked data are viral metagenome contigs — transcripts, isoforms, RNA-seq and
cDNA do not appear in it.

A defect in our own judge brief is also recorded: it quoted the record's well-posedness 3/3
rather than the entry's Gate 5 regrade to 2/3. That could only have made the judges more
lenient, and they still returned Provisional with both fatal attacks Partial.

## UQ-54: a prior that predicts the quantity and never measures it

The question asks whether in-cell cryo-ET state censuses match the occupancy predicted by
mass action from in vitro affinities and measured intracellular free ligand, and what the
sign and size of the discrepancy is across complexes. Gate 5 downgraded novelty from Medium
to Low on two priors. Both were opened:

- **The metalation calculators only predict.** They combine in vitro dissociation constants
  with intracellular available metal concentrations to predict fractional occupancy and
  discuss literature agreement case by case, reporting **no independently measured in-cell
  protein occupancy anywhere**. They supply two of the candidate's three terms and never the
  third — which is the one the question is about.
- **The in-cell NMR paper lacks the free-ligand term.** One protein (carbonic anhydrase II),
  four sulfonamide ligands, HEK 293T cells. Every Table 3 affinity figure is real and in nM,
  but the MZA in-cell value is not an independent measurement and intracellular free ligand
  is **held known by design rather than measured in situ**. Its conclusion is that in-cell
  Kd is similar to in vitro.

No late Gate 4 was run, and the entry says why: Gate 4 ran properly in the batch with three
adversaries and no Unresolved attack. What fell was the judge's Gate 2 ground, not an
adversary's, and the late-Gate-4 rule is for the latter.

The entry had written that withdrawing the disguised-known ground "would not rehabilitate
this entry". That sentence is retracted in place: it conflates not reaching Validated with
staying Rejected. Its objection — that the candidate's informative and measurable regimes
overlap in no demonstrated instance — keeps all its force as the Provisional cap and the
first promotion condition.

## The two kills that held, with their figures corrected

**UQ-51** (does the human body's near-equal cell biomass per logarithmic size class
generalise, and does its slope move with body mass?) stays Rejected as asked-but-open.
Hatton et al. does pose the extension, but as **one conditional sentence** — "If we assume
that the ratios of different cell types in different tissues are constant across species,
we should expect a similar inverse cell size-count relation to hold in other mammals" —
not as the two independent assertions the kill cited. A third attributed sentence could not
be re-confirmed and is marked unverified. The paper's cross-species dataset is **cell size
only** for 33 cell types, with no counts. And **Savage et al. 2007 states no "X of 18"
count at all**: its Fig. 2 caption excepts only alveolar macrophages among the 14 strategy-i
types, so the record's "14 of 18" was the strategy-i type count read as a CI count.

One residual cuts the candidate's way. Hatton's reference 34 is confirmed as Fankhauser's
Quarterly Review of Biology 20:20-78 (1945), which **could not be read**; the salamander
compensation result was read instead in a *different* Fankhauser 1945 paper (J Exp Zool
100:445-455), where it is **per organ, not whole body**, and "only partial as a rule" in
haploids. So the salamander precedent is not a non-human whole-body result, which
strengthens rather than weakens the finding that no such census exists — none was found by
Gate 2, by either adversary, or by two further dedicated sweeps.

**UQ-52** (is essentiality a property of the gene or of how fast its product is removed?)
stays Rejected: the headline discordance rate is measured at proteome scale in the
candidate's own field. Both priors were re-identified at the publisher, settling a risk one
reader raised — JCB 224(2):e202409007 is Gameiro et al., a research article, not a
commentary. The arithmetic tension in its two figures is real and now resolved: **55% and
38% are not complements**, because 499/906 is over all essential ORFs while the 38% is over
essential proteins judged *degraded*. P2's 69% **cannot be reconstructed from the paper**,
whose Methods give two different YKO denominators (560 and 650) and no counts; its "11 of
281" carries no percentage, no stated pool, and describes candidates rather than confirmed
new essentials, five with prior low-throughput support. Both papers' comparator is the YKO
deletion collection, not CRISPR.

Against the candidate and for it at once: P1 reports that protein levels did **not** differ
between essential proteins with and without a fitness defect, which cuts against residual
protein as the cause. The explanation is genuinely open — but un-asked rather than asked,
and the candidate leads with the rate that is answered.

## Routing

Two rows, one of them narrowed, and the narrowing is the part worth reading.

**UQ-51** routes cleanly: posed as a conditional expectation, premise supplied, measurement
absent everywhere we looked.

**UQ-52**'s row was drafted at Gate 5 as the composite decomposition — does the discordance
track half-life once depletion extent is conditioned on? A first verification pass withdrew
it outright, having found that neither paper poses half-life as an explanation for the
discordance. That was too strong. Three independent readers agree P2 contains, verbatim and
untested, "milder depletion could also reflect longer protein half-lives". The paper poses
half-life as a cause of milder *depletion*, and separately poses incomplete degradation as a
cause of the missing fitness defect; it never joins them. So the composite is not routed —
unposed, and on the entry's own analysis not identified on a stratum selected on
degradability, which correlates with the half-life regressor — while the **posed narrow
link** is routed on its own terms: does measured half-life predict the published per-strain
depletion extent? Tier A, a desk re-analysis. The row says which limb is which and why.

## What to carry forward

1. **A kill from a reader that two earlier readers contradicted is the first thing to
   open.** UQ-53's rejection came from adversary 3 after adversaries 1 and 2 said
   Provisional, and both of its fatal grounds lost a leg on first-hand reading.
2. **A prior that predicts a quantity without ever measuring it is not a disguised known**
   for a question about the gap between prediction and measurement. That is UQ-54's whole
   reversal in one line.
3. **Same author, same year, two different papers** — again. The Fankhauser compensation
   claim lives in J Exp Zool 100:445-455, not the QRB review cited beside it, and the QRB
   review could not be read either way.
4. **A title can be attached to the wrong real paper.** Chiu & Chao 2016 was cited under
   Willis's arXiv title; both papers exist and do different things.
5. **"The row is withdrawn" can be as wrong as "the row stands."** UQ-52's narrow link was
   posed verbatim; only the composite was not. Narrow the row, and say in it which limb was
   withheld and why.
6. **Two numbers can be real, quoted correctly, and not complements.** 55% and 38% sit on
   different denominators in the same paragraph, and a published percentage that cannot be
   reconstructed from the paper's own counts (P2's 69%, against denominators of 560 and 650)
   should be reported with that caveat attached.
