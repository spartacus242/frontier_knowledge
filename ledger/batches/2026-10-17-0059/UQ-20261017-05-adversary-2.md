## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)
Physicists quote an error bar with every measurement. Take many published measurements of the SAME physical quantity and ask how well those error bars predicted the actual spread of the measurements: if the quoted sigmas were honest, the deviations divided by the sigmas should behave like unit-variance draws; in practice they do not, and the multiplier needed to fix them is a "calibration factor". The candidate asks one thing only: does that multiplier differ between measurements whose analysis was BLINDED (hidden signal region, hidden offset/scale, salted data) and measurements that were not, once you compare within the same quantity and control for era and intrinsic precision — or is the blinded/unblinded split so tangled with era and collaboration size that the historical record cannot answer it at all? It deliberately does NOT ask whether blinding moves central values (that limb is conceded to be asked-and-open). So: not "are physics error bars too small" (answered: yes, repeatedly) but "does the analysis protocol predict how wrong the error bar is".

## What the opened pages actually say
- **Croft & Dailey 2011 (arXiv:1112.3108), new prior the record missed.** 637 measurements of 12 cosmological parameters; accuracy gauged as N_sigma from the WMAP7 value. Pre-2000: "an average underestimate of the error bars by a factor of ~2"; peculiar-velocity methods likewise "roughly 2"; recent accuracy "generally what would be expected given the quoted error bars"; of 28 post-2003 Omega_Lambda measurements "only 2 are more than 1 sigma from the WMAP results", chi2 per data point 22.7, and "without this outlier, the chi2 per data point is only 0.26". Stratified by technique, parameter and YEAR. No blinding/masking covariate; the single occurrence of blinding is a recommendation in the abstract: "Wider use of blind analyses in cosmology could help to avoid this."
- **López-Corredoira (arXiv:2411.13621), second new prior the record missed.** 163 H0 measurements 1976-2019 against a weighted average; chi2 = 575.7 for N = 163; after removing 27 outliers (>2.8 sigma), chi2 = 161.3 for N = 136; tail fit P(>x sigma) = (0.93 +/- 0.06) exp[-(0.720 +/- 0.013)x]; 2001-2019 subsample chi2 = 195.2 for N = 85. The words "blind" and "masked" do not occur; era handled only as a subsample, never modelled.
- **Golub, arXiv:2311.13542 (spot-check of the record's load-bearing critique).** Real; R. Golub, 22 Nov 2023, no journal ref. Argument and anecdote only, no data analysis; five cases (Millikan/Feynman, Galison, pi->e nu, Irvine gravitational measurement, KTeV), not three as the record states — immaterial to the grade. On uncertainties it says blinding "can severely inhibit the search for systematic errors" and that "overzealous blinding (of the sign) was incompatible with a proper consideration of systematic errors"; it says "there is surprisingly little evidence for experimenter bias in physics research"; it poses NO open question and proposes no study. So the record's characterisation of it is substantively fair (its cost claim is about the systematic-error budget), and the candidate's endpoint is the measurable form of Golub's own central claim.
- **Lund 2025 (10.1007/s10838-024-09706-4)** is "Bessel and the Epistemology of Observational Relativity" — 19th-century personal equation, conceptual, no corpus, no blinding. Fetch spent, off target; reported for completeness.
