Question (canonical, V0 as intaken): For a published human cell atlas as actually sampled and processed, what is its completeness function — the probability that a cell population is recovered and reported as a distinct cluster, as a joint function of that population's frequency and its transcriptional distinctness — and what does that function imply about how many in-principle-detectable populations the atlas missed?

Question (canonical, R1 — one Gate 1 reformulation, adopted, for well-posedness only; both versions retained): For each of K published human cell atlases that release raw reads plus pipeline code, inject real cell populations (borrowed from other tissues or other donors, mixed at the read level so ambient-RNA and doublet structure are preserved) into the atlas's own raw data at controlled frequencies f and at pre-specified distinctness levels d, and re-run the atlas's own published pipeline end to end at its own published clustering resolution, scoring an injected population as RECOVERED when >=50% of its cells fall in one cluster of purity >=p that the pipeline reports as distinct. Then: (a) what is the recovery-probability surface R_atlas(f,d), with its 50% contour and bootstrap bands; (b) do the contours of different atlases coincide after conditioning on cells profiled and on what the dissociation protocol delivered; and (c) integrating 1 - R_atlas against a stated prior over (f,d) for populations the dissociation protocol actually delivered, what is the expected number of recoverable-in-principle populations the atlas did not report, and how sensitive is that number to the prior?
Gate 1 note: R1 changes no content; it fixes the recovery criterion, the resolution rule, the injection construction, and the conditioning that V0 left implicit. V0 alone is not well-posed, because "recovered and reported as a distinct cluster" has no threshold and no resolution, and "missed" has no conditioning set.

Alternative formulations:
- (lay) What is a cell atlas's detection limit, stated the way an astronomical survey states completeness, and what does it imply about how many cell types remain unseen at that depth?
- (operational) If real cell populations of known rarity and known transcriptional separation are injected into a published atlas's raw data and pushed through that atlas's own pipeline, at what frequency-by-distinctness contour does recovery fall below 50%?
- (metrological, neighbour-field idiom) What is the limit of detection of a cell atlas as an assay for cell populations, where the analyte's concentration axis is population frequency and the second axis is analyte separability from matrix?

Motivating gap: Every modern sky survey publishes a completeness or selection function derived by injecting synthetic sources into the real data and re-running the pipeline; no catalogue claim is usable without it. Cell atlases publish cell counts and comprehensiveness language instead (Human Cell Atlas white paper, arXiv:1810.05192), and downstream users treat atlases as reference denominators for mapping and for adjudicating "new cell type" claims. The pre-intake search returned only method-ranking work (PMC12840603, a 2025 review of rare-cell-type detection strategies in multi-sample data; PMC6180488, cell-type identification under low sampling coverage). Those benchmark METHODS on synthetic or downsampled data; the transplanted object here is a property of a PUBLISHED ARTEFACT — its own completeness contour under its own pipeline — and the missed-population estimate that follows. That difference is the whole candidate and also its main vulnerability, so it is stated in the open. Gate 2 has now found that the second half of the candidate (the missed count) exists in print under richness-extrapolation vocabulary (P1) and that the two axes (abundance x transcriptional separability) exist in print as predictors of METHOD performance (P2); the contour-of-a-published-artefact object was not found.

Presuppositions:
1. The raw data and the exact published pipeline of the target atlases are available and re-runnable. PARTLY CONTESTED: several atlases release raw reads and code, others do not; atlas selection is constrained by this.
2. "Transcriptional distinctness" admits a pre-specified, cross-atlas-comparable parameterisation. CONTESTED: candidate metrics include number of differentially expressed genes above a fixed effect size, or distance in a fixed reference embedding; the choice changes the contour's axes and must be pre-registered with sensitivity analysis. Gate 2 note: P2 (Liu et al. 2026) uses a "transcriptional separability" construct for the same purpose, which both supports feasibility and narrows the candidate's claim to originality on this axis.
3. Injected populations are realistic enough that their recovery probability transfers to real biology. CONTESTED — the central methodological risk; mitigation is to inject real populations borrowed from other tissues or donors at controlled frequencies, with ambient-RNA and doublet structure modelled, rather than parametric simulations.
4. "Number of populations missed" is a meaningful quantity. CONTESTED: cell-type ontology is itself disputed, so the primary estimand must be recoverable clusters at a fixed resolution rule, with the missed count reported as a derived, prior-dependent figure.
5. Atlases are used as if comprehensive. ESTABLISHED by usage: reference mapping, novel-cell-type adjudication, and power calculations for follow-up studies all lean on them.
6. Detection depends mainly on frequency and distinctness rather than on dissociation and capture bias. CONTESTED: dissociation loss can make a population undetectable at any sequencing depth; the completeness function must be declared as conditional on what the dissociation protocol delivered, which bounds the claim.
7. NEW, surfaced at Gate 1 and not in the intake: the published pipeline is mechanically re-runnable. CONTESTED and the sharpest well-posedness defect. Published atlases are human-in-the-loop artefacts — iterative subclustering, expert annotation, marker inspection, resolution chosen by eye. An injection run reproduces the automated part plus a declared surrogate annotation rule, so the estimand is the recovery probability of the machine pipeline, not of the artefact as published, and the sign of the gap is not known a priori (curators may be better than the automated rule at noticing a small distinct cluster, or worse, having no reason to look). This must be declared as the estimand, with a human-adjudication subsample on a small set of injected runs to bound the gap in at least one direction.

What would count as an answer: Per-atlas recovery curves from injection-recovery runs through each atlas's own published pipeline (plus the declared surrogate annotation rule of presupposition 7), reported as a 50%-recovery contour with confidence bands in (frequency, distinctness) space; plus a forward estimate of missed detectable populations obtained by integrating the completeness function against a stated abundance-distinctness prior, with explicit sensitivity to that prior and explicit conditioning on the dissociation protocol. Pre-committed outcomes: (i) contours differ materially between atlases, so cross-atlas rare-population claims are not comparable and a completeness statement should accompany atlas releases; (ii) contours are similar and shallow, so comprehensiveness claims are approximately safe and few populations are missed; (iii) the missed-count integral is prior-dominated, so only the contour is reportable — declared in advance as an acceptable outcome, so the question cannot be rescued by a vague answer. Added at Gate 1: (iv) the human-adjudication subsample shows the surrogate annotation rule misstates recovery by more than the contour's own bands, in which case the automated-pipeline contour is reportable but the artefact-level claim is not.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
vs P1 — same target field and the same second-half question ("how many cell types are we missing"), opposite epistemics. P1 runs the BACKWARD route: take the clusters the pipelines already reported, treat samples as incidence draws, and extrapolate unseen richness with Chao2. That estimator is blind by construction to exactly what the candidate measures: it conditions on the detection process rather than characterising it, and its homogeneity-of-detectability assumption means a population that is undetectable at any abundance in that pipeline (low distinctness, or lost in dissociation) contributes nothing to Q1/Q2 and so cannot raise the estimate — which is how a six-sample isocortex can come out "fully profiled". The candidate runs the FORWARD route: measure R(f,d) per artefact and integrate 1-R against a declared prior. The candidate therefore supplies the detection function P1's estimator silently assumes, and would be the calibration that decides whether P1's "fully profiled" verdicts are findings or artefacts of equal-detectability. But P1 does occupy the candidate's missed-count slot in print, so the derived-count limb is NOT un-asked; only the contour limb is. This is the strongest novelty threat on the record.
vs P2 — shares the candidate's two axes almost exactly (abundance x transcriptional separability) and shares real-data-based recovery experiments, so the candidate cannot claim the axes as new. Three differences, all load-bearing: (i) the varying unit in P2 is the TOOL — eleven dedicated rare-cell detectors are ranked — whereas the candidate's varying unit is the published ARTEFACT, detected by its own pipeline at its own resolution, which is what a downstream user actually relies on and what no atlas reports; (ii) P2 builds rarity by DOWNSAMPLING a cell type the dataset already contains and already resolved, which cannot probe populations the pipeline would never have surfaced and leaves the ambient-RNA and doublet structure of the new mixture wrong, whereas the candidate injects foreign real populations at the read level; (iii) P2 reports association of performance with abundance and separability, not a recovery-probability surface with a 50% contour and bands, and reports nothing about how many populations any atlas missed. P2 is nonetheless the formulation a reviewer would hold up first.
vs P3 (and the scPower/SCOPIT family) — these answer "how many cells must I draw to capture k cells of a population at frequency f", i.e. completeness of the SAMPLING step under a multinomial/beta-binomial model, with no pipeline and no distinctness axis. They are the standard tool users currently substitute for the question, and their adequacy is the candidate's outcome (ii): if contours are shallow and similar across atlases, the sampling-only calculators are enough and the candidate's answer is "nothing further needed". The candidate measures the composition of sampling with QC, integration, clustering, resolution choice and annotation — the part that turns captured cells into a reported cluster, where the distinctness axis lives.
vs the neighbouring-field practice (metagenomic internal standards, query 9; flow/CyTOF LOD; astronomy injection-recovery, queries 1 and 7) — the method is routine THERE and absent HERE, which is the transplant's whole content; and in every one of those fields the published completeness statement is one-dimensional in concentration, while the candidate's second axis (separability from the matrix) is specific to catalogues whose entries are defined by clustering.
vs the detectability-threshold theory (query 11) — statistics and statistical physics have exact thresholds in (cluster size, separation) for planted structures and high-dimensional mixtures, which is the asymptotic shape of the candidate's contour. None of it is instantiated on a real published artefact under that artefact's own pipeline, and the gap between a theoretical threshold and a pipeline's measured contour is itself part of what the candidate would report.

Significance — consequence: If contours differ materially between atlases (outcome i), then every cross-atlas statement about a rare population's presence, absence or frequency is confounded by the reporting artefact's own detection function, and the correct unit of comparison becomes completeness-corrected frequency rather than reported frequency; atlas releases acquire a completeness statement as a release artefact, exactly as survey catalogues did; "we did not see this population" becomes a bounded claim with a stated detection probability; and P1-style richness extrapolations must be re-done with unequal detectability. If contours are similar and shallow (outcome ii), a specific worry about the reference infrastructure is retired on evidence, the sampling-only calculators (P3, scPower, SCOPIT) are vindicated as sufficient, and comprehensiveness language is licensed up to a stated depth — a genuinely useful negative. If the missed-count integral is prior-dominated (outcome iii), the count is declared unreportable in advance and the contour alone is the deliverable, which still changes what an atlas publishes. If the human-adjudication subsample breaks the surrogate annotation rule (outcome iv, added at Gate 1), the finding is that artefact-level completeness is not estimable without the curators, which is itself a consequential limit on auditing published atlases.
Significance — audience: (a) atlas consortia and their data platforms — HCA, CZI CELLxGENE, the Human Lung Cell Atlas, Tabula Sapiens, Gut Cell Atlas, BICCN/BRAIN — who decide what accompanies a release, and would act by publishing a contour beside the cell count; (b) reviewers and editors adjudicating "novel cell population" claims, who today have no denominator and would gain an operational test (is the claimed population outside the reporting atlas's contour?); (c) Cell Ontology / cell-type nomenclature curators, who must decide when absence from an atlas is evidence; (d) rare-cell-detection method developers (P2's own audience, which already asks the abundance x separability question of tools and would gain the artefact-level baseline those tools are supposed to beat); (e) groups using atlases as reference denominators for deconvolution, reference mapping and label transfer, and for power calculations of follow-up studies, who would substitute a pipeline-aware sensitivity term for a sampling-only one; (f) funders choosing depth against breadth. Significance — fecundity: once a measured completeness function exists per artefact, these become askable and were not before: completeness-corrected cross-tissue and cross-condition frequency comparison (the deconvolution denominator problem); optimal design as a contour-lifting problem (cells vs donors vs protocol vs resolution rule, each with a measurable effect on R); protocol-level completeness as a comparable quantity across dissociation, single-nucleus and spatial assays, which turns the long-standing dissociation-bias literature from a caveat into an axis; calibration of richness extrapolators against a measured detection function, i.e. a repaired version of P1; completeness-weighted integration of multi-atlas meta-references; an operational definition of "new cell type" relative to a stated contour; and the empirical-versus-theoretical comparison against the planted-structure detectability thresholds from query 11.
Significance — anchoring: the open question of how many human cell types exist (P1; the immune-cell counting debates); the unsettled definition of a cell type, which the candidate deliberately sidesteps by fixing a resolution rule; the reproducibility of rare-population claims; the known-but-uncomputed dissociation and capture bias; the gap between detectability thresholds proved in statistics and anything measured on real catalogues; and, by construction, the astronomical selection-function practice whose absence here is the anomaly.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism, argued): the primary mechanism is a disciplinary boundary between resample-impossible and resample-cheap sciences. Astronomy's completeness culture exists because the survey is the only access to the sources: you cannot re-observe a faint galaxy under better conditions on demand, so characterising the catalogue's own blind spots is the only route to a comparable claim, and the selection function became mandatory. Single-cell biology is resample-cheap: if you suspect a missed population you sort, enrich, go deeper, or build a better detector — so the field's effort flows forward into assays and detection tools (P2's eleven methods) rather than backward into deficiency curves for artefacts already published. This is reinforced by a second, structural mechanism: genomics benchmarking convention evaluates METHODS, not DATASETS. Tools compete and are ranked; a published atlas is a resource to be used and cited, and there is no venue, incentive or norm for publishing a sensitivity deficiency curve for someone else's atlas, nor any incentive for a consortium to publish its own. Two supporting mechanisms: the estimand looked unaskable while "cell type" remained disputed, so the field's first move in this direction (P1, 2025) borrowed an ecology estimator that needs no detection model; and the feasibility threshold is recent — containerised atlas pipelines, public raw reads, read-level mixing tools and the compute to re-run a whole atlas many hundreds of times are all newer than the atlases themselves. Caveat to be tested at Gate 4 attack 7: these mechanisms explain the silence on the CONTOUR, but P1 shows the community was not silent on the missed COUNT, so the reason-for-silence account is only as wide as the contour limb.
