## Question (canonical)

**Version A (as submitted).** For data drawn from a generating process whose entropy rate H is known exactly by construction, does the loss achievable by a transformer family converge to H as parameters and tokens grow, or to a strictly positive floor H + Delta, and is Delta a property of the architecture rather than of the data?

**Version B (Gate 1 reformulation — the one permitted reformulation, for well-posedness only; both versions stand in the record).** Under byte-level modelling (declared rule: the model's token set is the process's own terminal alphabet under a bijective map, so per-token cross-entropy and per-symbol entropy rate are in nats per symbol on the same conditioning and base), for each of two pre-declared process families — (i) a fixed 8-state discrete HMM with declared transition and emission matrices, H computed by the mixed-state/Blackwell integral to a stated numerical tolerance at least 10x finer than the equivalence band, and (ii) a declared PCFG in Chomsky normal form with declared production probabilities, H per terminal symbol computed in closed form from production entropies and expected derivation length — and for each of two pre-declared architecture families (a pre-LN decoder-only transformer; one non-attention gated-linear-recurrent/SSM family) trained at matched FLOPs on fresh samples, what is Delta = (asymptote of achieved cross-entropy) − H in nats per symbol, with Delta declared indistinguishable from zero if its 95% interval lies inside +/-0.01 nats/symbol? Asymptote estimator pre-declared as E in L(N,D) = E + A/N^alpha + B/D^beta, Huber-fitted over the ladder with E's interval by bootstrap over ladder points, plus one infinite-data sensitivity arm (fresh tokens at every step, only N varying).

**What the repair presupposes (tested as hard as the clause it replaces, per the 2026-10-22 18:33 rule).** Three new presuppositions, and the first is adverse: (R1) that byte-level modelling does not itself manufacture the floor. It does, in at least one published regime — Rajaraman et al. (arXiv:2404.08335, read in full, below) show character-level transformers on an order-2 Markov source stalling at the best i.i.d. loss rather than converging to the exactly known entropy rate, and recovering near-optimality only once BPE is applied. So Version B's declared rule puts the candidate squarely inside the one regime where a positive gap above a known H is already demonstrated, which makes the repair cut against novelty rather than for it. (R2) That an 8-state HMM's entropy rate is computable to a tolerance far finer than 0.01 nats/symbol — true only up to a numerical integral over the mixed-state simplex, not in closed form. (R3) That E is identifiable from a bounded ladder; Henighan et al. state in their own footnote that they "have not encountered a clear irreducible loss in the range of model sizes that we have explored" (§3.4, fn 9, read at full text), which is a published statement that the asymptote is not visible in an accessible range.

## Alternative formulations

1. Is the "irreducible loss" term of a neural scaling law the entropy of the data, as the standard reading of the parametric form assumes, or the sum of the data's entropy and an architecture-specific approximation floor?
2. What is the asymptotic excess loss Delta = lim(achieved loss) − H of a transformer on a process of exactly computable entropy rate, and how does Delta differ across architectural families at matched compute?

**Fresh-reader note on alternative 1.** Judged on its own words, alternative 1 fails the object test: its blank is "the correct interpretation of a term in a published parametric fit", which is an artefact of research, not a computational object. The canonical formulation (and alternative 2) is what carries the entry; alternative 1 should not be the headline wording.

## Presuppositions

1. A process family exists whose per-token entropy rate is exactly computable UNDER THE TOKENIZATION the model uses. **CONTESTED, and now partly resolved against the candidate:** Version B declares byte-level/bijective modelling, which fixes the bookkeeping — but arXiv:2404.08335 shows that for Markov sources the character-level regime is exactly where transformers fail to reach H, so the declared rule does not isolate an *architecture* floor from a *representation* floor. Any answer in the "architecture floor" direction under Version B is confounded with the tokenization mechanism already published.
2. The achieved loss over the declared compute ladder extrapolates to its asymptote well enough for Delta to be bounded away from zero. **CONTESTED, and now adversely sourced:** Henighan et al. §3.4 fn 9 (read): "We have not encountered a clear irreducible loss in the range of model sizes that we have explored." Version B's pre-declared estimator and equivalence band are the mitigation, not a resolution.
3. The process is learnable in principle by the architecture, so a non-zero Delta is an approximation floor rather than a mis-specified task. **Flagged contested:** Rajaraman et al.'s order-2 switching source is representable by a transformer in principle yet is not learned at character level, so presupposition 3 and the architecture-floor reading come apart — a non-zero Delta can be an *optimisation* floor, which the candidate's Delta does not separate from an approximation floor.
4. Fresh samples can be drawn without limit, so the data-limited term can be driven arbitrarily small. **Established** by construction for HMMs and PCFGs.
5. Entropy rate per token and cross-entropy per token are on the same footing (same base, same conditioning). **Established** by Version B's declared rule; left implicit in Version A.
6. (Added by Gate 1.) The pre-declared sets are enumerated, not gestured at: process families and architecture families are both listed by membership in Version B, and the entropy-rate computation rule is named for each. *Which of the two the entry did: enumerated the members.*
7. **Measurand exemption DECLARED, not merely considered.** The clause "a strictly positive floor would be indistinguishable from data entropy in every published fit" is the very failure the question exists to report; it is the measurand, not a false premise, and is not graded as presupposition failure. (It is, separately, factually too strong — see priors — and that is a Gate 2 novelty finding, not a presupposition kill.)

## What would count as an answer

For at least two process families with analytically computed H and at least two architecture families, a ladder of newly trained models with achieved cross-entropy on fresh samples; an asymptote estimated under the pre-declared estimator with confidence intervals; and Delta = asymptote − H reported with its interval, in nats per symbol. Delta robustly above the equivalence band and differing across architectures answers in the architectural-floor direction; Delta inside the band vindicates the identification. A null is a pre-declared admissible answer.

## Significance — consequence

If Delta is robustly above zero and architecture-dependent, every published irreducible-loss estimate is an upper bound contaminated by the architecture that produced it, and the practice of reading L∞ as "the entropy of text" is an over-claim; compute-optimal extrapolations anchored on L∞ would be mis-anchored, and Delta becomes a new axis on which to compare architectures that compute-matched loss comparisons cannot see. If Delta is inside the band, the identification is vindicated on exactly specified data and the standard reading earns a ground-truth licence it has never had.

## Significance — audience

Scaling-law practitioners who fit E in L(N,D) and quote it as data entropy; architecture researchers who currently compare families only by achieved loss at matched compute; the information-theoretic ML and computational-mechanics communities that already build exact-H processes (P2, Shai et al.) but at scales where asymptote fitting is meaningless. They would act by reporting Delta alongside fitted E, and by treating a cross-family loss difference at matched compute as decomposable into reducible and floor components.

## Significance — fecundity

Delta as a measurable architecture invariant; Delta-matched rather than compute-matched architecture comparison; whether Delta scales with a process's statistical complexity (Crutchfield-Young C_mu) rather than its entropy rate; whether optimisation floors (P2's unigram attractor) and approximation floors are separable by construction; and whether a positive Delta on exact-H processes predicts anything about the unknown H of natural text.

## Significance — anchoring

The Kaplan/Hoffmann exponent discrepancy and its resolution into optimiser and warmup effects; the long-standing "entropy of English" estimates (Shannon 1951, Brown et al. 1992, Teahan 1996) that scaling-law L∞ values are informally compared against; the belief-state-geometry line (arXiv:2405.15943, unopened) which already needs the optimal loss as a reference; and the tokenization-theory result P2 proves.

## Reason for silence

A disciplinary boundary plus an instrument gap, argued rather than asserted: the identification is stated by its own source as an idealising assumption with "caution is warranted" attached, so inside scaling-law practice it reads as a known approximation rather than an open empirical claim and attracts no experiment. Meanwhile the groups that possess exact-H processes — tokenization theory, computational mechanics, interpretability-with-ground-truth — work three to five orders of magnitude below the scales at which asymptote fitting is informative, and report the gap qualitatively (P2's dashed-line figure, P1's "considerable gap") because at their scale a fitted E is unidentifiable. No single group has had both the exact-H process and the compute ladder. **This mechanism is credible, and it also explains why the question is only partly un-asked** — which is itself a reason to doubt a High novelty finding here, exactly as Gate 4's attack 7 would require.

## Scores

**Well-posedness: 2/3.** Version B enumerates its sets, declares the estimator, the band and the units, and is settleable in principle. One point withheld because the terminal estimand's identifiability is contested by a published statement from the very paper that defines the term (P3 §3.4 fn 9), and because Version B's declared byte-level rule does not separate an architecture floor from the representational floor P2 already demonstrates — so a positive Delta would be ambiguous between presuppositions 1 and 3.

**Significance: 3/3.** Consequence, audience and fecundity are all concrete and the anchoring is to live problems; the audience already quotes the quantity the answer would correct.

## Strongest remaining objection

That the question is the experiment its own source's hedges call for, and that two groups have already taken it far enough to report a positive gap on both named process families — so what remains un-asked is not the question but one estimator (a pre-declared asymptote with an interval), which is a methodological refinement of P1 rather than a new question about the world.
