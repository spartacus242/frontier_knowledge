## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take the engineer's notion of metal fatigue — a part survives one big load but dies after enough
small ones — and ask whether a bacterium's wall behaves the same way. Concretely: trap single
*E. coli*, repeatedly dilute the medium so the cell is osmotically inflated by a fixed step that one
such step would not burst, wait a fixed interval, repeat. Three questions. (a) Does the chance of
bursting on the n-th inflation depend on n itself, once you allow for how hard the cell has ever been
hit? (b) Plot the number of inflations a typical cell survives against the size of the step on log-log
axes: is it a straight line (a Basquin / Wöhler stress-life law), and is there a step size so small
that cells never burst however often you repeat it (an endurance limit)? (c) If you give a cell some
inflations at one size and some at another, do the fractions-of-life used add to 1 at death
(Palmgren-Miner)? All three in growing cells and in cells whose wall synthesis is blocked, the second
arm being there to ask whether repair erases damage as fast as cycling makes it.

In one line: **is bacterial lysis a strength problem (set by the biggest hit) or a damage-accumulation
problem (set by how many hits), and does the engineer's bookkeeping for the second case transfer to a
structure that rebuilds itself while you load it?**

## 4. My own derivations, labelled as mine

**D1 (exact identity — the dissolution result).** Let per-cycle failure probability be q_i within
block i, independent across cycles (the no-damage, memoryless null). Define N_i as the candidate
itself defines it — *median* cycles-to-lysis at that amplitude — so N_i = ln(0.5)/ln(1−q_i). At the
median failure point of any multi-block sequence, Σ n_i·ln(1−q_i) = ln 0.5. Divide through by ln 0.5:
**Σ n_i/N_i = 1, exactly, for any amplitudes in any order, with no damage state whatsoever.** This is
algebra, not an approximation, and it is Palmgren-Miner's prediction verbatim — including the
order-independence the record names as Miner's signature.

**D2.** If q(ΔΠ) = c·ΔΠ^k then N_median ≈ ln2/q = (ln2/c)·ΔΠ^(−k), i.e. **log N = a − b log ΔΠ**: the
Basquin stress-life form is the *generic* prediction of the no-damage null whenever per-cycle hazard
is a power law in amplitude.

**D3.** Converting S-D's printed point estimate: at q = 0.344 per cycle, median cycles-to-failure =
ln(0.5)/ln(0.656) = **1.64 cycles**. My arithmetic on their point estimate; they print no CI on 34.4 %,
so I carry none, and this is a conversion of their number, not a result of theirs.

**D4 (monotone-step argument).** The candidate's control variable is a *step* in external osmolarity.
Repeated steps of fixed size in one direction, from a bounded osmolarity range, require intervening
steps of equal size in the other direction. Instantiated in the field's own instrument by S-A's
protocol: "switching between LB with 500 mM NaCl and LB with no NaCl".

**D5.** The record's Gate 1 arithmetic caps the attainable budget at ~10¹–10² cycles. The record's own
P1 quotation puts first irreversibility at "about 450 fatigue cycles". 450 > 100. My arithmetic;
and per the record's own caveat the amplitude that 450 belongs to is not carried by that sentence and
the system differs, so this **bounds expectations** and proves nothing about *E. coli*.
