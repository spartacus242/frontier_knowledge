## 1. Restatement in my own words (not the generator's)

Take whatever rules a DNA-synthesis vendor uses to decide that a stretch of sequence is too hard to make (or too expensive), write those rules down as a scored checklist with a date and a platform label, slide it along a reference genome in fixed windows, and ask: how much of the genome fails the checklist; is the failing part disproportionately the part that carries annotation people care about (coding exons, promoters, CpG islands, structured-RNA genes, repeats, enhancers) compared with sequence that merely has the same base composition; and do published "genome-wide" library screens that ordered their DNA new actually lose the regions the checklist flags, as much as their own QC says they lost?

Compressed: **is "genome-wide" in the synthesis-based assay literature a biology claim or a procurement claim, and can the procurement boundary be drawn on a genome map and scored against annotation?**

## 5. Recommended verdict

**Rejected** — attack 4 Unresolved: the pre-specified annotation set is half-composed of classes defined by the same sequence features the synthesis classifier scores, so the pre-committed band can be reached tautologically and is set by the null-matching choice rather than by evidence; P4 is asserted as ESTABLISHED on reasoning that is false for CpG islands, low-complexity sequence and repeat families, and the record offers no response. Promotion would require a V2 that (a) fires its bands only on annotation defined independently of the classifier's feature set (protein-coding exons, enhancer catalogues) and reports the composition-defined classes as descriptive only, and (b) establishes that a reject-or-reprice decision surface exists for array-synthesised pools rather than only for clonal gene synthesis. Disguise is Partial, not passed: Sc2.0 redesign rules and the DNA Scanner publication were located and left unopened.
