## Question (canonical)

**V2 (reformulated at Gate 1 — this is the canonical form):** Over a pre-declared frame of (site, issued value, issue date) triples drawn from dated standards and published quantile maps — 50-year wind speeds, 100-year flood discharges, 100-year significant wave heights — restricted to issued values that carry an explicit nominal annual exceedance probability *p*, what is the pooled realized exceedance rate in the post-issue site-years, estimated under a declared spatial-dependence model and reported as a ratio to nominal *p*; and under a pre-declared decomposition, how much of any departure is attributable to (a) issue-era estimator bias, measured by re-running the issue-era estimator against a modern estimator on the identical issue-era record, (b) parameter and record-length uncertainty at issue, and (c) post-issue trend?

**V1 (as intake, recorded per Gate 1 rule):** For design values issued in dated standards and published quantile maps — 50-year wind speeds, 100-year flood discharges, 100-year significant wave heights — what is the realized rate of exceedance at the issued sites in the years since publication, and under a pre-declared decomposition how much of any departure from the nominal rate is estimator bias at the time of issue rather than subsequent nonstationarity?

The reformulation is for well-posedness only. It changes three things and nothing else: it restricts the frame to issued values admitting a frequency reading (which converts the contested "nominal rate is a frequency" presupposition into a reported classification count on the way in, rather than a premise); it fixes the estimand on the *rate* scale as a ratio to nominal with a declared dependence model, rather than leaving "realized rate" to be read off site by site; and it gives limb (a) an operational definition (issue-era estimator vs modern estimator on the same issue-era record) so the decomposition is checkable rather than rhetorical.

## Alternative formulations

- Have the 100-year values we published actually been exceeded about once a century?
- Treating each issued design value as a probabilistic forecast carrying a stated exceedance rate, what are its out-of-sample verification statistics, and does the miscalibration sit in the estimator or in the climate?

## Motivating gap

The silence is structural rather than anecdotal. Extreme-value practice is dense with how-answers — estimator choice, regionalisation, record extension, nonstationary GEV, climate-adjusted quantiles — and standards are revised by re-estimating on longer records. The issued product itself, a dated number for a named site with an explicit nominal exceedance rate, is never scored against what subsequently happened, although meteorology verifies ordinary forecasts as a matter of routine and decades of out-of-sample data now exist for maps issued in the 1980s and 1990s. The nonstationarity literature argues about trend with no calibration baseline for the issued values themselves, which is precisely what this question would supply.

Gate 2 added two concrete pieces of evidence for the gap being real rather than assumed. First, the detection infrastructure exists and its output is discarded: an automated monitor at Colorado State flags, daily, grid points exceeding the 100-year 24-hour recurrence threshold, and publishes no counts, no expected counts and no realized rate — exceedances are produced as news, not accumulated as a verification score. Second, the field's own count-based machinery is applied *in-sample only*: Kjeldsen & Prosdocimi (2023) test the distribution of annual exceedance counts across 842 UK gauges and find them overdispersed relative to Poisson, but against thresholds chosen to give 1 or 3 exceedances per year in the *same* record used to fit — never against an issued design value out of sample. The habit of checking counts is present; it is never pointed at an issued number.

## Presuppositions

1. **Issued values are retrievable at site level with their issue dates.** Plausible for superseded ASCE 7 wind maps, published flood-frequency reports and national hydrological yearbooks. The digitisation effort is real and may bound the frame. *Not contested, but it bounds scope.*
2. **Enough sites have continuous records since issue to give power.** CONTESTED and binding: a 100-year value with 40 subsequent years gives an expected 0.4 exceedances per site, so the estimand exists only pooled over many sites, which forces a declared spatial-dependence model because one storm exceeds many sites at once. *Flagged; V2 makes the dependence model part of the question rather than a nuisance.* Gate 2 found independent support that the dependence is non-negligible and in a known direction: the negative-binomial/geometric overdispersion of annual exceedance counts reported by Kjeldsen & Prosdocimi (2023), strongest in groundwater-dominated catchments, is exactly the effect that would make a naive binomial interval too narrow.
3. **The measurement system did not change in a way that moves the exceedance count** — gauge relocation, rating-curve revision, anemometer height and averaging-time changes. CONTESTED: these must enter as covariates to control, not as noise; the rating-curve limb is itself a known confounder in the flood case, and the 3-s-gust/fastest-mile averaging-time change is the matching confounder in the wind case.
4. **The nominal rate is interpretable as a frequency.** CONTESTED: some standards publish values that are explicitly not quantiles but design values carrying embedded conservatism and spatial smoothing. V2 handles this by restriction — classifying which issued values admit a frequency reading is an input to the frame and a reported number, not a premise.
5. **Estimator bias at issue and nonstationarity since issue are separable with the available information.** CONTESTED, and the point the question stands or falls on. Separation requires re-running the issue-era estimator on the issue-era record (which recovers (a) directly, and is the part V2 makes operational) plus either sites where trend is independently estimable or a declared instrumental structure. Limb (c) is identified only under that declared structure; this is named as the strongest remaining objection rather than resolved.
6. **The sites' records are of sufficient quality in the exceedance tail specifically** — where gauges fail, are destroyed or saturate. CONTESTED: this is a selection mechanism that removes exactly the observations the estimand needs, and it biases the realized count downward, i.e. toward a spurious clean null.

## What would count as an answer

A pooled verification over a pre-declared frame of (site, issued value, issue date) triples that reports the realized exceedance rate with a spatial-dependence-aware interval against the nominal rate, together with the pre-declared three-way decomposition: (a) estimator bias, recovered by re-running the issue-era estimator on the issue-era record and comparing with a modern estimator on the same record; (b) parameter and record-length uncertainty at issue; (c) trend since issue.

A clean null answers it: realized rates inside the nominal interval with nothing attributable to (a) would validate the practice of revising by re-estimation and would remove a standing worry about the issued values' calibration. A finding in either direction tells map-issuing bodies, insurers and the adaptation literature whether the gap between nominal and realized exceedance is a statistics problem or a climate problem. Because the clean null is informative, the question cannot be answered the same way whatever the evidence shows.

## Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, with sources)

**Prior 1 — Simiu, E., Wilcox, R., Sadek, F., Filliben, J.J. (2003), "Wind Speeds in ASCE 7 Standard Peak-Gust Map: Assessment," *Journal of Structural Engineering* 129(4):427.** https://www.nist.gov/publications/wind-speeds-asce-7-standard-peak-gust-map-assessment *(partial read — NIST landing page only)*
- Unit of analysis: the "superstations" and their component stations underlying the ASCE 7 peak-gust map.
- Endpoint: the map's issued design wind speed values (direction of error in either sense).
- Reference/comparator: the authors' own account of the differentiated extreme wind climate, built from the same historical station records; the page does not state the comparator formally.
- Estimand: bias of the map's values relative to a re-estimation of the extreme wind climate from those same records, attributed to four construction faults (superstation pooling, mixing stations of unlike geography, omitted data, geography-blind smoothing).

**Prior 2 — Svoboda, V., Barešová, L. (2026), "Design flood estimation and its uncertainty under current climate in the Czech Republic," EGU General Assembly 2026, abstract EGU26-18045.** https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-18045.html
- Unit of analysis: Czech gauging stations (annual peak discharge series), with emphasis on the Jeseník region.
- Endpoint: design flood quantile *magnitude* at stated return periods — the 100-year flood, and the 1–10-year floods.
- Reference/comparator: the existing, already-issued Czech design flood estimates.
- Estimand: the design flood quantile under current climate, with its uncertainty. Reports that Q100 may be underestimated by 10–20% at some stations while the 1–10-year values may be overestimated, motivated by two extreme floods in the Jeseník region within ~30 years (the 2024 event rated at ~500-year return period). Contains no exceedance-rate statistic and performs no attribution of the mismatch to estimator bias or to nonstationarity.

**Prior 3 — Kjeldsen, T.R., Prosdocimi, I. (2023), "Use of peak over threshold data for flood frequency estimation: An application at the UK national scale," *Journal of Hydrology* 626:130235.** https://iris.unive.it/retrieve/740b91b1-1d9b-4e63-a8a8-4c4ab4452d86/KjeldsenProsdocimi2023JoH.pdf *(full text read)*
- Unit of analysis: UK gauged catchments — 939 (AMS), 842 (POT1), 767 (POT3).
- Endpoint: 2- and 100-year design flood magnitudes with bootstrap standard deviations; and, separately, the distribution of the *annual count* of threshold exceedances.
- Reference/comparator: AMS-based estimates (GUM/GEV/GLO/Kappa) on the same catchments and the same records.
- Estimand: the T-year quantile under each series type plus its sampling variability; chi-squared goodness-of-fit for exceedance counts, finding negative binomial and geometric to fit better than Poisson, most markedly in groundwater-dominated South-East catchments. ## Novelty — how this differs from each

**vs Prior 1 (Simiu et al. 2003).** Same object — issued ASCE 7 map values — and the sharpest published critique of them, but the estimand is a contemporaneous estimator-vs-estimator bias measured against the *same* records the map was built from. There is no issue-date structure, no post-issue observation, no rate-scale statistic and no decomposition. In the candidate's terms this is an attempt at limb (a) alone, conducted without the realized-exceedance quantity that gives (a) something to explain, and without (b) or (c). The candidate's distinctive move is that (a) is recovered *in order to be subtracted from* a measured realized rate; Simiu et al. compute a quantile-scale discrepancy that no observation ever adjudicates. Caveat: this is a partial read of the landing page, so the difference is asserted on the abstract's own framing, not on the full method.

**vs Prior 2 (Svoboda & Barešová 2026).** This is the closest item found and the reason the grade is Medium rather than High. It shares the motivation almost exactly — a national institute asking whether its issued Q100 is too low in light of what has since happened. It differs in the two things the candidate is about. (i) Scale: it reports a *magnitude* discrepancy (10–20%) obtained by re-estimating on a longer record, not a *frequency* statistic; a 10–20% quantile shortfall and a realized exceedance rate are different estimands, and the mapping between them depends on the tail shape the analysis is itself trying to establish. (ii) Attribution: it does not separate estimator bias from trend — it is the revise-by-re-estimation practice the candidate proposes to audit, performed on one country's stations, and its re-estimate *includes* the post-issue events, so the comparison is not out-of-sample in the candidate's sense. The candidate would treat this abstract's own numbers as one datum inside a pooled, issue-dated frame and would ask what Svoboda & Barešová cannot ask: of that 10–20%, how much would an issue-era estimator have got wrong on the issue-era record alone?

**vs Prior 3 (Kjeldsen & Prosdocimi 2023).** Nearest in *machinery* — it is the field's own count-of-exceedances analysis at national scale, and it even establishes the dependence structure the candidate needs (overdispersion relative to Poisson). It differs in the two features that make the candidate a verification rather than a fit: the threshold is chosen to deliver 1 or 3 exceedances per year *in the record being fitted*, not read off a published design value; and the counts are in-sample, from the same data as the fit, so the analysis can diagnose distributional mis-specification but can say nothing about whether an issued number was calibrated. It is the clearest demonstration available that the field counts exceedances routinely and has never pointed the count at a dated issued value out of sample.

**Negative stated in the method-owning field's vocabulary (per the Gate 2 rule).** The method here is not hydrology's; it is coverage-test backtesting of an issued interval or quantile — Kupiec unconditional coverage, Christoffersen conditional coverage, violation ratios — which is owned by financial risk management, with probabilistic forecast verification (reliability diagrams, scoring rules at extreme quantiles) as the second owner. Query 11 searched that vocabulary explicitly and query 7 searched the verification vocabulary; both returned mature method literature with no application to issued environmental design values. The negative is therefore recorded in the method field's own words, not only in hydrology's.

## Significance — consequence

**If the realized rate is materially above nominal and limb (a) carries most of it:** the field has an estimator problem, not only a climate problem. Revising maps by re-estimating on longer records with the same estimator family would be shown to propagate the defect, and the correct response is a margin or a changed estimator at issue, not merely more data. This directly cuts against the default reading of every exceedance as evidence of nonstationarity.

**If the departure sits in (c):** the adaptation and attribution literature gains the thing it currently lacks — a calibration baseline measured on the issued values themselves rather than on refitted curves — and the case for climate-adjusted quantiles is made on the issued product rather than on model output.

**If the realized rates sit inside the nominal interval with nothing attributable to (a) (the clean null):** the practice of revising by re-estimation is validated, a standing worry is removed, and the "1-in-100 events every few years" discourse is shown to be a perception effect of spatial pooling and nominal-rate misreading rather than a calibration failure. This is an informative null, which is what makes the question non-vacuous under attack 5.

**If the frame itself collapses** — too few issued values admit a frequency reading, or the tail-selection mechanism in presupposition 6 removes the needed observations — that is itself a reportable finding about what issued design values can be held to.

## Significance — audience

Named bodies that would act, each with a specific action: the ASCE 7 wind load subcommittee (whether to carry a calibration margin rather than only re-estimate; the Simiu critique has stood since 2003 without an observational adjudication); USGS and the Bulletin 17C/17B community and FEMA's flood-mapping programme (whether the 1%-chance values issued in Flood Insurance Studies of the 1980s–90s were calibrated, which bears on both map revision priority and the defensibility of superseded maps); the UK Environment Agency and FEH custodians (Kjeldsen and Prosdocimi are inside this community and already run the count machinery in-sample); the Czech Hydrometeorological Institute, which by Prior 2 is asking a neighbouring question right now and would be a first adopter; NOAA HDSC for Atlas 14/15; reinsurers and cat-model validation functions, who run coverage backtests on financial exposures and would recognise the statistic immediately; and the IPCC-adjacent adaptation literature, for which "statistics problem or climate problem" is load-bearing.

## Significance — fecundity

Answering it makes askable: which estimator families were systematically mis-calibrated at issue, and by vintage — a question with a direct bearing on how long a revision cycle should be; whether issued design values should carry a declared calibration margin, and how large; whether the realized rate differs by variable (wind vs discharge vs wave height), which would separate a statistical cause from a physical one; what the spatial-dependence-corrected effective sample size of a national design-value map actually is, hence how much verification power any country can ever have; and whether the same as-issued scoring should be applied to the other dated numbers engineering issues under a stated exceedance probability — seismic hazard maps being the obvious next frame, where the same structure (dated issued map, nominal rate, decades of subsequent observation) holds.

## Significance — anchoring

Touches the stationarity dispute directly ("stationarity is dead" vs "stationarity is undead: uncertainty dominates the distribution of extremes") by supplying the calibration baseline both sides argue without; the post-Harvey public anomaly of successive "500-year" events, for which the NWS HDSC AEP report is a single-event answer and no pooled answer exists; the long-standing Simiu et al. critique of the ASCE 7 map, unadjudicated by observation for twenty-three years; and the overdispersion finding of Prior 3, which is an unexploited ingredient of exactly the dependence-aware interval the candidate requires.

Significance score: **3/3** — consequence argued in all four branches including an informative null, audience named to specific committees with specific actions, fecundity concrete, anchoring to two live disputes.

## Reason for silence

Three mechanisms, compounding, none of them "no one thought of it":

1. **A disciplinary boundary that runs exactly through the statistic.** The method is owned by forecast verification and financial risk management (coverage tests, reliability diagrams); the data are owned by hydrology and wind engineering; the artefact being scored is owned by standards committees. Query 11 found the method literature and the data literature in the same search returning disjoint results — the transplant has not been made. Statisticians own estimators and are rewarded for new ones; committees own issuance and are not research bodies.

2. **Nobody owns the superseded issue, and the incentive runs the wrong way.** A committee that has already revised a map has nothing to gain from scoring the version it replaced, and a plausible amount to lose — the superseded values underwrite built infrastructure and, in the FEMA case, litigated flood-zone determinations. The retired document is institutionally dead, which is also why its digitisation is nobody's budget line.

3. **The statistic has no single-site owner, because it has no single-site existence.** With an expected 0.4 exceedances per site over 40 post-issue years, no individual gauge, basin, or national study of one region can produce the quantity; it exists only pooled across many sites and many issue vintages. Every institution's natural unit of work is therefore too small to see it, and the pooled version requires a declared spatial-dependence model that is itself a research problem. This is why the field's output takes the two forms Gate 2 actually found: the per-event AEP report (USGS, NWS HDSC — right scale, wrong aggregation) and the in-sample count diagnostic (Kjeldsen & Prosdocimi — right aggregation, wrong sample). The monitor at Colorado State completes the picture: exceedances are detected daily, published as events, and never accumulated.

## Strongest remaining objection (for Gate 4's benefit, not a verdict)

Presupposition 5. If re-running the issue-era estimator on the issue-era record is not actually possible at scale — because issue-era reports do not document the estimator, the record as it stood at issue, or the regionalisation pooling group well enough to reproduce — then limb (a) is not recoverable, the three-way decomposition degenerates into realized-rate-versus-everything, and the question reduces to the much less interesting "have design values been exceeded more often than nominal," which Prior 2 is already approaching country by country. The second objection, in order: presupposition 6's tail-selection mechanism biases the realized count downward and so biases the study *toward* the clean null, which is the one outcome that would be reported as reassurance.
