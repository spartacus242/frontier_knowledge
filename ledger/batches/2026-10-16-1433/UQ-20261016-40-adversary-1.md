# UQ-20261016-40 — Adversary #1 log

## Restatement (my own words)
Take a fixed list of EMPIAR deposits where both the raw particle picks (or an uncleaned stack) and the final kept particle list are public. (a) In what fraction of them does a map built only from the thrown-away particles pass a pre-declared "this is a real, different state" test — a resolution floor, a map-to-model fit floor, and a deviation from the deposited map larger than random same-sized subsets of the kept particles produce? (b) If several people process the same micrographs independently and blind, how much do their reject sets overlap (Jaccard), and does any of their maps show a feature the deposited map lacks? Underneath: is the keep/discard split a fact about the specimen or a fact about whoever processed it?

## Recommended verdict
No attack is Unresolved; attacks 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 are Partial and novelty does not hold (Gate 2 Low stands: the existence limb is demonstrated by P2 and the complement/random-subset mechanics by P1; PMC10711021 confirmed not a prior) -> **Provisional at best**, and only for a re-intaken (b)+(c) version with a within-curator replicate control and a repaired distinct-state criterion; the intake version as written should be routed as already-asked (P1/P2).

Single strongest killing point: limb (b)'s "data or curator" dichotomy omits algorithmic stochasticity, so the proposed Jaccard statistic cannot attribute disagreement to curators without a within-curator replicate baseline the design does not include; combined with a distinct-state criterion that penalises exactly the compositional states (P2-type) it is meant to detect.
