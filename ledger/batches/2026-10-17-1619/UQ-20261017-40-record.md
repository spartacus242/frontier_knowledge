Domain(s): Medicine — diagnostic safety; measurement of diagnostic error; chart-review methodology. Method-owning neighbours: diagnostic test accuracy methodology (review/incorporation bias, QUADAS-2), psychometrics (criterion contamination), patient-safety record-review methodology (hindsight bias), clinical informatics (copy-forward / note propagation).

Question (canonical — Gate 1 reformulation, the version carried forward):
On one case set reviewed twice under a pre-registered protocol, what is the signed difference in the classified diagnostic-error rate between (A) standard unblinded chart review with the full record available, and (B) review in which the index encounter's own diagnostic assertions and every downstream repetition traceable to them are redacted, while all subsequently generated findings, test results and diagnoses not traceable to the index assertion are retained — reported with its uncertainty, a measured index of blinding success, and the same contrast computed on a subset whose diagnosis is anchored to an index-independent reference (autopsy, mandated external re-adjudication, or a registry with independent ascertainment) to fix the bias direction?

Question (Gate 0 intake version, recorded as required):
In the chart-review methods used to measure diagnostic error, how much of the measured error rate is determined by the reference diagnosis being downstream of — and often inherited from — the index clinician's own label, and what is the measured rate when the reference diagnosis is established blinded to the index encounter's diagnostic assertions?

Reformulation rationale (one reformulation used, well-posedness only, no scope change): the intake phrase "how much of the measured error rate is determined by" names no estimand and no comparator; "blinded to the index encounter's diagnostic assertions" does not say what happens to the downstream inheritances of those assertions, which is where the whole difficulty sits. The reformulation fixes (i) the estimand as a signed paired difference in classified error rate on one case set, (ii) the redaction target as index assertions plus traceable repetitions, with non-traceable downstream evidence retained, (iii) blinding success as a measured rather than asserted quantity, and (iv) the anchored subset as the device that converts an unsigned difference into a direction. Scope, population and field are unchanged.

Alternative formulations:
- (lay) Does diagnostic momentum hide diagnostic error from the very instruments built to count it?
- (counterfactual) If the same cases were re-adjudicated with the initial diagnosis redacted, how far would the published diagnostic-error rate move, and in which direction?
- (method field's words) Is the reference standard of diagnostic-error chart review conditionally dependent on the index assertion it is used to judge, and what is the magnitude of the resulting review/incorporation bias in the estimated error rate?

Motivating gap: The field is dense with how-to-measure instruments — Safer Dx and the Revised Safer Dx, e-triggers, the Global Trigger Tool, SPADE look-back/look-forward, care-transition and missed-opportunity reviews — and thin on why-questions about whether the measurement is identified. All of them take later recorded diagnoses as truth. Diagnostic momentum and the inherited diagnosis are named inside the same field and the same journal (Blume, Diagnosis 2026, below), which implies the later recorded diagnosis is partly caused by the earlier one. The expected direction is undercounting: a wrong label that propagates is never flagged, because the record agrees with itself. The Safer Dx validation paper (opened, below) names hindsight bias in its limitations and does not estimate it, and its reviewers were blinded to the error outcome while being given up to a year of subsequent EHR documentation — i.e. blinded to the wrong thing for this purpose. No validation paper found reports a redaction arm.

Presuppositions:
- P1. Diagnostic labels propagate through the record, so the later diagnosis is not independent of the index one. ESTABLISHED as a named phenomenon in the candidate's own literature — Blume, "The inherited diagnosis and diagnostic error," Diagnosis 2026 (abstract opened) names inherited diagnoses and diagnostic momentum as a risk factor; magnitude unmeasured and is the thing at issue.
- P2. Chart-review instruments use subsequent documentation as their reference standard. ESTABLISHED from the instruments' own definitions and confirmed in the opened Safer Dx validation paper ("evaluated EHR data up to 1 year after the index visit").
- P3. A redaction protocol can remove index-encounter diagnostic assertions and their downstream inheritance while leaving the presenting features reviewers need. MOST CONTESTED, and sharper than the intake stated: deciding which downstream text is "inherited" requires knowing what was independently ascertained, which is the quantity at issue, so the redaction rule is partly circular. If the later diagnosis IS the inheritance, redacting it removes the reference entirely and arm B has nothing to adjudicate against. FIXED only partly, by pre-registering inheritance-tagging rules, reporting inter-annotator agreement on the tagging itself, and reporting arm B both with and without borderline-traceable text.
- P4. Some case subset has a reference standard independent of the record — autopsy, mandated external re-adjudication, a registry with independent ascertainment — to fix the direction of the bias. PARTLY ESTABLISHED; autopsy-discrepancy literature exists (Q5) but such subsets are small and selected, so transportability is limited and the direction is fixed only locally.
- P5. Reviewers can be kept blind in practice. CONTESTED; writing style, test ordering and referral patterns leak the index impression, so blinding success must be measured (reviewer guess of the index label, with agreement statistics), not asserted.
- P6 (added here). The classified error rate is the quantity consumers of this literature actually use. PLAUSIBLE: downstream figures (national burden estimates, institutional dashboards, instrument-validation accuracy) are built from per-record error classifications, so a bias in that classification propagates.

What would count as an answer: A paired re-adjudication experiment on one case set with two independent blinded review arms — full chart versus chart with index-encounter diagnostic assertions and their traceable repetitions redacted — reporting the difference in classified diagnostic-error rate with its uncertainty, a measured index of blinding success, inter-annotator agreement on the inheritance tagging, and the same contrast on an index-independent anchored subset that fixes the bias direction; plus a restatement of at least one published error rate as a bound with stated tightness. A null difference answers the question in the negative and licenses the existing rates as unbiased with respect to this channel. A large difference converts every published rate into a bound of newly estimated looseness. A difference that reverses sign between the anchored subset and the full set is a third outcome and would indicate the redaction removes diagnostic evidence rather than removing contamination — i.e. it falsifies P3 rather than answering the question.

Significance — consequence: If the difference is large, every chart-review-derived diagnostic-error rate becomes a bound of newly estimated looseness in a known direction, and the national-burden estimates and institutional dashboards built on those classifications inherit the correction. The standard defence — that reviewer noise attenuates rather than biases — fails, because contamination by the index assertion is signed, not symmetric. Remedies are then cheap and specifiable: a redaction arm as a required validation element, and a reported blinding domain in diagnostic-safety measurement reporting. If the difference is null, that is a positive result the field can use: it licenses the existing rates against this channel, retires a ritual limitations-section caveat with an estimate instead of a hedge, and redirects measurement-validity effort to reviewer reliability and case selection. If the sign reverses between the anchored subset and the full set, the finding is about P3 — redaction removes diagnostic evidence, not contamination — and that too is informative, because it would mean the field's instruments are not identifiable with available records at all and must be anchored externally.
Significance — audience: (i) The AHRQ diagnostic-safety measurement programme and the authors of its state-of-the-science work on operational measurement of diagnostic safety — their output IS measurement validity, so a signed bias estimate is directly consumable. (ii) The Safer Dx / Revised Safer Dx developers (Singh, Meyer, Giardina) — a redaction arm is an addition to an existing validation protocol on a case set they already hold, and their own limitations section already names the worry, so the barrier is an estimate, not a concept. (iii) SPADE and e-trigger developers (Newman-Toker and colleagues), whose look-back/look-forward designs take the later coded diagnosis as truth by construction and would need to state the direction of the resulting bias. (iv) Global Trigger Tool and care-transition / missed-opportunity reviewers, who could add the blinding-success measure at near-zero cost. (v) Journal editors and reviewers applying STARD- and QUADAS-style appraisal to diagnostic-safety studies, who currently have no signalling question for this instrument class. (vi) Anyone pricing malpractice or regulatory exposure off published error rates, for whom a bound replaces a point estimate.
Significance — fecundity: Is the contamination larger in specialties and settings where label propagation is heaviest (primary care, psychiatry, chronic-disease labels) than where each encounter re-derives its own assessment (emergency medicine, pathology)? Does ambient AI documentation, which regenerates the assessment from the transcript, increase or decrease inheritance — and does it therefore shrink or inflate measured error rates relative to the human-written era, making pre- and post-ambient rates incomparable? Can inheritance be tagged programmatically (index-assertion string and paraphrase overlap in later notes), turning the redaction arm from a manual exercise into a corpus-scale instrument? Is the reviewer's noise floor itself index-dependent, which would recast inter-rater-reliability results in this literature? Does the same contamination affect adverse-event and quality-of-care measurement generally, wherever the record is both the subject and the judge? And does the anchored-subset contrast give a transportable correction factor, or only a local one?
Significance — anchoring: (a) The unexplained spread in published diagnostic-error rates — roughly 5% in outpatient estimates, around 30% in the Safer Dx primary-care samples, and autopsy major-discrepancy rates in another range again — for which the candidate supplies a mechanism that predicts the ordering by how much downstream record the method consumes. (b) The autopsy-versus-chart-review gap, which this reframes from a difference in case mix into a difference in reference-standard independence. (c) The field's standing complaint that there is no gold standard for diagnostic error, which has been a reason to stop rather than a question to answer. (d) QUADAS-2's reference-standard-blinding domain, which has no instrument in this literature to attach to. (e) The inherited-diagnosis and diagnostic-momentum line (Blume 2026), which currently has clinical-warning status and no measurement consequence drawn.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanisms, strongest first):
1. The caveat absorbed the question. Hindsight bias is a ritual limitations-section line in this literature; naming a bias discharges the obligation to estimate it. Evidenced directly in the opened Safer Dx validation paper, which names hindsight bias as a possible confounder of its own error determinations and then does nothing with it. A quantity that is routinely acknowledged is rarely measured.
2. Disciplinary boundary between the field that owns the DATA and the field that owns the METHOD. Diagnostic safety builds instruments to count errors; diagnostic-test-accuracy methodology owns review bias, incorporation bias and reference-standard blinding, and psychometrics owns criterion contamination. The QUADAS machinery is applied to index tests, and a chart-review error instrument is not formally treated as a diagnostic test at all, so its reference standard is never appraised by the people who have the tools. Evidenced by query 9 (QUADAS material with no application here) and query 11 (the method term is live outside medicine and absent inside this problem).
3. Incentive asymmetry. The field's public purpose is to establish that diagnostic error is large and underappreciated. A study whose most likely publishable finding is "our rates are biased, direction uncertain" weakens the advocacy without replacing the number, and no funder owns that downside. The symmetric null is also unrewarding to publish.
4. Cost in the scarcest input. Double adjudication of one case set doubles physician-reviewer time — the binding constraint in every study in this literature — for a methodological rather than a substantive result, and the redaction step was not cheap until EHR text could be programmatically located and masked at scale.
(Not offered: "no one thought of it". The mechanism is named in the same journal and the bias family is named in the method field; what is missing is an owner with the incentive and the tooling.)
