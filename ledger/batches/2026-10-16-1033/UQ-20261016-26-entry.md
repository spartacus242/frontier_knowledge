Question (canonical): For a standardized airborne-eDNA sampling protocol, what is the locality specificity of a detection — the probability that a detected vertebrate taxon was actually present inside the pre-specified survey unit during the sampling window — and how does detection probability for an independently verified point source decay with distance from it?
(Gate 1 reformulation retained in the record: for a named protocol in a named landscape class, (i) the fitted P(detect | taxon at distance d, meteorology m) with half-distance and CI per taxon group, and (ii) conditional on that function and an enumerated off-unit source inventory, the proportion of a survey's taxon list attributable to off-unit sources, with CI.)

Alternative formulations:
- When an air sample yields a species' DNA, how far away could that animal have been?
- What is the spatial support of a single airborne-eDNA sample, as a calibrated detection-probability-versus-distance curve against verified animal locations, plus the share of the taxon list from off-unit sources?

Motivating gap: Airborne-eDNA metabarcoding recovers taxa absent from the sampled enclosure (Clare et al. 2022; Lynggaard et al. 2022), reportedly treated as "unexpected" or possible transport/contamination without quantifying rate or source distance, while eDNA is being standardized for regulatory biodiversity work (CEN/TC 230/WG 28; EN 17805:2023). Gate 2 finding, as corrected on the judge's post-Gate-5 verification (2026-10-16; independent fresh-context checker, 10 queries, 2 refused, log in batches/2026-10-16-1033/UQ-20261016-26-verification.md): the gap is refuted on its own decisive test, because Clare et al. 2022 — the motivating paper the gap says left distance unquantified — did run read count against distance from the enclosure as a named model (LR = 2.51, df = 1, p = 0.113) over 0-276 m with the 27 in-enclosure points excluded, confirmed to the decimal. The record's wider claim that BOTH limbs are addressed is struck: the distance limb is answered in a literature with numbers, while the off-unit-share limb is posed and NOT answered (see Novelty - how this differs and Routing).

Presuppositions: (1) per-taxon detection probability estimable — partly established for a single qPCR target (Kroos et al. 2026: three distance levels, coefficients only, no fitted curve), contested for metabarcoded assemblages; (2) survey-unit boundary pre-specifiable with in-unit presence verified — established at zoos/captive parks, contested in open landscape (Kroos could not exclude free-living wallabies at 1 km); (3) off-unit sources enumerable — CONTESTED and binding; published work substitutes a modelled wind catchment for an inventory; (4) detections not dominated by distance-independent lab/field contamination — contested (Westerduin et al. 2023, origin inconclusive); (5) decay modellable as a function of distance and meteorology — split by readout: significant negative distance effect for targeted qPCR (Kroos), no read-count/distance relationship for metabarcoding (Clare, LR = 2.51, p = 0.113); (6) regulatory use of airborne eDNA for presence/absence — contested, current regulated use is aquatic, no air-specific standard verified in draft; (7) a protocol-stable "sample" exists — contested, falsified by sampler dependence (active 64.4% vs passive 11.1% at 10 m).

What would count as an answer: a calibration experiment with independently verified source locations and samplers at pre-specified distances, yielding (i) a detection-probability-versus-distance curve per taxon group with half-distance and CI and (ii) the off-unit share of a taxon list with CI; a flat curve would be a decisive negative. Against this criterion, as corrected on verification: design (i) is executed only in part. Kroos et al. 2026 used a captive source and samplers at three distance classes (10/100/1000 m, plus an in-enclosure proof-of-concept arm that is not a distance class with a reported detection rate) over 108 analysed samples (88 distance + 20 proof-of-concept; 110 is the planned total), and fitted binomial GLMs and a Bayesian logistic model with distance as a predictor, reporting coefficients and observed frequencies. It does NOT fit a detection-probability-versus-distance function, so no curve, half-distance or per-taxon-group kernel is in hand; what is in hand is a signed three-level effect. Limb (ii) is NOT answered at all: Clare et al. 2022 label residency (17/25 detected mammal and bird species resident; the 32% non-resident figure is this ledger's own arithmetic, 8/25, not Clare et al.'s wording, and they hedge several of the eight as possible zoo stock or food), Stewart et al. 2026 report model-catchment agreement, and the UK national survey attributes exotics to contamination - none partitions a taxon list by whether a taxon's source lay inside or outside a defined survey unit. What is missing is therefore the whole of limb (ii) plus the curve, not only a half-distance transform.
