## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take one cell. Sequence every one of its mitochondrial genomes individually, each at close to its full 16.5 kb length, so that you know which mutations sit on the *same physical molecule*. For each molecule, count how many de-novo **somatic** point mutations it carries (haplogroup-fixed and cell-recurrent sites excluded by a stated rule). Now ask what shape that set of per-molecule counts has. Are the counts what you would get by scattering the cell's mutations independently over its genome copies — a Poisson whose mean is that same cell's per-base de-novo frequency multiplied by the called molecule length — or are they clumped, with a few molecules carrying several mutations and the great majority carrying none, which is what you would expect if some genome copies are used as templates over and over while most are hardly copied at all? The headline number is the within-cell variance/mean of those per-molecule counts, with a confidence interval, plus a fitted negative-binomial shape parameter, in two pre-named cell types (human skeletal-muscle fibre segments; cultured primary dermal fibroblasts).

### C1, tested — the headroom clause
S1 is the only published instrument of the required kind, and its **own** acknowledged per-molecule error contribution is printed: "**0.03 SNVs** in the Nanopore iMiGseq data" (Results), arising from "one error in ∼1600 unique 16 556-bp molecules in the UMI labeling step" (Methods). Against that, the nearest per-base de-novo somatic frequency I could open is S2's printed median for ~10-month mouse **skeletal muscle**, 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ nucleotide substitutions/bp.

**MY ARITHMETIC (labelled as mine; S2's medians are carried as point values because the served text gives no interval, and I say so rather than implying one):**
- λ_bio = 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ /bp × 16,569 bp = **0.020 de-novo mutations per full-length molecule.**
- So the instrument's **own acknowledged error term (0.03/molecule) exceeds the biological per-molecule expectation (0.020/molecule) by ≈1.5×**, before any Nanopore consensus error is counted.
- The **discriminating observable** is molecules carrying ≥2 mutations. Per 10³ molecules, Poisson pairing gives 10³·λ²/2 ≈ **0.20** molecules biologically, versus **≈0.45** from the labelling error term alone (10³·0.03²/2). The spurious doubles exceed the real ones by more than twofold.

**Direction/scope check on the quoted strings.** S1's "thus representing a miniscule fraction of the SNVs (0.03 SNVs…)" — the opening words scope it to *this one error type as a fraction of the paper's own SNV set*, and the clause **ASSERTS** that the term is small **relative to a haplogroup-dominated SNV total**. It is small relative to ~18 SNVs/molecule of mostly fixed variants; it is **not** small relative to 0.020 de-novo somatic mutations/molecule. The paper is not wrong and I am not quoting it against itself: I am applying its own number to a different numerator, and saying so.

**C1 grade: CONTESTED on sources I opened, pointing FALSE.** The record itself concedes the clause is "partially supported, not established" and "the single clause most likely to route this question rather than answer it"; what I add is that at the only per-base frequency I could open, the clause points the wrong way rather than merely being unestablished.

### C3, tested — the event-count clause, and the strongest leg
This one needs no instrument at all; it is a property of the object.

**MY ARITHMETIC on S2's printed median:** one cell holding 10³ mtDNA copies contains 10³ × 16,569 = **1.66 × 10⁷ bp** of mtDNA. At 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ de-novo substitutions/bp that is **≈20 de-novo somatic point mutations in the cell's entire mtDNA complement**, of which the informative subclass — two on one molecule — is expected at **0.2**.

The within-cell index of dispersion D = s²/x̄ over n = 10³ molecules is then, to first order, a function of the doubleton count alone (my arithmetic, n = 1000, 20 mutations total):
- 20 singletons, 0 doubletons → x̄ = 0.0200, s² = 0.01962, **D = 0.981**
- 18 singletons + 1 doubleton → x̄ = 0.0200, s² = 0.02162, **D = 1.081**
- 16 singletons + 2 doubletons → **D ≈ 1.16**

So "the index of dispersion within one cell, **with a confidence interval**" — the entry's own headline number — is a statistic on a Poisson(0.2) count. Its per-cell confidence interval spans the entire hypothesis space, and the statistic is near-discrete rather than continuous. **This is a bound set by copy number × genome length × mutation frequency, not by any sequencing technology**, so no instrument improvement removes it. The only repair is pooling across the ~50 cells, which converts the estimand into a cross-cell pooled quantity — precisely the denominator the candidate defines itself against in its Motivating gap.

**C3 grade: FALSE** on my arithmetic from two opened sources.
