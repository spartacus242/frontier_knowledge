Domain(s): Biology — single-cell genomics methodology, tissue-atlas composition. Adjacent: compositional data analysis; two-phase/destructive-sampling estimation.
Question (canonical): V1 (scored as canonical, retained per Gate 1): in droplet scRNA-seq of dissociated tissue, is the ambient-RNA ("soup") profile a quantitative record of the cell types destroyed during dissociation — does deconvolving it against a pre-declared reference recover the identity and relative mass of the lost populations well enough to correct that run's cell-type composition estimate? V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, well-posedness only): for a tissue block split across modalities, does the soup profile — deconvolved against a pre-declared signature matrix, converted from RNA mass to cell counts via independently measured per-type RNA content and per-type degradation — give a per-type missing-count estimate whose calibration slope on the (in-situ minus droplet) deficit is distinguishable from 0 and from 1, with interval?
Alternative formulations: Can the RNA in the empty droplets tell us which cells the dissociation killed, and in what proportion? / Treating ambient RNA as a destructive-sampling byproduct rather than contamination, how accurate is a soup-based estimator of composition loss against matched snRNA-seq or in-situ counts of the same block?
Motivating gap: Intake joined two routinely reported facts — every droplet run carries soup, and dissociation destroys cell types differentially — and asserted nobody had tested whether the soup matches the profiled cells. Gate 2 found that assertion false: Janssen et al. (Genome Biology 2023) deconvolve the empty-droplet profile against a cell-type reference, report that "the contamination profiles do not always reflect the cell type proportions of the sample", and interpret the shift as PT cells being "more vulnerable to dissociation procedure than other cell types". The gap as written does not exist.
Presuppositions: (1) soup comes predominantly from dissociation lysis — CONTESTED (nuclei-surface adhesion, erythrocyte leakage, pre-existing extracellular RNA); (2) soup indexes WHICH cells were lost, not how much RNA survivors carry — CONTESTED and contradicted in print (Caglayan: ambient profile "primarily from the most abundant cell types"); (3) lysate mRNA degrades in a modellable, type-specific way — CONTESTED nuisance; (4) an orthogonal reference (snRNA-seq, in situ) measures truth more faithfully — CONTESTED, must be pre-declared with its own error; (5) soup well mixed across a run — partly testable; (6) published proportions are load-bearing for atlas and DA claims — established; (7) enough archived datasets retain empty-droplet barcodes for a retrospective limb — CONTESTED and UNQUANTIFIED, no published denominator found.
What would count as an answer: pre-registered benchmark over K tissues, one block measured three ways (droplet scRNA-seq with empty droplets retained; snRNA-seq; orthogonal in-situ count), soup deconvolved against a pre-declared signature matrix. YES = soup-derived missing mass per type tracks the (in-situ minus droplet) deficit above a pre-declared threshold, calibration slope and interval reported, type-dependent degradation modelled. NO = soup statistically indistinguishable from a weighted average of recovered cells once degradation and per-type RNA content are accounted for. Deliverable either way: the shift in published proportions after soup-based correction, with the archival denominator stated.

Strongest remaining objection (rewritten post-verification): the question is unanswered, and the field has already built on an answer to it. SoupX, the most used background tool in this area, rests on the soup being "approximately uniform sampling of the cells in the sequencing batch" and supports that with background-to-cell expression correlations of 0.71-0.96 (median 0.86); CellBender models the ambient profile as "a weighted average of total expression" and demonstrates it on heart data where the top removed genes track the highest-UMI cell type. Measuring the candidate's quantity is therefore testing a deployed modelling assumption rather than opening an unconsidered one, and NO is the direction two toolchains presuppose. Compounding it, the measurement is not yet identified: converting ambient RNA mass to cells destroyed needs per-type mRNA content, published for no solid tissue in anything opened across three passes, so the YES and NO arms stay confounded with per-type RNA content and per-type degradation.
Promotion requires: (1) an independently measured per-type mRNA-content conversion for the tissue under test, or a design that estimates it inside the experiment, so a composition mismatch is attributable to cells rather than to transcripts per cell — and the one candidate source, Monaco et al. 2019, opened and graded, since it covers PBMC immune types and may not transfer to a solid tissue; (2) the two unreadable supplements resolved, DIEM's Fig. S12b-e and Janssen's Additional file 1, because the first is the entire published basis for cell-type-specific debris and the second holds the only numeric form of P1's composition comparison; (3) the archival denominator the record names, the share of deposited droplet datasets retaining empty-droplet barcodes, which bounds the retrospective arm; and (4) a stated position on attack 6 — what a measured answer changes for a field whose two main background tools already assume one. Nothing less promotes it: (1) is what makes the estimand identifiable and (4) is what makes it worth estimating.
