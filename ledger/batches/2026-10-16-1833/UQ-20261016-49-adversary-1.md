## Restatement (my own words)

Take two pretraining runs that are identical in every respect except that one does its matrix arithmetic in BF16 and the other in a block-scaled 8- or 4-bit format (MXFP8, MXFP4/NVFP4). Bin held-out tokens by how often they occurred in the training corpus. Is the extra loss paid by the low-precision run the same in every bin, or does it rise (or fall) as the bin gets rarer? Report that as a slope against log frequency, decide whether it is distinguishable from seed noise, and then check whether the slope lines up with how often gradient entries belonging to each bin were flushed to zero because they shared a block scale with a much larger entry. The generator's thesis is that rare data pays more; the record itself concedes the sign is open.

## Evidence quotes I rely on (verbatim from pages I opened)

- Bagdasaryan et al. 2019 (1905.12101): "accuracy of DP models drops much more for the underrepresented classes and subgroups."; "training on the data of the underrepresented subgroups produces larger gradients, thus clipping reduces their learning rate"; "random noise addition has the biggest impact on the underrepresented inputs." Scope: DP-SGD (per-example clipping + isotropic Gaussian noise), image/NLP classifiers; NOT block-scaled quantization noise, which is signal-dependent (set by the block maximum), so the transfer is an analogy, not a theorem.
- ICML 2025 "Understanding the Unfairness in Network Quantization": the paper "considers two widely used algorithms: Post Training Quantization (PTQ) and Quantization Aware Training (QAT)"; attributes class-wise disparity to "the gradient norm of the group loss function and (2) the trace of the group loss function's Hessian matrix"; "Class imbalance is to blame for unfairness, because of negative correlations between the group size and the gradient norm."; scope limit: "we focus on quantizations only on weights"; CNNs (ResNet18/50, VGG19) on UTK-Face, FER2013, (Imbalanced-)CIFAR-10/MNIST; LLMs only as future work.
- Marchisio et al. 2024 (2407.03211): "In this work, we focus on post-training quantization"; "languages are disparately affected by quantization, with non-Latin script languages impacted worst"; "Multilingual data is an example of long tail data."; "Automatic metrics severely underestimate damage from quantization."
- Quartet II (2601.22813): no stratification by domain/language/frequency; "NVFP4 was shown to have enough range for the scales not to underflow"; the paper's framing is unbiased gradient estimation.
