Question (canonical): Estimated within individuals from their own paired organs (breast, lung, kidney, testis, ovary), is the side-specific cancer hazard proportional to that side's measured at-risk epithelial cell number, or does a residual left-right asymmetry in carcinogenesis survive once per-side cell number is accounted for? Both adversaries judged the repaired wording; it keeps the estimand in bodies and does not change the verdict.
Alternative formulations: (1) Do the familiar laterality excesses in paired-organ cancers (right lung, left breast, right testis) amount to nothing more than the larger side carrying more cells at risk, or is there a side-of-body effect on carcinogenesis itself? (2) Fitted within person on paired organs, what is the exponent relating a side's cancer hazard to its at-risk cell number, and does it equal the 1.0 an independent-cell-of-origin theory requires?
