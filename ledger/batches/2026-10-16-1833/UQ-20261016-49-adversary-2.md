## Restatement in my own words

Train the same model twice on byte-identical data in the same order with the same seed, once in BF16
and once in a block-scaled low-precision format (MXFP8, then MXFP4/NVFP4). Score held-out tokens
separately in bins defined by how often each token appeared in the *training* corpus. Question: does
the BF16-to-low-precision held-out loss gap depend on the bin — specifically, is the slope beta of
that gap against log training-corpus frequency distinguishable from zero against a BF16 seed-to-seed
null — and does beta move with the fraction of gradient entries that the format's shared per-block
scale flushes to zero, measured for the same strata? Secondary: the same contrast by entity/fact
frequency decile and by language resource tier. A tightly bounded flat slope counts as an answer.

In plainer terms: is the published "4-bit training reaches parity" claim an average that hides a
rarity-graded tax, and if so is the shared block exponent the thing levying it?
