## REGIME

**Kumar et al. 2012, in its own words.** Population / unit of analysis: multigene and
genome-scale sequence alignments analysed under substitution models - "Bias is introduced in
phylogenomics because statistical analyses require a model of sequence evolution, which can only
approximate the true, but unknown, evolutionary process." Operating condition: genome-scale data
where site-sampling error is near zero, **short internal branches** ("Whenever two nodes are
separated by a short branch"), and underparameterized models ("Bias will occur when the models used
for analyses are underparameterized"). Instrument: the phylogenetic bootstrap and Bayesian posterior
probabilities on ML/Bayesian trees. Threshold: "a bootstrap support of >=95% ... roughly equivalent
to the classical hypothesis-testing P value <= 0.05". **The regime is keyed to BRANCH LENGTH, DATA
SCALE and MODEL FIT. It is not keyed to divergence depth, and the served text contains no
gene-tree/species-tree layer at all** (no occurrence of "gene tree", "species tree", "coalescent",
"lineage sorting", "organismal").

**Larson et al., in its own words.** Unit of analysis: a cytoplasmic locus tree compared against
nuclear gene trees and an inferred species tree, under the multispecies coalescent. Population:
"Because the concept of cytonuclear dissonance applies to the phylogenetic history of lineages and
clades, our discussion will largely focus on inferences made at evolutionary scales above the level
of individuals or single populations" (reconstruction, 3 fragments). Instruments: comparison of the
cytDNA tree to nuclear gene trees (Method I), simulation under a fitted model (Method II), and
branch-length tests. Biological assumptions: cytoplasmic genomes "haploid and uniparentally
inherited" in most plants and animals, with cytDNA Ne "expected to be four times lower than nuclear
loci in a diploid" **"Under idealized conditions"**, a figure the authors immediately qualify -
"However, this varies greatly depending on several biological factors and is difficult to measure
empirically". Extension: "Many of the ideas discussed here are also relevant to studies of symbionts
and their hosts, particularly bacterial endosymbionts".

**And the decisive regime fact for this entry: Larson explicitly DENIES that divergence depth is the
operative variable** (reconstruction, 3 consecutive fragments):

> "Importantly, it is the length of branches that determines how much ILS there is, not the age of
> branches." "Phylogenetic conflict arising from ILS is the same for recent divergence and ancient
> divergence, and does not decrease over time"

**Does the claim sit inside each regime?** For Kumar: a both-ways claim sits inside his regime only
where the conflict is between analyses of modelled sequence data on a SHORT branch; the entry's
"deep divergences where the gene tree equals the organismal tree" is not Kumar's stated condition,
and the gene-tree/organismal-tree equation appears nowhere in the served text. For Larson: the
non-error reading sits inside his regime only above the individual/population level and only as one
scenario among five that his decision structure distinguishes (Scenarios 1-5), with error retained
as a live cause throughout.

## BEARING ON THE LEDGER ENTRY

**STRIKE the "BOTH WAYS" leg as written.** It does not fail because the two readings agree only in
a narrow regime. It fails earlier, on the quotations:

- The non-error reading's load-bearing element - well-supported differing branches as the reliable
  signal of real discordance - is the **inverse** of Larson's sentence, which gives a necessary
  condition and then expressly keeps gene tree inference error, misidentified orthologs, alignment
  issues, assembly errors and species misidentification on the table even for whole cytoplasmic
  genomes.
- Two of the four Larson strings are inexact (a class narrowed to a member; a hedge stripped and a
  word inserted), and one of those two sits next to a sentence that contradicts the use the entry
