Domain(s): Other STEM — seismology and operational earthquake information (target field: NEIC/JMA/GeoNet-class reporting agencies, tsunami-warning and automated-response operators, parametric risk transfer/ILS). Method field: econometrics of statistical-agency data revisions (news-versus-noise, revision rationality on successive published vintages). Source method: cross-field transplant.
Question (canonical): For earthquakes with M >= M0 in a declared region and window, reading back from agency A's own versioned event history (or an archived snapshot series or dated bulletins) the magnitude m_tau that A had published at latency tau after origin, and fixing "final" as A's value m_D at a declared date D: (a) is the revision r = m_D - m_tau mean-zero (bias in magnitude units, with CI) and orthogonal to the settlement-instant information set (depth, region, station count/azimuthal gap, magnitude type, local time, m_tau itself), reported as a news-versus-noise decomposition; and (b) over the publicly documented population of parametric contracts and automated response thresholds keyed to A's published magnitude, how many trigger decisions flip under m_D rather than m_tau, with the sign of each flip and the money or action at stake?

Alternative formulations:
1. Are earthquake magnitude revisions news or noise?
2. Is the magnitude that settles a parametric earthquake contract a biased estimate of the magnitude the catalogue ends up with, and does the bias run towards or away from payout?
3. Intake version: treating the magnitude published at a fixed settlement latency as a forecast of the finally published magnitude, is that forecast efficient, and what share of keyed trigger decisions would have come out differently under the final magnitude?
Motivating gap: Parametric earthquake instruments and automated response rules settle on a named agency's reported magnitude as published at a named time, while the agency keeps revising that magnitude for days to weeks. A revision process designed for scientific accuracy is load-bearing for binary financial and emergency decisions. The mechanism is documented in a real case (Artemis, 19 Apr 2014: USGS "initially recorded it as a M7.5", "subsequently downgraded to a M7.2", with the MultiCat Mexico 2012 bond discussed "had the initial magnitude of 7.5 been maintained") and in a 2026 near-miss (USGS Mww 7.3 against a M7.4 Class B threshold). What is absent is any measurement.
Presuppositions:
- Vintage read-back (CONTESTED, now partly sourced): USGS retains origin product versions with updateTime ("Other versions remain available for review and comparison."), and for one large NEIC event the pt/at/us versions at 6/8/18 min are kept; but no `usauto` origin is kept, for 283 of 441 ComCat events of one day the earliest retained version was already reviewed, GeoNet keeps history 365 days only, and the one independent snapshot archive found begins 2026-07-05. Recoverable for tau from roughly 6-20 min onward on large NEIC events; not recoverable for the earliest automatic vintage, and unverified for JMA.
- Contract document kind (CONTESTED, one truncated instance opened): IBRD CAR Mexico 2024 prospectus supplement keys payout to "Earthquake Event Parameters provided by the applicable Earthquake Reporting Source", carries an "Event Parameters Acquisition Period Extension Notice", warns sources "may make available from time to time several different reports which may show different levels of accuracy", and makes the calculation agent's determination final and binding absent manifest error. Annexes A-C were truncated, so thresholds, the reporting source's name and the window length are unread. Consequence: tau is a window with a variable end that the study must impose, not read off the contract.
- A "final" magnitude (converted): fixed by declared rule (value as of D); a declared convention, not a world-claim.
- The binding agency is the one named in the contract (CONTESTED): for tsunami-warning operators the structure fits less cleanly, since the centres publish their own origins minutes before NEIC's, so their early value and their "final" belong to different producers.
- Power (CONTESTED, unverified): few large events, non-stationary network coverage and a changing magnitude pipeline may power only a bias test; declared in advance with the bias-only fallback.
- NOT presupposed: that revisions are biased or that any trigger flips. An unforecastable revision with zero flips is a full answer.
What would count as an answer: a regression of r = m_D - m_tau on the settlement-instant information set as a news-versus-noise decomposition, bias stated in magnitude units with CI; plus a tabulated count over the declared contract and threshold population of decisions that flip under m_D, with sign and stake. A clean negative is pre-registered as a full answer.

Significance — consequence: If yes (biased and/or predictable revisions with flips), three things change: contracts settle on a later vintage or sharpen their revision mechanism; the agency attaches an uncertainty, not merely a status flag, to the value it knows is load-bearing at a named latency; and the ILS market gets a priceable basis-risk component currently attributed to hazard rather than to measurement. If no, current practice is licensed and the recurring near-miss argument is closed as tail noise.

Significance — fecundity: the same test on depth and location (where revision is larger); whether revision sign depends on proximity to a contractual threshold (an agency-incentive question); optimal settlement latency as a decision problem trading revision risk against liquidity; multi-agency disagreement at a fixed instant as an uncertainty proxy; and the same apparatus on other official physical statistics that settle contracts (reanalysed hurricane intensity, gauge stages, degree-days).
