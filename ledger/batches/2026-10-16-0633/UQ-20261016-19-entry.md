Question (canonical): In a corpus of shared-facility assay records (iLab/PPMS/LIMS, per-run time stamps, user, fund code, sample count) restricted to linked (laboratory, project, assay, sample class) cells whose runs fall in a project window ending at the submission date of a facility-attributed paper, what is the distribution of the attempt-to-publication ratio R = runs logged / runs traceable to a published figure panel (denominator read off the paper's stated n of independent experiments); how does R partition between-laboratory versus between-project; is R associated with the direction of the linked published result; and does any association survive a negative control of calibration/QC assays?
Alternative formulations:
- What is the file-drawer ratio inside the laboratory — how many times was the experiment run before the version that appears in the paper?
- Can billing-grade facility logs reconstruct the unreported attempt count behind a published figure, and is that count associated with whether the finding is positive?
Motivating gap: New-instrument candidate. Selective reporting is claimed to have been measured only from published artefacts (p-curves, excess significance) or self-report, while facility management systems now hold per-run, per-user, time-stamped records for years because finance requires them for cost recovery. Neves & Amaral (eLife 2020;9:e56626) state that detecting unreported experiments "requires information readers don't have" and propose prevention rather than measurement. NOTE: this gap statement was falsified at Gate 4 — see adversarial log, attacks 2 and 4.
Presuppositions: P1 facility records are retrievable and linkable to projects then papers (contested); P2 a logged run is attributable to a published figure (contested); P3 crux — rerun count carries reporting content, with optimisation/faults independent of result direction (contested); P4 an ethics/consent path exists for billing records identifying individuals (contested); P5 facility-mediated assays represent the lab's experimental work (contested; benchtop work leaves no telemetry); P6 retention windows reach back past the publication lag (contested, checkable per facility). Not enumerated by the record and added at Gate 4: P7 the billed unit and the paper's "n independent experiments" are the same kind of thing.
What would count as an answer: A distribution of R over a defined corpus (N linked project-assay cells and matched papers), per assay type, with a within-laboratory variance component, plus the pre-specified result-direction test and the calibration-assay negative control. R near one for most assays is as informative as a large mean. A quantified demonstration that the linkage cannot be made at acceptable error rates is a feasibility verdict, not an answer, and must be reported as such.

Strongest remaining objection: R is not a well-defined ratio. The numerator is whatever the facility bills — a booked session, a lane, an injection — and the denominator is the paper's count of independent biological replicates; these are different units, related by a price list and a plate layout rather than by anything about the research process, and the relation differs per assay. So R can fall far below or far above 1 for reasons that carry no reporting content, and the per-assay distribution, the cross-assay comparison and the between-laboratory variance decomposition compare quantities of different kinds. Nothing in the Gate 1 reformulation addresses this: P2 covers attribution of runs to figures, never commensurability. Compounding it, the design's inferential load sits on the result-direction test, which is run on a corpus conditioned on publication (a collider that removes exactly the many-runs-then-null cells) and in which legitimate re-replication of nulls predicts the same sign, so even a clean association would not separate selection from ordinary practice.
Suggested first step toward an answer (pointer only — do not take it): Before any corpus is assembled, establish whether a commensurable unit exists at all: for one facility, tabulate what each service actually bills (session, hour, lane, sample, injection) against the "n independent experiments" statements in a hand-audited sample of its acknowledged papers, and see whether any assay class admits a one-billed-unit-to-one-independent-experiment mapping. Separately, redo Gate 2 in the protocol-to-publication cohort and facility-bibliometrics vocabularies the adversaries used, and reposition any surviving question explicitly as the run-level refinement of that family.
**Judge's post-Gate-5 verification (2026-10-16, independent fresh-context sweep, 12 queries, 0 refusals):** the falsification of the motivating gap is CONFIRMED and is stronger than the record states. Prior work links independently created pre-publication process records to the publication record to measure non-reporting in at least three families: animal-ethics protocol cohorts (Wieschowski 2019 — 67% publication; van der Naald 2020 — 26% of animals reported; a French NHP/ALTEX study — 1,751 of 3,649 authorised animals appearing in publications); observatory time-allocation records (ESO non-publishing programmes, 52.9% return and 36.8% of allocated time to non-publishing programmes — mutually confirming the entry's 47.1%/~37% figures; ALMA ~90%; XMM-Newton ObsID linkage — 16% of observations and 13% of observing time never used in any paper); and protocol/registry/grant/regulatory comparisons (the 37 studies tabulated by Li 2018). The sharpest counterexample, which the record does not cite, is **Chan et al. 2004**: it uses ethics-committee protocols rather than published artefacts AND shows self-report failing — 86% of trialists denied unreported outcomes that their own protocols proved existed. So "selective reporting has only ever been measured from published artefacts or self-report" is false and is struck. A narrower gap does survive, and is logged for a successor rather than for this entry: nothing located measures attempts-per-reported-result in the strict sense (runs executed per run reaching a figure). Every existing denominator is either allocated resource (ESO time, animals authorised, XMM observing time) or planned analyses (Boonacker 2011: 69% of prespecified subgroup analyses unreported, 76% of reported analyses post hoc; Kasenda 2014; Chan 2004's outcome-level 50%/65%), never executed experimental attempts, and none is derived from instrument run logs. The XMM reuse factor (5–15x, up to 30x) runs the other way — datasets per paper, not attempts per figure — and the one effort aimed at run-level attempt records (the ICML 2026 ELN dataset proposal) carries no publication linkage and no numbers. None of this disturbs the verdict: the fatal ground is attack 4, the non-commensurability of R, which is independent of the gap question.
