# UQ-20261016-16 — Adversary #1 log (Gate 4)

Date: 2026-10-16. Role: adversary (sole job: kill the candidate). Budget used: 9/9 web searches, 4/4 fetches.

## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Modern patients with a proven infection: when you take the highest temperature recorded before anyone gives them paracetamol, is that number drifting downward year by year — and if so, is it drifting down only as fast as the temperature of non-infected people measured on the same machines in the same hospital at the same hour, or faster? In plain terms: as the population's resting temperature slid down over the last century, did the *size of the fever spike* on top of it shrink too, or did the whole curve just slide down intact? The policy point hanging on the answer: if only the floor moved, lowering the 38.0/38.3 °C fever cut-off restores lost sensitivity; if the spike itself is smaller, lowering the cut-off does not restore it, because the signal is smaller and not merely offset.

Two things I note about the restated object before attacking it. (1) The feasible version is no longer a historical question at all — it is a 1995–2025 EHR regression of (episode peak − contemporaneous afebrile reference) on calendar year. (2) The quantity sought is roughly 0.03 °C per birth decade, i.e. of order 0.1 °C across the whole feasible window. Everything below turns on that number being small.

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional** — nothing Unresolved, but Disguise is only Partial (bibliographic arm never executed, and I found an unlogged close neighbour, Niven CCM 2013, on my first well-aimed query), so novelty does not hold firmly enough for Validated; attacks 4, 6, 7 and 8 are Partial.

**Single strongest killing point:** Niven et al., Crit Care Med 2013 shows that inside a six-year window in one health system, care-process and measurement change cut the fraction of ICU patients recorded as febrile by half — an order of magnitude more movement than the ~0.1 °C of Δbaseline that Protsiv's rate predicts across the candidate's entire 1995–2025 feasible window. The confounders in P7 therefore dominate the signal in the only arm that is feasible, the pre-specified "clinically meaningful" bound of 0.2 °C is wider than the whole predicted effect, and the record's claim that this quantity became computable only with mature EHRs is falsified by the same paper.
