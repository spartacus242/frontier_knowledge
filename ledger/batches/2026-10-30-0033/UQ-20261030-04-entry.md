Domain(s): Biology — mitochondrial genetics and genome replication (single-cell single-molecule sequencing of full-length mtDNA)

Question (canonical): Within a single cell, is the number of de-novo somatic mutations carried by each individual near-full-length mtDNA molecule distributed as the Poisson expectation computed from that same cell's per-base de-novo mutation frequency times the called molecule length, or is it overdispersed — a minority of molecules carrying a disproportionate share of the cell's mutations, as a replication hierarchy among genome copies would produce?
Reformulation note: this text is V2, the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation, scored as canonical. It fixed the numerator class to de-novo somatic with a stated exclusion rule, parameterised the Poisson mean explicitly, and enumerated the cell-type set (skeletal-muscle fibre segments; cultured primary dermal fibroblasts, cardiomyocytes admissible as substitute post-mitotic type).

Alternative formulations:
V1 (as submitted): Within a single cell, is the number of de-novo mutations carried by each individual mtDNA molecule distributed as the Poisson expectation from that cell's per-base mutation frequency, or is it overdispersed — a minority of molecules carrying most of the mutations, as a replication hierarchy among genome copies would produce?
V3 (lay): Do a cell's mitochondrial mutations sit one to a genome copy, or do a few copies carry several each?

Motivating gap:
Somatic mtDNA burden is reported as a per-base frequency pooled over the hundreds to thousands of genome copies in a cell, with molecule identity discarded in the denominator. A per-molecule instrument exists (Bi et al. 2023, iMiGseq), so the joint distribution of mutations along one physical molecule is obtainable; whether replication is uniform across copies or hierarchical leaves its signature only in that joint distribution. The intake's stronger claim — that the per-molecule mutation SET was previously unmeasurable — was withdrawn in the Gate 0-3 pass: iMiGseq has reported per-molecule haplotypes and variant linkage since March 2023. The question survived intake as unmeasured, not as unmeasurable.

Object: the answer would be a fact about individual mitochondrial DNA molecules inside single cells — molecules, on the pass list.
Deletion: if every paper, database and protocol vanished tonight, the mtDNA molecules in cells would still fix the answer; the subject matter does not vanish with the literature.

Presuppositions:
1. CONTESTED and load-bearing: individual near-full-length molecules can be called accurately enough that two or more true de-novo somatic mutations on ONE molecule are callable above error.
2. Labelled prerequisite: single cells isolable with mtDNA complement intact, no inter-cell mixing (per-cell barcoding plus contamination control).
3. Variants separable into de-novo somatic vs haplogroup-fixed, by the stated exclusion rule.
4. CONTESTED, graded Unverified by the record itself: a Poisson null is the uniform-replication null, so overdispersion is diagnostic of a hierarchy.
5. CONTESTED: enough replication in the chosen cell types for a hierarchy to leave a signature — adverse to the design, since the only cell types with demonstrated 10^3-10^4 molecule yields are oocytes, where the signature is least expected.
6. Extraction does not fragment multi-mutation molecules into apparent singletons.

What would count as an answer:
At least ~50 single cells per cell type in the two enumerated types, each yielding at least ~10^3 individually sequenced near-full-length molecules, with a per-molecule error model from a matched-length known-sequence spike-in. Headline number: the within-cell index of dispersion (variance/mean) of de-novo somatic mutations per molecule, with a confidence interval, against the Poisson expectation from that cell's own per-base frequency times called length, plus the fitted negative-binomial shape parameter.

Significance — consequence:
If overdispersed beyond the error model: mtDNA replication is not uniform across copies, a minority of templates is copied repeatedly, and every pooled per-base burden figure corresponds to more mutation-free genomes plus a small heavily-mutated subpopulation — which bears on heteroplasmy threshold effects and gives clonal expansion a mechanism other than random intracellular drift. If Poisson at the measured depth: replication is effectively uniform, a positive result for the relaxed-replication family and a bound on template reuse. Partly deflated at Gate 4 on a mouse figure, and that deflation is now narrower: at P2's printed 1.2e-6/bp, ~98% of molecules are mutation-free and ~2% carry all of a cell's mutations under the Poisson null itself, but at the human aged-brain 1.9e-5/bp the mutation-free share falls to about 73% (re-derived arithmetic on printed inputs, not a printed figure), so the "mutation-free majority" half of the consequence is already true without a hierarchy at the mouse figure and much weaker at the human one. The distinct surviving payoff is the replication-count heterogeneity Var(k)/E(k).
