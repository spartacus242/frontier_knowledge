Domain(s): Medicine / public health — global-health measurement and evaluation, epidemiology, health policy and programme monitoring. Method source field: macroeconometrics / official statistics (real-time data analysis, revision analysis). Source method: cross-field transplant.

Question (canonical):
- V0 (as intaken): Treating each annual release of a modelled global-health indicator series as a data vintage in the sense of real-time data analysis, how large are the retrospective revisions to the same country-year across vintages, are those revisions news or noise (forecastable from the prior vintage), and what fraction of published analyses that used an earlier vintage change their headline conclusion when re-run on the current one?
- R1 (Gate 1 reformulation adopted; one reformulation used): For a pre-specified indicator set of *country-year* modelled global-health estimates (primary: WUENIC DTP3 and MCV1 national coverage; secondary: UN IGME under-five mortality, MMEIG MMR) and a pre-specified vintage set (each annual release's full historical series, recovered from archived releases and country-profile files over a stated window): (a) what are the moments and serial structure of the revision r_{c,t}^{v->v+1} by country-year and release; (b) in a regression of the revision on the prior vintage's own level versus on the latest available value, which coefficient is non-zero — i.e. was the prior vintage an inefficient (forecastable, "noise-like") estimate of the current one, with the interpretation restated for a modelled quantity whose revision comes from model and input changes rather than late returns; and (c) in a pre-registered sample of published analyses that used an identifiable earlier vintage, what fraction of headline estimates change sign, cross a pre-declared decision threshold, or gain/lose significance when re-run on the current vintage?
- Reason for reformulation (well-posedness only): V0 left the indicator set, the vintage window, the unit (country-year vs aggregate) and the "headline conclusion" rule unspecified, so limb (c) was unfalsifiable and limb (b) had no stated estimand.

Alternative formulations:
1. (lay) Do the annual re-estimations of WHO/UNICEF immunization coverage and comparable modelled series revise history enough to overturn the studies, rankings and target assessments built on the earlier numbers?
2. (infrastructure) Does global health need a real-time (vintage) database of its headline indicators, and what would a revision-stability audit of those indicators show?
3. (method field's own words) Is the first-release vintage of a modelled global-health indicator an efficient forecast of its later vintages, and what is the vintage-sensitivity of the published empirical results that rest on it?

Motivating gap (as intaken, and as it stands after Gate 2):
As intaken: producers restate history annually (WHO's immunization-coverage page: "Coverage data are reviewed and the estimates updated annually"; the 2025 revision released 15 July 2026 with full historical country series), while global health has imported none of macroeconomics' revision apparatus — no vintage archive, no revision statistics, no audit of whether conclusions survive revision.
AFTER GATE 2 THE GAP CLAIM IS PARTLY FALSIFIED. The "no revision statistics" half is not true of the field's largest modelled series: Zavalis, Pezzullo and Ioannidis, "Instability of Global Burden of Disease Estimates of Deaths and Disability-Adjusted Life-Years From Major Risk Factors: A Meta-Epidemiological Analysis", JAMA Health Forum 2026;7(3):e260108 (opened on the publisher page), compares eight GBD iterations (2010-2023) for the same calendar years and publishes dispersion statistics (median range-to-mean 0.5 for deaths, 0.4 for DALYs; ~50% of death estimates with CV > 0.2; 96% of GBD 2021 sugar-sweetened-beverage death estimates outside the GBD 2019 uncertainty interval; processed meat 8th -> 13th in risk rank between 2017 and 2019). That is the candidate's limb (a) executed and published in a mainstream health-policy journal seven months before this candidate was generated, in the candidate's own field and its own words. What survives is narrower: country-year unit, coverage-type indicators, the news/noise (efficiency) test, and the conclusion-reversal rate.

Presuppositions (status after Gate 2):
- P1 Producers re-estimate historical country-years, not merely append the newest year — ESTABLISHED IN KIND, NOT VERIFIED IN DETAIL. The WHO landing page for the 2025 revision notes was opened and confirms a dated annual "revision" with technical notes (15 July 2026), consistent with the intake's quoted sentences; the notes PDF itself COULD NOT BE READ (fetch budget exhausted after 5 fetches), so whether all historical country-years move is still unchecked. Independently, the JAMA Health Forum paper establishes that a comparable modelled global-health series does restate the same calendar year across releases, often outside the earlier uncertainty interval.
- P2 Prior vintages are retrievable — CONTESTED and LIMITING, with one new weak datum: search results surfaced country-profile files at two coexisting paths (data.unicef.org/.../cp/immunisation/wuenic_<iso>.pdf and .../cp-local/wuenic-2026/wuenic_<iso>.pdf), suggesting at least two live vintages of the same profiles; neither was opened, so this is URL-level evidence only. Where vintages are not retrievable, the finding becomes a case for building the archive, which is itself an answer.
- P3 Published analyses can be tied to an identifiable vintage — CONTESTED, and newly load-bearing: query 9 found no study of vintage-citation practice in global health, so the sampling frame for limb (c) may have to be built before it can be drawn, and papers that report no release or download date are a quantifiable non-response stratum rather than a nuisance.
- P4 Revisions are large relative to the effects these series are used to detect — NO LONGER WHOLLY OPEN: for GBD risk-factor burden the answer is "yes, often larger than the producer's own prior interval" (JAMA Health Forum 2026). For WUENIC-type country-year coverage it remains unmeasured as far as this search reached.
- P5 News-versus-noise tests transfer to model-based estimates — CONTESTED; the genuine interface problem and the reason the transplant is not a pastiche. Gate 2 sharpened it: the nearest epidemiological neighbour (Tang et al. 2025, below) forecasts revisions that are *reporting backfill of counts*, a mechanism the Mankiw-Shapiro framing was built for; a WUENIC-style revision is a change of model, covariates and historical survey inputs, so "the prior vintage was an inefficient forecast" must be restated as a statement about the estimator's stability, not about an agency's information set.
- P6 "Headline conclusion" and the sample frame can be pre-registered well enough for a reversal rate to be well defined — flagged; R1 ties it to sign change, a pre-declared decision threshold, and significance change, which is the operationalisation the economics precedent used informally.
- P7 Someone acts on the numbers — ESTABLISHED in kind (programme targets, global monitoring frameworks, funder decisions), and strengthened: the JAMA Health Forum paper's existence and its "interpreted with caution and used with great care" conclusion show the audience is already activated.
- P8 Producer uncertainty does not already subsume the question — PARTLY ESTABLISHED by evidence, not by producer documentation: the JAMA paper's finding that revised point estimates frequently fall outside the previous release's 95% interval is direct evidence that within-vintage uncertainty does not bound across-vintage movement. The producers' own documentation on this point was NOT read (WUENIC notes PDF unread), so the P8 check is evidenced from the independent side only.

What would count as an answer:
(i) A vintage matrix for the pre-specified country-year indicator set with revision moments (mean, dispersion, serial structure) by country-year and release, and the share of revisions falling outside the superseded release's uncertainty interval;
(ii) prior-vintage-efficiency ("news or noise") tests per indicator, reported as the pair of regressions (revision on prior vintage; revision on latest value) with the modelled-estimate caveat of P5 stated in the interpretation;
(iii) a pre-registered re-run of a sampled frame of published analyses on the current vintage, reporting the fraction whose headline estimate changes sign, crosses a decision threshold, or loses/gains significance, plus the fraction of candidate papers excluded because no vintage could be identified;
(iv) a specification of what a global-health real-time database would contain.
Either direction acts: small unforecastable revisions license current practice; large or forecastable ones make vintage citation a reporting requirement and the archive a piece of infrastructure someone must build.

Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, all opened):
PRIOR A (nearest; same field, same words).
Zavalis EA, Pezzullo AM, Ioannidis JPA. "Instability of Global Burden of Disease Estimates of Deaths and Disability-Adjusted Life-Years From Major Risk Factors: A Meta-Epidemiological Analysis." JAMA Health Forum 2026;7(3):e260108, 13 March 2026. https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama-health-forum/fullarticle/2845973 (preprint: https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.14.25340145)
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS: risk factor x calendar year (66 risk factors, GBD levels 0-3), as absolute global deaths and DALYs; dietary risks additionally stratified by sex and World Bank region. NOT country-year.
- ENDPOINT: dispersion of point estimates for the same calendar year across releases - range-to-mean ratio and coefficient of variation - plus the share of revised point estimates lying outside the superseded release's 95% uncertainty interval, and shifts in risk rank.
- REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: the other GBD iterations (2010, 2013, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2019, 2021, 2023) for the same year; and the earlier release's own uncertainty interval.
- ESTIMAND: descriptive; no causal or forecast estimand is defined. Explicitly exploratory, not pre-registered. No test of forecastability from prior estimates; no re-running of any published downstream analysis.
PRIOR B (the method-field template, executed on another modelled series).
Johnson S, Larson W, Papageorgiou C, Subramanian A. "Is Newer Better? Penn World Table Revisions and Their Impact on Growth Estimates." Working paper, 5 November 2009. https://cgdev.org/doc/events/12.01.09/Is_newer_better_Sep_1_2009.pdf
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS: country-year PPP GDP levels and growth rates, differenced across PWT versions; also country-period averages (10-year, 29-year).
- ENDPOINT: revision moments (mean 0.1 pp and SD ~1.1 pp on 29-year average growth; annual-growth revision SD 5.39 pp), sign changes in growth (13% of countries), turnover of top-10/bottom-10 lists, regressions of absolute revision on data-quality grade, log GDP, number of ICP studies and distance from the benchmark year; AND replication of 13 published papers on PWT 6.2, of which 4 change substantively (Ramey & Ramey's volatility-growth coefficient loses significance; Jones & Olken's leader-death effects lose significance; 35% of Hausmann-Pritchett-Rodrik sustained-growth cases differ; Aghion-Howitt-Mayer-Foulkes' convergence threshold collapses).
- REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: PWT 6.1 vs 6.2 (same ICP benchmark) primarily; also 5.0/5.5/5.6/6.1, WDI and WEO.
- ESTIMAND: the vintage-induced change in a published coefficient or classification - i.e. exactly limb (c), for GDP.
- Recommends using the version closest to the timing of inquiry and demonstrating robustness across versions; does not propose an archive, and runs no formal news/noise or efficiency test.
PRIOR C (nearest epidemiological neighbour; the forecastability limb, wrong revision mechanism).
Tang J, Rumack A, Wilder B, Rosenfeld R. "Real-time Forecasting of Data Revisions in Epidemic Surveillance Streams." medRxiv 2025.05.12.25327058 (posted 17 November 2025). https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.12.25327058
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS: location x reference date, indexed by reporting lag (county/state COVID claims, state antigen positivity, MA DPH cases; extended to Puerto Rico dengue and national ILI).
- ENDPOINT: quantile forecasts of the finalized value at a target lag L (60/45/14 days), scored by weighted interval score against a flat-line "latest observation" baseline (e.g. claims at lag 14: WIS 0.12 vs >0.18; fractions at lag 0: ~0.16 vs >0.45).
- REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: the flat-line baseline that treats the current vintage as final.
- ESTIMAND: predictive accuracy of the eventual value; the revision is implicit. No news/noise decomposition, no formal predictability test, no downstream-conclusion analysis.
- MECHANISM: reporting backfill of counts plus a small share of corrections - not annual re-estimation of a model-based indicator.
Also found, not a prior but relevant to triviality: the rOpenSci `reviser` R package ("Analyzing Real-Time Data Revisions in R", blog 13 April 2026; vignette "Understanding Data Revisions") - the vintage/revision toolkit is now off-the-shelf, so limbs (a) and (b) are tooling-ready rather than methodologically hard.

Significance — consequence:
If revisions to country-year coverage/mortality estimates are small and unforecastable, current practice (download the current release, treat it as the history) is licensed and the question closes with a citable number. If they are large, the superseded release's uncertainty interval does not bound them (which Prior A already shows for a sibling series), and a non-trivial fraction of published analyses flip, then three things change concretely: vintage citation becomes a reporting item (as Prior B recommended for PWT and as journals never adopted), a vintage archive becomes infrastructure someone must fund and build, and target-attainment claims ("country X reached 90% DTP3 in year t") become vintage-relative statements. If revisions are forecastable from the prior vintage, the stronger consequence follows: the first release is an inefficient estimate and could be bias-corrected at publication, which is a change to the producers' own output, not just to users' practice.
Significance — audience:
(1) The estimate producers - WHO/UNICEF immunization (WUENIC), UN IGME, MMEIG, and IHME - who would have to publish revision triangles or an as-of API; Prior A shows this audience is already engaged and defensive. (2) Programme and target monitors - Gavi, the Immunization Agenda 2030 secretariat, SDG custodian agencies - whose target assessments are computed against whichever vintage was current. (3) Impact evaluators and health economists who use these series as exposure or outcome; for them limb (c) is directly about the validity of their published estimates. (4) Journal editors and reporting-guideline bodies, who already require data-availability statements and could require a release identifier at essentially zero cost. (5) The real-time-data community in macroeconometrics and official statistics, for whom a modelled (rather than reported) series is a new regime for their efficiency tests.
Significance — fecundity:
Opens: whether an optimally revision-corrected first release can be published (a producer-side nowcasting question); whether revision magnitude predicts data-system weakness well enough to serve as a cheap surveillance-quality indicator (Prior B found revisions tracked data-quality grade and distance from the benchmark year, which suggests it would); how to specify a vintage-robust estimator for panel analyses on modelled indicators; whether target-setting against a restated series creates a measurable incentive artefact; what the right null is for an efficiency test when the "forecaster" is an estimator rather than an agency.
Significance — anchoring:
Touches the long-running dispute over the reliability and comparability of modelled global-health estimates (IGME-vs-IHME divergence, Prior A's instability finding, the MMEIG round-to-round comparability caveats); the reproducibility literature's blind spot that a "reproducible" analysis script run on a restated input is not reproducible in result; the measurement-error literature on development statistics (Prior B and its successors); and the surveillance-revision/backfill programme (Prior C), which has built as-of infrastructure for counts while the annual modelled series have none.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism):
Missing instrument, created by a producer convention, reinforced by a disciplinary boundary. Macroeconomics could only ask the question after someone built the artefact: the vintage archive (the Philadelphia Fed real-time dataset) had to exist before revisions could be moments and before "news or noise" was testable. Global-health producers publish a single "current best estimates" series and silently restate its history, so the artefact that makes the question instrumentable does not exist and each user's downloaded CSV looks like the history rather than one vintage of it. On top of that, the people who would own the method (real-time data analysts and official statisticians) do not read immunization-coverage documentation, and the people who own the data treat release-to-release change as a methods-improvement narrative rather than a measurable quantity - which is visible in query 5's results, where the producers' own across-round documents are "overview of updates in estimation methods", not revision statistics. The silence is already breaking from the methodologists' side rather than the producers' (Prior A is by a meta-research group, not by a producer), which is itself evidence that the mechanism is a boundary rather than an absence of interest; and it is the reason the remaining limbs are likely to be asked soon by someone else.
