## Restatement in my own words

Take the EFIC rule's bargain at face value: because the patients cannot consent, the researchers must first consult "the communities in which the clinical investigation will be conducted and from which the subjects will be drawn." The question asks whether the people who actually turned up to, or answered, that consultation look like the people the trial then enrolled — same age spread, same sex split, same racial and ethnic mix, same socioeconomic standing, same places on a map — and, across a frame of completed FDA-regulated EFIC trials, how often the two groups are so different that the consultation effectively polled strangers. V2 adds a prior limb: in how many trials was anything about the consulted individuals even written down, and is it still retrievable. The method being imported is survey coverage/selection error: treat consultation as a sample and ask what population it covers.

So the question has two separable limbs: (a) a recording/retrievability census, and (b) a consulted-vs-enrolled composition comparison. The record's whole novelty case rests on the claim that nobody has done (b) with characteristics (only with counts), and that no venue exists where the two sides' demographics meet.

## The killing prior, read at the publisher

**Feldman WB, Hey SP, Franklin JM, Kesselheim AS. "Public Approval of Exception From Informed Consent in Emergency Clinical Trials: A Systematic Review of Community Consultation Surveys." *JAMA Network Open* 2019;2(7):e197591. doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2019.7591.** Opened at `https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2738626`. Checked against the candidate's four attributes:
- **Unit of analysis:** the consultation *survey respondent* and the *trial*; 27 trials' FDA-docket consultation submissions. (Candidate: the trial, with consulted and enrolled groups.) Same objects.
- **Endpoint:** EFIC approval by question type, *and* the demographic composition of surveyed vs enrolled groups. The candidate's limb (b) endpoint is the second of these.
- **Comparator:** the enrolled EFIC population's own race and sex distribution. This is exactly the candidate's comparator — not a census, not event type, not an eligibility registry.
- **Estimand:** pooled proportion differences between consulted and enrolled populations (race, sex) plus approval-by-demographic association. The candidate's estimand is a per-trial distributional-overlap statistic over the same comparison.

**Second, on the geographic limb: Farley P, Stephens SW, Crowley B, Collins SP, Wong MD, Panas AB, Dennis BM, Richmond N, Inaba K, Brown KN, Holcomb JB, Jansen JO. *Trauma Surg Acute Care Open* 2021;6(1):e000830, doi:10.1136/tsaco-2021-000830** (abstract read at PubMed; full text not opened). Title, as served: "Exception from informed consent trials: social-media-based community consultation campaigns are representative of target communities." Per the abstract: social-media consultation reach for the PHOXSTAT trial was compared, **by age and gender, against US Census data for the catchment areas of three trauma centers** (Los Angeles, Birmingham, Nashville), ~332 000 people reached; differences from the local population small; race, ethnicity and socioeconomic status named as needing further study. So the candidate's catchment/geography limb and its age-and-sex strata have a published single-trial instance, and even the candidate's "race/ethnicity and SES next" framing is the published paper's own stated next step.

Spot-check of the record's priors: Prior 2 (Dickert 2014, *Crit Care Med*) corresponds to PMID 24145834, which surfaced in four of my searches — real. Prior 1 (Silbergleit 2012, *Acad Emerg Med*) and Prior 3 (Sugarman 2009, *Resuscitation*) I did not independently re-verify for want of budget; nothing I saw contradicts them. The failure is not fabrication — it is that the three graded priors are all genuine and all aimed at adjacent objects, while the on-point paper sat unread in the record's own COULD NOT READ list, surfacing in every on-point query precisely because it is the paper that did the comparison.
