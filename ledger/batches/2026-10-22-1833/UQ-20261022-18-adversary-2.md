## 1. Restatement in my own words

Treat the whole image path of a shipped vision-language model — resize/tile, patch embedding, vision encoder, projector, language model, text answer — as if it were an imaging channel, and measure behaviourally how fine a periodic pattern it can still report correctly. Print a sinusoidal grating (or slanted edge) at a known spatial frequency and contrast, at a known orientation and phase, ask the model a two-choice question whose chance level is pinned, and sweep. Then report four things per model × input-mode arm: (i) the frequency at which orientation discrimination drops to 75% correct, and the contrast threshold curve; (ii) that frequency divided by the "nominal" limit the patch grid is supposed to impose; (iii) whether any surviving above-cutoff accuracy is the model tracking resize-kernel artefacts rather than the target; (iv) whether this one measured number predicts small-text / counting / dense-chart accuracy better than the two numbers model cards already print (input pixels, vision-token or patch count).

In short: an eye chart and a lens-MTF bench for the inside of a VLM, plus a test of whether the resulting number earns its keep against the nominal spec sheet.

## 3. The prior Gate 2 missed, opened in full

**Zhang H.*, Hu, Khayatkhoei, Ilievski, Sun — "Exploring Perceptual Limitation of Multimodal Large Language Models", arXiv:2402.07384.** READ LEVEL: **FULL TEXT** (PDF via the summariser; the served page is marked "Preprint under review" and gave no venue, so I carry it by arXiv ID — *author order as served on the PDF front matter; no DOI located*).

**REGIME:** population = BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, LLaVA-1.5, Qwen-VL-Chat, Fuyu-8B in the controlled studies (plus GPT-4V and Gemini-Pro-Vision in the GQA/TextVQA study); unit of analysis = image within condition within model; operating condition = synthetic centred text rendered at font sizes 2–20 and crop-upsampled 1×–5.5×, downsample–upsample "sampling rate" 2–20, 0–9 distractors, varied global location and patch-boundary alignment, fixed 224×224 input resize, **single contrast, no spatial-frequency, orientation or phase variable**; instrument = free-form answers scored by Gestalt pattern matching (GQA/TextVQA by inclusion/exact match), **no forced choice and no stated chance level**; threshold = a sampling-rate plateau identified by inspection; category = perceptual-limitation diagnosis.

What it prints that matters here:
- A **threshold number**: "the threshold of a sampling rate of 8 is consistently observed across all MLLM models" (Sec. 4.2, repeated in the Fig. 4 caption). *Sampling rate* here is a downsample–upsample factor, i.e. a bandlimit expressed as a decimation factor — **the candidate's limb (i) in disguise, on a different axis and with no criterion.** No accuracy criterion, no statistical test, no chance floor: the paper identifies the plateau by inspection and ties it to human legibility.
- **Patch geometry is in the paper**: Table 3 lists 14×14 patches for BLIP-2/InstructBLIP/LLaVA-1.5/Qwen-VL-Chat and 30×30 for Fuyu-8B ("Table 3 outlines the patch sizes and counts of the MLLMs evaluated in our study").
- **A patch-grid effect is measured**: "Model's performance is lower when target objects remain undivided by patch boundaries" (Sec. 4.5.2) — a phase-like dependence on the patch lattice.
- Size/location effects: BLIP-2 across size quantiles "16.71% and 21.83% on GQA and TextVQA, respectively"; GPT-4V and Gemini-Pro-Vision "7.32% and 6.39% performance gap on GQA and 9.05% and 3.31% on TextVQA"; Qwen-VL-Chat "a significant performance disparity of 58 points across different locations"; a 10-point drop with distractors at font size 8.

**PUBLISHED-IS-NOT-PERFORMED check:** it does **not** print a cutoff at a declared criterion, any cycles-per-image or cycles-per-patch quantity, any ratio to a patch-Nyquist limit, any aliasing attribution, or any rival-predictor regression — the fetch is explicit that "the paper does not directly test input resolution or token count as competing predictors", and Appendix C says "it is unclear which factor plays the most important role". Patch Nyquist, positional encoding, resize/interpolation kernel, dynamic tiling/AnyRes and aliasing are **absent**.

**NUMERATOR / DENOMINATOR / OVERLAP, both directions.** Its numerator = free-form answers scored correct by fuzzy string match, over a denominator of rendered synthetic-text images at a given font size / sampling rate / location / distractor count. The candidate's numerator = correct 2AFC orientation-or-phase judgements over presented target trials with an enforced chance level. *What the prior's class misses that the candidate includes:* contrast as a dimension, orientation, phase as a controlled variable, a criterion-defined threshold, a chance floor, resize-kernel aliasing attribution, any tiling arm (it fixes 224×224), and the cross-arm predictor comparison. *What the prior includes that the candidate excludes:* distractor count, global target location, and unconstrained free-form responses with no floor — a model that emits plausible text can score above zero with no information transmitted. Nearest-neighbour word check: it reports the sampling rate at which legibility **plateaus**, where the candidate asks the frequency at which discrimination falls to a **declared criterion** — a plateau and a criterion crossing are different quantities on different axes.

**This paper must be added to the record's prior list.** Its existence lowers the novelty margin (the "is the limit set by the patch grid" question was being asked in the architecture community in Feb 2024, with patch sizes tabulated and a patch-boundary effect measured) without publishing any candidate quantity.

Its two supporting legs are weaker and I do not rest the verdict on them — the dimension-count argument that the patch stage is not a decimating sampler is **my own arithmetic** on the same config's numbers and bounds possibility rather than practice, and the Disguise finding (arXiv:2402.07384) narrows novelty without publishing any candidate quantity.

### Nomination, with the kind of document named first
The check that would settle the remaining Unverified item (C6) needs a document of this kind: one that **runs a forced-choice prompt on at least two shipped VLMs**, **varies the stimulus down to and past the point of failure**, and **prints an accuracy floor at or near the stated chance level** — not a yes/no detection rate and not a free-form score. **None of the sources I opened is that kind**, and I am not nominating one, because the only two behavioural-readout papers in this record's neighbourhood are explicitly the wrong kind: prior 1's readout has no correctness constraint and 2402.07384's "is not a forced-choice task, and no chance level is stated". So this is settled by a pilot, not by a citation — and saying so is more honest than naming a title. For the novelty side, arXiv:2608.05122 (IRIS) is the one lead I would open next, and I flag it as a **guess from its title**: orientation selectivity in ViTs is the right subject matter, but I have not opened it and do not know whether it has any behavioural readout, any contrast axis or any printed threshold at all.
