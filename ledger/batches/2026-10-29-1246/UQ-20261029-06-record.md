Significance — consequence:
If both partials are non-zero and no collapse holds, alpha is a two-factor quantity and every use of it as a single data-free score is confounded by the data spectrum: two trained matrices with equal alpha can differ in both beta and T, so an alpha-based diagnostic compares models only at matched data spectrum — a condition nobody currently states, and one that cannot even be checked for released checkpoints, where beta is unknown. If instead alpha collapses onto beta^a T^b, that combination is a named invariant of training and alpha becomes an inverse instrument: given beta it reads off the effective temperature of a trained model, and given the recipe it reads off the data's spectral decay — a quantity otherwise unobservable from weights alone. Either answer also decides, empirically, whether the free-multiplicative-convolution structure of PRIOR 1's Eq. Reason for silence (candidate mechanism — a disciplinary boundary reinforced by an instrument constraint, not "nobody thought of it"):
The two axes are owned by communities whose instruments each forbid the other's knob. The data-spectrum exponent beta is a standard device of the solvable-scaling-law line, but that line's instrument is a closed-form or asymptotic risk under a prescribed Sigma, and its endpoint is always the generalisation error — it never forms a trained weight matrix to fit, so it has no occasion to report an ESD exponent. The alpha exponent is owned by the HT-SR line, whose instrument is a power-law fit to a RELEASED pretrained checkpoint; in that instrument beta is unknown and unsettable and there is no optimizer knob at all, so the question cannot even be posed without abandoning the instrument that made the anomaly visible. The third community, the RMT-model line, holds both factors but holds them abstractly: its Sigma is an arbitrary measure entering by free multiplicative convolution and its temperature is an entropic regularisation parameter tau never identified with eta/B, so it derives exponents in variables that no training run exposes. Holding both knobs at once requires giving up all three instruments and training a grid of networks on synthetic data — cheap in compute but belonging to none of the three method traditions, and yielding a result (a two-dimensional surface) that none of the three is currently set up to publish.
