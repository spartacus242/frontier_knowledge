Domain(s): Medicine — surgical pathology, cancer staging. Adjacent: registry epidemiology, measurement-error biostatistics, metrology (conformity assessment).
Question (canonical): For organ sites whose T category turns on knife-edge size thresholds, what is the distribution of specimen-handling-induced change in measured tumour size (in situ -> fresh gross -> post-fixation gross -> histologic), and what fraction of staged patients lie within that distribution's width of a threshold, so that their stage, adjuvant therapy and trial eligibility are set by specimen handling rather than by the tumour?
Alternative formulations: (i) How much of T-category assignment is produced by the specimen's journey from patient to paraffin block rather than by the tumour itself? (ii) If size were measured in situ or fresh instead of after fixation, how many patients per year would change T category, and in which direction? Gate 1 reformulation (one permitted, retained in full in the record) split the question into three limbs: (a) per-step shift distribution with observer variance components, protocol held fixed; (b) registry near-threshold probability mass with heaping modelled, as patients per year; (c) head-to-head prognostic discrimination of stage recomputed on fresh versus fixed measurements.
Motivating gap: Presupposition inversion — AJCC/UICC and CAP specify pathologic tumour size to the millimetre and hang categorical thresholds on it, though the tissue measured has been excised, deflated, fixed and sectioned, each step with documented dimensional effect, and the thresholds were calibrated on cohorts of undocumented handling. Gate 2 found the gap does not hold: the framing limb is a published paper's title in the candidate's own field and the fraction limb is a research gap stated in print. Verification overturns the first half of that and re-bases the second: P3's title is about the examination approach, not the specimen's physical state, and P2's stated gap is about terminal-digit preference, not handling. The gap as intaken therefore survives in narrowed form — the per-step dispersion is unpublished even in P1, and the near-threshold fraction is unpublished for any organ.
Presuppositions: 1. Handling changes measured size non-trivially and mostly one-signed [established by Gate 2 — Tran 2015: 4.6% fresh->fixed, 7.1% fixed->microscopic, 11.4% fresh->microscopic; the MEANS are now an input, but the adversary established that the DISPERSION is not: Tran reports P values and bare ranges (0-18.2%, 0-22.7%) with no SD, no CI and no observer-variance analysis at all, so limb (a)'s actual deliverable is unpublished even in the paper said to have executed it]. 2. Threshold crossings are consequential [established]. 3. Near-threshold size densities are retrievable from registries [established]. 4. Terminal-digit heaping is real [established: 34.8-38.7% of diameters end 0/5] but modellable well enough to recover near-threshold mass [contested, load-bearing for limb (b)]. 5. Paired multi-state measurement is feasible without compromising diagnostic material [contested; done once, n=34, one centre]. 6. Handling of the threshold-calibrating cohorts is documentable [contested, no source either way; without it "which measurement is correct" cannot close]. 7. In-situ size is the right measurand at all — the artefact may be baked into the calibration, so correcting it could degrade staging [contested; the dissolution risk, and the only limb with no located prior]. 8. [NEW, Gate 2, load-bearing] Handling-induced dimensional change is separable from grossing-protocol choice; Verbeke 2022's protocol effect (10 mm, 36%) looks larger than the fixation effect, so an unseparated design estimates a mixture. Sharpened at Gate 4: Verbeke's "microscopic corroboration" arm IS a macroscopic-versus-histologic contrast, i.e. one of the candidate's own four states, but varied jointly with plane count — so the published protocol effect sits directly on top of the candidate's gross-to-histologic step and is confounded with it in the only large study of the genre. Separability is now a design requirement, not an assumption.
What would count as an answer: (a) paired-measurement study at >=2 organ sites, protocol fixed, four handling states, reporting the per-step shift distribution with within- and between-observer components; (b) registry computation of probability mass within +/- the measured shift of each cut-point, converted to patients per year with pT handling-determined, heaping modelled; (c) pre-set-margin comparison of prognostic discrimination for stage on fresh versus fixed measurements in an outcome cohort. A small shift relative to threshold spacing, or unchanged discrimination, retires the concern and is equally an answer.

Significance — fecundity: Guard-banded staging (reported pT plus stated conformity risk, ISO 14253 terms); measurand definition for every quantitative CAP item carrying a threshold (DOI, margin distance, Ki67); joint threshold recalibration against a specified measurand rather than an inherited one.
Significance — anchoring: Stage migration and the Will Rogers phenomenon; radiology-pathology size discordance; gross-pathology interobserver reproducibility; terminal-digit preference as a registry data-quality defect; measurement uncertainty in laboratory reporting.
Reason for silence: The proposed mechanisms (vocabulary collision, since "tumour shrinkage" means therapy response; and a four-way split between metrology, epidemiology, pathology and registries with nobody owning the composite) are partly dissolved by Gate 2: there was no silence in the candidate's own field, only a thin per-organ literature without synthesis. The honest residual mechanism covers limb (c) only — no single group owns both handling-controlled measurements and long-term outcomes, and the answer could invalidate the measurement its own field reports. Gate 4 added a second mechanism that is better than either, and sourced: a reporting-convention gap. The papers that measured the shift never published the statistic the question needs, because their purpose made it irrelevant — Tran's stated goal is a correction factor "toward standardization of pathological reporting", and a correction factor needs a central estimate where a misclassification risk needs a variance. The field produced the former for a decade and therefore never produced the input the latter requires, and no systematic review that would have pooled the per-organ studies and exposed the omission was found.
