## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take the polynomial ring over a finite field with a prime number q of elements (q = 2, 3 or 5), and
look only at the monic polynomials of one fixed degree n. There are exactly q^n of them. Line them
up by treating the coefficient list as a base-q numeral — the constant term being the units digit,
the near-leading coefficient the highest digit — so that "the monic of degree n at position p" is a
bijection onto {0, …, q^n − 1}. Some of those monics are irreducible; call two of them *consecutive*
when no third irreducible of that degree sits between them in this lineup. Now fix a monic m of
degree 1, 2 or 3 and ask what residue class mod m each member of a consecutive pair falls into.

**The question:** do consecutive irreducibles land in the *same* class mod m less often than two
independent draws would (that is, is the normalised excess delta(a,a) negative), as Lemke Oliver and
Soundararajan reported for consecutive primes mod q in the ordinary integers; what happens to that
number as n grows with q held fixed; and does it change if the very same irreducibles are re-lined-up
by the mirror-image rule, where the *leading* coefficient becomes the units digit and the constant
term the highest digit?

In one sentence: *is the "consecutive primes rarely repeat their last digit" effect present among the
irreducible polynomials of a fixed degree over a small finite field, and does it depend on which of
two digit-significance conventions is used to say what "consecutive" means?*

## 7. The kill, written out so a verifier can check it in two minutes

**This is my own mathematics, not a source finding.** It is elementary, it depends on no paper, and it
is falsifiable by one small computation, which I name at the end. The record's Scores section and its
"Strongest remaining objection" both instruct the adversary to test exactly this; its presupposition 7
is where the crack is.

Let **m = t^d** with d ∈ {1,2,3}. These are monic polynomials of degree 1, 2 and 3, so they are inside
the record's own declared index set ("each monic m of degree 1, 2 or 3"). For f = t^n + Σ_{i<n} a_i t^i
with n > d,

  f mod t^d = a_0 + a_1 t + … + a_{d−1} t^{d−1},

because t^j ≡ 0 (mod t^d) for every j ≥ d. **The class of f mod m is exactly the tuple
(a_0, …, a_{d−1}).**

**Under order 1** (constant term least significant) the position is p₁ = Σ_{i<n} a_i q^i, so
(a_0,…,a_{d−1}) = p₁ mod q^d. The class is a *bijective* function of position mod q^d — the exact
analogue of n ↦ n mod q^d in Z, and equal class ⟺ the position gap is ≡ 0 (mod q^d). This is the
record's presupposition 7, and for order 1 it is correct (it holds for general m too: writing
f = t^n + t^d G + h with deg h < d, the map h ↦ class is a bijection within each aligned q^d-block).

**Under order 2** — the reformulation's second structured rule, "the reverse digit significance", so
the leading coefficient is the units digit — the position is p₂ = Σ_{i<n} a_i q^{n−1−i}. Then

  (a_0, …, a_{d−1}) = ⌊ p₂ / q^{n−d} ⌋ .

**The class is a function of the d MOST significant digits of the position.** Order 2 therefore sorts
the q^n monics of degree n into **q^d contiguous intervals of length q^{n−d}, each constant in class**.

Every monic irreducible of degree n ≥ 2 has a_0 ≠ 0 (else t divides it), so the irreducibles occupy
exactly the φ(t^d) = (q−1)q^{d−1} intervals with a_0 ≠ 0, each holding about (q/(q−1))·q^{n−d}/n of
them. Consecutive irreducibles therefore lie in **different** classes for at most φ(t^d) − 1 of the
≈ q^n/n − 1 adjacent pairs. Hence the same-class fraction is 1 − O(q^d · n · q^{−n}) and, for every
class a,

  **delta(a, a; t^d, n, q) → φ(t^d) − 1 > 0, exponentially fast in n.**

Concretely: q = 2, m = t² (φ = 2) gives delta → **+1**; q = 3, m = t³ (φ = 18) gives delta → **+17**;
q = 5, m = t³ (φ = 100) gives delta → **+99**.

**Three consequences, and they are what kill the candidate.**

**(i) The second limb is answered in half a page.** The reformulated question asks "does its sign and
magnitude change when the SAME irreducible set is re-ordered by a second structured rule". For
m = t, t², t³ the answer is *yes, the sign flips from negative to positive and the magnitude goes from
O(log n / n) to Θ(1)* — provably, before any computation, in the three lines above.

**(ii) The answer is not a fact about irreducible polynomials.** Nothing in the derivation uses
irreducibility. The same limit delta → φ(m) − 1 holds for **any** subset S of the degree-n monics with
a_0 ≠ 0 on S and |S| → ∞ — the squarefrees, the primitives, a random thinning. So the order-2 value is
a fact about the position-to-residue map of order 2, not about the irreducibles.

**(iii) It is the identical defect the record itself diagnosed, and the reformulation was built around
the swap.** Presupposition 6 rejects the submitted randomised-order control in these words: "delta
must come out near zero whatever the mechanism is; the control is a null calibration of the estimator,
not a mechanism test", and Gate 1's repair substitutes the two-structured-orders comparison on that
ground. But under order 2, **delta must come out near φ(m) − 1 whatever the mechanism is.** Both
"controls" are mechanism-independent. The repair replaced one uninformative comparison with another,
and the record states the repair's presupposition it tested was canonicity of the order on F_q —
it never tested whether the second order's comparison carries information. **Test the clause you
wrote to fix a problem exactly as hard as the clause it replaced.**

**(iv) The record's own presupposition 7 is stated unconditionally and is true of only one of the two
orders it had just declared.** "Within each block of q^{deg(m)} consecutive monics the map to classes
is a bijection" holds for order 1 and fails for order 2, where the map is constant on blocks of
