Question (canonical):
V1 (as intake): Has the lithium isotope composition of the industrially circulating lithium pool drifted measurably as production shifted between brine, hard-rock and recycled feedstocks and as isotopically fractionated lithium from enrichment entered the market, and if so does that drift move the "anthropogenic endmember" beyond the uncertainty assumed by published δ7Li source-apportionment of lithium contamination?
V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted — canonical): For a declared window (index case 2010–2025), construct the production-weighted δ7Li of the commercial lithium pool year by year from (a) feedstock-resolved δ7Li domains and (b) measured per-process Δ7Li for the route each feedstock takes to battery- or reagent-grade product, weighted by public annual production by deposit type and by the recycled share, with uncertainty propagated: (i) what is the year-on-year change Δδ7Li_pool and its interval, and (ii) does substituting the year-appropriate pool value for the anthropogenic endmember of a NAMED published apportionment (index case: effluent endmember 14.5 ± 4.3‰, 1σ, n = 6, Choi et al. 2019) move that study's inferred anthropogenic fraction outside its stated interval?

Alternative formulations:
- Is the anthropogenic lithium isotope signature used as a tracer a constant of the anthropogenic pool, or a moving target set by the supply chain that produced it?
- Do dated commercial lithium products, and datable archives downstream of a steady source, record a secular trend in δ7Li analogous to the Suess effect for carbon?

Motivating gap:
The inverted settled claim is that δ7Li discriminates anthropogenic from natural lithium because the anthropogenic endmember is distinct and stable. The presupposition already fails on the production side: IUPAC/CIAAW withdrew a single standard atomic weight for lithium because commercially supplied lithium is isotopically fractionated by industrial processing, replacing it with the interval 6.938–6.997 (ciaaw.org/lithium.htm; IUPAC technical report doi 10.1515/pac-2017-1002). Qi et al. 1997 found seven of 39 shelf reagents outside natural variation (δ7Li up to +3013‰). Desaulty et al. 2022 then showed that *ordinary* battery-chain lithium carries feedstock- and process-resolved δ7Li differences of 5–25‰, dwarfing analytical precision (±0.4‰, 2σ) and the ±4.3‰ carried by the one apportionment endmember found. Nobody has indexed that pool by time or carried it into an apportionment.

Presuppositions:
1. δ7Li of commercial lithium products varies by feedstock and process by more than analytical precision — ESTABLISHED and quantified (Desaulty et al. 2022: hard-rock domain < +6‰, brine domain > +11.3‰; ion-exchange purification Δ7Li > +8‰ at 95% yield; spodumene→hydroxide Δ7Li = +5.5‰ in one measured pair; reproducibility ±0.4‰ 2σ).
2. The commercial product mix changed materially over the window — ESTABLISHED on the tonnage side (hard-rock share rose sharply after 2017; recycled feedstock now entering), though Desaulty note sourcing is economic with "no preference for hard-rock- or brine-based lithium", which is what makes the mix volatile rather than trending.
3. Dated material exists to measure the drift directly — CONTESTED; the feasibility crux. No archive of δ7Li on dated products was found. The production-weighted route (V2) does not need it, which is why V2 is the canonical form.
4. Published apportionment studies assume a fixed anthropogenic endmember with a stated uncertainty — CONTESTED, and partly FALSIFIED for the index case: Choi et al. 2019 measured its own local effluent endmember contemporaneously (July 2015 campaign) rather than borrowing a literature value. So pool drift biases borrowed-endmember, cross-year and retrospective-archive uses, not contemporaneous locally-calibrated ones. Must still be verified study by study.
5. Lithium reaching the environment derives predominantly from the commercial pool — PLAUSIBLE, UNVERIFIED (geogenic and geothermal Li are large in many catchments; the claim is only needed for the propagation limb).
6. Fractionation during use, wastewater treatment and transport is small or correctable — CONTESTED. Desaulty measured no significant fractionation in cathode synthesis and cell manufacture, and none in carbonate→hydroxide conversion, but nothing downstream of the consumer.
7. Public production statistics can be partitioned by deposit type and recycled share at annual resolution with usable uncertainty — PLAUSIBLE, UNVERIFIED (USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries report country and total, not always deposit type).

What would count as an answer:
A production-weighted δ7Li time series for the commercial pool with a propagated interval, built either (a) from feedstock- and process-resolved δ7Li plus annual production by deposit type and recycled share, or (b) measured directly on products dated by manufacture year and on a datable archive downstream of a source of near-constant provenance; together with recomputation of at least one named published apportionment under the year-appropriate endmember, showing whether the inferred anthropogenic fraction moves outside its stated interval. A null is equally decisive: drift smaller than the endmember uncertainty those studies already carry shows the tracer is robust and closes the question. A third decisive outcome exists and should be pre-declared: drift large on the production side but irrelevant downstream because studies calibrate locally — in which case the answer re-aims the finding at provenance-forensics reference libraries instead of at apportionment.

Significance — consequence:
If yes (drift exceeds the endmember uncertainty): every δ7Li apportionment that borrows a literature anthropogenic endmember becomes year-sensitive, and retrospective archive work — sediment cores, archived sludge, long water-monitoring series — must use a year-appropriate endmember or report an inflated interval. Provenance-forensics reference libraries used for ethical-sourcing and battery-passport claims acquire a vintage, so a 2015-calibrated discriminant may misassign 2025 material. And the drift itself becomes a chronometer: a documented secular δ7Li trend in the anthropogenic flux is a dating handle on environmental Li inputs, the lithium analogue of the Suess effect.
If no (drift smaller than the uncertainty already carried): the tracer is validated as robust at the scale of existing uncertainties, borrowed endmembers are licensed, and a question that the IUPAC atomic-weight withdrawal makes look alarming is closed with a number. Either way the field gains a quantity it does not currently have.
Honest deflation: the index apportionment (P2) measured its own endmember contemporaneously, so a yes would not retract it. The consequence is concentrated on borrowed-endmember, cross-year, retrospective and forensic uses, which is narrower than the intake framing implies.

Significance — audience:
(i) Low-temperature isotope geochemists and hydrologists doing anthropogenic-Li apportionment (the Choi/Vigier and BRGM groups) — would pre-declare a year-matched endmember and a drift term in the mixing-model error budget rather than a single literature value. (ii) Mineral-provenance and traceability groups (BRGM; the EU battery-regulation due-diligence and battery-passport community) — would date their reference libraries and re-measure on a schedule, which P1's own closing remark already asks for. (iii) Chemical metrology (CIAAW, national metrology institutes) — an annual production-weighted value is directly relevant to whether the 6.938–6.997 interval should be reported with a time qualifier. (iv) Water utilities and environmental regulators now beginning to monitor Li, who need to know whether an isotopic source attribution will still read the same way in a decade.

Significance — fecundity:
Is there a measurable lithium Suess effect, and can it date anthropogenic Li horizons in sediment? Does recycled feedstock carry a distinct composite signature, giving an isotopic tracer of recycled content in a cell (a compliance measurement, not just a scientific one)? Can declared supply chains be audited against a time series rather than a static library? Where does the enrichment-derived tail (P3's +3013‰, and its 6Li-depleted complement) actually enter the environment, and is it detectable? Does within-year heterogeneity of the pool exceed between-year drift, which would change the estimand from a mean to a dispersion?

Significance — anchoring:
Touches the IUPAC/CIAAW withdrawal of a single Li standard atomic weight (an acknowledged anomaly with no time-resolved follow-up); P1's explicit open maintenance problem for provenance databases; the unexplained spread between anthropogenic endmember values reported in different catchments and years; and the general isotope-forensics problem — mature for Pb, absent for Li — of an anthropogenic endmember that moves because industry moved.

Reason for silence:
A disciplinary boundary with an incentive gap on each side. The production-side fact lives in chemical metrology (CIAAW, which states an interval and has no mandate to time-index it) and in mineral-provenance forensics, whose application wants a STATIC discriminant library — a time-varying signature is a liability for that use, so the community that holds the feedstock-resolved measurements has a positive disincentive to publish drift, and P1 discharges it as a maintenance footnote. The tracer side lives in low-temperature isotope geochemistry, which calibrates its anthropogenic endmember locally and contemporaneously from effluent and therefore never needs the pool value, so the gap is invisible from there. Add genuine newness of the data: the hard-rock share shifted after 2017, recycled feedstock is entering only now, and the one supply-chain δ7Li dataset is from 2022 — a time series needs a second epoch that only now exists. No taboo is required to explain this silence, and "nobody thought of it" is not being invoked.
