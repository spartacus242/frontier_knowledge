## Restatement in my own words

Take the tokenizers that real shipped multilingual models actually use — at least six of them, picked by a rule written down in advance and frozen at a specific repository commit. Push a fixed, dated, script-labelled pile of multilingual text through each one and back out again. Three questions. (1) How often does the text not come back the same — counted per document and per codepoint, both under strict byte-for-byte identity and under the weaker test "identical once you allow the normalisation the tokenizer itself declares" — and how much of the damage is harmless (a canonically equivalent respelling) versus meaning-changing (a dropped zero-width joiner, a lost combining mark)? (2) Where does the damage sit — which scripts, which orthographic features — and which step of the pipeline caused it (the normaliser, control-character stripping, missing byte fallback, the decoder's whitespace rule)? (3) If you throw away the documents that did not survive the round trip and recompute per-language held-out loss on what is left, how much of one specific published cross-language loss gap moves? In short: is the encoder quietly editing the text we grade models on, and if so, is some of the "this model is bad at Tamil" signal actually "our tokenizer mangles Tamil"?

## What the four opened papers establish

**TokEval (arXiv:2608.18062).** REGIME: population = 46 tokenizer configurations of which 44 are author-trained and 2 are unpinned off-the-shelf references (Mistral-Nemo, LLaMA-3); unit = tokenizer configuration × language, the fidelity metric's unit being a corpus *text*; operating condition = tokenizer-design axes varied, per-language BPB computed on FLORES+; instrument = the TokEval metric suite, §3.4 "Encoding Fidelity"; threshold = strict string identity for exact match, normalised character-level Levenshtein for CER; category = intrinsic tokenizer quality and its correlation with LM behaviour.
PUBLISHED IS NOT PERFORMED, applied: **I cannot name a reconstruction-fidelity number, and I checked for one specifically.** In the served text, exact match and CER are **defined and scheduled only** — Table 1 gives formulas, the metric row glossing it as "Strict round-trip reconstruction rate." (a metric-description cell: it asserts the definition, not a value). The numbers the paper does print nearby are a *different* metric: §5 cites "0.001" versus "0.008 or more" for **Character Split Rate** in Table 10, and aggregate BPB ranges "0.7116 to 0.7185" (val) and "2.618 and 2.657" (FLORES(all)) — averages, not per-language values, and not fidelity.
The one sentence I found that the record did not have, and the scope check on it: §3.4 states **"High exact-match failure with near-zero CER indicates minor systematic changes."** Its opening words scope a *conditional reading rule for the metric pair*; the clause **ASSERTS how to interpret the two metrics jointly** and does **not** assert that any tokenizer in the panel exhibited high exact-match failure, so it supplies no number and must not be read as reporting one. What it does show is that TokEval has already drawn, in CER form, the distinction between damage that is a minor systematic respelling and damage that is not — which is one of the three residues the record claimed as surviving.
NUMERATOR / DENOMINATOR / OVERLAP, both directions. Numerator: texts whose round-trip reconstruction is not exactly the original, causes enumerated as Unicode normalization, UNK substitution, whitespace handling. Denominator: a corpus-level average over **texts** in an unnamed per-language corpus; if that corpus is FLORES+, a "text" is a benchmark **sentence**, not a real-world document. What TokEval's class **misses** that the candidate includes: codepoint grain, criterion 2, the orthographic-feature decomposition, CIs, per-script strata (TokEval stratifies by **byte width**, "Character Split Rate is stratified and reported by byte width (2-, 3-, 4-byte)"), measured mechanism shares, revision-pinned shipped configurations, and limb (d) entirely. What it **includes** that the candidate excludes: 44 of 46 configurations are author-trained artifacts no model shipped with, and its text unit may be a sentence.

**TokSuite (arXiv:2512.20757) — the record's unopened PRIOR 3, now opened.** REGIME: population = **14 preexisting (shipped/off-the-shelf) tokenizers** — ByT5, GPT-2, Llama-3.2, Qwen-3, GPT-4o among them — with one LM trained per tokenizer on shared data/architecture; **revisions not pinned or reported**; unit = tokenizer × perturbation category, with non-English pooled into one "NEN" column; endpoint = relative multiple-choice accuracy drop under perturbation, e.g. Table 1 noise drops **0.21 (NEN) vs 0.15 (EN)**, defined as (Acc_can − Acc_pert)/Acc_can.
It **does not** measure round trip, reconstruction exact match, UNK rate or byte-fallback coverage; Table 2 lists OOV handling categorically per tokenizer (TokenMonster "Ignores Unknowns", mBERT "[UNK]", most others "Byte-fallback"); it reports no per-language loss and recomputes loss on no filtered subset. Appendix A states that NFKC normalization **"this can result in lossy detokenization"** — a description, not a measurement. Scope: that clause ASSERTS the candidate's P1 premise, in a second paper, over a population of shipped tokenizers; it does not deny it and does not quantify it.

**Qtok (arXiv:2410.12989).** REGIME: "13 distinct tokenizers from 58 models", selected from Chatbot Arena by licence and format, "we made the methodological decision to use only tokenizers from official repositories"; **no revision pinning**; unit = vocabulary token; endpoint = vocabulary composition by Unicode script and category. Round trip, losslessness, UNK and tokenizer-applied normalization: **absent**. Its Table 6 "Pure Unicode" column counts byte-like tokens per vocabulary (llama-2/mistral/qwen etc. 256; pythia 185, falcon 170, deepseek 184, llama-3 211, gemma 255, out of 256) — and the read's own caveat applies: **"This checks vocabulary presence, not behavior on input text."** So this bears on incomplete byte coverage at the vocabulary level only and must not be transferred to a round-trip rate. Internal consistency flagged: 13 distinct tokenizers against Table 2 group sizes summing to 60 and Table 1 open-model counts summing to 92, unreconciled.

**Don't Touch My Diacritics (arXiv:2410.24140).** REGIME: case studies, authors' own word ("case studies", "preliminary"); population = a few treebanks and news corpora (Hindi HDT, Arabic BN/PADT, Hebrew, Spanish) plus assorted models/tools; unit = text strings and codepoint sequences; endpoints = Hindi LAS, token counts, qualitative MT correctness, mT5 vocabulary coverage. **No share or rate of documents, sentences or tokens altered by normalization is reported**; Table 2's denominator is **one sample sentence per language** (Arabic LLaMA 62 raw vs 28 stripped; Hebrew mBERT 58 vs 20; Spanish LLaMA 63 vs 62). The only preprocessing-attributed performance figure is Hindi LAS 87.09 → 87.38 from applying NFKC, one run. Round trip / invertibility: **absent**. It does, however, operationalise the orthographic partition the candidate needs, stripping "via removing Mn-category characters".
