Question (canonical): For aliquot sequences under the sum-of-proper-divisors map, is the per-step multiplicative drift conditional on survival equal to the unconditional aliquot constant — does the mean of log(s(N)/N) over iterates N of sequences that have already survived k steps or reached d digits change with k or d, and is any change accounted for by the changing occupancy of Guy-Selfridge drivers among the survivors? Reformulation note: Gate 1's one permitted reformulation was spent pinning the frame to all 499 999 even seeds below 10^6 iterated to termination, a cycle, or a first 100-digit iterate (Bosma 2016's window), one row per iterate labelled d, k, survived/absorbed, driver class; both versions are in the record.
Alternative formulations: (1) Do long-surviving aliquot sequences drift upward more strongly than a random even integer of the same size, and by how much? (2) Is the aliquot constant a stationary drift for the iterated map, or only an average over starting values, and what is the drift in the quasi-stationary distribution of surviving iterates?
Motivating gap: Bosma & Kane average log(s(2n)/2n) over starting values and say in Remark 2.3 that this "needs to be combined with the persistance of drivers as in [7]"; Bosma 2016 computes survival counts by digit threshold and no growth rate. The intake did not know that the per-step drift at fixed k is a published theorem, nor that the survivor-term drift by size and by driver is a published table.
