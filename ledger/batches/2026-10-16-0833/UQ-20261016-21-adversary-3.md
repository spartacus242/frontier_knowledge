## Restatement (my words, not the generator's)

Take published multicentre RCTs whose primary endpoint is produced by, or whose intervention is dosed/titrated
against, a software-containing measuring device that the report names by model (oximeter, CGM, BP monitor, imaging
software, etc.). (i) For what share of them can an outsider prove, from release notes, FDA/EU recall and
adverse-event databases, or by asking the maker, that the manufacturer changed the device's firmware or calibration
algorithm while enrolment was running? (ii) Where such a date exists and patient-level data can be re-run, does the
treatment effect in the old-firmware period differ from the new-firmware period by more than the trial's own smallest
worthwhile difference? Short form: how often are trial endpoints silently re-calibrated mid-trial, can anyone find
out after the fact, and where they can, did it change the answer?

## Technical point used in attacks 1 and 4 (my own arithmetic; scope stated)

Under concurrent randomisation, both arms are exposed to the same mix of device eras in expectation. If a
calibration revision applies an identical additive shift c to every measurement in era 2, then within era 2
E[Y+c | Tx] - E[Y+c | Ctrl] = E[Y | Tx] - E[Y | Ctrl]: the between-arm mean difference is unchanged and the era
interaction is exactly zero. If the revision is a pure gain change g, the era-2 effect is g times the era-1 effect,
so the interaction equals (g-1) times the effect — for a device still "within the recommended standards for accuracy"
(Johnston 2011 as quoted in the record) this is a small fraction of the effect, not an MCID-sized difference.
SCOPE: this holds for continuous outcomes analysed as between-arm differences with the shift applied identically to
both arms. It does NOT hold (and an MCID-sized interaction is mechanistically possible) when (a) the device TITRATES
the intervention, so the revision changes the exposure actually delivered — the anchor case, where the outcome
(death) was not measured by the device at all; (b) the outcome is a threshold/binary derived from the measurement
(non-differential misclassification differing by era); or (c) the artefact is non-linear over part of the range
(Johnston 2011: "Values above 87% were elevated by up to 2%" — a compression confined to 87-90%). I cite no external
theorem for this; it is an identity plus a scope statement, labelled as my own derivation.

Single strongest killing point: limb (ii) is only mechanistically capable of an MCID-sized era interaction for

predetermined null across most of its corpus and its "incommensurable measurements" framing is a break-in-series
worry transplanted into a design that neutralises it. The anchor itself is a titration case whose outcome (death)
the device never measured.
