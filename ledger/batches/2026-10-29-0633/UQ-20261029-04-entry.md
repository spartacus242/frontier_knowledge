Question (canonical): In a thin crystal containing isolated substitutional impurities or vacancies of one declared species, measured by multislice electron ptychography at two or more specimen temperatures on the same individual defects: (a) does the per-atom mean-square thermal displacement u²(n) at coordination shell n from the defect differ from the value predicted by harmonic lattice dynamics for the same defective supercell (not the pristine host); (b) does R(n,T) = u²_meas(n,T)/u²_harm(n,T) vary with temperature — which a purely harmonic mass-and-force-constant perturbation forbids and a locally anharmonic or resonant defect mode requires; and (c) over how many shells n does any departure decay to the pristine-host value? The reformulation fixed the null from "linear-in-T" to the computed harmonic u²(T) curve, and from the pristine host to the defective supercell; both repairs are the generator's own algebra, labelled as such in the record, and neither changed scope.]

Alternative formulations: (V1, as submitted at intake) Around an isolated substitutional impurity or vacancy in a thin crystal, does the per-atom mean-square thermal displacement measured by MEP deviate from the harmonic-lattice prediction, does its temperature dependence on the SAME defect depart from linear-in-T as a locally anharmonic or resonant mode requires, and over how many shells does the departure decay? · (2) Is the lattice locally anharmonic around a single point defect, and how far does that anharmonicity reach? · (3) Does one impurity atom soften its neighbours' vibrations beyond the harmonic mass-and-force-constant change, and does the softening grow with temperature?
