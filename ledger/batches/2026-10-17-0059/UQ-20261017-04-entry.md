The claim check. Half one of the kill holds, with its wording corrected. Pallikara, Kayastha, Skelton & Whalley, "The physical significance of imaginary phonon modes in crystals", Electronic Structure 4, 033002 (2022), arXiv:2203.01244 — the entry's citation named the wrong fourth author and the wrong journal, and both are corrected here — states that "the forces are equal to zero, so that the linear term in u vanishes", that "the forces on the atoms in the initial structure are assumed to be zero", and that "a well-converged geometry optimisation is a prerequisite", adding that force-convergence criteria may need to be tighter than usual. What that supports is that the harmonic interpretation presupposes stationarity and that a converged optimisation is a prerequisite; it does not support the stronger "not well defined", which is struck. Half two holds for the two methods it was checked against, and only as published: TDEP (Hellman & Abrikosov, arXiv:1303.1145) starts from "Guess equilibrium positions, usually the ideal lattice positions" and "These forces are then used to move the atoms in a steepest descent scheme towards equilibrium positions", iterating to convergence; SSCHA (Errea, Calandra & Mauri, arXiv:1311.3083) has a trial Hamiltonian that "contains two group of parameters: the R_eq equilibrium positions and the Phi force-constants matrix" and "The minimization is performed with respect to all the free parameters". So both optimise the reference positions by construction. SCAILD could not be read, and code-level options for holding positions fixed were not checked, so the general claim that NO effective-harmonic method can flag a structure held at its deposited geometry is unsupported and is recorded as such.
