## Question (canonical)

**v1 (intake).** Given measured intracellular protein concentrations and the working concentrations and detection limits of the assays used to map interactomes, what region of the (dissociation constant × abundance) plane is invisible to every assay in use, and what share of the cell's plausible interactions falls in it?

**v2 (Gate 1 reformulation — the one permitted reformulation, for well-posedness only; both versions recorded).** For each assay class in current interactome-mapping use (HT-Y2H, AP-MS of lysates, binary recomposition assays of the MAPPIT/LUMIER/PCA type, proximity labelling, in-cell crosslinking MS), taking each assay's working concentration regime and detection limit from published protocols as a bounded interval and taking detectability to mean that a law-of-mass-action equilibrium complex fraction exceeds the assay's limit of detection at that working concentration: (a) where does each assay's detectability boundary lie in the (Kd, intracellular concentration) plane for one declared cell type, with the boundary reported as an interval; (b) what is the union of those regions, and hence the complement — the region invisible to the whole toolkit; (c) what share of pairs falls in the complement, under two explicitly declared denominators — (i) a declared reference interaction set whose members have independently measured affinities, and (ii) all pairs among proteins expressed in that cell type at measured copy numbers, weighted by a stated prior over Kd; and (d) restated under the window, what does at least one published "fraction of the interactome mapped" figure become?

v2 is the version graded below. It fixes v1's two vague limbs: the undeclared denominator behind "share of the cell's plausible interactions" and the unstated detectability criterion.

## Alternative formulations

1. What is the detection window of the interactome-mapping toolkit, expressed in Kd and copies per cell, and how much of the interactome lies outside it?
2. Is the published "fraction of the human interactome mapped" an estimate of coverage of the interactome, or of coverage of the part of it the assays can reach?
3. (metrology phrasing) What is the working range of the interactome-mapping method, in the analyte's own units, and what is the method's coverage error as distinct from its sampling error?

## Motivating gap

A how-dense, why-empty topic: the field has a large methodological literature (assay development, confidence scoring, positive/random reference sets, assay-complementarity analyses, headline coverage statements) and the sensitivity figure that feeds coverage estimates is obtained empirically, by running each assay against a reference positive set built from literature-curated — i.e. previously detected — interactions. The sensitivity figure therefore inherits the detection window rather than measuring it, and a coverage estimate built on it has no slot for what lies outside. Two things make the window computable rather than estimable: absolute protein copy numbers per cell are now available for human cell lines and several model organisms, and assay working concentrations and limits are documented in protocols.

**Correction forced by Gate 2 — the intake's strongest claim is overstated and is struck.** "Essentially no literature asking what its instruments can see" is false as written. Hein et al. 2015 (Cell) plots interactions on a stoichiometry × interactor-abundance plane, reads the plot's three borders as dynamic-range limits of the method and of the proteome, states "We observe only part of the entire interactome directly", and gives a figure for the number of *detectable* interactions in HeLa. A restricted sibling of this question has been asked and partially answered for one assay class, in that assay's own units. What survives is the narrower gap: no source opened here computes the boundary in the cell's units (Kd and concentration) from protocol parameters, none treats the union over assay classes, and none restates a published coverage figure conditioned on a window.

## Presuppositions

1. Each assay class has a working concentration regime and detection limit recoverable from published protocols. **PARTLY CONTESTED** — effective concentrations in Y2H nuclei and in AP-MS lysates are poorly documented; v2 therefore requires bounds-as-intervals and permits restriction to assay classes whose regimes can be bounded.
2. Absolute abundance measurements can be converted to concentrations with a defensible volume. **CONTESTED** — compartmentalisation and condensates make cell-average concentration the wrong quantity for some proteins; v2 requires a stated compartment model plus sensitivity analysis.
3. A law-of-mass-action equilibrium occupancy model is adequate to define detectability. **CONTESTED** — avidity, cooperativity, ligand depletion and frankly non-equilibrium assays break it; the output is a first-order map and must be declared as such. Note that the ligand-depletion and tight-binding bounds on what a binding measurement can resolve are themselves standard and sourced (Jarmoskaite et al. 2020), so the failure modes are nameable rather than unknown.
4. Reference positive sets used to calibrate assay sensitivity are themselves drawn from previously detected interactions. **VERIFIED at Gate 2 from the framework's own paper** — Venkatesan et al. 2009 built hsPRS-v1 by narrowing 17,297 literature-curated pairs from five databases to 188 and then to 107 multiply-published pairs; the random reference set is random ORFeome pairs with known interactions excluded, and the paper concedes "It is impossible to generate a set of negative interacting pairs with absolute confidence." The circularity limb stands.
5. The invisible band is non-empty — real interactions exist there. **CONTESTED, and partly what the question asks.** Partially supported, not established: Hein et al. report "Weak interactions have frequently gone undetected in interactome studies" and that "the prevalence of weak interactions is likely to be even more pronounced and their relevance vastly underappreciated" — but that is an inference from substoichiometric recovery, not independent placement of interactions inside a computed region. The limb must be answered by structural, genetic, in-cell crosslinking or proximity-labelling evidence located inside the computed region, and my searching did not find such a placement already done.
6. "The interactome" is a well-enough defined population that a share of it can be reported. **CONTESTED at intake; repaired in v2** by declaring two denominators explicitly.

## What would count as an answer

A computed map of the (Kd, abundance) plane, marked by which assay classes can detect a pair in each region, with the invisible region's boundary and the uncertainty on it; the share of pairs falling inside it under both declared denominators; and at least one published interactome-coverage figure restated conditional on the window (e.g. Venkatesan et al.'s ~8%-of-~130,000 recomputed as a share of the reachable region rather than of the interactome). A null answer stands on its own: if the union of assay regions covers substantially the whole plausible plane, published coverage statements are sound as stated and the silence was justified. The answer is falsifiable in both directions and the plane's axes are measurable quantities, so the question cannot be answered either way by rhetoric.

## Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, with sources opened)

**P1 — Hein MY et al., "A Human Interactome in Three Quantitative Dimensions Organized by Stoichiometries and Abundances", Cell 2015 (read in full; PDF mirror https://ir.xtbg.ac.cn/handle/353005/9804).** Closest prior found.
- Unit of analysis: bait–prey pair measured by quantitative AP-MS (1,125 GFP-tagged baits in HeLa BAC lines; 28,504 interactions, 5,462 proteins).
- Endpoint: three measured per-interaction quantities — enrichment significance at 1%/5% FDR, interaction stoichiometry relative to bait, and interactor cellular copy number.
- Reference/comparator: per-protein background sample sets (Welch's t-test), reciprocal pull-downs, PAM-SILAC label swaps, CORUM complexes.
- Estimand: the joint distribution of interactions in stoichiometry × abundance space, and the implied size of the AP-MS-**detectable** interactome in HeLa (80,000–180,000), with the plot's borders read as "dynamic range limits in the interactome (left border)", "in the proteome (top border)" and "the stoichiometry limit imposed by the relative cellular protein abundances (diagonal)".

**P2 — Venkatesan K et al., "An empirical framework for binary interactome mapping", Nat Methods 2009 (read in full; https://rothlab.csb.pitt.edu/publications/Venkatesan_NatMethods_2009.pdf).**
- Unit of analysis: assay × reference-set pair, aggregated to the interactome.
- Endpoint: four empirical parameters — screening completeness, assay sensitivity, sampling sensitivity, precision — e.g. Y2H-CCSB sensitivity 17%, MAPPIT 21%.
- Reference/comparator: hsPRS-v1 (literature-curated, multiply-published pairs) against hsRRS-v1 (random ORFeome pairs).
- Estimand: the size of the human binary interactome (~130,000, 95% CI ~73,500–199,700) and the mapped fraction (~8%). Explicitly contains **no** Kd, no copy numbers, and no concentration-based detection limit; "concentration" appears once, in the definition of the interactome as interactions that "can occur at a range of physiologically relevant protein concentrations".

**P3 — Ivanic J et al., "Influence of Protein Abundance on High-Throughput Protein–Protein Interaction Detection", PLoS ONE 4(5):e5815, 2009 (read in full; publisher route journals.plos.org).**
- Unit of analysis: individual protein (yeast, E. coli), each with a degree and an abundance.
- Endpoint: log(degree) vs log2(abundance) association; secondarily essential vs non-essential degree distributions (KS tests).
- Reference/comparator: Y2H networks vs affinity-purification (TAP, HMS-PCI) networks; raw datasets vs high-confidence subsets.
- Estimand: strength and significance of association (Pearson/Spearman with P-values). Confirmed to contain no detection threshold in copies/cell, no Kd, no undetectable region, and no coverage fraction.

**P4 (fourth, abstract only — not graded as a duplicate) — Zhang J & Shakhnovich EI, "Sensitivity dependent model of protein–protein interaction networks", arXiv q-bio/0612029 (2006, rev. 2008).** Unit: the network's degree sequence; endpoint: the apparent degree exponent γ; comparator: observed PPI degree distributions; estimand: γ's dependence on "experimental protein concentrations or other factors that affect how sensitively interactions are detected". So the idea that an assay's sensitivity acts as a threshold in the physical binding/concentration plane, and shapes what the map looks like, is already in the biophysics literature — but as a prediction about a scaling exponent, not a detection-window map or a share. Full text not read; nothing is inferred from its silences.

## Novelty — how this differs from each

**vs P1 (Hein 2015), the close neighbour.** Four differences, each load-bearing. (i) **Plane.** Hein's axes are interaction stoichiometry (an assay-internal observable) and interactor abundance; the candidate's axes are Kd and intracellular concentration — the cell's own units — and the candidate derives the boundary from protocol working concentration and LOD plus an occupancy model rather than reading it off the edge of a data cloud. Hein states no Kd and no limit in copies/cell or concentration. (ii) **Scope.** Hein characterises one assay class; the candidate's object is the **union** over assay classes and hence the complement that no assay reaches — which is exactly the quantity a coverage statement needs and which no single-assay study can produce. (iii) **Direction of inference.** Hein's "80,000–180,000 detectable interactions" is an extrapolation from detected data, so like Venkatesan's sensitivity it is estimated inside the window; the candidate computes the window from parameters measured outside the interactome literature. (iv) **Coverage restatement.** Hein does not re-express any published mapped-fraction figure as coverage-of-the-reachable-region; the candidate's limb (d) does. What Hein *does* do is enough to strike the intake's "no literature" claim and to cap novelty below High.

**vs P2 (Venkatesan 2009).** Same object (interactome coverage), opposite instrument. Venkatesan's estimand is a mapped fraction computed from empirically measured assay sensitivity; the candidate's estimand is the boundary of what any assay can reach, in physical units, and the mapped fraction conditioned on it. P2 is the thing the candidate proposes to re-interpret, and reading it confirms both that it does not contain the candidate's analysis and that its calibration set is literature-curated — i.e. it supplies the candidate's premise rather than its answer.

**vs P3 (Ivanic 2009).** Adjacent object, one axis, correlational. P3 asks whether abundance predicts measured degree; the candidate asks where in (Kd, abundance) space detection becomes impossible and how much of a declared population lies there. A correlation of degree with abundance is consistent with many windows and implies none; it also says nothing about affinity. Treating P3 as prior art on the candidate would be the "adjacent object" error this ledger has made before.

## Significance — consequence

If a large region is invisible to the whole toolkit: every published "fraction of the interactome mapped" becomes a statement about coverage of the reachable region, and the field's central completeness number needs relabelling rather than refining — a coverage-error problem, not a sampling-error problem, in the survey-methodology distinction the field's statements blur. Assay-development priorities change from raising throughput inside the window to extending the window, with a named target region. Network statistics computed on interactome maps (degree distributions, hub enrichment, disease-module significance) acquire a physically specified missingness model, which is a different and stronger object than the uniform-sampling assumptions usually used. If the union of assay regions substantially covers the plausible plane, the answer is a clean null: coverage statements are sound as stated, the inherited-sensitivity circularity is harmless in practice, and the silence was justified — which is itself worth having in print, since nobody has shown it.

## Significance — audience

(i) Interactome-mapping consortia and their coverage arithmetic (CCSB/HuRI lineage; the quantitative AP-MS groups) — they would attach a window to their published completeness figures or defend them against one. (ii) Assay developers — a computed invisible region is a specification document for the next method, in the units a biophysicist can design against. (iii) Database curators and benchmark builders (BioGRID, IntAct/IMEx, the PRS/RRS tradition) — a window implies the reference positive set is a sample of the reachable region, which bears directly on how confidence scores and negative sets should be built. (iv) Machine-learning PPI prediction — models trained on detected positives inherit the window as label bias; a computed window converts "our model generalises poorly" into a quantified domain-shift term. This audience exists and is already arguing about benchmark bias, so it is not an invented constituency.

## Significance — fecundity

Newly askable: what does the interactome's degree distribution look like after correction for a physically specified window (which is P4's question with the threshold measured rather than fitted)? Does the enrichment of disease genes in network hubs survive window correction, or is it an abundance artefact? Which structurally or genetically evidenced interactions sit inside the invisible region, and do they share properties (IDR-mediated, avidity-driven, condensate-resident)? Can a principled negative set be built from the *visible* region only, so that non-detection there is informative? And what is the window of a *predictor* — the analogous question for computational PPI methods.

## Significance — anchoring

Touches the interactome-completeness problem directly (P2); the long-standing low overlap between datasets from different assays, which a window model would partly explain as geometry rather than noise; the "weak and transient interactions are under-appreciated" thread that P1 states but does not quantify; the condensate/compartment problem, which enters as the contested concentration presupposition rather than as a side issue; and the benchmark-bias problem in structure-prediction-era PPI inference.

## Reason for silence

A disciplinary boundary compounded by an instrument that cannot see its own blind spot. Interactome mapping descends from genetics and mass spectrometry, where "sensitivity" is operationalised as a hit rate against a curated reference set; the quantities needed to compute a window — Kd, intracellular concentration, limit of detection at a stated working concentration — belong to biophysics, pharmacology and analytical metrology, which own the *method* but not the data. Because the field's calibration instrument (a literature-curated positive reference set, verified above in P2) is assembled from previously detected pairs, it is structurally incapable of sampling the region outside the window, so the quantity has no slot in the field's accounting and its absence produces no visible anomaly: the framework returns a sensitivity number either way. Two enabling changes are recent — absolute copy numbers per cell line, and the quantitative AP-MS that let P1 see method-set borders at all — which is why the restricted sibling appeared in 2015 and the general form still has not. "No one thought of it" is not the mechanism; the mechanism is that the field's measurement of its own sensitivity is taken inside the window, in units that cannot express where the window ends.
