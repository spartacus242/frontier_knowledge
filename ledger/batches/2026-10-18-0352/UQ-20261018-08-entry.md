Domain(s): Other STEM — fire safety engineering and building-code policy (target field; method from comparative-effectiveness epidemiology / ex-post regulatory evaluation). Source method: cross-field transplant.

Question (canonical): In a jurisdiction holding a retrievable register of performance-based ("alternative solution" / "performance solution" / equivalency) fire-safety approvals joinable to individual buildings, and with frame, covariate set and non-inferiority margin declared before any data pull: (A) for what fraction of buildings in the frame can design pathway be recovered, and how many code-relevant fire incidents accrue to the performance-based stratum, and (B) conditional on (A) clearing a declared power threshold, among buildings in which a fire actually occurred, does the performance-based stratum differ from otherwise-comparable prescriptively designed buildings — matched or weighted on occupancy class, height, construction era and suppression provision — in realized engineering outcomes, with flame and smoke spread beyond the compartment of origin as the primary endpoint and structural damage and casualties secondary?
(V2, the one Gate 1 reformulation. V1, as intaken: "Among buildings in which a fire has actually occurred, do those designed by performance-based fire engineering differ from otherwise-comparable prescriptively designed buildings in realized engineering outcomes — fire and smoke spread beyond the compartment of origin, and structural damage — once occupancy, height, era and suppression provision are matched?" V2 fixes the frame, makes the margin a pre-declaration requirement, and promotes recoverability to limb (A) so the question is answerable whichever way the records fall.)

Alternative formulations:
- Has performance-based fire design ever been evaluated against prescriptive design on field outcomes, rather than on design-stage modelling and cost?
- Treating design pathway as the exposure and the fire incident as the outcome, what is the effect of performance-based design on compartmentation failure in real fires, and can the regime's claim of equivalent safety be tested rather than assumed?

Motivating gap: Performance-based fire design has been permitted for ~30 years on an explicit claim of equivalent safety at lower cost. The question comparative-effectiveness research asks of any alternative regime — does it deliver equivalent outcomes in the population that actually experiences the event? — appears never to have been asked of it with building-level incident data. The field's evidential culture is the verification calculation, the full-scale test and the life-cycle cost model.

Presuppositions:
1. Design pathway is recoverable per building from approval records. CONTESTED — the principal feasibility risk and plausibly the reason for silence. Adversary 2 opened the Victorian BPC/VBA 2024 documentation audit: pathway is recorded per audited permit ("what compliance solution was used, e.g., DtS, performance solution, or a combination of these") but "practitioners are currently not required to lodge this documentation with the VBA" and "the VBA is not a central repository of information". No jurisdiction found with a building-linked queryable register.
2. Incidents linkable to building with spread-extent and damage fields. PARTLY ESTABLISHED — NFIRS and equivalents record spread extent; P1 demonstrates inspector-graded smoke/fire spread collectable at scale.
3. Enough incidents in performance-based buildings to estimate an effect. CONTESTED — hence the power threshold in limb (A).
4. Confounding addressable with recorded covariates. CONTESTED, deepest objection: pathway is chosen BECAUSE the building is atypical (confounding by indication). V2 requires E-value sensitivity analysis; it does not claim identification.
5. A defensible estimand exists. FLAGGED, addressed in V2 by requiring a pre-declared non-inferiority margin.
6. Outcome fields not recorded differentially in performance-based buildings. CONTESTED — large engineered buildings draw investigation teams; needs a measurement-equivalence check or negative-control endpoint.

What would count as an answer: a matched or weighted comparison on a frame and non-inferiority margin declared before any data pull, primary endpoint spread beyond the compartment of origin, with a quantified unmeasured-confounding sensitivity analysis and a measurement-equivalence check; OR a documented demonstration that pathway cannot be recovered for enough buildings to support any comparison — itself an answer, establishing that a thirty-year regime has been unauditable on outcomes by construction, and naming the record-keeping change that would make it auditable.

P1. Hammann et al., "Evaluating 900 Potentially Harming Fires in Germany: Is the Prescriptive Building Code Effective?", Fire Technology 2024;60(3):2041-2065 — link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10694-024-01560-6 (record at portal.fis.tum.de, read twice independently). P2. Significance — consequence: If limb (B) runs and non-inferiority holds, a thirty-year regime acquires its first field evidence for a claim it has asserted from models. If non-inferiority fails, a quantified excess in compartmentation failure attaches to a design pathway still being approved. If limb (A) fails, that is not a null but a governance finding: the regime has been unauditable on outcomes by construction, so no incident, inquiry or statistical series since the 1990s could have detected a systematic difference, and the fix is a recorded field rather than a research programme.

Significance — audience: Code bodies with a live decision on pathway scope — ICC, NFPA, ABCB (performance-requirement architecture and the Fire Safety Verification Method), MBIE, the Nordic authorities, and the UK BSR, which already escalates fires in fire-engineered buildings without recording the route. Concretely evidenced rather than invoked: the Victorian regulator is already spending audit resources on this pathway (13 FERs, ~100 performance solutions, 5-10% inadequately developed) with no outcome measure anywhere, and its own non-lodgement finding motivates the cheap action the question names. Also fire services and incident-data custodians, SFPE and the verification-method community (which would gain the acceptance criteria P3 found missing), and insurers, who price engineered buildings with no pathway-stratified loss series.

Significance — fecundity: Once pathway is a joinable exposure: does the design-stage margin in a submitted verification predict realized spread; do particular alternative solutions (atrium smoke exhaust, extended travel distances, ratings traded against suppression) carry distinct field signatures; do real fires in engineered buildings fall inside the design fire scenarios that approved them — the empirical calibration fire safety engineering has never had; does in-service drift from the approved performance basis show up in outcomes; and does the differential-investigation effect bias every existing spread statistic by building size.

Significance — anchoring: The equivalency assumption licensing the whole performance-based regime; P3's finding that no acceptance criteria were ever stated; P1's result that flame spread is controlled while smoke remains the threat, which makes smoke spread the discriminating endpoint; the ICC/Meacham programme to put a risk basis under performance objectives; Shergold-Weir Recs 12 and 14 on a central building-information database and on specifying in occupancy certificates where performance solutions were used; the post-Grenfell question of design-pathway traceability; and ex-post evaluation of performance-based regulation generally, where the NZ weathertightness precedent is the cautionary case.

Reason for silence: A disciplinary boundary reinforced by a records gap and a liability incentive — sharpened by both adversaries, not merely asserted. (a) The exposure field did not exist in approval records: Shergold-Weir Rec 14 (2018) is an instruction to create it, so the cohort form of the question was unaskable. (b) Where regulators do ask about pathway, they ask it as a documentation-quality question ("what compliance solution was used, e.g., DtS, performance solution, or a combination of these") with the records explicitly not lodged centrally, or as selective single-incident escalation (P4, self-described as biased). (c) P3 shows the regime never fixed the quantity that would constitute equivalence, so there is no custodian of the question. (d) The strongest positive evidence of structural silence is P1: a team inspected 900 fires, rendered a verdict on the prescriptive code, and recommended a performance-based code without asking how the buildings already approved that way had fared.

Not 3/3 — the margin's value cannot be fixed by the question alone, and under confounding by indication limb (B)'s estimand is conditional rather than secured; both adversaries independently found the two-arm exposure mis-specified against the records.)

Strongest remaining objection: the two-arm exposure does not exist in the records. Both adversaries, independently and from opened regulator sources, found that performance solutions are approved for parts of buildings inside otherwise deemed-to-satisfy designs — Shergold-Weir Rec 14's "the circumstances in which performance solutions have been used and for what purpose", and the Victorian audit's "DtS, performance solution, or a combination of these" with ~100 performance solutions across 13 fire engineering reports — so limb (B)'s "performance-based stratum vs otherwise-comparable prescriptively designed buildings" is a contrast between heterogeneous bundles whose traded clauses mostly do not bear on compartmentation, which dilutes any real effect and compounds the confounding by indication it is already exposed to. This is a mis-specification, not a refutation: it is why the entry is Provisional rather than Validated or Rejected.
