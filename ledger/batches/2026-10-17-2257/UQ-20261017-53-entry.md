Domain(s): Biology — transcriptomics / genome annotation (target field: long-read and reference-annotation community). Method transplanted from statistical ecology (unseen-species richness estimation). Source method: cross-field transplant.

Question (canonical): For a pre-declared gene set and a pre-declared isoform-individuation rule (splice-junction-chain identity at a declared 5'/3' tolerance), treating each of >= 2 independent deep long-read libraries per tissue as an incomplete sample of a gene's distinct spliced-isoform classes: (a) what does a false-positive-aware richness estimator (coverage-based rarefaction/extrapolation, Chao/Good-Turing extended with a contamination term calibrated per read class from spike-ins and technical replicates) give for the number of unobserved REAL isoforms per gene, with intervals; (b) does its prediction of how many new isoforms one further independent library reveals match a held-out library; and (c) is the fitted per-gene asymptote finite and depth-stable across all admissible contamination models, or does no admissible model close the curve — in which case "isoforms per gene" is a depth-indexed quantity, not an estimand? (V2; V1 and the Gate 1 reformulation rationale in batches/2026-10-17-2257/UQ-20261017-53-record.md)

Alternative formulations:
- Is "the number of isoforms of a gene" an estimable quantity with a finite asymptote, or an unbounded function of sequencing depth?
- What is the unseen-isoform richness of a human gene once single-observation reads are partitioned into genuinely rare isoforms and technical artefacts?

Motivating gap: Long-read studies keep adding novel isoforms dominated by single-observation transcripts and the discovery curve does not visibly close; the field's own 2025 review (Monzó, Frankish & Conesa, Genome Research 35:583, opened) documents the instability, reports a SIRV-derived artefact share of "between 10% and 15% of the sequencing output" that "only represents a possible upper limit", and contains no richness estimator, rarefaction, asymptote or unobserved count. Statistical ecology solves the structurally identical problem, but its estimators assume singletons are rare-but-real, which a separately measurable technical process (RT template switching, intrapriming, degraded 5' ends) falsifies here. The transplant was already made in this field at Hill order q=1 (Schertzer et al., per-gene isoform "perplexity") and stopped there.

Presuppositions: (1) isoforms individuable at a declared tolerance — CONTESTED, pre-declared; (2) per-read-class technical FP rate independently estimable from spike-ins/replicates — LOAD-BEARING, only bound-identified in opened sources; (3) reads near-independent after duplicate handling — CONTESTED for cDNA; (4) real per-gene repertoire finite — NOT ASSUMED, it is limb (c); (5) "real" definable without function, operationalised as reproducible across independent libraries and donors — DECLARED, and the point on which Gate 4 killed the entry; (6) richness estimators informative at per-gene sample sizes — SMALL-SAMPLE RISK; (7) enough independent deep libraries per tissue for a held-out test — unverified.

What would count as an answer: fitted coverage curves plus per-gene unobserved-real-isoform counts with intervals under a pre-declared contamination model calibrated from spike-ins and technical replicates, across >= 2 independent deep datasets per tissue, PLUS a held-out library test of the predicted new-isoform count. Answered either by curves that close (finite, depth-stable per-gene asymptote) or by a demonstration that none closes under any admissible contamination model (so annotation must report a depth-indexed quantity). Both branches reportable.

If none closes, "N isoforms per gene" is not an estimand and annotation resources, benchmarks and cross-tissue diversity comparisons are reporting a function of their own depth. Not weighed in the verdict: adversaries 1 and 3 both note the negative branch is already the field's stated working position.
