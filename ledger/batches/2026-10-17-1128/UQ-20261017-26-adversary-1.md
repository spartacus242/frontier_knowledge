## Restatement (my own words)

Take one real serving engine (vLLM, say), fix weights, dtype, temperature and engine version, and flip exactly one switch: speculative decoding on or off. The engine documents the switch as lossless. The candidate asks: does the distribution of completions the server actually emits move by more than it already wanders between two spec-OFF runs that differ only in which other requests happened to share the batch? Report that excess with a confidence interval and say whether it is as big as (a) one quantization step on the same hardware and (b) the smallest model-vs-model gap the field is willing to publish as a result. The candidate concedes that the speculative target pass has a different tensor shape and so can never be shape-matched; "matching" means request-level only.

The record's Gate 2 ground is that the question is already asked (arXiv:2607.17283 on a CPU float32 toy implementation; a dev.to post on vLLM with 1.2% greedy mismatch). The record itself flags that if A is judged off-target, the rejection rests on one blog post. I treated that as a prior, not a verdict, and ran the eight attacks.
