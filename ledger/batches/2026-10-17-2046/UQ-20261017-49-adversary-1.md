## Restatement (my own words)

Take one of the standard "is this web page worth training on" classifiers (FineWeb-Edu's educational scorer, DCLM's fastText, Nemotron-CC's ensemble), run it over one dated Common Crawl dump at its default cutoff, and ask: is the proportion of LLM-written documents in the KEPT pile higher than in the raw dump, by what factor, with the detector's false-positive/false-negative rates measured on hand-labelled samples drawn separately from the kept and the discarded piles and propagated into an interval — and does the factor rise as the cutoff is tightened on the same classifier? Secondary framing: the model-collapse literature parameterises risk by a synthetic fraction; is the post-filter fraction (the one models actually train on) larger than the web-level one?

## Own arithmetic (labelled; carries the paper's unit mismatch)
- Rogan–Gladen on the headline token share with the paper's cited Se = 1 − 0.0199 = 0.9801, Sp = 1 − 0.0005 = 0.9995: p = (0.275 + 0.9995 − 1)/(0.9801 + 0.9995 − 1) = 0.2745/0.9796 ≈ 0.280. The correction moves 27.5% to ~28.0%. MY ARITHMETIC; the paper prints no correction and no interval; the 0.05%/1.99% are the vendor's cited benchmark figures, not kept-stratum figures, and the 0.062% on 2021 crawls is an upper bound on FPR only for pre-ChatGPT text.
- Document-level enrichment E from the two pass rates needs the pre-filter document base rate p, which the paper does not print; the record back-solved p ≈ 0.142 from a TOKEN share, so its E ≈ 1.9 (FineWeb) and ≈ 4.4 (DCLM) are indicative only. What is solid without arithmetic is the sign: both pass-rate ratios are > 1 by a wide margin.
