Question (canonical — ORIGINAL, as submitted):
Is the failure of Williamson quadruples at the odd orders where exhaustive search has found none explained by an invariant of the order n, drawn from a list declared in advance, and does that invariant predict further failures at odd orders not yet searched?

Question (canonical — REFORMULATED, Gate 1, the one permitted reformulation; substance unchanged, computability clause repaired):
Among the pre-declared invariants (i)-(v) below, each a function of the odd order n alone and each actually computable at every odd n <= 100, does any take disjoint value sets on the Williamson-realisable odd orders and the four non-realisable ones (35, 47, 53, 59), and what does the separating invariant, if one exists, predict at 65 and 67 — the smallest odd orders whose Williamson existence is currently unknown?

Alternative formulations:
- Why does the first failing odd order admit no four symmetric circulant +-1 matrices whose squares sum to 4n times the identity, when every smaller odd order does — is there an arithmetic obstruction, or only a counting accident?
- Among a pre-declared list of invariants computable from n alone, does any separate the Williamson-realisable odd orders from the non-realisable ones, and what does it predict next?

Motivating gap:
Williamson's conjecture (quadruples exist at every odd order) is false. The failures are recorded by exhaustive computation and, in the three papers I opened at full text, never explained. Bright, Kotsireas and Ganesh state their aim as "increase the bounds to which Williamson matrices have been enumerated"; a dedicated term sweep of that paper (see Novelty) finds no occurrence of "explanation", "pattern", "predict", "scarce" or "rare" anywhere in it, and on order 35 it says only that it is "the smallest order for which Williamson matrices do not exist", with no reason given. The two 2026 papers respond to the failures by weakening the object (near-Williamson, complex Hadamard) rather than by asking what distinguishes the failing orders. Because a Williamson quadruple at odd n yields a Hadamard matrix of order 4n, an invariant explaining the failures bears directly on the Hadamard conjecture's construction supply.

Significance — consequence:
If YES — some member of (i)-(v) separates and predicts: exhaustive search is replaced, at least in part, by a predictive filter over orders, and the SAT+CAS pipeline already consumes exactly this kind of arithmetic input as pruning (P1's PSD test, compression and mod-4 corollaries), so the result is immediately usable rather than merely interesting. Because a quadruple at odd n gives a Hadamard matrix of order 4n, a predictable infinite family of failures would mark the Williamson route as known-dead there and redirect effort to the generalisations two groups are already building (near-Williamson, 8-Williamson, G-matrices).
If NO — every member takes overlapping values: the explanation relocates from arithmetic of n to the structure of the quadruples, and the counting-accident branch becomes the live hypothesis. That branch has its own consequence: it predicts further sporadic zeros and invites a bound on their expected number, which is a different kind of result from a theorem but still a result. Note that one member, (iii), is already settled negatively by a classical count (my argument above), so the negative branch is partly pre-paid — which lowers the cost of answering and also lowers the surprise of the negative.

Reason for silence:
A disciplinary boundary inside one field, plus an incentive gradient, and the two work together. The failures are produced by a computational-search community whose stated success metric is the enumeration bound — P1's abstract is "increase the bounds to which Williamson matrices have been enumerated" — so a zero is an output of the pipeline rather than a question put to it. The machinery that would answer the why-question (character sums, field descent, self-conjugacy, cyclotomic class groups) belongs to algebraic finite geometry, which works on objects reducible to a single group-ring equation attackable one character at a time; the Williamson condition is four coupled sequences and does not so reduce, which makes it an unattractive target for that apparatus. The two are reinforced by a third, specific fact: the arithmetic that IS available at the trivial character is non-obstructive at every odd n (my Jacobi argument above), so the obvious arithmetic probe returns nothing, and the natural next probe — the nontrivial characters, i.e. the DFT/PSD structure and the 2-cyclotomic coset orbits — was absorbed into the search as a pruning filter instead of being asked to separate orders. The filter got used and was never asked the question. "No one thought of it" is not the mechanism; the mechanism is that the one tool that could separate was consumed as plumbing.
