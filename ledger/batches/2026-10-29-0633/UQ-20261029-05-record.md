## Question (canonical)

**V1 (as submitted).** Do the plate/column habit transition temperatures of ice crystals grown from the vapour shift in D2O by the amount predicted if the transitions are pinned to a surface-premelting (quasi-liquid-layer) threshold — roughly the +3.8 K offset of D2O's melting point — or do they stay nearly fixed, as a step-generation kinetics mechanism with its own activation energies would require?

**V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted; scored as canonical).** For ice grown from the vapour in one chamber at matched supersaturation, by how many kelvin does each of the three habit transition temperatures move between H2O and D2O, and does the measured set of shifts agree with the shift computed from D2O's *own* premelting driving force (the distance from its sublimation line to the metastable prolongation of its condensation line, i.e. a fixed-Δp threshold) or with the near-zero shift a step-generation kinetics mechanism with its own activation energies would require?

Reason for the reformulation (well-posedness only): V1 fixes the premelting prediction at "roughly +3.8 K", i.e. at D2O's melting-point offset. The leading premelting thermodynamics paper I opened **denies that T − Tm is the governing variable** (MacDowell 2025, quoted below), so V1's decision rule assumes a scaling its own field's theory rejects. V2 makes the premelting prediction a *computed* quantity rather than an assumed one, which is what the discriminant needs in order to be able to come out either way.

**What V2 itself presupposes (the repair tested as hard as the clause it replaces):** that D2O's sublimation curve and the metastable prolongation of its condensation curve are known over roughly 230–273 K well enough to compute the fixed-Δp threshold to the equivalent of ±0.5 K in temperature. **I did not verify this** — my fetch budget was spent — so it is a flagged, unverified presupposition of the reformulation, and it is the first thing a Gate 4 adversary should attack. V2 does not move the question onto a frame of records: the rows, the object and the deletion test are unchanged (see Depth), so the DEPTH block survives the reformulation.

## Presuppositions

1. D2O ice crystals can be grown from the vapour under the same supersaturation control as H2O in one chamber, with habit classified by the same rule. — *plausible; isotopic purity and cost are the practical obstacles. Not independently checked in this run.*
2. D2O ice is Ih with essentially the same face set, so "plate" and "column" denote the same morphological categories. — *established (standard; not re-sourced in this run — my read level on this point is background knowledge, not an opened source).*
3. **Surface-premelting onset in D2O scales with its melting point (+3.82 K).** — **CONTESTED, and the contest is now sourced.** MacDowell 2025 (arXiv:2509.13221v1, full text read) states the natural distance measure "is neither T-Tm, nor p-pm, but the pressure difference Δp(T,p)", that the equilibrium thickness "need not be measured strictly in terms of a distance away from the triple point", and that it "is dictated by the distance of the sublimation line to the metastable prolongation" of the condensation line. **Clause direction: these clauses DENY the T − Tm scaling — they record a move away from it, not support for it.** This is the candidate's own pre-declared falsification condition and it is **met in its simple form**, which is why V2 replaces the assumed +3.8 K with a computed Δp threshold rather than keeping it.
   - *My own arithmetic, labelled as mine and not a source finding:* MacDowell also gives Δp = ρ_l Δh_sl (T − T_t)/T_t under Clausius–Clapeyron, which is monotone in distance below the triple point, so a fixed-Δp threshold still maps to a fixed temperature interval below Tm — only with a different prefactor. Using D2O constants **recalled from background knowledge and not opened in this run** (T_t ≈ 276.97 K, Δh_fus ≈ +4–5% vs H2O, liquid density ≈ +10.6%), the prefactor ratio is ≈1.14, so a transition at ΔT = −4 K in H2O would sit near ΔT = −3.5 K in D2O, i.e. an absolute shift near **+3.3 K** — inside V1's 3.0–4.5 K window but only ~0.3 K from its edge. That is why V1's decision rule is tighter than the theory's own prefactor uncertainty, and it is an inference of mine, not a published prediction. The search that would have grounded it is a query on D2O's sublimation-curve and fusion-enthalpy values; I was at the query cap.
4. The H2O habit transitions are reproducible enough in one apparatus to detect a shift of ≤4 K to ±0.5 K. — *established-ish, but transitions are sensitive to supersaturation and to trace residual gases.* Libbrecht & Bell (arXiv:1101.0127) is the source that tests the chemical-impurity confound and **rejects** the impurity hypothesis for the habit changes, which helps this presupposition rather than hurting it.
5. Residual H2O in the D2O vapour does not pin the growing surface to H2O behaviour. — *flagged; isotopic purity above ~99% must be demonstrated, not assumed. Not checked in this run.*
6. Habit transition temperatures are a property of the ice surface rather than of the chamber's vapour transport. — *contested; controlled by running both isotopologues in the same chamber at matched supersaturation. Note the matching rule must be stated in excess vapour density relative to each isotopologue's own ice saturation, because D2O's vapour pressure differs from H2O's; V2 says "matched supersaturation" and this is the rule it means.*

## What would count as an answer

Habit diagrams in temperature and supersaturation for H2O and D2O measured in the same chamber, with each of the three transition temperatures located to ±0.5 K in both isotopologues and residual-gas composition held fixed.

**The pre-declared set, enumerated (Gate 1's enumeration rule — I list the members rather than state a rule):** the three transitions are those conventionally placed in H2O near **−4 °C** (plate→column), near **−10 °C** (column→plate) and near **−22 °C** (plate→column) at moderate supersaturation. Their theory-side counterparts in the one on-point simulation I opened (Llombart, Noya & MacDowell 2020) are basal smoothening ΔT ≈ −4 K, second-bilayer completion ≈ −12 K and prismatic smoothening ≈ −27 K, expressed as ΔT below *that model's* triple point. The third pair (−22 °C observed vs −27 K simulated) is where the observed and simulated numbers diverge most, and **that paper's own prismatic values are internally inconsistent between its Figure 1 caption and its text** (reported below) — so the mapping of the third transition is the weakest link in the set and the entry says so rather than smoothing it over.

Decisive under V2: shifts agreeing with the computed fixed-Δp threshold at every transition imply premelting-pinned transitions; shifts under 1 K imply step kinetics; non-uniform shifts (one transition moves, another does not) would show the three transitions have different mechanisms, which is currently not a hypothesis in play and would be the most informative outcome.

## Significance — consequence

If the shifts match the computed fixed-Δp premelting threshold at all three transitions, surface premelting is the controlling variable and the structure-dependent attachment-kinetics account is displaced as the controller of habit; the Kuroda–Lacmann line wins against Sazaki's objection by a route that does not depend on resolving the film optically. If the shifts are under 1 K, a premelting threshold cannot be what sets the transitions, and the step-generation energetics account is the survivor — which would also mean the agreement Prior 1 reports between its simulated smoothening temperatures and the Nakaya transitions is a coincidence of one force field. If the shifts are non-uniform, the three transitions do not share a mechanism, a possibility no current model entertains.

## Significance — audience

Cloud microphysics and ice-nucleation parameterisation: habit sets fall speed, aggregation efficiency, radar depolarisation and shortwave scattering, and the plate/column boundaries are hard-coded by temperature in these schemes; a demonstrated premelting control would make the boundaries functions of the film's driving force rather than of temperature alone. Ice surface science (the MacDowell, Sazaki and Libbrecht groups, who are the parties to the dispute Prior 3 records). Water-model developers: TIP4P/Ice and its relatives carry no H/D distinction, so a measured shift becomes a benchmark for path-integral or quantum-nuclear corrections — note these are **three distinct groups**, not one group citing itself.

## Reason for silence

A disciplinary-boundary plus unbuilt-apparatus mechanism, not "nobody thought of it". Habit diagrams are produced by cloud-physics laboratories using free-running diffusion and convection chambers that are continuously supplied from, and vent to, a vapour reservoir — an apparatus in which a >99% D2O inventory would be lost to every cold surface and to every flush, so the experiment requires a closed, isotopically sealed growth chamber that the cloud-physics tradition has had no other reason to build. Meanwhile the surface-science groups who handle D2O as a matter of routine use it as a *spectroscopic reagent* — chosen to move an O–H band out of the way — and work on single flat facets under optical microscopes, where habit is not a measured category at all. The isotope is thus a cheap reagent in the field that cannot classify habit and an expensive apparatus problem in the field that can, and no community owns the crossing. The dispute it would settle also only became sharp recently: Prior 1 is 2020 and Prior 3 is 2025.
