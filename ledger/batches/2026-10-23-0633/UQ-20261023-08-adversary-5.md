## Restatement (my own words)

Take real, currently accredited general clinical laboratories that run automated chemistry/haematology analysers and keep middleware audit trails from at least two different vendors. Pick a list of routine quantitative analytes *in advance*, keeping only those for which the material you are going to use is documented to behave like a patient specimen (commutable). Now send each laboratory the *same material* twice, by two routes: once as an ordinary external-quality-assessment sample that the laboratory knows is an exam, and once smuggled in through the normal patient-specimen intake by one pre-named, ethics-approved route. Score both arms against one fixed acceptability window around a target assigned by a reference method. The question's primary output is one number per analyte: (blind failure rate) − (declared failure rate), signed. Two further outputs: what fraction of the smuggled specimens staff spotted, and how much of the gap is explained by things the middleware log can show — who ran it and at what grade, how many times it was run before a result was released, and how long it took to report.

In one line: *do laboratories do better on the exam they know they are sitting, by how much, and is it the extra handling that explains it?*
