## Restatement in my own words

Where a hospital can say which drugs and fluids went through which particular peripheral IV cannula, split the hazard of that cannula failing (separately for phlebitis, infiltration/extravasation, occlusion, dislodgement, infection) into pieces owed to the infusate versus the patient, the inserter, the ward and the device lot; report the infusate piece as a variance share on the log-hazard scale with an interval; and then test whether the fixed pH/osmolarity cut-offs that currently send drugs to central lines actually discriminate and calibrate against (i) that infusate-owed failure and (ii) tissue injury on extravasation, compared with an index fitted to the same data and cross-validated across hospital systems.

## What the opened pages say (the evidence the table rests on)

Bahl et al. 2022 (publisher page, opened):
- Failure: "The primary outcome was the premature catheter failure of US PIVC"; complications "included: phlebitis, infiltration, dislodgment, occlusion, and leaking"; modes not analysed separately in main text (details in S3 Appendix, not seen).
- pH: never mentioned. Osmolality: never mentioned. Osmolarity: once, in the Introduction, "based on infusate osmolarity thresholds that increase the likelihood of these complications." Nothing measured or analysed. => The record's demotion condition (a hidden pH/osmolarity secondary analysis) does NOT obtain. Novelty stays Medium, not Low.
- Model: "multivariable time-dependent extended Cox regression analysis"; vesicant exposure as time-varying covariate; adjusted for age, sex, ESRD, BMI, SBP, pulse; no frailty, random effect or clustering.
- Effect: univariable HR 1.31 (0.87–1.98); multivariable aHR 1.17 (0.76–1.81), p=0.477 in Results. The Abstract prints "aHR 1.71 (95% CI 0.76–1.81)", which is inconsistent with its own CI (my observation; 1.17 is the figure consistent with the interval). Crude failure 45.8% with vesicants vs 31.1% without (p=0.017).
- Exposure: "vesicants and irritant infusates as recognized by the INS were noted" (INS Standards, 8th ed.), vesicants and irritants pooled yes/no, plus dose count; individual analyses for IV contrast (n=51), vancomycin (28), dextrose solutions (14).
- Discussion: "Current safety guidelines on use of peripheral catheters for vesicants and irritants are vague"; MAGIC "do not recommend the peripheral route for non-peripherally compatible infusates"; "The evidence supporting the delivery of vesicant infusates via PIVCs is limited."
- Limitations include: "Patients sometimes had several lines, so the access point for the infusion was unclear"; medications grouped; reliance on nursing documentation.

Lanbeck, Odenholt, Paulsen 2002 (Lund record, opened; abstract-level):
- Prospective observational, 550 patients, 1,386 peripheral venous catheters. "The incidence of phlebitis was 18.5% with antibiotics and 8.8% without (odds ratio 2.34)." Dicloxacillin OR 5.74 and erythromycin OR 5.33 highest in univariate, multivariate and Cox analyses; benzylpenicillin, cefuroxime, cloxacillin elevated; ampicillin, imipenem/cilastatin, clindamycin, netilmicin, vancomycin not associated. Insertion site and age 51–60 risk factors; warfarin protective. pH/osmolality not mentioned in the abstract; the abstract refers to the group's earlier in-vitro cell-toxicity work on four antibiotics. No multilevel model mentioned.

Dugan, Le, Jew 2014 JPEN 38(7):847-851 (Ovid page, opened; abstract-level):
- Retrospective matched cohort, 352 paediatric patients on peripheral PN; >1000 vs <=1000 mOsm/L. Infiltration 17% vs 7% (OR 2.47, 95% CI 1.24–4.94); phlebitis-or-infiltration composite 45% vs 34% (OR 1.65, 1.07–2.54); multivariable OR 1.67 (1.08–2.52). Authors: "Data supporting a maximum osmolarity for PPN are limited"; recommend not exceeding 1000 mOsm/L. No mention of 600 or 900 mOsm/L.
