## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take a paper's simultaneously printed oxygen-consumption and carbon-dioxide-production numbers as two measurements of one underlying thing, so that their ratio has to sit inside what substrate chemistry allows. Then, over a frame of published animal-respirometry papers fixed before any data pull: (i) how often does the printed pair fail that arithmetic feasibility test, counted per printed value-pair, split by taxon, apparatus mode and decade, with the number of dual-gas papers itself counted rather than assumed; (ii) for each apparatus mode, which classes of measurement error such a failure can actually fingerprint — worked out from that mode's own equations rather than assumed — and only then what, if anything, the failure implies about the printed metabolic rate; and (iii) if the failing records are dropped or corrected under rules fixed in advance, does the cross-species mass-scaling exponent move outside its published confidence interval?

Not the same as: "is this one RQ plausible?" (asked and normatively answered in clinical practice), nor "how completely do respirometry papers describe their methods?" (asked and answered by the record's PRIOR 1).
