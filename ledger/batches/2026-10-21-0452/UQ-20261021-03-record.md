## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (canonical, V1 as handed in):
For a pre-declared set of objectively detectable medication-error classes in hospitals that electronically record each barrier's action on the same order (CPOE alert fired/overridden, pharmacist verification action, barcode administration scan outcome), what is the measured conditional dependence between consecutive barriers — expressed as a common-cause-failure beta factor — and by what factor does the realised probability that an error passes every barrier exceed the product-of-marginals value that defence-in-depth reasoning assumes?

Question (canonical, V2 — the one reformulation permitted at Gate 1, for well-posedness only; see Gate 1):
For each of a pre-declared set of medication-error classes, restricted per class to orders whose *barrier-capability set* contains at least two barriers (a barrier is in the set only if the class is within what it is instrumented to detect) and whose per-barrier outcome is joinable to the order record: (a) estimate P(barrier k+1 fails to stop the error | barrier k failed to stop it) against the same cohort's marginal P(barrier k+1 fails), with intervals; (b) express the coupling as a common-cause-failure beta factor (or alpha-factor set) with an interval; (c) report the ratio of the realised all-barrier passage probability to the prediction obtained by multiplying **that same cohort's own** marginals — the independence reference being the cohort's marginals and not any literature value; (d) bound the unobserved "passed every barrier and was never detected" stratum, with the bound's assumptions declared; and (e) record, for the sampled institution's own published barrier/FMEA/business-case analyses, whether any of them states a product of barrier failure probabilities, so that limb (c) is referenced to a stated rather than an imputed assumption wherever one exists.

Alternative formulations:
- Are a hospital's successive medication checks statistically independent, and if not, how much does adding another check really reduce the chance an error reaches the patient?
- Estimate the common-cause coupling between CPOE decision support, pharmacy verification and barcode administration for matched error classes, and the error in independence-based barrier calculations that follows.

What makes the transplant more than a pastiche is identification, and it survives inspection: reliability engineering estimates CCF from component tests and demand records in which every barrier is exercised *and observed*, whereas the stratum that matters here — errors that passed every barrier and caused no detected harm — is unobserved by construction, so the transplant forces a design choice (computable error labels on the order record, blinded re-review of a sample, or positive-control injection) that the home field never has to make.

Presuppositions (each marked established / contested):
1. Each barrier's per-order outcome is electronically recorded and joinable to the same order. CPOE and BCMA: established in principle (Galanter et al. 2013 demonstrates six years of per-alert CPOE records at order granularity; opened at abstract level). Pharmacist verification granularity: **CONTESTED** — many systems record only a verification timestamp, not what was checked. No source opened in this Gate 2 establishes that verification-content granularity exists at scale; this is the leading feasibility risk.
2. A ground-truth error label can be assigned independently of the barriers themselves. **CONTESTED and central**: without it the denominator is only the errors something caught, and beta is estimated on a selected sample. V2 limb (d) is the response, not a solution.
3. At least two barriers are nominally capable of catching each error class. **CONTESTED per class** and now partly evidenced in the direction of the problem: White et al. 2010 (opened) measured 0% detection (0/30) of seeded *clinical-decision* errors by a bedside checker under both checklists tested, i.e. a barrier structurally blind to a whole class. V2 handles this by defining failure only over a class's barrier-capability set; without that restriction limb (a) would silently count structural blindness as dependence.
4. Medicine's defence-in-depth reasoning implicitly multiplies barrier failure probabilities. **CONTESTED**: the opened sources are mixed. Radley et al. 2013 (opened) scales one barrier's per-order effect to a national error reduction while assuming a 0% effect on non-CPOE orders — a single-barrier multiplier, not a product across barriers. Anderson et al. 2003 (opened in full) assigns per-technology reductions (unit dose "as much as 80%", bar-coding "as much as 60%", lower bounds 40% and 30%) and reports a *combined* effect, but the letter does not state the combination rule; the rule is in the authors' 2002 article, which is **COULD NOT READ** (not opened, fetch cap). So I cannot establish from anything I opened that a published medication-barrier analysis multiplies across barriers. The only candidate instance is the secondhand ISMP Canada 1-in-400 figure above. V2 limb (e) converts this from an assumption into a recorded datum.
5. Event counts suffice to estimate a conditional probability at the needed precision. **CONTESTED**; requires a power statement before any data pull. Rare classes may admit only a bound. Galanter's 32 intercepted wrong-patient errors in 127,320 alerts (0.25 per 1,000) is a usable order of magnitude for how thin one class can be.
6. Barriers act in a fixed order. **CONTESTED**: pharmacy verification and administration can interleave; the model must handle order-of-arrival explicitly.

Reformulation used (one, as permitted), and why: V1 left two things underspecified that would have made limbs (a) and (c) answerable either way. V2 restricts to the class's barrier-capability set. V2's limb (c) is a ratio with a null of 1 and a two-sided interval, and the "what would count as an answer" clause commits in advance to a null being informative, so the agenda is defused rather than smuggled.

## Gate 3 — Significance

**Consequence (argued).** If beta is materially greater than zero, then the realised probability an error traverses the whole chain exceeds the independence prediction by a computable factor, and every "add another check" decision is systematically overvalued by roughly that factor — which inverts the remedy, from adding a fourth barrier to decorrelating the three that exist (different modality, different information source, different operator, different time). That is a different procurement and a different accreditation requirement. The direction is not idle speculation: the opened 2019 review names automation complacency ("it's often assumed that whatever the computer says is correct") and confirmation bias as mechanisms that would couple a human verifier to an upstream electronic barrier, and White et al.'s 0/30 on clinical-decision errors is a measured instance of a barrier blind to a whole class — a structural beta of 1 for that class, if the class were in scope. If beta is indistinguishable from zero at a stated minimum detectable coupling, that is the first quantitative defence of medication defence-in-depth, and it would license the independence-style arithmetic the field currently uses without evidence (the 1-in-400 figure being the recorded, unverified example).

**Audience (specific, and shown to be active).** ISMP and ISMP Canada, which already advise against "sole reliance" on independent double checks (opened 2019 review) and would gain the quantity their guidance currently asserts qualitatively; hospital P&T and medication-safety committees deciding whether a proposed extra check buys anything; health-IT vendors and informatics groups configuring alert/verification/scan workflows, for whom "which barrier pair is decorrelated?" is a design question with no current answer; HFMEA practitioners, whose ordinal *detectability* scores would be replaceable by measured conditional detection — query 5 returned HFMEA risk prioritisation in a hospital pharmacy, so that practice is live; accreditors setting double-check and barcode-scanning requirements. The audience's uncertainty is documented rather than assumed: a systematic review found insufficient evidence that double checking reduces errors or harm (secondhand, Koyama 2019 via the opened review), which is precisely a community that cannot currently price redundancy.

**Fecundity.** Answering it makes askable: which barrier *pairs* are decorrelated by modality, and is modality diversity the right design variable rather than barrier count; whether automation complacency is estimable as a named alpha-factor cause rather than a narrative; whether HFMEA detectability should be re-specified as a measured conditional probability; whether the same beta can be estimated for the other healthcare barrier chains that share the architecture (transfusion bedside check, surgical count, specimen labelling, radiotherapy chart check); and whether accreditation should require evidence of *independence* of a required check rather than its existence.

**Anchoring.** Touches: the unresolved double-checking effectiveness question in medication safety; the Swiss-cheese model's holes, which the 2005 *BMC Health Serv Res* critique already questions as a metaphor and which nobody has made quantitative (query 3); alert override and alert-fatigue research, which measures override rates but never the downstream conditional; HFMEA's ordinal detectability; the IPL independence criterion in LOPA, which has never been applied in healthcare (query 8); and, in this ledger, UQ-20261016-62's own recorded observation that CCF in a redundant confirmatory channel is mature in reliability engineering and unapplied in medicine.

**Primary mechanism — a disciplinary and committee boundary with no owner of the joined record.** Each barrier is owned, measured and reported by a different service to a different committee: CPOE alerting and override rates by clinical informatics/CDS governance, verification by pharmacy, scan compliance by nursing informatics.
