Question (canonical): In nanopore adaptive ("Read Until") sequencing, where the instrument ejects reads mid-translocation on a reference-dependent decision taken on each read's first few hundred to few thousand bases, what signed bias does that data-dependent selection induce in estimands computed from the retained reads — reference-allele fraction and heterozygote recovery at target loci, insertion/SV recall by size class, repeat-length distribution, per-site methylation fraction — and is the bias correctable from the run's own retained output, i.e. the ejected reads' first-chunk prefixes joined by read_id to the logged sequence/unblock action, with the decision rule replayed over them to reconstruct a selection propensity?

Motivating gap: Adaptive sampling is a new instrumental mechanism in which the instrument takes a data-dependent decision about which molecules to finish. Every evaluation opened by the record or by the three adversaries scores it on on-target yield, enrichment fold, coverage, or the decision's own classification accuracy. The rule is reference-dependent, so in enrichment mode the molecules most likely to fail the first-chunk test are those carrying divergent haplotypes, large insertions or breakpoint-spanning structure — the classic shape of informative selection, where the retained sample is biased for the very features the experiment is run to find. BOSS-RUNS names the mechanism in its Discussion and declines to quantify it.
