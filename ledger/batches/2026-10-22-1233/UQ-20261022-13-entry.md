Question (canonical): For earthquakes with M >= M0 in a declared region and window, reading back from agency A's own versioned event history (or an archived snapshot series or dated bulletins) the magnitude m_tau that A had published at latency tau after origin, and fixing "final" as A's value m_D at a declared date D: (a) is the revision r = m_D - m_tau mean-zero (bias in magnitude units, with CI) and orthogonal to the settlement-instant information set (depth, region, station count/azimuthal gap, magnitude type, local time, m_tau itself), reported as a news-versus-noise decomposition; and (b) over the publicly documented population of parametric contracts and automated response thresholds keyed to A's published magnitude, how many trigger decisions flip under m_D rather than m_tau, with the sign of each flip and the money or action at stake?

Alternative formulations:
1. Are earthquake magnitude revisions news or noise?
2. Is the magnitude that settles a parametric earthquake contract a biased estimate of the magnitude the catalogue ends up with, and does the bias run towards or away from payout?
3. Intake version: treating the magnitude published at a fixed settlement latency as a forecast of the finally published magnitude, is that forecast efficient, and what share of keyed trigger decisions would have come out differently under the final magnitude?
Motivating gap: Parametric earthquake instruments and automated response rules settle on a named agency's reported magnitude as published at a named time, while the agency keeps revising that magnitude for days to weeks. A revision process designed for scientific accuracy is load-bearing for binary financial and emergency decisions. The mechanism is documented in a real case (Artemis, 19 Apr 2014: USGS "initially recorded it as a M7.5", "subsequently downgraded to a M7.2", with the MultiCat Mexico 2012 bond discussed "had the initial magnitude of 7.5 been maintained") and in a 2026 near-miss (USGS Mww 7.3 against a M7.4 Class B threshold). What is absent is any measurement OF THE TWO CANDIDATE QUANTITIES — a signed revision statistic over an agency's own successive published vintages, and a share of keyed decisions that flip under the later vintage. It is NOT the case that nothing directional has been measured on early earthquake magnitudes: Sardina, McCreery and Fryer (2017), Paper N° 193, 16WCEE, print over n=647 that 60% of PTWC rapid bulletin magnitudes ran high and 24% ran low against GCMT, that 68% of the Mw >= 7.6 events ran low, and that the sign flips at Mw ~ 7.45. That structure is cross-producer against an external catalogue, with no decision-flip share; it is not the candidate's quantity, but any claim that the directional question is unmeasured is struck.
