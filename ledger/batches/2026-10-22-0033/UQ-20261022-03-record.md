Question (canonical, V2 — the one Gate 1 reformulation, used only for well-posedness; V1 below):
On a pinned release of the ProteinGym substitution DMS benchmark (217 DMS assays, ~2.7M missense variants, as the repository README states), restricted to the eligible subset of assays whose ProteinGym-distributed raw (pre-preprocessing) files or source deposits carry >=2 independently selected replicate measurements per variant — with the eligible share itself reported as a primary result — and under estimators declared before any data pull (primary: variant-level between-replicate reliability on the merged-score scale, Spearman-Brown stepped up to the k replicates actually merged into the released DMS_score; sensitivity arms: a rank-reliability analogue, an Enrich2/DiMSum per-variant-standard-error-based reliability, and a reliability-heteroscedasticity-stratified arm): (a) what is each eligible assay's own test-retest reliability, with bootstrap confidence intervals; (b) when each published zero-shot predictor-assay rank correlation is disattenuated by that assay's reliability, what share of the apparent headroom to perfect prediction (1 - rho_observed) is assay measurement error rather than model error, with its uncertainty; and (c) does the published ranking of predictors change — reported as Kendall tau between the published and disattenuated orderings plus a count of pairwise ordering reversals among a pre-declared top-k?

Question (V1, as intaken): For the deep-mutational-scanning assays in a pre-declared variant-effect benchmark, what is each assay's own test-retest reliability estimated from its released replicate measurements, and when predictor-assay correlations are disattenuated by that reliability, how much of the apparent headroom to perfect prediction is assay measurement error rather than model error, and does the published ranking of predictors change?
(Reformulation rationale, recorded per Gate 1: V1 leaves three things to the analyst that decide the answer — which assays are eligible, which reliability estimator is used on a rank-based metric, and what "share of headroom" means. V2 pins the frame, declares the estimator plus three sensitivity arms, makes the eligible share a reported result rather than an assumption, and fixes the re-ranking outputs. No change of subject; this is the one permitted reformulation.)

Alternative formulations:
- A1: How good can a variant-effect predictor look against an assay that disagrees with itself, and where is the benchmark's real ceiling once the assay's own noise is removed?
- A2: Is the variant-effect-predictor leaderboard a ranking of models, or partly a ranking of which assays happen to have been measured precisely?

Motivating gap: These benchmarks score predictors by rank correlation against DMS values treated as the criterion, while measurement theory has held since Spearman (1904) that a correlation with a noisy criterion is attenuated by the square root of the criterion's reliability — so each assay sets a ceiling below 1, and a predictor's distance from 1 mixes model error with assay error.

Presuppositions:
1. A usable share of the benchmark's assays released replicate-level measurements rather than only merged scores — CONTESTED and decisive. PARTLY CHECKED here: ProteinGym distributes a raw pre-preprocessing layer for the whole substitution benchmark (500MB), and documents per-assay how DMS_score was derived from it, so a replicate layer exists and is already assembled; but the README does not state how many of the 217 raw files carry per-replicate columns, and that share is NOT established by anything opened in this record. If the share is small the question mutates into a reportability question, as the intake concedes.
2. Replicate agreement estimates the error that actually attenuates the predictor-assay correlation — i.e. replicates are independent at the relevant level (independent selections/libraries) rather than technical re-reads of one selection — CONTESTED, and the regime question on which the whole correction's validity turns. Unresolved by anything opened; it is per-assay and must be adjudicated from each assay's own design, which is why V2 makes replicate independence an eligibility criterion rather than an assumption.
3. Disattenuation can be applied to the rank-based correlations these benchmarks use, under an estimator declared in advance — only partly established; rank and non-linear measures have no single canonical disattenuation estimator, hence the pre-declared primary plus three sensitivity arms.
4. Benchmark rankings are consequential enough that a re-ranking would change behaviour — contested, testable from documented use in model selection and clinical-interpretation pipelines; not verified in this record.
5. Benchmark assay values are not themselves derived from or filtered by predictor outputs — established by construction (ProteinGym's DMS_score comes from the source assay's raw files; stated so it is checked, not assumed).
6. Assay reliability is roughly homogeneous across the variant range within an assay, or else heteroscedastic reliability is modelled rather than averaged — CONTESTED and unverified; DMS error is known to depend on read depth and on position in the dynamic range, so V2 carries a heteroscedasticity-stratified arm.

What would count as an answer: Per-assay reliability coefficients with confidence intervals computed from released replicates; disattenuated predictor-assay correlations under the pre-declared estimator with its sensitivity arms; the share of residual variance attributable to assay error, with uncertainty; and the re-ranked leaderboard reported with the pre-declared rank-change statistic and a count of pairwise ordering reversals. Uniformly high reliability answers it the other way — the ceiling is near 1, model error dominates, the leaderboard stands as published, and continued pursuit of the last tenth of Spearman is licensed rather than questioned. A small eligible share answers a different, still-reportable question (how much of a flagship benchmark's criterion layer is auditable at all).

Significance — consequence: If reliabilities are low and heterogeneous, then (i) the distance from a published Spearman to 1.0 is not headroom a better model can take, (ii) part of the between-predictor spread on the leaderboard is a function of which assays were measured precisely, and (iii) the fix is already standard next door — report per-assay reliability as a benchmark field and offer a ceiling-normalised score alongside the raw one, as Brain-Score does ("The score is normalized by this benchmark's ceiling such that 1 means the model matches the data to ceiling level"). If reliabilities are uniformly high, the answer is equally usable the other way: the ceiling is near 1, model error dominates, the published ranking stands, and the field's pursuit of the last tenth of Spearman is vindicated rather than questioned. A third outcome is informative and likely: if only a minority of the 217 assays expose replicate structure in the distributed raw layer, the finding is that a flagship benchmark's criterion layer is largely un-auditable, which is a reportable fact about the benchmark rather than a null.
Significance — audience: (1) Benchmark maintainers — ProteinGym, FLIP/FLIP2, MaveDB — who already distribute the raw layer and already publish an uncertainty of a different kind (a between-assay bootstrap SE on aggregates); a per-assay reliability column and a ceiling-normalised track are a concrete, cheap change to an artefact they control. (2) Model developers choosing between protein language models and inverse-folding models on leaderboard margins of a few hundredths of Spearman, who currently cannot tell a margin from criterion noise. (3) Clinical-variant-interpretation groups calibrating MAVE evidence (the PS3/BS3 line of work surfaced by query 5), who need assay reliability for their own purposes and would inherit the per-assay estimates. (4) Funders and consortia deciding whether the marginal dollar buys another assay or another replicate of an existing one.
Significance — fecundity: Makes askable — whether per-assay reliability should be a required MaveDB/ProteinGym metadata field; whether a ceiling-normalised protein benchmark changes which architectures look best; whether recent model gains concentrate on low-reliability assays (an artefact channel with a clean test); whether the supervised-track MSE comparisons are attenuated in the same way or differently; and the allocation question of replicates versus variants for benchmark assays, which connects directly to ledger entry UQ-20261015-18's arm (b).

Reason for silence: A boundary drawn inside the artefact itself, plus two incentive asymmetries. (i) ProteinGym's curation collapses each assay to a single "DMS_score" column in the files evaluation actually touches, while the replicate-bearing raw files are distributed separately and documented only as provenance ("details on how the DMS_score was created from the raw files"); the reliability information is therefore one zip file away from every scoring script and in nobody's evaluation path. (ii) The vocabulary that owns the correction lives elsewhere — psychometrics (Spearman 1904) and model-vs-measurement benchmarking (Brain-Score's ceiling) — and the uncertainty protein benchmarking does report is a bootstrap over assays, a different quantity that occupies the slot where a reliability estimate would go and makes the gap look already addressed. (iii) Incentives point the same way for both parties who could do it: a disattenuation raises every predictor's correlation and compresses the differences the leaderboard exists to display, so it is unattractive to benchmark builders, and it reframes a model author's hard-won gain as partly recovered assay noise. On the assay side, replicate agreement is published as a QC gate and then discarded, not released as an error model. Note that the silence is partial and must be reported as such: P2 says in print that the benchmarks "need to be corrected", so what is missing is the performance, not the thought — which is itself the cleanest kind of reason for silence (the work is cheap to recommend, tedious to do, and its most likely headline is that everyone's number goes up).
