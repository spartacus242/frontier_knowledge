Domain(s): Medicine — surgical pathology and cancer staging (oncology decision thresholds). Adjacent: cancer-registry epidemiology, measurement-error biostatistics, metrology (conformity assessment).

Question (canonical, as intaken): For organ sites whose T category turns on knife-edge size thresholds, what is the distribution of the specimen-handling-induced change in measured tumour size (in situ -> fresh gross -> post-fixation gross -> histologic), and what fraction of staged patients lie within that distribution's width of a threshold, so that their stage — and their adjuvant-therapy and trial eligibility — is set by specimen handling rather than by the tumour?

Gate 1 reformulation (the one permitted, for well-posedness only; both versions retained): For a pre-specified set of organ sites whose pT category turns on a size or depth cut-point, and holding the grossing PROTOCOL fixed (same number of measurement planes, same macro/micro corroboration rule, same observer pool), (a) what is the within-specimen distribution of the dimensional change in the maximum tumour dimension across four defined handling states — in situ/imaging, fresh gross before fixation, post-fixation gross, histologic on the block — decomposed into within-observer, between-observer and between-step components; (b) applying that distribution to a registry's recorded size density, with terminal-digit heaping modelled rather than assumed away, what probability mass lies within +/- the step shift of each cut-point, expressed as patients per year whose pT category is determined by handling state; and (c) in a cohort with outcomes, does stage recomputed on fresh measurements discriminate better, worse or equivalently (pre-set equivalence margin) than stage recomputed on fixed/histologic measurements? Why the reformulation was needed: the intake version left "specimen handling" undefined in a way that silently includes the grossing protocol, which is a different artefact already studied (see P3), and it bundled three estimands into one sentence.

Alternative formulations (as intaken):
- How much of T-category assignment is produced by the specimen's journey from patient to paraffin block rather than by the tumour itself?
- If tumour size were measured in situ or fresh instead of after fixation, how many patients per year would change T category, and in which direction?

Motivating gap: Presupposition inversion — AJCC/UICC manuals and CAP cancer protocols treat pathologic tumour size as a property of the tumour, specify it to the millimetre and hang categorical thresholds on it, yet the measurement is taken from tissue that has been excised, deflated or collapsed, fixed and sectioned, each step with a documented dimensional effect, and the prognostic thresholds themselves were calibrated on cohorts whose handling is undocumented. The claimed gap was that the field's existing work is per-organ shrinkage studies and radiology-versus-pathology discordance, and that nobody had asked what the artefact does to the decision lattice the thresholds define. **Gate 2 finding: the gap does not hold for the framing limb or for the fraction limb.** The question is the title of a 2022 paper in the candidate's own field (Verbeke, Cancers), which computed the fraction of staged patients whose T category changes with the examination approach (45% of 315 pancreatic resections); and the under-staging quantification is an explicitly stated research gap in a 2020 national-registry paper (Tsuruda, Acta Oncologica), i.e. asked and still open rather than un-asked.

Presuppositions (status after Gate 2):
1. [contested at intake -> ESTABLISHED by Gate 2] Handling changes measured size by a non-zero, mostly one-signed, organ-specific amount. Tran 2015 (kidney): fresh->fixed 4.6%, fixed->microscopic 7.1%, fresh->microscopic 11.4%, all p<0.01, radiologic 12.1% larger than fresh. Equivalent per-site studies exist for NSCLC, head and neck, and breast. This is no longer part of the question; it is an input to it.
2. [established] Threshold crossings are consequential (adjuvant recommendations, registry stage distributions, trial eligibility). Unchallenged.
3. [established] Size densities near thresholds are retrievable from registries recording size in millimetres (SEER size fields; Cancer Registry of Norway).
4. [contested at intake -> ESTABLISHED as a real phenomenon, still contested as modellable] Terminal-digit heaping is documented nationally: 38.7% of mammographic and 34.8% of histopathologic diameters end in 0 or 5 (Tsuruda 2020); 97.2% of histopathologic diameters are whole millimetres. That it can be *modelled* well enough to recover near-threshold mass remains contested and load-bearing for limb (b).
5. [contested] Paired measurement of the same specimen at >=2 handling states is feasible without compromising diagnostic material or adding ischaemic time. Supported by Tran 2015 having done it prospectively in 34 nephrectomies, but at one site, one surgeon, tumours <=5.5 cm.
6. [contested, unaddressed by Gate 2] The handling of the cohorts that originally calibrated the thresholds is at least partly documentable. No source found either way; without it the "which measurement is correct" limb cannot be closed.
7. [contested — the dissolution risk, stated deliberately] That in-situ size is the right measurand at all: because the prognostic evidence base was itself built on fixed measurements, the artefact may be baked into the calibration, in which case correcting it would degrade staging. Gate 2 found no study that tests this, and it is the only limb with no located prior.
8. [NEW, surfaced by Gate 2, load-bearing] That handling-induced dimensional change is separable from grossing-protocol-induced measurement choice. Verbeke 2022 shows the protocol effect is large (mean 10 mm, 36% relative, T-stage change in 45% of cases) — plausibly larger than the fixation effect (4.6-11.4%). If the two are not separated by design, limb (a) estimates a mixture and limb (b) attributes to fixation what belongs to grossing.

What would count as an answer (three things, pre-registered): (a) a paired-measurement study at >=2 organ sites with a fixed protocol — imaging/in-situ, fresh gross, post-fixation gross, histologic — reporting the per-step shift distribution with within- and between-observer components; (b) a registry computation of the probability mass within +/- the measured shift of each threshold, converted to patients per year whose pT is handling-determined, with heaping modelled; (c) a head-to-head test of prognostic discrimination when stage is recomputed on fresh versus fixed measurements in a cohort with outcomes — this adjudicates "correct the artefact" against "the artefact is already inside the calibration". A finding that the shift is small relative to threshold spacing, or that discrimination is unchanged, retires the concern and is equally an answer.

Significance — consequence: If handling-induced shift is large relative to threshold spacing and discrimination improves on fresh measurements, CAP protocols and AJCC would need a specified measurand state and possibly correction factors, and registry stage distributions become partly an artefact of local practice. If the shift is small, or discrimination is unchanged, the concern retires with a bounded null that positively justifies current practice. Discounted by Gate 2: P3 has already established the consequential version of this for pancreas (45% T-change) without changing the manuals, which is evidence about how much the field acts on such findings.
Significance — audience: real and standing — CAP Cancer Protocol committees, AJCC/UICC TNM panels, ICCR dataset authors, NAACCR/SEER registry standard-setters, and the oral-cavity and pancreas pathology communities that already argue about measurement rules. Verbeke's own conclusion ("consensus on an accurate method is required") is that audience asking for exactly this.
Significance — fecundity: would open guard-banded staging (a reported pT plus a stated conformity risk, in ISO 14253 terms), measurand definition for every quantitative CAP item that carries a threshold (DOI, margin distance, Ki67 index), and the question of whether threshold recalibration should be done jointly with a specified measurand rather than inherited.
Significance — anchoring: stage migration and the Will Rogers phenomenon; radiology-pathology size discordance; interobserver reproducibility in gross pathology; terminal-digit preference as a registry data-quality defect; measurement uncertainty in laboratory medicine reporting.
