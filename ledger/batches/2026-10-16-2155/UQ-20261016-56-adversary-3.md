## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

For species that have both a chromosome-level assembly in a public archive and a published karyotype in a compiled count database, how often does the number of assembled pseudo-chromosomes disagree with the cytogenetic haploid number, in which direction, and in which clades (limb i); and if assembly-derived counts are substituted for karyotype counts in a ChromEvol-type model on a fixed tree, do the inferred polyploidy / demi-polyploidy / fission / fusion rates move more than random count noise at the measured error rate would move them (limb ii)? The record's own alternative formulation 1 — "How often does a chromosome-level genome assembly contradict the karyotype of record for the same species, and in which direction does it err?" — is the plain-language form of limb (i), and it is the form I searched for in other vocabularies.

## Presupposition tests against the literature

- P1 (assembly pseudo-chromosome count is a measurement of n): contested in practice by two opened primary sources. In the DToL ecosystem the compiled karyotype is explicitly used for "assessment of whether the assembly estimate matches the expected karyotype" and "confirming the chromosomal pseudomolecules" (Blaxter et al. 2024); the leading Lepidoptera cytogeneticists state that "Classical cytogenetics is also needed to validate chromosome level genome assemblies" (Lukhtanov & Pazhenkova 2025). Where a karyotype exists, the archived count is therefore downstream of a comparison with that karyotype; the archive records post-validation counts. How pervasive this is, I could not quantify (no source gives a share). The Hi-C input-parameter mechanism remained unsourced (Fetch (b)).
- P2 (name matching): not tested this run.
- P3 (enough species): SUPPORTED for Lepidoptera by Lukhtanov & Pazhenkova 2025 (452 assemblies, more than half with light-microscopy counts). Also note the record omits the Animal Chromosome Count database (Román-Palacios et al. 2021), which enlarges the animal side of the frame.
- P4 (ChromEvol sensitivity to realistic count error): not tested; the record concedes it must be demonstrated. The record's own COULD NOT READ item (Syst Biol 70(5):1061) remains unread by me too.
- P5 (neither count is a gold standard): contested by practice — both opened cytogenetics sources treat the karyotype as the validator of the assembly. A defensible methodological stance, but not the field's.
