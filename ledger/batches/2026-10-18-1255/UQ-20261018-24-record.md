Question (canonical):
V1 (as intaken): Using facility-and-scanner protocol dose — the dose a given facility actually delivers for a given examination class, estimated from machine-reported dose structured reports — as an instrument for an individual patient's absorbed dose, does the instrumental-variable estimate of the radiation dose–response for subsequent cancer agree with the conventional observational estimate in the same cohort, and by how much does it differ?

V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted, for well-posedness only; V2 is the version scored and the one Gate 4 should attack):
In a record-linkage cohort holding (i) individual per-examination machine-reported dose indices (DICOM Radiation Dose Structured Reports), (ii) facility, scanner-unit and protocol identifiers, (iii) recorded indication, and (iv) cancer-registry follow-up — define the instrument Z for patient i as the LEAVE-ONE-OUT mean machine-reported dose index (DLP, and CTDIvol) for the SAME examination class at the SAME facility-scanner-protocol in the SAME calendar quarter, excluding i's own examinations, mapped to the same organ-dose scale as the individual exposure by a declared conversion (SSDE/size correction plus a named organ-dose model) with its error model propagated; and, conditioning on a conditioning set DECLARED BEFORE ANY DATA PULL (indication/presenting syndrome, age, sex, body region, examination count, calendar quarter, and presentation-site stratum) and after a declared minimum lag:
(a) FIRST STAGE — what is the coefficient of individual organ dose on Z and its conditional F / partial R^2?
(b) IV ESTIMATE — what is the two-stage (2SLS or control-function) estimate of excess relative risk per unit organ dose, with its confidence interval, under the declared dosimetric error model?
(c) THE ANSWER — what is the SIGNED DIFFERENCE between (b) and the conventional adjusted estimate computed on the SAME person-years, with an interval, judged against an equivalence margin declared in advance?
(d) EXCLUSION-RESTRICTION BATTERY — does the declared battery pass: covariate balance across deciles of Z; >=2 negative-control outcomes with no plausible radiation aetiology at that latency; and a falsification using non-ionising-imaging (MRI/ultrasound) volume at the same facility as a sham instrument?
Pre-declared: an IV interval too wide to separate concordance from discordance at available registry scale IS an answer, reported as such.

Alternative formulations:
- Is the CT dose–cancer association confirmed when the only dose contrast used is the one created by WHERE the patient was scanned rather than by how ill they were?
- How much of the observed CT dose–cancer dose–response survives when dose is identified solely from between-facility protocol variation?
- (Method-owner field's words) Does a provider-preference instrument — here, facility/scanner protocol dose rather than a prescribing share — reproduce the conventional dose–response for a continuous iatrogenic exposure, and what is the gap?

Motivating gap:
The newly measurable quantity is the realised per-examination patient dose of essentially every scan, machine-reported, carrying facility, scanner-model and protocol provenance, accumulated at population scale in dose registries via DICOM RDSR (ACR Dose Index Registry and national equivalents) and linkable to the individual. Before this, epidemiology imputed dose from phantom surveys, machine-setting archives and protocol questionnaires. The standing anomaly this makes attackable: the observational CT–cancer literature (multinational paediatric EPI-CT and its predecessors) is contested on essentially one point — confounding by indication and reverse causation, since sicker children are scanned more — and the field's defences are exclusions, lag periods, latency modelling and sensitivity analyses rather than a different source of variation. Dose for the SAME examination varies several-fold between facilities for reasons unrelated to the patient; that variation is a candidate instrument. Opened first-hand: the conventional side of the comparison already has a published number — Mathews (ViCBiostat seminar, 26 Mar 2015, ~11 million young Australians) reports ERR 0.056 per mSv (95% credibility interval 0.047–0.065) at lags as short as 2–3 years and notes it exceeds Life Span Study estimates at higher doses. No IV counterpart to that number was found in 11 queries.

Presuppositions:
1. Machine-reported dose indices can be converted to organ dose with a DECLARABLE error model, largely SHARED within scanner/protocol, and that error is propagated rather than ignored. ESTABLISHED-ADJACENT, part-verified: the conversion machinery exists and is a mature medical-physics literature (Q9 returned Duke/Samei DLP-to-risk and DLP-to-organ-dose conversion-coefficient work and an Insights into Imaging review); the propagation of SHARED error into an IV first stage is not established and is part of the work.
2. The instrument is strong: facility/scanner protocol dose materially shifts individual dose. PLAUSIBLE, NOT SHOWN IN-COHORT: between-facility and international dose variation is documented (Q2, Q10: Maastricht international-variation record; UCSF scanner-settings work; lung-screening dose surveys reporting >50% variation attributable to settings), but a first stage must be shown in the actual cohort, net of the declared conditioning set.
3. CONTESTED (central vulnerability): the exclusion restriction — conditional on where and why the patient presented, protocol dose affects cancer risk only through dose. High-dose facilities may differ in case mix, referral patterns, deprivation, imaging frequency and follow-up/ascertainment intensity. Requires the limb-(d) battery; the restriction is not testable, only falsifiable.
4. CONTESTED: monotonicity/homogeneity sufficient for a local interpretation, and compliers (patients whose dose is shifted by facility) are policy-relevant. The LATE may not be the population dose–response radiation protection wants, which makes limb (c)'s signed difference partly an ESTIMAND difference rather than purely a confounding measure. This is the sharpest internal weakness and is flagged, not resolved.
5. CONTESTED by registry vintage: per-examination dose records linkable to the individual exist for long enough before cancer onset. Most dose registries begin around 2011, so the design is latency-limited (leukaemia/brain tumours first; solid cancers not yet) unless PACS-era dose metadata can be reconstructed retrospectively. The feasible calendar window is part of the answer.
6. Cancer-registry linkage is legally permitted in the chosen jurisdictions and loss to follow-up is not differential by facility. UNVERIFIED.
7. NEWLY FLAGGED AT GATE 2, UNVERIFIED AND LOAD-BEARING: the conventional comparator's dose contrast may ALREADY be substantially between-facility. EPI-CT-class dose reconstruction assigns dose partly from facility-and-period machine-setting/protocol surveys, so the conventional estimate may rest on grouped (Berkson-type) facility-era dose assignment rather than on truly individual dose. If so, the IV contrast is less "a different source of variation" than the candidate asserts, and limb (c) measures the removal of patient-level contrast plus the purging of patient-level confounders, not a clean source change. I could not open the IARC EPI-CT dosimetry document (fetch budget exhausted; see access log), so this is recorded as a flagged risk, NOT as a finding. Gate 4 should test it first.

What would count as an answer:
A pre-registered IV analysis in a cohort with individual machine-reported dose, facility/scanner/protocol identifiers, recorded indication and cancer-registry follow-up, reporting: the IV estimate of ERR per unit organ dose with its CI, side by side with the conventional estimate on the SAME person-years; the first-stage strength (conditional F, partial R^2); the signed difference with an interval against a pre-declared equivalence margin; and the full declared exclusion-restriction battery (balance across Z deciles, negative-control outcomes, non-ionising-imaging falsification), with dosimetric error propagated. The question is ANSWERED by the signed difference with intervals. Concordance would largely retire the confounding-by-indication objection to the observational literature; discordance would quantify it for the first time; an uninformatively wide IV interval is a real answer and would tell radiation-protection bodies this identification route cannot settle the dispute at currently available registry scale.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
vs P1: same literature, same vulnerability, DIFFERENT IDENTIFICATION. P1's unit is person-time by lag and its device is a latency/mixture model that reclassifies cancers by time since scan; the dose contrast remains the patient's own imputed dose, so patient-level confounding by indication is addressed by excluding early follow-up, not by changing the source of variation. The candidate's estimand is an IV/LATE dose–response identified ONLY from leave-one-out facility-scanner-protocol dose, with the conventional estimate as an explicit same-person-years comparator and the signed difference as the reported answer. P1 provides the conventional number the candidate would be differenced against; it is the comparator, not the question.
vs P2: both are instrumental-variable estimates of a radiation→cancer effect, but the instrument, exposure scale, unit and endpoint all differ. P2 instruments an UNDEFINED binary/latent "irradiation history" with germline genotype at SNP level, two-sample, OR per undefined unit, breast cancer only. The candidate instruments a CONTINUOUS organ dose in mGy with an exogenous provider-side variable, one-sample with individual data, ERR per unit organ dose, and requires a first stage in the cohort. P2 cannot deliver a dose–response and cannot be compared with a conventional ERR per mGy; it also does not touch confounding by indication in a scanned population. On the ledger's own warning about priors aimed at an adjacent object: P2 is an IV paper, but it is not an IV estimate of a DOSE–RESPONSE.
vs P3: P3 is the method-owner field's own treatment of the design, and it is the closest thing to a template — but its exposure is binary treatment choice, its endpoint is a 12-month continuous biomarker, and it has no dose, no latency, no shared measurement error and no exclusion-restriction battery of the kind the candidate declares. Transplanting it requires (i) a continuous first stage, (ii) propagation of SHARED dosimetric error that is correlated with the instrument itself (a structural problem P3 never faces, because a prescribing share carries no measurement-error model), and (iii) a latency-limited outcome. The interface therefore carries real problems rather than being Field A × Field B.

Significance — consequence: If the IV estimate agrees with the conventional one within a pre-declared margin, the principal standing objection to the paediatric-CT literature — that the dose–response is manufactured by sicker children being scanned more — loses its empirical footing, and the risk coefficients that feed justification/optimisation policy become materially harder to dismiss. If it disagrees in sign or magnitude, the field acquires the first quantification of the confounding it has only ever bounded by sensitivity analysis, and coefficients such as the opened ERR 0.056/mSv would need restating. If the IV interval is uninformative, radiation-protection bodies learn that registry-scale between-facility variation cannot settle the dispute, which redirects effort to dosimetry and to longer follow-up rather than to a fourth round of sensitivity analyses.
Significance — audience: ICRP, UNSCEAR and NCRP committees that set and defend low-dose risk models and have to adjudicate the LNT dispute; the IARC EPI-CT investigators and the national paediatric-CT cohorts (Australia, UK, Netherlands, Nordic), who would gain or lose a defence; ACR Dose Index Registry and national dose-registry operators, for whom this converts a QA/benchmarking asset into an epidemiological instrument and raises a governance question about facility-identified outcome research; radiology societies and paediatric imaging programmes, whose justification messaging rests on the contested coefficient; and health-econometrics/pharmacoepidemiology methodologists, who gain the first continuous-dose provider-preference application.
Significance — fecundity: Makes askable — does the same instrument work for interventional fluoroscopy and nuclear-medicine administered activity, where dose spread between operators is larger? Is the facility-level dose instrument strong enough to estimate a dose–response for non-cancer endpoints (cataract, cardiovascular)? Does the complier population differ systematically from the dose-optimised population, i.e. does optimisation under diagnostic reference levels destroy the instrument and make the design a closing window? Can the same leave-one-out device identify dose-responses for other machine-reported iatrogenic exposures (ventilator settings, infusion-pump-recorded dose)? And the methodological one: how should an instrument be used when the exposure's measurement error is SHARED within instrument level?
Significance — anchoring: The LNT controversy and the low-dose-risk estimate discrepancy (the opened Mathews number exceeds Life Span Study estimates per unit dose, an anomaly the field reports without resolving); the confounding-by-indication/reverse-causation critique of the paediatric-CT cohorts; the shared-vs-unshared dosimetric-error problem in radiation epidemiology; the diagnostic-reference-level optimisation programme, which is actively removing the between-facility variation this design depends on; and the open methodological problem of provider-preference instruments for continuous exposures.

Reason for silence: Two mechanisms, one of them unusually specific.
(a) NEW INSTRUMENT, with a hard start date: individual-level, machine-reported, facility-and-scanner-attributed per-examination dose only exists at scale since DICOM RDSR deployment and dose-registry launch around 2011. Before that, the facility's delivered dose was exactly what had to be IMPUTED, so it could not serve as an exogenous regressor for the individual.
(b) DISCIPLINARY BOUNDARY, inverted-sign version: radiation epidemiology classifies between-facility dose variation as NUISANCE and as a QUALITY DEFECT, not as exogenous contrast. Its dosimetry programme spends its effort averaging that variation into a dose estimate (Q6: facility/period protocol surveys, shared/unshared error, 2D Monte Carlo), and its protection programme spends its effort ELIMINATING the variation through diagnostic reference levels and optimisation (Q2, Q9, Q10: dose-variation surveys framed as reduction targets). A field whose two main programmes are "average it away" and "make it go away" does not reach for the same variable as an instrument. Meanwhile the field that owns the method — health econometrics/pharmacoepidemiology — has developed provider-preference IVs almost exclusively for BINARY treatment choice (P3, confirmed in the opened text), so the continuous-dose case was never on its agenda either, and neither field's literature contains the other's object. This is a boundary mechanism with a direction, not "no one thought of it".
(c) Candidate incentive/governance mechanism, PLAUSIBLE BUT UNVERIFIED and flagged as such: dose registries are operated as benchmarking services under participation agreements that deliberately shield facility identity, so facility-identified, outcome-linked research may be contractually awkward for precisely the party holding the instrument. I ran no query on registry governance terms and did not open any participation agreement; Gate 4 should either verify or drop this limb.
