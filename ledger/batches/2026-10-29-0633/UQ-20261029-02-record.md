## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (canonical, as submitted): For a solid melting as size-selected nanoparticles, does the solid-liquid interfacial free energy itself depend on the interface's curvature — is there a nonzero solid-liquid Tolman length delta_sl in gamma_sl(R) = gamma_inf(1 - 2*delta_sl/R + ...) — and what are its sign and magnitude relative to the interfacial width?

Alternative formulations (as submitted):

Motivating gap (as submitted, and partly corroborated below): nanoparticle melting-point depression is universally fitted with a Gibbs-Thomson relation that presupposes gamma_sl is curvature-independent; residual size dependence is charged to premelting, shape or support effects. CNT critical radii (1-3 nm) sit where a Tolman term would be largest.
- **Corroboration from a source I opened:** Ma, Li & Lu 2025 (a 2025 *primary* modelling study, not a review — see Prior 4) writes the solid-liquid interfacial energy sigma_sl0 in its Eq. (7) entirely in bulk quantities (nearest-neighbour distance, melting enthalpy, vibrational entropy, molar volume, gas constant) **with no diameter term**, while separately adding size-dependent surface-energy and distortion terms. So the live nanoparticle-melting modelling literature does treat gamma_sl as curvature-independent. The motivating gap's premise about current practice holds.

## Gate 3 — Significance (argued)

- **Consequence.** If V2 returns |delta_sl| well above 0.02 nm with a definite sign, every Gibbs-Thomson fit to nanoparticle melting below ~5 nm carries a systematic error in gamma_sl, and the 1-3 nm critical nuclei of CNT acquire a first-principles curvature correction with an experimental anchor rather than a simulation one. If it returns |delta_sl| < 0.02 nm for real metals, that would be a genuine surprise given two simulations saying otherwise, and would localise the discrepancy to model potentials or to the crystal-nucleus-in-fluid geometry.
- **Audience, and how it would act differently.** Nucleation theorists would stop treating the capillarity approximation's failure as a fitting nuisance and start carrying a measured delta_sl term (Prior 2 describes the non-constancy of gamma with curvature as a failure of the capillarity approximation). Nanoparticle-melting modellers would change their functional form: Prior 4 is a 2025 primary study whose Eq. (7) has no diameter term in sigma_sl0, so this is a live practice, not a historical one. Solidification and pharmaceutical crystallisation would gain a curvature correction at the nucleus scale where their rate predictions spread by orders of magnitude.
- **Fecundity.** Is delta_sl orientation-dependent in real faceted metals as Prior 1 finds it to be in Lennard-Jones? Does the sign flip between melting (liquid outside) and crystallisation (solid inside)? Does delta_sl correlate with the interfacial width measured independently? Does a measured delta_sl reconcile the simulated and experimental homogeneous-nucleation rates for one substance?
- **Anchoring.** The orders-of-magnitude spread between CNT-predicted and measured homogeneous nucleation rates; the long-standing disagreement among experimental gamma_sl values for the same metal obtained at different particle sizes; the liquid-skin-versus-homogeneous-melting dispute in nanoparticle melting; the breakdown of the capillarity approximation named in Prior 2.
- **Significance score 2/3.** Docked one: the sign and non-zero-ness are already established in simulation by two groups, so an experimental number would confirm and calibrate rather than open a question; and the measurement may not be achievable as posed (see well-posedness).

## Reason for silence

For **V1 there is no silence** — two groups published on it, and that is the Gate 2 finding. For **V2** the mechanism is a **disciplinary boundary plus a fitting-form gap**, which is one of the LEDGER's acceptable mechanisms and not "no one thought of it": the Tolman length is owned by the statistical mechanics and nucleation-simulation community, which works in model potentials and reduced units and (Prior 1) cites no experimental value; nanoparticle melting Tm(D) is owned by the nanocalorimetry and in-situ TEM community, whose standard model (Prior 4, 2025) writes sigma_sl0 from bulk quantities and absorbs residual size dependence into premelting, surface-stress and distortion terms. The two communities do not share a fitting form in which delta_sl is a free parameter, so neither is positioned to report it. A secondary mechanism is genuine degeneracy: at least three physical terms (delta_sl, size-dependent latent heat, premelting-shell thickness) enter Tm(R) at the same 1/R^2 order, so the experiment has been unattractive rather than unthought.
