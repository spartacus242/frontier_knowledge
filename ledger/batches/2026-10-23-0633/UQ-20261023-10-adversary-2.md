## 1. Restatement in my own words (not the generator's)

Hospitals routinely turn a drug's dose DOWN when a patient's kidneys stop working. Kidneys often start working again days later. The question asks whether the dose goes back UP, and when: in a cohort built from linked medication-administration and lab records, for a pre-declared list of drugs whose labels print a renal threshold and one full dose per indication, take every episode where the given dose sat below the labelled full dose while clearance sat below that drug's label threshold; start a clock at the moment clearance climbs back above that same threshold and stays there; then report (a) the cumulative incidence of getting back to the full labelled dose, treating stopping the drug, discharge and death as competing events, (b) how often the dose never comes back before one of those events, (c) how much drug was never given, as administered-over-labelled dose across the post-recovery window, (d) whether the chart says the omission was a decision or says nothing, and (e) per site, whether the deployed renal dosing rules can fire on a dose that is too LOW for a clearance that has risen.

In one line: **renal dose reduction is suspected of being a ratchet. The question asks for the release-time distribution of the ratchet, the share that never releases, and the drug never given — and whether the local alerting can even see that direction.** The benefit of restoring is explicitly NOT asked.

I note for the record that the entry **declares the measurand exemption out loud** (presupposition 4): whether restoration would have been clinically right is measured as the documented-deliberate/undocumented split (limb d), not assumed. I therefore did not spend any attack on "a low dose may have been correct" — that clause is a measurand here and attack 4 does not reach it. I say this rather than grading around it.
