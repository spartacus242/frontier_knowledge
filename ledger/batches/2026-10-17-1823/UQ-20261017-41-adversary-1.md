# UQ-20261017-41 — Adversary #1 log

## Restatement (my own words)

Take every Starlink satellite with a NORAD ID, label it by the Falcon 9 launch it rode on, its hardware version and its target shell. Decide in advance what counts as "died young" (stopped stationkeeping / started decaying within T days, with no sign SpaceX did it on purpose), plus two alternative definitions. Fit a hazard model in which each launch gets its own random bump to the failure rate, after adjusting for how much geomagnetic storming each satellite saw while climbing to its shell, which shell, and which version. Ask: how big is the spread of those launch-level bumps (tau^2, and the median hazard ratio between two random launches), and if you feed that spread instead of a single fleet-average reliability into the debris/PMD-compliance models, how does the *distribution* (tail) of the number of dead objects left in orbit change?

In short: are some Starlink launches statistically "lemon batches" after you account for the known explanations, and does that matter for debris forecasting?
