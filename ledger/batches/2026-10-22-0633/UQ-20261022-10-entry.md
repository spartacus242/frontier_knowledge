## Verification of this entry's sources (post-batch, 2026-10-22)

Four fresh-context checkers were each handed one claim and its source, with the entry's citations but not its reasoning; the fourth opened five actual field safety and end-of-support notices. Logs: `VERIFY-okun-2024-abandonment.md`, `VERIFY-keogh-2012-pip.md`, `VERIFY-sheldon-pipotiazine.md`, `VERIFY-uq10-support-withdrawal-notices.md` in this folder. **Verdict unchanged: Provisional. More of this entry's reasoning changed on verification than on any other entry in this batch.**

**1. Okun 2024 holds as read, and its regime is narrower than the cap it carries.** On the full text the checker read, it prints **no** count, **no** prevalence, **no** incidence and **no** outcome rate; "prevalence" and "incidence" do not occur; the only person-numbers are one undenominated NeuroVista anecdote and the composition of the consensus panel. The "defines without evaluating" characterisation is accurate and, if anything, understated: the paper also relays no count or rate from its 7 included articles, and it **does not call for** enumeration or outcome measurement — "registry", "track", "surveillance", "incidence" and "prevalence" are all absent, and its forward-looking ask is for "guidelines, policies, and laws". Two qualifications now bear on the Medium cap. **Regime:** this source's regime is active implantable **neurotechnological** devices, DBS, VNS and SCS, with the **article** as its unit (734 screened, 7 included). Inside DBS/VNS/SCS the cap is well founded; pitched at implanted devices broadly, or at non-neurological implants, Okun 2024 is **silent in that regime rather than prior art in it**, and the cap would need another source to carry it. **Kind of anticipation:** a definition citation is not an evaluation. It anticipates *defining* abandonment and anticipates nothing about *measuring* it, so if this candidate's contribution is enumeration, prevalence or an outcome rate, this source does not touch it. One hazard for anyone rewriting the entry: do not let the surrounding insolvency discussion migrate into the definition, whose support clause is actor- and cause-agnostic ("Failure to fulfill reasonable responsibility for medical, technical, and/or financial support prior to the end of an implantable device's labeled lifetime") — manufacturer exit is the paper's motivating example, handled in the body, not a trigger enumerated in the definition.

**2. Keogh 2012 REFUTES the support-withdrawal framing on four of six sub-claims, and this is the sharpest finding of the batch.** The two numbers hold: "an estimated 35,000 women" is printed verbatim at paragraph 23 as the denominator of a 0.6% rupture rate over MHRA reports 2007–2009, and it counts women in the UK **estimated to have PIP implants**, that is recipients, not women still implanted and not a post-explantation residual, which the report never gives. "By a factor of around 2-6" is printed twice, in the Summary and at paragraph 17(i), backed by printed component rates (PIP 1.2% at 5 years and 3.1% at 10; other brands 0.2–0.4% and 0.5–1.1%) — but it is meaningful **only inside reported-adverse-event ascertainment**, the report itself saying the true rate is higher and that cross-brand comparison is not on "a truly comparable basis", and a separate 3–5 factor applies to local clinical signs and is a different claim. What refutes: the **construct** is product defect and manufacturing fraud ("the non-medical grade silicone illegally used by PIP", "after the discovery of the fraud in March 2010") and the report says **nothing** about PIP's insolvency or exit, its one "no longer in business" sentence concerning **private clinics'** aftercare obligations with the NHS backfilling consultation, scanning and removal — a payer and provider exit construct, not a device-support construct; **no post-exit period** is defined or measured, outcomes being indexed to time since implantation with the post-discovery era **excluded** as a reporting-bias confounder; there is **no support construct at all** — no programming, parts, external components or remote monitoring anywhere in the document, a passive gel prosthesis having nothing to withdraw, which makes it different **in kind** and not merely in degree; and there is **no matched comparison**, only aggregate unmatched "other brands" with market-share-asymmetric denominators and bias adjustment in place of matching. Net: good prior art for a product-defect mass-notification and aftercare-liability event, including the genuinely relevant point that a private provider's commercial exit can strand patients and shift aftercare onto a public system — and **not** prior art for a device-support-withdrawal question.

**3. Sheldon is prior art on METHOD, not on exposure, and the entry should name it as such.** It holds in its weak form: a real cohort, a market withdrawal as the exposure, hard clinical endpoints (discontinuation, acute care use) over a defined post-withdrawal window, in a named jurisdiction, on 137 patients in 2022. So the appeal to novelty must not rest on "nobody has measured clinical outcomes after a product left the market", because this paper did. It fails in the form that would sink the candidate: its estimand is **which of three available substitutes a patient received and how they fared**, which cannot be evaluated where substitution is impossible, and a result proved under substitution is silent, not supportive, outside it. Whether this entry's novelty survives therefore depends on whether its claim is pitched at the method or at the non-substitutable exposure; pitched at the method, this paper weakens it materially. Two things must not be recorded as settled: **anything numerical**, since the checker did not open the paper and the figures in hand are abstract-level, denominator-uncertain and interval-free; and **"no comparator"**, which is Unresolved and distinguishable in Methods only. A routing correction: the batch's confident "this journal is open access at journals.sagepub.com, try that directly" did not serve this reader — SAGE failed as well as figshare (403) and PMC (blocked), and a successor should start at `api.figshare.com/v2/articles/23488739` through WebFetch to recover the `ndownloader` link to the 287.82 kB PDF.

**4. Five exit notices opened, and the instrument question decomposes three ways.** Of the five documents opened, **3** are genuine exit, discontinuation or end-of-support notices for an implanted device or a constituent of one; the other 2 are a recall of unimplanted stock and a certification-driven distribution hold, and are the wrong kind of document for this question. **Holds and now extends beyond the regime it was formed in — open-ended constituents:** 3 of 3 implant-level notices leave the constituent list open, one on its face with "including" and "for example" plus "may require support for many years", one enumerating nothing, one naming two ending items and leaving software, technical support and troubleshooting unaddressed. The entry's recorded vulnerability, that this was an inference from how the document kind is designed rather than a reported result, is **partly discharged**: it is a reported result in 5 of 5 documents, and for the first time in an **abrupt commercial exit**, the sub-kind the entry listed as untested. **Holds for the implant, fails for the constituent — no end-of-support date:** 0 of 3 implant-level notices date the loss of any support constituent, while the one constituent-level notice dates it day-precise (Cochlear Kanso 1, 30 September 2025). So "no end-of-support date is printed" must be re-scoped to **whole-device exit notices**, and what this shows is not that the event-date rule is inoperable but that it is **mis-grained**: dates appear when the unit of the notice is a named constituent, which is the granularity the rule itself asks about. **Fails — no recipient count:** P2 is falsified, as recorded above. The proportions, reported as found and not as advice: carried a patient count 1 of 3 (1 of 5 overall); carried a dated end of support 1 of 3, and that date is for an external constituent, so for the implant itself 0 of 3; carried a dated commercial exit event 2 of 3 (one day-precise, one month-only); carried a closed constituent list 0 of 3; said something about clinical consequences or patient management 3 of 3, ranging from explant/exchange/continue guidance down to "there is nothing they need to do at this time" with no clinical risk statement.

**5. What this does to well-posedness, stated as a finding and not as a score.** The defect cited to hold well-posedness at 2/3 — that the declared event-date rule dates nothing — **survives for the implant-exit sub-kind**, and now has three documents under it rather than two, including the sub-kind the entry said was untested. But three of the surrounding supports move: the uncountability presupposition is falsified by a printed figure; the two documents the entry nominated as decisive are the wrong kind and cannot decide it; and the exit itself is datable and published, so "dates nothing" is accurate only about support constituents, never about the event. What the sources show is that the gap is a **granularity and unit problem** — notices date constituents when they name constituents, and date exits always — rather than the absence of datable public facts. Two of the five promotion items are therefore no longer useful and are struck above. Whether the remainder leaves the score at 2/3 or lifts it is a judgement for whoever rewrites the canonical wording, and this entry does not pre-empt it.
