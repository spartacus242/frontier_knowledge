Question (canonical): V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted; V1 in the record) — restricted to clinician-mediated interventions and defining overlap contemporaneously (one clinician delivering care in the same calendar window in two clusters in different treatment states): (a) across a pre-declared frame of health-system cluster-randomised and stepped-wedge trials, what is the distribution of (i) the share of clusters with at least one such clinician and (ii) the share of patient-contacts they deliver, measured from trial rosters or payer/EHR provider identifiers rather than from the trial report's prose; and (b) in trials whose provider identifiers are retrievable, how far does re-analysis under a crossed clinician random effect or a bipartite-interference-aware estimator move the point estimate and interval against the published analysis?
Alternative formulations: Is the cluster the right randomisation unit when the agents who deliver the intervention practise in several clusters at once? / Measured on real trials rather than assumed away as "contamination", how large is the bias from clinicians who work on both sides of a cluster randomisation?
Motivating gap: Intake (presupposition inversion): cluster and stepped-wedge designs are justified by between-cluster independence, shared staff were believed to appear only as a listed limitation and never as a measured quantity, and statistics now has estimators for bipartite delivery. Gate 2 falsified the premise: the phenomenon is named ("therapist contamination") and counted twice in the candidate's own literature, and the bias limb has one published, explicitly indeterminate answer. Gate 4 falsified it a second time in two further vocabularies. What is left unpublished is the instrument (provider identifiers, not text), the denominators (clusters and patient-contacts, not articles), the frame (health-system pragmatic/SW-CRT, not mental-health complex interventions) and the within-trial re-analysis.
Presuppositions: 1. Clinician-to-cluster affiliation during the trial is recoverable from rosters/administrative data — CONTESTED at Gate 2 (only 57% of 100 protocols name who delivered the intervention, so unrecoverable for a literature-assembled frame), then EVIDENCED at Gate 4 for the within-trial route (Wilson-Aggarwal et al. 2022 locate staff by ward and day from Epic pseudonymous HCW identifiers plus door-access logs). 2. Exposure travels with the clinician — CONTESTED; V2 repairs it by restricting to clinician-mediated interventions, but V2's contemporaneity rule then excludes the larger channel for durable training exposure (trained in period 1, practising in a control cluster in period 3), so the instrument undercounts what it prices. 3. Published estimates assume no cross-cluster agent sharing — ESTABLISHED. 4. Some trials permit a crossed or interference-aware re-analysis — the crossed random effect is estimable; the causal interference contrast is NOT identified from a single realised cluster-level assignment, which the record concedes. 5. Overlap biases rather than only inflating variance — CONTESTED, with a published prior pointing the other way. 6. V1's "or period" limb is ~100% by design in a stepped-wedge trial and is the already-named quantity carry-over; V2 drops it.
What would count as an answer: A measured overlap distribution (clusters with any contemporaneous cross-state clinician; share of patient-contacts they deliver) over a pre-declared frame, from provider identifiers, plus at least one re-analysis under a crossed-clinician or interference-aware model reporting the movement of the estimate and interval. Overlap near zero retires the listed limitation for that frame; overlap common with no movement answers the bias limb in the negative. Both directions informative; the frame-level limb is executable only by trialists or a consortium holding site-level provider data, which is what caps well-posedness.

Significance — consequence: If overlap is common and re-analysis moves estimates, the cluster is the wrong randomisation unit for clinician-mediated interventions, CONSORT's cluster extension and SW-CRT reporting need a provider-affiliation item, and a crossed-clinician sensitivity model becomes an expected SAP row. If overlap is common and nothing moves (the direction P1's weaker test already points), a routinely listed limitation is retired with a number. If overlap is near zero, the same, more cheaply. Independent of the bias limb, the measured crossing fraction and professional-level ICC would supply the power-calculation input P2 states is missing.
Significance — audience: Cluster and stepped-wedge methodologists, the NIH Pragmatic Trials Collaboratory and comparable embedded-trial programmes, CONSORT/SPIRIT cluster-extension authors, CTU trial statisticians, and the design-methods authors of P2 who cannot parameterise their own power calculations. Institutionally evidenced: NIHR commissioned a full HTA report on this concern in 2007 that ended by calling measurement a priority.
Significance — fecundity: What estimand is defined when the delivery agent, not the unit, carries exposure (the bipartite analogue of the informative-cluster-size debate); should clinician-intervention combinations be randomised instead of clusters; does provider overlap explain part of the attenuation of pragmatic-trial effects; what is the design-efficiency cost of restricting a trial to non-overlapping clinicians (P2 gives the direction, not the price).
