## Restatement in my own words (before reading the generator's framing back)

Open-weight masked-diffusion language models at 7–8B (LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B) publish perplexity numbers that are not likelihoods: they are derived from a variational **upper** bound on negative log-likelihood. Autoregressive baselines in the same tables publish an exact chain-rule likelihood. So the released comparison pits a pessimistic estimate against an exact one. The candidate asks two things. **(a)** A magnitude: at 7–8B, how many nats per token of the published number is bound slack rather than model quality — the difference between the published bound and an unbiased or two-sidedly sandwiched estimate of the diffusion model's own log-likelihood, with Monte Carlo error bars. **(b)** A consequence: once that bias is removed, do the published diffusion-versus-autoregressive verdicts still hold?

Two things about the restatement matter for what follows. First, clause (a) names its target as "the model's actual log-likelihood", which for an any-order model is not one quantity: at minimum there is the uniform-order mixture the ELBO bounds, the distribution a deterministic test-time unmasking policy induces, and an oracle-order maximum. Second, clause (b) is the significance clause and is a *comparison* question, answerable by anyone who can compute any tighter number at all — it does not require clause (a)'s decomposition.
