## Findings
1. Citation: Wang JPZ, Lindsay BG, Cui L, Wall PK, Marion J, Zhang J, dePamphilis CW. "Gene capture prediction and overlap estimation in EST sequencing from one or multiple libraries." BMC Bioinformatics 2005;6:300. Published 13 Dec 2005. doi:10.1186/1471-2105-6-300. (Springer page truncates author list with "..."; DOAJ lists the same seven.) Title is not about "zero-class estimation" nor "number of genes in a library" alone; the zero-class wording is only a method step.
2. Unit = GENES. "Let N be the number of genes represented with transcripts in the cDNA library." (Results and Discussion, Compound Poisson process model). Paper estimates unobserved genes (n0, zero class), not isoforms.
3. Splicing is treated as a source of bias, not the thing counted. "If we had defined a "gene" as a distinct transcript, then this estimate will be biased downward" (Discussion; truncated quote). "The gene number estimation can be inflated if many genes have multiple splicing forms in the expression pool." (Discussion). Summariser also reported: ESTs from different splicing forms can fall into different contigs, inflating small clusters (paraphrase-level, not verified verbatim).
4. Both figures EXIST. "Errors from different sources can bias the number of observed genes upward by 35% – 40% [25-27]." (Background). This is about the number of OBSERVED genes (EST clustering errors), not N. "the estimates of N before and after ISO error correction were 12030 vs 9155 and 12085 vs 9492 respectively" (Discussion). N = number of expressed genes in the library; the two pairs are the two 5' EST sets, root (12030 -> 9155) and ABGR above-ground organs (12085 -> 9492). ISO = insufficient overlap error, a CLUSTERING error (false separation of sibling ESTs), not isoforms. Abstract's 9,155 (root) matches the corrected value. Do not read "ISO" as isoform.
5. FOUND. Compound Poisson process; unknown abundance distribution estimated by penalized conditional nonparametric MLE; bootstrap CIs (summariser-reported). Future sample of S1 ESTs = Poisson process on [1,1+t], t=S1/S (quoted). Real data predictions at 0.5S, 1S, 1.5S, 2S (Table 3). Multi-library overlap also covered.
