## WHAT THE SOURCE SAYS (quotations as served)

Quotations below marked **[reconstruction]** were assembled from ordered consecutive segments returned under the summariser's ~125-character quotation cap, joined only at verified consecutive junctions, with no inference filling any gap.

### (a) Doves at RER 0.58–0.69 — YES, with three qualifications

Printed in a **sentence of running text in Results, p. 1039**:

> "Unexpectedly low (0.58–0.69) RER values were also seen in four doves during hours 3–14 of trials."

- **Species:** the dove ***Columbina inca*** Lesson (Inca dove), mean mass 38.8 g.
- **Number of animals:** **four** doves showed the low values, out of **ten** doves in the study ("Trials were conducted on 10 individuals of each of the three species."; figure captions: "N=10 for each species.").
- **Where printed:** running text, Results p. 1039. RER over time is also plotted in **Fig. 4**, but the 0.58–0.69 range is *stated in the sentence*, not read off the figure.

**Grammar on both sides of the cut.** The sentence is preceded by the kangaroo-rat sentence and followed by:

> "Measured thermal equivalents of consumed oxygen (hO₂) were commonly outside the conventionally expected range (Fig. 5)."

Nothing in the surrounding clauses reverses the claim. The preceding sentence supplies the interpretive frame the dove sentence's "also" points back to: the values are "below the minimum anticipated for exclusive reliance upon lipids as a metabolic substrate in a fasting animal."

**"About eleven hours" is not the source's phrase.** The source prints **"during hours 3–14 of trials"**. Eleven hours is *somebody else's arithmetic* on that interval (14 − 3 = 11), and it is arithmetic on a *window within which the low values were seen in four doves*, not a statement that any one dove held a sub-0.7 RER continuously for eleven hours. The source does not print "11 h", and does not say the low values were "sustained". Nor is the window per-animal: four different doves contributed, and the paper's own dove mean over hours 7.5–9.0 was 0.76 and "increased during the latter portion of trials", which is hard to reconcile with eleven hours of continuous sub-0.7 in the group.

**The surrounding dove result, [reconstruction] from 2 consecutive segments:**

> "In doves, RER also declined and averaged 0.76 during the middle of experimental trials (hours 7.5–9.0) but increased during the latter portion of trials."

### (b) Three kangaroo rats at 0.65–0.70 — YES

Printed in a **sentence of running text in Results, p. 1039**; **[reconstruction]** from 2 consecutive segments:

> "During the final 7 h of experimental trials, three kangaroo rats exhibited RER values of 0.65–0.70, below the minimum anticipated for exclusive reliance upon lipids as a metabolic substrate in a fasting animal."

- **Species:** the kangaroo rat ***Dipodomys merriami*** Mearns, mean mass 37.6 g.
- **Number:** **three** of **ten**.
- **Window:** "the final 7 h" of trials — a *different* window from the doves' hours 3–14.

The paper's kangaroo-rat group mean is higher: "RER declined and averaged 0.78 in kangaroo rats" over "the last 10 h of measurements". So 0.65–0.70 is a **three-animal subset**, not the kangaroo-rat central value.

### (c) "accurate within 0.4%" — YES, but it is an instrument validation, not an animal-measurement accuracy

Two formulations, in two sections.

**Methods, p. 1038 — the measurement that produced the figure** ([reconstruction] from 2 consecutive segments):

> "A measured mass of 100% ethanol was burned for 6 h inside ... Measurements of water vapor, CO₂ production and O₂ consumption were within 0.4% of values expected from the stoichiometry of ethanol combustion."

**Discussion, pp. 1040–1041 — the back-reference, which is the sentence the ledger quotes:**

> "As noted above, our measurements of respiratory gas exchange are accurate within 0.4%."

Its immediate context ([reconstruction] from 2 consecutive segments):

> "There are three classes of possible errors in our measurements: those arising from respirometry, those from direct calorimetry, and those from changes in body temperature."

and the sentence after:

> "Errors arising from direct calorimetry also are quite unlikely to explain our results."

**This is the formulation that is hedged by its own derivation, and it matters.** The Discussion sentence is **not** a free-standing accuracy claim: it opens "As noted above", and what was noted above is an **ethanol combustion test** — a known chemical burn, 6 h, no animal — against *stoichiometrically expected* values. The 0.4% is therefore the agreement of the gas-exchange train with a known combustion, i.e. **instrument accuracy under a deliberate calibration test**, not a 0.4% error bar on RER measured from a live bird over 17 h. The rhetorical job the sentence does in the paper is narrow and defensive: it is one of three error classes being ruled out as an explanation for the direct-vs-indirect discrepancy. Using it as "their respirometry is accurate to 0.4%, therefore a measured RER of 0.58 is real to within 0.4%" extends a calibration-regime number into an operating-regime claim the paper does not make.

### (d) "the extensive series of assumptions" — YES, it is theirs; and there is a second, differently-worded formulation

**Discussion, p. 1042** — the phrase as the ledger has it, whole sentence, **[reconstruction]** from 3 consecutive segments:

> "Candidate explanations for the discrepancies lie in the extensive series of assumptions that subsume the implicit view that animal respiratory and energy metabolism can be usefully described by a relatively constrained set of catabolic processes, such as oxidizing lipids completely to CO₂ and H₂O."

**What it attaches to:** not to respirometry in general, and not to the low RER values. It attaches to **"the discrepancies"** — the paper's central finding that respirometric and direct-calorimetric estimates of heat production diverge — and the assumptions named are specifically those embodying the view that metabolism can be described by *a constrained set of catabolic processes*, with complete lipid oxidation as the example.

**Second formulation, Introduction p. 1035** — different wording, same target, and this one carries the thermodynamic anchor; **[reconstruction]** from 2 consecutive segments:

> "Indirect estimates of animal metabolic heat production rely, however, upon an extensive chain of assumptions linking them to Hess's Law of Constant Heat Sums."

Note: "extensive **chain** of assumptions" (Introduction) vs "extensive **series** of assumptions" (Discussion). Both are the authors'. Neither carries a citation; both are the authors' own framing. Related assumption sentences, as served:

- Introduction p. 1035: "This is typically accomplished using one of three assumptions." / "The third of these three assumptions is based upon the contrasting RER values characteristic of each nutrient class."
- Introduction p. 1036: "Problematically, these assumptions used to estimate hCO₂ and hO₂ can readily be violated."
- Methods p. 1038 ([reconstruction], 2 segments): "The second approach for which mean absolute errors were calculated is the common assumption that fasting animals rely entirely upon lipid catabolism and thus exhibit thermal equivalents equal to those of a mixed lipid substrate (Table 1)."

### (e) Mechanisms offered for a sub-0.7 value — **NOT what the ledger's framing implies**

**The authors offer no mechanism for a sub-0.7 RER.** This is the finding that matters most for the entry. The word "anaerobic" never appears in the same sentence or paragraph as an RER value, and no sentence in the paper ties bicarbonate storage, biosynthesis or any other mechanism to the 0.58–0.69 or 0.65–0.70 numbers. The mechanisms are offered for a **different quantity**: the discrepancy between respirometric and direct-calorimetric **heat production / thermal equivalents**.

What the authors actually print, Discussion p. 1042 ([reconstruction] from consecutive segments, in the order served):

> "For example, one complication is that respirometry can estimate only aerobic heat production whereas direct calorimetry measures the sum of both aerobic and anaerobic catabolism."

> "Accounting for anaerobic heat production would only exacerbate discrepancies in cases in which thermal equivalents are unexpectedly low, however."

> "Additional complications might include, at a minimum, storage of carbon dioxide in the form of bicarbonate (e.g. Harrison, 1989; Harrison and Kennedy, 1994), consumption of oxygen by processes that release little heat (e.g. formation of oxygen radicals; Fridovich, 1998), and biochemical synthesis."

> "For example, synthesis of tripalmitin from glucose is characterized by a respiratory quotient of 2.75 and thermal equivalents of hCO₂=7.8 kJ l⁻¹ and hO₂=21.5 kJ l⁻¹ (Blaxter, 1989)."

> "It therefore is possible that shifts in T_b may partially account for the effects observed."

And from the Introduction, p. 1036, where violations of the assumptions are enumerated ([reconstruction], consecutive):

> "Fasting animals may rely on non-lipid substrates, for example, by catabolizing body protein or food stored within the gut (e.g. Ankney et al., 1991)."

> "Finally, incomplete substrate oxidation can occur (Kleiber, 1961; McLean and Tobin, 1987)."

> "This could, for example, include production of ketone bodies during lipid catabolism."

> "A second basis for violating the common assumptions used to estimate hCO₂ and hO₂ is that the animal may simultaneously be involved in synthetic processes that consume or produce either CO₂ or O₂."

**Three things to note.**

1. **Anaerobic heat production is offered and then explicitly ruled out in the relevant direction.** The authors state that accounting for it "would only **exacerbate** discrepancies in cases in which thermal equivalents are unexpectedly low". So the paper does not support anaerobic metabolism as the explanation for its own low values — it says the opposite about the low-value cases.
2. **Bicarbonate CO₂ storage and biosynthesis are attributed to others** (Harrison; Harrison and Kennedy; Blaxter) and are hedged — "might include, at a minimum" — and are offered as explanations of thermal-equivalent discrepancy, not of RER.
3. **Biosynthesis is cited with an RQ of 2.75, i.e. far above 1**, not below 0.7. Tripalmitin synthesis from glucose is quoted as a mechanism that *raises* RQ. It is not offered as a route to a sub-0.7 value.

The terms *gluconeogenesis*, *hypoventilation* and *unsteady state* do not appear in the text.

### Largest error, for completeness

Abstract: "We find that conventional respirometric estimates of energy expenditure may incorporate large errors (up to 38%)". Text: "with the maximum mean error for a 30 min period reaching 38% (Fig. 7)" — kangaroo rats, under the RER-based linear-interpolation approach assuming protein is conserved. The 38% is a **maximum mean error over a 30-minute period**, not a whole-trial error.

## REGIME

What the paper establishes, it establishes in this regime, in its own terms:

- **Species and n:** three species, **"Trials were conducted on 10 individuals of each of the three species."** — kangaroo rat *Dipodomys merriami* (37.6 g), Inca dove *Columbina inca* (38.8 g), quail *Coturnix communis* (158 g). Figure captions: "N=10 for each species." Doves and kangaroo rats "were collected from the wild under permits from federal and state authorities"; "Quail *Coturnix communis* L. were purchased from local aviculturalists."
- **Fed/fasted state:** **"For 24 h prior to the start of each trial, the animal was provided with water and rice ad libitum."** Then in-chamber, **"with no access to food or water."** So the animals entered the chamber **recently fed on rice ad libitum**, and fasted only for the duration of the trial. This is the opposite of the "prolonged fast" the paper itself criticises in the prior literature ("typically fasted for prolonged periods"). The paper's own Results confirm the animals were *not* postabsorptive at the start: "Respiratory exchange ratios (RER) in all species were initially near or above 1.00, suggesting extensive reliance upon carbohydrate as a catabolic substrate (Fig. 4). Values of 1.01–1.20 were observed in individuals of all species during the first 4 h of experimental trials; this was exhibited by four kangaroo rats, five quail and one dove." [reconstruction, 4 consecutive segments] and "Over the course of 4–5 h of fasting, RER declined".
- **Ambient temperature:** **"at 25°C ambient temperature (T_a), with no access to food or water."** Water bath held at "25.000±0.005°C."
- **Instrument:** a **purpose-built combined device** — "We constructed a combined direct calorimeter/respirometry chamber." Direct calorimetry by gradient-layer thermopile: "A copper-constantan thermopile was affixed to the exterior of each face". Respirometry: "through a carbon-dioxide analyzer (Li·Cor LI-6252)", "and then an oxygen analyzer (Sable Systems FC-1B)", "to a dewpoint hygrometer (Sable Systems RH200". Validated by burning "A measured mass of 100% ethanol ... for 6 h inside" the chamber.
- **Duration:** **"Each trial lasted at least 17 h"**; "All trials began between 13:00 h and 15:00 h"; animals "kept in darkness in the calorimetry chamber".

**Does the claim sit inside this regime? For (a), no — "fasted resting doves" misdescribes it in two ways.**

1. **"Fasted" overstates it.** These doves were fed rice ad libitum until the trial began and the low RER window starts at hour 3 — within the period the paper itself reports RER near or above 1.00 from carbohydrate reliance. The paper never calls them postabsorptive, and the data say they were not postabsorptive on entry.
2. **"Resting" is not the paper's word for them, and the paper does not establish a resting, basal or standard metabolic state.** The words "resting", "basal" and "standard metabolism" appear in the paper only in a *cited* example about birds ("resting metabolism averages 24% higher during the active phase of the daily cycle than during the rest phase") and in reference *titles*. The animals were in darkness, which supports quiescence, but trials began at 13:00–15:00 h — the **active phase** for a diurnal dove — and the paper offers no activity monitoring, no steady-state criterion and no minimum-value selection.

**Would a comparative basal-metabolic-rate compilation select these animals? No.** A BMR compilation requires postabsorptive, rest-phase, inactive, thermoneutral animals at a measured minimum. These animals fail on at least three counts as the paper describes them: (i) **not postabsorptive** — fed ad libitum to the trial's start, with RER 1.01–1.20 in the first 4 h; (ii) **wrong circadian phase** — trials begun at 13:00–15:00 h, the active phase for diurnal doves and quail (and the rest phase for a nocturnal *Dipodomys*, so the phase error runs in opposite directions across species); (iii) **thermoneutrality not established** — 25°C is asserted as the ambient temperature but the paper nowhere states that 25°C lies within the thermoneutral zone of any of the three species, nor reports a thermoneutral zone. On top of that, the reported figures are **whole-trial means, windowed means and individual ranges**, not minimum metabolic rates, and body temperature was changing ("shifts in T_b").

**What the paper measures — unit of analysis, endpoint, comparator, estimand, in its own words.** Unit of analysis: the individual animal over a ≥17 h trial, with sub-hourly resolution (errors computed over "30 min" periods). Endpoints: metabolic heat production by direct calorimetry; O₂ consumption, CO₂ production and water vapour by respirometry; RER as their ratio; and "thermal equivalents of consumed oxygen (hO₂)" and of CO₂ (hCO₂). Comparator: the **respirometric estimate against the simultaneous direct-calorimetric measurement on the same animal at the same time** — direct calorimetry is the reference standard. Estimand: the **error** of the respirometric estimate, reported as mean absolute error and as "the maximum mean error for a 30 min period", under each of the conventional conversion assumptions (Table 1). RER is an **intermediate, reported-as-observed variable** in this design, not the paper's endpoint.

## DEFECTS FOUND

1. **The ledger's route label is wrong or unverifiable. The earlier agent labelled its route "S1 (supplementary information)". Every number in sub-claims (a)–(e) is in the main text** (Results p. 1039; Discussion pp. 1040–1042; Methods p. 1038). There is no supplementary file involved. This is a sourcing defect: the entry points at a location that does not hold the content.
2. **"About eleven hours" is the ledger's arithmetic, not the source's figure.** The source prints "during hours 3–14 of trials". The paper does not print "11 h" and does not use the word "sustained". The interval is a window in which **four different doves** showed low values, not a duration any single animal held a sub-0.7 RER.
3. **"Fasted" is wrong as the paper describes the protocol.** Animals had "water and rice ad libitum" for the 24 h **prior to** the trial; the only fast is the trial itself. The low-dove window opens at hour 3, overlapping the period the paper reports RER of 1.01–1.20 from carbohydrate reliance. Calling these "fasted doves" imports the prolonged-fast regime the paper explicitly criticises in the prior literature.
4. **"Resting" is not established and is not the paper's word for these animals.** No activity monitoring, no steady-state or minimum criterion, trials begun in the diurnal species' active phase, thermoneutrality of 25°C never asserted for any of the three species.
5. **The 0.4% is a calibration-regime number used as an operating-regime number.** It is the agreement of the gas train with the stoichiometry of a 6 h ethanol burn, back-referenced in the Discussion ("As noted above") while ruling out one of three error classes. It is not a 0.4% accuracy on RER measured from a live animal.
6. **(e) as posed does not have the answer its framing presumes.** The authors offer **no mechanism for a sub-0.7 RER**. Anaerobic heat production, bicarbonate CO₂ storage and biosynthesis are offered for the **thermal-equivalent / heat-production discrepancy**, a different quantity. This is a **granularity gap** — the paper discusses mechanisms one level up from where the entry wants them — not an absence of discussion.
7. **Anaerobic heat production points the wrong way in the paper's own text.** "Accounting for anaerobic heat production would only exacerbate discrepancies in cases in which thermal equivalents are unexpectedly low, however." If the entry cites this paper for anaerobic heat production as an explanation of its low values, it cites it against the authors' stated direction.
8. **Biosynthesis is cited with RQ 2.75, i.e. above 1.** Blaxter's tripalmitin-from-glucose example is a mechanism that **raises** RQ. It is not offered as a route below 0.7.
9. **Two formulations of the assumptions phrase exist and are not interchangeable.** Discussion: "extensive **series** of assumptions" attached to "the discrepancies" and to a constrained set of catabolic processes. Introduction: "extensive **chain** of assumptions" attached to Hess's Law of Constant Heat Sums. Neither attaches to the low RER values. If the entry quotes "the extensive series of assumptions" as the authors' explanation *of the sub-0.7 RER*, that attachment is not in the source.
10. **The ledger's title is truncated** — the served title ends "...of energy expenditure".
11. **Subset means are not group means.** 0.58–0.69 is four of ten doves; 0.65–0.70 is three of ten kangaroo rats. The group means the paper prints are 0.76 (doves, hours 7.5–9.0) and 0.78 (kangaroo rats, last 10 h). Any use of 0.58–0.69 as "the dove RER" overstates it.
12. **Front-matter date as served is chronologically impossible** — "Accepted 30 December 2005" on a 2005 volume-208 paper. Reproduced as served and flagged, not corrected; I had no landing page to check it against.

## BEARING ON THE LEDGER ENTRY

**(a) PARTLY.** The numbers and the species are right and are printed in running text at Results p. 1039: four of ten Inca doves, *Columbina inca*, RER 0.58–0.69, hours 3–14. But "about eleven hours" is the ledger's own subtraction on "hours 3–14", "sustained" is not in the source, and "fasted resting" misdescribes animals fed rice ad libitum until the trial began and run in darkness during their active phase with thermoneutrality never asserted.

**(b) YES.** "During the final 7 h of experimental trials, three kangaroo rats exhibited RER values of 0.65–0.70" — verbatim, Results p. 1039, *Dipodomys merriami*, three of ten.

**(c) YES as a quotation, NO as the entry uses it.** "As noted above, our measurements of respiratory gas exchange are accurate within 0.4%" is verbatim (Discussion, pp. 1040–1041), but it back-references an ethanol-combustion calibration and is deployed to rule out one of three error classes. It is instrument accuracy against a known burn, not accuracy of an animal RER.

**(d) YES as a quotation, NO as to attachment.** "the extensive series of assumptions" is theirs (Discussion p. 1042), but it attaches to **the direct-vs-indirect discrepancies** and to the premise that metabolism reduces to a constrained set of catabolic processes — not to the sub-0.7 RER values. A second formulation in the Introduction ("extensive chain of assumptions linking them to Hess's Law of Constant Heat Sums") must be reported alongside it.

**(e) NO.** The authors offer **no mechanism for a sub-0.7 RER value.** The mechanisms the entry lists are real and are theirs, but they are offered for the thermal-equivalent discrepancy; anaerobic heat production is explicitly said to **exacerbate**, not explain, the unexpectedly low cases; and the biosynthesis example carries RQ 2.75, above 1, not below 0.7.

**Net.** The source is open, readable at the publisher PDF, and real, and its two headline numbers survive exactly. What does not survive is the **regime** the entry wraps them in — "fasted resting" doves of the kind a BMR compilation would select — and the **mechanistic attribution** in (e). On the paper's own description these animals are fed-then-fasting, active-phase, thermoneutrality-unestablished, whole-trial-mean animals, which is precisely the population a comparative basal-metabolic-rate compilation excludes. An entry that uses this paper's sub-0.7 RER values as evidence about **BMR-grade postabsorptive resting animals** is using a result outside the regime in which it was established; the paper is silent there, not supportive.
