Scope note: Gate 2's "Low / already answered" verdict reached me void (withdrawn by checkers). I treated it as a prior, not a verdict. I attacked V2 as restated below. I did not reopen MethylSaferSeqS myself; where I use its numbers I use the checkers' facts (1)-(6) as instructed and say so.

## Restatement (my own words)

Take tissue that is a mixture of many cells, not a clone. Sequence it so that each individual DNA molecule (one original strand, barcoded) yields both (a) whether it carries a somatic point mutation at some position and (b) its 5mC calls. Now throw away the mutated base itself and any CpG the mutation made or unmade. Question: over a window of the remaining nearby CpGs, is the amount of methylation on a read different for reads that carry the mutation than for reads from the same locus, same sample and same strand that do not — tested as a matched within-locus comparison (conditional logistic), split by mutation class, with a class where no effect is expected and a model for how often a per-read 5mC call is wrong?

In one line: **is a read's local methylation density associated with whether that read carries a variant at the locus?** The somatic/non-clonal frame, the exclusion rule and the stratification are the design around that question, not the question.

## The find that decides this (sourced)

**epialleleR** — Nikolaienko O, Lønning PE, Knappskog S, "epialleleR: an R/Bioconductor package for sensitive allele-specific methylation analysis in NGS data", *GigaScience* 12 (2023) giad087, doi 10.1093/gigascience/giad087 (citation quoted from the package README, https://github.com/BBCG/epialleleR/).

README, quoted: the package can "**test for the association between epiallele methylation status and sequence variations (`generateVcfReport`)**".

Vignette, quoted (https://bioc-release.r-universe.dev/epialleleR/doc/epialleleR.Rmd): `generateVcfReport` "calculates frequencies of single nucleotide variants (SNVs) within epialleles" and "**tests for the association between SNV and epiallelic status using Fisher Exact test**"; "Base counts and the test's p-values are included in the returned value"; p-values "are given separately for reads that map to the '+'" and likewise the '-' strand; "NA values are shown for the C->T variants on the '+' and G->A on the '-'" because "bisulfite conversion makes their counting impossible".

Paper, quoted (OUP page): read-level VEF is `Ca/(C + T)` with read-level thresholding that "defines a subpopulation of epialleles of interest"; at region level "VEF = Na/N", the "ratio of read pairs passing the threshold to all read pairs"; default thresholds include a "minimum average methylation beta value of 0.5 for CpG sites"; VCF input lets the tool "quantif[y] the balance or skewness of methylation between alleles"; the paper names "single-nucleotide variations or mutations within the immediate proximity" as possible *cis*-factors for epimutations; Figure 1 describes an epiallele that "represents the product of incomplete bisulfite conversion".

Mapped onto the ledger's four attributes:

| | epialleleR `generateVcfReport` | Candidate V2 |
|---|---|---|
| Unit | the read (read pair) | the molecule |
| Endpoint | that read's methylation density over the region, thresholded ("epiallele status") | that molecule's 5mC density over a window |
| Comparator | reads carrying the other base at the same VCF position, same sample, strand-separated | mutation-free molecules matched on locus, sample, strand |
| Estimand | association between base identity and read methylation status (Fisher exact, base counts, per strand) | matched conditional-logistic OR, same association |

Unit, endpoint, comparator and estimand all coincide. What the candidate adds is design hygiene on the same question: somatic rather than any variant, a non-clonal tissue frame, exclusion of the CpG the mutation creates or destroys, class stratification, a negative-control class, a per-read error model, and conditional logistic in place of Fisher. Fisher's exact test on a 2x2 within one locus *is* the exact conditional test for that locus's stratum; stratifying it is a routine extension, not a new estimand.

## The already-tabled bisulfite-conversion attack — my assessment: it does not kill, and I decline to carry it

- The 77% figure (IQR 75-79%) is **per library**, per fact (4); the paper reports no per-molecule conversion rate. Inferring "roughly a quarter of unmethylated cytosines read as methylated on each molecule" from a library average is a unit substitution, and it is contradicted by the same paper's own observations: 1.3% of 398 C's and 3.1% of 65 C's apparent methylation at cytosines the mutation had already moved out of CpG context (fact (2)) — a per-site false-positive floor of a few percent, not ~23%. (That comparison is my own reading of the checkers' quoted numbers; I did not reopen the preprint, and the 3.1% rests on 65 C's, so its own uncertainty is wide — roughly a couple of events.) The "correlated across the window because conversion failure is a per-molecule chemical event" step is asserted, not sourced; I found no primary source for it within budget and I will not score a kill on it. **Status: unsourced, Partial at best — not used.**
- On its merits: both arms of a within-locus, within-sample matched contrast come from the same library and the same bisulfite reaction, so a shared conversion deficit is misclassification of the *exposure measurement* that is common to both arms. Common, non-differential misclassification of this kind attenuates a contrast toward the null and costs power rather than manufacturing one; it would bias the contrast only if non-conversion were differential by mutation status, and the window excludes the mutated base, leaving only second-order sequence-context effects. I flag the scope limit honestly: this is my own measurement-error reasoning, not cited, and it cuts against the *bounded-null* branch of the question, where attenuation and a true null are indistinguishable without a measured conversion rate.
- Per-read conversion QC **is** available here, and that is partly sourced: fact (2)'s comparator cytosines are non-CpG trimer C's used as conversion controls, and epialleleR's paper describes a read-level epiallele that "represents the product of incomplete bisulfite conversion" with simulations at ~99.75% conversion — i.e. read-level conversion state is a modelled, detectable quantity, not an unmeasurable one. A mammalian read carries tens of non-CpG cytosines, so the per-read error model the candidate pre-specifies is constructible from the read's own data. The conversion objection is therefore a power-and-calibration problem the candidate already names, not a defeater.
