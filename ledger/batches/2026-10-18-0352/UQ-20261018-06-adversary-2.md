# UQ-20261018-06 — Adversary 2 (independent; first adversary log not read)

## Restatement in my own words

Pick one environmental network and one thing it measures (river discharge, or tide-gauge surge, or gust speed). Fix a list of (station, storm-window) pairs in advance, where the storms come from an outside catalogue and not from the station's own trace, and get each pair's true peak from something other than that station's own sensor (high-water marks, a survey, neighbours, a hindcast), with a stated error model. Then ask two things. (a) Empirically: how does the chance that the sensor stops reporting during the window rise with how big the event actually was — a failure hazard h(y) in the units of the measurand, with error bars; is it flat? (b) Statistically: if you refit GEV/POT treating a lost peak as censored (either with an explicit non-ignorable selection model, or as interval-censored from below by the last value the gauge managed to send), do the 50- and 100-year return levels go up or down relative to the standard fit on the published annual maxima, and by how much?

In short: are the records used to set design values systematically missing their own largest entries because the instrument died of the thing it was measuring, and does fixing that move the design numbers?
