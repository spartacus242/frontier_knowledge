Question (Gate 0 V1, as intaken — recorded per the one-reformulation rule):

Alternative formulations:
- How old is the medicine when the patient actually gets it, how much does that vary between products and channels, and does the variation matter clinically?
- Treating the distribution chain as an uncontrolled exposure now made visible by serialization, can per-package provenance be linked to potency assays and patient outcomes to test whether "within expiry" is a sufficient statement of product quality?

Motivating gap:
Serialized, interoperable package-level tracing was built for anticounterfeiting and, as a by-product, makes per-package provenance — manufacture date, custody steps, dwell times — a queryable quantity at national scale. Stability testing and expiry dating certify potency under LABELLED storage for a BATCH; the realised exposure of an individual package (how long it sat where, in whose custody) is uncontrolled and was, before serialization, unmeasurable. No body joins that provenance to potency or to outcome, and expiry is treated as binary.

Significance — consequence:
If YES (provenance predicts potency or a potency-sensitive endpoint within labelled shelf life): expiry dating is revealed as a lossy summary of a quantity that is now measurable per package, which bears directly on how stability data are used — labelled shelf life is set from batch stability under label conditions, and a demonstrated realised-exposure effect would argue for either shortening dating for products with long distribution tails or managing remaining shelf life at dispensing (FEFO at the patient interface, channel-specific limits) rather than only expiry. It would also make serialized provenance a quality-surveillance instrument: the first routinely collected, population-scale covariate for product quality, analogous to what claims data were for drug safety.
If NO: current binary expiry practice is defended with direct population-scale evidence instead of batch-level inference, which is itself consequential — it would retire a standing worry (mail-order heat, long distribution dwell, near-expiry dispensing) that currently generates intermittent journalism (query 10) and local policy without measurement, and it would set an upper bound on how much a provenance-aware dispensing policy could buy.
Either way, limb i alone produces a number nobody has: how old the medicine is when the patient gets it, and how much of that is within-product rather than between-product.

Significance — audience:
(1) Regulators setting stability and dating policy (FDA OPQ/CDER, EMA Quality Working Party, ICH stability guideline revision) — a positive finding is an argument for conditioning dating or labelling on realised distribution profiles; (2) pharmacopoeial and standards bodies (USP, Ph.Eur.) deciding whether monograph limits should be checked at the patient end; (3) hospital and chain-pharmacy quality units and PBMs/mail-order operators, who choose inventory rotation rules and shipping practice and currently have no quality endpoint to optimise against; (4) WHO prequalification and LMIC procurement, where minimum remaining shelf life at delivery is already a contract term set by convention and not by measured quality (query 4); (5) the serialization data holders and DSCSA governance bodies, for whom this is the first non-compliance use of the asset they built.
How they would act differently: a non-null limb ii changes rotation and channel rules before it changes dating; a non-null limb iii is what would move a guideline. A null changes nothing and that is a usable result for (3) and (4), who are spending money on shelf-life terms.

Significance — fecundity:
Makes askable: is labelled expiry the most informative single statistic a package can carry, versus a provenance-conditioned remaining-potency estimate? Does channel heterogeneity create a quality inequity (mail-order in hot climates, rural long-tail distribution, 340B/specialty paths)? Can serialized provenance be used as a routine quality-surveillance stream — a pharmacovigilance analogue for product quality, with signal detection on dwell-time anomalies? Should stability protocols be re-weighted from label conditions to the measured realised-exposure distribution? And, methodologically: is the distribution chain an instrumentable natural experiment, with dwell-time variation as an instrument for delivered dose?

Significance — anchoring:
Touches the expired-drug-potency literature and SLEP-type findings (prior B's genre), the substandard-and-falsified-medicines burden estimates (where "degraded" is one of the categories and is the hardest to attribute), the lot-to-lot outcome-variation question (ledger UQ-20261016-27), cold-chain integrity programmes and VVM practice (prior A), and the standing unexplained-variance problem in narrow-therapeutic-index products where dose-response is tight and delivered content is assumed exact.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism — three mechanisms, compounding; this is the Gate 4 attack-7 answer to beat):
NEW INSTRUMENT crossed with a DISCIPLINARY BOUNDARY and an INCENTIVE GAP. (a) New instrument: package-level provenance only became a queryable quantity with interoperable serialization, built as an anticounterfeiting compliance artefact; before it, the realised exposure of an individual package was not a variable anyone could hold. (b) Boundary: the field that owns the DATA (supply-chain compliance and IT, vendors like the one whose brief was opened) has tracing and recall questions and no quality-science estimand — its own literature treats the data as a pedigree-generation and efficiency problem (prior C); the field that owns the METHOD (clinical pharmacology, pharmacoepidemiology) has the estimand and no access, and largely does not know the data exist. Prior B is the boundary made visible: provenance fields were collected on the sample form and never analysed. (c) Incentive: the data are commercially confidential and held by trading partners, and a finding of in-expiry subpotency implicates a named manufacturer, wholesaler and channel — so neither the data holder nor the manufacturer has a reason to run the analysis, and the one party with both access and an outcome interest (a large dispenser) carries the liability if the answer is positive. Stability science meanwhile resolved "is it potent?" into a batch-level, label-condition design that makes expiry sufficient by construction, leaving realised per-package exposure with no place to be a variable.
