## WHAT THE SOURCE SAYS (quotations as served)

### Document type and front matter

A UK government **EXPERT GROUP REPORT advising the Department of Health** — a
policy-advisory review that also commissioned and analysed new data collection. It
is not a primary peer-reviewed study, not a protocol, not a clinical guideline, not
an editorial.

Remit, as served in 5 fragments from the Introduction:

> "the expert group set up under my chairmanship" / "to review evidence of the
> potential risks to health of the silicone gel breast implants" / "made by the
> French company Poly Implant Prothèse (PIP)" / "to advise the Department of Health"
> / "on policy in relation to women who received these implants."

(5 fragments. The served text reads "in Englanda" at one point, a footnote marker
fused to the word; the summariser said it omitted that word. So the remit is
England-scoped and I could not read the footnote.)

Front matter as served: cover title "Poly Implant Prothèse (PIP) breast implants:
final report of the Expert Group" (with the è accent); **reader box title differs —
"Poly Implant Prothese (PIP) Breast Implants: Final Report of the Working Group"**
("Working Group", not "Expert Group"); author field Department of Health, NHS
Medical Directorate; chaired and signed by Sir Bruce Keogh, NHS Medical Director;
18 June 2012.

**"Keogh B et al." is not front matter.** This is a Department of Health corporate
publication with a named chair and signatory. There is no author byline list of the
"Keogh B, X Y, Z A" form on the document. Anyone citing it as "Keogh B et al."
supplied that author string themselves.

### (a) The 35,000 — YES, but it is a DENOMINATOR

Appears in paragraph 23. **Reconstruction from 4 ordered consecutive fragments:**

> "The MHRA's own monitoring of adverse events shows that, between 2007 and 2009,
> 201 ruptures of PIP implants" / " had been reported, representing a rupture rate
> of about 0.6% based on an estimated 35,000 women" / " with PIP implants; over the
> same period 70 ruptures were reported from other brands" / " who had a larger
> share of the market."

The exact string handed to me, "an estimated 35,000 women", is **present verbatim**,
and the figure is printed as a page of prose, not a table.

Note the grammar as served at the last junction — "other brands **who** had a larger
share of the market" — reproduced as served, not corrected.

**What the 35,000 counts.** It is the **estimated number of women in the UK with PIP
implants, used as the denominator of a rupture-rate calculation** over the MHRA
reporting window 2007–2009. It is an estimate, not a register count. Three things
follow:

1. It is **not** a count of women still implanted after the 2012 explantation wave.
   The report **does not state how many of the 35,000 still have the implants in
   place.** Asked directly, the only UK explantation figures it gives are partial:
   "Since January 2012, surgeons have carried out a substantial number of
   explantations of PIP implants." and "To date, 761 questionnaires have been
   returned." (para 19). The 35,000 denominator predates that wave.
2. It is **not** the report's largest cohort figure and must not be swapped for one.
   Paragraph 14 prints "In response to this request we received information on some
   240,000 implants given to 130,000 women" and "detailed findings from 5,600
   explant operations" — that 130,000 is **all brands**, from the provider
   questionnaire, a different unit entirely.
3. A parallel French figure exists and is a different country: paragraph 21 prints
   "7.5% of the estimated 30,000 French women with PIP implants". 35,000 (UK) and
   30,000 (France) are easy to cross-contaminate.

### (b) The 2–6 factor — YES, stated twice, and the two formulations differ

**Formulation 1, Summary.** Reconstruction from 2 consecutive fragments:

> "PIP implants are significantly more likely to rupture or leak silicone than other
> implants, by a factor of around 2-6," / "and this difference is detectable within 5
> years of implantation;"

**Formulation 2, paragraph 17(i).** As served:

> "PIP implants are more likely to fail than other implants, by a factor of around
> 2-6"

and, same sub-point:

> "the divergence between PIP and other implants is already apparent after 5 years"

Per rule (4): the Summary version names the endpoint as **"rupture or leak
silicone"**; the paragraph 17(i) version names it as **"fail"**. Neither carries an
external citation — both rest on the report's own retrospective analysis of the
provider returns. Neither is hedged beyond "around".

**The underlying printed numbers.** Paragraph 17(ii), reconstruction from 2
consecutive fragments:

> "the failure rate for PIP implants is estimated on the basis of these reported
> adverse events" / "at 1.2% at 5 years, rising to 3.1% at 10 years"

and the comparator figures as served:

> "This compares with a failure rate for other brands of silicone gel implant of
> 0.2 - 0.4% at 5 years" ... "and 0.5 - 1.1% at 10 years"

So the 2–6 range is the ratio band implied by those four numbers
(1.2/0.4 = 3.0, 1.2/0.2 = 6.0, 3.1/1.1 = 2.8, 3.1/0.5 = 6.2), re-derived at the
report's own time points. The arithmetic coheres: the report prints both the ratio
band and the component rates, so the "2-6" is not a figure someone else computed.

**A SECOND, DIFFERENT factor exists in the same paragraph and is not the same claim.**
Paragraph 17(iii), as served:

> "PIP implants are more likely than other implants, by a factor of around 3-5, to
> result in local clinical signs"

3–5 is for **local clinical signs**, a different endpoint from rupture/failure.
Paragraph 17 also records **no** excess of "capsular contraction, haematoma or
cancer" (served as paraphrase, not quotation — I did not get that clause verbatim).

**Follow-up.** 5 and 10 years **since implantation**. The 17(ii) sentence itself does
not spell the time base out; the explicit anchor is elsewhere — paragraph 20 refers
to "the rupture rate at 10 years after implantation", and paragraph 33(iii) to
patients who "may need surgery at some stage within 10 years of implantation". The
report does **not** use the word "cumulative". Underlying data window: provider
returns for 2001–2011, requested February 2012.

**The report's own caveat on its own number.** Paragraph 17(ii), 2 consecutive
fragments:

> "However the true underlying failure rate, including "silent" ruptures, will be
> greater than this" / "for the reasons set out above;"

### (c) The CONSTRUCT — PRODUCT DEFECT / FRAUD, not manufacturer exit, not support withdrawal

Paragraph 2, 2 consecutive fragments:

> "In our interim report1, published in January 2012, we reviewed the data then
> available on the toxicity" / " of the non-medical grade silicone illegally used by
> PIP and on the rates of rupture of the implants."

Paragraph 18(i), as served: **"after the discovery of the fraud in March 2010"**.
Paragraph 29, as served: women "assumed in good faith that their implants contained
medical grade silicone". Paragraph 2(vii), as served: "recognised however the
anxiety of many women who received PIP implants in good faith" / "on the assumption
that they were manufactured in accordance with EC standards".

So the event is a **manufacturing fraud / product defect**: a device built with
silicone the manufacturer was not approved to use, discovered March 2010.

**On the manufacturer's corporate status: the report says nothing.** I searched it
for "liquidation", "insolvent", "insolvency", "ceased", "closed down", "out of
business" and "Mas" (Jean-Claude Mas). **No match for any of them.** The report does
not state that PIP closed, was wound up, went insolvent, or stopped trading. Per
rule (9), that silence is not a denial — it is an absence of the fact in this
source, and a support-withdrawal or manufacturer-exit question cannot be sourced to
this document.

**The trap.** The phrase **"no longer in business" DOES appear** — and it is about
**private healthcare providers**, i.e. the UK clinics that implanted, **not the
manufacturer**. Paragraph 2(x), reconstruction from 4 consecutive fragments:

> "endorsed the decision of ministers that, where a private provider was no longer
> in business" / " or was unwilling or unable to meet their moral and legal
> obligations towards their patients," / " the NHS should offer a consultation, a
> scan where appropriate, and removal (but not normally replacement)" / " of the
> implants where a woman and her doctor decided this was the right thing to do."

This is the **nearest thing in the entire report to a support-withdrawal
construct**, and it is a different animal: a **service provider's commercial exit
creating an aftercare gap, backfilled by the public health system**. It is about who
pays for and performs the consultation, scan and explant — not about the device
losing manufacturer support, firmware, parts or monitoring. A reader scanning for
"no longer in business" and finding it here would mis-attribute it to PIP.

Note also the aftercare obligation is framed as "moral and legal obligations", and
the NHS fallback explicitly offers "removal (but not normally replacement)".

### (d) POST-EXIT PERIOD — NOT defined, NOT measured; March 2010 is used the OPPOSITE way

There is no post-exit exposure window in this report. March 2010 appears in two
roles, and in neither is it an exposure period whose outcomes are measured.

**Role 1, descriptive.** Paragraph 21, 2 consecutive fragments:

> "Data from the French regulator ANSM (formerly AFSSAPS) show a very sharp increase"
> / " in the number of ruptures reported after March 2010, and a further upturn after
> December 2011."

That is a rise in **reporting**, not in failure.

**Role 2, and this is the decisive one — March 2010 is a SENSITIVITY EXCLUSION, i.e.
the post-discovery era is treated as a NUISANCE to be removed.** Paragraph 18 lead-in,
as served: "We carried out a large number of additional analyses to test the
robustness of these findings." Then 18(i), reconstruction from 5 consecutive
fragments:

> "the excess problems for PIP implants may be partially the result of reporting
> bias" / "(the greater tendency for women to seek explantation or for clinicians to
> report problems)" / "after the discovery of the fraud in March 2010, but this
> accounts only for some of the excess" / "excluding data from 2010 onwards still
> results in a clear and statistically significant excess risk" / "on all measures
> for PIP implants"

So the report **excludes** the post-discovery data to check that its excess-risk
finding survives; it does not measure outcomes within that period as a period of
interest. The failure rates themselves are indexed to **time since implantation**
(5 years, 10 years), i.e. **over the implant lifetime regardless of the
manufacturer's status**.

This is the exact inverse of a post-exit-period design. Anyone citing this report as
a precedent for "outcomes measured after the manufacturer went away" has the
direction of the analysis backwards.

A second robustness check, 18(ii), 2 consecutive fragments:

> "relatively few PIP implants were used for NHS breast reconstructions" / "but an
> excess risk was still observed in this group"

### (e) SUPPORT construct — NONE, explicitly

Asked directly whether the report anywhere discusses implant programming, software,
batteries, replacement parts, external components, remote monitoring, or ongoing
technical support for the implants: **"None."** No such discussion anywhere in the
document.

This is the expected answer for the device class. A PIP implant is a **passive
silicone gel prosthesis**. It has no programming to orphan, no battery to replace,
no external component to discontinue, no telemetry to switch off. **There is no
support to withdraw.** The only "support" analogue in the report is human clinical
aftercare by the implanting provider — see (c) — which is a service relationship,
not a device dependency.

### (f) COMPARATOR — "other brands" as an aggregate; NO matching

**No patient-level matching of any kind is described.** PIP is compared with "other
brands" as an undifferentiated group, with **bias adjustment and sensitivity
analyses substituting for matching**.

Data sources, as served. The commissioned retrospective collection, paragraph 14 /
February 2012: "In February 2012, at the request of the expert group, MHRA contacted
all the major providers of breast implants", seeking returns on PIP and other brands
for 2001–2011 across NHS and private providers, yielding "some 240,000 implants
given to 130,000 women" and "detailed findings from 5,600 explant operations".
Prospective arm: explant questionnaires via the surgical associations ABS, BAPRAS
and BAAPS, of which "To date, 761 questionnaires have been returned." Plus MHRA
spontaneous adverse incident reports, ANSM (French regulator) data, the US FDA core
studies, and a Berry and Stanek follow-up study.

The report itself concedes the comparability problem. Paragraph 15, 2 consecutive
fragments:

> "This illustrates the difficulty in ensuring that rupture rates for different
> implants" / "are compared on a truly comparable basis"

And paragraph 23 concedes the denominator asymmetry in the same breath as the 35,000:
the other brands "had a larger share of the market".

**The ascertainment mismatch, which is the sharpest thing in the report for anyone
reusing its headline number.** Paragraph 15, 3 consecutive fragments:

> "For primary augmentation, rupture rates of 10% (95% confidence interval 7-14%) at
> 10 years" / "have been reported for the Allergan implants" / "and of 14% (7-24%)
> at 8 years for the Mentor implants, using a subgroup being followed up by MRI"

Those are **FDA-required core studies with an MRI-followed subgroup** — active
imaging surveillance. They give **higher absolute rupture rates for non-PIP implants
(10% at 10y, 14% at 8y) than the report's own PIP figure (3.1% at 10y)**, because
the ascertainment is completely different. The report presents this only as a broad
comparison — the Summary says the PIP estimates "can be broadly compared with the
reported rupture rates of 10% at 10 years for Allergan" — and paragraph 15 states it
does not regard such comparisons as like-for-like. I could not obtain a verbatim
sentence in which the report declares the Allergan/Mentor figures comparable or
incomparable to the PIP figures; the summariser's reading is that it does not claim
comparability.

Attribution, per rule (5): the cancer conclusion is **not the Expert Group's own
finding**. Paragraph 2(i), 2 consecutive fragments: "We i endorsed the advice from
the French cancer institute2 that PIP implants" / " were not associated with a higher
risk of cancer than other breast implants". The group endorses; the French cancer
institute found.

## REGIME

**Population.** Women in England/UK who received PIP silicone gel breast implants,
remit-scoped to England (footnote unread). Comparator population: recipients of all
other implant brands reported through the same UK provider channels, 2001–2011.

**Unit of analysis.** Mixed and inconsistent across the report, which matters.
Rupture/failure rates are per **implant-or-woman from reported adverse events**
(paragraph 23's 0.6% and 2.2% are reports over an estimated 35,000 **women**;
paragraph 14's collection counts 240,000 **implants** given to 130,000 **women**).
Anyone converting between implant-denominated and woman-denominated rates is doing
arithmetic the report does not print.

**Endpoint.** "failure" / "rupture or leak silicone" as **captured by spontaneous
adverse-incident reporting plus retrospective provider questionnaire returns**, not
by imaging surveillance. The report says explicitly that silent ruptures are missed
and the true rate is higher. Secondary endpoint: "local clinical signs" (the 3–5
factor). Explicitly null endpoints: capsular contraction, haematoma, cancer.

**Comparator.** Aggregate "other brands", unmatched, with acknowledged market-share
asymmetry in the denominators.

**Estimand.** A relative risk expressed as an unmodelled ratio band ("a factor of
around 2-6") plus absolute failure percentages at 5 and 10 years since implantation.
No confidence intervals are printed on the PIP 1.2%/3.1% figures or on the 2–6 band;
the only intervals I found are on the imported FDA figures (7–14%, 7–24%).

**Threshold / time base.** 5 and 10 years post-implantation; divergence stated as
already visible by 5 years.

**Causal regime.** A **manufacturing-fraud product defect** discovered March 2010,
with the post-discovery period handled as a reporting-bias confounder to be excluded.

**Does the claim handed to me sit inside this regime?** Split verdict:
- Sub-claims (a) and (b) sit **inside** it, with unit caveats: the 35,000 is a
  denominator of women implanted, not of women still implanted, and the 2–6 factor is
  valid only inside reported-adverse-event ascertainment, not as an absolute rupture
  risk.
- Sub-claims (c), (d), (e) and (f) sit **outside** it. The report establishes a
  product-defect event with lifetime-indexed outcomes, no post-exit window, no
  support dependency and no matched comparator. Cited for a support-withdrawal
  question, this source is **silent, not supportive.**

## DEFECTS FOUND

1. **"Keogh B et al." is a fabricated author string** (rule 7). The front matter
   gives a Department of Health corporate publication with Sir Bruce Keogh as chair
   and signatory. There is no "et al." byline list. Anyone citing "Keogh B et al."
   took it from a short form someone else wrote.

2. **Title discrepancy inside the document itself**: cover says "final report of the
   **Expert Group**", the reader box says "Final Report of the **Working Group**".
   Reported as served, not reconciled.

3. **The 35,000 is a denominator, and its unit is "women with PIP implants" at
   2007–2009 — not women still living with the device.** The report nowhere states
   how many of the 35,000 remained implanted. A ledger entry reading it as a count of
   people currently carrying an unsupported device would be a unit error.

4. **Confusable cohort figures.** 35,000 (UK, PIP) vs 130,000 women / 240,000
   implants (UK, all brands, provider returns) vs 30,000 (France, PIP). Three
   different units in one document.

5. **"No longer in business" refers to private PROVIDERS, not to the manufacturer.**
   This is the single most mis-attributable phrase in the report and the one most
   likely to have generated a false manufacturer-exit reading.

6. **The report contains no statement at all about PIP's corporate fate** — no
   liquidation, insolvency, administration, cessation or Mas. A manufacturer-exit or
   insolvency claim cannot be sourced here. (Silence, not denial.)

7. **March 2010 is used as a bias EXCLUSION, not as a post-exit exposure window** —
   the opposite of the design a support-withdrawal precedent would need. Citing the
   report for post-exit outcome measurement inverts what it did.

8. **The 2–6 factor is ascertainment-bound and absolute rates do not travel.** The
   report's own imported FDA/MRI figures for other brands (10% at 10y Allergan, 14%
   at 8y Mentor) exceed its PIP figure (3.1% at 10y). Quoting "2–6 times more likely
   to fail" without the reported-adverse-event qualifier detaches it from the only
   regime in which it holds — the same detachment-from-qualifier defect flagged in
   the last batch.

9. **Two endpoints, two factors.** 2–6 is rupture/failure; 3–5 is local clinical
   signs. Conflating them would overstate.

10. **Two formulations of the headline claim**, Summary ("rupture or leak silicone")
    and 17(i) ("fail"), neither externally cited, neither hedged beyond "around". The
    Summary version adds the "detectable within 5 years" qualifier.

11. **No uncertainty printed on the load-bearing figures** (rule 8): the PIP
    1.2%/3.1% and the 2–6 band carry no confidence intervals. Only the imported FDA
    figures do.

12. **The cancer finding is endorsed, not found** (rule 5): it belongs to the French
    cancer institute.

13. **No matched comparator**; market-share asymmetry conceded by the report itself
    in the same sentence as the 35,000.

14. **My own read is summariser-mediated.** All long quotations are reconstructions
    from ordered consecutive fragments (counts stated: 2, 3, 4 and 5 fragments at
    various points). Fragment junctions are where an error would hide. I did not
    complete any cut string by inference, and the summariser **refused one bulk
    verbatim request outright**, so a byte-exact transcription of paragraphs 17, 21
    and 23 is still not in hand.

15. **Page count not established**; the England-scoping footnote marker ("in
    Englanda") was not readable.

## BEARING ON THE LEDGER ENTRY

**(a) holds.** "an estimated 35,000 women" is printed verbatim, paragraph 23, as the
denominator of a 0.6% rupture rate over MHRA reports 2007–2009. It counts **women in
the UK estimated to have PIP implants**, i.e. recipients — **not** women still
implanted, and not a post-explantation residual. The report never gives that
residual.

**(b) holds, with a hard qualifier.** "by a factor of around 2-6" is printed twice,
Summary and paragraph 17(i), backed by printed component rates (PIP 1.2% at 5y, 3.1%
at 10y; other brands 0.2–0.4% at 5y, 0.5–1.1% at 10y) over follow-up measured from
implantation. The factor is only meaningful inside **reported-adverse-event
ascertainment**; the report itself says the true rate is higher and that cross-brand
rate comparison is not on "a truly comparable basis". A separate 3–5 factor applies
to local clinical signs and is not the same claim.

**(c) refutes the support-withdrawal framing.** The construct is a **product defect /
manufacturing fraud** — "the non-medical grade silicone illegally used by PIP",
"after the discovery of the fraud in March 2010". The report says **nothing** about
PIP's insolvency or exit. The one "no longer in business" sentence concerns **private
clinics' aftercare obligations**, with the NHS backfilling consultation, scanning and
removal — a payer/provider-exit construct, not a device-support construct.

**(d) refutes.** No post-exit period is defined or measured. Outcomes are indexed to
time since implantation across the whole implant lifetime, and the post-discovery era
is **excluded** as a reporting-bias confounder.

**(e) refutes, and this is the decisive one.** There is **no support construct at
all** — no programming, parts, external components or remote monitoring anywhere in
the document. A passive gel prosthesis has nothing to withdraw. The construct is
therefore **different in kind** from a support-withdrawal question, not merely
different in degree.

**(f) refutes.** No matched comparison. Aggregate "other brands", unmatched,
market-share-asymmetric denominators, bias adjustment in place of matching.

**Net.** This report is **good prior art for a product-defect mass-notification and
aftercare-liability event**, including the narrow and genuinely relevant point that a
**private provider's commercial exit** can strand patients and shift aftercare onto a
public system. It is **not prior art for a device-support-withdrawal question**: no
support dependency, no manufacturer-exit finding, no post-exit measurement window, no
matched comparator. Two of the six sub-claims land inside its regime with unit and
ascertainment caveats; four land outside it, where the source is silent rather than
supportive.
