## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take engineering failure analysts — the people who look at a broken part and say "fatigue", "stress-corrosion cracking", "ductile overload" and so on — and test them the way fingerprint and firearms examiners were tested after the 2009 NAS report. Break specimens in the lab so the mechanism is known by how they were made; give the specimens to analysts working alone and blind; fix the menu of answers and allow "can't tell"; and randomise what else each analyst is told: who hired them (which should not matter), and a service history that is true, false or absent (which legitimately does matter, so the manipulation is of truth, not relevance). Report how often they pick the wrong mechanism, how often they decline, how much they disagree with one another, and how much each kind of context moves those numbers. The novelty claim is that nobody has measured ATTRIBUTION accuracy of failure analysts against constructed truth — only DETECTION (NDT probability of detection) and only reproducibility of measured values (toughness round robins).
