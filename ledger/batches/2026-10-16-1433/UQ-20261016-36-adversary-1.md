## Restatement (my own words, before reading the generator's framing twice)

Inside one clinic or manufacturing facility, take cryopreserved units of the same specimen type, frozen under one protocol. Does *where in the tank a unit sat* — its vessel, canister, rack and height, and the time-weighted temperature that position implies from the facility's own thermal map — explain any of the leftover unit-to-unit variation in how well units do after thawing, once cohort, calendar date, donor and operator are accounted for? Two numbers are wanted: the share of residual variance carried by static storage coordinate, and the change in the post-thaw endpoint per 10 °C of time-weighted storage temperature. The identifying variation is meant to come from re-racking and tank transfers, which move a unit's position without moving its cohort, date or donor. The intake version bundled two further limbs (cumulative neighbourhood-retrieval exposure; a between-facility decomposition of published survival spread); Gate 1 kept only the static-coordinate limb, and that is the question I attack.

## Spot-check of the record's cited priors

- **Prior 1 real and accurately characterised.** PMC10433674 confirms: Zhang H, Ye D, Wu Y, Li Y, Huang X (2023), DOI 10.1186/s12884-023-05879-w; 9,200 vitrified-warmed cycles (3,313 D3 / 4,530 D5 / 1,357 D6); bystander counting confirmed verbatim — "if the cane was loaded with another straw before, the other straws in this cane were marked as two times to their EAF"; coordinates logged — "the position in the specially appointed cane, canister and tank of every straw were recorded"; position **not** among the adjusted covariates; nulls as quoted (D3 EAF≥10 clinical pregnancy OR 0.867, 95% CI 0.672–1.119); storage time rose across EAF groups (D3 ≈83 → ≈301 days, P < 0.001) and was adjusted for. Two corrections to the record's gloss: the paper declares **no** primary/secondary endpoint hierarchy (the record's "primary clinical / secondary survival" split is the record's own), and EAF is explicitly an **event count, not a duration** — "EAF is calculated by the number of exposed-to-air during the procedure rather than duration time of exposed-to-air."
- **Prior 2 real but mis-cited and, more seriously, mis-read.** The work is Sansinena MJ, Santos MV, Taminelli GL, Zaritzky NE, *Theriogenology* 82(3):373–378, August 2014 — a peer-reviewed journal article, not the "Universidad Católica Argentina" repository item the record cites, and under a different title ("Implications of storage and handling conditions on glass transition and potential devitrification of oocytes and embryos"). Tg −126 to −121 °C and devitrification onset −109 °C confirmed. **But** the CONICET record describes the two modelled temperatures (−50 °C, −70 °C) as "two vapor-phase temperatures within the dewar" and the scope as exposure "either during storage or upon its removal", concluding that **vapour-phase storage** of vitrified material carries devitrification risk. The record's load-bearing re-reading — that Sansiñena et al. locate the risk in a transient *neck* temperature a unit passes through when lifted, and therefore not in the stored position — is not what the cited source says. Scope caveat on my own claim: I read the repository record's description, not the full Theriogenology text, and the fetch declined to reproduce the abstract verbatim; so this makes the record's reading unsupported, not its converse established.
- **Prior 3 corroborated as real** (DOI 10.3390/app16157700, ISSN 2076-3417, surfaced independently in my query 1); I did not re-open it, so its quoted figures stand unverified by me. Note on arithmetic: Prior 3 reports monitoring-point *averages* and between-point *spreads*, so the warmest single point in the non-automated full case is somewhere above −165.4 °C and, if the spread were symmetric about the mean (my assumption, not the paper's), around −160 °C — still ~35 °C below Tg. That is my arithmetic on their numbers, not their conclusion.
- **New find, not in the record:** Vysekantsev IP, Kudokotseva OV, Petrenko TF, Gurina TM, Groshevoy MI, Martsenyuk VF, Koshchiy SV, "Effect of Storage Temperature Variations on Viability of Cryopreserved Cells of Pro- and Eukaryotes", Problems of Cryobiology and Cryomedicine (2020). Abstract: "There was experimentally found the probability of extradeath of microorganism, human embryos' and tumour cells when varying the temperature from –196 up to –130 and –100°C." So the sub-/near-Tg storage-temperature dose question has been asked experimentally, in Ukrainian cryobiology, for human embryos among other cells — in a controlled design, with no position variable and (in the abstract) no numbers. It is not the candidate's observational position-based estimand, but it blocks any "obviously zero" dismissal of the temperature-dose limb.

## Note to the judge on the Gate 2 ground

I was told not to inherit the Low grade, and on inspection it does not survive intact for the question actually on the table. Prior 1 is real and quoted accurately, but it is aimed at a different object: its exposure is a retrieval-**event count** (explicitly "rather than duration time"), its unit is the transfer **cycle**, its endpoints are clinical, and it demonstrably did not analyse the static coordinate it had logged. The candidate's reformulated estimand — a variance share for static coordinate plus an effect per 10 °C of time-weighted temperature — is not what Zhang et al. estimated, and nothing I found in eight other vocabularies estimates it either. Further, the record's mechanism-falsification rests on a mis-reading of its own Prior 2: Sansiñena et al. warn that vapour-phase *storage* carries devitrification risk, which is close to the opposite of the use the record puts them to. What actually kills the candidate is not duplication and not falsified physics; it is attack 6 — the answer has no one who would act on it.
