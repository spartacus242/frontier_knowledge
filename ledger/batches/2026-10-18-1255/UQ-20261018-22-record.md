Domain(s): Medicine — emergency and critical-care clinical research; research ethics and regulatory science. Method field: survey methodology / coverage-and-nonresponse error.
Source method: presupposition inversion

Question (canonical):

V1 (as intaken): For completed trials conducted under exception from informed consent (EFIC), how closely does the population that took part in the mandated community consultation match the patients the trial actually enrolled — in site catchment, age, sex, race/ethnicity and socioeconomic position — and in what fraction of trials is the overlap so low that the consulted community and the enrolled population are effectively disjoint?

V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted; scored as canonical): Over a declared frame of completed FDA-regulated EFIC trials (the 105-trial EFIC census as the starting frame, restricted to the 77 completed as of its close), with a consultation-participant group individuated before any data pull by a declared rule (a group of consulted individuals whose own characteristics were recorded — survey respondents, event attendees, interviewees or random-digit-dial samples — as distinct from designated community representatives), and with enrolled-patient characteristics taken from each trial's 50.24(a)(7)(iii) public disclosure or primary publication: (a) in what fraction of trials were consultation-participant characteristics recorded at all, and in what fraction are they retrievable now; (b) among the retrievable trials, what is the distribution of a pre-declared distributional-overlap statistic (total variation distance and standardised mean differences over pre-declared strata: age band, sex, race/ethnicity, and an area-based socioeconomic measure) between consulted and enrolled groups, together with a separate geographic measure of overlap between consultation activity location and enrolment catchment; and (c) under a pre-declared disjointness rule, in what fraction of trials are the two groups effectively disjoint?

Reformulation rationale (well-posedness only): V1's "the population that took part in the mandated community consultation" is not well defined for trials whose consultation was conducted with designated representatives rather than with a sample, and V1 leaves the non-recording fraction implicit. V2 makes the individuation rule explicit, makes the non-recording fraction co-primary rather than attrition, names the frame, and fixes the statistics in advance. No change of subject.

Alternative formulations:
- Did the people consulted before an EFIC trial resemble the people the trial went on to enrol?
- Treating community consultation as a sampling exercise from the at-risk population, what is its coverage and its selection bias, trial by trial?

Motivating gap:

The settled claim being inverted: EFIC research is ethically permissible in part because the community from which subjects will be drawn is consulted beforehand. The regulation's operative text, read at eCFR this run — 21 CFR 50.24(a)(7)(i), reconstructed from three consecutive segments returned separately by the summariser (fragment junctions verified by continuity of the single sentence; labelled a reconstruction):

  "Consultation (including, where appropriate, consultation carried out by the IRB)" / "with representatives of the communities in which the clinical investigation will be conducted" / "and from which the subjects will be drawn;"

Two facts established first-hand this run make the gap concrete rather than rhetorical.

1. The rule is asymmetric about demographics, and the asymmetry runs in exactly the direction that makes the comparison impossible. § 50.24(a)(7)(iii) requires post-completion public disclosure of information "to apprise the community and researchers of the study, including the demographic characteristics" / "of the research population, and its results" (same reconstruction method, consecutive segments). So the ENROLLED side's demographics are a regulatory deliverable. There is no parallel requirement anywhere in § 50.24 for the CONSULTED side: eCFR confirms the rule sets no criteria for who qualifies as a representative, no representativeness standard, and no documentation requirement for the consulted group. The regulation therefore mandates one half of the candidate's comparison and not the other.

2. The frame exists and nobody has used it this way. "A Census of Clinical Trials Conducted Under the US Exception from Informed Consent Rule" (medRxiv 2022.08.23.22279138, read in full this run) enumerates 105 EFIC trials (recruitment from 1994/1996 to the April 2022 search close; 77 completed, 10 recruiting, 10 registered-not-active, 5 planned-not-conducted, 3 early planning), assembled from four prior reviews (52), registry/PubMed/Google Scholar/FDA-docket searches (37), author correspondence (13 of 99 authors contacted, 65 replies), and community-engagement reviews (3). Its abstracted variable list is condition, pilot status, adult/paediatric, recruitment start/stop years, enrolment count, and early termination with reasons. It abstracts nothing about the community consultation — it used engagement papers only as a route to finding trials — and it reports no enrolled-patient age, sex or race/ethnicity. So the census that would make the audit tractable was built and deliberately stopped short of both of the candidate's two variables.

The literature's own metric on the consultation side is acceptance, not coverage. Consultation-methods papers report what their respondents thought; trial reports separately publish who was enrolled; the only thing anyone has linked across the two is counts (see Prior 1). Whose community was consulted was the explicit substance of the PolyHeme controversy (Grassley correspondence to FDA, 2006; CIRCARE's compiled record), which is the anomaly that makes the silence odd.

Presuppositions:
1. CONTESTED and probably binding: consultation-participant characteristics were recorded and are retrievable for enough trials to support a frame. Partly undermined this run: § 50.24 requires no such record, so for an unknown share of trials the data were never created. V2 therefore makes the non-recording fraction a co-primary result, not attrition. Consultation-methods papers that survey participants (e.g. Prior 2's 2,612-respondent survey) show the data sometimes exist; whether they exist at trial-frame scale is the first thing an audit would learn and is itself reportable.
2. Enrolled-patient characteristics are published at comparable granularity for the same trials. STRENGTHENED this run: 50.24(a)(7)(iii) makes enrolled demographic characteristics a required public disclosure. Whether compliance is actual and whether granularity matches the consultation side's strata remain to be checked; the census's finding that FDA proprietary protection makes it "impossible for external researchers to identify EFIC trials or cross-check search results" is a warning about frame completeness, not about this presupposition.
3. CONTESTED: "representativeness of the consulted group" is a faithful operationalisation of the regulation's intent. The text says "representatives of the communities", which may mean designated spokespeople rather than a sample; eCFR sets no criteria either way. A defensible answer must report under both readings, and V2's individuation rule separates them by construction.
4. The enrolling catchment is identifiable (site lists, and where available enrolment geography), so a catchment-overlap measure is computable. Untested; weakest of the four on the geographic limb, since consultation-event locations are rarely published at better than city level.
5. CONTESTED: community consultation is protective in a way that depends on WHO is consulted. Some hold its function to be public notification and institutional legitimacy rather than surrogate consent; under that reading low overlap is not a defect and the result's significance is confined to the surrogate-consent reading. This bounds significance, not well-posedness.
6. Trial-level demographic reporting is not so coarse, or so often absent, as to make the overlap statistic uninformative.

What would count as an answer:

A pre-registered audit of the declared frame reporting, per trial: (a) whether consultation-participant characteristics were recorded at all, and whether retrievable now (co-primary with (b)); (b) a pre-declared distributional-overlap statistic over pre-declared strata, plus a separate geographic catchment-overlap measure between consultation activity and enrolment; (c) a pre-declared rule for "effectively disjoint". The question is answered by the joint distribution of overlap statistics across the frame and the non-recording fraction, reported separately under the sample reading and the designated-representatives reading. Both directions act: high overlap defends the rule as implemented; low overlap, or widespread non-recording, supports a concrete reporting requirement — publish consultation-participant demographics alongside the enrolment demographics that 50.24(a)(7)(iii) already requires — which IRBs and FDA could impose without amending the rule.

- vs Prior 1: same two data sources, different variable. Prior 1's unit is the event/hub and its only cross-link to enrolment is a count-vs-count association; the candidate's unit is the trial and its endpoint is a distributional-overlap statistic between the composition of the consulted group and the composition of the enrolled group, plus a geographic catchment overlap. Significance — consequence:
If overlap is high across the frame, the rule as implemented is defended empirically for the first time, and the surrogate-consent reading of consultation gains the evidential support it has never had — which matters because the PolyHeme controversy was argued entirely without this evidence. If overlap is low, the protective story told about EFIC is factually wrong under its own strongest reading, and the remedy is specific and cheap: 50.24(a)(7)(iii) already compels disclosure of enrolled demographic characteristics, so requiring the consulted group's characteristics in the same disclosure makes the comparison computable prospectively at no new data-collection cost for trials that already survey consultation participants. If the dominant finding is non-recording — plausible, since eCFR confirms the rule requires no record of who was consulted — then the answer is that the system cannot currently tell whether its own safeguard reached the right people, which is itself a finding an IRB can act on and is the one result that is unavailable from any existing document.

Significance — audience:
(i) IRBs reviewing EFIC protocols, who must make the (a)(7)(i) finding with no criterion to apply; a distribution of overlap statistics across 77 completed trials gives them a benchmark and a question to put to investigators. (ii) FDA (the office that issued the 2013 EFIC guidance, which per this run's reading of the rule adds no representativeness criterion either), which could add a documentation expectation to guidance without rulemaking. (iii) Emergency-research networks that run consultation at scale and already collect participant surveys (NETT/SIREN, the ROC successors) — Prior 1's 17 hubs and Prior 2's 12 centres show both the infrastructure and the data habit exist. (iv) The consultation-methods literature itself, whose outcome measure is acceptance; a coverage statistic reframes its own results as weighted or unweighted estimates. (v) Bioethics and regulatory-science scholars litigating the surrogate-consent vs public-notification reading, who currently argue it without data.

Significance — fecundity:
Reweighting the existing acceptance literature to the enrolled-population distribution becomes possible and becomes obligatory (does the 54%/71% figure survive?). Whether catchment overlap predicts the enrolment disparities Prior 3 measured becomes askable. Whether low-overlap trials attracted more opt-outs, complaints, or post-hoc controversy — i.e. whether coverage has any observable downstream consequence — becomes a testable question rather than a rhetorical one. And the non-recording fraction, once published, defines the minimum reporting change needed to make all of the above answerable prospectively.

Significance — anchoring:
The PolyHeme controversy (Grassley/Senate Finance correspondence with FDA, 2006; CIRCARE record), whose explicit substance was whose community had been consulted. The standing debate on whether community consultation accomplishes anything, which Prior 1 itself concedes is supported only by purely observational metrics. The justice question Prior 3 opened and answered on the enrolment side only. And the survey-methodology coverage-error family, which supplies off-the-shelf estimators (undercoverage–nonresponse decomposition, R-indicators) that this literature has never borrowed.

Reason for silence:
Primarily a record and disciplinary boundary with a regulatory asymmetry at its centre, confirmed first-hand at eCFR this run: § 50.24(a)(7)(iii) makes the ENROLLED group's demographic characteristics a required public disclosure, while nothing in § 50.24 requires any record of WHO was consulted. So one half of the comparison is a regulatory deliverable and the other half is an optional research artefact — there is no form, submission or publication in which the two meet, and for some trials the consulted side was never recorded at all (a missing-instrument mechanism, not an oversight). Compounding this, the two halves live at different units of analysis in different literatures: consultation data are reported per EVENT in community-engagement methods papers (Prior 1), enrolment demographics are reported per PATIENT in trial reports, and the EFIC census that could have joined them abstracted neither. Second, an incentive gap: the people holding consultation records are the investigators who ran the consultation and obtained the approval it supported, so a low-overlap finding impugns their own IRB finding; and the engagement literature's chosen metric — acceptance — treats respondents as a population to be persuaded rather than a sample to be audited, which is a framing that makes coverage invisible rather than uninteresting. Third, a conceptual bridge was missing: reading consultation as a sampling exercise requires importing coverage-error vocabulary from survey methodology, which (per query 8) the engagement literature has not done.

Scores: Well-posedness 3/3 · Significance 2/3

Well-posedness 3/3 justification (V2): the frame is named and already enumerated by a published census; the overlap statistics, strata, disjointness rule and the consulted-group individuation rule are all pre-declarable; the non-recording fraction is co-primary, so the question has a determinate answer even in the regime where the data largely do not exist; and the contested normative reading of "representatives" is handled by reporting under both readings rather than by picking one. The question is descriptive and cannot be answered the same way whatever the evidence shows.

Significance 2/3 justification, argued not asserted: not 3/3 because presupposition 5 genuinely bounds it — under the public-notification/legitimacy reading of consultation, low overlap is not a defect, so the result's normative force is confined to the surrogate-consent reading, and that reading is contested rather than settled. The actionable consequence is also a reporting requirement rather than a change in who is protected, which is a real but second-order practice change. Not 1/3 because the audience is specific, currently decision-making without the number (IRBs making an (a)(7)(i) finding with no criterion), and because the most likely finding — widespread non-recording — would be directly actionable against a regulatory text whose asymmetry has been verified.

Strongest remaining objection (for the adversary to press): the pre-emptive reading of presupposition 1. If consultation-participant characteristics turn out to be recorded for only a handful of the 77 completed trials, limb (b)'s distribution is computed on a sample so small and so self-selected (the trials whose investigators chose to survey and publish) that it cannot speak for the frame — and limb (a) alone, "the records mostly do not exist", is close to derivable in a page from the fact that § 50.24 never asked for them. That makes the candidate's weight rest on a quantity (the exact non-recording fraction) whose value may be nearly known a priori.
