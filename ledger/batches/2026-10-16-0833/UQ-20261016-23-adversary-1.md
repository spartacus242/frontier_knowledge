# Gate 4 — Adversary #1 log — UQ-20261016-23

## Restatement (my own words, before engaging the record's framing)

Food-animal regulators have a cookbook for "how long after you dose the animal is the milk unfit to sell": run a residue-depletion study to a specified design (so many milked animals, so many timed samples, the licensed dose, an assay sensitive enough), fit the depletion curve, and read off the time at which an upper one-sided statistical bound on the curve drops under a legal residue limit. Human medicine makes the same decision for a nursing mother ("wait 6 hours", "pump and dump", "no interruption needed") with no such cookbook.

The candidate asks two separate, checkable things. (A) Take a fixed list of the drugs most often prescribed to breastfeeding women; for each, go to the published human milk concentration-time data and score it, criterion by criterion, against the veterinary study-design requirements — did enough mothers get sampled, often enough, far enough into the tail, with a sensitive enough assay, at the normal dose? Report the fraction passing each criterion. (B) For whichever drugs pass, actually run the veterinary arithmetic: put an upper tolerance bound on the depletion curve and find when it crosses each of several candidate infant-exposure limits (since no legal limit for human milk exists, you try a pre-declared menu of them). Then lay the resulting wait-times next to what mothers are told today and report how far apart they are, drug by drug, in hours.

So: a data-adequacy audit using another regulatory discipline's yardstick, plus a what-if recomputation of breastfeeding interruption advice. It is not a proposal to adopt the veterinary standard, and it does not claim current advice is wrong.

It evaluates milk-discard strategies and concludes a 3-day discard; exposures are expressed as a fraction of maternal on-treatment concentration ("0.4%-1.7% and 0.1%-1.8% of on-treatment") read at "the upper 95th percentile of the predicted peak concentrations"; **no infant-exposure threshold is stated**; 2 drugs.
- `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10338611/` — **the decisive page.** Damoiseaux D et al., "Predicting Chemotherapy Distribution into Breast Milk for Breastfeeding Women Using a Population Pharmacokinetic Approach", Clinical Pharmacokinetics 2023;62(7):969-980. It computes, per drug and per milk-production scenario, **how many days of milk must be discarded for cumulative RID to fall below each of a pre-stated set of candidate limits — 1%, 0.1% and 0.01%** — reading off upper prediction limits, and says of the limits: "Values of 1%, 0.1%, and 0.01% were chosen for demonstration purposes", because of "the lack of information regarding safe doses of oral chemotherapies (in breast milk) in infants". ## Novelty effect of this gate (adverse findings, stated plainly)

1. **Limb B's construct is already published, narrowly.** Damoiseaux et al., Clin Pharmacokinet 2023;62(7):969-980 computes milk-discard days for 3 drugs against a pre-stated *set* of candidate infant-exposure limits (1%/0.1%/0.01% cumulative RID, admitted arbitrary) read off upper prediction limits. The record flagged this as the live risk and said novelty would drop to Low if it held; on the text, it holds **partially**: same device (menu of limits -> interval), same direction (upper bound of the predicted curve), but 3 oncology drugs rather than a drug list, prediction intervals rather than an upper one-sided tolerance limit (the source is explicit: CIs and 95%/80% prediction intervals, no tolerance limit), no data-adequacy census, no comparison against documented current advice, no veterinary standard. So limb B is pre-empted in *shape* and not in *scope or standard*.
2. **An undisclosed corpus census exists.** The Manitoba "Medications in human milk" thesis systematically reviews 224 studies across 186 medications for how milk was collected and assayed. That is limb A's enumeration and extraction step already performed once, without the externally-fixed numeric criteria. The record did not find it; it should be in the entry as a nearest prior.
3. Record's cited priors spot-checked: Monfort 2025 real and accurately characterised; PubMed 37798909 real (CPT:PSP 2023;12(12):1931-1944) and the record's cautious guess about it confirmed; PMC10338611 real (Clin Pharmacokinet 2023); EMA CVMP children's-intake MRL position paper confirmed as a real EMA scientific guideline. No fabricated citation found.

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional** — nothing Unresolved (2, 7, 8 Resolved; 1, 3, 4, 5, 6 Partial), so not Rejected; but novelty does not hold at Medium-with-clear-air, because limb B's "menu of candidate limits -> upper-bound discard interval" construct is already published for three drugs (Damoiseaux 2023) and limb A's corpus enumeration already exists over 186 medications (Manitoba thesis), so this is Medium novelty with two close neighbours — Gate 5's Provisional case. To promote: restrict the claim to the census-against-an-external-numeric-standard plus the tolerance-limit-vs-prediction-interval distinction, and read FDA media/124749 and the EMA CVMP milk-withdrawal guideline to establish that no human-side decision rule already exists.
