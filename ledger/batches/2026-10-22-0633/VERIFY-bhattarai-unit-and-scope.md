## CLAIM

A ledger rejection holds that this source **already prints the candidate's quantity**.

The candidate's quantity is: **the share of RELEASED PATIENT RESULTS that is not the first
valid measurement of that analyte on that specimen**, where the replacing measurement may be
a rerun, **a dilution**, a delta-check or critical-value repeat, **or a cross-instrument
confirmation**.

The source's candidate-matching figure is **701 (0.73%) of 95,757**. The question put to me
is whether that 701/95,757 is the candidate's quantity, a SUBSET of it, or something on a
different denominator.

Six sub-claims (a)-(f), each to be answered from the paper's own Methods wording.

**Verdict on (a): the earlier agent is CONFIRMED, on all three limbs.** The unit is one

### (b) Dilutions

**The word appears exactly ONCE in the entire paper**, and it is in the **Discussion**, not the
Methods.

**RECONSTRUCTION from 3 consecutive fragments, junction overlaps "mandatory verification" and

> Elevated repeat frequencies in β-hCG and AFP may be attributed to mandatory verification of
> clinically critical or borderline results, dilutional reruns, and instrument flags, which

Grammar on both sides of the cut, checked as instructed. The sentence **before** it is about
urine albumin and small-sample-size artefact; the sentence **after** it is about body-fluid
analyses, sample heterogeneity and manual handling. So the dilutional-rerun remark is one item
in a **three-item post-hoc attribution, hedged "may be attributed to", confined to two
analytes (β-hCG and AFP)**. It is an interpretive gloss on a high per-analyte rate. **It is
not an inclusion criterion and it is not in the Methods.**

The Results decomposition names a category the Methods does not:

**RECONSTRUCTION from consecutive fragments, Results:**

> The most common reason for repeat testing was values falling beyond critical result
> threshold. Critical results accounted for 311 repeats (44.37%); of which 185 repeats for
> high value and 126 repeats for low value. It was followed by analyzer flags/errors
> (37.09%), out of linearity (9.55%) and inconclusive/inconsistent patterns (8.98%) as shown
> in figure 1.

**Figure 1 caption, verbatim:** "Figure 1. Number of repeat tests performed due to various
reasons."

**The paper never defines "out of linearity" and never says a dilution was performed.** The
phrase is used as a bare category label. The paper does not print an **n** for it — 9.55% is a
bare percentage against an unstated denominator (9.55% of 701 is about 67; **that is my
arithmetic, the paper prints neither the n nor the denominator**).

**Answer to (b), with the inference labelled as inference:** a dilution re-run above linearity
would be a re-analysis of the same analyte **from the same original aliquot, before
reporting**, so it **satisfies the definition sentence**, and "out of linearity" is plainly
the category such an event falls in. So dilutions are **most likely INSIDE the 701**. But the
Methods' **enumerated cause list omits them**, the paper **nowhere states that dilutional
reruns were counted**, and the only occurrence of the word is a hedged Discussion attribution
about two analytes. On the Methods' own wording this is **SILENT**: inside by construction from
the definition plus the category label, **not inside by any statement the paper makes**.

One further ambiguity the paper does not resolve, which I flag rather than infer past.

### (f) Share REPEATED vs share REPLACED

**The paper reports the share that WERE REPEATED.** Its frequency outcome is, in its own words,
"the proportion of repeated tests to total tests for each analyte". That is an event rate, not
a replacement rate.

**On which value was reported out, the paper never says so directly.** The two nearest
statements:

> ...performed before the initial result was reported. (Methods, the definition)

> The time difference between the availability of the initial result and the final verified
> result due to repetition was used for the calculation of test delay. (Methods —
> RECONSTRUCTION from consecutive fragments)

"released" and "report out" are **NOT PRESENT** anywhere. The exact string "final result" does
not occur; "final test results" (Results) and "Final Test Result" (Table 4 header) do.

So: the definition **implies** that the initial value never went out and the final verified
value did, and the delay construct **requires** that reading. But **the paper states it
nowhere**, and it never reports how many of the 701 repeats produced a **materially different**
released value. Table 4 is a paired initial-vs-final comparison with p-values and **no count of
value-changing repeats**. A repeat that **confirms** the first value is counted in the 701
exactly as a repeat that **overturns** it. **SILENT** on the replacement share.

## REGIME

The regime in which this paper establishes its 0.73%:

- **Population:** all biochemical tests performed in **one** clinical biochemistry department
  of **one** Nepali tertiary teaching hospital over **one** month (14 May - 14 June 2024).
- **Unit of analysis:** the individual **test**, as recorded in **one analyser's logs**. Not
  the patient, not the order, not the specimen, not the released report, not the laboratory.
- **Event counted:** a re-analysis of the same analyte **from the same original aliquot**,
  **before the initial result was reported**, arising from one of **four enumerated causes**
  (critical value, analytical error flag, implausible/inconsistent result, QC failure during
  the run).
- **Capture instrument:** analyser log extraction. Anything not in that one analyser's log is
  invisible to the study by construction.
- **Denominator:** tests **PERFORMED** — 95,757 in the text across 117 test types; 93,706 in
  Table 3's total row; 88,706 as that table's rows actually sum.
- **Threshold/category:** causes are the paper's own four in Methods and its own four in
  Results, **and the two lists are not the same list** (see DEFECTS).
- **Estimand:** a descriptive one-month per-analyte repeat **frequency**, with no interval
  estimate and no stated analysis plan calling for one.

**Does the claim handed to me sit inside that regime? No.** The candidate's quantity differs
from this in the numerator's breadth, in the numerator's *kind*, and in the denominator — three
separate ways, set out next.

## DEFECTS FOUND

**1. The decisive one: the 701 is NOT the candidate's quantity, and it is NOT a clean subset
of it either.**

Taking the candidate's definition literally — *released results that are not the first valid
measurement of that analyte on that specimen* — the 701 diverges in **both** directions:

*Narrower than the candidate (events the candidate counts and the 701 does not):*
- repeats **on a new sample** — excluded in the paper's own words;
- repeats **requested after reporting** — excluded in the paper's own words;
- **cross-instrument confirmation** — no second analyser exists in the paper, and the data
  source is one analyser's logs, so uncapturable;
- **delta-check triggered** repeats — the word never appears; not an enumerated cause;
- anything outside **one department, one month, 117 test types**.

*Not a subset of the candidate (events in the 701 that fall OUTSIDE the candidate's
numerator):* the candidate says **first VALID** measurement. Where the first run was voided by
an **analytical error flag (37.09%)** or a **QC failure during the run**, or read **out of
linearity (9.55%)**, the first run was **not a valid measurement at all** — so the released
value **IS** the first valid measurement, and that released result is **not** in the
candidate's numerator. On the paper's own decomposition that is **up to 46.6% of the 701**
before counting QC failures (my arithmetic on the paper's printed 37.09% and 9.55%; the paper
prints no n for either and prints no QC-failure share at all).

**So the overlap between the two quantities is partial in both directions.** "Subset" would
already have been the wrong word; "the same quantity" is further wrong still.

**2. Different denominator, stated in the paper's own verb.** The paper's is tests **"were
performed"**. The candidate's is results **RELEASED to the patient record**. The paper's
outcome is defined tests-to-tests. These are not the same denominator, and the paper never
offers the released-results one.

**3. The Methods cause list and the Results cause list are not the same list.** Methods
enumerates four: critical value, analytical error flag, implausible or inconsistent result,
**quality control failure during the analytical run**. Results enumerates four: critical
results 44.37%, analyzer flags/errors 37.09%, **out of linearity 9.55%**, inconclusive/
inconsistent patterns 8.98%. **"Quality control failure" is NOT PRESENT in the Results
sentence and NOT PRESENT in Figure 1's caption** — it occurs only in the Methods. **"Out of
linearity" is NOT PRESENT in the Methods** — it occurs only in the Results. One category has
been swapped for another between the stated inclusion rule and the reported decomposition, and
the paper does not acknowledge it. A reader cannot tell whether QC-failure repeats were folded
into "analyzer flags/errors", dropped, or renamed.

**4. "Out of linearity" is never defined and is never called a dilution.** The ledger's own
framing asked whether the 9.55% category means dilution. The paper's answer is: it does not
say. The word "dilutional" appears once, in a **hedged Discussion attribution** about β-hCG and
AFP, three sentences into a run of post-hoc explanations for high per-analyte rates. **Treating
9.55% as a measured dilution share would be reading a Methods category through a Discussion
remark.**

**5. Published is not performed, on the replacement share.** The paper **MEASURES** the repeat
frequency and **PRINTS** it (0.73%, Abstract and Results; 0.75%, Table 3). It **does not
measure** the share of released results that were **changed** by a repeat. Table 4 **DEFINES**
and **RUNS** a paired initial-vs-final comparison and prints p-values, but prints **no count or
share of value-changing repeats** and no n per row. The replacement share is **never printed in
any table, figure or sentence.**

**6. No n for three of the four Results categories.** Only n=311 is printed. 37.09%, 9.55% and
8.98% are bare percentages against an **unstated** denominator, and the figure that would carry
the counts did not render. Any n a downstream record quotes for those three is **somebody's
arithmetic**, not the paper's printed figure.

**7. Silence recorded as silence, not as prohibition.** On dilution-as-an-inclusion-rule, on
cross-instrument confirmation, on delta checks, on which value was released, and on whether
95,757 counts test events or analyser runs, the Methods is **SILENT**. None of those silences
is the paper ruling anything out, and none of them is the paper ruling anything in.

**8. Not a defect in the source, but worth stating:** the paper does nothing wrong by counting
what it counts. It defines its unit tightly and in one clear sentence, which is more than many
papers do. The defect is in the **rejection**, which read a tightly-scoped single-analyser
pre-report re-run rate as if it were a released-results replacement rate.
