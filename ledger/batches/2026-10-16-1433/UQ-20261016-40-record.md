Question (canonical) — Gate 0 intake version:
For deposited single-particle cryo-EM reconstructions whose raw micrographs and final particle lists are both public, does the rejected complement — the particles the published pipeline discarded — support a reconstruction meeting a pre-registered criterion for a distinct conformational or compositional state rather than noise, and is the retained/rejected partition a property of the data or of the curator?

Question (canonical) — Gate 1 reformulation (one allowed, for well-posedness; this is the version graded):
Over a pre-specified frame of K EMPIAR entries that deposit an uncleaned particle stack alongside a published final stack, (a) in what share of entries does a reconstruction from the complement (deposited picks minus final stack) satisfy a pre-registered three-part distinct-state criterion — resolution better than a fixed threshold, map-to-model correspondence above a fixed cutoff, and deviation from the deposited map exceeding the 95th percentile of deviations produced by size-matched random subsets of the retained stack; and (b) when M curators independently process the same micrographs blind to one another and to the deposited result, what is the Jaccard overlap of their rejected sets, and does any of their maps carry a feature absent from the deposited map?
(Both versions recorded; the reformulation only fixes the criterion and the frame, and splits the second limb into a measurable overlap statistic.)

Alternative formulations:
- What is in cryo-EM's discard pile, and is the deposited structural record a biased subsample of the molecular ensemble?
- If several groups independently curated the same micrographs, how much would their rejected particle sets overlap, and would any of those sets yield a state absent from the deposited map?

Motivating gap:
The field is dense with how-answers about discarding and near-silent on what is discarded as a population. Attrition is universally reported (picked to retained, routinely 50-90% removed) and the methods literature keeps improving removal (2D/3D classification as standard practice; vendor protocols for "cleaning junk"; neural sorters such as the ANTIDOTE metadata-driven 3-D particle-sorting preprint, bioRxiv 2025.10.06.680814), while the validation literature keeps improving checks on the RETAINED map (gold-standard FSC; independent-particle-set cross-validation). The assertion that the discard pile is junk (ice, carbon edges, aggregates, damaged copies) is rarely measured against the alternative that it is enriched in exactly the minority states classification is built to remove.
Gate 2 finding that revises this gap: the gap is narrower than the intake assumed. The existence limb has been demonstrated at least once in the candidate's own literature (ALK/EMPIAR-10930, below) and complement reconstruction has been performed and scored against size-matched random subsets in a 2025 filtering preprint. What remains unmeasured is the pooled, pre-registered, corpus-level version and the curator-overlap statistic.

Presuppositions (established / contested):
1. Raw micrographs (EMPIAR) and final particle lists are both available for a usable number of entries, with particle identity traceable to micrograph coordinates. CONTESTED at intake; PARTLY ESTABLISHED by Gate 2 — EMPIAR-10930 deposits an uncleaned stack of ~18 million particles alongside the final stack, so entries of the required shape exist; their number across the archive is unknown and is itself a first measurement.
2. The deposited picking step can be reproduced closely enough that the reconstructed complement is the real one, not an artefact of a different picker. CONTESTED and load-bearing. Mitigated only for entries that deposit the uncleaned stack directly (then no re-picking is needed); for the rest it remains the chief threat.
3. "Interpretable as a distinct state" admits a pre-specified, non-circular criterion. The Gate 1 reformulation supplies one (resolution threshold + map-model correspondence + deviation exceeding matched-random-subset null). Partly validated externally: the 2025 filtering preprint already uses size-matched random subsets as a comparator, so the control is operable.
4. There is enough signal in the complement for any reconstruction at all at the available particle counts. ESTABLISHED for at least two entries: the filtering preprint refined the lowest-scoring subsets and reached 4.38 A from the lowest-scoring 90k particles of EMPIAR-10093 versus 4.08 A from the top 84k. This presupposition is no longer contested.
5. Rejection is at least partly operator-dependent. CONTESTED but supported anecdotally: the ALK reanalysis reports that "6 of the 10 most populated classes after initial 2D classification do not correspond" to the original's final classes. If a modern sorter is deterministic given one pipeline version, limb (b) degenerates into a question about pipeline choice rather than curators — a different and easier question.
6. The specimens studied actually possess minority states at frequencies in the range classification removes. Contested per specimen; demonstrated for ALK-ALKAL2 (2:1 alongside 2:2).
7. Downstream users treat a deposited map as representative of the target's conformation (structure-based drug discovery, MD starting structures). Only partly established; required for significance.

What would count as an answer:
Three deliverables against a pre-specified set of K EMPIAR/EMDB pairs. (a) Per entry, whether the complement reconstruction meets the pre-registered distinct-state criterion, scored against matched-size random subsets of the retained stack as controls — this is what separates a real minority state from the resolution and orientation-bias artefacts of any small subset. (b) M curators, blind to each other and to the deposited result, processing the same micrographs: the Jaccard overlap of their rejected sets, plus whether any resulting map carries a feature absent from the deposited one. (c) A pooled statement of the share of entries in which discarded material supported a reproducible distinct state. Answered negatively if complement reconstructions are indistinguishable from the matched random-subset controls and curator overlap is high. Answered positively if even a minority of entries yield a reproducible state from discarded material, which would give EMDB/EMPIAR a reason to require deposition of the full pick list and the rejection record, as crystallography requires unmerged reflections.

Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, all opened):
P1. "Automated filtering of particle images in single particle cryoEM", bioRxiv 2025.11.12.688030 (2025). https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.12.688030
 - Unit of analysis: individual particle images (50k-particle batches from the eBIC pipeline; author-deposited stacks from five EMPIAR entries).
 - Endpoint: 3D refinement resolution, B-factor, cryoEF angular-efficiency score; pipeline gate at best-3D-class resolution < 11 A.
 - Comparator: RELION class ranker, one expert's manual class selection, SIZE-MATCHED RANDOM SUBSETS, and the full unfiltered set.
 - Estimand: whether a score-thresholded retained subset preserves full-set reconstruction quality (one entry matched the author-reported resolution with 28.4% of particles).
 - It DOES reconstruct from the complement: "we performed 3D refinement jobs with the lowest-scoring 12k and 18k particles"; EMPIAR-10093's lowest-scoring 90k gave 4.38 A vs 4.08 A for the top 84k; conclusion framed as "low scoring particles are less informative". It explicitly leaves the candidate's question open: "the filtered-out particles may exhibit different conformation(s) of the molecule, implying heterogeneity" — speculation, with no 3D classification or compositional analysis of the rejected set, and no curator-variability analysis.
P2. "Overcoming cryo-EM map anisotropy reveals ALK-cytokine assemblies with distinct stoichiometries", bioRxiv 2024.08.08.607122 (2024); published as PMC12017499; coordinates PDB 9G5I. https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.08.607122
 - Unit of analysis: particles of ONE deposited dataset — EMPIAR-10930's uncleaned stack of ~18 million particles — and the 3D classes derived from them.
 - Endpoint: recovery of a 2:1 ALK-ALKAL2 assembly; map isotropy (cFAR) and resolution.
 - Comparator: the original 2:2 map (EMD-24095, 2.3 A), the 2:1 crystal structure (De Munck et al.), and the authors' own refinement without orientation rebalancing.
 - Estimand: that the deposited data contain a 2:1 population the original analysis did not carry forward; reprocessing yields a 3.2 A isotropic map of it.
 - Design: single-dataset case study. The 2:1 particles came from the deposited uncleaned stack, not from re-picking. It also documents curation divergence: "6 of the 10 most populated classes after initial 2D classification do not correspond" to the original's final classes, and notes the original supplementary 3D classes already resembled the 2:1 complex but were not pursued.
P3. "Cross-validation tests for cryo-EM maps using an independent particle set", Ortiz, Stanisic, Rodriguez, Rampp, Hummer, Cossio, arXiv:1908.01054 (2019). https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.01054
 - Unit of analysis: the 3D density map at each refinement iteration, low-pass filtered at several cutoffs.
 - Endpoint: BioEM posterior probability of each filtered map given a small held-out control particle set.
 - Comparator: the gold-standard two-half-set procedure; known overfitted vs non-overfitted benchmarks.
 - Estimand: whether a refined map is overfitted to noise (does it generalise to particles it was not refined against).
 - It uses the complement purely as a test set for the retained model — "not used for the refinement" — and never asks whether the excluded particles are a distinct state. Confirms the candidate's own characterisation.
Secondary, characterised only from titles/abstracts and NOT graded on: the first SPA Map Challenge (Heymann et al., 2018) — unit = submitted final map, endpoint = map quality/model agreement, comparator = other groups' maps, estimand = achievable spread in map quality; it compares outputs, not curation decisions, and never examines rejected sets. Significance — consequence:
If yes (even a minority of entries): the deposited map is a curated selection rather than the ensemble's representative, and EMDB/EMPIAR acquire a concrete reason to require deposition of the full pick list and the rejection record — the direct analogue of crystallography's unmerged-reflection requirement, which exists precisely because the merge step is a lossy curator decision. P2 already supplies one worked instance (a new PDB entry, 9G5I, extracted from someone else's deposited data), so the policy argument has a proof of concept; what the pooled estimate would add is the expected yield per archive-wide policy change, which is what an archive actually needs to justify a mandate. If no (complement maps indistinguishable from matched random subsets, curator overlap high): the field's universal "it's junk" assertion acquires its first systematic evidence, and the deposition mandate can be declined on measured grounds. The asymmetry is useful either way, which is the strongest thing about the question.
Significance — audience:
EMDB/EMPIAR and the wwPDB validation task force (deposition requirements; the archiving-recommendations document arXiv:2311.17640 is the live venue); cryo-EM methods developers building sorters (P1, ANTIDOTE) who currently validate only on retained-map quality and would gain a second, state-level acceptance criterion; reanalysis groups (P2's authors) who would gain a prior on which entries are worth reprocessing; and downstream consumers — structure-based drug discovery and MD groups that take a deposited map as the conformation — though that last link is only partly established and should not carry the argument.
Significance — fecundity:
Opens: what is the per-entry yield function (complement size vs state recovery probability)? Is operator-dependence in rejection predictable from specimen properties (expected state frequency, orientation distribution)? Can a rejection record be made a validation artefact with its own metric, as FSC is for the retained map? Does the attrition fraction itself, once computed corpus-wide, correlate with later reanalysis success? Does the same audit apply to cryo-ET subtomogram curation and to crystallographic outlier rejection?
Significance — anchoring:
Touches the cryo-EM validation problem (what can be validated about a map beyond its own resolution), the reproducibility-of-processing problem the Map Challenge opened and did not close, the archive-completeness debate in arXiv:2311.17640, and, by analogy, the screening-epidemiology problem of unverified screen-negatives — where the method for estimating a false-negative fraction without verifying every negative is mature (JRSS-C 2004) and transplantable.
Score: Significance 2/3 — real audience and a decidable either-way consequence, discounted because P2 has already made the policy argument's existence case and the marginal value of the pooled estimate, while real, is incremental rather than agenda-setting.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism):
Mainly an incentive-and-ownership gap compounded by a vocabulary effect. A complement reconstruction is, by construction, a worse map of a less interesting thing than the one already deposited, so no structural biologist is rewarded for making it, and no methods developer is rewarded for publishing that their sorter throws away signal; the work that does get done is relabelled as "reanalysis" of a specific biological system (P2) rather than as a study of the discard pile, which is why a corpus-level question never forms. Secondary mechanisms: an archive gap (intermediate selections are generally not deposited, so until entries like EMPIAR-10930 began carrying uncleaned stacks the audit was technically impossible), and a validation culture that is map-centric — gold-standard FSC and its successors all score the retained map, so the field has no metric whose denominator is the rejected set. The silence is therefore partial rather than total, which is consistent with the Low novelty grade: the question is being answered one specimen at a time under another name.
