## What the verification pass changed

Six fresh-context checkers, one per load-bearing kill or prior and two on the decisive one (78 searches, 45 fetches, 22 refusals or hard blocks). **For the first time in fourteen batches the pass moved no verdict.** All three kills survived. What it changed instead was the accuracy of nine load-bearing characterisations, two of which would have been quoted wrongly into the permanent record, and it settled two access failures that had looked identical and were not.

- **UQ-42's kill is the strongest this ledger has recorded, and it was worth two checkers.** The claim was that a paywalled standard normatively requires something, read once by one adversary — exactly the shape of claim that should not stand on a single reading. Both checkers reached ISO's own official preview of the primary document and returned clause 4.3.1 c), page 5: *"The natural frequency and damping coefficient of the reference invasive blood pressure monitoring equipment"* … *"shall be examined and optimised to meet dynamic requirements for each subject."* Normative ("shall", in clause 4, not the informative annex), per subject, both quantities named, both acts required. Three limits came with it, all of them things the record implied and the clause does not say: it prescribes no method (the words "resonance", "fast flush" and "square wave" appear nowhere in it), it sets no numeric threshold, and it belongs to Part 3 alone — ISO 81060-2, SP10, the 2018 Universal Standard, the AHA and ESH statements, IEEE 1708 and IEC 60601-2-34 were all unreadable, so nothing here licenses a claim about "the standards".
- **And UQ-42 leaves behind a real gap that is not the one the candidate asked about.** The standard requires the characterisation; the one validation study opened reports none at all and marks clause 4.3.1 PASS on the strength of naming the bedside monitor; routine practice does no quantitative characterisation; and nobody has published an apportionment of validation error between device and reference line. That is a conformity-assessment gap, not a silent standard, and the distinction is exactly why the candidate's premise fails.
- **UQ-45's kill holds on Hales and weakens on Lam.** Hales 2013 §3.5 is confirmed on every claim including the arithmetic — 77 hours, 64 GB, 10³ FIT/Mbit giving the paper's printed ≈39.4 (my redo: 39.42 decimal GB, 40.37 binary, which pins it to the decimal conversion) — and both quoted sentences appear verbatim. Lam 1991 needs narrowing on three of four points: the CRAY-1A rate is literature-reported rather than measured, the run length is an admitted guess (3,000 hours revised to ~2,000), the "two different programs" cross-checked only selected sample cases and are not framed as separating hardware from software, and "infinitesimal" is scoped to all the A2's a real plane would generate. The second limb — fault injection into a verified proof checker — is confirmed as having no literature at all.
- **UQ-44's fatal ground is confirmed and its illustrative figure is struck.** No borrowing tolerance expressed as a molecular or biomarker distance exists; published tolerances are in calendar recency, outcome drift and covariate balance. But there is no 2-to-7-year recency limit anywhere — the only numeric rule of thumb found is hedged and indication-specific ("more than say 10 years old for docetaxel in this indication should be avoided"), the per-year drift figures are observed magnitudes rather than proposed bounds, and Pocock's recency criterion is qualitative with no numeric covariate-balance cutoff.
- **Two of UQ-44's three "closest own-field papers" are not priors, and the third cannot be read by anyone.** Chaturvedi 2011 (JCO 29(32):4294-4301) is the excluded prevalence-trend case. The "Western Poland" paper is Majewski et al. 2026, Front Oncol 16:1651941, read in full and not the candidate's design — and it carries the candidate's own artefact unraised: it compares two five-year windows eighteen years apart whose blocks were 24-28 against 6-10 years old, and antigen decay biases Ki-67 downward in the older arm, the same direction as its headline result (Ki-67 higher in 2016-2020, p = 0.02). Osaki et al. 2026 has **no open-access copy in existence** (OpenAlex and Unpaywall: is_oa false, both URLs null, pmcid null), so the eleven failed routes were not a routing defect and the next attempt should be an interlibrary request, not another mirror hunt.
- **UQ-43's prior was never paywalled.** The full text is CC-BY at the OPUS Augsburg repository and was found on the first search. Reading it moved the candidate's standing in both directions and on balance strengthened it: the prior's endpoint is *closer* than the record allowed (DegRate is defined by an 80% failure threshold and the paper itself says it is "an end-of-life benchmark, not a local slope", i.e. a reciprocal transform of a failure time), while its method is further away — no censoring indicator, no survival vocabulary anywhere, and test duration not even retained as a covariate. Most of all, the prior contains the candidate's anomaly without naming it: "Approximately 55% (344 out of 627) of DegRates in the regression dataset are estimated using partial cycling data because the 80% end-of-life threshold was not reached", handled by linear extrapolation from the last reported cycle. That is 54.9% of the estimation sample consisting of right-censored failure times under a name that never mentions censoring, with no direction of bias stated and no question asked about whether cells that failed to reach threshold differ from those that did.
- **UQ-41's prior art covers one arm and the record implied it covered both.** RLIBM-32 is confirmed as a complete format-uniform census of binary32 with no usage weighting anywhere, so arm (ii) under uniform weighting is genuinely prior art. But Table 1 tabulates counts of inputs with wrong results *per library against a common oracle*, and two libraries can both be wrong on an input and still agree with each other, so those counts cannot be differenced into a disagreement count. Arm (i) — how often deployed implementations disagree with **each other** — is untouched by it, as are GPU libraries and fast-math variants, which the paper never covers. Four further corrections: "exhaustive" never appears in its evaluation text, Table 1's unit is a count and not a rate, its "sampled" belongs to polynomial generation and weights by representable-value density rather than usage, and the library set is eight columns rather than two.
