### 2.2 Chen et al. 2021 — NOT an instance; what it does instead

Chen, Zhen; Jiang, Yi; Shao, Yu-Tsun; Holtz, Megan E.; Odstrčil, Michal; Guizar-Sicairos, Manuel; Hanke, Isabelle; Ganschow, Steffen; Schlom, Darrell G.; Muller, David A. (front matter as served). What it does with the Debye-Waller factor: ADOPTS (from XRD literature) and MEASURES BY QCBED, then USES the result as a subtraction term. Ptychography itself does not deliver a DWF here. Served sentences, main text (Fig. 3 discussion): "the thermal broadening factor for Pr from both XRD (37) and QCBED is 0.23 Å"; "compared to the measured total broadening of 0.28 Å for our reconstructed image."; "In other words, most of the measured broadening is already accounted for by the thermal vibrations in the sample."; "depending on whether the QCBED or XRD result is adopted" (on the O#2 resolution). Supplement, "Resolution and thermal fluctuations": "XRD has large uncertainties in determining DWFs from light elements"; Fig. S17 caption: "in close agreement with reported single crystal X-ray diffraction results".

Numbers printed (per the summariser; Table S2 and Fig. S17 caption): QCBED-fitted isotropic B: Pr 0.737, Sc 0.595, O#1 0.842, O#2 1.145 Å². Single-crystal XRD (ref. 23 per text): 0.74 / 0.60 / 0.84 / 0.90 Å². Polycrystalline XRD: 0.88 / 0.70 / 1.00 / 1.00 Å². Thermal broadening (FWHM): Pr 0.23, Sc 0.20, O 0.28 Å (Table S1). Ptychographic column FWHM: Pr 0.44, Sc 0.45, O#2 0.54 Å; fitted total broadening Pr 0.28, Sc 0.25, O 0.34 Å (Table S1, Fig. S12).

Why this matters for item (2) even though it is not an instance — two near-1 comparisons are printed, neither of them "ptychography vs standard":
(a) QCBED vs single-crystal XRD, B-factor ratios Pr 0.996, Sc 0.99, O#1 1.00, O#2 1.27 — MY ARITHMETIC from Table S2 values. This shows the electron-based standard the brief names (QCBED) landing within ~1% of XRD for the heavy atoms in a 3-D oxide, in the same microscope family. It is a calibration ROUTE for ptychographic amplitudes, demonstrated on the same specimen, but it was not applied to a ptychographic DWF.
(b) Ptychographic total column broadening vs XRD/QCBED thermal broadening for Pr: 0.28 / 0.23 Å = 1.22 in width, (1.22)² ≈ 1.48 in B-equivalent terms — MY ARITHMETIC. The ptychographic width is a total (thermal + residual instrument) and so must be ≥ the thermal width by construction; the comparison therefore constrains direction, not accuracy. MY INFERENCE, labelled as mine: a 6× B-deficit of the hBN kind would have put Chen's Pr column FWHM near 0.23/√6 ≈ 0.09 Å, which is not what Table S1 prints (0.44 Å column, 0.28 Å broadening). So Chen's width-based estimator in a 3-D oxide shows no large DOWNWARD deficit relative to the XRD/QCBED thermal width; it is biased, if at all, upward. This is not a measurement of ptychographic DWF accuracy; it is an incompatibility argument I made.

Internal defects the summariser flagged in the Chen supplement (not verified by me character-by-character): the Methods formula σ_DWF = √(B/8π) does not reproduce 0.23 Å from B = 0.74 Å² whereas √(B/8π²) does (0.0968 Å σ → 0.228 Å FWHM, my arithmetic); a simulation-input B(Pr) = 0.12 Å² is described as "larger" than the experimental ~0.74 Å²; Table S1's O entries are garbled; reference numbering for XRD sources differs between text and Table S2.

## 4. Item (4) — is absolute accuracy discussed as a known limitation anywhere?

On what I could open, the absolute-accuracy limitation of ptychographic thermal-displacement recovery is stated in ONE source only: Gladyshev et al. themselves, about their own reconstruction. Sharpest, hedged formulation (served fragments, arXiv:2309.12017v2, simulated-Si section per the summariser): "we may underestimate the strength of correlations due to the resolution in these reconstructions being limited" … "but we can explore the directionality and relative differences between neighboring bonds." Sharpest unhedged: "The absolute values of the mode frequencies are heavily affected by the underestimated correlation strength." (hBN section; object = frequencies). Causes the authors name: slice/layer averaging, a 10-object-state basis insufficient for TDS and the Debye-Waller factor, 2 nm z-resolution.

Chen 2021 contains NO statement on the accuracy of a ptychographic DWF, because it does not extract one; its accuracy statements concern the Gaussian convolution model ("approximately modelled as a convolution of one Gaussian function with the static, frozen potential") and dependence on the adopted reference ("depending on whether the QCBED or XRD result is adopted"). Schnitzer et al. contains nothing on the point. Zhang (not opened by me) states per the record that single-atom amplitudes are "noisy and exhibit large spread" — a PRECISION statement, with a ±1–2 pm per-image SYSTEMATIC also quoted (record line 45), which is an accuracy-type figure but not a comparison to an independent standard.

I found NO second, independent source attesting a general absolute-accuracy limitation of ptychographic thermal-displacement measurement. Not opened and possibly relevant: Susi 2025 (phase-magnitude quantification), PRB 110 064102, the in-situ heating paper. So on my evidence the kill rests on ONE paper's self-reported deficit with estimator-specific causes named by its authors, not on an independently attested general limitation.

The MEASURED half of the kill is confirmed at first hand by a subject-only route: the number, its units, its denominator (per lattice, in-plane, correlation-summed), its location and its direction (≈1/6 of the powder-XRD standard) are as the entry states, and the authors' own diagnosis is as the entry and adversary 2 render it.
