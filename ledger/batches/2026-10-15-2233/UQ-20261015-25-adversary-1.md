# UQ-20261015-25 — Adversary #1 log (Gate 4)

## Restatement (my words, not the generator's)
Take the last several checkpoints of one training run that are all effectively tied on held-out loss. (a) How much do their benchmark scores, refusal rates, jailbreak success rates and agentic task success differ from one another? (b) Is that difference bigger than the item-resampling error bar that currently gets printed next to a single checkpoint's score? (c) If the developer shipped the one that looked best on loss, or best on a dev suite it then reports, how inflated is the published number relative to a random tied sibling scored on fresh items?

## Recommended verdict
**Provisional at best** — no Unresolved on 1, 2 or 4, but Partials on 2, 4, 7 and 8 and a novelty section that must be rewritten with Heineman et al. (arXiv:2508.13144) as N1 and Xu-Li-Salas (arXiv:2605.18852) and Rajabi et al. 2024 added; novelty is Low–Medium, not Medium. To promote: (1) show on one real annealed run that the measurement-error loss band contains >1 checkpoint, or re-pose the band without burning another reformulation; (2) confirm a public dense terminal post-training checkpoint set exists; (3) re-grade novelty strictly against the residual (spread vs item-CI, two-rule optimism, safety/agentic arms).

Strongest killing point: the candidate's central quantity — SD of benchmark scores across the final n checkpoints of a single run — is already a named, measured, published concept ("noise", Heineman et al., NeurIPS 2025, OLMo 2 1B–32B, 30 benchmarks, with a reporting recommendation and a scaling-law consequence attached), which the record's 11 queries never surfaced; and the R1 loss band built from loss measurement error is likely near-degenerate under LR annealing, so the "shippable set" as defined may not exist.
