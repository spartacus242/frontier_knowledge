## Restatement in my own words (not the generator's)

Take one *E. coli* cell, hold it in a device that can switch the medium around it, and hit it repeatedly with the same hypo-osmotic down-step — each step chosen weak enough that it usually does not burst the cell on the first go. Then ask an engineer's question about a machine part: does the chance of bursting depend on **how many times** the cell has been hit, over and above how hard the hardest hit was? If it does, (a) does the number of hits to burst fall off as a straight line on log–log against step size over at least four step sizes, the way a metal's S–N curve does; (b) is there a step size so small the cell never bursts however many times you repeat it (an endurance limit); and (c) if you mix two step sizes in two blocks, do the fractions of life used at each add up to 1 at the moment of bursting, the way Palmgren–Miner says? Do all of this twice: once in cells that are building new wall, once in cells that are not.

In one line: **is bursting a strength problem (set by the worst load) or a wear-out problem (set by the load count) — in a structure that repairs itself while you test it?**

It has already narrowed its own motivating universal, already graded itself 2/3 on well-posedness, already flagged the three presuppositions its repair introduced, and already declared the measurand exemption rather than hiding behind it. That makes the cheap attacks unavailable and means a kill here has to be mechanical or retrieval-based, not rhetorical.
