## Restatement (my own words, before reading the attacks)

Grow ice from heavy-water vapour instead of ordinary water vapour, in the same chamber at the same (per-isotopologue) supersaturation. The crystals flip between plate-like and column-like habit at a few characteristic temperatures. Do those flip temperatures move when H is replaced by D, and by how many kelvin each? The candidate wants to read the answer as a mechanism test: a move equal to the offset computed from D2O's own premelting driving force (the pressure gap between its sublimation line and its metastable liquid line) would say the flips are pinned to surface premelting; essentially no move would say they are set by step-generation kinetics.

## Attack 4 — conditioning clauses, numbered, each tested against something I opened

The question as it now stands (V2, repair included) needs the following to be true before it can be asked:

1. **Three habit transition temperatures exist in H2O, pre-declared near −4, −10 and −22 °C, and "each of the three" is a well-defined member of a fixed set.** — **CONTESTED (sourced).** Libbrecht 1910.09067, as served: "The text does not mention transitions near -4, -10, or -22 C. It describes plate-to-column transitions near -2, -5, -15, and -30 C, with a -6 C crossover in the Figure 4 data." Kobayashi 1967 (opened) places the step-velocity extrema at −6 °C and −11 °C and reports the field's historical disagreement (Hallett's −6 °C vs Mason's −11 °C). So the field's leading quantitative model enumerates FOUR transitions at other temperatures, and the historical literature disagrees on where the first lies. The record's "conventional" three-member set is one convention among several. **Mitigation:** V2 measures the transition temperatures in both isotopologues in the same chamber, so the H2O positions are a measurand, not an assumption; what is presupposed is only the member count. Not fatal, but the pre-declared set does not match the leading model's enumeration and the entry must say which set it adopts and why. REGIME of the Libbrecht source: H2O crystals grown in laboratory chambers, semi-empirical model, habit at modest supersaturation.

2. **D2O's sublimation curve and metastable-liquid vapour pressure are known over ~230–273 K to the equivalent of ±0.5 K.** — **TRUE for the two warmer transitions, MARGINAL for the coldest (sourced).** Herrig et al. 2018, as served: "an auxiliary equation for the sublimation pressure of ice Ih is presented"; "The sublimation-pressure equation for ice Ih (temperature range from 210 K to 276.969 K) is"; expanded (k = 2) uncertainties "0.5% at temperatures above 255 K, 5% at 225 # T/K # 255, and 10% at 210 # T/K , 225" (the # and comma are the summariser's rendering of ≤ and <; reproduced as served, flagged); Table 1 "Triple-point temperature Ttp 276.969 K"; Section 5.1.1 includes "vapor pressures of the metastable subcooled liquid at temperatures below the triple-point temperature", and the abstract says "The equation matches available data for the metastable subcooled liquid." REGIME: pure D2O, fluid EOS with an auxiliary ice-Ih sublimation equation, 210–276.969 K, correlation uncertainties, no mixtures. **My own arithmetic, not a source finding:** Δp ≈ p_liq,meta − p_sub, which 22 K below the triple point is of order 0.2 p_sub (using the H2O ratio e_w/e_i ≈ 1.22 at −20 °C from background knowledge as a proxy). A 5% (k=2) error in p_sub is then ~25% of Δp. With d ln p/dT ≈ L_sub/(RT²) ≈ 0.095 K⁻¹ for ice near 253 K, 5% in pressure is ≈ 0.5 K in temperature. So the fixed-Δp threshold for the coldest transition is computable only to about the ±0.5 K the record demands, before adding the metastable-liquid uncertainty, while for the two warmer transitions (above 255 K in D2O) 0.5% maps to ≈ 0.05 K and the clause is comfortably true. The record's flagged presupposition therefore **survives**, with the third transition marginal — consistent with the record's own warning that the third pair is the weakest link.

3. **"A step-generation kinetics mechanism with its own activation energies would require" a near-zero shift** — i.e., the second arm of the discriminant is what the kinetics side actually predicts. — **CONTESTED (sourced), and this is the load-bearing finding of this log.** The field's leading step-kinetics model is Libbrecht's semi-empirical SDAK model, and it pins its own transition structure to premelting onset. As served from 1910.09067: "growth is typically limited by the nucleation of new island terraces from surface admolecules"; "As the QLL develops, the ice/vapor step energy transforms into an ice/QLL step energy"; "surface premelting provides enough disorder to significantly reduce the E-S barrier"; and, decisively, **"the position of the dip is determined by the onset of surface premelting on the relevant facet surface"**. Clause direction: these sentences ASSERT a premelting dependence inside a step-nucleation kinetics model. The summariser also returned: "I describe a semi-empirical molecular model of the surface attachment kinetics"; "the placement of the SDAK dips was chosen to achieve this match with the morphology diagram"; "Neither the width nor depth of the dip structure in Figure 8 is well constrained by measurements"; "it is not yet possible to calculate step energies at any temperature on any ice surface". REGIME: H2O only, laboratory vapour growth, semi-empirical attachment-kinetics model with no isotope term. **Consequence:** the question's two arms are not exclusive. A shift matching the D2O premelting threshold would be consistent with Kuroda–Lacmann/MacDowell AND with Libbrecht's step-kinetics model, because both pin the transitions to premelting onset; the "near-zero shift" arm is attributed to no source and I found no model that predicts it. **My own reasoning, labelled as such:** even a kinetics model with no premelting term would be expected to shift under H→D substitution, because step energies and desorption barriers scale with hydrogen-bond strength and D2O's sublimation enthalpy and melting point are both higher than H2O's; the search that would ground this (D2O vs H2O step-energy or sublimation-enthalpy comparison) was not run. **Why this is not fatal:** the terminal estimand — the shift in kelvin of each transition — is well-defined and physically meaningful regardless, and a near-zero shift would still falsify premelting pinning of any kind. What falls is the question's "decisive under V2" reading and the Significance claim that a premelting match "displaces the attachment-kinetics account". So: Partial, not Unresolved.

4. **D2O ice grown from vapour is Ih with the same face set, so "plate" and "column" mean the same thing.** — **UNVERIFIED.** Nothing I opened bears on it. The APS abstract title "Thermal Expansion of Single-Crystalline H2O and D2O Ice" (returned, not opened) suggests D2O single crystals are routinely made, title level only. Kills nothing.

5. **Residual H2O in D2O vapour does not pin the surface to H2O behaviour, and >99% isotopic purity can be held in a growth chamber.** — **UNVERIFIED.** Nothing opened. Kills nothing.

6. **H2O transitions are reproducible in one apparatus to ±0.5 K.** — **CONTESTED at the historical level (sourced):** Kobayashi 1967 records that Hallett found the first extremum at −6 °C and Mason et al. at −11 °C, and that Kobayashi's own data match Mason's. That is a between-apparatus disagreement of ~5 K on where a feature sits — larger than the shift sought. The record's "same chamber, matched supersaturation" design addresses it, and Libbrecht & Bell's impurity test (record's Prior 2, not reopened) helps; but the premise that a ≤4 K shift is resolvable to ±0.5 K rests on no opened measurement of within-apparatus repeatability. Partial.

**Measurand exemption declared:** the H2O transition positions (clause 1) and their reproducibility (clause 6) are quantities V2 measures in the same chamber rather than assumes, so attack 4 does not reach them as presuppositions; I grade them as context only. The exemption does NOT cover clause 3, which is a claim about what a class of theories predicts, not something the experiment measures.

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional.** No attack is Unresolved. Attack 2 is Partial only on one unopened PMCID-identified document returned three times by my own searches; attack 4 is Partial because the question's second arm — that step-generation kinetics "would require" a near-zero isotope shift — is contested by the leading kinetics model itself (Libbrecht 2019: "the position of the dip is determined by the onset of surface premelting on the relevant facet surface"), while the terminal estimand survives. The record's own flagged presupposition (D2O Δp computable to ±0.5 K) survives on Herrig et al. 2018 for two of three transitions and is marginal for the third.

**Single strongest killing point:** the discriminant is not a discriminant. Both named mechanisms, in their leading published forms (Kuroda–Lacmann/MacDowell on the premelting side; Libbrecht's SDAK step-nucleation model on the kinetics side), tie the habit transitions to premelting onset, so a measured D2O shift matching the premelting threshold would not adjudicate between them, and no opened source predicts the "near-zero shift" the question attributes to kinetics. The entry must either name the model that predicts near-zero or reframe the question as a yes/no test of premelting pinning.

**Promotion conditions:** (1) convert PMC5676873 at idconv to a DOI and open it at the publisher; (2) rewrite the second arm of V2 to a model that actually predicts a near-zero shift, or drop the dichotomy and keep the kelvin-shift estimand with a single premelting-threshold comparison; (3) reconcile the pre-declared three-member set with Libbrecht's four-member enumeration (−2, −5, −15, −30 °C) and say which is adopted.

**Nomination by kind:** what would settle clause 3 is a document that STATES, for a step-nucleation kinetics model of ice habit WITHOUT a premelting term, the predicted dependence of the transition temperatures on hydrogen-bond strength or on isotopic mass — i.e., a model paper that prints an activation-energy or step-energy scaling and applies it to habit. I opened none of that kind and know of none; the nomination is a kind, not a named source, and I say so rather than dress a title as a lead.
