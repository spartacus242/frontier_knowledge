## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take a thin crystal that contains isolated point defects of one species (a substitutional impurity, or a vacancy). Image the *same individual* defects with multislice electron ptychography at two or more specimen temperatures. For every atom, extract its mean-square thermal displacement u², index it by which coordination shell n it sits in relative to the defect, and divide the measured value by what harmonic lattice dynamics predicts for that same site in a supercell that already contains the defect. Then ask three things: (a) is that ratio R(n,T) different from 1 at the first shell; (b) does R change when the temperature changes — the question asserts that pure harmonicity forbids this and that a locally anharmonic or resonant defect mode requires it; and (c) how many shells out does any departure persist before the atoms behave like pristine-host atoms. Headline number: R(1,T) and ∂R(1)/∂T.

### Attack 4 (run first and weighted heaviest, per instruction) — Presupposition failure

**The clauses the question needs TRUE before it can be asked at all, as a numbered list, read literally:**

4. **C4.** The same individual defect can be re-found and re-measured after a temperature change.

**C3 — CONTESTED, and the contesting is MY OWN REASONING, not a source finding.** Two independent problems with the discriminator.
(i) **Bulk anharmonicity already produces a temperature-dependent R at every site.** Real crystals expand and have explicit anharmonic self-energies, so measured u²(T) departs from a fixed-volume harmonic prediction at *all* n, far shells included, with no defect involved. The literal reading of limb (b) therefore has a predictable answer — yes, R varies with T — for reasons that have nothing to do with the defect; only the shell *contrast* R(1,T)/R(n→∞,T) carries local information, and that contrast is **not** the declared terminal estimand (R(1,T) and ∂R(1)/∂T are). **Read level of the supporting literature: TITLES ONLY, NOT OPENED** (ORNL "Temperature dependence and anharmonicity of the Debye-Waller factor"; repository.ias.ac.in/56037; two Purdue dissertations on DWF anharmonicity in Cu/Ag/Pb and alkali halides). So this leg is **Partial**, and I say so rather than dressing it as sourced.
(ii) **MY OWN ARITHMETIC**, from the standard single-mode harmonic result ⟨u²⟩ = (ħ/2Mω)coth(ħω/2k_BT) — which the record itself writes, and which I verified by a dedicated question is **not** printed in the one source I read in full (Gladyshev prints only D = (k_BT/m)[⟨ũũ*⟩]⁻¹; "The text gives no coth, Bose-Einstein, or harmonic-oscillator expression for MSD"). I did not open a source printing the coth form in this session. If the computed harmonic frequency is in error by a factor (1+ε), then R → (1+ε) in the zero-point limit (coth → 1, u² ∝ 1/ω) and R → (1+ε)² in the classical limit (coth(x) → 1/x, u² ∝ 1/ω²). So **a perfectly harmonic world plus an imperfect force-constant calculation gives a temperature-dependent R**, drifting by a factor (1+ε) across the range. For a 5% frequency error that is a 5% full-range drift — the same order as the anharmonic effect sought. The record's presupposition 2 covers only a *static* uncertainty comparison and does not cover this T-drift. Partial, because the algebra is mine.
