## Restatement (my own words)

Take the archive of a plant or pipeline where the same welds have been looked at by several different inspection techniques (X-ray film, hand-held ultrasound, phased-array ultrasound, eddy current), none of which was ever checked by cutting the weld open. Treat each weld's true flaw state as a hidden variable and each technique's call as a noisy test of it, exactly as epidemiologists treat several imperfect diagnostic tests with no gold standard (Hui–Walter latent-class model). The question is (a) whether, once you pre-declare which techniques share errors and which flaw-size bands you care about, the data can pin down each technique's real-world hit rate and false-call rate at all, and (b) if so, how those field numbers compare with the laboratory detection curves that whichever inspection-interval or fitness-for-service rule actually uses — with the identification of that rule and of its detection-curve source being part of the work rather than assumed.
