## Restatement (my own words, before reading the generator's framing again)

For time windows in which GNSS was misbehaving — windows whose dates come from evidence produced somewhere other than the clocks being judged (regulator bulletins, NOTAMs, ADS-B interference products, NAGUs, constellation/ground-segment anomaly notices) — how badly did real, already-installed GNSS-disciplined clocks actually mis-serve time to the equipment that depends on them? Report it as a distribution of worst-case |time error| per window and per receiver/clock model (or holdover-oscillator class) across fleets run by independent operators, and alongside it the share of units that broke the numeric time-error limit written in whichever standard governs that unit's application, with a Clopper–Pearson interval and with the share of the intended denominator lost to missing logs stated.

Two things I note about the shape before attacking. The frame clause (a) is an **availability** condition over a **disjunction of four record kinds**, and it is a **necessary conjunct** of a three-part frame rule — not an instrument declaration and not sufficient for anything. The question declares **no instrument**. I quote the clause I attack in each row rather than paraphrasing it.

**Measurand exemption, declared out loud rather than graded around (rule i):** V2's closing clause reports "the share of the nominal denominator lost to missing records", and presupposition 6 exempts both exceedance-occurrence ("the question does NOT presuppose that exceedances occur") and public obtainability ("Reporting that the records needed are not publicly obtainable is likewise a finding about the measurement system"). So **record availability and public non-obtainability are measurands here, not presuppositions, and attack 4 does not reach them.** I do not grade them as false premises. They remain a *feasibility* defect, which is where the record's own 2/3 well-posedness already sits.
