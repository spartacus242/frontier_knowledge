Domain(s): AI architecture — evaluation methodology (TARGET field, deciding novelty and holding the acting community: AI evaluation / AI-progress measurement). Method transplanted from (i) survey and psychometric missing-data theory (ignorability, non-ignorable missingness, trend comparability under a changing response mechanism) and (ii) index-number theory (substitution bias, new-goods bias, chain drift under item turnover).

Question (canonical): For one named, dated model x benchmark score panel published as the basis of a capability trend series — the Epoch Capabilities Index, 179 models x 38 benchmarks, 1324 observed scores — with each cell's observation indicator R_ij and each benchmark's first/last-report dates recovered: (0) does a unidimensional latent model fit the panel well enough for "the trend" to be a defined object (pre-step, not assumed); (a) does Pr(R_ij = 1) depend on fitted latent capability theta_i and fitted benchmark difficulty b_j after conditioning on the pre-declared covariate set Z = {model release date, developer, open/closed weights, benchmark age, benchmark licence/access, citation count, evaluation cost}; (b) under a pre-declared family of MNAR departures — a shared-parameter selection model with sensitivity parameter gamma on theta, and a pattern-mixture alternative indexed by the mean shift delta between observed and unobserved cells — what are the SIGNED changes in the fitted theta-vs-date trend's intercept and slope, expressed as a change in the implied doubling time, with Manski-style worst-case bounds; (c) does that change exceed the series' own reported uncertainty? (R1, the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation; V0 as intaken is recorded alongside it in the record.)

Alternative formulations: 1. Do AI progress curves bend because models improved, or because benchmarks were replaced whenever they stopped discriminating? 2. Is the benchmark panel behind published capability indices missing-at-random, and what does non-ignorable missingness do to the AI timelines extrapolated from it?

Motivating gap: A panel whose items enter because they are hard for current models and stop accumulating scores once saturated is missing-not-at-random ON the quantity being measured — the canonical setting of non-ignorable missingness in test linking and of substitution/new-goods bias in price indices. The ECI stitches ~40 benchmarks across ~200 models under a latent model, motivated because "Most benchmarks saturate too quickly to study long-run AI trends"; its stated limitations name benchmark gaming and comparability, not the entry/exit selection mechanism, and quantify no such bias. Confirmed at Gate 4 against the method paper itself (arXiv:2512.00193): none of missing, missingness, MAR, ignorable, unobserved or imputation appears in its visible text, and a code search of its repository returns 0 hits for "at random" / ignorable / MNAR / "selection bias". Incomplete coverage is handled by a filter ("we further filter out models that have only been evaluated on <=3 of the benchmarks"; "20 of the benchmarks do not have enough data to perform the analysis"), with no observation model anywhere.
