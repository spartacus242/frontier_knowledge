## Restatement (own words)

The modded-nanogpt leaderboard is an append-only list of ~90 successive code changes, each claiming to train a GPT-2-class model to val loss 3.28 on 8×H100 faster than the previous record (45 min down to under a minute). The rules test the *loss* statistically (p<0.01 over several runs) but accept the *time* on a comparison against the prior record. The candidate asks: if every record were re-timed many times on several nodes — once in its own era's software and once in one modern environment — what fraction of the cumulative log-speedup sits in step-downs that are statistically distinguishable from timing noise; how much of each accepted change still pays when re-applied at a larger scale or on the repo's second target; and how many old records no longer run.

## Evidence (verbatim quotes from pages opened; my arithmetic labelled)

**E1 — Appendix A of 2506.22419v2 (PDF, opened):** heading "A Reproducing ground-truth speedruns on our hardware"; "where we report the mean and standard deviation of three runs"; cluster named as "our AWS cluster"; "We can see that the two curves track closely each other" (Figure A.1 = official record time vs reproduced time per record; no numeric table in the chunk; Table E.1 lists official repository times). So: the 21 records existing in May 2025 were each re-run **three times**, on one common cluster, and reported with **dispersion**. The record's pre-declared condition reads: "If that appendix reports several repetitions per record with dispersion, the candidate's limb (i) is substantially answered for the 2025 portion of the series and novelty falls to Low on the re-timing limb." That condition is met.

**E2 — README rules (opened; also verbatim via code search):** "2. Attain ≤3.28 mean val loss. (Due to inter-run variance, submissions must provide enough run logs to attain a statistical significance level of p<0.01 that their mean val loss is ≤3.28. ... For submissions which improve speed by optimizing the systems performance, without touching the ML, this requirement is waived.) 3. Not use any extra `torch._inductor.config` or `torch.compile` flags. ... 4. Run faster than the prior record when baselined on the same hardware." Also: "Official records are timed on 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs from https://app.primeintellect.ai/". P7 is therefore verified as quoted — and rule 4 is a **same-hardware paired baseline**, i.e. a variance control on the time axis that the record's framing ("the record axis carries none") omits.

**E3 — README record table (opened):** 92 distinct records (94 rows); #1 = 45 min (05/28/24); #92 = 0.665 min (08/30/26). Two maintainer re-timing rows for #21: "not a new record, just re-timing #21 with the updated rules" (2.997 min, 02/01/25) and "not a new record, just re-timing #21 with latest torch" (3.014 min, 05/24/25). Toolchain-only records are labelled as such: #25 "Upgrade PyTorch to 2.9.0.dev20250713+cu126", #66 "Torch 2.10". My arithmetic: the latest-torch re-timing of #21 differs from the updated-rules re-timing by +0.57% (3.014/2.997), single runs each, uncertainty unknown. My arithmetic on the log-share: ln(45/2.997)/ln(45/0.665) = 2.709/4.215 ≈ **64% of the cumulative log-speedup sits in records 1–21**, exactly the portion Appendix A re-timed with n=3 and std (uncertainty: using the #21 re-timing time rather than its original row changes this by <1 point).

**E4 — Per-record replicated timing tables in the ledger itself (code search, repo files):** `records/track_1_short/2026-05-20_BigramsSignTrick/pr299/statistics.md`: "20 runs, step 1480/1480. | Run | val_loss | train_time | 0cf91274 | 3.2763 | 84237ms | 14564bfb | 3.2806 | 84392ms |"; `.../baseline/statistics.md`: "10 vanilla runs, step 1480/1480. ... | 02ed0a75 | 3.2783 | 85415ms | 22ed8a4c | 3.2792 | 85452ms |"; `records/track_1_short/2026-06-11_RecursiveFromBest/this_pr/statistics.md`: "13 runs, step 1398/1398 ... 77933ms ... 77446ms". Only two rows of each table are visible in the search fragment, so no dispersion estimate is drawn; what is established is that contributors publish 10–20 timed runs of the PR **and** a 10-run baseline of the prior record, i.e. replicated, paired timing on the record axis.

**E5 — Fixed-iteration design (code search):** `train_steps = args.num_iterations; for step in range(train_steps + 1)`; logs `train_time` and `step_avg`. The run length is fixed in advance; the stochastic outcome is val_loss, which is where rule 2 puts the p<0.01 test. The time axis therefore carries only systems noise (per-step time), not training stochasticity; step-count reductions survive re-timing by construction.

**E6 — Transfer-limb prior (abs opened):** Wen, Hall, Ma, Liang, "Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers and Where to Find Them", arXiv 2509.02046v1 (2 Sep 2025): "we conduct a systematic study of ten deep learning optimizers across four model scales (0.1B-1.2B parameters)"; "all the fastest optimizers such as Muon and Soap, use matrices as preconditioners"; "the speedup of matrix-based optimizers is inversely proportional to model scale, decreasing from 1.4x over AdamW for 0.1B parameter models to merely 1.1x for 1.2B parameter models." Scope: matched-token pretraining at 0.1–1.2B, not the speedrun's 3.28 target; it covers the optimizer class (the speedrun's Muon records), not architecture/systems records.

**E7 — Cost mechanism check (my arithmetic, sourced to E3):** the record's silence argument assumes "a generous mean of ~20 min per run" over ~77 records → ~2,500 node-hours. Rule 4 makes the series (nominally) monotone and #21 = 2.997 min, so 71 of 92 records are under 3 min; a crude upper bound on the mean record time is (21×45 + 71×2.997)/92 ≈ 12.6 min, and the true mean is far lower because records 2–20 are also well under 45 min. The record's cost figure is overstated by at least ~1.6× on this bound alone, and contributors already run 10–20 repetitions per record for the loss rule (E4), so the marginal cost of timing statistics for those records is nil.

## Recommended verdict

**Rejected** — Unresolved on attack 2 (Disguise, limb i) and attack 4 (Presupposition failure); also Unresolved on 7. By the record's own pre-declared condition, Appendix A of arXiv 2506.22419 (mean and standard deviation of three re-runs per record on one cluster, for the 21 records holding ≈64% of the cumulative log-speedup) drops limb (i) to Low novelty; and the premise that the series' time axis is unreplicated and uncontrolled is contradicted by rule 4's same-hardware baselining, the multi-log rule, the repo's own 10–20-run statistics.md tables with 10-run baselines, and the maintainers' re-timing rows. Routing: limb (i) belongs as an extension of 2506.22419 Appendix A (more repetitions, a second node, a separation test, records 22–92) and of the repo's statistics.md practice; limb (ii) for optimizer records is answered by Wen et al. 2025 (arXiv 2509.02046) and for other records belongs in the model-ladder / small-scale-proxy literature. What would revive a successor: a question restricted to the 2026 portion (records 22–92, cross-node arm, non-optimizer transfer), framed as "aggregate and extend the existing replicated timings", not as an audit of an untested axis.
