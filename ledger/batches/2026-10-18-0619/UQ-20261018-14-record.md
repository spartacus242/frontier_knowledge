## Question (canonical)

**V2 (Gate 1 reformulation — the one permitted; scored as canonical).** Over the modded-nanogpt speedrun's record series (the 8×H100 / FineWeb val-loss ≤ 3.28 track), under a protocol declared before any run — (a) per-record re-timing with ≥5 repetitions on ≥2 distinct 8×H100 nodes, run in **two arms**: A1 each record in its own period-correct pinned software environment, A2 all records in one common modern environment; (b) a step-down between consecutive records *i−1 → i* counted as **surviving** only if the two re-timed time distributions separate at a pre-declared level (two-sample test at p<0.01, the same level the record rules impose on loss); (c) the surviving **share** computed multiplicatively, as the sum of log time-ratios of surviving step-downs over the total log ratio of the series' cumulative speedup; and (d) a pre-declared transfer ladder (the repo's own second target as one rung, plus a 2–4× parameters-or-tokens rung), on which each accepted record's change is re-applied and its **retained speedup** reported with a CI from ≥3 repetitions —

what share of the cumulative recorded wall-clock speedup survives (i) re-timing and (ii) transfer; and how many records no longer run at all, by failure class?

**V1 (intake version, retained).** Over the modded-nanogpt speedrun's record series, what share of the cumulative recorded wall-clock speedup survives (i) re-timing each record's own code with repetitions on matched hardware, given that the record rules impose a p<0.01 test on mean validation loss but no variance control on time, and (ii) transfer, meaning the accepted change still pays when applied at a pre-declared larger scale or different target?

Why reformulated (well-posedness only, no change of subject): V1 leaves three things undeclared that decide the answer by construction — (1) the **software-environment policy** (period-correct vs common environment; the Meta/UCL speedrunning benchmark excludes one record precisely because "its speedup comes from a PyTorch upgrade", so this fork can move the headline number on its own, and the two-arm design converts the fork into a reported decomposition rather than a hidden choice); (2) the **survival decision rule** (V1 says "survives" without a test; V2 mirrors the record's own p<0.01 onto the time axis); (3) the **accounting rule** for "share of cumulative speedup" (times compose multiplicatively, so the share must be log-additive, not a difference of seconds). V2 also fixes the unit of analysis at the **record**, not the idea, which is how the intake's bundling presupposition is honoured rather than assumed away.

## Alternative formulations

1. How much of the speedrun's recorded speedup is measurement and how much is method?
2. Which accepted records in a public optimization ledger are indistinguishable from timing noise, and do the survivors pay off outside the fixed target they were selected on?
3. (lay) The training-time record fell from 45 minutes to about 90 seconds. If you ran every record five times on two machines, how much of that fall would still be there?

## Motivating gap

Source method: new-instrument question. The instrument is a public, dated, append-only competitive record of an optimization process on a fixed objective, with per-record code and logs: "train a neural network to ≤3.28 validation loss on FineWeb using 8× NVIDIA H100s". Its acceptance rules (quoted in the intake from a full reading of github.com/KellerJordan/modded-nanogpt on 2026-10-18) impose "enough run logs to attain a statistical significance level of p<0.01" that "their mean val loss is ≤3.28", plus "Run faster than the prior record when baselined on the same hardware" — i.e. a one-sample test against a fixed **loss** threshold, and an unreplicated **time** comparison. The variance control sits on the axis that is not the record; the record axis carries none. **Verification status of that quotation: NOT independently re-verified in this Gate 2.** GitHub MCP is scoped to this ledger's own repo (access denied for KellerJordan/modded-nanogpt) and the 5-fetch cap was spent on priors. The rules text is therefore intake-asserted here and is listed below as a contested presupposition, not as an established premise.

Why it matters now rather than two years ago: third parties have made the series load-bearing. METR's research note (2026-04-21) reads the series as evidence on AI R&D progress and reports "36 contributors have submitted 77 records", "cutting the training time from 45 minutes to 1.43 minutes, a 31x speedup", decomposed by idea depth and provenance. The Automated LLM Speedrunning Benchmark (arXiv 2506.22419, NeurIPS 2025) turns 21 records into 19 agent tasks whose score, the **fraction of speedup recovered**, divides by the per-record step-down t_i − t_{i+1} — so a step-down inside the noise floor makes that denominator partly noise by construction. Prime Intellect ships the speedrun as an agent environment. Muon's path from this series to frontier-scale use is the standing example that its step-downs are real method.

## Presuppositions

P1. Historical record configurations are still runnable. **CONTESTED** (driver/kernel/dependency drift). Partial evidence both ways: 2506.22419 states it re-ran every ground-truth record on its own cluster as of ~May 2025, which implies the then-existing 21 records ran; nothing opened speaks to the ~77-record series or to 2026 toolchains. The count of non-runnable records is itself a reportable output, not a blocker.
P2. 8×H100 wall-clock is reproducible across nodes and repetitions to within a stated dispersion. **CONTESTED — measuring this dispersion is half the question.** The HPC literature found by search (arXiv 2208.11035 "Not All GPUs Are Created Equal"; IEEE TPDS 2026 "Quantifying Performance Variability in GPU Clusters") treats node-to-node and run-to-run GPU variability as a measured, non-negligible quantity; neither was opened, so no magnitude is imported here.
P3. "Transfer" is definable without unlimited compute, via a pre-declared ladder rather than frontier replication. **ESTABLISHED by construction** in V2 (a second in-repo target plus a 2–4× rung).
P4. Accepted records are separable into changes. **CONTESTED** — V2 removes the dependence by fixing the unit at the record; per-idea attribution is explicitly out of scope.
P5. The loss target is comparable across the series (FineWeb validation split, tokenizer, token count fixed across record versions). **CONTESTED, must be verified per record, not assumed** — and if it is not fixed, that is a finding about the ledger, not an obstacle.
P6. The record's own p<0.01 loss rule was honoured per record. **CONTESTED until checked** from the submitted logs.
P7. The rules text is as quoted (see Motivating gap). **CONTESTED here** — intake-asserted, verification route unavailable within caps. If the rules in fact require replicated timing, limb (i)'s premise weakens and the question degenerates toward a reproduction exercise; this is the cheapest check an adversary can run and it should run it first.

## What would count as an answer

Per-record re-timed distributions (≥5 repetitions, ≥2 distinct 8×H100 nodes), in both environment arms, against each record's claimed time; from them a count and a log-additive compute-share of step-downs that fall inside the measured dispersion (fail the p<0.01 separation); plus, on the pre-declared ladder, the retained speedup per accepted record at larger scale with CIs; plus a count of records that no longer run, by failure class. Pre-committed readings: **all real** = every step-down separates and every retained speedup's CI excludes zero — which makes the series a trustworthy ladder and is itself worth knowing for the benchmarks built on it; **partly measurement** = a material share of the cumulative log-speedup sits in non-separating step-downs; **partly toolchain** = the two arms disagree, locating that share in environment drift rather than in either noise or method; **selected-on-target** = step-downs survive re-timing but retained speedup at the next rung is zero within CI.

## Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3 opened, with unit / endpoint / comparator / estimand)

**P1. Jiang et al., "The Automated LLM Speedrunning Benchmark: Reproducing NanoGPT Improvements", arXiv:2506.22419v2 (NeurIPS 2025) — OPENED (main text; Appendix A not read).**
- Unit of analysis: a **record transition** ⟨R_{i−1}, R_i, t_i, hint set m⟩; 19 tasks drawn from 21 records (May 2025), record 7 excluded because "its speedup comes from a PyTorch upgrade".
- Endpoint: wall-clock time on one 8×H100 node to val loss 3.28 on FineWeb; headline metric **FSR**, fraction of speedup recovered.
- Comparator/reference: **human record times re-measured on their own hardware** — "training times for human records based on rerunning each ground-truth record on the same hardware configuration"; "Appendix A reports the near exact reproduction of training times for human records on our cluster."
- Estimand: mean FSR across tasks per (model, scaffold, hint-level) cell. Repetition is reported for **agent** runs ("mean FSR ... across 3 full search runs"; "mean and std over 3 runs"), not for record re-runs; the main text gives no dispersion for any individual record timing, no noise-floor test on step-downs, and no p<0.01 rule.

**P2. METR, "Evidence on AI R&D Progress from NanoGPT", research note, 2026-04-21 — OPENED.**
- Unit: the **individual record** (77 records, 36 contributors; four recent records co-credited to agent systems).
- Endpoint: the **recorded** wall-clock time to 3.28 as published, plus hand-assigned labels per record (optimization depth, provenance). Labels are descriptive, not measured outcomes.
- Comparator: each record against the previous best time; human progress as baseline for agent contributions; external long-run efficiency-gain estimates for context.
- Estimand: the cumulative recorded speedup (~31×) broken down by depth and provenance — explicitly "attributions of the recorded improvements, not counterfactual estimates". No re-timing, no repeated runs, no timing-noise discussion, no reproducibility count. On transfer it states the worry and stops: "Training GPT-2 on 8×H100s is not frontier pretraining", footnote "small-scale-only tricks likely won't" generalize.

**P3. Mattson et al., "MLPerf Training Benchmark", MLSys 2020 — OPENED.**
- Unit: one training session of a benchmark on a submitted system.
- Endpoint: time to reach a fixed quality threshold (time-to-train) — the same endpoint class as the speedrun.
- Comparator: other runs of the same system; the same system across rounds.
- Estimand: reported time-to-train as a **trimmed mean over retained runs**. Verbatim: "DNN training involves many stochastic influences that manifest in substantial run-to-run variation"; "MLPerf requires that submissions provide several runs of each benchmark to stabilize timing"; "Vision tasks require 5 runs to ensure 90% of entries from the same system are within 5%; all other tasks require 10 runs to ensure 90% of entries from the same system are within 10%"; "MLPerf drops the fastest and slowest times, reporting the arithmetic mean of the remaining runs as the result." Those 5%/10% figures are **rule design targets, not reported measurements of observed spread**, and the paper gives no CV for time-to-train and never asks whether a reported gain sits inside the noise.

**P4 (named, NOT OPENED — grounds no grade).** Fichte, Hecher, Szeider, "A Time Leap Challenge for SAT-Solving", arXiv:2008.02215. Found at query 10; its published design (running old and new competition solvers across old and new hardware to split algorithmic from hardware progress over a record series) is, on its title and abstract alone, the closest executed analogue of limb (i)'s two-arm decomposition. ## Novelty — how this differs from each

- **vs P1:** same object, orthogonal estimand. P1 measures **agent capability** and treats the record series as a fixed, valid substrate; the candidate asks whether that substrate's step-downs are measurable at all. P1's record re-timing is a single-point sanity check with no reported repetition count, no node-to-node arm, no dispersion, no per-step-down test, no log-additive share, and no transfer rung. The sensitivity runs the candidate's way: because FSR divides by t_i − t_{i+1}, a step-down inside the noise floor destabilises P1's own denominator — so the candidate's limb (i) is a prerequisite for P1's metric, not a rival to it.
- **vs P2:** same object, adjacent endpoint. P2 partitions the **recorded** 31× by **idea type**; the candidate asks how much of that 31× is **recoverable under repetition** and how much **pays at another scale**. P2's unit is the record-as-published; the candidate's is the record-as-re-run. P2 states the transfer worry in a footnote and measures nothing on it.
- **vs P3:** the method prior, different unit and no series estimand. P3 institutes exactly the variance control the speedrun lacks, on the same endpoint and hardware class, which is why it is the comparator that makes the candidate's premise credible rather than a complaint. But its unit is a **system submission** in a synchronised round, not a record in an append-only improvement ledger; it never asks what share of a series' cumulative claimed speedup lies inside dispersion, and it publishes no measured dispersion to borrow.
- **Negative, stated in the method-owning field's vocabulary** (per the ledger's rule): in HPC performance-variability engineering (query 5) and in empirical algorithmics / competition re-evaluation (query 10), both the measurement design and the hardware-vs-algorithm decomposition are mature, named problems with executed instances. Nothing found in either vocabulary is pointed at this record series, and nothing in the ML vocabulary (queries 1–4, 6, 9, 11) reports a timing distribution per record, a dispersion-relative surviving share, or a retained-speedup-at-scale figure for any accepted record. The one adjacent published fact is categorical, not a share: P1 excludes exactly one record because its speedup came from a PyTorch upgrade — an existence proof that at least one recorded step-down was toolchain rather than method.

## Significance — consequence

**If mostly real:** the series is a trustworthy ladder. The ~31× becomes a defensible datum for AI-R&D-progress arguments (P2's use), the FSR denominators in P1 are sound, and the speedrun's minimal rule set is vindicated — a public optimization record can run on an unreplicated time comparison without accumulating noise, which is a non-obvious and useful methodological result in its own right.
**If partly measurement:** every artefact built on the per-record step-down inherits a noise floor nobody has stated. Concretely, FSR scores (P1) on small-step-down tasks are partly arbitrary; agent-vs-human comparisons scored in recovered seconds need re-scoring on surviving seconds; and the headline cumulative figure needs a dispersion attached before it can carry a progress claim.
**If partly toolchain (the two arms disagree):** the series is partly a record of PyTorch and kernel progress, which splits "algorithmic progress" from "toolchain progress" on a dated series — the quantity the efficiency-trend literature estimates indirectly.
**If it survives re-timing but not transfer:** the records are real measurements of something selected on one target, which is a statement about what a fixed-target optimization ledger can and cannot certify — directly relevant to anyone reading step-downs as general technique.

## Significance — audience

1. **The speedrun's maintainer and contributors** — the actionable output is a rule change with an off-the-shelf precedent: require k timed repetitions and report a trimmed mean (P3's rule, verbatim transplantable), and publish a minimum detectable speedup so that a submission smaller than the noise floor is not acceptable. This community demonstrably acts on rules: it already imposes p<0.01 on the loss axis.
2. **Builders of agent-research benchmarks on this substrate** — P1 (Meta/UCL), P2/METR (both the April note and the Expenditure Horizon application), Prime Intellect's environment. They need the noise floor of their own denominator; this is the most direct consumer and it is sourced, not asserted.
3. **Training-systems and optimizer researchers** deciding which speedrun techniques to carry upward. Muon is the precedent that makes the series worth mining; a retained-speedup table at a declared rung tells them which other records are worth the port.
4. **ML metascience / benchmark governance**, as the first measured case of an append-only public optimization record audited on its own record axis.
Honest discount: frontier teams do not adopt a technique because of a two-second step-down, and a null result ("all real") would change no one's behaviour except to retire a worry. The force of the question sits with audiences 1 and 2, both of whom have published artefacts that depend on the answer.

## Significance — fecundity

Opens: a noise-floor-aware design for public optimization records (what minimum detectable improvement must a record rule enforce, given the objective's measurement variance?); the period-correct-vs-common-environment decomposition of a dated ledger into method and toolchain progress (the SAT time-leap design, as far as this record's searches went, never transplanted to ML); whether P2's depth/provenance labels **predict** survival and transfer, which would turn a descriptive taxonomy into a forecasting instrument; whether agent-vs-human research comparisons should be scored in surviving rather than recorded speedup; and the general governance question of how to accept records against a stochastic objective when the record axis is the one without a test.

## Significance — anchoring

The small-to-large non-transfer anomaly (Narang et al. 2021; Kaddour et al. 2023's finding that efficient-training gains vanish against a properly-decayed baseline at matched compute — both known to this ledger from UQ-20261016-53's record rather than opened here); the algorithmic-vs-hardware progress attribution problem (SAT time-leap, unopened; efficiency-trend estimates cited by P2); adaptive overfitting to a fixed target; and MLPerf's variance control (P3) as the standing fix that this record series has not adopted.

## Reason for silence

Three mechanisms, compounding.
**New instrument, newly load-bearing.** The series is an instrument of a kind that barely existed two years ago, and it only became worth auditing when third parties began reading its step-downs as data — P1 in mid-2025, P2 and the Expenditure Horizon application in 2026. The audit becomes interesting exactly when the ledger stops being a hobby.
**Disciplinary boundary, in the direction the ledger's rule names.** The method lives with HPC performance-variability engineering and empirical algorithmics (queries 5, 10), which own the measurement design and the hardware-vs-algorithm decomposition but have no interest in a GPT-2 leaderboard. The ML groups that did touch the series arrived with a different question — can an agent reproduce it — and so re-timed the records as a sanity check for their own denominator (P1) rather than as an audit of the ledger.
**Incentive and cost.** The people with matched 8×H100 access and standing in the series are its contributors; auditing a volunteer leaderboard's past records has no reward and some social cost. And the bill is real: my own arithmetic on round assumptions — ~77 records, a generous mean of ~20 min per run, 5 repetitions, 2 nodes — is of order 2,500 node-hours, i.e. ~20,000 H100-hours, before the transfer ladder. That is small for a lab and prohibitive for the individuals who hold the relevant expertise, which is a textbook incentive gap rather than an absence of thought.
