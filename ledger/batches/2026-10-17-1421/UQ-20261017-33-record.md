Domain(s): Biology — computational-biology research methods and metascience. Frame restricted to non-clinical biological analyses (genomics, phylogenetics, single-cell, image quantification); clinical and diagnostic pipelines excluded. Method transplanted from software engineering (mutation testing, mutant score, the coupling effect) and reliability engineering (software fault injection).

Question (canonical):
V0 (intake, recorded): For published biological analyses whose code and data are archived and re-runnable, what fraction of realistic single-point faults injected into the analysis pipeline are both silent — passing every quality-control gate, log check and sanity plot the pipeline itself emits — and consequential, changing the paper's headline claim past that paper's own stated threshold, and does that fraction depend on the analysis class rather than on the authors' software practices?
V1 (Gate 1 reformulation, ADOPTED — one reformulation used, for well-posedness only): Over a pre-declared frame of published non-clinical biological analyses that re-run end-to-end from archived code and data and reproduce their published headline numbers to a declared tolerance, and over a pre-registered, frequency-weighted catalogue of single-point faults derived from evidence (bug-fix commits in the tool versions actually used, correction and retraction notices, issue trackers): what is the per-fault probability that an injected fault is SILENT — the run completes and trips no hard failure, no flagged threshold and no visibly anomalous sanity plot among those the pipeline itself emits, judged by a pre-declared rubric applied by assessors blinded to fault status, with inter-rater agreement reported — AND CONSEQUENTIAL — the paper's pre-extracted headline claim changes past that paper's own stated threshold (sign flip of a named effect, crossing the paper's own significance threshold, change in top-k membership of a reported ranked list, or the reported interval no longer containing the original point estimate)? Secondarily, in a crossed analyses x fault-class design, how does that probability partition into analysis-class, fault-class and per-paper variance, with the authors' software practices (tests present, CI, containerisation) entered as per-paper covariates? And in a pre-declared higher-order arm (fault pairs), does the single-fault estimate bound the two-fault rate as the coupling effect would predict?

Alternative formulations:
(1) If a biologist's published analysis had one plausible bug — an off-by-one in coordinates, a swapped strand, a mis-sorted join, a unit mismatch, a mis-specified contrast — how often would nothing in the pipeline notice and the conclusion still change?
(2) What is the mutant-kill rate of biology's de facto test suite (its QC plots, logs and reviewer intuition), scored against the biological conclusion rather than against the code?

Motivating gap: Software engineering measures fault sensitivity by injecting small realistic faults and asking what the test suite kills; reliability engineering injects faults into systems and asks what the detectors catch. Computational biology imported the DUAL technique — metamorphic testing, which supplies an invariant where no oracle exists (Chen et al., BMC Bioinformatics 2009; later arXiv:1904.08007 on protein-function prediction tools) — and so measures TOOL-level invariant violation. Both opened and read (see priors): their unit is a program, their test set is relations the testers authored, and their endpoint is MR violation. The quantity the transplant makes available is different in unit, endpoint and estimand: not whether a tool violates an invariant, but what share of plausible faults in a SPECIFIC PUBLISHED ANALYSIS are simultaneously invisible to that analysis's own QC output and sufficient to move its claim past its own threshold. The field's evidence on this is anecdotal (famous column-swap and coordinate bugs, spreadsheet gene-symbol corruption, retractions traced to one line of script) and so supports no rate, no ranking of fault classes, and no specification for the assertions that would catch them. Gate 2's own-words query (Q6) returned only blog and forum material on 0-/1-based coordinates, which is direct evidence for the "anecdotal" characterisation.

Presuppositions:
1. A reproducible corpus exists — enough published non-clinical biological analyses re-run end-to-end from archived code and data to support class-level estimates. CONTESTED: computational-reproducibility rates are low; the recoverable fraction both bounds the frame and must be reported as a primary finding, not a footnote.
2. "Realistic fault" is specifiable non-arbitrarily. CONTESTED: requires a frequency-weighted catalogue derived from evidence (bug-fix commits in the exact tool versions used, correction/retraction notices, issue trackers), pre-declared before any injection; otherwise the estimand is an artefact of the injector's imagination. The estimand is therefore explicitly conditional on catalogue C and must be reported as such.
3. "Headline claim" and "changed" are operationalisable per paper. DEFENSIBLE with a pre-declared extraction rule (named effect direction, the paper's own significance threshold, top-k of a reported ranked list, reported interval); papers with no quantitative claim are excluded in advance by that rule.
4. Single-fault injection is informative about real errors — i.e. real faults are mostly single and localised (the coupling effect). CONTESTED and NOT assumed: the coupling effect is debated in its home field, so the design carries a higher-order (fault-pair) arm to test rather than inherit it; without that arm the presupposition travels untested.
5. The quantity is not already measured by metamorphic testing or by pipeline-variability studies. This was the live novelty question and was settled by READING, not by title inference: Chen et al. 2009 (tool unit, MR endpoint), arXiv:1904.08007 (tool unit, pass/fail endpoint) and Kiar et al. 2021 (numerical-noise perturbation, variability endpoint, no QC gate assessed) each differ on unit, endpoint or injected object. Residual risk named below (arXiv:2608.27100, COULD NOT READ).
6. Estimates are conditional on silence by construction: a fault that trips an existing gate is a SUCCESS of the pipeline, so the denominator is injected faults, not papers, and the two marginal rates (killed by existing gates; consequential given silence) must both be reported. ESTABLISHED as a design requirement; flagged because getting the denominator wrong makes the headline number uninterpretable.
7. Added at Gate 1: "silent" is partly counterfactual. A pipeline may emit a warning in a log nobody reads, or a sanity plot whose anomaly only a specialist would see. CONTESTED: the rubric must define silence over EMITTED artefacts judged by blinded assessors, and report agreement; what a given author would have noticed is not estimable.
8. Added at Gate 1: the frame selects on software practice. Analyses that re-run are a better-practice subset, so the class-versus-practice limb is range-restricted by construction and must be reported with the realised range of the practice covariates, not as an unrestricted comparison.

What would count as an answer: An interval-estimated silent-and-consequential fraction over a pre-declared frame of successfully reproduced analyses, with the fault catalogue, the injection protocol, the silence rubric and the claim-change rule all registered before the first injection, and a crossed (analyses x fault classes) design with per-paper random effects separating per-paper from per-class variance. The report must state (i) the recoverable-corpus fraction, (ii) the share of injected faults killed by existing gates, (iii) the silent-and-consequential fraction with interval, (iv) the per-class ranking, and (v) the higher-order-fault arm's result — without (v) the coupling-effect presupposition is carried untested. Pre-committed readings: "current practice is safe" = the fraction is near zero across classes, a strong positive result for existing QC and a redirection of reproducibility effort away from code faults; "it is not" = a ranked list of fault classes no existing gate catches, directly usable as the specification for new assertions in nf-core, Galaxy, Bioconductor and GATK-style pipelines; "not estimable" = the recoverable corpus is too small or too practice-skewed for class-level estimates, which is itself a reportable finding about the archive.

Significance — consequence: If the fraction is near zero across classes, that is a strong positive result about infrastructure nobody has been able to claim on evidence: the QC that nf-core, Galaxy, Bioconductor and GATK-style pipelines already emit would be shown to be a de facto test suite with real kill power, and the reproducibility agenda's emphasis on code faults could be redirected toward design, data and analytic flexibility, where the measured rates already are. Significance — fecundity: it defines a pipeline "QC coverage" metric — the biology analogue of a mutation score, but scored against conclusions — which makes several further questions askable: which single assertion would have caught the largest share of historical corrections and retractions; whether fault sensitivity trades off against pipeline complexity or tool count; whether class-level sensitivity predicts which published claims later fail replication; how to price code review against independent re-analysis; whether the coupling effect holds in data-analysis code at all (the higher-order arm); and whether assertion density, not test count, is the right software-quality covariate for scientific code. It also gives benchmark developers a target other than tool accuracy.
Significance — anchoring: the computational-reproducibility literature's unresolved gap between "re-runs" and "is right"; the anecdotal canon of analysis-code errors (column swaps, coordinate conventions, the ~20% Excel gene-symbol corruption figure, retractions traced to one script line) that supports no rate; the oracle problem in scientific-software testing (P1, P3), which this reframes by using the paper's own claim as the oracle; the coupling-effect debate in mutation testing; silent data corruption in HPC; and the multi-analyst/multiverse results on analytic variability, which bound the variability from defensible choices but say nothing about faults.

Reason for silence: Three mechanisms, the second being the distinctive one. (1) Disciplinary boundary at the instrument: the method's owners (software-engineering researchers) score mutants against a TEST SUITE, and a published analysis has no test suite, so the endpoint "this paper's claim" lies outside their instrument and outside their venues — hence P1, P3 and arXiv:1904.08007 all retreat to the tool, which is the object their methods can grade. Biology's own methodologists, symmetrically, evaluate tools and benchmarks because tools are reusable and comparable, while a published analysis is a one-off artefact that nobody owns after publication and that no benchmark track rewards studying. (2) Incentive and venue gap bordering on taboo: deliberately breaking a NAMED published analysis to see whether its own QC notices is adversarial toward identifiable colleagues and reads as an accusation, even when the finding is about infrastructure rather than about the authors. The genres that exist are the ones that avoid this — method re-analyses critique methods, and the forensic-bioinformatics genre is reserved for cases where harm is already suspected — so there is no safe framing or venue for "I injected faults into fifty colleagues' papers". A design that reports only aggregate, anonymised, class-level rates is the missing conceptual move, and it is a social move, not a technical one. (3) Feasibility threshold: re-running a published analysis a few hundred times with injected faults needs containerised workflows, archived raw data and cheap compute, which is a development of roughly the last five years (nf-core/WorkflowHub/Zenodo-era archiving); before that, presupposition 1 failed outright. Note the honest tension: mechanism (3) also predicts that someone will do this soon, and the unread arXiv:2608.27100 may be exactly that, one field over.
