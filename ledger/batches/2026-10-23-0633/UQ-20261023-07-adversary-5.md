## Restatement in my own words

Take a hospital that really does transfuse one red-cell unit and then re-checks the patient. Red-cell units are counted, not weighed: the bag's actual grams of haemoglobin are not part of the order. The question, as it now stands after Gate 1's single permitted repair (V2), asks three things of one such service, in non-bleeding adult inpatients, with each issued unit's haemoglobin mass on the record at issue and a declared 12–48 h post-transfusion blood draw: (a) of the episode-to-episode spread in how far the haemoglobin rises, how much is traceable to how many grams were in the particular bag (patient blood volume, pre-transfusion Hb, diagnosis, operator held in the model, and the blood-volume estimator's own error carried); (b) treating "whichever compatible unit the inventory handed over next" as the assignment mechanism and reporting a covariate-balance test, does the chance of a second unit inside 48 h move with the grams in the first bag; (c) what change in the 48-h second-unit rate would mass-based issuing at the same Hb target be predicted to produce. In one line: **is "one unit" a dose, and does the luck of the bag decide who gets stuck for another one?**

I took the Gate 1 measurand declaration at face value and applied it out loud (rule i): the *size* of the variance share, the *sign and steepness* of the slope, and the statement that bedside delivered grams are presently unmeasured are OUTPUTS, and I did not attack them as premises. I also treated V2's demanded covariate-balance test as a reported result, not an assumption. What is NOT exempt, and is where I spent attack 4, is the clause that a *recorded per-unit haemoglobin mass exists on issued units at all*.

## Attack 4 — presupposition failure, run the way the brief requires

The conditioning clauses of **V2 as it now stands, repair included**, written out literally and separated from anything a study would measure:

1. A service exists that operates a **documented single-unit-then-reassess policy** for adult inpatients.
2. **Non-bleeding** adult inpatients can be identified and separated inside that frame.
3. **Each issued unit's haemoglobin mass is recorded at issue**, with unit-to-recipient linkage. (The exposure. The question cannot be asked without it.)
4. A **12–48 h post-transfusion haemoglobin** exists for those episodes.
5. Issued haemoglobin mass **varies** across the compatible pool (non-degenerate exposure).
6. **Inventory-driven issue order** within the ABO/Rh- and specification-compatible pool functions as an assignment mechanism whose balance is testable.
7. Recipient **blood volume is estimable** from height, weight, sex, with its own error carriable.
8. **Second units within 48 h occur** often enough under a one-unit policy to carry a hazard.

Grades, each against a document:

1. **TRUE for the population, UNVERIFIED for the policy document.** JCI Insight 152598, Methods: "we included all adult patients who received a single RBC unit during 1 or more transfusion episodes", 102,043 evaluable single-unit transfusions — adult single-unit episodes exist in bulk and are retrievable. I opened no policy document (the transfusionguidelines.org single-unit audit PDF and Choosing Wisely Canada 1391 were returned and not opened), so "documented policy in force" is not something I checked.
2. **UNVERIFIED.** PMC6449900, on stable **non-bleeding** adults in nine ICUs, was returned by my query 9 and I did not open it; it is the kind of document that would settle this. I record that rather than an absence.
3. **CONTESTED, on sources opened — and this is the only clause that bites.** Nothing anyone has opened records a *measured* per-unit haemoglobin mass on issued units: JCI Insight 152598 states the unit's content "Not directly measured or reported" and uses donor fingerstick Hb as a proxy; Roubinian 2019 assumes a constant "~89 grams Hgb per transfused unit" and its Discussion defers the work to "normalize for donor hemoglobin levels"; DeSimone 2023's only donor haemoglobin value is a fingerstick concentration (14.4 g/dL, SD 1.1) and its delivered-quantity exposure is "Issued aliquot volume in mL/kg", a volume; Saqlain 2022 records no content or volume; Atilla 2017's per-unit figure is **calculated as "donor Hb level x 450 mL"** in a Hemosoft field, with no labelling of units, and fired in only 41.3% of episodes. Against that, the clause as *written* says "recorded", not "measured", and a calculated field is a record — so one instance satisfies it literally, and Epah 2022 predicts the quantity to about 1.43 g mean absolute error, which is a route to a recorded figure. **Contested, not false.** The clause's weakness is an instrument weakness: the only recorded instrument anyone has opened is donor capillary Hb times a **constant** volume, which carries no information about the unit's actual volume. **MY OWN REASONING, NOT A SOURCE FINDING:** an exposure of that form is close to a monotone transform of donor haemoglobin, so limb (a) measured with it would be approximately "what share of increment variance does donor Hb explain" — and donor Hb is already an exposure in both large adult cohorts (fingerstick Hb in JCI Insight 152598; deferred-for-future-work in Roubinian). I did not run the search that would have grounded the attenuation magnitude (capillary-vs-venous Hb error against the true between-unit spread in grams), and the between-unit spread itself was served to nobody, so I cannot put a number on it.
4. **TRUE (weakly sourced).** Saqlain 2022 Methods samples "12-18 hours after transfusion"; Roubinian's 24-h episode definition is the same practice. Such a draw is routinely present.
5. **Not a presupposition to attack — measurand-adjacent, and I declare it.** The magnitude of the spread is the thing (a) reports. That the spread is non-zero is treated as given by sources I opened (JCI's Discussion reasons about "factors associated with increased RBC unit mass"). The printed spread figure (Epah's 38.5–79.9 g) was served at no read level to anyone, so I give no number.
6. **UNVERIFIED, and partly a reported result rather than an assumption.** I opened nothing on issuing order. **MY OWN REASONING:** a unit's haemoglobin mass is fixed at collection and does not drift in inventory, so oldest-compatible-first ties issue order to **storage age**, not to grams, unless collection method or donor mix correlates with residence time; and the record's own worry about specification-directed units is handled by V2's restriction to the specification-compatible pool. V2 also pre-declares a covariate-balance test, which converts part of this into something the study reports. I did not run the search that would have grounded it either way.
7. **UNVERIFIED** (not checked).
8. **TRUE, sourced.** JCI Insight 152598 models "need for additional RBC transfusion within 48 hours" in adult single-unit episodes and prints ORs on exposed subsets of n = 43 and n = 247 — the event is recorded, frequent enough to model, and already modelled.

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional** — nothing Unresolved, so not a Rejection; but novelty does not hold clean: L2's question form is asked and answered in adults with proxy unit-level exposures (JCI Insight 152598, 48-h additional-transfusion ORs 2.35 and 1.54), L3's policy form was randomised by Atilla 2017, and what is left un-asked is narrower than the record claims — the conjunction with a recorded per-unit haemoglobin **mass** as the exposure, which no source opened measures.

**Single strongest killing point:** the record's "L2 is unaddressed in everything returned" is false on a paper my own search returned and I opened — JCI Insight 152598 models "need for additional RBC transfusion within 48 hours" in 102,043 adult single-unit transfusions and prints unit-level ORs of 2.35 (1.16–4.76, p = 0.017, n = 43) and 1.54 (1.03–2.30, p = 0.035, n = 247) in its Results, so the second-unit limb survives only on the identity of its exposure, not on its question form.

**What would settle the clause I could not close (nominated by KIND, per the rule).** Clause 3 needs a document that (i) covers units actually **issued**, not quality-control samples, (ii) is **per unit** rather than per batch, and (iii) **prints a haemoglobin mass in grams for that unit**, or defines a database field holding one.
