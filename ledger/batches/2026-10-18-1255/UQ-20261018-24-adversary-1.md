## Restatement (my own words, before engaging the record's framing)

Children and adults who get CT scans are not a random sample: the sicker you are, the more and the heavier the scanning, so a dose-cancer association computed from patients' own doses may just be measuring how ill they were. But the *same* examination carries several-fold different dose depending on which machine at which hospital with which local protocol you happen to be scanned on, and since roughly 2011 that per-scan delivered dose is machine-reported and individually linkable. So: take the dose that a given hospital-scanner-protocol *typically* delivers for your examination class in that quarter (computed leaving your own scans out), use it as the only thing allowed to move your dose, and estimate the cancer dose-response from that between-facility shove alone. Then run the ordinary patient-dose analysis on exactly the same person-years and subtract. The question asks for three numbers and one battery: how hard the instrument shoves (first stage), what the shoved estimate says (IV ERR per unit organ dose), how far it sits from the ordinary estimate and in which direction (the answer), and whether the instrument survives falsification (balance, negative-control outcomes, an MRI/ultrasound sham). A confidence interval too wide to tell concordance from discordance counts as an answer.

In one sentence: *does the CT dose-cancer slope survive when the only dose variation used is the variation created by where you were scanned rather than by how sick you were, and by how much does it move?*

## Presupposition 7, tested (this is the substantive finding of this Gate 4)

The record's own instruction was to test presupposition 7 first. I opened the IARC EPI-CT dosimetry document. It assigns machine settings from GROUPED probability distributions at hospital x machine category x examination type x age group x period, not from each patient's own recorded parameters. Quoted as served: "PACS dataset was not used at the individual patient level"; "Linkage between PACS and RIS data was only possible for a relatively small subset of CT scans (30,000)"; "PACS data were only available for scans performed after 1995"; "For earlier scans, we obtained information on scan parameters from national surveys"; "Only 23% of the participating hospitals provided information on machine settings"; "Values of parameters shared among members of a subgroup" were "sampled once from the appropriate PDFs for the entire subgroup", against "unshared dosimetric parameters (for example, anatomical area explored)" sampled per scan; the design "maintains a separation of shared and unshared error". The document does not use the term "Berkson".

Consequence, stated precisely, because it cuts both ways and the record's framing gets one side of it wrong:

(i) AGAINST the candidate's significance narrative. The flagship contested observational estimate's per-examination dose contrast is ALREADY largely facility-and-era level. The candidate's instrument Z is therefore close in content to the exposure variable EPI-CT already uses, rather than being "a different source of variation" from the contested literature. It follows that concordance in the candidate's RDSR cohort would NOT "largely retire the confounding-by-indication objection" to EPI-CT, because the EPI-CT coefficient is not identified off patient-level dose in the first place; in that cohort the endogenous, patient-level channel is predominantly SCAN COUNT and presentation, which the candidate's design CONDITIONS ON rather than instruments. The record's claim at line 35 and the first sentence of Significance-consequence must be restated before this entry is written.

(ii) NOT against the question. V2's comparator is the conventional adjusted estimate computed on the SAME person-years of the SAME RDSR cohort, where individual dose genuinely varies within facility-protocol-quarter (patient size, tube-current modulation, scan length). Against that comparator, Z and the individual exposure are not the same object and limbs (a)-(d) remain well defined. So presupposition 7 damages the external inference the candidate advertises, not the internal posedness of what it asks.
