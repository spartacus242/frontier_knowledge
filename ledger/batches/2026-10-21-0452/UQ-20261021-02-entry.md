Question (canonical): Taking the decision-maker as ascertained by a declared gold standard (observation or blinded record review, with an agreement statistic) and EHR order/audit-log fields as a candidate proxy: (a) under a pre-declared attribution rule, what is the misclassification rate of "recorded prescriber" as an indicator of the decision-maker, per drug class, with the no-identifiable-decider share reported separately; (b) what is the proxy's sensitivity/specificity against that gold standard; and (c) on one cohort, one estimand and one comparator, what is the signed change in a prescriber-preference IV estimate (and its conditional F / partial R-squared) and in a prescriber-targeted feedback effect when exposure is reassigned from the recorded prescriber to the attributed decider, against a pre-declared equivalence margin? (V2, the Gate 1 reformulation; V1 — which defined the decider by audit-log fields — is retained in the record.)

Alternative formulations:
- Does the name on the prescription belong to the person who chose the drug, and does it matter to the studies and interventions that assume it does?
- Treating "recorded prescriber" as a mismeasured indicator of the decision-maker, what are its misclassification rate and the resulting bias in prescriber-preference IV first stages and in prescriber-feedback effect estimates?

What would count as an answer: (i) per-class misclassification rate with CI against the gold standard plus the no-decider share; (ii) proxy sensitivity/specificity; (iii) side-by-side IV estimates (recorded prescriber vs attributed decider) on one cohort, with change in conditional F / partial R-squared and signed change in the point estimate against a pre-declared margin; (iv) feedback effects re-estimated on reassigned exposure. The intake's negative branch ("misattribution under 2-3% in every class") is already excluded for at least one setting by Ross et al. (61%).
