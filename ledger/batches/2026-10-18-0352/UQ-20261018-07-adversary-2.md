## Restatement (my own words)

Industry's lithium does not all come from the same kind of rock. Salar brines carry heavy lithium (δ7Li around +8 to +11‰ in South America, +16 to +31‰ in Qaidam), spodumene carries light lithium (−0.3 to +6.0‰), and refining shifts a product a further few ‰ off its feed. The feedstock mix has moved over 2010–2025 (hard-rock share up, recycled material starting). The candidate asks: (i) build a production-weighted δ7Li for the commercial lithium pool year by year out of published deposit-type δ7Li, published per-route process offsets and public annual production shares (incl. recycled share), with a propagated interval, and report the year-on-year change; and (ii) swap that year-appropriate number in for the "anthropogenic endmember" of one named published mixing-model apportionment (Choi et al. 2019, effluent 14.5 ± 4.3‰, 1σ, n=6) and see whether the inferred anthropogenic fraction moves outside that study's stated interval. In short: is the "anthropogenic lithium" isotope endmember a constant, or a quantity that drifts with the supply chain — and does the drift matter downstream?

### My own arithmetic (labelled as mine, not any source's conclusion)

From Desaulty's domain MEDIANS (+2.8‰ hard rock, +9.8‰ S-American brine, +24.3‰ Qaidam brine), a convex-combination pool mean moves by roughly 2‰ for a 30-point swap of hard-rock for S-American brine share (e.g. 30/60/10 → 60/30/10 gives 9.2‰ → 7.1‰ on my numbers). The production shares in that illustration are MY assumption — query 9's production-share search returned only unopened commodity profiles — so this is indicative only, and I explicitly do not claim it as a bound: because the Qaidam domain median sits ~21‰ above the spodumene median, a large change in the Chinese-brine share could in principle move the pool mean well past Choi's ±4.3‰ (1σ). Carrying the sources' uncertainty: the domain IQRs are 6.3‰, 3.4‰ and 15.3‰ wide at n = 20, 103 and 20, so the domain medians themselves are loosely constrained and a propagated pool interval may be wider than the drift it is meant to detect. Conclusion I will stand behind: the computation is short; its OUTCOME is not pre-determined by inspection.

Scope check I withdrew: I initially intended to argue that at industrial yields mass balance forbids large product-vs-feed offsets, so the pool must equal the mine-weighted mean and the process terms are second-order. Desaulty's own text defeats that in this regime — "even a 95% yield causes strong fractionation", ~80% evaporation recovery with "Li losses ... highest" where fractionation occurs, and Honra's +2.4 to +4.5‰ refining offsets. I am not asserting that impossibility.
