## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take deceased-donor kidneys or livers for which somebody still has the temperature trace of the box or the perfusate from cross-clamp to reperfusion. Boil each trace down to one number: time spent at each temperature, weighted by how fast chemistry runs at that temperature (an Arrhenius integral with the activation energy fixed in advance), plus an extra penalty for time spent below freezing. Then ask two things. (1) Prediction: if you put that number into a risk model that already contains cold ischemia time, does the model discriminate and calibrate better for delayed graft function and 1-year graft function? (2) Mediation: does the cold ischemia time coefficient shrink when the number is added — i.e. has the clock been standing in for an unmeasured thermal history all along?

Stripped of the pharmaceutical vocabulary, this is: *does a nonlinear average of storage temperature, times duration, beat duration alone?* Note for later that the record itself supplies the equivalence ("equivalently, duration combined with the ICH-Q1A mean kinetic temperature"), because MKT is *defined* as the single temperature reproducing the Arrhenius integral. So the proposed exposure is exactly the pair (duration, MKT) — no more, no less.

Retrospective cohort, 113 grafts / 94 deceased donors on hypothermic machine perfusion; perfusate temperature recorded at 5, 15, 60, 180 min; 21 (19%) DGF. ## Recommended verdict

**Rejected** — Unresolved on attack 2 (Disguise) is fatal under Gate 4's rule, and attack 7 (Reason for silence) is also Unresolved; the salvageable residue is the narrower mediation question "how much of cold ischemia time's estimated effect is unmeasured thermal history?", which should be re-entered as its own candidate without the Arrhenius wrapper.

**Single strongest killing point:** MKT is defined as the temperature that reproduces the Arrhenius integral, so the proposed dose is exactly (duration, MKT), and over the realised 0-10 C band MKT differs from the plain mean temperature by ~0.3 K — so the candidate's distinctive construct is numerically "duration x mean storage temperature", and that question has already been asked and partly answered in the same organ, for the same outcome, from the same pump-log data the record names as its only feasible source: Sweet et al., *Prog Transplant* 2023;33(4):341-347 (PMID 37964564), T5min >10 C vs <=10 C, OR 4.5 for delayed graft function — a paper the record's 11-query Gate 2 missed.
