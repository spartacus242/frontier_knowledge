## 1. Restatement (my own words)

When a quantum-computing paper reports a hardware result from a cloud processor (IBM and peers), the vendor has also been publishing a near-daily calibration record for that processor (T1, T2, gate and readout errors). Look up the device's calibration quality on the day(s) each paper's experiment ran, rank it against the same device's quality over the surrounding ~two months, and ask: are the run days systematically among the device's better days? Prerequisite sub-questions: what share of papers give enough information (backend + date) to do the lookup, and does the vendor's record still reach back to those dates? A rank distribution that is flat means no selection; a pile-up near the top is a measurable "good-day" effect on the published record, whatever its cause.

## 4. Recommended verdict

No attack reached Unresolved; attacks 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 are Partial, and the record's own well-posedness score is 2/3 with P1 now evidenced as failing for retired devices → **Provisional**. Promotion requires: (i) a documented retention audit of live-device history depth on the current IBM Quantum Platform plus the coverage of third-party mirrors (Zenodo 4898393, IEEE DataPort, caslab) against a named paper corpus; (ii) replacing the primary F with a selection-blind construction (device-level aggregate or pre-fixed qubit subset) so the null is exact for the headline estimand; (iii) carrying the reserved-vs-open-queue contrast in the pipeline so the composite is partly decomposed. Strongest killing point: the "archivally recoverable" device state exists only while a device is online — IBM's runtime returns `None` for properties of retired backends (issue #2255, open, quoted above) and historical lookup was unsupported on the cloud channel as late as 2023 — so the feasible sample is a shrinking, recency-biased slice of the literature that the record never verified; second: the primary figure of merit is biased toward favourable ranks under the null by regression to the mean on run-day qubit selection (my own unsourced reasoning; a fallback F avoids it).
