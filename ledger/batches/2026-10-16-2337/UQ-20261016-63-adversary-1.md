## Restatement (my own words)

Every year WHO/UNICEF (immunization coverage), UN IGME (under-five mortality) and MMEIG (maternal mortality) re-publish their whole historical country-by-year series, not just the newest year. The candidate wants to (a) line up each year's release as a "vintage" and measure how much the number for the same country and year moves from one release to the next, and whether those moves have structure over time; (b) test, by regressing the revision on the earlier release versus on the later one, whether the earlier release was a predictably biased guess of the later one (the macroeconomists' "news versus noise" test), while admitting that here the revision comes from changing models and inputs rather than late data returns; and (c) take a pre-registered sample of published papers that used an older release, re-run them on the current release, and count how many flip sign, cross a policy threshold, or change significance. It also asks what a "real-time database" of these indicators would need to contain.
