Question (canonical, Gate 1 version B — used for grading):
Train k >= 3 sparse-MoE language models under one recipe differing only in seed (router/expert initialization and data order), at a scale at which OLMoE's router-saturation curve reproduces. Fix in advance a probe corpus, a layer set, top-k, and an expert matching rule M (Hungarian matching on per-expert activation profiles over the probe corpus). Then measure, with CIs: (i) A_cross, the cross-run agreement of the token-to-expert partition after M (ARI and NMI), at the 1%-of-pretraining checkpoint and at the end of training; (ii) S, the same agreement computed within a run between its 1% checkpoint and its own final checkpoint — the quantity OLMoE reports as ~60% top-8 saturation; (iii) A_0, a null built by expert label permutation and by matching one run's checkpoint to an unrelated run; and (iv) the share of between-seed variance in terminal held-out loss and downstream benchmark scores predictable from 1%-checkpoint routing statistics. Report A_cross - A_0 and A_cross / S per layer and depth, and the sensitivity of all four to the load-balancing auxiliary-loss weight.

Question (Gate 1 version A, as intaken — recorded, not graded):
In sparse mixture-of-experts language models, is the token-to-expert partition that saturates within the first ~1% of pretraining canonical — reproduced, up to expert relabelling, by independent runs of the same recipe differing only in seed and data order — or an arbitrary early lottery, and does the early-saturated routing state predict the run's terminal held-out loss and downstream scores?
(Reformulation was made only for well-posedness: version A leaves "canonical" undefined, names no matching rule, no null and no estimand, and does not say against which reference agreement is to be judged. Version B fixes the comparator triple (A_0, S) and makes the prediction limb a variance share. One reformulation used; the Gate 1 budget is spent.)

Alternative formulations:
(1) Do two MoE runs of one recipe differing only in seed converge, after optimal expert matching, on the same expert specialization — or does each freeze a different arbitrary partition in its first ~20B tokens?
(2) How much of the run-to-run variance in a sparse MoE's final quality is already determined by the routing decisions fixed in the first 1% of pretraining?
(3) (clustering-stability phrasing) Is the router's induced clustering of tokens over experts stable across resamplings of the training stochasticity, or is it a stability-zero partition that only looks structured because it is frozen early?

Motivating gap:
Verified anchor (read 2026-10-17, arxiv.org/html/2409.02060v2, OLMoE, Muennighoff et al., §5.1): "after 1% of pretraining (5000 steps or 20B tokens)" "up to ~60% of routing to the top-8 activated experts has already saturated", rising to "~80%" at 40% of pretraining, against a random baseline of "8/64 = 12.5% for k = 8"; the paper's own framing is that saturation "corresponds to whether the router weights are still learning which expert will process certain data". That measurement is WITHIN one run — intermediate checkpoints of OLMoE-1B-7B against its own final checkpoint — and the paper does not compare routing across seeds or independent runs (confirmed in the same read). The field therefore holds a quantified report that MoE routing is effectively decided in the first 1% of training and no measurement of whether what is decided is the same thing twice.
Strengthened at Gate 2: the saturation observation is not unique to OLMoE. FLAME-MoE (arXiv:2505.20225, opened at Gate 2) states in its own abstract that "routing behavior stabilizes early in training" and that "experts increasingly specialize on distinct token subsets" — a second, independent report of the same phenomenon, likewise within-run and with no seed replicates reported. So presupposition 1 moves from CONTESTED to partly corroborated.
Weakened at Gate 2: the semantic reading of expert specialization that gives the "arbitrary" branch its sting is already under attack from inside the field. "The Illusion of Specialization" (arXiv:2601.03425, opened) reports mean cross-domain Jaccard of top-k expert sets of 0.8735 in OLMoE-1B-7B, 0.8670 in DeepSeek-V2-Lite and 0.8670 in Qwen3-30B-A3B, with mean Gini of the Expert Contribution Index 0.8957/0.9207/0.9465, and concludes specialization is "far less pervasive than commonly believed". That is a within-model finding about domain-invariance, not a cross-run finding, but it discounts part of this candidate's stated consequence.

Its abstract reports no seed replicates and no seed variance. Presuppositions:
P1. Router saturation as OLMoE defines it generalizes beyond OLMoE-1B-7B. Was CONTESTED (one run, one architecture, one aux-loss setting); now PARTLY CORROBORATED by FLAME-MoE's independent "routing behavior stabilizes early in training" (opened). Still unquantified outside OLMoE.
P2. Experts in independently trained runs can be matched identifiably, so a cross-run partition comparison is meaningful. CONTESTED, and now with machinery: arXiv:2509.11348 (opened) characterizes MoE symmetry as "permutations acting on both the expert components and the gating function" and supplies "a matching algorithm that enables alignment between independently trained MoEs" by solving a Linear Assignment Problem on gate weights or Gram matrices. That the problem is tractable in weight space does not establish that a functional one-to-one correspondence exists; the candidate's pre-declared matching rule and label-permutation null carry this.
P3. A token-to-expert partition is a well-defined comparable object once tokenizer, top-k, context length and a fixed probe corpus are fixed. Established by construction (and the same recipe fixes the tokenizer across seeds, so UQ-20261016-47's tokenizer-variance problem does not arise here).
P4. Independent runs of one recipe differ measurably in terminal quality. Established for dense LMs; ASSUMED for MoE at the scale used. Gate 2 found no published seed-variance number for MoE terminal loss (one query; see Q11), so the magnitude the prediction limb regresses against is itself unmeasured — the design must report it.
P5. Saturation is not merely an artifact of the auxiliary load-balancing loss pinning routing early. CONTESTED; a plausible mechanism, which is why the aux-loss-weight sweep is part of the design rather than an extension. Gate 2's aux-loss query (Q7) returned the specialization-regularization and router-z-loss literature (e.g. arXiv:2505.22323 "Advancing Expert Specialization for Better MoE", arXiv:2506.14038 similarity-preserving routers) but nothing testing early-freeze-as-aux-loss-artifact; none opened, so this is an unopened negative.
P6. k >= 3 runs are affordable at a scale where OLMoE's saturation curve reproduces. CONTESTED feasibility. A smaller-scale replication of the saturation curve is a precondition, not an assumption. Partly supported by FLAME-MoE's released 38M-1.7B ladder and by the existence of seeded 1.7B launch scripts in a third-party repo (filename evidence only).
P7 (added at Gate 1). The within-run saturation level S is the right upper reference for A_cross. Contested: S and A_cross are different objects (temporal self-agreement vs cross-run agreement after matching), so A_cross/S is a ratio of two agreements, not a fraction of a ceiling. Declared as descriptive; the inferential comparison is A_cross against A_0.

What would count as an answer:
Train k >= 3 MoE runs identical but for seed (router/expert init and data order) at a scale where OLMoE's saturation curve reproduces, having first verified that it does. Pre-register (a) the matching rule — Hungarian matching on expert activation profiles over a fixed probe corpus, per layer — and (b) the null, built both by expert label permutation and by matching one run's checkpoint to an unrelated run (different recipe or different data). Report, with CIs: cross-run partition agreement after matching (ARI and NMI) against that null and against within-run saturation; whether 1%-of-training routing statistics predict terminal held-out loss and benchmark scores, with the between-seed variance decomposed into predicted and residual parts; and the sensitivity of all of it to the aux-loss weight. Three outcomes, each a measurement with an interval: canonical (A_cross near S, far above A_0), arbitrary (A_cross indistinguishable from A_0), or a measured canonical core with an arbitrary remainder (A_cross strictly between, with the core localized by layer and by token frequency). Secondary pre-registered endpoints: per-layer depth profile of agreement, and agreement of the DOMAIN-level routing signature (the object OLMoE's and DeepSeekMoE's specialization analyses actually report) as distinct from the token-level partition.

Significance — consequence:
If canonical (A_cross near S, far above A_0): the early-frozen partition is a property of the recipe and data, not of the run, which licenses three things currently done on faith — reading individual experts as objects with transferable meaning (expert-level interpretability), transferring expert-targeted surgery recipes (pruning, merging, upcycling) between runs of the same recipe, and treating the 1%-checkpoint routing state as a legitimate early predictor of a run. It also makes the aligned-MoE ensemble/merge that Prior 1's algorithm enables functionally meaningful rather than merely loss-connected, and gives expert-offloading work a run-independent target to cache against.
If arbitrary (A_cross at A_0): every published per-expert specialization analysis — OLMoE's and DeepSeekMoE's domain/vocabulary tables, and Prior 3's standing-committee finding — becomes a statement about one run that does not transfer, and the correct reporting unit for specialization becomes the distribution over runs, not the single-run table. Expert-level interpretability acquires a mandatory per-run caveat, and claims of the form "expert 17 handles arithmetic" lose their status as findings about the architecture. Note the limit, argued rather than asserted: engineering that operates on a SHIPPED model (pruning, offloading, caching) is not broken by run-to-run arbitrariness, because it is re-derived per model anyway; what breaks is scientific generalization and any method that assumes an expert's role is predictable from the recipe.
If a measured core with an arbitrary remainder (the likely outcome, given Prior 3's concentration numbers): the size and location of the transferable core is the number pruning, merging and interpretability methods actually need and nobody has. A layer-resolved core/remainder profile would say at which depths expert identity is a real object.
In all three cases the prediction limb returns something currently unmeasured: how much of between-seed terminal-quality variance is readable at 1% of pretraining — a kill-criterion for failed runs if the share is high, and a direct refutation of early-routing determinism if it is near zero.

Significance — audience:
(1) MoE pretraining teams that publish specialization analyses and intermediate checkpoints and would have to change what they claim from them — OLMo/OLMoE, FLAME-MoE (whose abstract makes the early-stabilization claim), DeepSeek and Qwen MoE groups. (2) The MoE compression/merging line: expert pruning, MoE upcycling, expert merging, and specifically the permutation-symmetry/LMC group behind arXiv:2509.11348, which now has an alignment algorithm for independently trained MoEs and no functional validation of what its matching aligns — the candidate's activation-based agreement number is exactly that validation. (3) Expert-offloading and serving researchers (e.g. the ICLR 2026 "local routing consistency" line), for whom whether routing structure is run-specific decides whether caching policies can be recipe-level. (4) Interpretability groups that treat an expert as a unit of analysis, including the authors of Prior 3, whose "illusion of specialization" claim is currently single-run-per-model. (5) Statisticians who own clustering stability and label-switching identifiability, for whom a 64-expert router trained on 20B tokens is an unusually large and consequential instance of their own problem.
Held below 3/3 because the engineering audiences (2)-(3) act per-model and would mostly not change procedure on either answer, and because one branch's news is partly pre-empted by Prior 3.

Significance — fecundity:
Does canonicity scale with expert granularity (DeepSeekMoE-style fine-grained experts) or with expert count, and does it have a knee? Is there a canonical core that identifies which experts are "real" and can be used as a pruning criterion computable at 1% of training? Does the aux-loss weight trade load balance against canonicity, and is there a setting that buys both? Is the early-frozen partition a lottery in the Frankle-Carbin sense — can it be rewound and transplanted, i.e. does initializing a fresh run with run A's 1%-checkpoint router reproduce run A's partition and quality? Does the canonical core align with the standing committee of Prior 3, or is it the complement?

Significance — anchoring:
OLMoE's own reported-but-uninterrogated saturation (§5.1) and FLAME-MoE's independent "stabilizes early"; the dispute over whether expert specialization is semantic at all (Prior 3 vs the specialization tables it criticizes); permutation symmetry and linear mode connectivity in MoE (Prior 1) and the model-merging programme generally; routing consistency as a serving problem; the dense-model early-phase/lottery-ticket and instability literature, which has an analogous "when is the run decided?" question and no MoE counterpart; and, in statistics, clustering stability and label-switching identifiability, where "is this partition reproducible under resampling?" is the canonical form of the question.

Reason for silence:
Four mechanisms, none of them "no one thought of it".
(a) Instrument and cost: until an open, documented multi-run MoE pretraining platform existed (FLAME-MoE, 2025), k >= 3 from-scratch MoE runs at a scale where the saturation curve reproduces were affordable only to labs that have no reason to spend compute re-running one recipe at a new seed. The one measurement that would have been cheap — comparing two existing runs — was unavailable because nobody releases two seeds of the same MoE recipe. (Filename-level evidence that this is now changing: seeded 1.7B FLAME-MoE launch scripts in a third-party public repo.)
(b) The symmetry makes the question look ill-posed rather than open.
