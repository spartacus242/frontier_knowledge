Domain(s): Biology — invertebrate molecular biology / RNA quality metrology; biodiversity genomics sample QC (EBP, Darwin Tree of Life). Method-owning neighbours: measurement metrology (calibration transfer, commutability), screening-bias epidemiology (verification bias by subgroup).

Question (canonical, Gate 0 as intake):
Given the hidden break that splits 28S rRNA in most protostomes, has the use of RIN-type integrity thresholds in sequencing QC produced a measurable, break-correlated deficit — in sample rejection and re-extraction rates, and in which animal taxa have transcriptome data at all — and what RIN value in a break-bearing taxon corresponds to the same true degradation state as a given RIN in a break-free taxon?

Question (canonical, Gate 1 REFORMULATION — the version to carry forward; one reformulation used, for well-posedness only):
In a defined frame of RNA submissions carrying a recorded integrity score and a documented accept/reject or re-extract decision (one or more sequencing-facility or consortium QC logs, e.g. Sanger/Darwin Tree of Life sample-tracking records, supplemented by public BioSample/ENA records where RIN or RQN was deposited), with each source taxon assigned a 28S hidden-break status from sequence prediction or electrophoretic profile:
(a) PRIMARY, association limb — what is the difference between break-bearing and break-free taxa in (i) mean recorded integrity score and (ii) the per-sample rejection and re-extraction rate, adjusted for preservation route, tissue, extraction protocol, input mass and body size, with denominators stated?
(b) PRIMARY, calibration limb — in matched RNA from one break-bearing and one break-free species subjected to the same graded degradation series, what is the mapping RIN_break(d) → RIN_free(d) at equal true degradation d measured independently (DV200, spike-in-calibrated fragment-length distribution, or library 3' bias), and therefore what numeric threshold correction does a RIN ≥ 7-type gate require in a break-bearing taxon?
(c) SECONDARY, exploratory — after conditioning on taxonomic research effort, body size, collection difficulty and preservation route, is break status associated with whether a taxon has any transcriptome at all?

Alternative formulations:
- Has a vertebrate-trained RNA-quality metric been quietly selecting which invertebrates get sequenced?
- What is the mapping between RIN and actual RNA degradation in taxa whose 28S migrates as a single peak, and what has the absence of that mapping cost in rejected samples and missing transcriptomes?

ROUTING NOTE (carried from intake and confirmed): "build a break-aware integrity metric" is an ASKED-BUT-UNANSWERED open problem — Natsidis et al. pose it in print, Casanova Gomez et al. echo it as a recommendation — and belongs in the open-problems handoff. Limb (b) of the reformulation is the natural first step toward that asked problem and therefore carries LESS novelty than limb (a); the un-posed question is the realized-consequence limb (a), with (c) as its riskiest extension.

Presuppositions:
1. Break status is assignable per taxon from sequence or electrophoretic profile data. ESTABLISHED in silico by Natsidis et al. 2019; CONTESTED within Malacostraca/Pancrustacea, where Casanova Gomez et al. 2026 find graded profile classes rather than a clean dichotomy — forcing either a three-level or a continuous exposure variable.
2. QC outcomes are recoverable: facility or consortium records of integrity values, rejections and re-extractions can be obtained, or public submission metadata records RIN/RQN. CONTESTED — the principal feasibility risk; query 9 found no precedent for repository-scale mining of deposited RIN values, so the question may be forced onto a single consortium's logs under a data agreement.
3. RIN or RQN thresholds were in fact applied as accept/reject gates to invertebrate samples rather than as advisory numbers. CONTESTED and variable by facility and project. Note the counter-evidence inside the literature: Deleo et al. describe manual compensation ("higher anomaly thresholds upon detection of the additional peak(s) or signal"), so some facilities may already correct informally — which is itself one of the answers the question admits.
4. A confounder-adjusted comparison is possible: body size, preservation route, collection difficulty and taxonomic research effort correlate with both break status and data availability. CONTESTED, and the hardest part of the design; limb (c) is uninterpretable without it, which is why (c) is demoted to secondary.
5. A reference measure of true degradation independent of the 28S peak exists (spike-in-calibrated fragment-length distribution, DV200, or 3'-bias in the resulting library). ESTABLISHED as routine practice within one matrix (query 10: RIN-vs-RQI and alternative-metric evaluations are a standard design); never run across break status.
6. (Added at Gate 1) The exposure is the ribosomal architecture, not the handling: heat/denaturation artefacts (McCarthy 2015, Winnebeck 2010, Deleo 2018) can mimic a break-type profile, so the design must hold denaturing treatment fixed or the association limb measures protocol, not biology.

What would count as an answer:
(i) From facility or consortium QC logs, or from submission metadata where RIN/RQN is recorded: the integrity-score distribution and the rejection/re-extraction rate for break-bearing versus break-free taxa, with denominators stated and preservation/tissue/protocol held fixed or adjusted; plus, as a secondary limb, the effort-adjusted association between break status and the existence of any transcriptome for a taxon.
(ii) A calibration experiment: matched RNA from break-bearing and break-free species degraded in a graded series, with RIN regressed on an independent degradation measure, yielding the equivalence mapping and therefore the numeric threshold correction.
Either a measured deficit, or a credible null in which break status is unrelated to rejection once effort, preservation and denaturing protocol are accounted for, answers the question. A finding that facilities already compensate manually and that the deficit is therefore small is a real answer, not a failure.

Significance — consequence:
If the association limb is positive at the size the mechanism implies (a RIN ≥ 7-type gate applied to taxa whose large-subunit signal is split in two), then a quantifiable share of invertebrate RNA submissions were refused for a property of their ribosomes rather than the condition of their RNA — and the cost is not abstract: these are often field-collected, permit-bound, hard-to-recollect animals, so a rejection converts into another expedition or a dropped taxon. The deliverable from limb (b) is immediately actionable and small: a number (e.g. "RIN 5.x in a break-bearing taxon ≈ RIN 7.0 in a break-free one at matched true degradation") that a facility can write into an SOP without new software. If the answer is null — break status unrelated to rejection once preservation, tissue, protocol and effort are accounted for — that too changes something: it would show the informal compensation Deleo et al. describe is effectively universal, retire a worry that four papers have now put into print untested, and redirect the effort toward the metric-development problem alone.
Significance — audience:
(1) Sequencing-facility and consortium sample-QC teams (Sanger/Darwin Tree of Life, EBP-affiliated nodes, university core facilities that publish invertebrate-specific Bioanalyzer advisories — query 2 found one such advisory already in circulation), who set the gate and pay for re-extraction. (2) Instrument vendors' algorithm groups: the precedent is concrete, not hypothetical — taxon-/matrix-specific RIN variants already ship (a plant-RNA RIN document surfaced in query 10), so a break-aware variant is a change of a kind the vendor has made before. (3) Invertebrate transcriptomics and comparative-genomics labs who currently discard or re-collect samples on a number they cannot interpret. (4) Funders and sample-logistics planners in biodiversity genomics, for whom a re-extraction rate differential is a budget line.
Significance — fecundity:
Once a break-conditioned degradation measure exists: does library 3' bias at matched true degradation differ by break status — i.e. does the depressed RIN also MIS-PREDICT downstream data quality, or merely mislabel it? Do published invertebrate expression estimates carry a break-correlated degradation covariate that silently confounds cross-phylum expression comparison? Does the same gatekeeping argument transfer to DNA-side gates (fragment-size and purity thresholds) for taxa with unusual genome architecture? And the general form the case makes askable: how many other QC instruments in molecular biology are model-organism-trained, and which non-model lineages do they systematically under-score?
Significance — anchoring:
Directly on P1's own stated desideratum and on Casanova Gomez et al.'s "rRNA-aware quality control" recommendation. On the contested taxonomy of break status within Pancrustacea (the 2026 profile-diversity result), which the exposure definition must absorb. On the known taxonomic unevenness of genomic data, where break status has never been a candidate explanatory variable. On the metrology problem of commutability and calibration transfer, and on the screening-epidemiology problem of a threshold calibrated in one population applied to another — both mature, both unapplied here.

Reason for silence:
A disciplinary boundary compounded by a self-erasing denominator. The anomaly is owned by comparative molecular zoology and phylogenetics (the Telford lab, the arthropod rRNA-profile literature), whose members do not hold the outcome data. The outcome data — integrity values, accept/reject decisions, re-extraction requests — live in core-facility LIMS and consortium sample-tracking systems as operational records, not research data; they are not published, not indexed, and are the sort of thing a facility has no incentive to audit against its own historical gate. Query 3 confirms no such statistics exist in public, and query 9 confirms nobody has even mined the RIN values that ARE deposited. Critically, a rejected sample leaves no record in INSDC, so the loss is invisible to anyone reasoning from public archives: the mechanism destroys its own evidence, which is exactly the condition under which a consequence gets asserted repeatedly and measured never. Finally, the methods that would frame the question (calibration transfer / commutability; verification bias by subgroup) belong to chemometrics, clinical metrology and screening epidemiology, which have no reason to read a paper about 28S rRNA in spiders.
