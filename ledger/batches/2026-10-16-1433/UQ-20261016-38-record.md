Question (canonical, as intaken):
Treating AI-crawler exclusion (robots.txt and equivalent blocks) as survey nonresponse, is the exclusion mechanism ignorable given the domain-level covariates a crawler can observe, and — using pre-exclusion Common Crawl snapshots of now-excluded domains as a directly observed nonrespondent stratum — how large is the induced bias in corpus composition and in the behaviour of a model trained on the post-exclusion corpus?

Question (Gate 1 reformulation, one permitted, for well-posedness only — recorded alongside, not replacing, the above):
Fix the frame as the set of registrable domains contributing tokens to a named snapshot corpus (e.g. FineWeb-Edu or C4) crawled before a reference date T0 = 2023-01-01, and assign each domain to the respondent or nonrespondent stratum by its robots.txt state at a reference date T1 (e.g. 2026-01-01) as dated from Wayback-archived robots.txt. Then: (a) for the pre-specified statistic set S = {WebOrganizer topic mix, register/format mix, factual-reporting score, language, document publication date, document length}, what is the Groves nonresponse bias of each statistic, i.e. (exclusion rate in tokens) x (respondent-minus-nonrespondent difference), computed on the directly observed pre-T0 content of the nonrespondent stratum? (b) Is the exclusion mechanism ignorable with respect to crawl-observable domain covariates — does propensity reweighting on those covariates close each gap, and what residual survives? (c) Does a same-recipe training run on the post-exclusion corpus differ from one on the pre-exclusion corpus on pre-registered evaluations?
This reformulation fixes the frame, the two reference dates, the statistic set and the three estimands, which the intake version left to be constructed; it does not change the object.

Alternative formulations:
1. Can the bias that AI-crawler blocking introduces into a training corpus be estimated at all, or only its rate?
2. Is robots.txt-based exclusion of training data missing-at-random with respect to features a crawler can see, and what does a formal nonresponse-bias analysis give as the size of the resulting shift?

What would count as an answer:
Three deliverables. (i) A Groves-sense nonresponse-bias analysis — bias of a corpus statistic = exclusion rate x (respondent minus nonrespondent difference on that statistic) — computed on the directly observed excluded stratum for the pre-specified statistic set. (ii) An ignorability test: whether propensity reweighting on crawl-observable covariates closes that gap, with the residual reported; a surviving residual is a finding of MNAR exclusion, i.e. that no post-hoc correction can work, and is the most consequential possible answer. (iii) A controlled training comparison at fixed small-to-mid scale, same recipe, pre-exclusion vs post-exclusion corpus, scored on pre-registered evaluations. A negative answer — small respondent-nonrespondent differences on every pre-specified statistic despite a large exclusion rate — would establish that the restriction surge is a licensing problem and not a measurement problem.
Gate 2 finding on this field: (iii) is done; (i) is done in part (topic and format mix, token share) with a "minimal difference" verdict; the stated negative answer is the published result. Only (ii) is unexecuted.

Significance — consequence:
If the exclusion mechanism is ignorable given crawl-observable covariates, corpus builders can repair composition by reweighting, and the restriction surge becomes an access-cost problem rather than a measurement problem. If it is MNAR — the most consequential outcome — no post-hoc correction can work, and every corpus statistic and every claim of "web-representative" pretraining data inherits an uncorrectable and unbounded bias. The force of this is substantially reduced by A's published finding that the composition difference on measured axes is minimal and the trained-model gap near zero on general benchmarks: the ceiling on the bias magnitude for at least one major corpus is already known to be small, so the remaining consequence is mostly about whether that smallness is robust outside education-filtered FineWeb-Edu and whether the residual sits in specific capabilities (A's medical signal and its memorization reduction suggest it does).
Significance — audience:
Corpus builders who would act on a reweighting recipe or on an MNAR verdict (HuggingFace FineWeb team, AI2/Dolma, Common Crawl Foundation, the Apertus-style compliant-training groups); the Data Provenance Initiative, whose own papers defer this step; survey methodologists and official statisticians already extending TSE to found data (TED-On, the AAPOR 2026 course), for whom a crawl-coverage application is an unclaimed object; and the policy audience currently inferring corpus harm from rate data alone. Real and identifiable. But note that A, aimed at the same audience, already supplies the headline answer, so marginal action on this candidate as posed is limited.
Significance — fecundity:
Opens: the same analysis for ToS-only, paywall, login-wall and CDN bot-detection exclusion as separate nonresponse mechanisms with separate propensity models (B's numbers make ToS the dominant one by an order of magnitude); drift of the nonrespondent stratum since its last crawl, i.e. whether pre-exclusion snapshots stay a valid proxy; whether the exclusion-propensity model is itself gameable; and what the MNAR structure implies for the frame problem — whether "the crawlable web" can be an estimand at all.
Significance — anchoring:
Touches the "will we run out of data" scaling-constraint literature, the data-provenance and licensing debate, the known absence of a sampling frame for the web (a standing problem in corpus linguistics since Biber), and the general MNAR-correction impossibility results in missing-data theory.
Reason for silence:
Candidate mechanism, applicable only to the surviving ignorability limb and not to the headline (which was not silent): a disciplinary boundary with an incentive gradient across it. The nonresponse-bias apparatus is owned by survey methodology and official statistics, which have transplanted TSE onto AI training data only where the data are human-generated labels (AAPOR 2026 course: missing ANNOTATORS, raking, propensity weighting, MRP) and onto platform behavioural traces (TED-On), never onto crawl coverage; meanwhile the ML-side groups who hold the corpora have an incentive to frame exclusion as a compliance and licensing question — A's own title is "Can Performant LLMs Be Ethical?" and its estimand is a performance gap — because that is the framing their legal exposure rewards, and a published MNAR verdict would assert an uncorrectable defect in their own artefacts. The enabling data (dated Wayback robots.txt archives alongside pre-surge Common Crawl snapshots) only became jointly available in 2023–2024, which is why even the asked version of the question is barely two years old.
