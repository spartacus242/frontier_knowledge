Question (canonical) — Gate 1 version 2 (tightened; one reformulation used, for well-posedness only):
For a pre-registered frame of benchmark items and an open-data model whose full pretraining corpus is indexed (OLMo/Dolma, Pythia/Pile, LLM360, DCLM), retrieving every corpus occurrence of an item's stem above a fixed fuzzy-match threshold and classifying each occurrence into {states the gold answer, states a non-gold answer, states no answer, gold label itself erroneous}: (a) what are the occurrence-weighted and item-weighted fractions in each class, with CIs and a reported extractor precision/recall on a human-adjudicated sample; and (b) under a pre-specified design that conditions on item difficulty estimated from models with zero occurrences of that item, is accuracy on items whose answer-stating occurrences are non-gold-dominated (>=50% disagree with gold) below accuracy on occurrence-free items matched on that difficulty — i.e. is the contamination correction to a reported score signed, and how many score points does it move when non-gold-dominated items are removed rather than retained?

Question (intake version 1, recorded per Gate 1):
For benchmark items whose text is retrievable in an open pretraining corpus, what fraction of the retrieved occurrences state an answer that disagrees with the benchmark's gold label, and does a model's accuracy on wrong-answer-contaminated items fall below its accuracy on matched uncontaminated items — so that the effect of contamination on a reported score is signed rather than uniformly inflationary?
(What the reformulation fixed: it named the exposure index (open-data models only), fixed the "wrong-answer-contaminated" set by an explicit dominance threshold, and replaced "matched uncontaminated items" with difficulty estimated from zero-occurrence models, which removes part of the model-dependent-matching circularity. Nothing in the substance changed.)

Alternative formulations:
- When a benchmark question leaks into pretraining data, does the leak carry the right answer — and what happens to the score when it does not?
- Is decontamination removing a penalty as well as a bonus: what share of leaked benchmark items teaches the model a non-gold answer, and what does that do to the measured score?

Motivating gap (source method: anomaly mining):
Contamination work now retrieves the actual training documents containing a benchmark string (WIMBD, infini-gram, OLMoTrace over Dolma/Pile/RedPajama) and reports occurrence COUNTS plus score inflation; none found reports what the retrieved occurrence ASSERTS. Decontamination filters delete items by string match regardless of the answer carried, which presumes a one-signed effect. Three facts sit unjoined beside this: (1) benchmark gold labels are themselves wrong at ~6.5% on a 3,000-item MMLU re-annotation (MMLU-Redux / "Are We Done with MMLU?", NAACL 2025 — NOT OPENED by me, see access note); (2) web copies of benchmark items live on quiz sites, homework-help scrapes and forum threads where the stated answer is often wrong or disputed; (3) TruthfulQA's premise is that models reproduce frequently stated falsehoods. Gate 2 then turned up the anomaly in the wild: Search-Time Data Contamination (opened) reports that on GPQA "retrieving a HuggingFace contaminated source does *not* improve the accuracy relative to uncontaminated sources, even lowering it to an extent" — observed, unexplained, and not attributed to the answer carried by the retrieved copy.

Presuppositions:
1. A non-trivial number of benchmark items are retrievable in at least one open corpus — ESTABLISHED by existing contamination work (and, at inference time, 1-4% HuggingFace-copy retrieval rates in arXiv:2508.13180).
2. The retrieved occurrence usually contains, or can be parsed to contain, a stated answer — CONTESTED. Many occurrences are bare question banks with no key. Needs an operationalization plus a reported extractor error rate; if the "no answer stated" class dominates, limb (b) loses power and the answer is a NO by low exposure, which is itself reportable.
3. "Gold label" is well-defined for the items studied — CONTESTED (MMLU-Redux ~6.5%), so "gold itself erroneous" must be a separate annotation class, never folded into disagreement.
4. Matched uncontaminated comparison items can be constructed — CONTESTED; matching on difficulty is model-dependent. Version 2 pins difficulty to zero-occurrence models, which helps but does not fully identify the contrast (see strongest objection).
5. Corpus occurrence proxies model exposure — ESTABLISHED only for open-data models, so the frame must be OLMo / Pythia / LLM360 / DCLM, not closed models.
6. Occurrence frequency, not merely presence, is measurable — ESTABLISHED by infini-gram-class indexes.

What would count as an answer:
A study over open-data models that (i) for a pre-registered item frame retrieves every corpus occurrence of each item stem above a stated matching threshold; (ii) classifies each occurrence's stated answer into {agrees with gold, disagrees with gold, no answer stated, gold itself erroneous} with a human-adjudicated sample plus an automatic extractor at reported precision/recall; (iii) reports those fractions with CIs; (iv) estimates, under a pre-specified matching or regression design, the accuracy contrast between non-gold-dominated items, gold-dominated items and zero-occurrence items.
YES: a measurable share of occurrences disagree AND accuracy on non-gold-dominated items sits delta points BELOW matched clean items, CI excluding zero.
NO: disagreement is rare (<~2%), or the contrast is null within a stated minimum detectable effect.
Either way the practice-relevant number is computable: the change in a reported score when wrong-answer-contaminated items are removed rather than retained.

PRIOR A — Jiang, Kumar, Zhou et al., "Investigating Data Contamination for Pre-training Language Models", arXiv:2401.06059 (2024). https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06059v1 (OPENED)
- Unit of analysis: one GPT-2 pretraining run from scratch per (dataset x contamination condition x repetition factor).
- Endpoint: downstream metric — SST-2 accuracy, MMLU, SQuAD F1, ROUGE on CNN/DailyMail.
- Comparator: the original/uncontaminated GPT-2 trained on the same corpus.
- Estimand: change in the downstream metric caused by injecting evaluation input text ("text contamination") or input+prompt+gold answer ("ground-truth contamination"), and its shape in the repetition factor.
- Verified content: injected copies always carry the gold label; the paper has NO mislabeled or counterfactual arm. It does report sub-baseline drops: "a distinct U-shaped performance trend in response to increasing contamination factors" on SST-2/MMLU/SQuAD, with performance that "initially improved but started to decline when the factor reached around 10 repetitions" and "in some instances dropped below the baseline level". It does not retrieve natural occurrences or classify what any occurrence asserts.

PRIOR B — Schaeffer, Kazdan, Abbasi, Liu, Miranda, Ahmed, Barez, Denisov-Blanch, Puri, Biderman, Mireshghallah, Koyejo, "Test Set Contamination of Generative Evaluations", arXiv:2601.04301v3. https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04301v3 (OPENED)
- Unit of analysis: a separately pretrained 34M-344M-parameter Qwen3-architecture model at a fixed MATH test-set replica count R (0 to 3162); secondarily, problems binned by solution length.
- Endpoint: Math Verify score; test-set cross entropy on gold references.
- Comparator: the R=0 uncontaminated corpus (FineWeb-Edu-Dedup), plus rephrased/perturbed test variants.
- Estimand: performance and loss as a function of contamination dose and compute, including an irreducible error E(R) scaling fit.
- Verified content: contamination is synthetic and "every contaminated copy contains the gold solution"; no wrong-answer arm; no corpus retrieval or occurrence classification; no accuracy below the uncontaminated baseline (at high temperature contaminated models fall only TO uncontaminated performance). Its two "gets worse" results (overtraining raising contaminated models' test cross entropy, SFT raising test loss for high-contamination models) are dose-dilution effects, not answer-content effects.

PRIOR C — Han, Mankikar, Michael, Wang (Scale AI), "Search-Time Data Contamination", arXiv:2508.13180v1, 12 Aug 2025. https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13180v1 (OPENED)
- Unit of analysis: an individual benchmark question (HLE, SimpleQA, GPQA) answered by a search-based agent (Sonar Pro / Reasoning Pro / Deep Research).
- Endpoint: pass@1 accuracy (LLM-judged for HLE/SimpleQA).
- Comparator: uncontaminated samples within the same benchmark and agent; ablations with HuggingFace blocked, no search, only HF, date cutoff.
- Estimand: the accuracy difference attributable to a retrieved HuggingFace copy of the item.
- Verified content: contamination is flagged purely by URL substring match ("huggingface" + benchmark name); the retrieved page's stated answer is never compared with gold and no agree/disagree fraction is reported. Contamination rates 3.36-3.44% (HLE), 0.99-1.20% (SimpleQA), 1.90-4.15% (GPQA); HLE contaminated-vs-uncontaminated accuracy gaps >10-20%; blocking HF costs ~15% on the contaminated subset. Significance — consequence:
If YES (a measurable disagreement share and accuracy below matched clean items): the standing assumption that a contaminated score is an UPPER bound on clean ability is wrong in direction for a subset of items, so the sign of the contamination correction becomes an empirical quantity rather than a convention. Decontamination stops being pure hygiene and becomes an intervention with two opposing components — it removes inflated items and ALSO removes depressed ones — which means a decontaminated score can be lower OR higher than the raw one for reasons unrelated to memorization, and the usual "we report post-decontamination numbers, so our comparison is conservative" sentence loses its warrant. Filters acquire a new design axis: answer-aware deletion, or re-weighting rather than deletion. If NO (disagreement <~2%, or a null contrast within a stated MDE): the one-signed assumption gets its first direct empirical licence, string-match decontamination is vindicated as answer-blind, and the residual non-monotonic contamination effects in prior A are isolated as dose phenomena rather than content phenomena — a real result, because at present nobody has checked.

Significance — audience:
(i) Benchmark and harness maintainers (lm-evaluation-harness, HELM, Open LLM Leaderboard, the decontamination scripts shipped with MMLU-Pro/GPQA-class releases) — they would add an answer-polarity field to contamination reports and could publish a signed correction instead of a one-sided caveat. (ii) Open-data pretraining groups (AI2/OLMo-Dolma, EleutherAI/Pythia, LLM360, DCLM) — they already run the indexes (infini-gram, OLMoTrace, WIMBD) that make the measurement a query rather than a new instrument, and they are the only groups for whom corpus occurrence licenses an exposure claim. (iii) Eval-integrity and benchmark-audit practitioners, including the MMLU-Redux-style label-auditing line and the Scale AI search-time-contamination line, who would gain a mechanism for the GPQA lowering they already report. (iv) Downstream consumers who read eval scores as bounds in procurement and policy documents.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: answer-aware decontamination and its calibration; wrong-answer leakage as a contamination canary distinguishable from right-answer leakage (and whether the two have different detectability, which bears on D); whether the disagreement share differs systematically by benchmark, subject and source genre (quiz site vs forum vs scraped dataset mirror), which would predict which benchmarks are penalized rather than inflated; the interaction between corpus-side disagreement and benchmark-side label error (an item whose gold is wrong and whose web copies are right is the mirror case, and its score effect has the opposite sign); whether imitative-falsehood behaviour (TruthfulQA) and benchmark contamination are the same phenomenon measured at two scales; and whether the frequency-weighted polarity of occurrences, not their count, is the right contamination covariate.

Significance — anchoring:
Touches (1) the unexplained non-monotonicity in the contamination-effect literature — prior A's U-shape with below-baseline drops, prior B's overtraining/SFT reversals; (2) prior C's directly reported and unexplained accuracy lowering on contaminated GPQA items; (3) benchmark label-error rates (MMLU-Redux ~6.5%, cited but not opened here) and the question of how label error and leak polarity compound; (4) the poisoning dose-response literature's established result that a tiny fraction of false-assertion tokens measurably degrades answers (Alber et al., Nature Medicine), which supplies the mechanism but has never been run at benchmark-item granularity; (5) the decontamination-practice question of whether string matching is the right filter primitive.

Reason for silence:
Instrument shape plus a disciplinary split, not oversight. The corpus indexes that made this askable (WIMBD 2023, infini-gram 2024, OLMoTrace 2025) were built to answer "is it there, and how often" — their return type is a count and a span, so the natural next question in their own vocabulary is "how much inflation per occurrence", and the CONTENT of the occurrence never became a variable; reading what the document asserts requires an answer extractor and human adjudication, which is annotation work in a line that prides itself on being annotation-free. Second, the community that routinely measures "what a training document asserts and what that does downstream" is the poisoning / misinformation community, which works at corpus level with injected documents and publishes in safety venues, while contamination is owned by evaluation-methodology people whose unit is the benchmark score; neither side's standard object is "the polarity of the naturally occurring copies of one item". Third, decontamination is shipped as pipeline hygiene — a string matcher with no answer slot — so the practice itself has nowhere to put the finding, and no one maintains the matcher who would be the natural asker. Fourth, an incentive asymmetry: the hypothesis predicts that some reported scores are too LOW and that decontamination can remove a penalty, which no model developer, benchmark author or leaderboard operator is motivated to go looking for, since every existing caveat is written in the inflation direction.

A near-zero answer would still be informative, but it would not change anyone's practice, so this is not a 3.

Strongest remaining objection (for the Gate 4 adversary to press):
Identification of limb (b). Whether an item's web copies carry a wrong answer is not random: items that reach quiz sites and homework-help scrapes with bad keys are plausibly the obscure, badly worded, ambiguous or genuinely contested ones — the same properties that make an item hard for a model and make its gold label more likely to be wrong. So "accuracy on non-gold-dominated items is lower" is predicted by item quality alone, with no contamination mechanism at all. Estimating difficulty from zero-occurrence models mitigates this only if occurrence count is itself unconfounded with difficulty, which it is not. Without an injection arm (train a matched open model on the corpus with the non-gold occurrences removed, or added at controlled dose) limb (b) may be unidentifiable from observational corpus data, which would reduce the candidate to its descriptive limb (a) — still unasked, but a smaller question. Secondary objections the adversary should test: whether presupposition 2 collapses (if most occurrences are keyless question banks, the disagreement denominator may be too thin for CIs); and whether public-discourse neighbour D plus prior A's wrong-key gap together amount to the question already being on the table, which would make this Low.
