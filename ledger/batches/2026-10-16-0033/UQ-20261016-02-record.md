Source method: presupposition inversion (inverted claim: "compendium severity grades encode risk of harm")

Question (canonical — V0, as submitted at intake): For each drug-interaction compendium whose severity grades drive EHR alerting, what is the rate of attributable patient harm among co-exposed patients within each severity class — does the grading discriminate harm at all, and would re-grading the same interaction pairs by observed outcomes change which alerts fire?

Question (canonical — R1, the single Gate 1 reformulation; this is the version carried forward): Treating each of K commercial interaction compendia (e.g. Lexicomp, Micromedex, First Databank) as a classifier whose severity label is the configuration surface for EHR alert firing, on a pre-specified sample of interaction pairs stratified by each compendium's severity class and deliberately over-sampling pairs the compendia grade discordantly, with every pair estimated in two or more linked databases under one pre-registered design family (active-comparator new-user cohort, plus self-controlled case series where its assumptions hold), the estimand being harm attributable to the combination rather than to either drug alone (combination vs. single-exposure contrast), the primary outcome a commensurable composite pre-specified across all pairs (ADR-coded or all-cause hospitalisation, or death, within 90 days of co-exposure initiation) with pair-specific clinical outcomes as secondary, and pairs failing pre-specified identifiability diagnostics (negative-control calibration, covariate balance, minimum co-exposure counts) reported as a named "unidentifiable" stratum rather than dropped:
  (a) what is the distribution of pair-level attributable risk within each compendium's severity class, and what share of total between-pair variance in measured harm is explained by class membership;
  (b) does severity class rank pairs by measured harm better than chance — a pre-specified rank-discrimination statistic with a pre-specified minimum — and does it do so equally across the K compendia;
  (c) in one or more health systems' alert logs, holding sensitivity for total measured harm fixed, what alert volume fires under compendium-derived versus outcome-derived classes, presented as a reclassification table?
Reason for reformulation (well-posedness only, no scope change): V0 left three things undefined that decide whether any evidence could settle it — the estimand (V0's "attributable harm" does not say attributable to the combination versus to either drug, which is exactly where the one existing partial answer turns), the commensuration rule (ranking pairs by "harm" requires one harm scale across heterogeneous clinical outcomes, otherwise the discrimination statistic in (b) is undefined), and the disposition of pairs that cannot be identified. R1 fixes all three and adds the pre-specified minimum for the discrimination statistic so that a null is declarable rather than arguable.

Alternative formulations:
1. Do the "major" and "contraindicated" labels in Lexicomp, Micromedex and First Databank predict excess adverse outcomes in linked claims or EHR data, and do the three gradings rank the same pairs in the same order of measured harm?
2. What is the positive predictive value for harm of the severity class that triggers an interruptive drug-interaction alert, and how much alert volume could be removed at fixed sensitivity for harm if the class were derived from outcomes instead of from compendium opinion?
3. (Lay) Are the drug-combination warnings that stop a prescriber mid-order sorted by how often patients are actually hurt, and would sorting them by measured harm change which ones appear?

Motivating gap: The inverted presupposition is that compendium severity grades encode risk of harm — the reason EHRs fire interruptive alerts on the top classes and P&T committees configure firing rules from them. The anchoring anomaly is that the compendia disagree with each other on severity for the same pair; disagreement among graders means at most one ordering can be right, yet nobody has audited any of the orderings against measured outcomes. Gate 2 confirmed the anomaly from a source I opened (Vitry, BJCP 2006/2007: 33% of Micromedex "major" interactions are "moderate" in Drug Interaction Facts and 29% the reverse; Spearman 0.546 for severity grading; the author names "lack of good epidemiological evidence" as the likely cause). Gate 2 also confirmed, from the AHRQ final report I opened, that the largest funded programme on making DDI alerts meaningful (Malone, R01HS25984, 2023) compared compendia to each other (Fleiss kappa median 0.11, "slight agreement"), estimated harm for a handful of individual pairs, and re-graded severities at a deployed site by pharmacist judgement — explicitly not against outcome data. Gate 2 materially corrected the intake in two places: PMC2000587 is Vitry 2006, not the Abarca 2004 concordance study the intake attributed to it; and the intake's claim "no outcome-validation of severity classes found" is wrong for one slice — see nearest prior P1.

Presuppositions (each marked established / contested / testable):
1. Compendium severity grades determine which DDI alerts fire — ESTABLISHED. Vendor severity levels are the configuration surface; the AHRQ report documents a site re-tiering 103,486 DDIs by severity level and cutting clinician alert rates ~17.1 to ~1.8 per 100 orders.
2. Compendia disagree materially on severity for the same pair — ESTABLISHED (Vitry 2006, opened; Malone 2023 kappa 0.11, opened).
3. Harm attributable to a co-exposure is estimable at scale from observational data with acceptable confounding control — CONTESTED. Confounding by indication and frailty is severe; some pairs will be unidentifiable. R1 handles this by reporting them as a named stratum, not by assuming it away.
4. A class-level harm rate is meaningful, i.e. classes are not so heterogeneous that the class summary carries no information — CONTESTED, and answered directly by limb (a)'s variance decomposition rather than assumed.
5. "Harm" is commensurable across interaction pairs well enough to rank them — CONTESTED, and the residual weakness of R1 even after the composite outcome is fixed: a pair whose harm is a non-hospitalised laboratory abnormality is systematically under-weighted by a hospitalisation/death composite, so (b) tests discrimination for serious harm specifically, not for all harm. Stated, not hidden.
6. Reducing alert volume is a goal because alert fatigue causes harm — FLAGGED. Override rates above 90% are documented; the harm link is inferential. Note that limb (c) does not depend on this: it is stated at fixed sensitivity for harm, so it reports a volume/capture trade-off whatever the reader believes about fatigue.
7. Enough pairs carry sufficient co-exposure counts to estimate class-level rates — TESTABLE from the data, currently UNVERIFIED. For discordant pairs specifically (the deliberately over-sampled stratum) this is the most likely failure mode, since pairs the compendia argue about may be rare precisely because one compendium's warning suppressed the co-prescription.
8. The combination-versus-single-exposure contrast is the right estimand — ESTABLISHED as necessary by P1, which found 38 of 51 flagged pairs raised against non-exposure but little difference against single exposure. Any version of this question that compares co-exposed patients to unexposed patients answers a different and misleading question.

What would count as an answer: Per-compendium class-level harm distributions with the variance decomposition from (a); a rank-discrimination statistic per compendium against its pre-specified minimum from (b), with the unidentifiable pairs reported as a named stratum and its size stated; and the reclassification table from (c) giving alert volume and harm capture under outcome-derived versus compendium classes at matched sensitivity. Three pre-committable verdicts: (i) classes discriminate serious harm and the orderings agree — the presupposition holds and the grading is vindicated; (ii) classes discriminate but the K compendia order pairs differently — at most one grading is usable and the table says which; (iii) classes do not discriminate measured serious harm within the identifiable stratum — the configuration surface carries no harm information, which settles the question and is directly actionable. Minimum disconfirmation of the whole exercise: the unidentifiable stratum swallows most sampled pairs, in which case the honest output is that the audit is not feasible at pair level with current data, reported as such.

Significance — consequence: If YES (classes discriminate serious harm and the K orderings agree): the presupposition holds, the configuration surface is vindicated, and the field's attention moves cleanly to contextualisation (which patients, which doses) with the severity axis settled — a positive answer with real content, since nobody currently knows this. If YES-but-divergent (classes discriminate but the compendia order pairs differently): at most one grading is usable, and the reclassification table names which — immediately decision-relevant for every health system choosing a knowledge-base contract, and newly awkward for vendors selling severity as a product feature. If NO (no discrimination within the identifiable stratum): the top-class label that fires interruptive alerts carries no information about serious harm, which means the current alert burden is not a sensitivity/specificity trade-off at all but a cost with unmeasured benefit; P&T committees would then have an evidence basis for configuring from an outcome-derived list, and the ~89%-reduction results already achieved by expert judgement (P3) would need re-reading as plausibly harmless rather than merely bold. A null also retrospectively converts P1 from an antibiotic curiosity into the first cell of a general finding.
Significance — audience: (i) P&T and CDS governance committees, who today pick a firing threshold on a vendor severity scale with nothing to calibrate it against, and who would gain a defensible configuration rule plus, importantly, medicolegal cover for turning alerts off — currently the binding constraint, since there is no authority to point to. (ii) Knowledge-base vendors (Wolters Kluwer/Lexicomp, Merative/Micromedex, FDB), who publish severity policies (Merative's MDX Drug Interactions Policy surfaced at query 11) and would have to state whether their scale is an evidential-documentation scale or a harm-rate scale — these are currently conflated in the Hansten/Horn axes. (iii) Pharmacoepidemiology networks (Sentinel, OHDSI/EHDEN, CPRD), for whom this is a new and well-defined use of large-scale estimation machinery they already run — auditing a deployed classifier rather than hunting signals. (iv) Regulators and policy bodies setting alert-burden and CDS expectations, who currently have no measurement of what alert volume buys. HONEST DISCOUNT, and it is a real one: P1 already published a null-leaning result for 51 BNF-flagged pairs in 2022, and there is no evidence in anything I opened that BNF warnings or any alerting configuration changed as a result. That is direct evidence that this audience has so far not acted on exactly this kind of finding, which is why significance is scored 2 and not 3.
Significance — fecundity: Whether the two axes vendors publish (severity and documentation/evidence quality) should be separated, with only one wired to firing. Whether discordant pairs are discordant because they are genuinely heterogeneous in effect or because the graders read different evidence — answerable once pair-level harm exists. Whether harm concentrates in identifiable patient strata rather than in pairs, which would reframe the whole surface from pair-level to patient-level and give contextualisation work (P3's algorithms) an outcome target it currently lacks. What the suppression bias is worth: pairs with low co-exposure counts may be rare because a warning worked, which turns presupposition 7's failure mode into its own estimable question. Whether the same audit method transports to the other opinion-graded classifiers wired into EHRs — potentially-inappropriate-medication lists, allergy cross-reactivity tables, duplicate-therapy rules.
Significance — anchoring: the compendia-discordance anomaly (P2, Abarca 2004); the alert-fatigue/override literature (>90% override rates, and the burden-and-tiering work at query 9) whose central quantity — harm per alert — has never been measured; the expert-consensus high-priority DDI list (Phansalkar 2013) as the field's substitute for measurement; large-scale population-level estimation with negative-control calibration (OHDSI SCCS tooling, ALCAPONE) as the instrument that makes a many-pair audit affordable; and the open methodological problem of confounding by indication in co-exposure studies, which limb (a)'s unidentifiable stratum reports rather than solves.

Reason for silence: Four mechanisms, none of them "no one thought of it", and the strongest is the second.
(1) Disciplinary boundary with no owner. Severity grades are produced by commercial drug-information publishers in a monograph-editorial tradition descended from Hansten/Horn, where the axes are clinical severity *if it occurs* plus documentation quality — neither is a population rate. Attributable-risk estimation lives in pharmacoepidemiology, which is organised around one exposure (or one pair) at a time for a regulatory question. The grading-as-a-classifier is nobody's unit of analysis: informatics treats it as configuration input, epidemiology treats it as pair selection, and drug-information science treats it as editorial output.
(2) Incentive and liability gap. The graders are vendors selling the grading; a published null devalues the product, and health systems hold the alert logs under content licences. More binding: P&T committees need *a* defensible label for medicolegal protection, so an audit showing the label uninformative creates exposure with no ready replacement — the rational institutional move is not to measure. Note the asymmetry this creates: a positive result benefits the vendors, a null benefits nobody who holds the data.
(3) Instrument recency. A class-level audit needs hundreds of pairs each powered for a rare outcome in multiple databases with calibrated confounding control. That became affordable only with federated common-data-model networks and automated large-scale estimation with negative controls, roughly the last 5-8 years. Before that the only economically feasible unit was the single pair — which is exactly what the literature consists of.
(4) Problem reframing absorbed the energy. The field named its problem "alert fatigue" — a volume problem, whose natural solution is consensus triage — rather than "classifier validation", a measurement problem. Once the high-priority-list framing existed (2012-2013) and then prevailed, the validation study had no venue and no funding line: P3 is the demonstration, having had the data and chosen concordance and pharmacist re-grading instead.
