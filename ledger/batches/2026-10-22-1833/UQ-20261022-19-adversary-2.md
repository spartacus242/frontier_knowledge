## Restatement (my own words, before engaging the framing)

Take the published picture that reasoning models get more accurate the more reasoning tokens they emit. Two
questions. (1) Is that curve a *treatment* effect — give the same item more tokens and it is likelier to be
solved — or largely a *selection* effect, because harder items happen to elicit longer traces and also fail
more? Measure it by randomising a token budget over the same item set and contrasting the within-item slope
against the pooled slope on naturally emitted length, reporting the share of the pooled slope that is
selection, per difficulty band and per truncation convention. (2) Is a model *stopped* at N reasoning tokens
the same animal as a model that *chose* to stop at N — tested as a distribution-equivalence test on matched
items at a pre-declared margin?

In one line: is the test-time-scaling curve confounded by item difficulty, and is a forced cap behaviourally
equivalent to a natural stop.
