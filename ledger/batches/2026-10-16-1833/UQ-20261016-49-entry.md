Question (canonical): For matched pretraining runs differing only in numeric format (BF16 control vs MXFP8 vs MXFP4/NVFP4; identical data, order, seed, token count), what is the slope beta of the per-stratum held-out loss gap to the BF16 control against log training-corpus frequency, and does that slope (i) differ from zero against a seed-variance baseline and (ii) track the per-block flush-to-zero rate of gradient entries resolved by the same strata? (Gate 1 reformulation, one allowed; Version A — "is the low-precision loss penalty uniform across the data distribution or does it grow with rarity?" — recorded in batches/2026-10-16-1833/UQ-20261016-49-record.md.)

Alternative formulations:
1. Does 4-bit block-scaled training cost the same everywhere in the corpus, or does it quietly delete the long tail?
2. Is the published aggregate penalty of low-precision training an average hiding a frequency-dependent gradient?
3. (Neighbour's words) Is aggregate validation loss a sufficient statistic for accepting a training numeric format, or does the format have a disparate impact on under-represented strata?
(Gate 4 note: formulations 1-2 still carry the "deletes the tail" thesis that the opened evidence puts in doubt; they should be neutralised before any promotion.)

Motivating gap: Block-scaled sub-8-bit pretraining reached production hardware in 2025-26, so the question can for the first time be put to the training process rather than to post-hoc compression, and the decision it bears on (which format for a nine-figure run) is being made on aggregate loss alone. NVIDIA's 10T-token NVFP4 report (arXiv 2509.25149) contains one visible non-uniformity — Code 59.52→56.67, HumanEval+ 59.93→57.43, MBPP+ 59.11→55.91, Winogrande 80.58→78.77 — disposed of with "We suspect the difference could be due to noisy evaluations": an anomaly reported and not asked about. Adversary #3 verified these numbers in the PDF and added a correction the record omitted: on the paper's only data-side strata NVFP4 beats FP8 (Multilingual 77.93→80.24, Global MMLU 74.00→74.94, MGSM 81.87→85.53), which weakens the anchoring anomaly and leaves the sign genuinely open.

NEW, raised at Gate 4, unaddressed — that per-stratum FTZ is well defined under the named recipe. 2509.25149 applies stochastic rounding to gradients in both Dgrad and Wgrad and a 16x16 Random Hadamard Transform to Wgrad inputs, and keeps embeddings, the output head, norms and 16% of linear layers in BF16/FP32. Cross-token co-location exists only in the Wgrad path, which is exactly where the RHT mixes 16 tokens per quantized entry.

What would count as an answer: matched runs at >=2 scales in BF16/MXFP8/MXFP4; pre-registered stratification (primary: token log-frequency bins; secondary: entity-frequency deciles, language tier); primary readout = per-stratum loss gap fitted against log frequency, beta with CI; decision rule fixed in advance ("tail-differential" = beta != 0 against a >=3-seed BF16 null; "uniform" = beta ~ 0 with CI excluding a pre-stated practically relevant magnitude); per-bin standard errors reported and the fit weighted; mechanism check = per-stratum FTZ tracking beta; intervention check = block size / stochastic rounding / block-layout permutation must move beta if the block-scale story is right. A flat, tightly bounded slope is a publishable answer. Gate 4 adds two requirements the record does not meet: the normalization of the gap (absolute nats vs ratio to the aggregate gap) must be pre-registered, and a capacity-matched BF16 control (or capacity-normalized beta) is needed so a positive slope is not pre-explained by reduced effective parameter count.

Nothing in print asks or answers this question. Its three ingredients exist in three separate literatures and no source joins them. (1) Matched-control block-scaled sub-8-bit pretraining exists and is reported only in aggregate: “Pretraining Large Language Models with NVFP4” (arXiv:2509.25149, 12B hybrid Mamba-Transformer, 10T tokens) compares against a higher-precision FP8 run of the same recipe with the unit of analysis the benchmark CATEGORY — General, MMLU-family, Math, Multilingual (Global MMLU and MGSM aggregates), Code, Commonsense — never decomposed by rarity: no token-frequency bucketing, no per-language split inside the multilingual aggregate, no long-tail-knowledge split. The only deficit it discusses is coding (“The exception is the coding task, where NVFP4 falls slightly behind”), attributed to possibly noisy evaluation. The MXFP8 recipes paper (arXiv:2506.08027) was never opened, so a rarity ablation inside it is unlikely but unchecked. (2) Rarity-stratified loss measurement exists, aimed at a different knob: “The Fair Language Model Paradox” (arXiv:2410.11985) bins per-token loss by corpus frequency in powers of 3 and shows aggregate loss staying flat while low-frequency-token loss degrades — exactly this question's uniform-versus-graded contrast — but the intervention is weight decay, and precision appears only as a setup detail. (3) Non-uniform degradation from numeric compression exists post-training: “How Does Quantization Affect Multilingual LLMs?” (arXiv:2407.03211, unit = language and script class, Japanese -1.7% automatic against -16.0% human), “The Uneven Impact of Post-Training Quantization in Machine Translation” (arXiv:2508.20893), and the compression-fairness lineage (arXiv:1911.05248, arXiv:2010.03058); with DP-SGD's disparate-subgroup result (arXiv:1905.12101) alongside. Every one of those treats an already-trained network or private training, so none can speak to a penalty accumulating during pretraining, which is where the shared-exponent mechanism would act.
So the record's “general question already asked and answered in three other vocabularies” is correct as stated and does not reach the instance: none of the three concerns block-scaled low-precision pretraining with a shared per-block exponent, and the one pretraining paper that does reports aggregates only. Mechanism-side searches returned nothing on point — block-floating-point and shared-exponent vocabulary gave hardware documentation with no data-distribution endpoint; heavy-tailed rate-distortion results (arXiv:2411.08549, arXiv:2212.14562) are about sources, not a model's loss on rare data; “Stochastic Rounding for LLM Training” (arXiv:2502.20566) has no subpopulation endpoint; the closest mechanistic framing (arXiv:2608.08188) is weight-only PTQ at 2-4 bits with no rarity axis. One informal signal that the framing is circulating unstudied: a May 2026 blog post on an int4 quality cliff on the long tail — not print, not block-scaled, no controlled comparison.
