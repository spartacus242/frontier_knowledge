## Restatement in my own words

Grow ice crystals from the vapour in a single chamber, once with ordinary water vapour and once with heavy water (D2O) vapour, at matched excess vapour density relative to each isotopologue's own ice saturation. Locate the three temperatures at which the crystal habit flips between plate-like and column-like (conventionally near −4 °C, −10 °C and −22 °C in H2O) in both isotopologues, to about half a kelvin. The headline number is the set of three temperature shifts, in kelvin. Then ask which of two mechanisms the shifts fit: if the transitions are set by a surface-premelting (quasi-liquid-layer) threshold, they should ride with the heavy isotopologue's own thermodynamic reference and move by of order three to four kelvin; if they are set by step-generation kinetics with their own activation energies, they should barely move. A third outcome — the three transitions moving by different amounts — would mean they do not share one mechanism.

Put plainly: **does the Nakaya habit diagram translate along the temperature axis when you make the water heavy, and by how much at each boundary?**
