Question (canonical) — V1 (as intaken): In shipped low-precision training configurations (BF16 parameters with BF16 or block-quantized 8-bit optimizer state, versus FP32 master weights), what fraction of (parameter, step) update attempts leaves the parameter bitwise unchanged or materially distorted by the arithmetic of the update path, and is that fraction concentrated on identifiable parameter groups — rare-token embedding rows, normalization gains, small-magnitude weights — so that those parameters are effectively frozen for a measurable share of the run and for a rising share as the learning rate decays?

Question (canonical) — V2 (the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation, for well-posedness only): Over a declared frame of configurations C = {BF16 parameters + BF16 Adam state; BF16 parameters + bitsandbytes-style block-quantized 8-bit Adam state; FP32 master weights + BF16 compute (reference arm)}, with ANNIHILATION of a (parameter, step) attempt defined as bitwise equality of the parameter's stored representation before and after the shipped update, and DISTORTION defined as a relative L2 deviation of the realized per-parameter delta from the delta an FP32-master-weight replay of the same step would have applied, exceeding a threshold declared before any data pull: (a) what is the annihilation rate and the distortion rate per (configuration, parameter group, training-phase bin), estimated from instrumented runs at >= 2 model scales in each of pretraining, SFT and RL post-training; (b) is the rate concentrated on a pre-declared partition of parameter groups (embedding rows binned by training-corpus token frequency, RMSNorm/LayerNorm gains, weights binned by magnitude decile, attention vs MLP projections); and (c) does the rate move monotonically with learning-rate-schedule phase, against a seed-replicate band — a PREDICTION, not an assumption, since Adam's normalized update magnitude partly offsets LR decay?

Alternative formulations:
1. How many of a training run's updates never land, and which parameters quietly stop learning before the run ends?
2. Is "the model was trained for N steps" true of every parameter, or does the precision of the update path freeze a structured subset partway through?
3. (Numerical-analysis words) What is the realized stagnation rate of the weight-update recurrence in shipped low-precision optimizers, and is it stratified by operand magnitude?
4. (Distributed-systems words) What is the per-step weight-update sparsity induced by BF16 absorption, and is the sparsity pattern structured by module?

Motivating gap (as intaken): Inverts the most basic presupposition of training practice — that at step t the optimizer's computed update changes the parameter it is applied to. The swamping mechanism (an update smaller than one ulp of the weight rounds away) is textbook and is the stated motivation for mitigations the ecosystem already ships: Kahan summation for BF16 master weights in torchao/optimi, stochastic rounding in BF16-training proposals, FP32 master weights as the Megatron/DeepSpeed default, block-wise quantized states in bitsandbytes AdamW8bit. The claimed gap was that the field ships fixes whose necessity has never been quantified in a real LLM run: no reported realized annihilation rate, no incidence structure by parameter group, no dependence on the LR phase.

GATE 2 FINDING ON THE MOTIVATING GAP: the gap claim is false as stated. The realized annihilation rate HAS been reported, in a real LLM setting, at five model scales, with the ulp-absorption mechanism explicitly named and arithmetically predicted, and with an FP32-master-weight comparator arm (arXiv 2602.03839). A second paper reports the bitwise-unchanged fraction broken down BY MODULE GROUP and by source-weight magnitude decile (arXiv 2606.13657). Both were opened and read.

Presuppositions (status after Gate 1/2):
1. Annihilation is well defined and cheaply measurable per (parameter, step) by bitwise comparison of the parameter tensor before and after the shipped update — ESTABLISHED, and now established empirically: arXiv 2602.03839 Definition A.2 operationalises exactly this and computes it at 0.5B-7B.
2. BF16-master-weight and 8-bit-optimizer-state configurations are in wide enough use to carry a stake — CONTESTED at intake, needing a declared frame since FP32 master weights are the pretraining default. V2 declares the frame. Partly resolved: the prior literature's regime (RL post-training with BF16 policy weights, LR ~3e-6) is squarely inside the frame and is where the bite is.
3. Annihilation is not already eliminated by default in the configurations that matter — CONTESTED at intake, partly the question. Gate 2: NOT eliminated; it is ~99% per step in the RL regime, and arXiv 2602.03839 reports it remains >99.4% even with FP32 master weights and BF16 compute when the measured object is the BF16 cast (read from the fetched PDF's description of Figure 8; I did not inspect the figure pixels, and the sign of that comparison is the one number in this record I would want re-verified).
4. "Effectively frozen" has a behavioural consequence rather than being a harmless null — CONTESTED, and still contested after Gate 2. Neither prior supplies a matched-arm held-out-loss consequence; arXiv 2602.03839 reports MATH/MBPP pass@1, not loss, and does not run Kahan/SR arms. This is the candidate's one genuinely unmeasured limb.
5. Instrumenting the update path does not change it — SATISFIED by construction (compare pre/post tensors rather than altering arithmetic).
6. The LR schedule's decay drives the rate monotonically upward — CONTESTED at intake and left as a prediction in V2. Gate 2 found no study of schedule-phase dependence; arXiv 2602.03839 holds LR constant and arXiv 2606.13657 compares only AdamW@1e-6 vs SGD@1e-2 as an optimizer ablation.
7. (Newly surfaced, and adverse) The predicted concentration on SMALL-MAGNITUDE weights — FALSIFIED IN DIRECTION by published data. ulp scales with |w|, so a fixed-size update is LESS likely to be absorbed on a small weight. arXiv 2606.13657 finds visible updates cover 24.99-48.57% of the smallest-magnitude 10% of source weights against 10% under random placement, i.e. updates land preferentially on small weights. The candidate's own stated incidence hypothesis is backwards on one of its three named groups.

What would count as an answer (V2): Measured annihilation and distortion rates per (configuration, parameter group, training-phase bin) from instrumented runs at >= 2 scales, with the incidence structure named; plus a matched-arm comparison (Kahan summation / stochastic rounding / FP32 master weights against the shipped arm, same data order and seed) giving held-out loss and the per-group behavioural consequence. "Nothing here" = rate below ~1e-4 of update attempts everywhere outside the final decay, no parameter-group concentration, no matched-arm loss difference. "Something here" = a named group with a rate high enough that an optimizer maintainer can say which configurations need compensated summation and from which point in the schedule.
