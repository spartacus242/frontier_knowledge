## Restatement in my own words

Take two micrometre-ish grains of water ice, press them together and let them sinter at a fixed temperature somewhere between 70 and 150 K, then pull them apart. Is the force needed to break that neck different if the grains are cubic / stacking-disordered ice rather than ordinary hexagonal ice, when everything else — temperature, contact time, load, grain radius and surface area — is held the same, with the stacking state actually measured on those grains? And second: hold such a bonded Isd pair at one declared temperature between 170 and 200 K, where cubic ice slowly turns hexagonal, and compare how its bond strength changes with a hexagonal pair held identically — does the transformation itself alter the bond?
