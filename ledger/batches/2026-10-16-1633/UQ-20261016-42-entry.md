Question (canonical): For continuous or intermittent noninvasive blood-pressure devices evaluated against a fluid-filled intra-arterial reference under a named standard (ISO 81060-3:2022 for continuous automated measurement; per-class analogues declared per dataset), restricted to datasets with retrievable per-pair records and per-line dynamic-response characterisation: (a) for systolic, diastolic and mean pressure separately, how do the device's bias, SD of paired differences and mean squared paired disagreement differ between non-resonant and resonant reference strata, expressed as the fraction of pooled mean squared disagreement contributed by the resonant stratum in excess of its sampling share; and (b) for how many devices does the standard's pass/fail statistic cross its threshold when the analysis is restricted to the non-resonant stratum? (Gate 1 v2; the single permitted reformulation. v1 is in the record.)

Presuppositions:
1. A nontrivial fraction of in-service arterial lines are dynamically distorted — established at one figure, one setting (30.7%); CONTESTED for generalisability, site and overdamping (prevalence unreported anywhere found).
2. Resonance is identifiable post hoc from a recorded waveform — CONTESTED; detectors exist (Romagnoli dP/dt_max; Rinehart overdamping, sens 98%/spec 92%) but derivation cohorts were sampled far faster than 125 Hz archived monitor data. Adversary 1 adds that f_n moves "by tens of Hz" with every irrigation, so a per-patient label is not even constant.
3. Validation datasets retain, or can regenerate, per-pair records permitting stratification — CONTESTED, untested by anyone; the binding feasibility risk.
4. The standard's pass/fail statistic is a function of the reference, so reference quality can move the verdict — the statistic is, but the framing limb FALSIFIED: ISO 81060-3:2022 cl. 4.3.1 c) is normative on reference dynamic response (adversary 2, primary text).
5. MAP is less distorted than systolic by a second-order catheter system — CONTESTED in the adverse direction (P2: positive and negative errors can cancel into a small mean error). v2 correctly tests SBP/DBP/MAP separately.
6. Device-validation studies do not currently check reference fidelity — FALSIFIED as a statement about the governing requirement (cl. 4.3.1 c), "shall", "for each subject"). Whether studies comply is unmeasured and is a different question.
What would count as an answer: A stratified re-analysis over a pre-specified frame of validation datasets with retrievable paired records, every reference line labelled by a pre-registered fidelity rule (fast-flush where available, waveform-derived otherwise, classifier error propagated), reporting bias, limits of agreement and the standard's pass/fail statistic within strata — the resonant stratum's excess share of pooled MSE, and the devices, if any, whose verdict flips. A prospective validation with fast-flush characterisation of every line answers it directly. Full apportionment requires a declared pseudo-truth (fast-flush-identified inverse-filter reconstruction with propagated uncertainty), and the answer must say which estimand it reports. A null is admissible.

THE KILL IS CONFIRMED IN THE PRIMARY STANDARD, by both checkers independently, and the scope of what it establishes is narrower than the kill needs it to be — which does not save the candidate, because the premise it falsifies is the candidate's own.
(i) CONFIRMED, in ISO's own official preview of the primary standard (cdn.standards.iteh.ai sample for catalogue record 71161), clause 4.3.1 c), page 5: "The natural frequency and damping coefficient of the reference invasive blood pressure monitoring equipment" ... "shall be examined and optimised to meet dynamic requirements for each subject." All four elements of the claim hold and each was checked separately. It is NORMATIVE — "shall", sited in clause 4, "General requirements for the clinical investigation", not in the informative Annex A. It is PER SUBJECT — "for each subject", not per study. It names BOTH quantities. And it requires BOTH acts, examination and optimisation, not characterisation alone. Conformance for the clause is checked "by inspection of the clinical investigation report". The second checker reached the same text independently and confirmed the modality is "shall" and not "should".
(ii) The standard exists as described and the clause sits where the record says. ISO 81060-3:2022, "Non-invasive sphygmomanometers - Part 3: Clinical investigation of continuous automated measurement type", published 2022-12-16, edition 1, 36 pp, ISO/TC 121/SC 3, stage 90.92. Clause 4.3.1 runs a) to f), not a) to c): b) requires conformity to IEC 60601-2-34, d) levelling to the right atrium, e) removal of air bubbles and clots, f) permits modification of equipment that does not output waveform or beat-to-beat data. Those are adjacent normative requirements on the same reference line and belong in the record.
(iii) THREE LIMITS ON WHAT THE CLAUSE ESTABLISHES, all refutations of things the record implies. The clause prescribes NO METHOD: "resonance", "fast flush", "flush test" and "intra-arterial" appear nowhere in the readable text, so "ISO 81060-3 mandates a square-wave test per subject" would be an overreach. It states NO NUMERIC THRESHOLD, only "to meet dynamic requirements", deferring the numbers to IEC 60601-2-34, to bibliography Reference [1], and to informative clause A.2 — none of which could be read. And the finding belongs to Part 3 ALONE among documents actually verified: ISO 81060-2, ANSI/AAMI SP10, the 2018 Universal Standard, the AHA and ESH statements, IEEE 1708 and IEC 60601-2-34 were all COULD-NOT-READ, so nothing here licenses a statement about what "the standards" require. Part 2's reference is auscultation, for which natural frequency and damping are meaningless, and a secondary source asserting otherwise is flagged as doubtful.
(iv) THE CONFORMITY GAP IS REAL AND IS NOT THE SAME AS THE PREMISE. The requirement appears to go unevidenced in practice. The one ISO 81060-3 validation study opened (Lai et al. 2026) reports no dynamic-response characterisation of any kind and yet marks clause 4.3.1 PASS on the strength of naming the bedside monitor; a medRxiv source states that routine clinical practice performs no quantitative characterisation at all; and no publication was found that apportions a validation study's error between the device under test and the reference line, or that re-analyses a validation dataset restricted to non-resonant lines (NOT FOUND, not refuted). So three things separate cleanly, and the record should keep them apart: the STANDARD requires it normatively and per subject; the CLINICAL literature supplies the square-wave method the standard omits, as good practice; VALIDATION STUDIES, on a sample of one, do not report doing it. The gap between the first and the third is an informative absence about conformity assessment. It is not evidence that the standard is silent, which is what the candidate's framing needed.
(v) Prior 1 confirmed on its numbers and corrected on its reading. Romagnoli et al., Crit Care 2014;18:644: systolic invasive-minus-noninvasive bias 28.5 (SD 15.9) mmHg in the resonant stratum against 4.1 mmHg in the non-resonant one, 92 and 208 patients of 300. Four corrections travel with it. Per-stratum MEASUREMENT counts are not reported — only totals (11,610 IBP pulses, 1,200 NIBP measurements) — so no measurement-level count may be quoted by stratum. The resonance classification is a graphical three-region criterion in Figure 3 with no numeric cutoff printed; the Figure 2 values (AR 0.43, beta 0.28, 12.5 Hz) are a worked example and the dP/dt(MAX) 1.67 mmHg/msec figure is a proposed detector, neither being the grouping rule. The paper assigns no pass/fail verdict under any device standard and names none; its ">10 mmHg clinically relevant / >20 mmHg clinically unacceptable" threshold is a cited clinical-relevance claim, not a conformity criterion.
