## Restatement (my own words)

Take every published OER stability run in a pre-fixed comparable frame. Each run ends either because the overpotential drifted past a pre-fixed threshold (an "event") or because the author stopped it (a "censoring"). The candidate asks: (i) where do the stop times pile up, and how much of the mass sits on round numbers; (ii) what Kaplan-Meier / Weibull lifetime curve does the corpus support under that threshold; (iii) do authors stop sooner (or later) depending on the drift they were watching — i.e. is the censoring informative — tested by regressing stop time on the final-fraction degradation slope given design covariates; (iv) how long a test would be needed to separate two catalysts at 80% power. In short: are "stable for 100 h" claims a measurement of catalysts or of the field's stopping habits, and can censored-life-data methods tell the difference?
