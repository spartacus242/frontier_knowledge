## Restatement (my words, before reading the record's framing back)

A normal adult mammalian tissue is losing cells all the time. The cell-death field has a catalogue of
distinct death programmes defined by mechanism (apoptosis, necroptosis, ferroptosis, pyroptosis, and
others maintained by a nomenclature committee). The question asks for the catalogue's missing
denominator: among the deaths that actually happen in a healthy tissue, how many are of each kind?

Operationally (V2, the graded version): pick a tissue and a declared animal and physiological state;
pre-commit to an in-situ marker panel whose per-modality hit rate and false-positive rate you have
measured in that same tissue using deliberately induced deaths as positive controls; count
marker-positive dying cells per unit tissue by modality, reporting both the cells positive for more
than one modality's markers and the dying cells you cannot classify at all; then, wherever you know
or can bound how long a corpse of a given modality stays detectable, divide that out to convert the
static count into deaths per unit tissue per day; finally check that the modality-wise deaths sum to
an independently measured total turnover for that tissue.

Short form: every claim that modality X matters physiologically presumes a share nobody has counted.
Count it, for one tissue, with the counting error and the leftovers stated.

## Verified source material I rely on

From F2 (Sender & Milo 2021), quoted from the paper:
- "the total turnover rate of the human body is 0.33 +/- 0.02 x 10^12 (330 +/- 20 billion) cells d-1"; "The total turnover rate of human cellular mass is 80 +/- 20 g d-1."
- "About 86% of these cells are blood cells, mostly of bone marrow origin."
- Methods point values: erythrocytes 2.1 +/- 0.1 x 10^11 cells/d; neutrophils 6 +/- 1 x 10^10; small intestine 2 +/- 1 x 10^10; colon 1.7 +/- 0.6 x 10^10; stomach 1.3 +/- 0.9 x 10^9; epidermis 3.7 +/- 0.9 x 10^9; B cells 5 x 10^9; T cells 2 x 10^9.
- Methods, on routes: "About half of cells replaced daily go through apoptosis and phagocytosis by tissue macrophages" ... "the other half are those of the gastrointestinal tract and skin, which are shed".
- Methods caveat, with its neighbouring clause: "a quantitative analysis of them is still missing and we assume that they do not have a dramatic effect on the overall calculation."

**My own arithmetic, from their Methods point values, carrying their stated uncertainties:**
erythrocytes + neutrophils = (2.1 +/- 0.1)x10^11 + (6 +/- 1)x10^10 = (2.7 +/- 0.14)x10^11 cells/d.
Against their total (3.3 +/- 0.2)x10^11 cells/d this is **~82% of daily cell-number turnover, roughly 75-89% once their uncertainties are propagated**. Gut (small intestine + colon + stomach) + epidermis = ~4.2x10^10 cells/d, i.e. **~13% by number**. Their listed components sum to ~3.2x10^11, consistent with their stated total.
Consequence for reading their "about half": by their own mass figure the shed compartments (GI ~41%, skin ~4%) are ~45% of mass turnover but only ~13% of cell-number turnover. So "about half ... shed" is a **mass** statement; it is not half of the 330 billion cells. The record uses "about half" as a share of "the daily total" (cells/day). That is my inference from their two share figures, labelled as such.
