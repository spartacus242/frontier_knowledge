Domain(s): AI architecture — evaluation methodology (pre-deployment testing, external red-team cohorts, safety cases) is the TARGET field whose literature decides novelty. Method imported from ecology/biostatistics (closed-population capture-recapture, Chao-type richness under heterogeneous catchability) and software reliability (capture-recapture defect-content estimation in inspections). Neighbour vocabularies searched: security (vulnerability rediscovery / bug collision rates), epidemiology (multiple-systems estimation, list dependence), software inspection, audit sampling.

Question (canonical) — R1, adopted at Gate 1:
For pre-deployment exercises in which two or more institutionally independent teams evaluated the SAME model checkpoint under documented access, affordances and time budget: (a) under a pre-registered equivalence relation that maps each documented finding to a failure-mode class at a declared granularity, what is the pairwise and joint rediscovery rate of classes across teams, reported with inter-rater reliability on the matching itself and with sensitivity to one looser and one stricter relation; and (b) does that overlap support a non-vacuous estimate of the classes the exercise missed, where the estimand is declared as the discoverable-at-that-effort-and-scaffold population rather than "all failure modes", estimators are fitted in at least two families (one admitting heterogeneous catchability, one admitting positive list dependence), and the estimate counts only if a pre-specified vacuity test passes (finite upper confidence bound, CI excluding "unboundedly many") under explicitly declared non-closure?
Question (V0, intake, recorded per Gate 1 rule): "Across independent evaluations of the same model checkpoint by separate teams — the multi-institute pre-deployment exercises and labs' external red-team cohorts — what is the rediscovery rate of findings under a pre-registered equivalence relation on failure modes, and does that overlap support any non-vacuous estimate of, or lower bound on, the discoverable failure modes the exercise missed once closure and equal-catchability violations are modelled rather than assumed away?"
R1 changes exactly three things, all for well-posedness, nothing for scope: it fixes the unit (finding -> failure-mode class under a declared-granularity relation), it names the frame condition (>=2 institutionally independent teams, same checkpoint, documented access) as an inclusion criterion rather than an assumption, and it declares the estimand as effort-and-scaffold-conditioned, which is what makes a finite answer possible at all under non-closure.

Alternative formulations:
(1) Lay: When two red teams test the same model, how often do they find the same thing, and what does that overlap license us to say about what neither of them found?
(2) Cross-field: Can the rediscovery statistics used to argue about vulnerability stockpiling be computed for frontier-model evaluations, and do their assumptions survive the move from a fixed code artifact to a model whose failure population is created by elicitation?
(3) Method-field: Is a red-team cohort a valid multiple-recapture sample of a defect population, or is each team sampling a different population that its own scaffolds bring into existence?

Motivating gap: Safety cases, deployment decisions and emerging regulatory adequacy-of-testing language all rest on an implicit coverage claim, and no quantity in the field expresses coverage; findings are reported as lists of what was found. Two ingredients now exist for the first time: the same checkpoint is routinely handed to several independent teams under comparable access and some of those exercises publish (e.g. the NIST-hosted US AISI / UK AISI joint testing reports; UK AISI pre-deployment evaluations), and the home fields are mature (software-inspection capture-recapture with a decade of model evaluation behind it; security's rediscovery-rate work). The live problem is at the interface, not in the transplant: a code artifact has a fixed, individuable defect population, whereas a model's failure modes are produced by elicitation effort and by whichever scaffolds a team happens to bring, so closure and equal catchability fail in a way software inspection never faced, and no equivalence relation for "the same failure" exists. Whether the estimator survives that is the question; the empirical overlap number is itself unpublished and decision-relevant either way.

Presuppositions:
P1 Failure modes can be individuated by an equivalence relation stable enough to match two teams' findings — CONTESTED, and the largest single risk: if matching is unreliable the overlap statistic is undefined and the question partly dissolves. Mitigation is structural, not rhetorical: IRR on the matching is part of the deliverable, and the relation is pre-registered at a declared granularity with a looser and a stricter sensitivity arm.
P2 Two or more teams tested the same checkpoint with comparable access, affordances and time budget — VERIFIABLE PER EXERCISE; unequal access is a catchability violation to model, not a disqualification. Partially supported: joint US/UK institute testing reports and multi-tester system cards exist (see novelty log; the NIST joint-testing document was seen as a search result only, NOT opened).
P3 Findings are documented at comparable granularity across teams — CONTESTED: one team's single finding may be another's five; granularity mismatch is confounded with genuine non-overlap.
P4 The discoverable failure population is approximately closed over the exercise window — CONTESTED and probably false; new scaffolds create new classes. R1 therefore runs the estimator as a bound under declared non-closure and conditions the estimand on fixed scaffolds and effort.
P5 Finding sets from independent teams can be obtained at finding-level granularity and shared — INSTITUTIONAL/CONFIDENTIALITY RISK and the binding feasibility constraint: published system cards and institute reports summarise, and a who-found-what matrix is exactly what summarisation destroys. Fallback: a published joint exercise with per-team appendices, or a deliberately commissioned parallel red team.
P6 Teams are independent in the sense the estimator needs — CONTESTED: shared taxonomies, shared tooling and shared prior literature induce positive list dependence that inflates overlap and deflates the missing-mass estimate. (Directly corroborated in an adjacent setting: the public prompt-injection competition paper attributes its largest attack clusters to a handful of contributors and to participants converging on templates from shared familiarity with known techniques.)
P7 "Independent evaluation" is institutional rather than statistical independence — flagged explicitly so the two senses are not conflated in the write-up.

What would count as an answer: Three things, and the third decides.
(1) An empirical overlap matrix: finding sets from >=2 institutionally independent teams on one checkpoint, each finding mapped to a class under the pre-registered relation by >=2 blinded raters, reported with IRR (e.g. Krippendorff alpha / Cohen kappa on class assignment and on the cross-team match decision) and with the overlap recomputed under one looser and one stricter relation.
(2) Estimates from >=2 estimator families with CIs: at least one admitting heterogeneous catchability (Chao-type / Mh-JK, the model software inspection converged on) and at least one admitting list dependence (log-linear MSE with interaction terms, or a sample-coverage estimator with an estimated dependence correction), fitted on the >=2-list incidence matrix, with sensitivity to effort covariates (team-hours, number of scaffolds).
(3) A pre-specified vacuity test: the estimate counts as an answer only if its upper confidence bound is finite and the CI excludes the trivial "unboundedly many", under the declared non-closure and with the estimand stated as effort-and-scaffold-conditioned.
A clean negative is an equally good answer: a demonstration that measured overlap is driven by shared tooling and taxonomy rather than by independent sampling of a common population — operationalised as overlap collapsing toward chance once shared-scaffold and shared-taxonomy strata are conditioned on, or the dependence-corrected estimator's bound going unbounded. That would establish that coverage estimation of this kind cannot be imported, and would tell safety-case writers to stop implying coverage they cannot compute.
Evidential states that are NOT answers: an overlap number without IRR (P1 unresolved); an estimate fitted on two lists with no dependence model (indistinguishable from the dependence artefact); any estimate reported without the scaffold/effort conditioning (estimand undefined).

Significance — consequence: If the answer is YES (a finite, dependence-corrected bound with passing IRR), the field gets its first computable coverage quantity: a safety case could state "the exercise found an estimated X% of the classes discoverable at this effort under these scaffolds, 95% CI [...]", and two decisions currently made by intuition become computable — whether to commission an Nth external team, and whether to extend an existing team's budget instead. Regulatory adequacy-of-testing language acquires something to point at. If the answer is NO in the clean-negative form (overlap collapses to chance once shared-scaffold and shared-taxonomy strata are conditioned on, or the dependence-corrected bound goes unbounded), the consequence is sharper and arguably more valuable: coverage claims in safety cases are not merely unmeasured but unmeasurable by this route, and the correct intervention shifts from "more teams" to "deliberately decorrelated scaffolds and taxonomies", because the measurement failed for exactly the reason the cohorts are redundant. If the answer is undefined (IRR fails), the field learns that it cannot count its own findings, which indicts every report format that presents findings as an enumerable list.
Significance — audience: (i) AISI/CAISI-type testing programmes designing joint and sequential exercises on one checkpoint — they already run the exercises and currently have no way to report what the second team added over the first; (ii) lab external-red-team coordinators choosing cohort size versus per-team depth, and writing system-card evaluation sections; (iii) safety-case authors and the reviewers assessing confidence in them, who today argue coverage qualitatively; (iv) drafters of GPAI/code-of-practice adequacy-of-testing language, who need an operationalisation or an explicit statement that none exists; (v) the method-owning communities (software-inspection capture-recapture, MSE methodologists), for whom a non-closed, elicitation-generated population would be a new and hard test case. Honest limit: the action in (i)-(ii) depends on P5 (finding-level data sharing), and no document found shows any of these parties asking for a coverage statistic, so demand is argued from their decisions, not evidenced by their requests.
Significance — fecundity: becomes askable once answered — what the marginal return per evaluator-hour is across teams versus scaffolds; whether a red-team cohort can be given a diversity metric that predicts joint coverage; whether failure-class accumulation follows a Heaps-type law in elicitation effort and whether its exponent differs by capability domain; whether cross-team rediscovery rate predicts post-deployment incident discovery (the external validity check the whole construct needs); whether the same machinery bounds what automated red-teaming pipelines miss relative to humans; and whether "failure mode" can be given an equivalence relation stable enough to support any counting at all, which is a prerequisite for several other proposed coverage metrics.
Significance — anchoring: the vulnerability-stockpiling / rediscovery-rate debate (N2) and its policy use; software inspection's unresolved Mh-JK accuracy and its stated application gap (N3); the documented non-reproducibility of red-teaming headline numbers (arXiv:2606.05233); the qualitative coverage deficit named in safety-case confidence work (arXiv:2502.05791); the observed concentration and template-convergence of attack discovery in public competitions (N1), which is the candidate's list-dependence mechanism showing up as an anomaly nobody has treated as a measurement problem.

Reason for silence: Three mechanisms, one of them doing most of the work.
(1) Absent construct (primary): the estimator needs a countable unit, and the field has no equivalence relation for "the same failure mode". Where software inspection gets individuation free (a fault in a frozen artifact) and security gets it from the vendor's patch and CVE assignment, AI evaluation reports findings as examples and transcripts. With no unit, the missing-mass question does not present itself as a measurable one, so it is not asked rather than asked and abandoned.
(2) Disciplinary boundary: AI evaluation methodology imported psychometrics, benchmark statistics and adversarial ML; population-size estimation lives in biostatistics, ecology, official statistics and software inspection, and its application lists (disease surveillance, human-rights casualty counts, software faults) do not include model evaluation. The one neighbour that computes the analogous quantity (security rediscovery) is read in AI circles for its policy conclusion, not its estimator.
(3) Incentive and confidentiality gap: the input the estimator needs is a who-found-what matrix across teams, and that is exactly what NDAs, pre-deployment timelines and system-card summarisation destroy; worse, a published overlap number is unflattering in both directions — high overlap says the cohort was redundant, low overlap says the exercise missed most of the space — so no party running these exercises has an incentive to produce it.
Falsifiable prediction that would undercut this account: a joint-exercise methodology note or institute retrospective that considered and dismissed an overlap statistic. None was found, but grey literature was only sampled.
