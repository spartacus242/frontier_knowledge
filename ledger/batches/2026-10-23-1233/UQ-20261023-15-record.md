## Gate 3 — Significance (argued)

Consequence: If realized life is materially shorter than the declared design working life AND removals are dominated by non-structural causes, then two things move. (a) Code calibration: the lifetime failure probability implied by a target reliability over a 50- or 100-year reference period is being computed over an exposure that does not occur, which bears on whether current partial factors are conservative by an unintended margin — a live question for the committees that set them. (b) LCA/EPD: a declared reference service life longer than the realized one understates per-year impacts of everything normalised by it, which propagates into procurement thresholds. If instead realized life matches and removals ARE dominated by the design limit state, the codes' implicit reading is vindicated and the LCA normalisation is sound — a publishable closure, and the measurand exemption applies.
Audience: CEN/TC250/SC10 (EN 1990 basis of design) and the JCSS Probabilistic Model Code committee, who would act by stating whether the reference period is an exposure duration or a normalising convention and, if the former, by revisiting the period rather than the factors; ASCE 7 load committees; EN 15978/15804 PCR writers who set reference service life; and state DOT asset-management offices, who already act on this evidence — prior 1 recommends coordinating functional-improvement decisions at agency level, which is an audience demonstrably acting, not a hypothesised one.
Fecundity: makes askable (a) the inverse design question — what design working life would equalise nominal and realized exposure for a given asset class; (b) whether design-life extension investment pays when the modal exit is functional rather than structural; (c) the differential-obsolescence question across asset classes sharing one code; (d) whether the convention reading can be tested empirically at all.
Anchoring: the "early replacement" anomaly that prior 1's own group named in a later chapter title; the long-standing LCA sensitivity of results to assumed building/infrastructure lifespan; and the structural-reliability literature's treatment of existing structures, where remaining working life is assumed rather than estimated from realized exits.

## Reason for silence

A disciplinary boundary that runs along the instruments, plus an incentive gap. The method for realized exit distributions is owned by regulated depreciation and valuation engineering (Iowa survivor curves, average service life by account), whose purpose is rate-base recovery and which has no reason to attribute retirements to limit states or to compute a reliability index. The reference period and the target reliability are owned by structural-reliability code calibration, which takes the period as given by the code and calibrates factors within it. Neither field's instrument records the other's measurand: depreciation records retirement ages with no limit-state cause, and condition-inspection data records limit-state proxies with no retirement cause. Where the boundary HAS been crossed — US highway bridges — the crossing was done by transportation asset management (prior 1), which asked the cause question and not the code-calibration question, which is exactly why limb (ii) is a known and limb (iii) may not be. The incentive gap: a finding that the reference period overstates exposure argues for relaxing partial factors, and no code committee is rewarded for proposing that.
