Question (canonical): In reward models trained by the standard pairwise (Bradley-Terry) objective — whose likelihood depends only on reward differences within a prompt's comparison set, leaving the fitted reward identified only up to an additive function of the prompt — (i) what share of the variance in the scalar reward is that per-prompt offset, with CIs, for >= 3 released pairwise-trained reward models on >= 2 preference corpora, with the panel declared; (ii) is the between-prompt component reproducible across k re-fits with different seeds and permuted prompt order, or seed-contingent; and (iii) for each named shipped practice whose decision rule genuinely spans prompts, what is the decision-flip rate when the score is centred within prompt, computed under that practice's own stated decision rule, with a CI?
Reformulation note: this is V2, the one Gate 1 reformulation (V1 presumed every named practice states a numeric threshold; two do not). Gate 5 narrows limb (iii) further, from "each named practice that compares or thresholds reward across prompts" to each such practice whose rule is not shift-invariant — see the strike list under Adversarial log.

Alternative formulations:
1. Is a reward model's number a utility you can compare between two different questions, or only within one?
2. Does the reward scale have an origin, or only an interval structure per prompt, and what has treating it as absolute done to concrete decisions?

Motivating gap (source method: presupposition inversion): The pairwise logistic likelihood is invariant to adding any function of the prompt to every response in that prompt's comparison set, so the fitted reward is identified only up to a per-prompt additive term, while some shipped practice reads the scalar as an absolute quality score across prompts. STRUCK AT GATE 5, clause (a): the record claimed the per-prompt form "was never written down in either literature" because the candidate's own field restated the constant as item-independent (Sun/Shen/Ton Prop. 24, "for a constant C does not depends on i" [served text, grammar as served]). Both adversaries independently falsified this from a primary source they opened — DPO (arXiv:2305.18290) Section 5.1 Definition 1 defines reward equivalence as "r(x, y) - r'(x, y) = f(x) for some function f", attaches Lemmas 1-2, and calls it "a well-known under-specification issue with the Plackett-Luce family of models". Reward Collapse (arXiv:2305.17608) Theorem 1 likewise writes "a function c(x) depending on x". What survives is narrower and is the real gap: the per-prompt term is written down, proved irrelevant to the KL-regularised policy optimum, and never measured or audited for cross-prompt uses.

Significance — consequence: If the between-prompt share is large and seed-contingent, decisions taken on raw cross-prompt reward are partly reading a quantity the objective never pinned, and the fix is cheap and specific (centre per prompt, or pin the scale with cross-prompt anchor comparisons). If it is large but reproducible and monotone in a declared difficulty proxy, the finding inverts: the offset is a free, already-trained difficulty estimator and cross-prompt practice is licensed. If the share is small or flip rates are negligible at the practices' own rules, the question closes with a licence.
