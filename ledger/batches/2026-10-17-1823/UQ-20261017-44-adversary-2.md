## Restatement in my own words

Take the declarations in human-written machine-checked libraries (mathlib, blueprint projects) that
are labelled as formalizations of a specific published theorem. For each, line the Lean statement up
against the paper's statement, counting the paper's standing conventions ("all rings in this section
are commutative Noetherian") as hypotheses of the theorem. Then count two things separately: how
often the Lean version assumes strictly less than the paper (a hypothesis dropped or generalized, or
a weaker axiom basis), and how often it had to assume strictly more. Split each count into what a
machine can flag (axioms via `#print axioms`, binders declared but never used, typeclasses weaker
than the paper's hypothesis) and what humans must judge on a random sample, reporting inter-rater
agreement. Finally, ask whether the "assumes less" rate rises with the source paper's age, length or
subfield. The headline is a signed rate, so a finding that the two directions are equally common
counts as an answer.

## Single strongest killing point

The mechanical decomposition that carries the candidate's "new instrument" claim does not reach the
published statement. `#print axioms` and declared-but-unused-binder detection compare a Lean
declaration with its own Lean proof — which is exactly PRIOR 1's and PRIOR 3's library-internal
measurement, and, as my sweep shows, also TPTP axiom reduction's (2012) and ontology axiom
weakening's (2017) — while the published side has no axiom basis to compare against at all, so the
axiom disjunct can fire only on the small, non-random set of papers that declare AC/CH use. The one
sub-limb that touches the paper, "typeclass weakening relative to the published hypothesis", needs a
hand translation of the paper's hypothesis into typeclass terms and is therefore not mechanical.
What is left is a hand-adjudicated study of a hand-built frame: that both deflates the instrument-
recency half of the reason for silence and removes the feasibility advantage that was supposed to
distinguish this from reverse mathematics' per-theorem programme.
