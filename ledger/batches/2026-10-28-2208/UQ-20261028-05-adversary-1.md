## Restatement in my own words

Take the odd integers n < 65. At 35, 47, 53 and 59 exhaustive computer search has found no four symmetric circulant +-1 matrices A, B, C, D of order n with A^2+B^2+C^2+D^2 = 4nI; at every other odd n < 65 such a quadruple is known. The record names five functions of n alone — (i) ord_2 mod n and the number of 2-cyclotomic cosets; (ii) factorisation type and n mod 4; (iii) the count of four-odd-square representations of 4n under the trivial-character constraints; (iv) a per-character ("local") count of four-square representations of 4n at each n-th root of unity; (v) the class number h+ and 2-rank of Q(zeta_n)^+ — and asks: does any ONE of them take a set of values on the four failing orders that is disjoint from its set of values on the ~29 realisable orders, and if so, which side does that invariant put 65 and 67 on?

## My own arithmetic, labelled as mine (not a source finding), run offline in Python for odd n in [3, 69]

Members (i) and (ii) evaluated at every odd n <= 69. Failing orders in capitals.
- ord_2 mod n: 13 -> 12, THIRTY-FIVE -> 12, 39 -> 12, 45 -> 12, 65 -> 12; FORTY-SEVEN -> 23; FIFTY-THREE -> 52; FIFTY-NINE -> 58; 67 -> 66. So the value 12 sits on BOTH sides (13, 39, 45 realisable; 35 failing): the value sets of ord_2 are NOT disjoint. The values 23, 52, 58 are each taken by one n only in this range, which is injectivity, not separation.
- number of 2-cyclotomic cosets mod n: 35 -> 6 (shared with 21 and 69, realisable); 47 -> 3 (shared with 7, 9, 17, 23, 25, 41); 53 -> 2 and 59 -> 2 (shared with 3, 5, 11, 13, 19, 29, 37, 61). NOT disjoint.
- n mod 4: {3, 3, 1, 3} on the failures; both residues on the realisable side. n mod 8: failures {3, 7, 5, 3}; realisable orders take 1, 3, 5, 7. NOT disjoint. (One-directional curiosity, mine: no n = 1 mod 8 below 65 fails — 9, 17, 25, 33, 41, 49, 57 are all realisable; 65 = 1 mod 8. That is not disjointness and I draw nothing from it.)
- factorisation type: failures are one product of two primes (35) and three primes; both types occur on the realisable side. NOT disjoint. This confirms the record's own three-line argument on (ii).
- Member (iii): I re-derived the record's Jacobi argument. r_4(4n) = 24 sigma(n) and r_4(n) = 8 sigma(n) for odd n; representations of 4n by four even squares are in bijection with representations of n, so 24 sigma(n) - 8 sigma(n) = 16 sigma(n) > 0 representations of 4n use at least one odd square, and 4n = 4 mod 8 forces those to be all-odd. So four-odd-square representations exist at every odd n and (iii) cannot separate. Classical; my verification; I agree with the record.
- Member (v): I RECALL (van der Linden 1982, Math. Comp.; Miller 2014-2015) that h+(Q(zeta_n)^+) = 1 for every conductor n < 136 and that 136 is the first conductor with h+ > 1. If that recollection is right, (v) is the constant 1 (2-rank 0) on every odd n <= 100 and cannot separate. F1 did NOT confirm it (abstract only). UNVERIFIED; grounds nothing; recorded as my recollection.
- Member (iv): the record's wording — "the orbitwise count of representations of 4n as a sum of four squared moduli at each n-th root of unity" — does not say what set the four summands range over (all of Z[zeta_n]? the real subring Z[zeta_n + zeta_n^{-1}]? only the values a_0 + 2 sum a_j cos(2 pi jk/n) actually attained by symmetric +-1 sequences?). The count, its finiteness and its cost at n near 100 all depend on that choice. This is my own reading of the record's definition, not a source finding. I did not evaluate (iv).
Net, labelled as my inference: of the five members, (i), (ii) and (iii) are settled negatively on this page, (v) is very probably constant on the whole range (unverified), and only (iv) is open.
