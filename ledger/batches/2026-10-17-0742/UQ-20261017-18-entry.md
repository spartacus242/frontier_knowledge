Question (canonical): For a named threshold-linked hospital quality measure whose data elements come from human chart abstraction or discretionary clinician entry (e.g. a CMS IQR chart-abstracted measure such as SEP-1), and taking as the audit target the result of complete re-abstraction of all eligible charts rather than "truth": (a) does there exist a sequential re-abstraction sampling-and-stopping rule which, under a pre-registered bounded-per-case abstraction-error model, guarantees that the probability of stopping without escalating to complete re-abstraction, when complete re-abstraction WOULD reverse the hospital's payment or rating classification, is at most alpha; and (b) applying such a rule to a real hospital cohort, in what fraction of hospitals does confirmation of the reported classification at alpha = 0.05 require re-abstracting more than a pre-stated share (say 50%) of eligible charts?
Alternative formulations: (1) As intake (V1, retained in the record): what sequential re-abstraction sample would bound, at a stated risk limit, the probability that complete re-abstraction would move a hospital across its payment or rating threshold, and across a cohort, in what fraction is the reported margin to threshold smaller than documented abstraction disagreement implies? (2) How often is a hospital's penalty or rating an artefact of abstraction error rather than of care? (recorded as the weaker phrasing: it reintroduces the contested truth standard that V2 removes).
