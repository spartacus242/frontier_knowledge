Question (canonical, Gate 0 as intaken):
For open model releases that ship optimizer state alongside weights, how much more does an adversary learn about the final phase of training — document membership, presence of a data source, or reconstructable content — from (weights + Adam moment tensors) than from weights alone at matched attack strength, and what fraction of major open-weight releases ship that state?

Question (Gate 1 reformulation — the one permitted reformulation, for well-posedness only; both versions retained):
(a) CENSUS: over a pre-declared frame of releases (the N most-downloaded base/pretrained repositories on the Hugging Face hub at a fixed snapshot date, plus every family that declares full openness in the Model Openness Framework sense), what fraction publishes resumable optimizer state (Adam/AdamW first and second moments, or an equivalent), at what step granularity, in what file format, and under what licence?
(b) MEASUREMENT: fix one attack pipeline P (membership inference, data-source/attribute inference, and canary reconstruction) and one artifact A = (theta, m, v, t). Define the weights-only baseline as the IDENTICAL pipeline with (m, v) withheld — same attack family, same compute, same number of reference/shadow models, same query budget — so the only varied input is the moment tensors. Estimand: the signed difference in attack performance, delta = AUC(P | theta, m, v) - AUC(P | theta) (and the analogous difference in extraction rate at a fixed false-positive rate), with confidence intervals, estimated (i) on at least one public release that ships optimizer state and (ii) on a purpose-built run with canaries planted at known step offsets k before the checkpoint, reporting delta(k), its decay in k, and the stated power of the null.
Both limbs are required: the reformulation only pins the comparator, the estimand and the frame; it does not change what is being asked.

Alternative formulations:
1. Are Adam first/second-moment tensors a disclosure channel, or does their exponential-moving-average aggregation over millions of tokens destroy the signal that gradient-inversion attacks exploit?
2. Does the reproducibility norm of releasing resumable checkpoints widen the attack surface of an open release relative to releasing weights alone?

Motivating gap:
In federated learning, "what does the shared update disclose about the data?" is a routine question with a mature method family (Zhu et al. 2019 "Deep Leakage from Gradients"; Geiping et al. 2020 "Inverting Gradients" — BOTH CITED FROM MEMORY, NOT OPENED in this run, and therefore grounding no grade here). In open-release engineering the shipped artifact increasingly includes optimizer state for resumability and bit-exact continuation, and nobody asks what that state discloses beyond the weights.
Two corrections this run made to the intake's own picture of the gap, both from sources opened below:
- The believed flagship case is a NEGATIVE. LLM360's paper (arXiv:2312.06550) says in its framework section that it releases "intermediate checkpoints saved during their training, including model weights and optimizer states", but its AMBER section states: "we did not manage to save the optimizer states; we only saved model checkpoints for each data chunk" (AdamW re-initialised on each resume; 360 checkpoints, one per data chunk). So LLM360/Amber is a case where optimizer state was NOT shipped.
- The OLMo case is grounded but not fully confirmed end-to-end. allenai/OLMo's official checkpoint manifests (e.g. configs/official-1124/OLMo-2-1124-13B.csv) list public URLs of the form https://olmo-checkpoints.org/ai2-llm/peteish13/step1000-unsharded/ at ~1000-step granularity, and the repo's scripts/unshard.py writes "optim.pt" ("Saving optimizer state to %s") next to model.pt in exactly that unsharded layout, with olmo/checkpoint.py loading "optim.pt" on resume. So the published artifact format contains optimizer state by construction. A direct HTTP HEAD on .../step1000-unsharded/optim.pt could NOT be run: the environment proxy refuses olmo-checkpoints.org (curl (56) CONNECT tunnel failed, response 403) for optim.pt, model.pt and config.yaml alike. Recorded as a route that failed, not as a confirmation.
Technical content of the transplant (why this is not decorative): the second moment v is an EMA of squared gradients with horizon O(1/(1-beta2)) steps — about 1000 steps at beta2=0.999, each step over 1e6-4e6 tokens, so v aggregates 1e9-1e10 tokens; the first moment m has horizon O(1/(1-beta1)), about 10 steps at beta1=0.9. The two-timescale structure is a sharp prediction, not a vague one: any disclosure should be concentrated in m with a ~10-step decay and near-absent in v unless per-coordinate heavy tails survive the square-and-average. That regime sits far outside the single-step, small-batch regime where gradient-inversion attacks are characterised, so neither literature implies the answer.

Presuppositions:
P1. Some major open releases actually ship optimizer state. NOT ASSUMED — it is limb (a). Status after this run: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED AND PARTIALLY FALSIFIED. Falsified for LLM360/Amber (explicitly not saved). Supported by construction for OLMo-family unsharded checkpoints (public manifest + unshard.py writing optim.pt), with direct object-level verification blocked by the proxy. If the census returns near-zero the question collapses to a counterfactual about a proposed norm and must be re-posed; it does not currently look near-zero, but the premise rests on one family.
P2. Adam moments retain per-coordinate information about recently seen batches. ESTABLISHED by the update rule; only the magnitude and its decay in k are at issue.
P3. A membership/attribute/extraction protocol can be run either on a released artifact or faithfully replicated in a purpose-built run with planted canaries. ESTABLISHED (standard canary-insertion auditing practice).
P4. "More than weights alone" is identifiable only if the weights-only baseline is run at matched strength and compute. CONTESTED — an unmatched baseline manufactures a gap. The Gate 1 reformulation answers this by defining the baseline as the same pipeline with (m, v) ablated under identical budgets, which is why the reformulation was spent here.
P5. The quantity is decision-relevant in both directions. CONTESTED as significance; the named actors are open-release engineering teams and open-weight-release guidance bodies. See Gate 3.
P6. Measurement is done on released artifacts and on purpose-built runs with synthetic canaries, so no private data is attacked. Declared constraint, not a claim about the world.
P7 (new, implicit, now flagged): that moments are released in a form whose coordinates align with the released weights (same parameter layout, not sharded/fused/quantised beyond recovery). OLMo's unshard.py exists precisely because the native format is sharded, so for other families this is an open engineering question that the census must record, not assume.

What would count as an answer:
(i) Census: a published fraction with the frame stated — per release: optimizer state yes/no, step granularity, format, licence, and parameter-layout alignment.
(ii) Measurement: on at least one release that ships optimizer state, and on a purpose-built run with canaries planted at known offsets k in the last K steps, matched-strength membership, attribute-inference and reconstruction attacks with and without the moment tensors, reporting the AUC / extraction-rate difference with CIs and delta(k) decay. An answer is that signed gap plus its recency decay. A credible null — a gap whose CI contains zero at a stated power — settles the release-norm question in the permissive direction and is as publishable as a positive.

Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, with sources I actually opened):
NP1. Zanella-Beguelin, Tople, Paverd, Kopf, Brockschmidt, Salem et al., "Analyzing Information Leakage of Updates to Natural Language Models", ACM CCS 2020, pp. 363-375 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.07942 (opened, full text).
  Unit of analysis: a token sequence (phrase/sentence) in the update data D' \ D.
  Endpoint: differential score / differential rank, approximated by beam search; rank 0 = sequence recovered. Plus n-gram perplexity and Levenshtein distance to nearest match for real data.
  Reference/comparator: single-snapshot analysis of M_D or M_D' alone (explicitly reported as revealing substantially less), and prior single-model extraction (Carlini et al.).
  Estimand: the differential score of a sequence as evidence that it was added in the update; not a formal privacy bound.
  Observable: two WEIGHT snapshots, queried as black boxes for next-token distributions. Optimizer state is never part of the released artifact. Scale: LSTMs and a 4-layer Transformer on PTB / 20M-token Reddit / WikiText-103.
NP2. Guangnian Wan, Gongfan Fang, Xinyin Ma, Xinchao Wang, "Gradient Inversion Attacks Beyond SGD", ICML 2026 — https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/64051 (venue page opened; ABSTRACT ONLY, full text COULD NOT READ).
  Unit of analysis: a client's batch (images + labels) in a federated training round.
  Endpoint: reconstruction of inputs and analytically recovered labels; evaluated on ImageNet and PACS.
  Reference/comparator: existing gradient-matching attacks assuming vanilla SGD with observable raw gradients.
  Estimand: reconstruction fidelity from observed momentum-based parameter updates under Adam / AdaGrad / RMSProp, via an update-matching objective.
  Observable: the per-round parameter UPDATE produced by an adaptive optimizer — i.e. the adversary must invert the optimizer map to get at gradients. The page does not state batch sizes, round counts, or any use of released public checkpoints, and says public checkpoints shipping optimizer state are not discussed.
NP3. Thomas Michel, Debabrota Basu, Emilie Kaufmann, "Sequential Membership Inference Attacks", arXiv:2602.16596v2 (2026) — https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2602.16596v2 (opened; arXiv mirror page, abstract-level detail — flagged as a mirror, not the publisher PDF).
  Unit of analysis: the insertion step of a target canary within a training sequence.
  Endpoint: a decision of whether the target was inserted at a specific step; an optimal test (SeMI*) is given.
  Reference/comparator: "snapshot-independent baselines" — attacks using a single model snapshot.
  Estimand: statistical power of the sequential test, used to argue for tighter privacy audits of (DP-)SGD training.
  Observable: a sequence of model updates, white-box (gradients) or black-box (loss). Optimizer state is not mentioned.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
vs NP1: same STRUCTURE ("artifact pair discloses more than one artifact") but a different second channel and a different estimand target. NP1's extra information is a SECOND WEIGHTS SNAPSHOT separated by an update; the candidate's extra information is the OPTIMIZER STATE AT ONE CHECKPOINT, which is not a second snapshot and carries a different decay law (m ~ 10 steps, v ~ 1000 steps). NP1's unit is a sequence added between snapshots in fine-tuning-scale LSTM/Transformer runs; the candidate's unit is a document seen k steps before a pretraining checkpoint at 1e6-4e6 tokens per step. NP1 has no census limb and no matched-compute ablation discipline (its baseline is a different observable, not the same pipeline with a channel withheld).
vs NP2: NP2 is the closest METHOD prior and it does establish that adaptive-optimizer observables are attackable — but in the FL per-round regime where the observable is an update the adversary must invert to recover a gradient over a small client batch of images. The candidate's observable is the STORED m and v tensors in a public artifact, whose information content is an EMA over 1e9-1e10 tokens; the candidate's endpoints are membership / source-presence / canary extraction on text, not image reconstruction; the candidate requires a matched-strength weights-only baseline, which an FL inversion paper has no reason to run; and the candidate's limb (a) census does not exist in NP2 at all. vs NP3: NP3 pre-empts the SHAPE of the candidate's recency-decay sub-question (its unit IS the insertion step, its comparator IS single-snapshot) but with a different information source — the sequence of models / gradients / losses, not the optimizer moments — and in the service of DP auditing rather than release engineering. A release that ships optimizer state at one step is not a model sequence; conversely a release that ships many weight checkpoints is attackable by NP3 without any optimizer state. Significance — consequence:
If the gap is positive and non-trivial (say delta AUC >= 0.05 at a usable FPR, or any canary recoverable from m that is not recoverable from theta), then resumability and disclosure are in direct tension for the first time at the artifact level: releases would have to choose between bit-exact continuation and a measurably larger attack surface, and the mitigation is cheap and specific (ship v only, zero m, coarsen the step offset, or publish optimizer state at a lag). That is an actionable engineering recommendation, not an exhortation. If the gap is a credible null at stated power, the result is equally consequential in the permissive direction: it removes a speculative privacy objection to the strongest form of open release, and lets openness frameworks promote optimizer-state publication without hedging. Note the asymmetry that makes the null worth buying: the two-timescale prediction means a null on v with a positive on m would localise the whole effect in ~10 steps of training, which is both the most likely outcome and the most useful one, because it names the exact thing to withhold.

Significance — anchoring:
Hidden-state DP accounting and the last-iterate-versus-trajectory question (arXiv:2405.14457); the gradient-inversion family's open question of what survives aggregation (NP2 and the IJCAI'22 survey arXiv:2206.07284); the MOF I.5 component's unresolved "(when applicable)"; the live policy question of what "open-weight" release means in artifact terms. It also touches the reproducibility-versus-disclosure trade-off that the Amber negative illustrates from the other side: Amber's inability to save optimizer state is reported there as a reproducibility FAILURE, with no suggestion that it might have been a disclosure mitigation.

Reason for silence:
A disciplinary boundary with a mismatched threat model on each side, plus artifact novelty and an incentive gradient. The community that owns the METHOD (FL privacy, gradient inversion, DP auditing) defines its threat model around information IN TRANSIT during training — a per-round update seen by a server or eavesdropper, or an iterate sequence seen by an auditor — and under that framing the optimizer state is the attacker's nuisance (something to invert through, as in NP2) or the analyst's accounting detail, never a published object. The community that owns the ARTIFACT (open-release engineering, openness frameworks) enumerates release components for reproducibility, completeness and licensing: MOF and FMTI between them have no field, no indicator and no measured fraction for optimizer state, so there is no instrument in which the question could even be posed as a gap. The object itself — a downloadable tarball containing m and v for a frontier-scale pretraining run — only became common with the ~2023-onward full-openness releases, so there was nothing to ask about earlier. And the incentive runs the wrong way: the question's possible positive answer asks open-release teams to ship less, against the field's prevailing norm, while its likely answer (aggregation destroys the signal) looks like a null that nobody expects to be rewarded for publishing. "No one thought of it" is not the mechanism; the mechanism is that each side's framing makes the object invisible, and neither side's incentives pay for looking.
