Domain(s): Other STEM — mathematics (experimental mathematics, automated conjecture generation). Method field: statistics (selective inference, false-discovery rate).

Question (canonical, as intaken): For the enumerable corpus of machine-generated numerical conjectures that passed their generator's own numerical-agreement acceptance threshold, what is the realized rate of non-identities among those adjudicated so far, and does it agree with the rate implied by that threshold under an explicit null model of the generator's search volume?

Question (Gate 1 reformulation — one permitted, used; both versions recorded): Restricted to conjecture engines that publish BOTH a per-conjecture acceptance precision d and a recoverable search volume M (by report or by re-running the released code), and adjudicating each accepted conjecture into {proved new, proved but already known, disproved/non-identity, numerically unverifiable, open}: what interval (under censoring by the open class) covers the non-identity share, and how does it compare with the threshold-implied bound M*10^-d — with "already known" scored separately so rediscovery is not counted as a false discovery?
Reason for reformulation: well-posedness only. The intake version (i) left the denominator undefined across engines with staged thresholds, (ii) did not separate rediscovery from falsity, and (iii) presupposed documented search volumes (falsified below).

Motivating gap (as intaken, and as it stands after Gate 2): intake held that automated conjecture engines accept on a digit threshold with "no null measure over the search space defined, so the digit threshold is uncalibrated in a way nobody has quantified." Gate 2 falsified the central clause in weak form: both engines I opened state a coincidence-probability null, and one of them is multiplicity-aware.
- Raayoni et al. / Ramanujan Machine (arXiv:1907.00205, = Nature 590 (2021) preprint), opened: "the probability of finding a random match for an enumeration space of 10^9 and result accuracy of more than 50 digits, is smaller than 10^-40." No derivation shown, no expected-false-count per campaign, no FDR language — but the enumeration space IS carried in the bound.
- ESMA (arXiv:2212.09470), opened: accepts at 1000 decimal places ("Each case for which the numerical values are identical for up to 1000 decimal places is considered a new conjecture"); gives "the probability for a false positive ... roughly 10^-1000" plus a Berlekamp-Massey false-positive scaling ~(1/p)^(n-2L). Per-candidate only: it does not account for the number of candidates tested.
So the live gap is narrower than intake claimed: the nominal side exists (crudely), the realized side has never been tallied, and at the thresholds actually used (100-2000 digits for RM figures, 1000 for ESMA) the implied false-match expectation is so far below one that the calibration ratio is settled by one line of arithmetic. The non-degenerate residue is the 10-digit FIRST-STAGE filter in RM's MITM-RF, conjectures whose slow convergence makes the verification itself numerically doubtful, and LLM-produced conjectures with no numerical threshold at all.

Presuppositions (status after Gate 2):
1. A dated, enumerable corpus with recorded acceptance thresholds exists. PARTLY FALSE. Thresholds are stated at the ENGINE level (RM: MITM-RF filters at "a low precision (10 digits)" then re-evaluates; Fig. 3 results "tested numerically to 100 digits," some checked "for up to 2000 digits"; ESMA: 1000 decimals). Per-conjecture thresholds are NOT recorded: RM's Appendix A tables give convergence rates, not acceptance precision, and present "a sample of polynomial continued fractions," pointing to a website that is "regularly updated" (no dated snapshot verified). ESMA publishes code (RamanujanMachine/ESMA) and "a small sample of conjectures" only.
2. Search volumes are recorded per conjecture. FALSIFIED for both engines opened. RM reports no count of enumerated candidates (the 10^9 figure is a hypothetical inside the coincidence estimate); ESMA gives scheme parameters (62 sign patterns, N=50 terms, complexity O((2L+1)^(2(m+1))), "up to 50 different constants") and relative gains (+357% for e, +591% for tan 1) but no absolute totals. So the comparison requires re-running the released code, which changes the object from "the published corpus" to "my rerun" — a real, not decorative, design cost.
3. Resolution status is ascertainable for a usable subset. CONTESTED, and worse than intake feared. Neither paper gives an aggregate tally; labels are per-row ("known", "new and proven", "new and unproven"), and NEITHER PAPER REPORTS ANY DISPROVED CONJECTURE. The numerator of the realized rate is plausibly 0 in all published material, so the estimand is censoring-dominated and near-degenerate.
4. A null model over the search space can be specified. PARTLY ESTABLISHED, which cuts against the candidate: a per-candidate null is specified in both engine papers, and Odrzywolek (arXiv:2002.12690) gives a search-size-aware rule (below).
5. "False discovery" is definable for this object. HOLDS only with a pre-specified adjudication rule; the reformulation adds the "numerically unverifiable" class, because at 1000 digits the dominant failure mode is verification arithmetic and convergence, not digit coincidence.
6. Acceptance thresholds are stated in papers or code. VERIFIED at engine level (quotes above).
7. NOT presupposed: that any generator claimed calibration, or that machine conjectures are low quality. Upheld — and note RM/ESMA both say numerical agreement "does not substitute the need for formal proof," i.e. they explicitly decline the calibration claim.

What would count as an answer: per engine, (i) recover d and M (M by instrumenting the released code, since neither paper reports it), (ii) build an empirical null by re-running the engine against independent random target constants of matched complexity to get the expected count of threshold-passing coincidences, (iii) adjudicate the real output into the five classes and report the non-identity share as an interval under censoring, (iv) state the minimum d holding FDR below a stated level at the measured M. A finding that thresholds are conservative is a full answer. Gate 2 note: for d >= 100 this answer is obtainable without (i)-(iii) from M*10^-d, which is why the significance argument below scores low.

Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, all opened):
P1. A. Odrzywolek, "Criteria for the numerical constant recognition", arXiv:2002.12690 (2020). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.12690
 - Unit of analysis: ONE target constant z with stated error sigma, plus the stream of candidate formulas (RPN codes) enumerated for it.
 - Endpoint: return a candidate formula or declare failure; three credibility criteria (e-folding error drop-off, maximum-likelihood score Eq. 10 shrinking in the number k3 of unique values tested, compression ratio r = -log10 max(eps,sigma)/(K log10 n)).
 - Reference/comparator: a statistical null for random formula values (exponential Eq. 7 / Cauchy-like Eq. 8) plus expected e^(-N) error decay.
 - Estimand: the relative likelihood that a given formula is the true identity of z (a ranking), PLUS an explicit search-size rule: "Tested unique formulas count must not exceed 1/sigma", with the statement that beyond that "probability of random match approaches 1". Explicitly NOT an FDR: "Above criterion do not provide any numerical estimate for probability of successful identification."
P2. "Surprises in numerical expressions of physical constants", arXiv:1603.00299 (2016). https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.00299
 - Unit: generated expressions Z = g(f(A,B),C) over {1..9, pi, e, gamma, phi, -phi_b} with 7 operations, and the d-digit numbers they produce; search space "14^3 x 7^2 = 10^5.1".
 - Endpoint: coverage — the proportion of d-digit numbers matched by at least one expression.
 - Reference: a random d-digit number as the null.
 - Estimand: the a priori probability that a d-digit constant coincides with some expression ("the a priori probability for a 5-digit number like 1836.1 is only 1.2%"); heuristic estimate ~2.2% vs actual ~1.2%. No corpus of accepted conjectures; no realized-vs-nominal calibration; "false discovery rate" and "multiple comparisons" never appear.
P3. Raayoni et al., Ramanujan Machine, arXiv:1907.00205 (Nature 590 (2021) preprint), https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.00205 — read jointly with ESMA, arXiv:2212.09470, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09470
 - Unit: one accepted formula/conjecture.
 - Endpoint: probability that THIS match is random (RM: "<10^-40" at >50 digits for an enumeration space of 10^9; ESMA: "roughly 10^-1000" at 1000 digits, plus a Berlekamp-Massey scaling ~(1/p)^(n-2L)).
 - Reference: a random parameter set / random enumeration draw.
 - Estimand: a per-candidate (RM: space-scaled) false-match probability, used to justify the threshold. ESMA's is explicitly per-candidate and does not account for the number tested. Neither reports search volume, an aggregate resolution tally, or any disproved conjecture; neither claims calibration ("does not substitute the need for formal proof").

The candidate's second limb is nonetheless P2's estimand transposed to a larger space.
vs P3: P3 is the object of the candidate's question, and it already supplies the nominal side — one engine with the search space in the bound, one without. Significance — consequence: Thin, and thinner after Gate 2. If realized >> nominal, engines would have to raise d or publish search volumes — but at d = 100-2000 with M plausibly <= 10^12, M*10^-d is unimaginably below 1, so "yes, conservative" is near-certain a priori for the flagship engines and obtainable in a paragraph. The non-degenerate consequences are narrower: (a) RM's 10-digit first-stage filter, where M*10^-10 can exceed 1 and the staged design's survival rate is undocumented; (b) conjectures whose convergence is slow enough that 1000-digit "verification" is dominated by arithmetic/convergence error rather than coincidence — a different failure mode the question does not name; (c) LLM-produced conjectures, which often have no numerical threshold to calibrate, so the question does not reach them.
Significance — audience: automated-conjecturing and AI-for-mathematics groups (Technion RM group, graph-conjecture systems, OEIS-driven LLM pipelines), and referees receiving numerically-verified claims. Real community, but both engine papers already pre-empt the referee's question with a one-line bound and disclaim calibration, so it is hard to argue they would act differently. Would plausibly act on (a) and (b) above, not on the headline question.
Significance — fecundity: moderate. Opens: how to score rediscovery of known identities (the engines' "known" labels are unaggregated); whether verification-numerics error, not coincidence, is the real false-discovery channel in high-precision search; whether search volume should be a reporting requirement for conjecture engines; and whether a non-uniform null (algebraic near-identity families, structured constants) beats the digit-uniform null the engines assume — the last of these is the strongest surviving question and is NOT the one asked.
Significance — anchoring: Guy's strong law of small numbers (recalled, NOT opened — folklore anchor only); the Borwein "dubious identities" zoo and the PSLQ precision rules (both surfaced in search, NOT opened, so neither grounds anything here); the integer-relation precision theory confirmed to exist by query 4.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism): not a disciplinary-boundary gap but an irrelevance-of-margin mechanism. The field's acceptance precisions (10^2-10^3 digits) put the per-candidate coincidence probability so far below any plausible search volume that the multiplicity correction changes nothing a practitioner would notice, so the apparatus is never built; the quality worry that does bite in practice has a different name in the field's own words ("still requires a formal proof", "known*" rediscoveries), and that name absorbs the concern. Statisticians who own the method do not read continued-fraction search papers, and experimental mathematicians treat 1000-digit agreement as morally certain. This is a credible mechanism, but note it is a mechanism explaining why the answer is cheap — which is evidence for the Low grade rather than against it.
