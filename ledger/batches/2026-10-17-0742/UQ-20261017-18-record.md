Domain(s): Medicine — health-systems quality measurement and payment policy (TARGET field: its literature decides novelty; CMS/NCQA/HQIP measure and audit programmes are the community that would act). Method transplanted from election auditing / statistical audit theory (risk-limiting audits).

Question (canonical):
V1 (as intake): For publicly reported, threshold-linked hospital quality measures whose numerator or denominator depends on human chart abstraction or clinician-entered data, what sequential re-abstraction sample would bound, at a stated risk limit, the probability that complete re-abstraction would move a hospital across its payment or rating threshold — and across a cohort of hospitals, in what fraction is the reported margin to threshold smaller than documented abstraction disagreement implies?

V2 (REFORMULATED at Gate 1, for well-posedness; the one reformulation permitted — both versions recorded): For a named threshold-linked hospital quality measure whose data elements come from human chart abstraction or discretionary clinician entry (e.g. a CMS IQR chart-abstracted measure such as SEP-1, or a registry-based measure feeding a rating), and taking as the audit TARGET the result of complete re-abstraction of all eligible charts (not "truth"): (a) does there exist a sequential re-abstraction sampling-and-stopping rule which, under a pre-registered bounded-per-case abstraction-error model, guarantees that the probability of stopping without escalating to complete re-abstraction, when complete re-abstraction WOULD reverse the hospital's payment or rating classification, is at most alpha; and (b) applying such a rule to a real hospital cohort, in what fraction of hospitals does confirmation of the reported classification at alpha = 0.05 require re-abstracting more than a pre-stated share (say 50%) of eligible charts?

Why the reformulation matters: V2 retargets the estimand from "truth" to "the full re-abstraction result", exactly as an election RLA targets the full hand count of the paper trail rather than voter intent. This neutralises the contested presupposition that expert re-abstraction is ground truth, and makes limb (a) a risk statement about a reproducible object. V2 also pre-registers the error model and alpha, which limb (b) needs to be a number rather than an analyst's choice.

Motivating gap:
Threshold misclassification of hospitals has been quantified, but only for SAMPLING noise. VERIFIED AT INTAKE (not re-opened this session): Shen C, Wadhera RK, Yeh RW, "Misclassification of Hospital Performance Under the Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program", JAMA Cardiol 2021;6(3):332-335 — unit: HRRP hospitals FY2019 (1,633 AMI / 2,626 HF / 2,705 pneumonia, >=25 discharges); endpoint: condition-specific penalty status; reference: penalty status under "true" performance via bayesian deconvolution; estimand: % of hospitals with misclassified penalty status (20.9/13.5/13.2% under-penalised; 10.1/10.9/12.3% wrongly penalised; "up to 31%"). Its stated cause is that the measure "is a point estimate with a margin of error" — statistical sampling variation. No abstraction-error term, no audit or re-abstraction sampling design.
Election auditing holds the missing object: a risk-limiting audit bounds the probability that the reported OUTCOME stands uncorrected and escalates to a full count otherwise.
Existing validation audits sample a small fixed number of charts and report element agreement rates — a data-accuracy statistic about the hospital, not a bound on the decision. CMS IQR validation design remains RECALLED, NOT VERIFIED (see Access routing). The UK analogue WAS verified this session and shows the gap concretely: HQIP outlier guidance escalates on a time schedule, not on evidence, and asks at Stage 4 whether "the data originally supplied by the provider contained inaccuracies" while giving no criteria, no sample size and no risk limit.
The transplant's real work is at the interface: risk-standardised, shrunken, model-based measures are not an additive ballot tally, so RLA sample-size machinery does not carry over unchanged.

Presuppositions:
1. Threshold-linked measures with abstraction-dependent or clinician-entered elements feed a payment or rating decision — ESTABLISHED, but the frame must be scoped to abstraction-dependent measures, since much of HRRP/HVBP is claims-based.
2. Expert re-abstraction is closer to truth than the hospital's own abstraction — CONTESTED, and DISSOLVED BY V2: the audit target is the full re-abstraction result, so re-abstraction need not be truth, only the reproducible reference the programme already treats as authoritative. (Alternative formulation 2 below, "artefact of abstraction error rather than of care", does reintroduce truth and is the weaker phrasing.)
3. Element-level abstraction disagreement rates are published or estimable — ESTABLISHED this session: Garza 2022 reports all-field error 1.24% [1.14, 1.34] and populated-field error 3.04% [2.81, 3.30] against the EHR as gold standard; the Illinois APORS QC report gives per-field accuracy from 80.9% (date of diagnosis) to 96.7% (address).
4. The decision is a threshold or rank crossing, so a "margin" is well defined — ESTABLISHED for penalty programmes; CONTESTED for composite star ratings, where the margin depends on several measures and on other hospitals. Scope limb (b) to single-measure thresholds or state the composite margin explicitly.
5. Abstraction error can be represented as a bounded per-case perturbation of auditable units, which RLA sample-size machinery requires — CONTESTED and PART OF THE QUESTION. A ballot is bounded by construction (one ballot moves at most one vote); a chart's effect on a risk-standardised shrunken estimator is not. V2 keeps "no margin statistic exists for shrunken model-based measures" as an admissible answer.
6. Chart sampling to the required depth is operationally and legally feasible — CONTESTED; near-complete re-abstraction may be infeasible, which is itself part of what limb (b) would quantify.

What would count as an answer:
(i) A stated-risk audit procedure for at least one real threshold-linked abstraction-dependent measure: explicit abstraction-error model, derived sequential sampling and stopping rule with escalation to complete re-abstraction, and validation by simulation on real re-abstraction data showing realised risk <= nominal alpha.
(ii) An empirical margin-versus-error analysis across a hospital cohort: the fraction of hospitals whose penalty or rating classification cannot be confirmed at alpha = 0.05 without near-complete re-abstraction.
Either direction is an answer. "Almost every decision is confirmable from a small sample" retires the concern and justifies cheap fixed-n validation. "Many are not" makes current fixed-n validation audits unfit for the decisions they are attached to. "No margin statistic exists for shrunken model-based measures" is also an answer, and the most consequential, because it would say threshold payment on shrunken estimates is not auditable in the risk-limiting sense and would bear on measure construction.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
vs P1 (He/Selck/Normand 2014): same unit (hospital) and nearly the same endpoint (tier/threshold classification), so this is the CLOSE neighbour. It differs on reference and estimand, which is where the candidate lives. P1's error source is sampling plus between-hospital variance under a random-effects model; its estimand is the expected accuracy of an ESTIMATOR choice averaged over hospitals, and it is silent on abstraction or any other measurement error. The candidate's error source is abstraction disagreement in the data elements; its estimand is per-hospital and prospective — a bound on the probability that this hospital's reported classification survives complete re-abstraction — and it demands a sampling/stopping rule, an object P1 does not contain. P1 tells a programme designer which estimator misclassifies least; it cannot tell an auditor how many charts to pull.
vs P2 (Garza 2022): same error source, wrong unit and wrong endpoint. P2's unit is the field within a research QC case and its endpoint is the field-level error rate against the EHR; the candidate's unit is the hospital-measure decision and its endpoint is a threshold crossing. P2's threshold (4.93%) is a data-quality acceptability limit for the abstraction process, and its escalation is three more cases regardless of how close any downstream quantity is to any decision boundary. This is exactly the "data-accuracy statistic about the hospital, not a bound on the decision" structure the candidate names, now confirmed from an opened source rather than recalled.
vs P3 (HQIP outlier guidance): same decision shape (provider crosses a statistical boundary and consequences follow) and even the same escalation idea (re-analyse after data correction), but no statistical content at the escalation step. P3's trigger is a fixed 2SD/3SD alert and its escalation is a calendar; it asks whether the data were inaccurate without specifying who looks at how many records or what would settle it, and never relates the provider's distance from the control limit to documented data-error rates. The candidate replaces the calendar with a stopping rule and the question with an estimand. P3 is therefore evidence FOR the gap rather than prior art on it.
vs P0 (Shen 2021): identical unit and endpoint (HRRP hospitals, penalty status), different error source and different object. Shen's reference is "true" performance recovered by bayesian deconvolution of sampling noise, and its estimand is a cohort percentage misclassified; the candidate's reference is complete re-abstraction, its error source is abstraction, and its primary object is an audit design with a risk guarantee. Shen answers "how often is the penalty wrong because of noise"; the candidate asks "how many charts must be re-read before we can say this penalty is right, and for how many hospitals is that number near-total".
vs the method literature (elections/accounting): the RLA family (Stark; SHANGRLA; RiLACS; and accounting's stop-or-go sampling) owns the machinery but has, in every query tried here, been applied only to tallies and to monetary misstatement. The interface problem is real and not merely A x B: risk-standardised shrunken estimators are not additive over auditable units, so neither the ballot-level error bound nor the monetary-unit bound transfers, and whether a margin statistic exists at all is inside the question.

Significance — consequence:
If limb (b) returns a large fraction: the fixed-n validation audits attached to these programmes are mis-specified for the decisions they serve. The remedy is structural — margin-proportional sampling, escalation to complete re-abstraction, and an appeal process keyed to a stated risk — not better abstractor training or a tighter element-agreement target, which is where essentially all current effort goes (Garza 2022's 4.93% field threshold and the IRR-program vendor literature are the type case). A payer could no longer defend a penalty by citing an agreement rate.
If it returns a small fraction: the present cheap audits are vindicated for their actual purpose, the decision-risk worry is retired with a number, and the field gains a defensible reason to stop re-litigating abstraction accuracy as a fairness objection to public reporting. That is a publishable negative, which is the mark of a question rather than an agenda.
If limb (a) returns non-existence — no margin statistic for shrunken model-based measures: threshold payment on shrunken estimates is shown to be unauditable in the risk-limiting sense. That bears on measure CONSTRUCTION, implying a preference for measures whose dependence on auditable units is additive enough to support a margin, and it would give the long-running reliability/minimum-volume debate a new and sharper criterion.

Significance — audience:
(1) CMS and its measure/validation contractors (Hospital IQR validation, HVBP, HRRP, Care Compare star ratings): they own a sampling design that this question says is answering the wrong question; the deliverable is a replacement rule, not a critique. (2) NCQA HEDIS compliance auditors, whose audits have the same structure (sample for data accuracy, report rates) against rate-linked accreditation and bonus decisions. (3) HQIP and CQC in England: their own guidance, read this session, escalates outliers on a 10/5/25/20-working-day calendar and asks at Stage 4 whether the data were inaccurate while specifying no method — a stated-risk rule is a drop-in replacement for an acknowledged blank. (4) Hospital quality departments and IRR programmes, who would reallocate abstraction QC effort from uniform element agreement toward cases and elements that move the margin. (5) Health-measurement statisticians (the Normand/Dimick reliability tradition), for whom this reframes reliability as auditability. (6) The RLA and audit-sampling community, which has sought non-election applications and would gain a hard new case (non-additive, shrunken functionals).

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: measure design for auditability (which measure constructions admit a margin statistic under shrinkage?); risk-limiting audit theory for non-additive model-based functionals, including confidence sequences on a shrunken estimator of audited units — a genuine open statistical problem, not a corollary; optimal allocation of a FIXED national audit budget across hospitals by margin rather than by targeting heuristics; the strategic question, since an audit with a stated risk and a real escalation changes the incentive to shade discretionary elements, connecting this to audit-deterrence and gaming literatures; the same analysis for eCQM and clinician-entered elements, and for AI-assisted abstraction, where the error model is a model property and could be specified in advance rather than estimated from re-abstraction.

Significance — anchoring:
Touches Shen 2021's misclassification finding (supplies the missing second error source); the reliability and minimum-volume debate in hospital profiling; the SEP-1 abstraction-variability complaint; the CMS star-rating methodology controversy and current release delays; HQIP/CQC outlier management, where the blank is documented in the guidance itself; and, on the method side, the open problem of risk-limiting audits for social-choice and estimation rules that are not plurality tallies.

Reason for silence:
A disciplinary boundary plus an incentive gap, with a technical delay on top.
(1) BOUNDARY. Health-services quality measurement inherited its error vocabulary from psychometrics and hierarchical modelling: reliability, ICC, shrinkage, misclassification under sampling noise. In that vocabulary the natural question is "how reliable is the measure", and it has been asked exhaustively. "Bound, by design, the probability that the DECISION stands uncorrected" is a different object, and it belongs to election auditing and financial audit sampling, which share no journals, conferences, reviewers or funders with hospital quality measurement. Query 9 is the evidence: the exact phrase "risk-limiting audit" inside the clinical vocabulary returns nothing clinical at all.
(2) INCENTIVE. The validation programmes are built for pay-for-reporting compliance — does the hospital submit accurate data? — and a stated-risk audit would create an obligation nobody in the programme wants to propose: escalate to complete re-abstraction, and reverse the payment decision when the audit says so. CMS bears the administrative cost; hospitals already have case-by-case appeals they can win without conceding a general standard; abstraction vendors sell element-agreement improvement, which a margin-proportional design would partly obsolete. HQIP's calendar-based escalation is what this looks like when nobody wants the evidential version.
(3) TECHNICAL DELAY. The machinery that makes the question answerable is young: RLAs matured roughly 2008-2020 and their generalisation beyond plurality tallies (SHANGRLA, confidence-sequence audits) is more recent still.
(4) A MISREADING that makes it look absurd. Posing the audit target as complete re-abstraction invites "you could never re-abstract every chart" — precisely the objection that delayed RLAs in elections ("you can't hand-count everything"), and precisely the objection a risk limit answers, since the full count is the escalation branch, not the plan.

Strongest remaining objection (for Gate 4):
Significance inflation via mandate mismatch. CMS validation exists to police data submission under pay-for-reporting, not to adjudicate individual penalty decisions; a programme designer may answer that decision risk is already handled upstream by measure-reliability and minimum-volume requirements, and that the correct response to an unconfirmable margin is to widen the reliability threshold, not to build an audit.
