Domain(s): Medicine — respiratory medicine / clinical pharmacology of inhaled drugs. Adjacent: regulatory science (OINDP bioequivalence); aerosol science; measurement-error epidemiology.

Question (canonical):
In an inhaled-corticosteroid dose-ranging design in which nominal dose is randomised and per-patient delivered lung dose is independently estimated (recorded inhalation profiles plus device-specific in-vitro flow-to-dose characterisation, with actuation counts for adherence), how much do the fitted dose–response curve's maximum slope and ED50 differ when the exposure variable is estimated delivered dose rather than assigned nominal dose; and of the gap, what share is attributable to (a) dispersion of the nominal→delivered multiplier, (b) pharmacodynamic saturation, and (c) between-patient heterogeneity in corticosteroid responsiveness — with a stated test of whether the delivery multiplier is independent of baseline severity?
(V2, the Gate 1 reformulation; the one permitted reformulation was used. V1 asked whether the reported ICS flatness is "partly an artefact" of randomising label dose, and was re-specified because it named classical measurement error as the mechanism, which is wrong under randomisation.)

Alternative formulations:
- Does the ICS therapeutic ceiling reflect receptor/pathway saturation, or dispersion in the exposure variable every inhaled dose-ranging trial has used?
- If published inhaled dose-ranging trials were re-analysed on estimated delivered dose instead of prescribed dose, how far would the apparent plateau move, and what factor separates the two slopes?

Motivating gap:
Two literatures sit side by side and are never joined. The flat ICS dose–response is reported on nominal dose (Powell & Gibson, MJA 2003: "relatively flat, with little difference seen between doses of 100 μg and 1000 μg of fluticasone per day"; Masoli et al. 2004: ~200 μg/d fluticasone gives >=90% of maximal efficacy), and is the stated reason clinical-endpoint BE studies for generic inhalers are insensitive. Delivered dose depends strongly on flow, device and technique, and is characterised in aerosol science with no clinical endpoint attached. Gate 4 sharpened the gap into a demonstrated disciplinary boundary: the extrafine-vs-fine meta-analysis states "The amount of ICS depositing in the lungs determines the clinical efficacy" and then analyses on nominal dose ("Mean prescribed drug doses were calculated in terms of nominal dose"), while Assi/Chrystyn (Int J Pharm 2006) measures per-patient delivered dose in 20 severe asthmatics and reports no clinical endpoint at all.

Presuppositions:
1. Flat dose–response above low nominal doses is real and replicated. ESTABLISHED (two sources opened; corroborated by both adversaries at title/venue level).
2. Per-patient delivered dose is estimable from recorded profiles plus in-vitro flow–dose curves. ESTABLISHED AS A METHOD, but the record's "newly possible" framing is FALSIFIED: Assi et al. 2006 did exactly this with an Electronic Lung twenty years ago. Separately, the instrument/estimand pairing is wrong as written — flow profile x in-vitro flow-to-dose identifies emitted/fine-particle dose, not deposited lung dose (adversary 1, attack 4).
3. Between-patient delivered-dose variance is large relative to the nominal dose steps. CONTRADICTED IN ITS STATED FORM on the only opened measurement: fluticasone/Diskus FPD 20.4% (SD 4.8) of label, CV ~24%, ±1 SD ~1.6-fold against a 4–10-fold ladder (budesonide/Turbuhaler CV ~56%, ~2.8-fold). Escape, plausible but unsourced this run: FPD omits airway deposition, so true lung-dose σ is a lower bound of unknown slackness.
4. Exposure error predominantly non-differential w.r.t. outcome. CONTESTED; retained as an explicit limb of V2, not assumed. Severity-correlated airway geometry makes it differential roughly by construction (adversary 1).
5. Classical measurement error / regression dilution applies. FALSE AS STATED, caught and corrected at Gate 1; randomised nominal dose makes this Berkson-type, and the mechanism is mixture-induced flattening (convolution of log-shifted sigmoids). Adversary 2 independently verified the correction (Young's inequality: smearing can only broaden a transition).
6. Saturation does not fully account for the plateau. CONTESTED; the rival to be weighed.
7. Responsiveness heterogeneity flattens a group-mean curve on its own, by the same mixture mechanism, and is the explanation the field already gives. Hence a three-way apportionment.
8. Nominal dose was dispensed and taken as assigned, so adherence is a separate term. CONTESTED; V2 requires actuation-level data.

What would count as an answer:
Either (i) an analysis — prospective, or re-analysis of an inhaled dose-ranging trial with recorded profiles — reporting the endpoint's curve fitted on estimated delivered dose with a CI alongside the nominal-dose curve, the max-slope and ED50 difference, and the three-way apportionment with uncertainty; or (ii) a quantitative bound showing measured dispersion of the nominal→delivered multiplier is too small to generate the observed flattening, which answers no. Either branch must report whether the multiplier is independent of baseline severity, and must state how (a) and (c) were separated, since both enter the individual curve as a log-dose shift and are not separately identifiable from endpoint data alone.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
- vs Masoli 2004: same object and same question verb ("why is it flat?"), but the exposure variable is nominal throughout and the answer offered is heterogeneity of pharmacodynamic response. No term for a slope ratio between two exposure definitions. Closest prior; this is what caps the grade at Medium.
- vs Assi 2006: performs the candidate's measurement and stops there — the dependent variable is emitted dose, not a clinical endpoint, and there is no dose ladder and no curve. It supplies the instrument and the magnitude input, and withdraws the record's "new instrument" claim, but poses no apportionment.
- vs the extrafine meta-analysis: the field's own closest question-shape (does response track delivered rather than labelled dose?) with a group-level number, but a different unit and estimand — two formulations' group means on nominal dose, no deposition percentage, no curve on delivered dose, no slope ratio, nothing on error in the dose variable. Adversary 2 checked it as a candidate kill and found it cannot adjudicate the mechanism, since both arms are themselves smeared populations walking along the already-convolved curve.
- vs Proost 2021: the mechanism, stated drug-agnostically and without the clinical object; it predicts marginal flattening and names the (a)/(c) identifiability obstruction, but computes no inhaled-specific apportionment. Its existence is why the triviality and reason-for-silence attacks bite.

Significance — consequence:
If yes (delivered-dose curve materially steeper, steepening not absorbed by the responsiveness term): the therapeutic ceiling becomes partly a property of the exposure variable, making clinical-endpoint insensitivity for OINDP BE a fixable design problem and making delivery optimisation a quantified alternative to dose escalation. If no (dispersion too small): the saturation/responsiveness account gains a number for the first time, supporting step-down guidance and the regulatory case for in-vitro plus PK surrogates. Both branches move a decision. Adversary 2 narrows the stake: the record's own Masoli datum caps the efficacy available to apportion at <=10% of maximal over 200→1000 μg, and smearing implies the individual ED90 sits below the group ED90, so a "yes" moves the ceiling down, not up.

Significance — audience:
(i) Regulatory inhalation scientists at FDA OGD and EMA, on record (Gate 2 query 4) wanting "the most sensitive dose-response approach", and demonstrably working on sources of variability and power for locally-acting PD equivalence; (ii) respiratory guideline committees whose dose-step tables encode the plateau (and, per adversary 2, the German/Spanish literature encodes it as equivalence tables on label dose); (iii) inhaler and smart-inhaler manufacturers, for whom an attenuation factor is the first efficacy-denominated argument for flow sensing; (iv) pharmacometricians in model-informed development for inhaled products.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens the same apportionment for inhaled bronchodilators, antibiotics and insulin-type products; opens the within-patient between-actuation reproducibility of the multiplier as a floor on any inhaled trial's power; opens randomising delivered rather than label dose, which no inhaled trial has done; opens the symmetric question for the side-effect curve, whose route differs; and, per adversary 2, opens splitting (c) into a potency limb and a responder/Emax limb, which no delivery correction can touch.

Significance — anchoring:
The ICS therapeutic-ceiling anomaly; insensitive clinical endpoints for orally inhaled products; the unresolved in-vitro/in-vivo correlation dispute (presupposition 2 is the same dispute, which is why a "yes" would be contested); and what randomisation does and does not protect when the randomised variable is a proxy for the exposure of interest.

Reason for silence:
Primary mechanism, a three-sided disciplinary boundary, SURVIVES and is better evidenced after Gate 4 than in the record: aerosol science holds per-patient delivered doses with no clinical endpoint (Assi 2006), clinical respiratory medicine holds the endpoints and analyses on nominal dose (extrafine meta-analysis, Powell & Gibson), and the correction machinery sits in measurement-error epidemiology, which has never been pointed at inhaled dosing (two independent negatives, in regression-calibration and in radiation-dosimetry vocabularies). The extrafine meta-analysis states both halves in one paragraph: deposited amount determines efficacy, and the analysis is on prescribed dose. Secondary mechanism, "new instrument", is WITHDRAWN: the capability is twenty years old, and the group that had it published the delivered doses without posing the apportionment. Third mechanism, incentive asymmetry (neither guideline writers nor generic sponsors benefit from reopening the flatness), is independent of the withdrawn leg and untouched.
