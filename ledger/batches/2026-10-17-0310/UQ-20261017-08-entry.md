Domain(s): AI architecture — open-release artifact engineering and the data layer (method transplanted from federated-learning / DP privacy auditing)

Question (canonical): For open model releases that ship optimizer state alongside weights, how much more does an adversary learn about the final phase of training — document membership, presence of a data source, or reconstructable content — from (weights + Adam moment tensors) than from weights alone at matched attack strength, and what fraction of major open-weight releases ship that state?

Alternative formulations:
1. Are Adam first/second-moment tensors a disclosure channel, or does their EMA aggregation over millions of tokens destroy the signal gradient-inversion attacks exploit?
2. Does the reproducibility norm of releasing resumable checkpoints widen the attack surface of an open release relative to releasing weights alone?
(Gate 1 reformulation retained, one permitted, spent on pinning the comparator: limb (a) CENSUS over a pre-declared frame; limb (b) MEASUREMENT of delta = AUC(P | theta,m,v) - AUC(P | theta) under one fixed pipeline P with (m,v) ablated at identical compute/shadow-model/query budgets, plus delta(k) decay and stated power of the null. Full text in batches/2026-10-17-0310/UQ-20261017-08-record.md.)

Motivating gap: In FL and DP auditing, "what does the shared update disclose about the data?" is a routine question with a mature method family; in open-release engineering the shipped artifact increasingly includes optimizer state for resumability and nobody asks what it discloses beyond the weights. Two instruments that would own the fraction (Model Openness Framework arXiv:2403.13784 I.5; Foundation Model Transparency Index arXiv:2310.12941) have no such field, indicator or count.

Significance — consequence: a positive, non-trivial delta puts resumability and disclosure in direct tension at the artifact level, with cheap specific mitigations (withhold or zero m, coarsen the step offset, publish state at a lag); a credible null at stated power removes a speculative privacy objection to the strongest form of open release. Note the Gate 5 correction: with betas [0.9, 0.95] in the actual releases, the "withhold m, ship v" mitigation loses the clean 10-vs-1000-step asymmetry the record claimed for it, so the mitigation space is narrower than argued.
Significance — audience: open-release engineering teams that ship resumable state (AI2/OLMo at ~1000-step granularity; EleutherAI for gpt-neox and Pythia final-step; BigScience for BLOOM/BLOOMZ) and hub-side policy; openness-framework authors (MOF I.5 has the component with no disclosure guidance, FMTI has no indicator); privacy auditors, who gain a white-box threat model strictly between weights-only and full trajectory that exists in the wild. Reason for silence: a disciplinary boundary with a mismatched threat model on each side, plus an incentive gradient. The method-owning communities (FL privacy, gradient inversion, DP auditing, unlearning, TDA) demonstrably know the object carries information — they decouple it, entangle it, privatise it, and feed {m_t,v_t} into attribution — but each frames it as in-transit, in-training or in-accounting, never as a published object; the artifact-owning side frames it as storage cost and demand (Pythia README) or reproducibility (Amber), and has no field in which the gap could be stated (MOF, FMTI). Adversary #2 searched specifically for dismissive folklore ("aggregation obviously destroys it") and found none, so "asked and dismissed informally" is unevidenced. Verification (post-batch, two fresh-context checkers plus a direct read, logs UQ-20261017-08-verification.md and -verification-np2.md): NP2 is still unread, but its abstract has now been read three times independently and it excludes the released-checkpoint framing at abstract level. The verdict does not move; the cap narrows from "the one thing that would make this a disguised known is unverified" to a residual confined to the paper's body.
**What the abstract settles.** "Gradient Inversion Attacks Beyond SGD" (Wan, Fang, Ma & Wang; ICML 2026 poster 64051; OpenReview RNDJpm1kUW) frames its threat as federated learning in which an adaptive optimizer is used, so that "the observable signal is not raw gradients but momentum-based parameter updates" — updates exchanged during training and seen by a server or eavesdropper, not tensors read out of a published checkpoint. Its contributions are an analytical label-recovery rule from those updates and an update-matching objective over dummy inputs, said to strengthen plain-SGD attacks as well. Experiments are on "datasets such as ImageNet and PACS", both image datasets; no text or language-model experiment appears in the abstract. A keyword sweep of the abstract on a third read returns ABSENT for every one of checkpoint, released, published, optimizer state, moment (as a word), second moment, m_t and v_t.
That bears directly on the entry's strongest objection. NP2's observable is a sequence of updates during training; the candidate's is a single released checkpoint's stored first and second moment buffers, with a weights-only arm at matched attack strength. NP2's regime is small-batch image reconstruction; the candidate's is an un-noised EMA over ~2e6-token text batches with membership and source-presence endpoints. On the abstract, NP2 is a neighbour in the FL gradient-leakage line rather than the candidate's question in other words.
