# UQ-20261016-21 (device firmware era in RCTs) — Adversary #1 log

Budget used: 9/9 web searches, 4/4 fetches. Searching stopped at the cap; no workarounds attempted.

## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)
Take all published multicentre RCTs whose primary endpoint is produced or titrated by a device that runs software, and whose report names the device model. (i) For how many of them can an outsider, working from vendor release notes, FDA/EU recall and incident databases, direct vendor enquiry or the trial report itself, prove that the vendor changed the device's firmware or calibration algorithm while patients were still being enrolled? (ii) In those trials where that can be proved AND the patient-level data can be re-run, does the treatment effect in the "old firmware" patients differ from that in the "new firmware" patients by more than the trial's own minimum clinically important difference (or, failing an MCID, by more than half the width of the trial's primary CI), after noting how far firmware era is just a proxy for calendar time? The motivating case is BOOST-II, where a Masimo oximeter algorithm revision halfway through enrolment coincided with the mortality effect appearing only in the revised-algorithm era.

## Recommended verdict
Provisional at best (no attack Unresolved; Partial on 2, 4, 5, 7, 8). Novelty must be regraded: the record's "no cross-trial reanalysis" is false (Askie 2018 NeOProM is a pre-specified five-trial software-era interaction, P=.03), the GAP statement must be narrowed for CONSORT-EHEALTH and the FDA DHT guidance, and the "null would retire the concern" sentence contradicts the lower-bound framing. Novelty confidence should drop to Low-Medium: the census across device classes is still un-asked as far as nine queries can tell, but the only device class with a demonstrated mechanism has already had both limbs done for it by the trialists.

Strongest single killing point: the "beyond the index trial" interaction question that the record treats as un-asked was asked, pre-specified and answered across five trials (three with mid-enrolment software changes) in NeOProM (Askie et al., JAMA 2018: interaction P=.03 for death), leaving only a census whose retrieval limb is, by the record's own words, "an upper bound on nothing" and whose interaction limb needs per-site install logs that none of its retrieval routes can supply.
