## Restatement in my own words

A tree, a coral colony or a clonal plant is not one tissue but many modules (branches, polyps/fragments, ramets), and somatic mutation makes those modules genetically non-identical. Population genomics nevertheless takes ONE module per organism and reports per-individual heterozygosity, pairwise kinship, allele frequencies and LD-based Ne as if the organism had a single genotype. The question asks us to treat "which module got sampled" as a measurement-error factor and measure its size: run a crossed design (many individuals x >=3 modules each, matched depth, module provenance recorded, in a tree and in a colonial/clonal marine organism), estimate for each of four named estimands the between-module-within-individual variance component against the between-individual component, with intervals, with a technical component estimated from replicate libraries and chimeras classified separately from mosaics. In short: is the number the field reports partly a function of which branch the sampler could reach, and by how much, per estimand? A measured near-zero share is a real answer (it licenses current practice); the question is a variance question, not a biology-of-mosaicism question.
