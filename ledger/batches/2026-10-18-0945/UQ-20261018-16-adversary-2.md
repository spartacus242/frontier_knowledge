## Restatement (my words, before reading the generator's framing twice)

Take every microbial strain that two or more independent public culture collections advertise as the same strain (ATCC = DSMZ = NCTC = CIP = JCM ... cross-references, or StrainInfo identifier links). Fix that set first. Then buy the accession from each collection, sequence the material each one actually ships, and ask three things: how far apart are two collections' holdings of "one" strain (split into SNVs, indels, IS movement, plasmids, prophage); in what share of pairs is that distance bigger than the distance between two ampoules of a single lot from a single collection, measured in the same study with the same pipeline; and does the distance grow with the number of subcultures and the years since the strain was deposited. Side product: how often "equivalent" turns out to mean two different original isolates that were wrongly linked, rather than one lineage that drifted apart.

So: is "the same strain" one organism worldwide, and if not, is the drift a property of custodial history that can be modelled?
