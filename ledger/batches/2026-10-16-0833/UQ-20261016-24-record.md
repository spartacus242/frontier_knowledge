Domain(s): STEM — quantum computing (cloud-accessible superconducting/trapped-ion processors); metascience of hardware benchmarking; measurement/metrology of reported results

Question (canonical, as intaken): Joining the public per-device daily calibration archives of cloud-accessible quantum processors to the run dates of published results obtained on them, are published results drawn disproportionately from favourable calibration epochs — that is, does each device's own figure of merit at the reported run time sit systematically above its distribution over the surrounding period?

Question (canonical, Gate 1 reformulation — the version to carry forward; the single permitted reformulation, made only for well-posedness, not scope): For a pre-specified corpus of papers reporting results executed on named cloud-accessible quantum processors, and a pre-specified per-device figure of merit F built from archived calibration fields whose definition is stable across a declared comparability window (same hardware revision, same recalibration methodology; e.g. median two-qubit gate error over the qubits actually used, falling back to a declared device-level aggregate when the qubit subset is unreported): (a) what fraction of published results can be located in time to the archive's granularity at all, and what fraction of those fall inside a window the vendor archive still covers; and (b) among the locatable-and-covered results, is the distribution of the percentile rank of F at the reported execution window — computed within a pre-specified symmetric comparison window around that window (e.g. ±30 days, truncated at revision boundaries) — uniform on [0,1], or shifted towards favourable ranks, and by how much, reported both as a shift in expected device quality and, for the subset of result types where the mapping from F to the headline number is monotone and pre-declared, as the implied shift in that number?

Both versions recorded per Gate 1. The reformulation changes nothing substantive: it pins the estimand (percentile rank within a declared comparison window), names the denominator of the locatability limb, and makes the figure-of-merit construction and revision-boundary truncation pre-specified rather than left to the analyst. Confirmed to be a question, not a thesis: the null (uniform ranks) is a fully acceptable and reportable outcome, and the record explicitly disclaims the misconduct reading.

Alternative formulations:
- Do quantum-computing papers report runs made when the machine happened to be good, and can that selection be measured from the vendor's own calibration history?
- What is the percentile rank, within a device's own recent calibration record, of the device state behind each published result obtained on it?

Motivating gap: New-instrument candidate. Vendors of cloud-accessible processors publish per-qubit calibration data (T1, T2, single- and two-qubit error rates, readout fidelity) retrievable for past dates, while papers name the backend and often a date or window — so the device's physical state at the moment a published result was produced is, for the first time, an archivally recoverable number. Drift on these devices is substantial over hours to days and is itself well characterised (arXiv:2105.09472; arXiv:2208.07219; "Temporal Analysis of Quantum Errors in NISQ Computers", IEEE DataPort; the long-term IBM calibration study at caslab.io/publications/xu2023long.pdf), and users reasonably re-run when a device "looks bad" — error-aware layout and just-in-time transpilation tools (arXiv:2606.27508; arXiv:2005.12820) are built precisely to exploit the current calibration state. That sensible practice is also a selection mechanism acting on the published record. Gate 2 found the normative half of this already stated in the candidate's own field — arXiv:2411.08860 names post-hoc selection of best results as a reporting failure ("from which best results are selected post hoc", Principle 4) — but found no attempt to measure it, and no join between a vendor calibration archive and a literature corpus. The gap is therefore narrower than intaken: the worry is on record; the quantity is not.

Presuppositions:
- P1 (CONTESTED, load-bearing): historical calibration data are retrievable for the periods the published results fall in. Vendor retention depth is a policy matter and may be short; the feasible study may be restricted to recent years or to devices whose archives were independently mirrored. If retention is too shallow, the question is well-posed but not yet answerable and should be routed rather than attempted. Gate 2 did not verify retention depth for any vendor (budget exhausted); this remains unchecked and is the first thing Gate 4 or any first step must settle.
- P2 (CONTESTED): published papers report enough detail to locate a run in time. Many report only a backend name; the locatable fraction is itself a reportable quantity and bounds the achievable sample size. Partially addressable from an existing corpus (arXiv:2607.08348 screened 127 papers manually and ~5,000 automatically on reproducibility indicators including a "hardware description" item), but the fetched text of that paper reports no backend/date/calibration-metadata fraction, so the number is not in hand.
- P3 (CONTESTED): a figure of merit comparable across time can be constructed from the archived fields. Recalibration methodology, qubit retirement and hardware revisions change what the fields mean, so comparability windows must be pre-specified and truncated at revision boundaries.
- P4: the null is well defined — absent selection, run-time percentile ranks are uniform over the pre-specified comparison window. Holds by construction of the rank, but only if the comparison window is fixed before any rank is computed, and only under exchangeability of calendar time within the window (see strongest objection: queue access, reservation windows and vendor pre-demonstration tuning are candidate violations of exchangeability that are not author-side selection).
- P5: papers' headline numbers depend monotonically on the chosen figure of merit for at least a subset of result types, so a rank shift translates into an implied shift in the reported number. Supported for at least one result class by Senapati et al., QCE 2023 (correlation between the authors' own daily QML test accuracy and hardware calibration features), but on their own runs, not across the literature; the monotone subset must be declared in advance.
- P6 (NOT presupposed, stated to block a misreading): that any selection is misconduct, or that authors concealed re-runs. The question is the magnitude of an incentive-driven selection on the published record, whatever its channel.

What would count as an answer: A pre-registered pipeline over a named paper sample and named backends reporting, per result, the percentile rank of F at the reported run window within the pre-specified comparison window; the test is uniformity of those ranks (KS, or a one-sided mean-rank test with a pre-stated effect size), with the effect reported both as a shift in expected device quality and, where the mapping is monotone, as the implied shift in the paper's headline number. "Ranks uniform" retires the concern and is a full answer. "Ranks concentrated high" yields a quantified reproducibility correction factor that referees, vendors and benchmarking consortia could require authors to report (run timestamp plus contemporaneous calibration snapshot). The fraction of papers that cannot be located in time at all is part of the answer, not a failure of it. A demonstration that archive retention does not reach the literature is a feasibility verdict, not an answer, and must be reported as such.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
- vs arXiv:2411.08860: that paper asserts the practice and prescribes disclosure; it defines no estimand, uses no archive, and measures nothing. The candidate converts the assertion into a pre-registered, falsifiable quantity with an explicit null, and makes the archive — not author self-report — the evidence. The difference is real but it narrows the gap materially: the question is not unthought-of in its own field, it is unoperationalised, so novelty cannot be graded High.
- vs arXiv:2609.27704: same family (selection bias inflating hardware results), different selection channel and different evidence. Larrucea's selection acts within one lab's own optimisation at finite shots, and the evidence is prospective re-measurement; drift appears there as a *confounder to be differenced away*. The candidate makes drift the selection variable itself, acts at the level of the published record rather than one campaign, and is retrospective and archival, so it needs no new device time and covers papers whose authors are not participating.
- vs arXiv:2607.08348: that work measures artefact-side reproducibility indicators (does the paper ship code, an environment, a hardware description, an executable pipeline). It does not recover the device's physical state at run time, which is the candidate's whole object. The candidate's locatability limb (P2) is adjacent to that corpus and should be built on it rather than duplicate it — and because the fetched text reports no such fraction, the limb is not yet answered elsewhere.
- vs the process-record-join family (ESO/XMM/animal-ethics): those measure *whether* allocated resource produced a publication — a binary or count, unconditional on publication. The candidate measures a continuous instrument-state percentile *conditional* on publication. That inverts the conditioning and, unlike UQ-20261016-19's ratio, keeps numerator and denominator in the same units (the same archive field at different times), which is why the commensurability defect that killed the sibling entry does not apply here.

Significance — consequence: If ranks are shifted high, the output is a correction factor on the published NISQ record with a concrete, cheap reform attached: require a run timestamp plus the contemporaneous calibration snapshot, which vendors already serve and which turns an unauditable claim into an auditable one. It would also give a quantitative, archive-based account of a standing puzzle — why hardware results replicate poorly across groups and across time — that currently rests on assertion (F2) and on one campaign's prospective re-measurement (F4). If ranks are uniform, a live accusation in the field's own methodology literature is retired with data rather than argued about, which is worth having precisely because the accusation is already being made rhetorically; it would also redirect the reproducibility effort towards the artefact-side failures already measured (24.4% code availability, 64.5% non-execution, F1). Either way the locatability fraction (P2) is a usable number on its own: it bounds what any retrospective audit of the NISQ literature can ever establish.

Significance — audience: (i) Benchmarking standard-setters — the QED-C/Metriq and benchmark-standardisation community (arXiv:2303.02108), and the IEEE QCE reproducibility track where F5 and the drift-characterisation work are published — would act by adding a timestamp-plus-snapshot field to reporting checklists, a change that costs an API call. (ii) Vendors (IBM, Google, IonQ, Rigetti, Quantinuum) set the archive retention policy on which P1 depends and publish the calibration-metric documentation that query 12 surfaced; a measured shift gives them a reason to lengthen retention and publish immutable snapshots, and a null gives them a defence of the current record. (iii) Referees and editors in quantum information, who presently have no way to ask "was this a good week for that machine?" (iv) The reproducibility-measurement group behind F1, whose five-indicator framework the candidate extends with a sixth, physically grounded indicator. (v) Metascientists studying hardware-dependent reproducibility, who do not currently know these archives are queryable by date.

Significance — fecundity: Opens (a) the same test on error-mitigation overhead and advantage claims, where sensitivity to device state is largest; (b) a tractable mechanism sub-question — do reserved/dedicated-access runs (a priced vendor product) show a larger rank shift than open-queue runs, which separates one innocent channel from author-side selection without touching intent; (c) whether the rank shift predicts cross-group replication failure, making the correction factor testable rather than merely descriptive; (d) whether vendors tune devices ahead of demonstration campaigns, which the same archive can show directly as a calendar effect around announcement dates; (e) transplant of the measurable itself — the state-percentile-at-publication statistic — to GPU-cluster benchmark claims, accelerator beam quality, telescope observing conditions and clinical-assay instrument state, none of which, per queries 5 and 6, currently compute it.

Significance — anchoring: The NISQ reproducibility problem as currently documented (F1's execution failures; F5's accuracy/calibration correlation); the drift-characterisation literature (arXiv:2105.09472, arXiv:2208.07219, the IEEE DataPort temporal-error study, the long-term IBM calibration study), which supplies the data and stops at the device; the quantum-advantage replication disputes, where "which run, when" has repeatedly been the crux; benchmark standardisation (arXiv:2303.02108) and vendor control-plane openness (arXiv:2605.15233), which decide whether the needed metadata exists; and the winner's-curse result (F4), which would acquire a publication-level counterpart. Via prior 5 it also anchors to the facility-records-to-publications tradition in astronomy and preclinical biology.

Reason for silence: Two mechanisms, both structural, neither "no one thought of it".
(1) Disciplinary boundary with an asymmetric readership. The calibration archives are an operations artefact maintained so that users can pick qubits *now*: the tools built on them (error-aware layout, just-in-time transpilation — query 13) all read the current snapshot, and the people fluent in them are compiler and hardware researchers. The people who study publication-level selection effects work on artefacts — code, text, p-values, metadata (F1, prior 8/Neves & Amaral) — and have no reason to know that these archives are addressable by past date. The join requires one party to care about the other's object, and the two parties do not read each other. This is the same boundary the astronomy and preclinical process-record studies had to cross, and there it took an insider (ESO analysing its own programmes) to cross it.
(2) Incentive and custodianship gap. The only actors with frictionless access to deep archive history are the vendors, who would be publishing a statistic about selection in their customers' papers obtained on their machines; the only actors with a motive are outsiders, for whom retrieval depends on retention policy the vendors control and do not advertise (P1, unverified). Meanwhile the field's own methodology literature discharged the concern rhetorically (F2, Principle 4) rather than measurably, which removes the pressure that usually converts a named sin into a measurement programme: once a practice has been condemned in print, quantifying it looks like re-litigating a settled point rather than opening a new one.
Weaker, supporting: the data are nominally public but practically latent — reconstructing a historical snapshot means per-date API calls or a mirrored dump, with no advertised bulk-history endpoint, so the archive behaves like a new instrument that has been switched on for years without anyone pointing it at the literature.

- Well-posedness 2/3, not 3/3. Promotion to 3/3 requires: a verified retention audit for at least two vendors showing coverage of a usable paper window, and a pre-registered F shown to be comparable across at least one documented recalibration-methodology change.
- Significance 2/3, not 3/3. Consequence, fecundity and anchoring hold, and the reform implied by a positive result is unusually cheap. Two deductions: the audience's demonstrated responsiveness is weak — the ledger's own verification of UQ-20261016-19 found that the two closest process-record-to-publication precedents (26% of approved animals unreported; 47% non-publishing ESO programmes) produced no reporting-rule change — and a positive result would be a composite of author-side selection, queue and reservation access patterns, and vendor pre-demonstration tuning, which is still a usable correction factor but is not the clean attribution the framing invites. Strongest objection to carry into Gate 4 (stated here so the adversary is not handed a softened record): calendar time inside the comparison window is probably not exchangeable, for reasons that are not author-side selection. Devices are tuned hard around commissioning, upgrades and announcement campaigns; reserved and dedicated access — which serious published work disproportionately uses, and which is a priced product — is itself scheduled into good periods by the vendor; and queue availability correlates with device state. Any of these produces high ranks with no author choosing anything, so a positive result measures "published results come from better-than-typical device states" and not the incentive-driven selection the motivating gap describes. The candidate's disclaimer (P6) partly absorbs this by declining to attribute, but the significance case leans on the attributed reading, so the objection bites on Gate 3 even where it spares Gate 1.
