## 1. Restatement (my words, not the generator's)

Trees, corals and clonal plants are built of repeated parts, and each part carries its own somatic mutations. Population geneticists sequence one part per individual and report heterozygosity, kinship, allele frequencies and an LD-based Ne. If a different branch / nubbin / ramet of the same individual had been sequenced, how much would those four numbers move? Expressed as a variance component (between-module-within-individual) relative to the between-individual component, per estimand, with an interval, after a technical component is subtracted using replicate libraries and after fused-genotype chimeras are set aside from somatic mosaics; measured in at least one tree and one coral/clonal marine organism on an N individuals x M>=3 modules crossed design.

Load-bearing structure as I see it: the question is only non-trivial if (a) somatic discordance between modules is large relative to the heterozygous-site denominator and (b) that discordance is separable from technical error at the depth the field uses. The candidate's own record names (a) as "the crux" and (b) as "contested".

## 4. Evidence read, with what it does and does not contain

(a) Schmid-Siegert et al. (Napoleon Oak, Quercus robur; bioRxiv full text, OPENED). Two terminal branches at ~40X and ~42X WGS, meristems "exposed for 234 years"; "increasing the total number of confirmed SNVs separating the two genomes to 17"; "All confirmed SNVs were heterozygous, as expected for novel somatic mutations"; authors' higher-end estimate of total oak somatic SNVs "ranges from 46.9 to 113"; "We identified 1,832,554 heterozygous sites common to both samples"; ~650.6 Mb searchable assembly; candidate pipeline: 314,865 initial candidates, 1,536 assessed, "Only seven of the 1,536 candidates assessed were validated by PCR-seq"; "we did not assess the presence of larger somatic changes such as copy number variants"; no population-genetic estimate reported.
   MY OWN ARITHMETIC (labelled; not the authors' conclusion): the per-individual observed heterozygosity computed from branch A vs branch B differs by at most the number of branch-specific fixed somatic heterozygous sites over the heterozygous-site denominator: 17 / 1,832,554 = 9.3e-6, or 113 / 1,832,554 = 6.2e-5 at the authors' higher-end total. Uncertainty carried: (i) the denominator counts sites heterozygous in BOTH branches, so the true per-branch denominator is slightly larger (makes the ratio smaller); (ii) indels, CNVs and TE insertions were not assessed (could add some count, but to reach even a 1e-3 relative shift would require ~1,800 additional fixed somatic events, >15x the authors' upper bound for SNVs); (iii) one tree, two branches, one species. Scope: this is the candidate's "long-lived tree" taxon at the depth needed to call somatic variants; it is exactly the regime the record flagged as its Triviality exposure. A kinship estimator that averages over the same sites cannot shift by more than the changed-site fraction times the maximum per-site weight; with standard MAF filtering the ≤113 branch-specific singletons are removed outright, and the allele-frequency and LD-Ne limbs then see zero module effect (the MAF/Pcrit-filtering practice is my recall of standard pipelines, not sourced here, and is not load-bearing for any status below).
   Technical-vs-somatic order of magnitude (own arithmetic from the authors' counts): 314,865 candidate discordant sites vs 17 confirmed — roughly four orders of magnitude of technical discordance above the somatic signal in a standard caller at 40X.

(b) Oury et al. 2024, Scientific Reports, "Investigating the potential roles of intra-colonial genetic variability in Pocillopora corals using genomics" (nature.com, OPENED — the paper the record could not read). Design: 96 colonies sampled, 93 analysed; "The three nubbins (< 1 cm3 each) within a colony were collected from apical branch tips"; target capture of 1,248 UCE + 1,385 exon loci; "276 nubbins (without replicates) and 59,032 SNPs with 40.9% missing data"; mean depth 46.3X; extraction/sequencing replicates included. Results: 18/93 colonies (~19%) with IGV = 7 mosaics + 11 chimeras; Gaussian-mixture antimode at 1.0% dissimilarity "defined as the threshold to distinguish colonies presenting IGV or not"; "the numbers of IGV and mosaic colonies were extremely sensitive to the threshold defined"; chimeric nubbin pairs account for "more than half of the allelic differences found" (58.8%; 30,799 SNPs from 21 chimeric pairs); chimeric colonies' maximal distance "about 10 times higher than other colonies"; mosaic pairs: 80,529 allelic differences in 14,324 SNPs; "A single allelic difference cannot evidence IGV"; "Only one representative of each clonal lineage was kept for assignment tests" (15 clonal lineages at a 1% threshold). VERIFIED ABSENT: no heterozygosity, allele-frequency, Fst, kinship, Ne, AMOVA or nested variance analysis; no re-run of any analysis with a different within-colony nubbin; no sampling-design recommendation beyond the threshold definition and "the inclusion of sequencing replicates". Stated "roles" of IGV are adaptive (diversity, plasticity, "a potential lifeline and a source of genetic innovation").
   MY OWN INFERENCE (labelled, NOT load-bearing for any status): if the 80,529 mosaic allelic differences are spread over ~21 mosaic nubbin pairs (7 colonies x 3 pairs; pair count not confirmed from the page), that is ~3,800 differences per pair, ~6.5% of 59,032 SNPs, inside a capture target of a few Mb — a density of order 1e-3 per bp, three to four orders of magnitude above what somatic point mutation produced in the oak over 234 years (17-113 events over 650 Mb). Whatever the "mosaic" class in this dataset is, most of it is not somatic point mutation; the authors' own sentence that the counts are "extremely sensitive to the threshold" points the same way.

(c) Bailleul, Stoeckel & Arnaud-Haond 2016, RClone, Methods Ecol Evol (archimer PDF, OPENED). "slightly distinct MLGs may belong to the same clonal lineage"; the package exists "to define clusters of MLGs belonging to the same genet"; it simulates "a pseudo-observed generation of pure sexual reproduction" to set the distance threshold; "somatic mutations could lead to the first peaks of the synthetic distribution"; it provides "genotypic richness and evenness indices calculation based on MLGs or MLLs". No quantified bias figures; no discussion of which ramet to sample. Cites Arnaud-Haond et al. 2007a for the MLL concept.
