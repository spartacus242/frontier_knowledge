## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Take a big molecular machine whose bound states you can literally count, one particle at a time, inside a frozen intact cell by cryo-ET. Ask: is the fraction of particles you count in each bound state the fraction you would have computed from a test-tube binding constant plus the concentration of free ligand actually available inside that cell? And if it is not: across several different machines, does the mismatch point the same way and by about the same amount (so there is one "cellular correction factor" modellers could apply), or is it different for every machine (so no correction transfers and each one must be measured)?

Stripped down: the candidate is a prediction-versus-measurement test of in-vitro-to-in-cell parameter transfer, where the novel element is not the comparison but the instrument supplying the observed term (particle counting in situ rather than a spectroscopic or assay readout).

## What the three opened papers actually say

Metalation calculators (bioRxiv 2022.05.06.490408): the calculators "predict metal occupancies based upon a population of intracellular metal-buffering sites", with "Metal affinities ... entered as dissociation constants" and availabilities derived from metal-sensor responses ("Buffered concentrations of available metals were thus derived from their established relationships"). So the three-term assembly the record says nobody performs — in vitro Kd, a measured intracellular available/free ligand concentration, and in-cell occupancy — is performed here, across many proteins, as a published tool. Comparison with observed in-cell metalation is case-by-case and qualitative: HypB "reassuringly predict[ed] ... predominantly metalated" with no observed occupancy reported; SodA predicted at 3.4% Fe occupancy aerobically against reported Fe mis-metalation, which the authors attribute to the affinity inputs ("The reported affinities do not follow the Irving-Williams series") and which a single altered Mn affinity moves to 72.9% predicted Fe occupancy. Occupancy predictions are point values; standard deviations are propagated only into the availability/free-energy terms ("Standard deviations were calculated based upon the triplicated determinations of buffered concentration"), not into occupancy. Disagreement is treated diagnostically: "Simulations using the metalation calculators might predict aberrant metalation that is indicative of erroneous values."

Acta Cryst D 2021 (in-cell NMR, carbonic anhydrase II + inhibitors in human cells): intracellular Kd values are determined in living cells from "fractions of CA II bound to each ligand" and compared with in vitro values for the same pairs; the paper's own verdict is "The intracellular affinities were similar to those obtained in vitro." Reported values (Table 3, as extracted): MZA 37 nM in vitro and in cells; SLC 79 +/- 3 in vitro vs 95 +/- 8 and 111 +/- 11 in cells; AAZ 15 vs 18 +/- 1; ETZ 0.45 +/- 0.02 in vitro vs 1.6 +/- 0.3 and 3.7 +/- 0.6 in cells. MY ARITHMETIC on those stated values, propagating the stated SDs as relative errors in quadrature: ETZ is weaker in cells by 3.6 +/- 0.7x and 8.2 +/- 1.4x; SLC by 1.20 +/- 0.11x and 1.41 +/- 0.15x. The paper itself attributes the ETZ gap to incomplete equilibration at low external ETZ, so the 8x is not the authors' conclusion and I do not use it as one. Critically for the candidate: intracellular FREE ligand is NOT measured — the method either holds free ligand "constant and known (i.e. in the NMR bioreactor)" or assumes "both ligands are in a large molar excess with respect to the protein". So this line measures the observed occupancy and the in vitro Kd but skips the candidate's hardest term.

Nano Lett. 2024 (Simoncelli, SMLM): "we directly determine protein-protein association (Ka) and dissociation (Kd) constants in cellular environments"; fractional occupancy appears as "the probability that one position is coordinated". Two systems only (CD3zeta self-association as a 2D Ka; pCD3zeta-pZAP70 as a pseudo-3D Kd from "nine FOVs and 11 cells"), with in-cell Kd "(1.0 +/- 0.4) microM" and "(0.6 +/- 0.2) microM" contrasted against "the stronger nanomolar-range affinity observed for ZAP70 in SPR experiments". The paper states no fold-change; the ~3-orders figure is MY inference from the two stated ranges and is of doubtful meaning anyway, since a 2D/pseudo-3D in-cell constant and a 3D SPR constant do not share units. Not framed as a transferability test, and no error budget over labelling efficiency, localisation precision or proximity threshold.
