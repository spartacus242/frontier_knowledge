# UQ-20261017-30 — Adversary 1 log

## Restatement (my own words, before reading the record's framing as authoritative)
Across published LLM/agent benchmark runs that ship per-item logs with error fields, how much of the scored item pool failed because of evaluation plumbing (timeouts, rate limits, container crashes, parse failures) rather than because the model answered wrongly; does the choice of handling those plumbing failures — exclude the item, count it wrong, or rerun until it clears — move headline scores and model orderings beyond resampling noise; does plumbing failure hit some models more than others (making exclusion biased, not just lossy); and how many publications even say what they did?

## Pages opened (4 fetches) and what they say — quotes are as returned by the fetch tool's extraction of the page text
1. arXiv:2605.21404 (twelve-paper disclosure audit). Five schema fields: benchmark identity, harness specification, inference settings, cost reporting, failure breakdown. The only failure-related field is "Failure breakdown: a structured taxonomy of why an agent failed each task it failed", with example categories "environment error", "time-out", "grader timeout", "tool-use error". Table III: disclosed 3/12 (AgentBench, HumanEval, MBPP), partial 4/12, absent 5/12 (SWE-bench, OSWorld, GAIA, MLE-bench, GSM8K), mean 0.42. NO criterion on how errored runs enter the denominator; no retry-policy or error-rate figures. So limb (e) as worded (fraction STATING the convention) is NOT answered here; a near-neighbour (fraction disclosing structured failure counts) is.
2. https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/scoring-policy.html (Inspect AI, UK AISI harness). Scorer exceptions "recorded in sample.error", the sample produces no score; worked-example table has a column "Counts in denominator?" with the errored sample marked "no (errored)"; with one errored sample of four, accuracy = 1/2 with scored_samples = 2, unscored_samples = 1. `score_on_error` "runs the scorer on whatever partial state was reached and counts the result" and the example states accuracy "would read 1 / 3" — i.e. the page documents BOTH candidate estimators (drop vs score-as-failure), names which is the default, reports the unscored count separately, and shows the score moving between them. `retry_on_error` and `fail_on_error` (default True aborts on first error; numeric sets a tolerance) are the retry/abort conventions. The page does not compare conventions empirically.
3. arXiv:2607.09510 (Failure as a Process, Terminal-Bench, 21 model-scaffold systems). "From a raw pool of 3,843 executions, we retain the 89 tasks completed by all 21 model–scaffold systems, yielding 1,794 valid trajectories"; "1,197 runs terminated abnormally or failed to complete" out of a theoretical 5,040, which "mainly stem from infrastructure and harness conditions" and were EXCLUDED; "Dropping timeout and incomplete trajectories may introduce bias, but such trajectories are outside our study scope." Environment root-cause share among the 1,184 failed trajectories: 9.4%. MY ARITHMETIC: 1,197/5,040 = 23.7% of the theoretical grid lost to abnormal termination (the paper does not state this percentage and gives no uncertainty; the 5,040 is a theoretical count, not realised runs).
4. arXiv:2605.12131 (Rollout Cards: A Reproducibility Standard for Agent Research). Per the fetched text: the spec has NO field requiring disclosure of how failed/errored rollouts are handled or of an error rate (closest: failure status in the rollout record, a "drops manifest" of filters/exclusions applied). Its audit of 50 repositories: "None of the 50 repositories reports how often rollouts failed alongside the accuracy or score"; "11 scored entries silently absorb catastrophic failures into the reported score"; "31 drop failures with no visible counter"; "9 log failures but do not surface them beside the reported score." (Counts over 51 scored entries since simple-evals is split.) Last ~11k characters of the page not read.

## Unit-of-analysis check (the ledger's commonest error)
Candidate's unit: the evaluation ITEM within a published evaluation; endpoint: score and rank under two estimators plus one separate estimand; estimand contrast: drop vs score-as-failure on the same items; limb (e) unit: the publication.
- Rollout Cards' audit unit is the REPOSITORY (what the code does), not the publication (what the paper states). It classifies de facto conventions into absorb / drop / log-only and reports the share of each; it does not recompute scores. Same object (failure-handling convention in evaluation pipelines), different unit from limb (e) — adjacent, not identical, and I grade it as such.
- Inspect's page is a single harness's policy, not a cross-evaluation study; it shows the two estimators are named, defaulted and documented in the field.
- Failure as a Process is a single-benchmark anatomy that applies the drop convention plus listwise deletion; it is evidence the share is large and the convention set exceeds three, not a convention comparison.
None of the opened pages computes the candidate's limb (b) — counted pairwise rank reversals between drop and score-as-failure across published evaluations. That residue is real and I do not claim otherwise.
