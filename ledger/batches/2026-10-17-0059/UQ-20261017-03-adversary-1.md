# UQ-20261017-03 — Adversary #1 log

## Restatement (my words)
Conjecture engines such as the Ramanujan Machine accept a formula when it matches a target constant to d digits. Of the formulas they have accepted and that anyone has since adjudicated, what fraction turned out to be false coincidences (not rediscoveries, not open) — and is that fraction consistent with the chance count M·10^-d you would predict from how many formulas (M) the engine actually tried? Reformulated version restricts to engines where both d and M are recoverable (by report or by re-running released code) and asks for a censored interval on the non-identity share versus M·10^-d.

## My own arithmetic (labelled)
RM's quoted figures: enumeration 10^9, >50 digits -> 10^9 × 10^-50 = 10^-41, consistent with the paper's "<10^-40" (so the per-candidate bound is a straight product, i.e. it is already the Bonferroni/expected-count form). At the acceptance precisions actually used (RM Fig. 3: 100 digits; ESMA: 1000 digits), even a wildly generous M = 10^20 gives expected false count ≤ 10^-80. No uncertainty is attached to these inputs by the sources; they are order-of-magnitude statements, and my figures inherit that status.
