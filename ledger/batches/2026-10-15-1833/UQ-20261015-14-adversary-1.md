## Restatement (my words, not the record's)

Put a known number of people in a room, run their air past a HEPA unit some of the time,
and measure two things at once: how much human DNA is floating in the air (split by
particle size, and split into a "came out of a mouth" portion and a "came off skin"
portion) and how much of the air has already been breathed by someone, inferred from CO2.
Divide the first by the second. The claim under test is that this quotient behaves the way
a real shared-breath exposure tracer would have to behave: it should not care how many
people are in the room (because both numerator and denominator are per-person), but it
should drop when the filter runs and should differ between particle sizes and between the
mouth-derived and skin-derived portions, because CO2 is a gas and DNA rides on particles.
A third, pre-declared outcome is that the measurement is simply too noisy — sampling
recovery plus person-to-person shedding differences larger than the filter and activity
effects — in which case nothing can be concluded and the line closes.
