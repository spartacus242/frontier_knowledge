## Restatement (my own words)

Nuclear-data libraries are judged by how close their calculated k_eff comes to measured k_eff on ICSBEP critical benchmarks, and criticality-safety practitioners turn that same C/E scatter into a bias and bias uncertainty inside the Upper Subcritical Limit. But the libraries were themselves shaped by looking at those benchmarks. The candidate asks: if you remove a benchmark from the set that shaped the library, how much worse does the library do on it — in pcm and in units of the benchmark's own stated uncertainty — and how much does the derived bias, bias uncertainty and USL move? Because most released libraries (ENDF/B, JEFF, JENDL) were shaped by expert judgement and ad-hoc scaling rather than a refittable fit, V2 splits the frame: regime A (formal assimilation, refittable) gets a true leave-one-out number; regime B (expert feedback) gets only an association between "was used in production" and the residual; both feed a ΔUSL.

Gate 2's claim is that the regime-A core was asked and answered by Rochman et al. 2018 §8.3.
