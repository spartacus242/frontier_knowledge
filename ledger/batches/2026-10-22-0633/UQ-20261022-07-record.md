Question (canonical):

V1 (as intaken): In randomised placebo-controlled trials of a drug class that is simultaneously obtainable outside the trial, what fraction of placebo-arm participants has the active class detectable in their own stored trial biospecimens at scheduled visits, and by how much does that measured off-protocol exposure attenuate the trial's estimated treatment effect?

V1 → V2 changes only specification, not object: V2 pins (i) frame declaration before specimen pull, (ii) the denominator as participant-VISITS not participants, (iii) assay LOD *and* false-positive rate *and* detection window against the visit interval, (iv) estimand pre-declaration ahead of unblinded assay results, (v) the governance-refusal share as a reported quantity. Alternative formulations:
- How many people in the placebo group are actually taking the real drug — measured in their blood rather than asked on a form — and what does that do to the trial's answer?
- Treating off-protocol exposure as a measurable contamination of the control condition, what is its assayed prevalence per visit and what is the effect estimate under one pre-declared contamination-adjusted estimand?

Motivating gap:
Source method: presupposition inversion. Trials police adherence in the ACTIVE arm biochemically (plasma drug levels, metabolite ratios) and ascertain the CONTROL arm's exposure by self-report on a concomitant-medication CRF — inverting the burden of proof exactly where the comparison lives. The inverted presupposition is written down explicitly in the current methodological literature: Rytgaard et al. (arXiv:2605.05859, 8 May 2026), building time-varying estimands for concomitant medication on the LEADER liraglutide-vs-placebo trial, is rendered by the summariser as noting that "the active treatment is not available" to placebo participants. Significance — consequence:
If the assayed prevalence is materially above the assay's false-positive rate, then every placebo-controlled trial of a widely obtainable class carries a bias of KNOWN SIGN — toward the null — that is currently neither measured nor declared, and the estimand framework has no category for it: P3 shows the machinery exists for record-ascertained drop-in of other classes while rendering control-arm access to the study class as nil. The consequence is not a vague "trials may be biased": it is a signed, bounded correction to published effect estimates for a named class in a named window, plus a design requirement (retain and pre-commit to assay control-arm specimens) that costs little at protocol stage and is impossible to retrofit. If the prevalence is indistinguishable from the false-positive rate, the answer is equally consequential in the other direction: it retires a standing worry that currently has no measurement behind it, and it licenses continued self-report ascertainment of control-arm exposure on evidence rather than on assumption — which is what P1's zero does for one trial in one regime and what nothing does for the GLP-1 regime. The governance-refusal share is a third consequence: it measures, auditably, how much of the trial enterprise's own stored evidence is unavailable for methodological self-examination.

Significance — audience:
(i) Trial methodologists and the estimand community — P3's authors are already building time-varying interventions on control-arm concomitant medication in exactly this trial family; a measured same-class prevalence would add an intercurrent-event category they would have to handle, and they have shown they handle new ones by constructing estimands for them. (ii) Regulators reading estimands under ICH E9(R1): an intercurrent event ascertained by assay rather than by CRF has no current slot, and the sign of the bias runs against the sponsor, so a regulator is the natural party to require it. (iii) Biobank governance committees and IRBs, who would have to rule on release for a purpose absent from the original consent — and whose refusal rate the question makes a reported quantity rather than invisible attrition. (iv) Meta-analysts and HTA bodies, who pool placebo-controlled estimates across exactly the 2023–2026 window in which the class was obtainable outside the trial. (v) Bioanalytical laboratories of the HPTN Clarke type, who already run this screening at scale on trial specimens with a stated LOD and would be the performing party.

Significance — fecundity:
Answering it makes askable: what the sign-and-magnitude prior for control-arm contamination should be for other widely obtainable classes (OTC analgesics, nicotine products, off-label small molecules); whether assay-based control-arm monitoring belongs in protocols as a design element rather than a post hoc audit; what consent language would license future contamination audits without reopening a primary analysis; whether the measured attenuation reconciles any part of the persistent trial-versus-real-world effect gap for the class; and — the question P1's zero raises and cannot answer — whether contamination tracks the class's availability for the trial's own INDICATION rather than its availability as a molecule. It also makes the assay-versus-CRF concordance in the control arm a reportable trial characteristic, which is a measurement nobody currently produces.

Significance — anchoring:
Touches (a) the ICH E9(R1) intercurrent-event taxonomy, which classifies by what happened and not by how it was ascertained, and has no assay-ascertained category; (b) the drop-in / treatment-switching adjustment literature, which P2 and P3 anchor and which ascertains exposure from records throughout; (c) the active-placebo literature surfaced by query 10, which addresses the adjacent and distinct presupposition of what the placebo CONTAINS; (d) the biochemical-verification tradition in smoking-cessation trials (query 9), which solved the same misclassification problem for abstinence decades ago and whose solution was never transplanted to drug-class exposure in a control arm; (e) the unexplained-placebo-arm-improvement anomaly in obesity and cardiometabolic trials, for which off-protocol exposure is a measurable candidate explanation that has not been measured.

Reason for silence:
A conjunction of four mechanisms, no one of which is "nobody thought of it."
(1) DISCIPLINARY BOUNDARY, the primary mechanism. Bioanalytical exposure measurement is owned by clinical pharmacology and is scoped by protocol to the active arm's PK and adherence; control-arm exposure is owned by data management as a concomitant-medication CRF field. The two live in different documents, different vendors and different SOPs, and no one's remit spans them. P1 is the exception that proves the boundary: iPrEx assayed placebo specimens only because a case-control drug-level design needed matched controls for specificity, and having obtained the result it reported it in one clause and did not treat it as a prevalence.
(2) INCENTIVE GAP. A measured contamination can only lower the sponsor's own reported effect, or, if zero, produce a null that is hard to publish. The analysis has no natural funder: the party with the specimens has the least to gain, and the parties who would act (regulators, meta-analysts) do not hold the specimens.
(3) GOVERNANCE FRICTION. The assay must be run on stored specimens for a purpose not in the original consent, which requires an affirmative release decision that nobody is currently asked to make — so the default is not refusal but non-request, which leaves no record and therefore no visible gap.
(4) NEW AVAILABILITY REGIME. Until recently the presupposition was near-true by supply constraint rather than by design: a class obtainable at scale outside the trial's prescribing channel, for the trial's own indication, is what the US GLP-1 compounding and telehealth market made real in 2023–2026. P1's zero in 2010 is consistent with the presupposition having been approximately correct in its own regime, which is exactly why no one revisited it when the regime changed.
