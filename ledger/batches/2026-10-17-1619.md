## A cited paper that does not exist

The strongest objection against UQ-40 rested on two quantified priors. One of them is not a paper.

"Elmore 2022, RR 1.52 for upgrading and 1.38 for downgrading against a consensus panel" was introduced by that candidate's second adversary and used as load-bearing evidence in **four of the eight attacks** (Triviality, Disguise, Significance inflation, Reason for silence). Two independent readers in sequence — twelve searches and twelve fetches between them, the second searching the two figures themselves by any author and any year — could not locate it. The nearest real paper, opened, is Elmore et al., *JAMA* 2015;313(11):1122-1132: 115 pathologists, 6,900 interpretations against a consensus reference, 75.3% concordance (95% CI 73.4 to 77.0), reporting over- and under-interpretation as percentages with no relative risks and no upgrade/downgrade wording anywhere. The only 2022 papers Crossref lists under that author are a metacognition study (read to ~100,000 of 102,500 characters; neither figure appears, and there is no manipulation of a previous interpretation) and a viewing-processes paper.

It changed no verdict, because no attack on UQ-40 was Unresolved and adversary 1's independent limb already set the worst status on each of those four attacks. But every remaining occurrence in the entry is now flagged inline, and two specific sub-claims are struck: attack 2's reading that the paper "randomises the index assertion shown to a re-reviewer against an index-independent panel", and attack 6's upgrade to Resolved on the strength of it.

The lesson is narrow and worth keeping: an adversary can produce a citation with an author, a year and two three-significant-figure effect sizes, and nothing in the pipeline catches it except trying to open it. Verification exists for exactly this.

## The other quantified leg also fell

UQ-40's second prior, Wu et al., *Radiology* 2014;270(3):717-735, is real but is not what the entry made of it. Its 2.0% (43 studies) against 12.1% (5 studies, 95% CI 4.4 to 29.4) major-discrepancy contrast is a **between-study subgroup comparison inside a meta-analysis**, not a manipulation of blinding within one case set, and its reference standard is a second radiologist's read — not independent of the index interpretation in the sense this candidate needs. So the generic question has been quantified once, weakly and between studies, rather than "twice in index-independent substrates". UQ-40 stays Provisional, but the cap has changed shape: it is now held down by well-posedness, by an incomplete search record, and by its own adversary's design-internal objections, not by prior art.

One new unread lead now stands ahead of it and was added to its promotion conditions: Whiting et al., "Evidence of reference standard related bias in studies of plain radiograph reading performance: a meta-regression" (PubMed 17151064), which names the candidate's mechanism in its title and is blocked from this environment.

## When the adversary and the record disagree, open the source

Twice more today, and both times the direction was the same as on the biology batch: the record was right.

**UQ-38.** The first verification reader reported that Seiberth et al. 2020 contains no discussion of choices the published CKD-EPI equation leaves to the implementer. A full re-read found all four that the record had named: BSA by "Mosteller's or Du Bois'" equation, with only 5 of 47 calculators computing BSA at all; the variant question; the verbatim "there appears to be no general recommendation on an upper age limit"; and the creatinine assay, with 9 of 47 explicitly requesting standardised creatinine. The record quoted the paper correctly. What **did** fall is the kill's gloss on it: the paper does not separate a coding mistake from a specification choice (NOT FOUND on a full read — its causes are a defect taxonomy), there is no under-determination *audit* and no sensitivity analysis, and its input set is five hypothetical cases with no extremes of age and no missing values, narrower than the grid the candidate proposes. N1 drops from a four-attribute match to three.

**UQ-37.** The kill said Moerbeek & Safarkhani 2018 "explicitly request the candidate's limb-(a) parameter". The paper's scarcity sentence is verbatim and about **variance components**: "For cluster randomized trials with crossed random effects, such estimates are hardly available in the literature". It issues no call to measure how often clinicians cross clusters, and reports no crossing fraction and no professional-level ICC of its own. So it supports this entry's *significance* case — a named consumer who cannot parameterise its own power calculations — and not its *Disguise* kill. That claim is struck.

## Why no late Gate 4 ran

LEDGER.md requires Gate 4 re-run from scratch when verification withdraws the ground a rejection rested on. It was considered for UQ-38 and deliberately not run, and the reasoning is in the entry so it can be checked: the ground is attack 2's Unresolved from both adversaries, and that ground rests on Rahaman 2023 and Bierbrier 2014, **both of which were confirmed at first hand this pass by readers who had not seen the adversaries' reasoning**. What fell was a decoration on a third prior. A ground that survives first-hand verification of its own sources is not a withdrawn ground.

Rahaman came back confirmed on every load-bearing attribute: an SMT-solver audit of the published reference tables themselves (12 scores, 5 with errors, 8 coverage and 3 non-overlap violations, 418 rows with 319 checked), the source-versus-implementation separation verbatim ("It is unclear how developers are supposed to cope with this incorrect specification"), threshold straddling discussed, 100% raw multiple-rater agreement with no kappa anywhere, no declared input sampling rule, and no mention of EHRs. Two new corrections against the paper itself: it is internally inconsistent on its own headline (abstract "5 of 12 scores", a later passage "4 of 11"), and its Table 4 threshold column carries no stated crossing *rate*, so the 13-of-16 tally that circulated in this batch's logs is a reader's own count and must not be cited as published.

## What to carry forward

1. **Try to open every cited prior, including the adversary's.** One citation in this batch had an author, a year and two effect sizes and does not exist. Nothing but a fetch catches that.
2. **A ground that survives first-hand verification of its own sources is not a withdrawn ground.** Late Gate 4 is for a fallen ground, not for a fallen decoration on a ground — and saying which, in the entry, is part of the work.
3. **A promotion condition can be ill-formed.** UQ-36's item (2) asked a reader to beat a band in the wrong measurand. Conditions that name a number should name the measurement it came from.
4. **Check whether a "new lead" is a paper already in the entry.** UQ-36's Bradford lead and its nearest prior are the same paper under two repository records with different author orders.
5. **The same page still answers differently by route.** Gerry 2020 returned NOT FOUND for a string via HTML and FOUND via PDF, the second time this has happened on a single source.
6. **Generated index counts are still unreliable** — UQ-39's record claimed 134 entries against a committed 125. Corrected inline, as in the biology batch. Three batches running; worth fixing at the prompt rather than per entry.
