## What this batch adds to the method

- **A quotation can exist in no document at all, and this batch produced three more.** The "15% to 30%" unidentifiable share UQ-15 attributed to the NCHRP report is in none of the three documents opened, sits in a different paper from the one the entry cited it to, and its opening words scope it to the *prior* literature rather than to that group's own result. UQ-14 attributed "Only 37.3% of servers were rated 'Adequate'" to a paper whose own sentence reads "Overall, only 37.3% of servers had Adequate performance overall." UQ-11 carried a quotation of Cui & Atkinson cut so that it dropped "make the assumption" and the reason clause, turning a declining-for-want-of-data note into a modelling claim. The rule added to `LEDGER.md` after the 06:33 batch fired on its first run.
- **A summariser refusal is a fact about the request.** UQ-15's record said the NILIM cells had never been served verbatim and nominated two files as the untried route; the cells came back on a transcription-style request, and the two nominated files are a cover page and a preface, nominated without checking their kind. The same pattern appeared on three other documents across the batch, each of which opened in one or three fetches at a route recorded as spent.
- **Two readers agreeing from no read is zero supports, not two.** UQ-14's two adversaries agreed on the synchrophasor limit because *neither* had opened a limit-bearing document; the figure they relied on turned out to be NIST's own hedged requirement estimate rather than the standard's printed limit, and the standard itself is still unopened with its paywall untested.
- **A lead nominated by kind is worth opening.** Three of this batch's five entries had one and none had opened it: live FDSN availability endpoints for UQ-11 (opened in the pass, true for two networks and false for a third, which struck one leg of the Gate 1 cap), the NCHRP report for UQ-15 (still unread, but two of the three routes the entry advertised as untried turn out not to exist), and the limit-bearing standards clauses for UQ-14 (opened, and they narrowed the kill rather than supporting it).
- **An argument's premises are checkable even when no paper is involved.** UQ-13's kill was pure algebra and it was tested as algebra: the identity holds, its quantifier does not, and the protocol supplies neither of the two readings that would make the question non-trivial. That is a well-posedness finding, and the cold reader that rejected the entry reached it independently and then sourced it to a frame member, which is why the rejection stands.
