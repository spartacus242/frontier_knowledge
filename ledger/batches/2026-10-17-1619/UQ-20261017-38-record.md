Domain(s): Medicine — clinical prediction models and clinical decision support; method borrowed from software engineering (differential/metamorphic testing of independent implementations of one specification). Gate 2 established that the target literature is not clinical epidemiology but mHealth safety / medical-app assessment and software verification of medical apps.

Question (canonical) — V0 (as intake): For a pre-declared set of widely used published clinical risk scores, do independently written faithful implementations return the same value for the same patient — and at what rate do they disagree enough to cross the score's own decision threshold, with each disagreement adjudicated into implementation error versus irreducible under-specification in the publication?

Question (canonical) — V1 (the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation; well-posedness only; both versions retained): For a pre-declared set S of published clinical risk scores each carrying a published action threshold, and for each score a set of I >= 3 inspectable independent implementations (open calculator sites, society apps, open-source packages, journal-appendix code), first partition the input space into the SPEC-DETERMINED region (input configurations for which the source publication fixes a unique score) and the SPEC-UNDETERMINED region (configurations it does not), using a rubric declared before any implementation is run; then, on an input sample drawn by a declared rule that fixes in advance the weight given to boundary values, unit edge cases and missing predictors: report (i) the pairwise and set-wise any-numeric-difference rate, (ii) the threshold-crossing disagreement rate, each reported separately over the two regions, and (iii) for the spec-determined region only, the share of threshold crossings adjudicated to implementation error by >= 2 independent raters with a reported agreement statistic — crossings in the spec-undetermined region being under-specification by construction rather than by adjudication.
(V1 exists because V0 is circular where it matters: "faithful implementation" cannot be defined on the inputs the publication does not determine, which is precisely the region the question is about. V1 removes the circularity by sizing that region first and making it a reported quantity rather than an adjudication outcome. V1 does not widen the question and does not rescue novelty — see Gate 2.)

Alternative formulations:
1. Is a published clinical risk score a well-defined function of its inputs, or only a sketch that each implementer completes differently?
2. What is the implementation-induced reclassification rate of clinical scores — how often does which calculator you open, rather than which patient you have, decide which side of the threshold the patient lands on?
3. (Method-field wording) What does N-version differential testing of deployed clinical score implementations against their published specification find, and how much of the divergence is specification under-determination rather than defect?

Motivating gap (as intake, and as Gate 2 revised it): Intake held that software engineering routinely asks whether independent implementations of one specification agree while medicine has never asked it of risk scores; that the adjacent medical literature compares DIFFERENT models (Framingham vs QRISK vs Pooled Cohort Equations) while treating each as a fixed function; and that model-reporting-adequacy work (TRIPOD/Collins line) makes under-specification a named measurable quantity. Query 1 reproduced exactly the different-models family the intake predicted, which appeared to confirm the gap. GATE 2 OVERTURNS THE GAP: the question has been asked and substantially answered in medicine's own literature and in the method-owning field, in a vocabulary the intake did not try. The apparent silence was an artefact of searching "risk calculator" + "inconsistent" rather than "calculator app accuracy", "systematic assessment" and "verification / partition violation". Neamtiu 2023 states the intake's gap in its own abstract ("There has been no systematic or rigorous effort to examine and verify score calculator apps") and then closes it, including the limb the intake named as most distinctive — the enumerated input configurations the publication does not determine.

Presuppositions (intake's five, as Gate 2 leaves them):
1. The published description is what clinicians and vendors believe they are using, so divergence from it is a defect not a local design choice — ESTABLISHED in practice; CONTESTED for deliberately localised EHR builds. Unchanged by Gate 2.
2. At least three inspectable independent implementations exist per score — ESTABLISHED and now demonstrated at much larger scale than the candidate assumed: 90 Android apps over 12 scores (Neamtiu 2023), 46 insulin-dose calculators (Huckvale 2015), 23 opioid-conversion apps (Haffey 2013). CONTESTED only for proprietary EHR builds, which remain unexamined by every prior opened or described.
3. Each disagreement can be adjudicated into coding error versus publication under-specification — ESTABLISHED as feasible, and already done informally: Neamtiu 2023 separates "Error Source #1: Inconsistent Reference Table" (specification) from "Error Source #3: Incorrect Score Calculation" (implementation) with three analysts manually cross-checking, and flags irreducible cases ("the paper itself is ambiguous" at HEART Age=65). No inter-rater statistic is reported there, which is the candidate's only surviving methodological increment on this limb.
4. Each score has a decision threshold whose crossing changes an action — ESTABLISHED; Neamtiu 2023 carries a "Meets or Exceeds Threshold" column (Table 4) and discusses errors that "straddle" the threshold.
5. A defensible input grid exists, including awkward regions — ESTABLISHED, and the awkward regions are exactly where the published priors already found the defects (SOFA Bilirubin=12.0 and Creatinine=5.0, APACHE II Age=44, HEART Age=65), which weakens rather than supports the candidate's claim that the grid design is an open problem.

What would count as an answer: A measured pairwise and set-wise disagreement rate across >= 3 implementations per score on a pre-declared input sample, reported as (i) any-numeric-difference rate, (ii) threshold-crossing rate, (iii) share of threshold crossings adjudicated to under-specification rather than error, with inter-rater agreement; plus a per-score under-specification audit enumerating the input configurations the publication does not determine. Near-zero threshold crossings on a grid that genuinely includes the awkward regions answers it in the negative. NOTE FOR THE VERDICT: the negative branch is already partly in hand — Bierbrier 2014 reports 98.6% output accuracy (17 errors in 1240 tests) and records that the Child-Pugh errors occurred at low score ranges and did not change the patient's class.
