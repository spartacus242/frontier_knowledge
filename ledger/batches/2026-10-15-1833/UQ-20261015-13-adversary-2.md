# UQ-20261015-13 — Adversary 2 log

## Restatement (my own words)

Take every controlled pre-approval trial of a newly approved drug, pool the treatment-emergent adverse-event preferred terms, and tabulate how many distinct terms were seen in exactly one patient, exactly two, and so on. Feed that spectrum to the ecologists' "how many species did we miss" machinery (Chao1 lower bound on total types; Good-Turing f1/n as the probability that the next event is a new type). The question is whether the resulting "number of AE types still unseen" for each drug predicts how many new terms the FDA/EMA label's adverse-reactions section acquires in the next five years, after adjusting for exposure, observed richness, class, biologic status and accelerated approval; and, secondarily, whether the terms later added were disproportionately the trial singletons. A within-programme phase-2-predicts-phase-3 test is supposed to tell a "the estimator fails on this data" NO apart from a "trial and market sample different populations" NO.

## Recommended verdict

Provisional — no attack Unresolved, but attack 4 reveals an uncontrolled background-morbidity confound (all-cause AE types vs attributed reactions) that also invalidates the record's positive-control attribution logic, plus singleton censoring in the named public data source; promotable to Validated if R2 adds a placebo-arm/serious-AE-restricted spectrum and demonstrates singleton-level data access for the cohort.

Key kill: the sampled community is all-cause AE types, so Chao1/coverage estimate unseen *background* types and the phase-2→phase-3 positive control passes on background richness alone — the record's claim that a NO is attributable to P2 does not hold as designed.
