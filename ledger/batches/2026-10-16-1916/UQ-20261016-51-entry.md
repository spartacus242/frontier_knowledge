Question (canonical): For a pre-registered item frame and an open-data model whose pretraining corpus is indexed (OLMo/Dolma, Pythia/Pile, LLM360, DCLM), retrieving every corpus occurrence of an item stem above a fixed fuzzy-match threshold and classifying each into {states gold, states non-gold, states no answer, gold itself erroneous}: (a) what are the occurrence- and item-weighted class fractions, with CIs and a reported extractor precision/recall on a human-adjudicated sample; and (b) conditioning on difficulty estimated from zero-occurrence models, is accuracy on non-gold-dominated items (>=50% of answer-stating occurrences disagree with gold) below accuracy on matched occurrence-free items — i.e. is the contamination correction to a reported score signed, and how many points does it move when non-gold-dominated items are removed rather than retained? Alternative formulations:

Motivating gap (source method: anomaly mining): contamination retrieval tools (WIMBD, infini-gram, OLMoTrace) report occurrence counts and score inflation; the record held that none reports what the retrieved occurrence asserts. What would count as an answer: a study over open-data models that retrieves every above-threshold occurrence for a pre-registered frame, classifies each into the four classes with a human-adjudicated sample and reported extractor P/R, reports class fractions with CIs, and estimates the accuracy contrast between non-gold-dominated, gold-dominated and zero-occurrence items under a pre-specified design. YES: measurable disagreement share and accuracy delta below matched clean items, CI excluding zero. NO: disagreement <~2%, or null within a stated MDE.

Verification (post-batch, fresh-context checker, log UQ-20261016-51-verification.md): the disguised-known kill is REFUTED in the part it rested on, and its second leg is contradicted.
Confirmed: the three categories do exist. Li et al. (arXiv:2310.17589, “An Open Source Data Contamination Report for Large Language Models”) define clean, input contamination and input-and-label contamination, and the OpenCompass contamination harness uses them as column labels (“accuracy - clean”, “accuracy - input contaminated”, “accuracy - input-and-label contaminated”) without formal definitions. Published contaminated-below-clean results are real: Li's Tables 2 and 3 show them for MMLU with LLaMA 65B, Mistral 7B and Baichuan2 7B, for HellaSwag with Llama-2 13B, Mistral 7B and Yi 6B, for ARC with LLaMA 7B and 13B, and for C-Eval with Mistral 7B.
Refuted: neither source records whether a matched copy states an answer that DISAGREES with the gold label. “Input-and-label contaminated” means only that the question and an answer both occur in the matched pages. The candidate's class is therefore not a sub-partition of an existing class — it is a different variable, and this is the ledger's dominant error mode (a real tool, accurately described, doing a different thing) recurring.
Contradicted: no primary source was found for “MMLU's keys were scraped from the very pages that would constitute its occurrences”, and the late adversary then found MMLU's own paper saying the opposite — that most questions came from sources where questions and answers are on separate pages. The thinness objection survives in a different form (keyless copies may dominate) and is recorded above as a risk to limb (a).

Scores: Well-posedness 3/3 · Significance 2/3 (the Gate 4 downgrade to 1/3 rested on the one-signed presumption having been “shown false” by the same tooling claim that has now failed; published contaminated-below-clean results do exist in Li et al.'s Tables 2 and 3, but with no wrong-answer condition in the design and, per the late adversary, against a released-BERT baseline rather than a matched clean run, so they establish that the sign is not uniformly positive without explaining why)

Strongest remaining objection (restated; the late adversary's, and it is about identification rather than novelty): limb (b) cannot be answered observationally. Wrong-answer contamination and intrinsic item difficulty are not separable in corpus data — items whose web copies disagree with the gold key are plausibly also items the key is wrong about, or items the web is confused about — so a signed correction needs an injection arm, not a conditioning design, and no source was found showing that a corpus majority answer drives the model's answer at all. Second, the reason-for-silence mechanism is weaker than the record claims: infini-gram mini already runs an LLM judge over corpus hits for answer presence, and Magar and Schwartz (arXiv:2203.08242) state “We assume that test instances appear in the pretrain corpus with their gold labels” and put the unlabelled case “beyond the scope of this paper” in a footnote. Together those suggest the case was noticed and set aside, not unseen — which is a weaker silence than an instrument gap. Third, and now the live risk to limb (a) rather than a kill: MMLU's own paper (arXiv:2009.03300) says its questions were “manually collected by graduate and undergraduate students from freely available sources online” and that most came from PDFs or websites where questions and answers sit on separate pages (summariser paraphrase, not an exact-string check). So keyless copies may dominate the occurrence population and make limb (a)'s answer-stating denominator thin — which the record already treats as a reportable NO.
