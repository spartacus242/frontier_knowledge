Question (canonical — Gate 0 as intaken):
In cohorts that have documented a decline in age-specific clinical dementia incidence across calendar epochs and that hold archived plasma from comparable age windows in each epoch, has the prevalence of biomarker-defined Alzheimer pathology (plasma p-tau217 positivity and its continuous distribution) declined in parallel, or has only the probability that a given pathology burden becomes clinical dementia declined?

Question (canonical — Gate 1 reformulation, used hereafter; one reformulation, for well-posedness only):
Within a single cohort study that has published an epoch-to-epoch fall in age-specific dementia incidence and that holds archived plasma drawn at a matched age window (e.g. 70-74) in each epoch: when all epochs' aliquots are assayed in one lot with bridging controls, (a) what is the age-standardised ratio of plasma p-tau217 positivity (later epoch / earlier epoch) and the corresponding shift in the full continuous p-tau217 distribution (quantile-by-quantile, not only a threshold), (b) what is the ratio of age-specific clinical dementia incidence to biomarker positivity in each epoch, and (c) does a pre-specified within-epoch storage-duration gradient analysis exclude assay drift as an explanation of whatever (a) shows?
[Both versions recorded. The reformulation adds: one assay lot + bridging, a named estimand (prevalence ratio, distributional shift, incidence/positivity ratio), and a pre-specified falsification test for the storage confound. It does not change the subject matter.]

Alternative formulations:
- Is the observed fall in dementia incidence a fall in pathology, or a fall in the expression of pathology as disease?
- Across birth cohorts measured at the same age in the same cohort study, how has the ratio of clinical dementia incidence to plasma-biomarker positivity changed?

Motivating gap:
The decline in age-specific dementia incidence in high-income cohorts is documented (Satizabal et al., NEJM 2016, ~20%/decade in Framingham; Alzheimer Cohorts Consortium 27-year trends). The debate over its cause has been conducted in terms of candidate mechanisms — vascular risk, education, cognitive reserve — with pathology measured, when at all, only at autopsy. Plasma p-tau217 immunoassays validated from ~2020 and clinically cleared in 2025 make amyloid/tau status measurable in archived plasma, so the pathology-versus-expression split becomes measurable in living-cohort samples from the exact cohorts that reported the decline, rather than in decedent brains from cohorts that did not. The two answers imply opposite programmes: a parallel pathology decline means risk-factor modification is preventing disease and projections should be revised downward on pathology grounds; stable pathology with falling clinical incidence means the population is compensating, pathology prevalence (and therefore eligibility for pathology-targeted therapy) is undiminished, and the decline is fragile.

Presuppositions:
1. p-tau217 measured in long-archived plasma is stable and comparable across storage durations and collection protocols — CONTESTED, and the central methodological risk, because storage duration is collinear with epoch. Requires bridging samples, parallel aliquots, within-epoch storage-duration gradients, a single assay lot. Gate 2 confirmed an active storage/pre-analytics literature exists (Fujirebio Lumipulse storage-dependent variability; differing stability of tau vs amyloid analytes and hence of ratios; prospective p-tau217 stability work), i.e. the presupposition is live and testable rather than assumed.
2. The cohorts' sampling frames are comparable across epochs — CONTESTED: participation has fallen over time and selection may differ by cognitive status.
3. A positivity threshold transportable across epochs exists — CONTESTED; threshold work is itself unsettled (multi-cohort threshold and head-to-head assay comparisons published 2025-2026), so the analysis must report the continuous distribution, not only dichotomised prevalence.
4. The clinical incidence decline is real rather than an artefact of diagnostic practice, help-seeking or ascertainment — CONTESTED in part; a competing explanation to be modelled, not assumed away.
5. Competing mortality and age-window comparability can be handled so epochs are compared at matched age and survival exposure — methodologically established, but it constrains which cohorts qualify.
6. p-tau217 positivity is a valid proxy for the pathology whose expression is in question — largely established against PET and autopsy in contemporary samples, weaker for pre-2000 specimens.
It is a question, not a thesis: both answers are stated, pre-specified, and neither is favoured by the design.

What would count as an answer:
Assay of archived plasma from at least two epochs of the same cohort (ideally replicated in a second cohort) under one assay lot with bridging controls, reporting epoch-specific age-standardised biomarker positivity and the full continuous distribution, the epoch prevalence ratio, and the ratio of clinical dementia incidence to biomarker positivity, each with confidence intervals and a storage-duration sensitivity analysis capable of excluding assay drift as the explanation of either direction. Parallel declines answer "pathology"; stable or rising pathology prevalence with falling clinical incidence answers "expression"; an inconclusive storage-stability analysis means the question is not yet answerable and says so rather than splitting the difference.

Asked-but-open: **Yes.** Source: Wharton et al. 2023 (P3), which states the pathological basis of the CFAS I-to-CFAS II decline is unknown because CFAS II has no neuropathology cohort; and P1/P2, which answer the autopsy version only in ROSMAP and attribute the decline to resilience without population-level pathology prevalence. The open problem belongs in `ledger/routed-open-problems.md`: "Does the secular decline in age-specific dementia incidence reflect falling pathology prevalence or falling expression of pathology?" — tier: tractable-with-samples (a measurable question whose obstacle is sample access and storage-stability validation, not conceptual).

Significance — consequence:
If pathology has declined in parallel: primary prevention is demonstrably altering disease, not only its expression; the pathology-prevalence inputs to dementia projection models should fall and not merely the clinical-incidence inputs; prevention trials gain a defensible pathology endpoint; and projected demand for pathology-targeted therapy falls with cohort replacement. If pathology is stable or rising with falling clinical incidence: the population is compensating, the eligible population for amyloid- and tau-targeted therapy is undiminished and will not shrink with cohort replacement, the decline is contingent on whatever sustains compensation (and so reversible — consistent with reports of the decline flattening), and prevention trials powered on pathology endpoints are powered on the wrong quantity. The two answers move the same planning numbers in opposite directions, which is why "argue, not assert" is satisfied: nothing currently distinguishes them in any cohort that reported the decline.
Significance — audience:
(i) Dementia-projection modellers and health-system planners (GBD-style and national capacity models) — they would carry an epoch-varying pathology-prevalence term instead of a stationary one; query 10 shows eligibility-sizing and wait-time models (USC health policy; Finland, ISPOR 2025) that today assume stationary pathology prevalence. (ii) Prevention trialists — choice between pathology and expression endpoints, and the expected event rate for either. (iii) The risk-reduction community (Lancet Commission-style) — whether modifiable risk factors are credited with preventing pathology or with buffering it, which changes the claim made for the 40%-of-cases figure. (iv) Anti-amyloid therapeutic planners sizing eligible populations, and payers behind them. (v) Biobank custodians, whose archived plasma becomes a primary rather than ancillary asset, and who would need to fund bridging-aliquot work.
Significance — fecundity:
Opens: whether the incidence/positivity ratio varies by education, APOE, vascular burden or birth cohort (i.e. who is compensating); whether epoch shifts sit in the whole p-tau217 distribution or only its upper tail (pathology onset delayed vs pathology attenuated); whether the same epoch contrast appears in GFAP/NfL (expression-side neurodegeneration) while p-tau217 stays flat, which would localise the change to the pathology-to-injury step rather than the injury-to-dementia step; whether storage-duration gradients can be turned into a general retro-assay calibration method for other archived-plasma analytes; and what an epoch-varying pathology prevalence does to lead-time and over-diagnosis estimates for population blood-biomarker screening.
Significance — anchoring:
Attaches to (a) the named open problem of the mechanism of the dementia-incidence decline, (b) the flattening/reversal reports in recent trend updates, (c) the cognitive-reserve/resilience construct, which lacks a population-scale pathology denominator, (d) the pathology-prevalence inputs of every anti-amyloid eligibility estimate, and (e) the pre-analytics literature on whether legacy biobank plasma can be assayed at all for p-tau217 — the candidate is in part a stress test of that.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism):
Primarily **new instrument plus a deterrent confound**, with a disciplinary-boundary component. The instrument is 1-5 years old (p-tau217 immunoassays validated from ~2020, clinical clearance 2025, assay head-to-heads and transportable thresholds still being published in 2025-2026), so the plasma route to an already-asked question has only recently existed; this also means the silence is partly mere recency and carries little evidential weight for novelty. The deterrent is that storage duration is perfectly collinear with epoch, so the obvious design looks confounded on its face and is easier not to propose than to defend — a measurement taboo rather than a conceptual gap. The boundary is that the decline literature is produced by epidemiologists working from incidence registers and the assay literature by neurochemistry groups working in memory-clinic and single-epoch population samples (H70 is the clearest case: the same group holds both the birth-cohort comparisons and the p-tau217 prevalence, published separately); no group's incentives place the epoch contrast in its own output. "No one thought of it" is not invoked: P3 shows someone did, in tissue vocabulary, and said it could not be done.
