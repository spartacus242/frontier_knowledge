## Restatement (my own words)

Wet-lab biology runs on a few thousand exact numbers that nobody re-derives: 37 °C, 5% CO2, 10% FBS, 4% PFA for 10 min, 1:1000 antibody, 95 °C for 5 min, 20-24 °C vivarium. Pick that population by a rule written down in advance (how often a given number+unit string occurs in a dated protocols.io / Methods corpus), flag beforehand the ones physics or the instrument forces, then chase each number backwards and sort it into one of four bins: it came from an experiment that actually optimised something and the setup still resembles today's; it came from such an experiment but under cell lines / media / reagents / instruments nobody uses now; it came from something that optimised nothing (a vendor insert, a textbook, an older protocol); or the trail dies. Report the four shares with intervals, two coders and an agreement statistic, forced parameters on their own line. Then for a random handful of the second bin, redo the original optimisation today and say whether the best value moved by more than a margin declared in advance. In one sentence: how much of biology's numeric furniture was ever measured, and does it still measure the same?

## New evidence I bring (opened, Q6)

biorxiv 10.1101/2022.12.20.521257 (PDF read; the PDF text I received carried no title/author block, so I cite the content to the URL and the title to the search result). It is the closest thing I found anywhere to the candidate's limb (b), one domain over: it brute-forces configuration spaces of bioinformatics tools and asks whether the default is the optimum. Measured, from the PDF: BLAST, 3,000 configurations exhaustively — **72% of configurations beat the default** and 13.33% reach the best result; FBA-CLI, 125,000 sampled from 1.28 × 10^16 — 1.58% beat the default, 95.14% give the worst outcome; FBA-GUI — the default IS the best value; MEGAHIT, 260 configurations — odds of beating the default 2.69-66.15% depending on objective; "in all subjects, more than 80% of results differ from the default." On provenance it is silent: it traces no value to an origin, and its only remarks near the topic are that undefined parameters "use the default value as determined by the program", that "Biologists, hence, often develop a laboratory-specific routine protocol of what to change and leave the rest of the options alone", and that documented parameter limits were in one case simply wrong. These are software/tool parameters; the paper explicitly treats wet-lab work as upstream context only.

Two consequences. (i) It is not a prior: the unit is a software knob with a machine-readable space and no provenance claim. (ii) It cuts against the candidate on informativeness: in the one sibling domain where limb (b) is cheap enough to run exhaustively, the answer is already published and is "yes, the optimum usually is not the default, and sometimes it is" — which is the two-sided answer the candidate expects to be news. That is the best-sourced version of the Triviality attack, and it is a Partial, not a kill: BLAST's parameter space is not 4% PFA for 10 min, and a software default's being beatable says nothing about the share of wet-lab constants that trace to an optimisation at all.

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional** — nothing Unresolved, so not Rejected; but Validated is doubly blocked: Gate 5 requires Well-posedness 3/3 against the record's 2/3 (rule-dependent denominator; limb (b) runnable only on a biased subsample), and Medium novelty with a close neighbour (P1, verified at the publisher and closer than the record allows, since it does carry a formula/value citation category) is by Gate 5's own definition a Provisional. Promotion would need: a rule-independent or explicitly multi-rule estimand, a pilot with a reported coder-agreement statistic, a characterisation of the state-(ii) re-runnable subsample against the full state-(ii) set, and one documented instance of a provenance audit changing a reporting standard (presupposition 5). The unqueried laboratory-animal-husbandry vocabulary should be swept in a later run.

**Single strongest killing point:** the headline four rates are not a rule-independent estimand, and on P1's own verified finding that methodological citations referring to a formula or value are "uncommon in all 3 fields", the trail most parameters would be traced along barely exists — so the audit's largest cell is likely "chain unresolvable", which indexes corpus digitisation and citation habit rather than evidential status; meanwhile limb (b), which the record itself says the question is not the question without, is executable only on the subsample of state (ii) whose original optimisation happens to be documented well enough to re-run.
