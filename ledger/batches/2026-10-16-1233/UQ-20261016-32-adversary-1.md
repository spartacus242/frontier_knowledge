## 1. Restatement (my own words, deliberately not the record's)

Cancer stage for several organs is decided by comparing one number — the tumour's largest dimension (or depth) in millimetres — against a fixed cut-point. That number is not read off the patient. It is read off a piece of tissue that has been cut out (so it has lost blood pressure, tension and, in hollow or aerated organs, shape), then soaked in formalin, then dehydrated, waxed and cut into a slide. Each of those steps is known to change the number, mostly downwards. The candidate asks two measurement questions and one validity question:

- **How big is the step-by-step change, and how variable is it?** Not the average correction factor — the *spread*, separated into the part that is the step itself, the part that is one pathologist's unsteady hand, and the part that is pathologist-to-pathologist.
- **How many real patients sit close enough to a cut-point that this spread decides which side they land on?** Expressed in patients per year, taking seriously that reported millimetre values pile up on multiples of 5.
- **Which state of the specimen is the number that the cut-points were calibrated on?** If the thresholds were originally fitted to shrunken, fixed measurements, then "correcting" for shrinkage would move patients *away* from the number the prognosis was learned on, and the correct action is the opposite of the obvious one.

So: not "does formalin shrink tissue" (it does), but "how much of the staging decision lattice is made of specimen-processing rather than of tumour, and which end of the processing chain is the number we should be using at all".

## 4. My own arithmetic (labelled, with the sources' stated uncertainty carried)

Derived by me from Tran 2015 (F2) and Tsuruda 2020 (record P2); not from any paper.

- Tran's fresh->microscopic shift is a mean of 11.4% with range 0-22.7% over n=34 and **no published SD**. As a crude dispersion surrogate for n~30 I take range/4 ~= 5.7 percentage points (this assumes approximate normality and treats the observed range as the effective support; it is a rough rule, not an estimate from the data, which were not published in a form permitting one).
- At a 20 mm cut-point (breast pT1/pT2): central shift 0.114 x 20 = 2.3 mm; at the top of Tran's range, 0.227 x 20 = 4.5 mm. So the half-width of the "at risk" window is somewhere between roughly 2 and 5 mm, a factor of ~2 depending on which "width" the question means.
- **But the width choice is not what dominates.** A +/-2.3 mm window around 20 mm spans reported integers 18-22; a +/-4.5 mm window spans 16-24. Both contain exactly one multiple of five (20 itself). Since Tsuruda reports 34.8% of histopathologic diameters ending in 0 or 5 and 97.2% being whole millimetres, the mass in either window is dominated by the 20 mm heap that sits at its centre. The estimate is therefore insensitive to the quantile chosen and almost entirely determined by how the heap is deconvolved — i.e. by the single assumption the record itself flags as contested (presupposition 4).
- Tran's stage-change count, 2 of 34 = 5.9%, carries an exact binomial 95% interval of about 0.7% to 19.7% (my Clopper-Pearson arithmetic, approximate). The paper states the count, not a proportion, and no proportion of this kind should be quoted from it as an estimate of anything.

## 5. The eight attacks

### Attack 1 — Triviality: can it be answered in a page from existing knowledge?

**Objection.** The ingredients look published. Per-step shrinkage percentages exist (Tran 2015: 4.6%, 7.1%, 11.4%); registry size densities exist (SEER, Cancer Registry of Norway); thresholds are fixed integers. Multiply the percentage by the threshold, integrate the registry density over the resulting window, and you have limb (b) on one page. Limb (a) is then just a bigger version of a study already done, and the whole thing is a calculation, not a question.

**Best response.** I tried the calculation (section 4) and it does not close. Two reasons, both from sources I opened. First, the quantity the question is about is the *width* of the shift distribution, and that width is not in the literature: Tran reports means with P values and bare ranges (0-18.2%, 0-22.7%), no SD, no CI, and **no inter- or intra-observer analysis at all** — so the within-observer / between-observer / between-step decomposition the question asks for cannot be assembled from published numbers, only guessed at (my range/4 surrogate is a guess, and I label it as one). Second, the integral is dominated by the digit heap: at breast's 20 mm cut-point both a 2.3 mm and a 4.5 mm half-window capture exactly one multiple of five, so the answer is set by how the 20 mm spike is deconvolved rather than by the shift at all. Deconvolving it is the contested step. And limb (c) — does stage recomputed on fresh measurements discriminate better, worse or equivalently — needs linked outcome data and cannot be done on any page.

**Status: Resolved.** A page yields an order-of-magnitude alarm, not an answer, and the one thing a page cannot yield is the thing the question asks for.

### Attack 2 — Disguise: is it a known question renamed?

**Objection.** Yes, and it has at least three other names, two of which the record never looked under. (a) **Laboratory medicine**: Farrell et al. 2026 (F4) compute exactly this estimand — simulate a measurement procedure's uncertainty over a real national population (10,000 draws x 6,326 NHANES subjects), count how many cross a clinical decision limit relative to the procedure the limit was derived on, call it the *incremental reclassification rate*, and attach fitness-for-purpose bands (optimal <1.5%, desirable <3.0%, minimal <4.7%). That is limb (b) with a name, a reference method, a population, and published acceptability thresholds — the candidate's own field just hasn't borrowed it. (b) **Educational measurement**: *classification accuracy* and *classification consistency* at cut scores, with Livingston-Lewis and Rudner estimators shipped as routine software (`irtQ::cac_rud`, `irt::classification_indices`) — the proportion misclassified at a cut-point given measurement error, computed as a matter of course. (c) **Metrology**: measurand definition and definitional uncertainty (search 2), which is presupposition 7 renamed. The candidate is therefore a known question wearing a pathology costume.

**Best response.** The structural match is real and it costs the candidate its methodological novelty, but it does not make it the same question, for one reason I can state precisely. In every one of those fields the perturbation is **replicate measurement error on an object that does not move**: Farrell perturbs the *device* (EQA-derived analytical performance; his abstract names no pre-analytical component), psychometrics perturbs the *score* around a fixed latent ability. The candidate's perturbation is a change in the object — the specimen is physically smaller at state 3 than at state 2 — so the shift is systematic and one-signed rather than zero-mean, and there is no fixed true value to be imprecise about. That difference is not cosmetic: it breaks the "true value plus noise" scaffold both frameworks are built on, and it creates a question those frameworks dispose of by assumption, namely *which state is the measurand*. Farrell gets to sidestep it because he has a privileged reference (the device the decision limit was derived on, the NHANES device); the candidate's analogue of that reference — the handling state of the cohorts that calibrated the T thresholds — is precisely what the record found no source for. So: the estimator is a renamed known thing; the input and the reference are not.

**Status: Partial.** The computational limb is a transposition of a mature, named, already-institutionalised method, which shrinks the candidate considerably and should be stated in the question rather than discovered after it. What survives renaming is a measurement study plus a measurand-definition problem that the sibling fields solve by fiat.

### Attack 3 — Combinatorial novelty: Field A x Field B with nothing at the interface?

**Objection.** Pathology shrinkage studies x misclassification-at-a-cut-point statistics. Both halves mature, the join mechanical: take their numbers, run our model.

**Best response.** There are two genuine problems at the interface, and I evidenced both. (i) The perturbation is a moving object, not noise on a fixed one (attack 2), so the standard machinery's estimand has to be redefined before it can be used — a one-signed systematic displacement of the measurand is not a misclassification probability, and whether the resulting quantity is a risk or a bias depends on the measurand choice. (ii) The two artefacts are entangled by construction. My read of Verbeke (F1) sharpens the record's presupposition 8 rather than merely repeating it: Verbeke's "microscopic corroboration" arm *is itself* a macroscopic-versus-histologic contrast — one of the candidate's own four states — but it is varied jointly with the number of measurement planes, and the third dimension in the 3D arms is **calculated** from slice counts rather than measured. So the published 10 mm / 45% protocol effect sits directly on top of the candidate's gross->histologic step and is confounded with it in the only large study of the genre. Designing an experiment that separates "the specimen got smaller" from "we looked at it differently" is a real design problem at the interface, not an assembly step.

**Status: Resolved.**

### Attack 4 — Presupposition failure

**Objection.** Three suspect premises. (i) That the guidelines "treat size as a property of the tumour" and leave the measurement unspecified — if CAP/AJCC/registry rules already specify which measurement to use, the alleged inversion is a straw man. (ii) That a threshold crossing changes treatment in current practice — in the flagship site, breast, chemotherapy decisions for node-negative hormone-receptor-positive disease are now substantially made by genomic assay, so a 19 mm/21 mm difference may be inert. (iii) That in-situ size is the right measurand (presupposition 7), which may be false.

**Best response.** (i) I checked, and the premise survives in the specific form the question needs, with a correction that *helps* the candidate. The registry coding rules I opened (F3) **do** specify a source priority — "final diagnosis, microscopic, or gross examination, in that order", synoptic report overriding text — and **say nothing whatever about fresh versus fixed tissue**. So the field has written rules at exactly this granularity for *which document and which modality*, while leaving *which physical state* unaddressed. That is a sharper and better-evidenced version of the intake claim than the intake made, and it has a consequence the record missed: because registries systematically prefer the microscopic value, the number that reaches SEER-type data is systematically the most-processed, most-shrunken one, so the direction of any handling bias in registry stage distributions is known a priori and one-signed. (ii) I could not verify this either way within budget. My search 12 shows genomic assays being compared against NCCN clinical criteria rather than replacing them, which is suggestive that size still enters the clinical-risk arm, but I opened no guideline and I will not grade on a suggestion. Recorded as unverified, not refuted; it is load-bearing for attack 6 and it is the presupposition a future reviewer should check first. (iii) Presupposition 7 is contested, but the record states it as contested and makes it a limb of the question rather than an assumption of it, which is the correct handling; it is graded under attack 8.

**Status: Partial.** No presupposition was shown false, and the central one was confirmed in a more precise form from a primary source. Partial rather than Resolved because one premise in the question's own significance clause — that the adjuvant-therapy consequence is live in current practice at the sites with the famous thresholds — I could not evidence, and because separability (presupposition 8) is now demonstrably violated in the only large published study of the adjacent artefact.

### Attack 5 — Vagueness: could it be answered no matter what the evidence shows, or never?

**Objection.** "Within that distribution's width of a threshold" names no quantile. One standard deviation, two, the full observed range? Choose generously and the headline is "a third of patients"; choose tightly and it is "two percent". A question whose answer is set by the analyst's choice of window is not falsifiable.

**Best response.** Two defences, one from the record and one from my own arithmetic. From the record: the Gate 1 reformulation fixes the window as "+/- the step shift", pre-specifies an equivalence margin for limb (c), and — the part that actually makes it falsifiable — names the null that would retire the concern ("a finding that the shift is small relative to threshold spacing, or that discrimination is unchanged"). A question that specifies in advance what would make it go away is not answerable-either-way. From my arithmetic: the window ambiguity turns out to be nearly inert at the cut-point that matters most. At 20 mm, a +/-2.3 mm and a +/-4.5 mm window both span exactly one multiple of five, so both capture the same digit heap and the answer barely moves. What does move the answer by a lot is the heaping deconvolution — and that is a stated, named, load-bearing assumption rather than a free parameter hidden in the phrasing.

**Status: Partial.** The quantile is genuinely unspecified in the canonical wording and must be fixed before the question is asked; but the indeterminacy that would actually let the answer float is identified and owned, not concealed.

### Attack 6 — Significance inflation: is the audience real, and would they act either way?

**Objection.** The audience is named as CAP, AJCC/UICC, ICCR, NAACCR/SEER. Test it against the nearest precedent: Verbeke published that T category changes in 45% of 315 pancreatic resections depending on how the specimen is examined, and concluded that consensus on an accurate method is required. Four years on, the manuals read as before. If a 45% effect in a major journal moves nothing, a smaller effect from a harder study moves nothing, and "would change the manuals" is inflation.

**Best response.** The precedent cuts less deep than it looks, for a reason I verified rather than assumed. Verbeke's exposure is a *grossing protocol* — planes measured, microscopic corroboration, a third dimension computed from slice counts — so acting on it means mandating a labour-intensive examination method across all pancreatic resections, which is expensive and contestable. Acting on the candidate's finding, if the shift is material, means writing one sentence specifying the measurand state in a protocol that already specifies source priority. That the standard-setters write rules at precisely this granularity is not speculation: the registry document I opened (F3) adjudicates final-diagnosis versus microscopic versus gross, imaging priority, invasive-component precedence, and margin exclusion — four measurand decisions, none of them about physical state. And the sibling discipline has already institutionalised the action: Farrell et al. propose that EQA providers report an incremental reclassification rate, with numeric fitness-for-purpose bands, i.e. laboratory medicine already treats "fraction reclassified at a decision limit" as a thing a standards body publishes limits on. The action pathway is demonstrably real in a field with the same structure.

**Status: Partial.** The audience is real and demonstrably operates at this granularity, and the cheap-action asymmetry is a fair answer to the Verbeke precedent. But I could not evidence that a pT crossing still changes treatment at the flagship site in current practice (attack 4(ii)), and I did no search establishing that Verbeke produced no protocol change — so the deflating half of this objection rests on the record's inference plus absence from my results, which is weak on both sides. Partial, with the specific gap named.

### Attack 7 — Reason for silence: what mechanism?

**Objection.** If this is well-posed and consequential, and the method has existed in three fields for decades, the burden is on the candidate to name a mechanism better than "nobody thought of it".

**Best response.** I found two mechanisms, and one of them is new and better than the record's.

*Mechanism A (evidenced, disciplinary nomenclature).* The estimand has a different name in every field that owns a piece: *incremental reclassification rate* in laboratory medicine (search 7, F4), *classification accuracy/consistency at cut scores* in psychometrics (search 5), *guardbanding / false-accept risk* in metrology (record's search 8), *misclassification from categorising an error-prone exposure* in epidemiology (record's search 6), *definitional uncertainty of the measurand* in metrological vocabulary (search 2), and *deformable registration with shrinkage correction* in imaging (search 3). None of those strings contains a word a pathologist would type. The record's vocabulary-collision point ("tumour shrinkage" means therapy response) is the mirror image of this and I take it as given.

*Mechanism B (new, and the one I would actually defend — a reporting-convention gap).* The papers that measured the thing did not publish the statistic the question needs, because their purpose made it irrelevant. Tran's stated goal is a correction factor "toward standardization of pathological reporting", so the paper reports means and multiplicative factors (1.05, 1.08, 1.13) and gives dispersion only as bare ranges, with no observer-variance analysis at all. A correction factor needs a central estimate; a misclassification risk needs a variance. The field has been generating the former for a decade and has therefore never generated the input the latter requires — and search 11 found no systematic review that would have pooled the per-organ studies and exposed the omission. That is a genuine mechanism, not a shrug: the literature is shaped by the wrong purpose, and the silence is a direct consequence of it.

**Status: Resolved.**

### Attack 8 — Dissolution: would it dissolve under analysis?

**Objection.** The hard limb dissolves into a definition. "Which measurement is correct" has no empirical answer: if the prognostic thresholds were fitted on fixed, processed measurements, then the fixed measurement *is* the calibrated measurand, shrinkage and all, and correcting it would degrade staging. The question then reduces to "pick a convention and write it down" — a committee matter, not a research one — and limb (b)'s alarming fraction evaporates, because patients were never misclassified relative to the measurand the thresholds were built on.

**Best response.** This is the sharpest attack and it does not fully dissolve, for a reason the clinical-chemistry analogue supplies. Farrell's design shows how a field with this exact problem makes it empirical without resolving the metaphysics: you do not need a true value, you need agreement with *the measurement procedure on which the decision limit was derived* — his comparator is the NHANES device, and the estimand is reclassification relative to it. Transposed, the candidate's limb (c) becomes a well-posed empirical question: does stage computed on state X discriminate better than stage computed on state Y, in a cohort with outcomes, against a pre-set equivalence margin — and the pre-registered null ("discrimination unchanged") is exactly the finding that would convert the whole thing into a bounded convention and retire it. That is an answer, not a dissolution.

**But the transposition has a precondition the candidate has not met.** Farrell can name his reference procedure. The candidate's reference — the handling state of the cohorts that calibrated the thresholds — is presupposition 6, for which the record found no source either way, and which I did not check within budget. If that is undocumentable, limb (c) loses its comparator and the question does collapse to convention-setting. So the dissolution risk is real, concentrated entirely on presupposition 6, and checkable before anyone spends money: establishing whether even one threshold's calibration cohort has documentable specimen handling is a cheap, decisive first probe that the record's own "suggested first step" does not mention.

**Status: Partial.**

## 6. Verdict and the single strongest surviving objection

Nothing fatal. Attacks 1 and 7 are Resolved; 2, 4, 5, 6, 8 are Partial; no attack on triviality, disguise or presupposition is Unresolved, so the candidate survives and is recorded as worth asking — but in a materially amended form, and its novelty grade should be *raised* from the record's Low to Medium on evidence the Gate 2 pass did not have. The reason is the task's own rule that a prior's comparator must be the candidate's comparator: I opened Verbeke 2022 and its exposure is the examination protocol — planes measured, microscopic corroboration, a computed third dimension — with no fixation or handling arm, no fixation details, and no mention of formalin shrinkage anywhere on the served page. It is a sibling aimed at an adjacent object, and the "own field, own words" kill was built on a title rather than on an exposure. I also opened Tran 2015 and found that the statistic the question is actually about — the dispersion of the per-step shift, decomposed by observer — is simply not published: means, P values and bare ranges only, no SD, no observer analysis. Limb (b) therefore cannot be assembled from the literature, which is a stronger defence of the candidate than the record mounted for itself.

The single strongest objection that survives is **attack 2, disguise-by-transposition**. The candidate's computational core is a mature, named, already-institutionalised quantity in a field with the same structure: laboratory medicine computes the fraction of a real national population reclassified across a clinical decision limit under a measurement procedure's uncertainty, calls it the incremental reclassification rate, benchmarks it against the procedure the limit was derived on, and has published fitness-for-purpose bands (optimal <1.5%, desirable <3.0%, minimal <4.7%) for it — and educational measurement has been computing classification accuracy at cut scores as routine software output for years. Once that is on the table, the candidate is not "an un-asked question about the decision lattice"; it is one unpublished input (the variance of the handling shift, which the correction-factor literature never reported because correction factors do not need it) plus one measurand-definition choice that both sibling fields dispose of by naming a privileged reference procedure — a reference the candidate cannot yet name, because whether the threshold-calibration cohorts' handling is documentable (presupposition 6) is unchecked. That does not kill it: supplying an unpublished input to a known estimator in a domain where no one has supplied it, and where the registry rules demonstrably specify every measurand dimension *except* the one at issue, is legitimate and consequential work. But it bounds the claim. The honest form of the question is "transpose the reclassification-risk framework to pT size, which requires first measuring a variance the field has never reported and first establishing which handling state the thresholds were calibrated on" — and the required amendments are: pre-specify the window quantile; require variance components rather than correction factors as the primary output; hold the grossing protocol fixed and separate the gross->histologic step from plane count, since Verbeke shows the two are confounded in the only large study; cite the lab-medicine and psychometric estimators rather than reinventing them; and probe presupposition 6 before anything else, because the whole third limb hangs on it.
