## Restatement in my own words

Take the published papers that ran a commercial LLM snapshot that has since been withdrawn, and that left behind their per-item model outputs plus enough configuration to re-run. Re-run those same items on whatever model the vendor's own dated documentation says to use instead. Two numbers are wanted. (a) Item by item, how often does the successor's output differ from the archived predecessor output — judged not against zero but against how much the predecessor disagreed with *itself* across repeat runs. (b) Across an enumerated set of such papers, what fraction of each paper's own stated conclusion flips when re-derived from successor outputs, with a confidence interval, under a rule declared in advance that maps each paper's claim and its own threshold onto a re-derivable decision, with inter-rater reliability on that mapping. The excluded share of the frame, and why each study was excluded, count as part of the answer.

Two things I note before attacking. First, the candidate is a **conjunction**: a quantity (per-item predecessor-successor distance), an instrument (archived outputs, because the predecessor is permanently unrunnable), and a cross-study rate (conclusion reversals over a frame). Each limb can be a known separately. Second, the record itself labels the candidate ASKED-BUT-OPEN, which by this ledger's own definitions ("Open problem: asked but unanswered. Not for this ledger") is a routing decision, not a novelty grade. Attack 7 is where that bites.
