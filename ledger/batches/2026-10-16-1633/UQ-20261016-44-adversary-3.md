## Restatement (my words, not the record's)

Take one pathology archive. Take one cancer diagnosis. Pull blocks from at least three accession
decades. Re-read every era's slides under today's criteria and re-stain from the block, so you are
sure you are comparing the same entity and not a drifting label. Run one pre-committed molecular
panel on all of it in a single randomised batch, together with controls chosen to be era-matched
(paired normal tissue from the same blocks; same-era blocks of other diseases). Then ask three
things: (a) how much does the tumour's molecular profile differ by accession era; (b) how much of
that difference is block-age/fixation damage, as estimated from the controls; (c) is (a) minus (b)
bigger than the between-cohort molecular difference that historical- and external-control borrowing
in trials "already tolerates". "No era effect separable from artefact" is allowed as the answer.

Note on structure, which drives my attack: (a) and (b) are a variance-decomposition study in
archival pre-analytics. (c) is the only limb that makes this a trial-methodology question, and
therefore the only limb carrying the record's Significance 3/3.
