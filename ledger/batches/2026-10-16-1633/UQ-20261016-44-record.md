Question (canonical) [R1 — the one Gate 1 reformulation, adopted]:
Within a single pathology archive, for one diagnosis held fixed by re-review of every era's slides under one contemporary protocol and by re-staining from the block, and on one pre-specified molecular panel assayed in a single batch-randomised run: (a) what is the accession-era effect on the tumour's molecular profile across >=3 eras, (b) what is the block-age/fixation-era artefact estimated from era-matched internal controls (paired non-neoplastic tissue from the same blocks and same-era blocks of other diagnoses), and (c) does the era effect in (a), net of (b) and reported with its interval, exceed the between-cohort molecular distance that external-control and historical-control borrowing already tolerates? "No era effect distinguishable from artefact at achievable sample size" is a pre-specified admissible answer.

Alternative formulations:
- (V0, as intaken) Now that molecular profiling of decades-old formalin-fixed archival tumour blocks is practical, is the molecular phenotype of a histologically and immunophenotypically identical diagnosis constant across accession eras, or has it drifted enough that historical patients are not biologically exchangeable with contemporary ones?
- (lay) Is a 1975 cancer the same disease, on the inside, as a 2020 cancer that a pathologist would give the same name?
- (trial-methods jargon) Is the exchangeability assumption behind historical and external control arms testable at the molecular level in archival tissue, and does it survive the test?
Both versions recorded; R1 differs from V0 only in well-posedness (named estimand, named artefact reference, named benchmark, named admissible null), not in subject.

Motivating gap: Historical and external controls are borrowed in trial design, in attribution of survival improvement to treatment, and in Bayesian dynamic-borrowing priors, and exchangeability is argued entirely from clinical covariates (stage, grade, markers, treatment era). The molecular layer went unchecked because archival material was treated as unreadable. That constraint has now been removed — decade-resolved DIA-MS proteomics of gastric-cancer FFPE from 1972-2022 was published in 2026 (Osaki et al., below) and copy-number assays have been shown workable on blocks back to the 1980s (Krieger et al. 2018). The known secular findings are all changes BETWEEN classes — stage and grade migration, the Will Rogers phenomenon, shifts in the mix of IHC-defined subtypes — whereas this asks whether the inside of a fixed class has changed: a different estimand on a different unit. Intake flagged one query in this vocabulary as a one-query negative; Gate 2 below replaces it with 11 and the negative does NOT fully survive: the design element (era-matched within-case controls over five decades) is already instantiated, and the era-vs-artefact split is where the unasked content now lives.

Presuppositions:
P1 Era-related assay artefact (fixation chemistry, block age, storage history) can be separated from era-related biology. CONTESTED and the crux. Partly supported empirically for the first time by Osaki et al. 2026, which reports measurable storage/era variance against dominant tissue-identity variance using paired within-case controls; but the separation rests on an untested invariance assumption — that the artefact acts on paired normal tissue and on same-era other-diagnosis blocks the way it acts on the tumour. That assumption is transported, not measured, and is the question's main identification risk.
P2 A histological and immunophenotypic class can be held fixed across eras, given that diagnostic criteria and the antibodies themselves changed. CONTESTED; requires re-review under one contemporary protocol and re-staining from the block, never reuse of the original report. Partly circular: antigenicity of old blocks is itself era-dependent (Krieger 2015 checked antigen integrity with a cytokeratin cocktail and lost 2/30 cases), so class-fixing is itself artefact-exposed.
P3 Archives retain early-era blocks in sufficient number with retrievable treatment and outcome data. Partly established, archive-specific (KPNC 1950-2010 and NCC Japan 1972-2022 both demonstrated), and selective retention is itself a selection mechanism to be reported, not assumed away.
P4 Exchangeability of historical controls is currently assumed rather than tested at the molecular level. Supported by this Gate 2 as a two-query negative (queries 2 and 10) in the trial-methods vocabulary — no molecular-level exchangeability test found; the borrowing literature found argues from covariates or from latent-exchangeability modelling of outcomes. Still a search-based absence, not a proof.
P5 A molecular panel exists on which "the same disease" would be expected to be era-invariant. CONTESTED; the panel must be pre-specified or the comparison is uncontrolled multiple testing. R1 requires pre-specification.
P6 Any detected drift would be attributable to exposure, treatment or detection-era biology rather than residual confounding by whoever the archive happens to hold. CONTESTED; an answer can establish non-exchangeability without establishing its cause, and R1 claims only the former.
It is a question, not a thesis: both answers are pre-specified and the null is declared admissible and informative.

What would count as an answer: within one archive, grade- and marker-matched cases of one diagnosis from >=3 accession eras, re-reviewed under one contemporary protocol, profiled on one pre-specified panel in a single batch-randomised run alongside era-matched internal controls (paired non-neoplastic tissue from the same blocks; same-era blocks of other diagnoses), with an estimand that separates the era effect from the block-age artefact estimated from those controls. The answer reports the era effect with its interval against the artefact's measured magnitude and states whether it exceeds the between-cohort molecular difference borrowing already tolerates. A null — no era effect distinguishable from artefact at achievable n — is an answer and is itself informative for borrowing designs.

Significance — consequence: If drift exceeds the artefact: the covariate-only justification for historical and external control arms loses its silent molecular premise in exactly the setting regulators have been most willing to accept it (rare and refractory oncology, where the external control is often years older than the experimental arm); dynamic-borrowing priors would need an era term, not merely a covariate-balance check; and part of the survival improvement attributed to treatment across eras reassigns to case-mix change INSIDE a fixed class, which is the one place stage- and grade-migration corrections do not reach. If drift is not distinguishable from artefact at achievable n: borrowing acquires its first positive molecular-level check rather than an argument from covariates, the magnitude of the measured artefact becomes the published ceiling on how much drift could be hiding, and pathology archives become a validation resource for external-control packages rather than a curiosity. Both answers move the same regulatory judgement, in opposite directions, which is what makes it a question rather than an advocacy exercise.
Significance — audience: (i) trialists and regulators who permit external control arms — they currently adjudicate exchangeability on stage, grade, markers and treatment era, and would gain or lose a molecular-level check; the external-control guidance surfaced in query 2 was not opened, so this claim rests on the practice being established, not on a quoted document; (ii) Bayesian dynamic-borrowing methodologists, whose priors (the NP-LEAP-style latent-exchangeability family found in queries 2 and 10) condition on outcomes and covariates with no era-biology term; (iii) cancer epidemiologists comparing historical with contemporary cohorts, including the Krieger programme itself, whose feasibility papers would have to be re-read as having measured something they declared a nuisance; (iv) FFPE pre-analytics and archival-proteomics groups (Osaki et al.), for whom "era shift" would stop being a single correction term; (v) archive custodians, whose retention policy becomes a scientific selection mechanism with a named consequence. The weak link, stated rather than hidden: nobody has published the tolerance threshold the candidate proposes to measure against, so the audience has no existing decision rule to update — supplying that benchmark is part of the work, not a prerequisite it can assume.
Significance — fecundity: whether drift concentrates in treatment-selected pathways (prior-therapy-era biology) or exposure-related ones (smoking, H. pylori, obesity, screening-era detection), which would turn the test into an aetiological instrument; whether prognostic models of a fixed class need an accession-era covariate, and whether old-cohort-derived signatures are miscalibrated on contemporary patients for that reason; whether the degradation-index machinery of palaeogenomics (query 11) can be imported as a general archival-tissue artefact normaliser; whether grade and stage migration are partly molecular rather than purely definitional; what an era term does to dynamic-borrowing operating characteristics; and whether the same test applied to non-neoplastic archival disease classes shows drift too, which would make it a property of archives rather than of cancer.
Significance — anchoring: the Will Rogers phenomenon and stage/grade migration (between-class secular change, whose within-class complement is unexamined); the live regulatory debate on external and historical control arms; the unexplained decade gradients inside the archival-feasibility literature (P3's CNV-event counts; P2's antigen-integrity losses), which are anomalies reported and not asked about; and the pre-analytics question of whether legacy blocks can be read at all, of which this is a stress test in the direction nobody runs it.

Reason for silence: Three mechanisms, compounding, and none of them is "no one thought of it".
(1) New instrument, genuinely recent: decade-resolved multi-omic profiling of 50-year-old blocks is 2026-vintage (P1), and the 2010s attempts (P2, P3) concluded that pre-1980 material was marginal. Before that the question could be asked but not answered, so it was not worth posing.
(2) Disciplinary boundary of an unusual shape: the material is held by pathology archives, the artefact term is owned by pre-analytics specialists, and the question is needed by trial methodologists and regulators who never touch tissue. The exchangeability vocabulary (queries 2, 10) and the archival-tissue vocabulary (queries 1, 3, 4) returned disjoint literatures with no shared citations in anything I opened.
(3) A professional reflex that forecloses the question: to a pre-analytics scientist, era-associated variation IS artefact by definition — it is the thing the field exists to remove. P1 states the reflex outright ("storage- and archival-era-associated shifts should be explicitly accounted for"), and P3 instantiates it by reporting a decade gradient in CNV events as a feasibility limit without naming a mechanism. A quantity that one field is professionally obliged to normalise away is not a quantity that field will propose as a signal, and the field that would want it as a signal does not know the quantity exists. Add a mild incentive gap: a finding of molecular non-exchangeability devalues the archive-based historical-cohort comparisons that the same groups publish, and no funder's protocol asks for same-era blocks of other diagnoses as controls.

Scores: Well-posedness 2/3 · Significance 3/3
Well-posedness 2/3 and not 3/3: the estimand, the panel pre-specification, the batch randomisation and the admissible null are all statable (so the question is well-posed in the ledger's sense), but identification of era-biology against era-artefact rests on an invariance assumption that the design measures nowhere — that the block-age artefact acts on paired normal tissue and on same-era other-diagnosis blocks as it acts on the tumour. P1's own finding that tissue identity dominates proteomic variance and that muscle is the most stable tissue is evidence AGAINST artefact-invariance across tissue types, which is exactly the assumption the control design needs. Second deduction: P2's class-fixing route is itself era-exposed, since re-staining depends on antigenicity that decays with block age, so "hold the class fixed" and "measure the artefact" are not independent operations. Neither defect is fatal and both are fixable by a stronger control panel (degradation-indexed normalisation, spike-ins, a reported sensitivity analysis over artefact-transport assumptions), which is why this is 2/3 rather than 1/3.
