## Restatement (my words, not the generator's)

Every WHO International Standard for a biological is eventually replaced. The replacement is assayed against the one it replaces in a collaborative study, and then simply *declared* to carry whatever unitage makes the field's answer come out continuous — usually a round number. So by construction the International Unit never moves: the current ampoule defines it. The candidate says: fine, but two things get thrown away at each handover — the small gap between the ratio the study actually estimated and the round number that was assigned, and the study's own spread, which is not carried into the new standard's stated value. Add those up over a chain of three, four, five generations, and ask whether the total is big enough to matter for the clinical cut-offs and pharmacopoeial potency limits that are written in IU as if the IU were a fixed thing — and big enough to matter next to the plain run-to-run sloppiness of the assays that apply those cut-offs.

Put bluntly: *is today's "1 IU" quietly a different quantity from the original one, by enough to invalidate a number in a guideline?* The candidate's own concession is that the point estimate of that drift is zero by definition, so what it is really asking for is a bookkeeping reconstruction (rounding residuals) plus a convolution of historical dispersions.

### What the two opened documents say

- **FDA on the 2009 USP heparin monograph change:** heparin made after 1 Oct 2009 is "approximately 10% less potent" than before; the change "will result in the USP Heparin Unit being harmonized with the international standard unit (IU)"; labelled doses unchanged; FDA "does not recommend that clinicians automatically or routinely increase a patient's heparin dose"; a 10% decrease is "not anticipated to have clinical consequences" in many situations, with a caveat for IV bolus before cardiovascular procedures when aPTT/ACT monitoring is delayed or absent.
