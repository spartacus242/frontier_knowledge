## 1. Restatement in my own words

Alloy systems sometimes have two or more thermodynamic descriptions (CALPHAD assessments) published independently by different groups. Each was fitted to experimental measurements. The question asks: if you build one experimental corpus for such a system, tag every measurement with the study it came from, and then score each competing description twice — once on the whole corpus (an "apparent" residual, recomputed by the analyst under one declared metric because the papers do not print a fit number) and once by leaving out one source study at a time (a "predictive" residual) — do the two scorings put the descriptions in the same order (Kendall tau), how big is the predictive-minus-apparent gap, and in how many systems does the winner change? A finding that the orders agree counts as an answer.

My reading of the operative move: the question imports the apparent-versus-validated-performance device from clinical prediction modelling and the free-R device from crystallography onto already-published, already-fitted parameter sets.

## 6. The kill: why C5 is false, as a dilemma, with the leg named

R1 names two availability conditions and no others. Verification fact (2), two aimed sweeps of the record, establishes that the answer-protocol contains **no re-fitting step** (refit, re-optimise, adjust, tune, calibrate, train: zero occurrences) **and no statement that the parameter set is fixed or frozen**, and declares **no fold aggregation**. So the central operation is undeclared between two readings, and both fail — each for a separately sourced reason.

**LEG A — the frozen reading (THE LEG THE VERDICT STANDS ON).** If the parameters are the published ones, then withholding study *k* estimates nothing from the retained studies, so every per-datum residual on the "held-out" study is numerically the same datum-level residual as in the apparent fit. Verification fact (1) — a verification checker's own arithmetic, which my brief instructs me to rely on without re-fetching, and which I label as that checker's arithmetic and not mine — states that the frozen-parameter leave-one-study-out residual equals the apparent residual **datum for datum** under pooled, datum-equal aggregation, and that under mean-over-folds aggregation the gap equals exactly `-(1/n) * sum_k (n_k - n/K)(r_k - rbar)`.
I do **not** assert the identity universally; that is the error the brief's rule (vi) names. I assert the sharper and aggregation-independent consequence, and it is the formula itself that gives it: whichever aggregation is chosen, the inputs are the *same in-sample* per-study residuals `r_k` and the study sizes `n_k`. Under pooled aggregation the gap is identically zero (tau ≡ 1, gap ≡ 0, zero top-rank changes — limbs (a), (b), (c) answerable in a line with no corpus and no digitisation). Under mean-over-folds the gap is non-zero, takes either sign and can reorder — the checker's worked counter-example gives apparent 1.8182 vs 2.0000 against fold-mean 5.5000 vs 2.0000, rank correlation -1 — but what it then measures is **the covariance between study size and study residual**, not any out-of-sample penalty. So the aggregation hedge defeats "the gap is zero"; it does **not** rescue "the gap is a predictive-minus-apparent penalty". Either way the quantity R1's limb (b) names does not exist.
DOC-A makes this concrete rather than abstract: **"All the experimental data in Refs. [22-25, 43-49] were employed in the present optimization."** For a real, named frame member, every study in the corpus is inside the fit, so no fold of it can be out-of-sample for that assessment while its parameters stand as published. The clause ASSERTS inclusion of the whole named corpus; it does not deny or qualify it.
MY OWN REASONING, MARKED: the step from "identical datum-for-datum to the apparent residual" to "therefore not a predictive residual" is mine, but it is definitional rather than substantive — "held-out" means the held-out data did not inform the parameters, and here they did.

**LEG B — the refit reading.** To make the hold-out genuinely predictive you must re-estimate each description on the retained studies, which requires the assessment's objective: which data entered and with what weights. That is **not** in either declared artifact. Sourced on two instances of the document kind:
- DOC-A §4, read in full by me: **"each piece of experimental data was firstly given a fixed weight"** — weights described, **no values printed**.
- Verification fact (5), ESPEI documents read at full text by the verification pass: the weight is hand-set at the top level of an input JSON keyed to one source and one output type, **outside** the TDB, and the TDB is ESPEI's *output*.
Instances opened/established for this premise: **two**. Instances NOT opened: Fries et al. 1991, any Calphad or JPED paper, the Lukas chapter (route failed). I therefore grade this premise **supported on two instances, not an enumerated universal** — and that is enough, because R1's clause claims sufficiency and one counter-instance defeats a sufficiency claim.
The candidate's best response here is real: the protocol declares its own common metric and item (4) mandates "a weighting-sensitivity panel ... under at least two pre-declared weighting schemes" (verification fact 2), so the analyst can supply the objective. MY OWN REASONING ABOUT THE PROTOCOL'S LOGIC, NOT A SOURCE FINDING: if the analyst supplies it, the predictive residual comes from parameters refitted under the analyst's objective while the apparent residual comes from the authors' published parameters fitted under an unknown objective, so limb (b) conflates an out-of-sample penalty with an objective substitution and limb (a) correlates an ordering of published parameter sets against an ordering of the analyst's refits. The search that would have grounded this leg and which I did not run: a study that refits competing published CALPHAD descriptions of one system under one common objective and reports both orderings. The nearest thing in hand is verification fact (7)'s note that arXiv:2604.22910 "REFITS competing descriptions of one element and then scores them" — in-sample AICc only, read by the verification pass, **not by me**. Because this leg's load-bearing step is my own inference, **Leg B alone would be Partial**.

**Which leg the verdict stands on if the other falls: LEG A.** Leg A is sourced to verification fact (1)'s formula and verification fact (2)'s aimed sweeps, plus DOC-A's own inclusion sentence read in full by me, with only a definitional step of my own.

**What the candidate's existing defences do not reach.** P4 is the nearest defence, and it is a deferral rather than an answer: record line 67, verbatim via verification fact (3), says P4 "is handled by requiring the protocol to declare its hold-out structure rather than by settling the point", and the structures it names are cross-PROPERTY and cross-COMPOSITION-REGION. Neither repairs Leg A — with unchanged published parameters, a cross-property hold-out is still the apparent residual on that property subset, because no parameter was estimated from the withheld property. And the measurand exemption does not cover C5: R1 reports tau and the gap as results, but it **assumes** that the gap is a predictive-minus-apparent quantity. That assumption is the one that fails.

## 8. Recommended verdict

The record's own demotion condition is untouched — PRIOR 1 (Wertz et al. 2018, DOI 10.1557/jmr.2018.61) is unread past its abstract by everyone, and arXiv:1902.01269 is unopened by everyone.

**What would reverse this rejection, nominated BY KIND and checked against the nominee.** Kind needed: a document that (a) covers published CALPHAD assessments as its population, (b) *perturbs* the data entering the optimisation — i.e. re-runs the optimisation on a subset of source studies — and (c) **prints** both the refitted description's residual on the withheld studies and the full-data residual, for two or more competing descriptions of one system. The protocol's own missing declaration would then be supplied by precedent. Named nominee: **Lukas, Fries & Sundman, "Experimental data used for optimization", DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511804137.005** — this is the right kind for the *weighting-convention* half only (it is the field's methodology text on what data enter an optimisation and how they are weighted, which is exactly Leg B's premise); it is **not** the right kind for (b) or (c), since a textbook chapter will not print a refitted held-out residual for competing descriptions. **I am saying plainly that I have no nominee of the full kind**; naming one from a title would be a guess.
