Question (canonical): In published LLM and agent evaluations that release per-item logs with error fields, on a pre-declared frame: (a) what share of the nominal denominator carries a harness-side failure, by class, under a hand-validated taxonomy; (b) holding the estimand fixed, how far apart are the drop-the-item (per-protocol) and score-as-failure (ITT) estimators in score, rank correlation and counted pairwise rank reversals; (c) separately, as a distinct estimand, what does retry-to-completion give; (d) does harness-failure probability correlate with model identity (making the drop estimator biased, not merely lossy); and (e) what fraction of published evaluations state their convention at all? (Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted; Gate 0 original in the record.)
Alternative formulations:
1. What is an evaluation's intention-to-treat score as opposed to its per-protocol score, and do the two rank models differently?
2. How often does a reported benchmark number depend on how the harness handled its own errors?
Motivating gap: Intake (silence mapping): the harness layer is dense with how-answers (retry policies, timeouts, answer-extraction, exception handling) and empty of why-questions about what the denominator is; a reported gap smaller than the harness-failure share cannot be read as a capability gap. Gate 2 confirmed the how-answer density but falsified "empty of why-questions": the question is posed and partly answered in the field's own words (retry bias, infrastructure noise, fault-side localisation), and both adversaries found further occupied vocabularies.

P2 a usable share of evaluations release error fields — UNVERIFIED (unrun; cannot kill, cannot pass). P3 rankings close enough to move — ESTABLISHED independently (MathArena reversal; Anthropic 6 pp spread, 3 pp skepticism floor). P5 no standard fixes the convention — SURVIVES for standards (Rollout Cards spec has no such field) but fails for harnesses (Inspect publishes a "Counts in denominator?" column and a default). P6 failure rates large enough to matter — ESTABLISHED AND PUBLISHED (5.8%/2.1%/0.5% by resource config, ~6% pod errors; 23.7% of one theoretical grid lost, adversary's own arithmetic).

- MathArena (matharena.ai/retry) INVERTS the rejection's reading of it. The sentence the record cited as its convention, "If all attempts fail, the solution is marked as incorrect", is verbatim present but sits under the heading "Fixed number of retries:" - the option the authors explicitly decline: "We prefer the first strategy and do not report model performance until a sufficiently stable implementation is available." The choice is posed as TWO options ("There are two commonly used strategies for retrying failed requests:") and dropping the item is not one of them; the only denominator remark is that under a hard cutoff "the denominator may be zero", in which case "accuracy is undefined". So the article poses retry-until-success against fixed-retries, not drop against score-as-failure. The rest is confirmed: "frequent retries can overestimate performance by up to 5 percent on MathArena benchmarks" is verbatim, though percent-versus-percentage-points is never stated and neither "percentage points" nor "pp" occurs anywhere; the reversal is real - "GPT-5.1 outperforms DeepSeek-v3.2 at high stability levels, but the ranking reverses at lower stability" - but holds "under a maximum-time failure model" in simulation; and 8855 model-problem pairs is right (written without a thousands comma, Figure 1 caption). Three absences are now settled by reading rather than assumed: no real-data share of runs hit by a harness failure (the failure arms are simulated, and "our historical data did not record the time taken per request"), no drop-the-item arm, and no count of publications stating their convention.
- Rollout Cards (arXiv:2605.12131) does not census what the record said it censuses. "None of the 50 repositories reports how often rollouts failed alongside the accuracy or score." is verbatim, as are the three counts - "In our audit, 11 scored entries silently absorb catastrophic failures into the reported score,", "31 drop failures with no visible counter", "9 log failures somewhere but do not surface them beside the reported score." - but they are over SCORED AUDIT ENTRIES rather than repositories, which the paper says itself ("Counts are over scored audit entries rather than mutually exclusive repositories;", with simple-evals split across two subsets), and 11 + 31 + 9 = 51 against 50 repositories. It measures what repositories report and do - reporting prevalence - not the share of runs hit by a harness failure. Its score movements come from a separate re-grading study of archived outputs, and only ONE is a denominator convention: SWE-bench Verified with missing patches counted as failures versus excluded, 15.6 against 13.3, a 2.3-point gap.
