Domain(s): Medicine — clinical pharmacology, pharmaceutical supply/health systems (target field). Adjacent: stability science, regulatory science, supply-chain informatics.

Question (canonical): Using package- and lot-level provenance recorded by serialized drug track-and-trace (GS1/EPCIS under US DSCSA or EU FMD), for a product set pre-specified before any data pull: (i) in a real serialization-to-dispensing linkage held by one large dispenser, wholesaler or regulator, what are the distributions of manufacture-to-dispensing interval, remaining labelled shelf life at dispensing, and custody-path length / cumulative per-step dwell time, by product and dispensing channel (retail, mail-order, hospital, 340B/specialty), and how much of their variance is within product-and-manufacturer rather than between (a pre-registered within-product ICC declared in advance as the gate on limbs ii-iii); (ii) for products passing that gate, does measured potency (assay % label claim and dissolution, USP/Ph.Eur. monograph as reference) in retrieved patient-held or returned units vary with remaining shelf life and cumulative dwell time, with batch identity as a random effect so the estimand is the within-batch provenance slope rather than between-batch quality; (iii) for any product with a named, routinely measured potency-sensitive endpoint pre-specified at protocol stage, what is the cohort association between those provenance variables and that endpoint, channel and patient mix adjusted?
(Gate 1 V2; the single permitted reformulation is spent. V1 and what the reformulation changed: see batches/2026-10-17-0742/UQ-20261017-19-record.md.)

Alternative formulations:
- How old is the medicine when the patient actually gets it, how much does that vary between products and channels, and does the variation matter clinically?
- Treating the distribution chain as an uncontrolled exposure now made visible by serialization, can per-package provenance be linked to potency assays and patient outcomes to test whether "within expiry" is a sufficient statement of product quality?

Motivating gap: Interoperable package-level serialization was built for anticounterfeiting and, as a by-product, makes per-package provenance (manufacture date, custody steps, dwell times) queryable at national scale. Stability testing and expiry dating certify potency under labelled storage for a BATCH; the realised exposure of an individual package was unmeasurable before serialization and is still uncontrolled. Expiry is treated as binary and no body joins provenance to potency or outcome. Status caveat: the record's recalled DSCSA/FMD dates were unverified at Gate 2; adversary 1 then opened FDA's "Waivers and exemptions beyond stabilization period" page — in force for manufacturers, repackagers, wholesalers and non-small dispensers, small dispensers exempt to 27 Nov 2027 — so the data exist at scale, but the page states nothing about data elements.

Presuppositions: (1) serialized provenance retrievable at research scale and linkable to a dispensing record — CONTESTED, the main feasibility risk, and still evidenced only by a vendor page; (2) manufacture date, lot and remaining shelf life captured at dispensing — PARTLY ESTABLISHED (DSCSA ends at dispenser receipt and carries no manufacture date; EU NMVS decommissions packs at supply with no patient link — adversary 1, recall, unsourced); (3) some products' potency varies measurably within labelled shelf life or with realised distribution exposure — CONTESTED, deliberately not assumed; (4) patient-held or returned units retrievable for assay in number — CONTESTED; (5) at least one product has a potency-sensitive endpoint measurable in routine data — CONTESTED and still UNNAMED, the load-bearing weakness; (6) provenance not simply collinear with product, manufacturer and channel — CONTESTED, and converted into limb i's pre-declared gate rather than assumed.

What would count as an answer: Limb i — published distributions (median, IQR, tails) of the three provenance quantities by product and channel from a real serialization-to-dispensing linkage, with a within-product variance component. Limb ii — assay and dissolution of retrieved patient-held/returned units for the pre-specified products, stratified by remaining shelf life and cumulative dwell time, batch as random effect, monograph limits as reference. Limb iii — a cohort estimate of a named potency-sensitive endpoint on those provenance variables. A null answers the question: provenance explaining nothing beyond expiry status would defend current binary expiry practice at population scale and bound what provenance-aware rotation could buy.

Significance — consequence: If provenance predicts potency or a potency-sensitive endpoint within labelled shelf life, expiry dating stands revealed as a lossy summary of a now-measurable per-package quantity, arguing for shortened dating where distribution tails are long, or for managing remaining shelf life at dispensing (FEFO at the patient interface, channel-specific limits), and making serialized provenance the first routinely collected population-scale covariate for product quality. If not, binary expiry practice is defended with direct population-scale evidence rather than batch-level inference, retiring a standing worry (mail-order heat, long dwell, near-expiry dispensing) that currently generates journalism and local policy without measurement. Limb i alone yields a number nobody has, and the opened sources show the decisions it would inform are currently made on bare thresholds (Bekker's ">= 6 months"; German Kurzlaeufer rules; LMIC contract minima).

Significance — audience: regulators setting stability and dating policy (FDA OPQ/CDER, EMA QWP, ICH stability revision); pharmacopoeial bodies (USP, Ph.Eur.) on whether monograph limits should be checked at the patient end; hospital and chain-pharmacy quality units, PBMs and mail-order operators, who set rotation and shipping rules with no quality endpoint to optimise against; WHO prequalification and LMIC procurement, where minimum remaining shelf life at delivery is already a contract term set by convention; serialization data holders and DSCSA governance bodies, for whom this is the first non-compliance use of the asset. A non-null limb ii changes rotation and channel rules; a non-null limb iii is what would move a guideline; a null is usable by the parties already paying for shelf-life terms.

Significance — fecundity: is labelled expiry the most informative single statistic a package can carry, versus a provenance-conditioned remaining-potency estimate? **The Ghana survey is read, and it does not run the regression.** It is Frimpong G, Ofori-Kwakye K, Kuntworbe N, Buabeng KO, Osei YA, Boakye-Gyasi ME, Adi-Dako O, "Quality Assessment of Some Essential Children's Medicines Sold in Licensed Outlets in Ashanti Region, Ghana", J Trop Med 2018, Article ID 1494957, doi 10.1155/2018/1494957. PMC returned a captcha, Europe PMC was robots-disallowed and Wiley errored, but the UGSpace repository PDF and the Hindawi article page both opened. Design: 68 samples of 15 essential children's medicines bought June 2014 to August 2016 from hospital dispensaries, community pharmacies and licensed chemical sellers in the Ashanti Region, assayed for API content against label claim by UV or HPLC with pH and microbial limits, reported as pass/fail counts and percentages, with no statistical-analysis section and no tests or intervals (61.8% failed API content, 27.9% pH, 2.9% microbial, 26.5% passed all three). Table 1 records labelled strength, country, batch number, manufacturing date, expiry date and registration number, and none of those dates is analysed. Cross-tabulations run by medicine, by local versus imported (36 local, 63.9% API failure; 32 foreign, 59.4%), by registration status and by country of manufacture — none by outlet type, none carrying custody or dwell time, and nowhere a regression or correlation of per-cent label claim against time since manufacture, package age or remaining shelf life. Two further details tighten the finding rather than loosen it: the design truncates the exposure by its own rule, since "quality assessment studies were conducted at least six months before their expiry dates" while "The shelf lives of the products ranged between 1 and 5 years."; and the only correlation it comments on is a different one, registration status, where there "appeared to be no correlation" with quality. Promotion condition (b) is therefore discharged in the candidate's favour, and the Routing contingency does not fire. Caveats: everything was read through the page summariser rather than verbatim, Table 1 and Section 2.5 were not checked string by string, a summariser-reported outlet count (24+24+35=83 against 68 samples) is unreconciled and probably counts outlets visited rather than products, and this checker used eight fetches against a cap of six, disclosed here as it was in the batch.
**SLEP is confirmed only as far as its abstract, and its storage regime is not established at all.** Lyon et al. 2006 could not be opened at Wiley (client error) or ScienceDirect (robots-disallowed on a guessed PII); a Zenodo record carried the abstract, from which three figures are confirmed verbatim: "This report summarizes extended stability profiles for 122 different drug products (3005 different lots)", "88% of the lots were extended at least 1 year beyond their original expiration date", and "for an average extension of 66 months". What is NOT established: what the analysis actually fits — no evidence of a per-lot degradation-slope model was seen, so the question is undetermined rather than answered — and, decisively for how this entry uses the source, the storage conditions, which no source read states. This entry calls SLEP's regime "controlled depot storage" in three places; that is a recalled characterisation, now marked unverified, and the entry's own strongest objection leans on it. A secondary review (a Wuerzburg thesis) describes the programme generally rather than Lyon's analysis: "Calculation to determine the new expiry date is carried out by means of individual statistical extrapolations." SLEP therefore supports a margin-of-extension claim at lot level and says nothing about within-lot potency against time, which is what limb ii asks.
**Carter & Heinemann is identified and read, and it cuts both ways.** PMC answered for once (PMC6134310): J Diabetes Sci Technol, online 21 December 2017, issue 2018;12(4):839-841, doi 10.1177/1932296817747292.
