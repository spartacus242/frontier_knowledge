Question (canonical): Do the individual DNA molecules that carry a somatic mutation differ in their own local methylation state from matched molecules without a mutation at the same locus in the same sample? (V1, as intaken.)
Alternative formulations:
- V2 (the one Gate 1 reformulation, for well-posedness): in single-molecule, single-strand-resolved sequencing of non-clonal somatic tissue, is local 5mC density over a pre-specified window **excluding the mutated base and every CpG the mutation creates or destroys** associated with a molecule's mutation status, versus mutation-free molecules matched on locus, sample and strand (matched conditional logistic, stratified by mutation class, with a negative-control class and a per-read methylation-call error model)?
- Is the methylation–mutation association a molecule-level fact or only a regional correlation?
Motivating gap: everything the field believes about methylation shaping mutation rate rests on **regional** density-vs-density correlation, which cannot separate a cis, molecule-level mechanism from regional confounders (replication timing, chromatin, repair access) that covary at the same scale; per-molecule mutation+5mC co-measurement (MethylSaferSeqS 2023; duplex/single-strand designs) now exists. The intake's claim that nothing pairs a mutation with the modification state of its own molecule is false — Gate 2 found that pairing in print.
Presuppositions: (1) per-read 5mC calls accurate enough — CONTESTED, no measured per-read error rate obtained; (2) current methylation informative about state when the mutation arose — STRONGLY CONTESTED, false in general (mark at a mutated CpG is physically destroyed), so the claim is an association only; (3) matched mutation-free molecules at the same locus/sample/strand are a valid comparator — partly contested, operational in P1; (4) per-molecule mutation calls are not damage/chemistry artefacts — addressed in principle by single-strand-resolved designs, harder under conversion chemistry; (5) enough mutant molecules at CpG-containing loci for power — UNDEMONSTRATED (P1: 11 and 65 mutant UIDs at two loci); (6) sample is non-clonal.
What would count as an answer: a matched per-molecule odds ratio (conditional logistic; matched on locus, sample, strand) for local 5mC density on mutation-bearing vs mutation-free molecules over a pre-declared window excluding the mutated base and mutation-created/destroyed CpGs, stratified by mutation class, with a pre-specified per-read error model and a negative-control class. A bounded null (tight CI around OR=1 at adequate power) would be equally informative.

**What MethylSaferSeqS actually reports.** Read at first hand from a repository copy of PNAS 120(15):e2220704120 and cross-checked against the medRxiv preprint (10.1101/2022.12.06.22283116), which agreed on every string checked. The method does link sequence and methylation for one original molecule: barcoded adapters carry 5-methylcytosine so they survive bisulfite, each strand is linearly copied about three times with a biotinylated dU-containing primer, the unmethylated copies carry the sequence into the mutation library while the original strands keep their methylation and go to the methylation library, and a shared UID joins them. So the instrument exists. But the mutant-versus-wild-type methylation comparison is reported **in two patients only, as a demonstration that the method works**, and the authors chose those two because the mutation sits "in a CpG dyad that affected the methylation status of the strands" — that is, the mutation itself creates or destroys the very CpG being measured, which is why the text reads "As expected". The numbers: for PIK3CA p.H1047R, "Of those, 11 (2.7%) contained the PIK3CA p.H1047R mutation", and "a high fraction of mutant UIDs (82% of 11) were methylated in the CGT trimer" against "only 1.3% of the 398 C residues in the analogous CAT trimer in the wild-type UIDs"; for TP53 p.R248W, 65 of 900 shared UIDs (7.2%) were mutant, with 98% of 835 wild-type UIDs methylated in the CCG trimer against 3.1% of mutant CCA trimers, the latter attributed to "incomplete bisulfite conversion".
**Why that is not the candidate's question.** The read-out is **the mutated base itself**, the cytosine of the CpG dyad, in a CGT/CAT or CCG/CCA trimer. The paper describes no flanking window and the preprint contains no comparison of flanking CpGs. A methylation difference at a cytosine that the mutation converts is a restatement of the sequence change, not independent epigenetic information. So the judge's reasoning inverts: moving the read-out window off the mutated base to flanking CpGs is not "a measurement-window swap ... keeping the same unit, comparator, sample and instrument" — it is the move that makes the quantity carry information the genotype does not already determine. The Gate 2 regrade to Low is withdrawn and novelty returns to Medium.
**The second prior is struck.** The entry credited the sharpened version with "borrowing its neighbourhood estimand from Kusmartsev 2020". Kusmartsev et al. 2020 (Genetics 214:809, "Cytosine Methylation Affects the Mutability of Neighboring Nucleotides in Germline and Soma") runs the other way: it is a population-level association in which methylation of a CpG predicts the incidence of flanking SNPs — "nucleotides in the close vicinity (+/-3 bp) of methylated cytosines mutate less frequently", measured as "the incidence of singleton SNPs" at each surrounding base. Different unit (population site, not molecule), different endpoint (mutation incidence, not methylation), and the opposite direction of inference. It is background on how methylation shapes mutation risk, not prior art for whether mutation-bearing molecules differ in their own flanking methylation. The attribution is removed.

**The conversion leg of the entry's own strongest objection, checked separately (logs -verification-conversion.md and -hidefseq.md).** That objection had a second, quantitative leg that the first verification pass had not checked, so it was checked before any promotion. Its numbers are where it puts them and its inference is not. Confirmed in the preprint: "average conversion efficiency of 77% (IQR: 75% to 79%)" for the LM libraries, and the 50C/3h protocol was chosen by trial and error for yield — "with 80% of unmethylated C's converted to U's", about 70% of input molecules retained — because 50C/16h gave over 98% conversion at the cost of "loss of half of the original DNA template molecules". So the trade-off is real and deliberate, and 23% of unmethylated cytosines going unconverted follows arithmetically from the library average. What is not supported is the step that made the objection fatal. The unit of that figure is the **library**, with the IQR taken across libraries; the paper reports no per-molecule conversion rate and no per-read or per-molecule methylation-call error rate anywhere in the text read, and defines fractional methylation as "the ratio of the total number of unconverted cytosines in CpG sites to the total number of CpG sites", aggregated across molecules per marker. The claim that conversion failure is a per-molecule event whose error is therefore correlated across the whole window is the adversary's own derivation, and the source measures neither the per-molecule rate nor its correlation structure. The magnitude is also overstated against the only in-paper measurement of it: at the TP53 mutant CCA trimer — a cytosine the mutation has already taken out of CpG context, so any apparent methylation there is pure conversion artefact — 3.1% of C residues read as methylated, not ~23%, a sevenfold gap the entry's arithmetic does not account for. Two further points of reasoning, labelled as reasoning rather than as sourced findings: a library-level conversion deficit is common to both arms of a comparison matched on locus, sample, strand and library, so it attenuates a contrast and costs power rather than manufacturing one; and conversion artefact is in principle per-read detectable from the non-CpG cytosines on the same molecule, which is standard bisulfite QC. Per the ledger's rule that a kill which cannot be sourced is Partial at best, **the conversion leg drops to Partial**: a real and material measurement problem, not a fatal objection.
