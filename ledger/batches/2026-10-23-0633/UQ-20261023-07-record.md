## Gate 0 — Intake (as received)

Question (canonical, V1): In adult inpatients transfused under a single-unit policy, what fraction of the between-episode variance in the post-transfusion haemoglobin increment is attributable to the measured haemoglobin mass of the specific red-cell unit issued, and does the probability of needing a second unit depend on which unit the patient happened to receive?

Alternative formulations (as received):
- Is "one unit" a dose — how much of the variability in haemoglobin response sits in the bag rather than in the patient?
- If a blood service labelled each red-cell unit with its haemoglobin mass and issued by mass instead of by count, how many fewer second units would be needed to reach the same haemoglobin target?

Motivating gap (as received): the single-unit policy ("transfuse one unit, then reassess") presupposes that a unit is a reproducible dose, while regulatory quality monitoring sets only a MINIMUM haemoglobin content per unit, so issued units span a range of haemoglobin mass and the delivered dose is unmeasured at the bedside.

Three limbs are distinguished throughout this record because they do not share a fate:
- **L1** variance share of the increment attributable to the measured Hb mass of the issued unit (plus the slope of increment on grams delivered);
- **L2** does the probability of a second unit depend on which unit the patient happened to receive (natural, un-selected variation in issued units);
- **L3** the counterfactual: under mass-based issuing at the same Hb target, how many fewer second units.

## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

## Gate 3 — Significance (argued)

- **Consequence (yes/no):** If a large share of between-episode increment variance sits in the bag, then "one unit" is not a dose, the single-unit audit metric is measuring an input that varies by tens of percent, and mass-labelling or mass-based issuing becomes a cheap, auditable intervention — with Prior 1 already indicating the direction of the resource effect. If the share is small with a flat slope, the convention is vindicated, the storage-and-donor-factor literature loses a candidate nuisance term, and the question retires. Both answers change something, which is the test.
- **Audience:** blood services' quality and inventory functions (who already assay Hb content on a sampled basis and would have to decide whether to assay all); PBM committees and guideline writers who own the single-unit recommendation and its audit indicator; regulators who set the per-unit MINIMUM content rather than a target or tolerance; and the vein-to-vein research groups (Prior 2's consortium) who hold the linkage but not the assay. Each would act differently: assay policy, indicator definition, specification form, variable list.
- **Fecundity:** opens unit-level dose labelling as a design object; a tolerance-based rather than minimum-based specification; variance-based rather than count-based transfusion audit; and a clean re-reading of null storage-duration trials in which unlabelled dose heterogeneity is a noise term that was never modelled.
- **Anchoring:** the single-unit policy's own evidence base; the expected-versus-observed increment literature; the storage lesion trials' nulls; and this ledger's own UQ-20261017-36 (nominal versus delivered inhaled dose), which is the same label-is-not-the-dose family in another modality.
- **Significance is dented, not absent:** the practice-facing half of the question has a randomized answer at one centre, so what remains is scale, mechanism and the variance attribution rather than the policy question itself.

## Reason for silence

A disciplinary boundary with an incentive gap on each side, which is a mechanism rather than an absence of imagination. Per-unit haemoglobin content lives in blood-component manufacturing as a regulator-facing, SAMPLED, pass/fail quantity against a minimum (JPAC Red Book 7.3.3 and the quality-criteria literature returned at query 7), so no service has an obligation that would make it assay every unit, and the figure that would be the candidate's exposure is therefore not generated for the units actually issued. The increment lives in the recipient's chart. The two are joined only in vein-to-vein research databases, which carry manufacturing METADATA (collection method, additive solution, irradiation, storage duration) precisely because that is what is recorded per unit at no extra cost — which is why Prior 2 can model donor and component characteristics on 23,194 recipients and still not have grams in the bag. And the one group that did bridge it (Prior 1) reached for the clinically actionable version, the randomized policy comparison, rather than the variance decomposition, because that is what a haematology service can act on. The candidate's L1 sits in the gap between an assay nobody is required to run and a linkage nobody built for it.
