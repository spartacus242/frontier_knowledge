## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take every pair of published phylogenetic papers that share enough taxa to be comparable after
you have reconciled their taxon names. In each pair, find the branches that paper A printed with
a ≥95% confidence label (bootstrap, UFBoot or posterior probability) and ask how often paper B
printed, also at ≥95%, a branch that cannot coexist with A's on the taxa the two share. Report
that frequency — separately per support type, and split by how much data the two papers share and
by era. Since two incompatible splits cannot both be splits of one true tree, that frequency is a
floor on how often a ≥95% label is wrong somewhere in the pair; compare it with what ≥95% is
supposed to mean. In short: **audit the field's confidence labels against the field's own
published disagreements, with no reference tree anywhere in the design.** The pre-registered
alternative answer is that the audit cannot be run because archives do not keep support with
topology — which the record says must then be reported as an availability/granularity finding.

**Single strongest killing point (a demotion, not a kill):** Thomson & Brown 2022 state that "virtually all phylogenomic analyses recover maximal support values", so in the genome-scale stratum the ≥95% conditioning that makes this question new is close to vacuous — and that is precisely the stratum where the conditioning would have mattered most, while the pre-genomic stratum where it still discriminates is the one whose support values are least often deposited (1 of 5 opened studies). The candidate's novel ingredient is therefore thinnest exactly where its significance argument is strongest.

**Nomination, justified by document kind rather than by title.** The clause still open is the repair's premise that study-to-study data overlap is computable from what papers report. The kind of document that could settle it is not a paper *about* overlap: it is **two or three ordinary empirical phylogenetics papers' Methods/Data-availability sections**, which must (i) cover the same taxa as another published study, and (ii) **print** a locus or alignment identifier resolvable to another study's loci — a gene-name table, accession list or alignment DOI. That is the premise's own document kind, and the check is two fetches. I nominate **no specific paper**, because naming one from a title is the guess this ledger has twice been burned by; the correct move is to draw two instances from the same taxon group as a known pair and read what they print.
