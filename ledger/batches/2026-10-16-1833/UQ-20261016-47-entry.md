Domain(s): AI architecture — tokenization and the pretraining data layer. Neighbours searched: metrology, psychometrics (standard error of equating), evaluation methodology / variance components, data compression (dictionary training), vector quantization.
Question (canonical): Holding corpus, algorithm, pre-tokenizer and vocabulary size fixed, draw k ≥ 5 independent fitting samples of size S from one pretraining corpus and fit one tokenizer per draw: (a) what is the between-draw divergence of the resulting tokenizers — vocabulary Jaccard, merge-sequence agreement, per-domain fertility SD on held-out text — as a function of S; and (b) when one model per draw is pretrained on identical text at matched byte and FLOP budgets, how large is the between-draw SD of validation bits-per-byte and of downstream scores, relative to two reference quantities fixed in advance — the seed-only SD under a single fixed tokenizer, and the published algorithm-choice / vocabulary-size gap? (Gate 1 reformulation, version B; version A, with "identical token budgets" and "loss-per-FLOP", is recorded in the record file.)
Alternative formulations: (1) Is a tokenizer a reproducible artifact of a recipe, or a sample statistic with a standard error nobody has reported? (2) When a paper reports that tokenizer A beats tokenizer B, how much of that gap lies inside the noise of refitting either tokenizer on a fresh sample of the same data?
Motivating gap: Pretraining recipes name two tokenizer design dimensions (algorithm, vocabulary size) and report the fitting sample's provenance and replication not at all, while BPE/Unigram fitting is a sample-dependent greedy estimation run on a slice chosen by a library default (SentencePiece `input_sentence_size` / `shuffle_input_sentence`). TokEval fits all 44 custom tokenizers on 10GB with no sampling or replicate detail; TokSuite puts from-scratch fits out of scope. Corrected by adversary #1: fitting-data *size* is in fact commonly reported (Dagan 10B chars; Schmidt et al. MiniPile 6GB; TokEval 10GB) — the defensible gap is that the sampling procedure and replicate count are unreported.
Presuppositions: (1) Tokenizer fitting is sample-dependent — true by construction; magnitude partly measured in bias form by arXiv:2502.20273, and now also in variance form (see Gate 4). (2) Published algorithm-choice and vocabulary-size gaps each come from one fit and one seed — VERIFIED (Ali et al. 2310.08754: 24 tokenizers / 26 models, one seed, no CIs, seeds explicitly dropped; TokEval; TokSuite). (3) Differently-tokenized models are comparable on one loss scale — contested; handled by bits-per-byte at matched bytes and FLOPs; TokEval's 46-model BPB comparison is precedent. (4) Compute suffices for k refits × ≥2 scales — the binding constraint (Ali et al.'s single-seed grid ≈ 59k GPU-hours). (5) Merge-sequence distance and vocabulary Jaccard are meaningful divergence measures — contested (2508.06621; 2305.03029), so fertility carries limb (a) and merge divergence is descriptive only. (6) The fitting sample is drawn from the same corpus as the pretraining data — design requirement, and the point of difference from tokenizer-transfer / mixture-composition work. Adversary corrections: (4') as written, "identical text" plus matched bytes AND matched FLOPs is over-constrained, since fertility differs between draws — one of the two budgets must be fixed and the other reported; (5') tokenizer fitting is not even deterministic given its input (huggingface/tokenizers #1794; frequency ties, Hayase App. C.7), so the within-draw control is non-trivial.
What would count as an answer: A variance-component decomposition with CIs. Limb (a): k ≥ 5 fits per cell on disjoint samples S ∈ {0.1, 1, 10} GB, everything else fixed; between-draw vocabulary Jaccard, merge agreement and per-domain fertility SD vs S, each against a within-draw control. Limb (b): one model per draw at ≥2 scales on identical text at matched byte/FLOP budgets, plus seed replicates under one fixed tokenizer; between-draw SD of BPB and downstream scores against (i) seed-only SD, (ii) the published algorithm-choice gap (Ali et al.'s ≈1.7-point monolingual average-accuracy gap; ≈9% best-vs-worst on ARC-Easy). Decision rule: "sample matters" = between-draw SD of the same order as the design gap; "does not" = at or below seed SD at the smallest S.

Significance — consequence: If between-draw SD is of the order of the design gap, every single-fit tokenizer ablation (Ali et al.'s ≈1.7-point averages, TokEval's 46 one-model conditions, TokSuite's 0.10-wide robustness spread) is reporting a design effect confounded with an unestimated variance term, and replicate counts plus a reported fitting corpus become mandatory. If it is at or below seed SD, current practice is licensed explicitly and a standing reviewer objection is retired. Either way the S-curve with its knee answers "how many bytes should I fit on?" in variance terms. Discounted by the verdict: Prior 4 already supplies the vocabulary-Jaccard half of that curve, and Schmidt et al. (arXiv:2402.18376, EMNLP 2024) report that even whole-algorithm swaps are "within the noise" at 350M–2.4B, which disputes the yardstick the consequence is stated against.
Significance — audience: Pretraining data/infrastructure teams choosing the fitting slice by default; the active comparative-tokenizer line (Ali et al. 2024; Dagan et al. 2024; Schmidt et al. 2024; TokSuite 2025; TokEval 2026; "Objective vs. Search" 2026), which states the missing comparison in its own limitations; evaluation-methodology researchers who publish variance budgets for seeds, data order and benchmark items but not for preprocessing artifacts; SentencePiece and HF `tokenizers` maintainers who expose subsampling knobs with no documented consequence. Adversary caveat: what those papers ask for is *model-run* noise estimates; none asks specifically for the fitting-sample component.
Significance — fecundity: How many refits must an ablation average over before a gap is publishable? Does the variance scale with morphological richness or low-resource status? Can it be engineered away (bagged or regularised fitting)? Is between-draw divergence a cheap screen for brittleness under domain shift, avoiding model training? Should a shipped tokenizer carry a stated uncertainty?
Significance — anchoring: The unreported fitting-sample-procedure gap; the contested cross-tokenizer loss-comparison problem; the ML variance-reporting programme (seeds, data order, benchmark items — not preprocessing artifacts); TokEval's unexplained instability of math/code tokenizer rankings under continued training; the mature standard-error-of-equating and finite-training-set-codebook literatures.
