# Adversary #1 — UQ-20261016-14

## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Pull the old genetic test reports in which a laboratory told a patient they had a recessive
disease because they carried two different mutations in the same gene — restricting to the
reports where nobody ever checked which parental chromosome each mutation sat on (no parental
testing, no read-backed phasing). Go back to the leftover DNA and use long reads to look
directly. Count what share turn out to have both mutations on one chromosome copy, leaving the
other copy intact — in which case the stated basis for the diagnosis does not hold. Then, among
those, count how many have no other hidden damaging change (deep intronic, structural,
methylation) on the intact copy, which is the share of reports that were actually wrong rather
than merely wrongly reasoned. Report both with confidence intervals, broken down by how far
apart the two mutations are, by gene, by report era, and by whether the lab leaned on a
population cis/trans prior; and report how much of the original report archive still had usable
DNA, since that is the part you can actually measure.

Two things worth flagging about the framing before attacking: (i) the interesting number is the
SECOND one, not the first — the first is a phasing statistic, and only the second is an error
rate; (ii) "false-positive diagnosis" presumes the report asserted more than it knew, which is a
claim about report language, not about biology.
