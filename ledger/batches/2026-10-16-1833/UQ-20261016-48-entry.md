Question (canonical): For pre-deployment exercises in which two or more institutionally independent teams evaluated the SAME model checkpoint under documented access, affordances and time budget: (a) under a pre-registered equivalence relation that maps each documented finding to a failure-mode class at a declared granularity, what is the pairwise and joint rediscovery rate of classes across teams, reported with inter-rater reliability on the matching itself and with sensitivity to one looser and one stricter relation; and (b) does that overlap support a non-vacuous estimate of the classes the exercise missed, where the estimand is declared as the discoverable-at-that-effort-and-scaffold population rather than "all failure modes", estimators are fitted in at least two families (one admitting heterogeneous catchability, one admitting positive list dependence), and the estimate counts only if a pre-specified vacuity test passes (finite upper confidence bound, CI excluding "unboundedly many") under explicitly declared non-closure? (R1, adopted at Gate 1; intake version V0 and the three changes R1 made are in the record.)

Alternative formulations: (1) Lay: when two red teams test the same model, how often do they find the same thing, and what does that overlap license us to say about what neither found? (2) Cross-field: can the rediscovery statistics used in the vulnerability-stockpiling debate be computed for frontier-model evaluations, and do their assumptions survive the move from a fixed code artifact to a model whose failure population is created by elicitation? (3) Method-field: is a red-team cohort a valid multiple-recapture sample of a defect population, or is each team sampling a population its own scaffolds bring into existence?

Motivating gap: Safety cases, deployment decisions and emerging adequacy-of-testing language rest on an implicit coverage claim, and no quantity in the field expresses coverage — findings are reported as lists of what was found. Two ingredients are newly present: the same checkpoint is routinely handed to several independent teams and some exercises publish (UK AISI / US AISI joint testing; UK AISI pre-deployment evaluations), and the method homes are mature. The claimed live problem is at the interface: a code artifact has a fixed, individuable defect population, whereas a model's failure classes are produced by elicitation effort and by whichever scaffolds a team brings.

What would count as an answer: (1) an empirical overlap matrix from >=2 institutionally independent teams on one checkpoint, each finding mapped to a class by >=2 blinded raters, with IRR on class assignment and on the cross-team match decision, recomputed under a looser and a stricter relation; (2) estimates with CIs from >=2 estimator families (one heterogeneous-catchability, one dependence-admitting) on the incidence matrix, with sensitivity to effort covariates; (3) a pre-specified vacuity test (finite upper confidence bound; CI excluding "unboundedly many") under declared non-closure, which decides whether (2) counts at all. A clean negative counts equally: overlap collapsing toward chance once shared-scaffold and shared-taxonomy strata are conditioned on, or the dependence-corrected bound going unbounded. Not answers: overlap without IRR; an estimate with no dependence model; any estimate without scaffold/effort conditioning.
