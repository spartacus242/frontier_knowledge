Question (canonical — Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted reformulation, used for well-posedness only):
In publicly released agent-benchmark run traces carrying per-LLM-call usage fields (HAL, `agent-evals/hal_traces`), under a rework rule declared before any trace is read and validated against a hand-coded sample with agreement reported — a rule defined by counterfactual non-use (a tool result or generated block whose removal from the context supplied to every later step would not change any later action) rather than by "no later step reads it", since in a flat context window every prior result is nominally read: (i) what share of consumed tokens and of recomputed dollars falls in the rework class, reported separately for solved and unsolved runs; (ii) on the cells the release's design actually identifies — generalist-vs-benchmark-specific scaffold within benchmark for the same model (the ~24 model comparisons HAL reports), and BrowserUse-vs-SeeAct on Online Mind2Web — how much of the variance in the rework share sits with scaffold, model and task, with CIs; and (iii) does re-ranking cost-effectiveness by net-of-rework cost reorder the published ordering BEYOND the reordering that total-cost-aware scoring already produces (SWE-Effi, arXiv:2509.09853), i.e. is the rework share non-constant enough across cells that it is not a monotone rescaling of total cost?

Question (Gate 0 original, recorded as required):
In publicly released agent-benchmark run traces, what share of consumed tokens and dollars is spent on work that is discarded — retried tool calls, abandoned branches, repeated failed actions, and output no later step reads — and is that rework fraction a property of the scaffold, the model or the task, such that ranking agents by net-of-rework cost reorders published leaderboards?

Reformulation rationale: three defects, all well-posedness, none scope-changing. (a) "Output no later step reads" is near-empty for a single-thread ReAct agent, where every earlier tool result sits in the context of every later call until compaction; replaced by counterfactual non-use, which is testable by replay. (b) The attribution limb as posed presupposes a grid HAL does not have (verified below: most benchmarks run ONE scaffold, so the scaffold factor is largely confounded with benchmark); restricted to the identifiable cells. (c) "Reorders published leaderboards" is answered trivially-yes by existing total-cost-aware scoring; restated as a contrast against that ordering, which is what makes the limb non-redundant.

Alternative formulations:
1. What is an agent run's scrap-and-rework rate in the sense manufacturing quality accounting uses it, and does the harness or the model own it?
2. If agent leaderboards reported useful tokens rather than total tokens, would the cost-effectiveness ordering change by more than total-token-budget scoring already changes it?

What would count as an answer: Three artifacts, unchanged in kind from intake, narrowed by the reformulation. (i) A rework measure fixed in advance, applied to the released traces, with inter-rater agreement against a hand-coded sample of steps reported, and the share given separately for solved and unsolved runs (the second is where the existing literature's number already sits). (ii) A variance decomposition of the rework share with CIs on the cells the release identifies, stating explicitly which facets are not identified and why. (iii) A re-ranking under net-of-rework cost reported as rank changes with uncertainty, AND as the incremental change against the total-cost-aware ordering, since the latter is published. A null — rework share roughly constant across scaffolds and models, so net-of-rework cost is a monotone rescaling of total cost — answers the question in the direction that total cost is a sufficient statistic and current reporting needs no change. A measure-dependence failure (the ordering flips with the leniency of the rule) is also an answer, and a negative one about the estimand.

Significance — consequence: If the rework share is large and scaffold-owned, then the cost column on every agent leaderboard is partly a measurement of the harness's retry and compaction policy rather than of the model, and two decisions change: leaderboard operators would report a decomposed cost (and a buyer could tell "this model needs 3x the tokens" from "this harness retries 3x"), and scaffold designers would get a target that total cost hides, because total cost conflates deliberate redundancy that works (SWE-Effi's Agentless, which generates many patches and tests on purpose) with loops that do not (its "lack of futility detection"). If the share is roughly constant across scaffolds and models, net-of-rework cost is a monotone rescaling of total cost, total cost is a sufficient statistic, and the correct finding is that current reporting needs no change — a publishable null that closes a line of advocacy. The honest deflation: a buyer pays TOTAL cost, so a net-of-rework ranking reorders a quantity nobody is billed for; its value is diagnostic (for the people who build scaffolds and plan serving capacity, for whom discarded tokens are still served tokens) rather than consumer-facing.
Significance — audience: (a) scaffold/framework designers — retry policy, compaction and branch pruning are exactly the knobs a rework share is a loss function for, and the audience is demonstrably live: SWE-Effi names futility detection as a gap, and retry-budget cost amplification is already a practitioner topic. (b) Leaderboard operators — HAL already publishes accuracy-vs-cost frontiers and the harness-disclosure line (2605.23950) shows this community acts on reporting-surface findings. (c) Serving-capacity planners — discarded tokens are served tokens, and this is the one audience for whom the gross figure is the right one and the decomposition tells them which workloads are compressible. (d) Procurement, conditionally: only if the share varies with the harness they can change.
Significance — fecundity: makes askable whether the rework share is a controllable scaffold parameter with a measurable frontier against accuracy (the budget-constrained-web-agent line, 2606.15017, is the nearest existing shape); whether rework share early in a run predicts failure better than existing triage signals (C's 0.581 suggests yes and gives the comparator); whether the share scales with model capability, which would make it a cheap proxy for agentic competence; whether the serving stack should price or schedule by predicted goodput, importing the speculative-decoding goodput machinery (2406.14066) at the agent-step layer; and whether benchmark design itself induces rework (HAL found agents searching HuggingFace for the benchmark instead of solving the task — rework with a provenance).
