## Restatement in my own words

Take every Starlink satellite as an individual unit with a NORAD ID, group the units by the launch that put them up, and ask: after you have allowed for (a) how much geomagnetic battering each unit personally took while it was still climbing to its operational altitude, (b) which shell it was going to and (c) which hardware generation it is, do units that flew together still fail together more than chance allows? The thing to be reported is not a yes/no but a number — the variance of a per-launch random intercept in a discrete-time hazard model, with an interval around it, plus the resulting spread in how two randomly chosen launches compare — and then what happens to a debris model's predicted *spread* (not average) of satellites that fail to deorbit when you feed that variance in instead of one scalar reliability number. Plainly: are some launch loads lemons, by how much, and does it matter to debris forecasting?

## Arithmetic I performed myself (labelled as mine, from the source's numbers)

From Guarnieri et al. Table 1 as quoted above: fleet efficiency 97.05% over the preceding 34 v1.5 launches, i.e. an early-loss probability p = 0.0295; Group 4-7 lost 38 of 49.

My own arithmetic, under a single common binomial p (the exchangeability null the candidate proposes to test): expected early losses in a 49-satellite launch = 1.45. P(X ≥ 38 | n = 49, p = 0.0295) = 1.5 × 10⁻⁴⁸ (log10 = −47.8); using the looser "97%" figure the source also states, log10 = −47.5. The source reports these as simple tallies and attaches no uncertainty to them; the inputs are integer counts (11 of 49 surviving) and one ratio, so there is no stated uncertainty for me to propagate, and I claim nothing beyond the order of magnitude. Reading: **launch-group exchangeability of early-life failure is already refuted by published numbers, by ~48 orders of magnitude, with no model fitting required.** What is *not* settled by this is the conditional question — whether anything survives removing the one storm-attributed group — and on that my arithmetic is silent, because the fetch returned only Table 1's totals, not its per-launch rows.
