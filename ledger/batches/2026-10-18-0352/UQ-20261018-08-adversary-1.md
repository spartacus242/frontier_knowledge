## Restatement (my own words)

Take every building that has actually had a fire. Some of those buildings got their fire-safety approval by an engineer arguing "this alternative design is at least as safe" (performance-based / alternative solution / equivalency), the rest by following the prescriptive rulebook. Nobody has checked, with real incident records, whether the engineered ones burned any differently — in particular whether fire or smoke got out of the room/compartment it started in more often. The candidate asks two things: (A) can you even find out, for a useful share of buildings and enough fires, which route a building was approved under; and (B) if so, is the engineered group non-inferior to matched rulebook buildings on compartment breach, with damage and casualties as secondary endpoints, judged against a margin written down before anyone looks at the data.
