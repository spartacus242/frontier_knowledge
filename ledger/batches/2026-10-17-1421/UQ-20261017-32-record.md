Question (canonical) — V0, as handed in at intake:
In the species-level trait databases used for comparative and allometric analysis, what fraction of trait records resolve to a shared primary determination rather than an independent measurement, and by how much do published scaling exponents and their confidence intervals change when records are deduplicated to primary sources and weighted by provenance?

Question (canonical) — R1, the single permitted Gate 1 reformulation and the OPERATIVE formulation (changed only for well-posedness: adds the frame, the frozen protocol, the primary-determination rule, the separate reporting of the unresolvable share, and limb (iv)):
For a set of species-level trait databases declared in advance (D >= 2 from: Genoud et al.'s mammal BMR compilation, AnAge, the Amniote Life History Database, PanTHERIA/TetrapodTraits, FishBase, TRY) and M >= 3 pre-registered published allometric or life-history relationships fitted on them, with the analysis plan and the written definition of a "primary determination" frozen before any tracing begins: (i) what share of the records entering each fit resolve, under a written citation-chain protocol with a dual-coded subsample and a reported agreement statistic, to a primary determination shared with at least one other record in the same fit, and what share is unresolvable (the two reported separately); (ii) what design effect and effective sample size does the resulting source-cluster structure imply; (iii) how far do the fitted exponent and its interval move when the fit is repeated under a provenance-clustered error model (cluster-robust by primary source, or a random effect per primary source) against the published fit; and (iv) how much of that cluster structure is already absorbed by the phylogenetic covariance PGLS assumes?

Alternative formulations:
- How many independent measurements actually lie behind a comparative dataset of N species, and does the effective sample size change the allometric exponent?
- Is the degrees-of-freedom count in comparative biology inflated by citation chains that converge on a single original measurement, and does correcting for that move the exponents the field argues about?

Motivating gap:
Comparative biology fits allometric and life-history relationships over species-level compilations (TRY, AnAge, the Amniote Life History Database, PanTHERIA, FishBase), treats one row per species as one observation, and corrects non-independence for PHYLOGENY and for nothing else. The untested presupposition is measurement independence: that rows descend from distinct primary determinations. Compilations are assembled substantially from handbooks, secondary compilations and earlier databases, so one mid-20th-century determination can be the ultimate source of values entering many later fits; shared primary sources induce correlated error, which PGLS does not touch.
CORRECTION REQUIRED TO THE INTAKE GAP STATEMENT (from sources opened at Gate 2, below): the intake claim that the field's exponent sensitivity "has been measured ... to which species and mass range are included, never to how many independent measurements are behind them" is partly false. Genoud, Isler & Martin (2018) traced 1739 mammal BMR entries for 817 species to 946 original publications, read 95% of them, flagged 57 entries whose original was unavailable, and reported how the PGLS exponent moves between data selections (ALL 0.737 +/- 0.009; SELECT 0.729 +/- 0.010; SPOILED 0.711 +/- 0.013). And Farrell-Gray & Gotelli (2005) named shared data across studies as a second, non-phylogenetic kind of non-independence in exactly the 2/3-vs-3/4 dispute. So the mechanism has been named in the candidate's own field, the primary-source tracing has been executed at scale for the flagship corpus, and what remains unmeasured anywhere found is the specific estimand: the source-cluster structure, its design effect and effective N, and a provenance-clustered refit. The candidate survives on that residue, not on the stronger "no one has looked" claim.

Presuppositions:
1. Trait records carry enough source metadata for primary-source resolution at scale, at least over a declared subset. CONTESTED — TRY, FishBase and AusTraits carry per-record sources; AnAge's are often secondary; some records have none. Partly RELIEVED by evidence: Genoud et al. resolved 1739 entries to 946 originals with only 3.3% (57/1739) unavailable, so feasibility is demonstrated for one database/trait. The unresolvable fraction is part of the answer, not a defect.
2. "Primary determination" is definable: a reported measurement on specified individuals. CONTESTED at the edges — re-analyses, pooled museum series, and values themselves derived from a regression or from imputation need an advance rule. The rule must be frozen before tracing; this is the frame's softest joint.
3. Shared primary sources induce correlated error, so treating records as independent understates uncertainty. Established in principle as statistics (design effect; unit-of-analysis error); its magnitude here is the unknown.
4. Published exponents are consequential — they adjudicate metabolic-scaling theory, parameterise vegetation and land-surface models from TRY, and feed conservation prioritisation. Established.
5. Phylogenetic correction does not already absorb the duplication, i.e. shared-source clustering is not congruent with the phylogenetic covariance PGLS assumes. CONTESTED — handbooks and single-lab programmes cover clades, so partial congruence is plausible and is itself a quantity to report (limb iv).
6. The published intervals are narrow enough that a provenance-clustered refit could cross a threshold the field treats as decisive. SUPPORTED for at least one relationship: Genoud's SELECT fit is beta = 0.729 +/- 0.010 S.E. and he states its 95% interval excludes BOTH 3/4 and 2/3; on a normal approximation (my arithmetic, from his reported S.E., not his figure) that interval is about 0.709-0.749, so a design effect as small as 2 (interval widening by sqrt 2, to about 0.701-0.757) brings 0.75 back inside it. CONTESTED for other relationships; the frame must pre-declare which disputes it can and cannot adjudicate.

What would count as an answer:
A provenance audit with the analysis frozen before any tracing. For the pre-declared databases and M relationships, trace every record's citation chain under a written protocol to a primary determination, dual-coding a subsample with a reported agreement statistic. Report (i) the share resolving to a shared primary source and the share unresolvable, separately; (ii) the implied design effect and effective N; (iii) the refitted exponent and interval under a provenance-clustered error model against the published fit; (iv) the congruence between the source-cluster partition and the phylogenetic covariance. "Presupposition harmless" = effective N within a pre-declared fraction of nominal N and exponents moving less than their published CIs. "Presupposition fails" = exponents moving outside them, or intervals widening past the margins used to separate competing scaling predictions. A null is usable: it licenses current practice explicitly for the audited relationships, which no one can currently do.

Significance — consequence:
If the shared-source share is high and clusters span species, effective N for headline fits falls and intervals widen. This is not abstract: Genoud's SELECT fit is beta = 0.729 +/- 0.010 S.E. and he states its 95% interval excludes BOTH 3/4 and 2/3. On a normal approximation that interval is about 0.709-0.749 (my arithmetic from his reported S.E., carrying his stated uncertainty; he reports S.E., not CIs). A design effect of 2 widens it by sqrt 2 to about 0.701-0.757 and puts 0.75 back inside — so the audit can flip a published "excludes both predictions" into "cannot separate them", which is the exact margin the 2/3-vs-3/4 literature argues over. A yes means two decades of adjudication rested on overstated precision. A no — small clusters, or clusters congruent with phylogeny so PGLS already absorbs them — gives the field an explicit licence for one-row-per-species independence over the audited relationships, which nobody can currently issue, and converts a standing worry (named by both priors, measured by neither) into a closed question.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism — disciplinary boundary plus an incentive/labour gap, with an instrument component):
Comparative biology's non-independence reflex was formed by Felsenstein 1985 and has been phylogenetic ever since; every tool the field reaches for (independent contrasts, PGLS, phylogenetic mixed models, phylogenetic effective sample size) parameterises one covariance matrix read off a tree. The vocabulary for the other covariance source — design effect, cluster-robust variance, unit-of-analysis error, overlapping samples — sits in survey statistics and evidence synthesis, so the question is close to unaskable in the field's native terms, and queries 4 and 7 show the two vocabularies have no contact. On top of that sits an incentive/labour gap that the record can evidence rather than assert: Genoud et al. read 946 original publications, and spent that enormous tracing effort on measurement conditions, because reviewers ask whether an animal was fasted, not how many distinct papers 817 values came from — so the field's one existing primary-source audit was pointed at reliability and discarded the cluster structure ("one of them was chosen randomly"). Finally, persistent per-record source identifiers are recent: until AusTraits-style source tables and DOI-level citation, the audit was only possible as heroic manual archival work, which is why the current tooling (prepR4pcm) still counts database names as sources.
