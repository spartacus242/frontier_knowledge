  [V0, as submitted] At a fixed model architecture, precision, batch shape and input/output length, does the GPU energy per generated token for LLM inference differ materially between the trained weights and the randomly initialised 'dummy' weights that serving benchmarks commonly load, and if it does, in which phase (prefill or decode)?
  Reason for reformulation (well-posedness only): V0 leaves "materially" without a margin, leaves a gap between its "yes" bar (>3–13%) and its "no" bar (≤1–2%), and does not say what "random at trained scale" means. It also admits MoE models, where random weights change expert routing and so change per-expert token counts, which is a shape change, not a value effect. R1 restricts to dense models, defines conditions (b) and (c), and gives margins that close the gap (below).
Alternative formulations: (1) Is shape-only energy modelling of LLM inference missing a data-dependent term, as shown by swapping trained weights for random ones while keeping every tensor shape and kernel identical? (2) Do the bit-level statistics of trained LLM weights, and of the activations they produce, make inference cheaper or more expensive in joules than an untrained model of the same size and shape?
Motivating gap: Gregersen, Patel & Choukse 2024 (arXiv:2409.18324, SSSC@SC24; opened) find that input values alone change GEMM power by up to ~38–40% at fixed shapes on A100, partly replicated on H100, V100 and RTX 6000, using synthetic 2048x2048 matrices only. They name LLMs only as motivation and leave "sort neural network model weights so as to reduce power draw" as future work. LLM energy work models energy from shape: WattGPU (arXiv:2607.02391; opened) predicts power from LLM metadata and GPU specs (MAPE ≤3.4% offline, ≤13.5% server). "From Tokens to Watt-hours" (arXiv:2607.26571; abstract opened) uses FLOPs, memory traffic and token counts. "From Prompts to Power" (arXiv:2511.05597; opened) fixes lengths on the grounds that "the number of operations is the same for each of the prompts" and never varies content. vLLM's dummy loader (source read) fills float weights uniformly in ±1e-3 (±1 for 8-bit floats) and int8/FP4 weights with uniformly random bytes, with the code comment "For accurate performance evaluation, we assign random values to the weights." Deng et al. 2026 (arXiv:2606.21869; abstract opened) report per-output-token energy varying up to 8.3x across languages and attribute it to "complex or rare scripts" without a hardware mechanism. 29 searches found no comparison of energy with trained versus random weights.
Presuppositions:
  P1 Value-dependent switching power shows up in board-level energy for real fused LLM kernels (attention, MLP, norms), not only in isolated GEMMs. CONTESTED. Mean-power evidence covers synthetic GEMMs only (Gregersen 2024). Evidence that weights leak at trace level from tensor cores exists (Kraken, arXiv:2603.02891, 2026, including exploratory LLM far-field), but trace-level leakage is not a change in mean energy. This is what the question tests.
  P2 Under R1 the conditions run identical kernels on identical work. ESTABLISHED for dense models with fixed shapes, where kernel selection depends on shape, but it must be verified per run (kernel-name logs). Risks: FP8 dynamic scaling computes scales from data; random weights may give non-finite activations, which must be counted and reported. MoE excluded.
  P3 Random or dummy weights are used in published performance or energy figures. PARTLY ESTABLISHED. The vLLM dummy loader exists and vllm-ascend benchmark configs set "load_format": "dummy". Use in published energy figures is UNVERIFIED: every energy paper opened (ML.ENERGY 2505.06371, Pronk & Zhao 2509.08867, Prompts to Power 2511.05597, Husom et al. 2407.16893) uses trained checkpoints. This weakens one consequence, not well-posedness.
  P4 Decode is mostly memory-bandwidth-bound and so dilutes any value-dependent compute power. CONTESTED. The traffic itself is value-dependent: Fallin & Burtscher (MCHPC'22) measure 24–28% more energy to move random data than zeros. Decode may therefore show an effect through HBM traffic. Part of what is asked.
  P5 Output length and batch composition can be held identical across conditions (ignore-EOS or forced tokens, fixed batch). ESTABLISHED.
  P6 Board energy counters or external metering resolve differences of ~1% over repeated, interleaved runs. ESTABLISHED WITH CAVEATS (NVML averaging and sampling windows; thermal drift). External metering is preferred.
  P7 Trained weights and "random at trained scale" weights differ in bit-level statistics. PARTLY CONTESTED. Trained-weight exponent entropy is ~2–3 bits, and the Gaussian-case bound is 1.6–2.67 bits (Yang et al. 2025, arXiv:2510.02676; opened). So (a) and (b) may be close in exponent statistics, and differences would come from heavy tails, outlier channels, correlation structure and the activations they induce. vLLM dummy weights (uniform ±1e-3, or uniform random bytes for int8/FP4) differ more.
  Gate 1 check: P1, P4 and P7 are flagged as contested and form part of what is asked. P2, P5 and P6 are established, with checks named. P3 is flagged as unverified and bears only on significance. It is a question, not a thesis: the direction is not presumed, and three outcomes are pre-specified.
What would count as an answer: A controlled experiment on ≥2 GPU types (e.g., A100, H100), ≥3 dense model sizes and ≥2 formats (e.g., bf16 plus fp8 or int4). Conditions: (a) trained weights; (b) per-tensor moment-matched Gaussian weights, with norm gains and embeddings at trained scale; (c) engine dummy weights; (d, mechanism probe) trained weights with rows and columns permuted consistently, so the distribution is kept and adjacency changes. Hold fixed: batch size, forced input/output lengths, engine, kernels (logged) and clocks (or a recorded power cap). Measure prefill and decode separately, either from prefill-only runs or phase timestamps against the energy counter. Run ≥10 interleaved repeats per cell. Report Δ = E_cond/E_trained − 1 per phase with a 95% CI, plus run time, since under a power cap a value effect appears as time instead.
  Pre-registered outcome bands for (b) and (c):
  - YES (material): the CI lies entirely beyond ±3% (about the best shape-only predictor error, WattGPU ≤3.4% offline MAPE) in some phase, on both GPU types.
  - NO (negligible): the CI lies within ±1% in every cell.
  - Otherwise: small but real, reported with its size and phase.
  Every outcome is settled by data. A secondary arm, outside the yes/no, varies prompt content (e.g., script or language) at fixed token counts with trained weights.
