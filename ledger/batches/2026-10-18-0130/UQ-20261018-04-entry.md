Domain(s): Medicine — perioperative care, infection prevention, sterile processing; surgical patient safety. Source method: new-instrument question (item-level instrument-tracking logs). Method-owning neighbours: reliability engineering (usage-based service life, wear-out hazard), hospital asset/clinical engineering.
Question (canonical): In hospital systems whose instrument-tracking system records per-ITEM cumulative reprocessing-cycle counts and per-item-to-case linkage, what are the three separately-estimated associations between an item's cycle count (and calendar age) at the moment of use and (i) intraoperative malfunction or exchange of THAT item, at the item-use level; (ii) sterilisation-process failure of the load or tray containing it, at the load/tray level; and (iii) NHSN-defined SSI at 30 and 90 days in the case it served, at the case level with the case's max or mean item cycle count as exposure — each adjusted for service line, surgeon, case complexity and tray type, each with a pre-declared negative-control exposure and a stated minimum detectable effect? (V2; V1 — "does accumulated cycle count and age predict malfunction, sterilisation-process failure and SSI", one verb over three units of analysis — is in the record; one Gate 1 reformulation used.)
Alternative formulations:
- Do reusable surgical instruments have a measurable service life in clinical use, and is current practice — retire on visible damage, or at a bench-validated cycle limit — consistent with it?
- Is the number of times an instrument set has been through decontamination a risk factor for anything that happens to the patient it is used on?
Motivating gap: per-item cycle histories linked to cases are a by-product of barcode/RFID tracking claimed to be unused as an exposure, with contamination research concentrated on flexible endoscopes by outbreak pressure and rigid-instrument life governed by bench cycle simulations. That claim is FALSE: Yoshikawa et al. 2019 took per-item cumulative use counts straight out of an RFID instrument-management-system log (34,390 operations) and used them as the exposure against instrument failure; Homma et al. 2023 did the same with cleaning counts against insulation failure. Surviving gap: only limbs (ii) and (iii), and the OR-record malfunction endpoint of (i).
Presuppositions:
1. Item-level cycle timestamps AND per-case linkage retrievable for research — CONTESTED, the key feasibility premise. Item-to-operation linkage exists in at least one deployment (P1 states instruments are linked to operations by use record and set ID); item-to-PATIENT-OUTCOME linkage unevidenced anywhere (P1: "We have not used any patient data in this study").
2. Cycle count is a meaningful wear exposure over practical lifetimes — CONTESTED and evidenced against in all three field tests: positive but negligible (P1, per-use OR 1.026, R² 0.032), null (P4, cleanings 281 vs 261 P = 0.27; age 7 vs 7 y P = 0.90), inverted in the neighbouring scope literature (P2, usage aOR 0.80, 0.64–0.995).
3. Intraoperative malfunction recorded consistently enough to be an endpoint — CONTESTED; both device-side priors used proxies (repair request; IF-detector reading), which is evidence the OR-record endpoint is not routinely available.
4. Confounding by tray type, service line, surgeon addressable — CONTESTED, unresolved; P1's own finding of non-uniform usage across nominally identical instruments is the mechanism of the entanglement.
5. Manufacturers' reprocessing limits are bench-derived not field-validated — VERIFIED for the lifetime arm (reprocessing-validation lab's own page: maximum lifetime "determined and defined" through simulated cycle series under DIN EN ISO 10993-1/-15), but a weaker asymmetry than claimed, because the field-side measurement has now been made twice.
6. SSI attributable at the level of an individual item's history — WEAK; no prior attempts it, and an unstated further premise of limb (ii) — that an item's wear can cause a load-level sterilisation failure (BI growth, wet pack) — has no source on the record at all.
What would count as an answer: in one or more systems with item-level tracking, a pre-registered cohort of cases linked to the specific trays and items used, estimating the association between a pre-declared exposure (cycle count at time of use; item age; cycles since last inspection) and three pre-declared endpoints — (i) OR-recorded malfunction or exchange, (ii) load/tray sterilisation-process failure (BI failure, wet pack, load recall), (iii) NHSN SSI at 30 and 90 days — adjusted for service line, surgeon, complexity and tray type, with a negative-control exposure (cycle count of an instrument type not used in that procedure) and a stated MDE per limb. Gate 1: well-posed in V2 (3/3); limb (iii) is pre-determined to be a null whose informativeness depends entirely on the MDE, which the answer-criterion does require.
