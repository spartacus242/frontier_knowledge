Adversary context: fresh. Handed the Gate 0–3 record only. Budget: 12 WebSearch (all used), 5 WebFetch (all used). No search or fetch was refused by the tool; both caps were reached, which is recorded below as an unrun check, not as a check that passed.

## Restatement in my own words (before attacking)

Take a fixed time window and a pre-named list of *public registers* in which somebody demands that content be taken out of LLM training material: DMCA notices filed against dataset/model hosts (via Lumen), dataset withdrawals forced by courts or done voluntarily, recorded consent withdrawals in research corpora, and retractions with DOIs. Count those demands. For each, record which law it comes under, how many arrive per unit time, how many retrievable things it names, and — where the named thing can be found inside a specific open corpus (Dolma, peS2o) — how many documents it is, what share of tokens that is, and how many retained documents it is tangled with. State honestly how much of the real demand stream is invisible (private settlements, sealed suits) rather than pretending the register is the population. Then compute the same numbers for the forget sets that TOFU, MUSE, WMDP and DUSK ship with, and ask whether those forget sets sit anywhere inside the measured spread or off to one side of it.

So: *who actually demands removal, in what shape and volume, and do the field's benchmark forget sets look anything like that?*

## The eight attacks

### 1. Triviality — **Partial**
*Objection.* Two different page-length answers are already available. (a) The direction of the comparison is asserted as background in the field: a 2026 recsys benchmark paper states that existing unlearning benchmarks delete large blocks (2%, 5%+) while real systems receive small continuous requests, without measuring anything (established fact handed to me re arXiv:2508.17076). (b) For at least one of the four named benchmarks the comparison is settled by the benchmark's own abstract: TOFU's forget set is a subset of "200 diverse synthetic author profiles" (arXiv:2401.06121, opened), i.e. LLM-generated data about people who do not exist and which is fine-tuned in rather than present in any pretraining corpus. Its document count, token share and co-document entanglement in Dolma are zero by construction, so "TOFU lies outside the support of any distribution of real demands against a real corpus" needs no census at all.
*Best response.* The census itself — legal basis × arrival rate × artifacts per demand × corpus footprint, with a coverage bound — is not derivable from anything published. The only public numbers I could find on the demand side are per-company compliance totals (xAI: 65 deletion requests in 2025) with no corpus mapping, no artifacts-per-demand and no copyright stratum; and 12 queries across 8 vocabularies turned up no census against a training corpus.
*Status.* **Partial.** The comparison limb is pre-settled for at least one named benchmark and pre-believed for the rest; the census limb is genuinely not a page of work. Non-fatal.

### 2. Disguise — **Partial** (8-query requirement met: 12 new queries run; one key check unrun)
*Objection.* This is a renamed question in at least four other vocabularies: library/archival practice (ARL survey of removal requests against digital collections), medical-research ethics (biobank withdrawal/refusal rates — the consent-withdrawal stratum, already measured for sample collections), compliance reporting (CCPA mandated request metrics, published annually, including by a frontier model developer), and recommender-systems benchmarking (ERASE, "A Real-World Aligned Benchmark", arXiv:2603.08341, 2026).
*Best response.* None of the openable near-misses measures a demand population *against a named training corpus*, and none compares a demand distribution to benchmark forget sets: the ARL survey's unit is institutions and its object is digital collections; biobank studies' unit is participants in a sample collection; CCPA metrics are per-company consumer-PI counts with no corpus mapping; the own-field position paper that could have posed it (arXiv:2410.02879, opened) argues dependency and target ambiguity instead. On the recsys line, the target field differs (interaction data, no token share, no legal-basis stratification) and the preceding paper in that line is established to assert rather than measure.
*Status.* **Partial.** I could not open ERASE (fetch cap), and three further near-misses are unopened. The response is plausible and partly evidenced, but the single closest renaming candidate is **Unverified, not cleared** — Gate 5 should treat opening arXiv:2603.08341 as the promotion condition. Not Unresolved: I have no source for a kill.

### 3. Combinatorial novelty — **Resolved**
*Objection.* Empirical-legal notice counting × unlearning benchmark auditing, with nothing happening at the join: run Lumen's API, run infini-gram, put the two tables side by side.
*Best response.* The join is where the hard object lives: a legal demand does not determine a document set. COPE's own removal exception (opened) shows the strata do not even share an end-state — "clearly defamatory, violates personal privacy", "the subject of a court order" and "serious health risk" license *different* remedies, and the default remedy is annotation rather than deletion. So mapping demand→forget set is a choice with consequences, which the record's P1 (arXiv:2608.14855, quoted in the record but not opened by me) independently reports as curator-dependent by more than an order of magnitude. There is a genuine interface problem: what is the forget set *of* a demand.
*Status.* **Resolved** on evidence I opened (COPE) plus the record's quoted P1 (which I did not verify myself, flagged).

### 4. Presupposition failure — **Partial** (sourced, severable)
*Objection.* V1 pre-registers four "enumerable public records of removal demands against LLM training data", and one of them is not a record of removal demands at all. Under the COPE retraction guidelines (opened), a retraction is by design *not* a demand to remove anything: "the online version of the article should be retained with a clear notice of retraction", because "Retaining the original work ensures transparency of the published record"; removal is reserved for "extremely limited cases" and even then "the metadata (title and authors) should be retained". Nobody who retracts a paper has asked for it to be taken out of a corpus. Feeding Retraction Watch's DOIs into a limb whose estimand is "demands per unit time" therefore counts tens of thousands of demands that no claimant made, and the record's own instrument argument for silence ("Retraction Watch went open on Crossref in 2023") leans precisely on this stratum.
*Best response.* Presupposition 5 is already flagged CONTESTED, V1 forbids pooling and stratifies by legal basis, and the record itself raises whether the strata require different end-states (suppression vs parameter removal vs provenance annotation). Drop or re-label the retraction stratum and three strata survive: DMCA notices against dataset/model hosts, court-ordered and voluntary dataset withdrawals, documented consent withdrawals. Presupposition 1 (recorded, enumerable demands exist) is strengthened, not weakened, by xAI's mandated report.
*Status.* **Partial.** The defect is real, sourced and quarter-of-the-frame wide, but severable by a repair the record's own stratification rule already anticipates. Explicitly **not Unresolved**: the response is a repair, not a circle.

### 5. Vagueness — **Partial**
*Objection.* "Do the published forget sets fall inside or outside the support of that distribution" has no decision rule. Support of what estimate, at what tolerance? With a small, admittedly biased register, any benchmark point sits outside the empirical support trivially; with the unobserved fraction handled by "a stated sensitivity envelope", no threshold separates YES from NO. And for TOFU the answer is fixed before the data arrive (synthetic profiles, quoted above), so that sub-answer is insensitive to evidence — the ledger's own failure pattern.
*Best response.* Every axis is a countable quantity (demands per unit time, artifacts per demand, document count, token share, co-document count), and NO is defined as coincidence on the stated axes within the stated coverage bound, which is at least a direction.
*Status.* **Partial.** Countable axes without a stated comparison rule. This is the one place I would knock the record's self-assessed well-posedness from 3/3 to 2/3. Non-fatal.

### 6. Significance inflation — **Partial**
*Objection.* The record's regulator claim is that the answer would supply "the first denominator for deletion-duty feasibility: how many demands per year". Part of that denominator is already published and legally mandated annually: xAI's CCPA transparency report (opened) gives, for 1 Jan–31 Dec 2025, **65 requests to delete received, 65 completed, 0 denied**, and the same CCPA metric obligation binds every large business, so the compliance-side counts regulators ask for already exist as a recurring series. Separately, the community said to be waiting for this census does not behave as if it is: the group that set out to build a "real-world aligned" unlearning benchmark did it by stipulating the request stream (established fact re arXiv:2508.17076; ERASE unopened).
*Best response.* 65 California consumer-PI requests at one company is not a demand distribution: no artifacts per demand, no corpus mapping, no token share, no copyright or court-order stratum, no coverage bound, and nothing at all about the benchmark comparison. Corpus curators and benchmark authors still have no reference distribution.
*Status.* **Partial.** The audiences are real; one headline significance claim ("no public denominator exists") is in part sourced-false.

### 7. Reason for silence — **Resolved** (but the record names the wrong mechanism)
*Objection.* Boundary, incentive and instrument explain why the measurement is *unrewarded and hard*, not why nobody *asked*. And the instrument leg is weak: Lumen notices and erasure requests are not new instruments (the DMCA-notice census was 2016), and the genuinely new register the record points at — Crossref/Retraction Watch, 2023 — belongs to the stratum that attack 4 shows is not a removal-demand record at all.
*Best response.* A credible asking-side mechanism does exist and is evidenced: the answer is treated as already known. A 2026 benchmark paper states the mismatch as background and proceeds to stipulate a request stream instead of measuring one (established fact re arXiv:2508.17076; also the 600-sample predefined stream in arXiv:2601.21682). A belief held as obvious is not posed as a question. Add the boundary leg — nobody sits where notice registers and token corpora meet — and silence is accounted for.
*Status.* **Resolved**, with the correction that the operative mechanism is "assumed-obvious, hence asserted rather than measured", not the new-instrument story. This also slightly deflates the candidate's novelty rhetoric: unasked here means unmeasured-because-presumed, not unthinkable.

### 8. Dissolution — **Partial**
*Objection.* Once pooling is forbidden (as V1 requires) and once retractions drop out (attack 4), "the joint distribution of real removal demands" has no single referent: what remains is a count of DMCA notices naming dataset hosts, a handful of dataset withdrawals, and some consent withdrawals — three small, legally incommensurable censuses with different end-states (COPE, opened, shows the end-states differ even inside publication ethics). The canonical question's "joint distribution" then dissolves into three bookkeeping exercises plus a benchmark table.
*Best response.* Stratum-level comparison against benchmark forget sets is still a measurement with a result, and the strata share the axes that matter for benchmark design (artifacts per demand, document count, token share, entanglement); the fragmentation is itself one of the findings the question is designed to produce, not a reason the question has no answer.
*Status.* **Partial.** It fragments rather than dissolves; what survives is smaller than the canonical sentence promises.
