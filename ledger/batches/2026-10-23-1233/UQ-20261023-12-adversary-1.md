## 1. Restatement in my own words

Calibration labs decide how often an instrument must come back by looking at the instruments that DID come back: how far off they were (as-found deviation against tolerance) as a function of how long it had been since the last calibration. That curve assumes the instruments that show up at elapsed time t are a fair sample of the fleet at t. The candidate asks: in some real fleet with good enough records (model, due date or interval in force, actual date, numeric as-found per characteristic, tolerance), is WHEN an instrument shows up (early, on time, overdue) statistically unrelated to HOW FAR OFF it is, once you account for elapsed time, model and how hard it is used? And if it is related, by how many days and what percent of the current interval does the recommended interval move when the analysis is corrected for that, and in which direction?

Reading the canonical V2 wording literally: it is an EXISTENCE question over fleets ("in at least one real field-instrument fleet whose records hold..."), with a dependence test (limb i), a bias-in-days figure (limb ii) and a sign (limb iii), conditioned on a "usage proxy" that is not named.
