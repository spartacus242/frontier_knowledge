## Restatement (my own words)

Before an emergency trial enrols people who cannot consent, the investigators are required to "consult the community". The candidate asks: for the ~77 completed US EFIC trials, did the people who actually showed up to that consultation (survey respondents, meeting attendees, phone-sample respondents) look like the patients the trial later enrolled — by age, sex, race/ethnicity, area deprivation and geography — and in how many trials were the two groups essentially different populations? It adds a prior limb: in how many trials was anything about the consulted people recorded at all? The record's novelty claim rests on the sentence "the only thing anyone has linked across the two is counts (Prior 1)".

## Key evidence

Henry et al. 2017 (opened). A random-digit-dial community consultation (933 calls, 500 completed surveys, 54% response) run in 2012 for the Toronto PROPPR site, targeted at high-violence postal codes; the authors then compared the consultation sample with the trial's actually enrolled participants. Summariser-returned fragments: there were "significant differences between our CC population and actual trial participants" for trauma mechanism, age, gender and ethnicity; "The trial cohort had higher proportions of penetrating traumas, males" and was younger, with higher proportions of Black, Asian, Latin American and mixed-race participants; geographically, "approximately 27% of the trial population met the prescribed postal code". Income and education were recorded on the consultation side but could not be compared because the trial enrolment log recorded only age, ethnicity and postal code. The authors conclude that violence-based geographic targeting was a poor way to find a representative community and question the value of phone-survey consultation. This paper surfaced in the record's own query 3 ("a TSACO 2017 paper") and was not opened.

Stephens et al. TSACO Dec 2025 (title known from the opened 2026 commentary; original not read). Title: "are market research surveys representative of the target community populations?"; the commentary describes it as comparing "the demographics of the survey respondents with that of the general population" to test whether respondents are "typical of the targeted communities". Comparator is the general/target community, not enrolled patients.

Stephens et al. JAMA Surgery 2024 (opened). 52 of 91 TAP-trial sites, 17,206 surveys; reports respondent sex (60.7% female) and median age 40.1; "Data on race and ethnicity are no longer collected by social media companies" and "Lack of such information makes it difficult to characterize the sample that has been reached"; states that "how community and representative should be defined" is an open limitation, and that "federal regulations do not define the components of adequate CC and PD".

Utah/SIREN worksheet (opened). Asks sites about the recruitment area's rural/suburban/urban mix, minority and affluent/poor populations, and "Will certain disadvantaged or vulnerable groups be over or under represented?"; does not ask for consultation-participant demographics or any comparison with expected enrollees.

## Recommended verdict

Rejected (Unresolved on attack 2, Disguise; also Unresolved on 7). Routing: already-asked — Henry et al., Trauma Surg Acute Care Open 2017;2:e000084 (consulted vs enrolled, single EFIC trial site, with the candidate's strata and conclusion), with the representativeness-of-the-consulted question now an active thread (Stephens et al., TSACO Dec 2025; Appelbaum & Newcomb, TSACO 2026). The frame-wide 77-trial audit is a reasonable research proposal in that thread, not an un-asked question. Promotion is not available on this record; a re-generated candidate would have to differ from Henry in something other than scale.

Single strongest killing point: a paper that surfaced in the record's own query 3 and went unopened (Henry et al. 2017) performs the candidate's exact comparison — consultation respondents versus actually enrolled patients on age, sex, ethnicity and geography for an EFIC trial — and reports "significant differences" and ~27% geographic overlap, falsifying the record's novelty claim that the only consulted-to-enrolled link in the literature is a count-to-count association.
