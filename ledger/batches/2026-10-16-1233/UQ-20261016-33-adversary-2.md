## 1. Restatement in my own words (before engaging the generator's framing)

Hospitals keep a closed book on opioids: what came into a ward's cabinet, what the records say went out (given to patients, wasted, returned), and what is left on the shelf. Nuclear safeguards has a standard way of asking how good such a book is: form the inventory difference, propagate the uncertainty of every term, and state the smallest steady leak the book can catch with 95% probability while crying wolf no more than once a year. Nobody has run that calculation on a hospital opioid book. The candidate asks for the number — the smallest steady monthly amount a nurse or anaesthetist could remove from one ward and still have the books, the vendor's alarm rule, or a sequential test fail to notice — and then asks whether that number is bigger or smaller than the amounts people were actually caught taking in prosecuted cases. If bigger, the mandated accounting is theatre and the fix is physical (blind counts, waste assay); if smaller, the repeated multi-year undetected cases are management failures, not statistical ones.

What I think the question actually turns on, which the framing understates: the ward's book has a line item ("wasted") that is filled in by the very person it is meant to catch, with no measurement behind it.

### My own derivation (labelled as mine, not a source's conclusion)

Take the record's own Gate 1 identity: ID = (BI + receipts) − (administered + documented waste + returns + EI). A diverter who withdraws a vial and records part as administered and the remainder as witnessed waste puts the diverted mass into the "documented waste" (or "administered") term. Every term of the identity is then satisfied and E[ID] = 0 for any removal M. So for the balance-only test (the record's test (c)), detection probability equals the false-alarm rate for all M: there is no finite minimum detectable rate. This is two lines of arithmetic on the record's identity; its only empirical input is that falsified waste/administration documentation is a real mode, which Fan 2019 states verbatim. Scope check, in the candidate's favour: the argument is mode-specific, not general — undocumented physical removal does move ID, so a finite limit exists for that path, and per-user tests (a)/(b) look at features outside the balance. I did not re-open Epstein 2016 within the fetch cap, so I assert no number for sigma_ID and do no arithmetic on the 15.8%/8.8%/5.2% error rates.
