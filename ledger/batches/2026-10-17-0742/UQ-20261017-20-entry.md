Domain(s): Medicine — laboratory medicine (pre-analytical quality), patient safety. Adjacent: causal inference (IV), quality engineering, haemovigilance.
Question (canonical): What happens to the patient whose specimen is rejected — among laboratory orders rejected for pre-analytical reasons, what fraction never yields a usable result, what is the delay to the first usable result, and what downstream clinical consequences follow, estimated with a design exploiting the operator- and draw-route-driven component of rejection?
Alternative formulations:
- When the laboratory rejects a haemolysed, clotted or insufficient sample, how often is the test never repeated, and does anyone come to harm?
- Treating pre-analytical specimen rejection as an exposure rather than a quality indicator, what is its causal effect on time-to-result, test abandonment and patient outcome?
- (Gate 1 reformulation V2, graded at Gate 2) In a linked LIS–EHR frame with documented recollection policy, rejected order as exposed unit and a non-rejected order for the same test, window and acuity stratum as comparator: (i) proportion with no usable result at 6 h / 24 h / never; (ii) order-to-first-usable-result distribution; (iii) effect on pre-specified downstream endpoints (time to recognition of a critical result, escalation/ICU transfer, LOS, additional venipunctures) under a declared identification strategy — phlebotomist identity, draw route or shift-level haemolysis propensity as instrument — with the exclusion restriction tested by falsification outcomes.
Motivating gap: Silence mapping — intake asserted the pre-analytical literature is dense with rejection rates and haemolysis indices and empty of patient-side questions. Gate 2 found this false for limbs (i) and (ii): the field has a paper titled almost exactly the question and publishes both headline quantities. Residual genuine gap is limb (iii) only.
Presuppositions:
1. Rejections recorded with reason, order ID and timestamp and linkable to the clinical record — ESTABLISHED.
2. Rejection has an operator-/draw-route-driven component independent of prognosis given acuity, usable as an instrument — CONTESTED; central threat (sicker patients have harder draws and more line draws).
3. "Never re-collected" separable from "deliberately abandoned as no longer needed" — CONTESTED and still open — P1 does not resolve it: its criterion is "failure to recollect or relabel a rejected specimen" with no time cutoff (read at first hand on verification; the "arbitrary six-hour window" this entry first attributed to P1 is not in the paper).
4. A counterfactual usable result exists; rejection is a correct refusal whose cost is delay/abandonment — ESTABLISHED analytically.
5. Downstream endpoints ascertainable at needed granularity — ESTABLISHED.
6. Rejection not already neutralised by reflex auto-recollection — CONTESTED, site-dependent; published abandonment 11.2% vs 48.3%, repeat delay 65 min median vs 121 h mean, so huge between-site spread.
What would count as an answer: (i) proportion with no usable result in pre-specified windows and never; (ii) order-to-first-usable-result distribution vs matched non-rejected orders; (iii) effect estimates on pre-specified downstream endpoints under a declared identification strategy with falsification tests. A null (prompt recollection, nothing downstream) is a clean answer and would strip the patient-safety warrant from rejection-reduction programmes. Criteria (i) and (ii) have already been met by published work.

P1. Karcher DS, Lehman CM. "Clinical consequences of specimen rejection: A College of American Pathologists Q-Probes analysis of 78 clinical laboratories." Arch Pathol Lab Med 2014;138(8):1003-1008 (citation verified at hsrc.himmelfarb.gwu.edu/smhs_path_facpubs/66; endpoints via captodayonline.com/Archives/0712/0712d_specimen_rejection.html, opened). Unit: rejected specimen aggregated to institution. Endpoint: abandonment (no corrected specimen requested or supplied) and processing delay to usable result, stratified by stat/non-stat, specimen type, bed size. Comparator: none vs non-rejected orders; institution-to-institution percentiles. Estimand: descriptive cross-institutional proportions and medians. Publishes overall abandonment 11.2% (median laboratory-attributed 1.3%, provider-attributed 5%); median delay 65 min (88 non-stat, 54 stat, 91 at >450 beds); and one quantified downstream consequence, 86.8% of rejected blood specimens leading to repeated phlebotomy. Its other consequences — inconvenience and discomfort, potential complications, potential transfusion for iatrogenic blood loss — are named, not measured, and the patient-safety link is explicitly "not specifically assessed in this study". Figures read at the publisher on verification; the earlier ones in this entry came from the CAP TODAY review and were not P1's.
P2. Jacobsz LA, Zemlin AE, Roos MJ, Erasmus RT. "Chemistry and haematology sample rejection and clinical impact in a tertiary laboratory in Cape Town." Clin Chem Lab Med 2011;49(12):2047-2050, DOI 10.1515/CCLM.2011.743 (publisher PDF opened). Unit: specimen (481 rejected of 32,910 over 2 weeks) plus 30 patient files. Endpoint: proportion repeated, time for repeat to reach lab, critical-range repeats, files where rejection "had an impact on patient care". Comparator: none. Estimand: descriptive single-site proportions plus unblinded holistic chart judgement. Publishes 1.46% rejection, 51.7% repeated (48.3% not), mean 121 h to repeat, 5.1% critical, 40% of 30 files judged affected.
P3. Phelan MP, Hustey FM, Good DM, Reineks EZ. "Seeing Red: Blood Sample Hemolysis Is Associated with Prolonged Emergency Department Throughput." J Appl Lab Med 2020;5(4):732-737 (OUP page opened). Comparator: visits with non-haemolysed potassium sample — it does have the comparator the others lack. Estimand: unadjusted mean differences, causality explicitly disclaimed. Publishes 349 vs 287 min overall; 429 vs 337 discharged; 292 vs 264 admitted.

Verification (post-batch, two fresh-context checkers against the same source, logs UQ-20261017-20-verification-karcher.md and -karcher2.md): **the Rejected verdict stands and is now read at first hand, but five of P1's figures as this entry gave them are wrong and P1's harm list is not P1's.** The first checker could not reach P1 at all — GWU record bibliographic only, PubMed client error, Europe PMC robots-disallowed, and a PMC article that turned out to be a different paper behind a captcha — and returned a clean COULD NOT READ, which left every P1 figure UNVERIFIED rather than refuted. The second checker reached the publisher's own article page at meridian.allenpress.com and read abstract plus Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion through the page summariser.
**What P1 actually publishes.** Confirmed as printed: the delay is a "median of 65 minutes", and the long-delay subgroup is "median = 91 minutes" at a "bed size of greater than 450 beds". Corrected: the split is "a median of 54 minutes for stat specimens and 88 minutes for nonstat specimens", not the ~60 and ~90 this entry gave. Refuted: the 21% abandonment figure is not in the paper — checked twice, not found; what the paper prints is "The overall specimen abandonment rate in this study was 11.2%", with a median laboratory-attributed rate of 1.3% and a median provider-attributed rate of 5%. Refuted: the "arbitrary six-hour window" this entry attributed to P1's abandonment definition is not there either — P1's own definition is "Specimen/test abandonment was determined by failure to recollect or relabel a rejected specimen", with no time cutoff, and the only "6" in the text is a six-week observation period. The harm list is wrong too: P1 does not name haematoma, phlebotomy-associated anaemia or nerve injury. It names "inconvenience and discomfort for the patient", "the potential for patient complications", and the "potential need for blood transfusion due to excessive iatrogenic blood loss". The provenance of the bad figures matters: they came from the CAP TODAY review of July 2012, which Gate 2 could open and which predates the 2014 paper by two years, so it describes an earlier Q-Probes cycle or preliminary data — this entry should never have attributed its numbers to P1, and the general lesson is that a trade-press review of a Q-Probes programme is not a substitute for the paper.
**Two findings cut towards the rejection rather than away from it.** P1 does quantify one downstream consequence this entry listed as unquantified: "In this study, 86.8% of rejected blood specimens led to repeated phlebotomy." And P1 names the patient-safety limb itself as not assessed — "Although not specifically assessed in this study, longer delays in test result availability" ... "may have an even greater negative impact on patient safety" — which states limb (iii) as open more sharply than anything the entry had. Confirmed as the entry described it: the comparator is institution-to-institution percentiles and stat/non-stat and bed-size subgroups within rejected specimens, with no non-rejected comparator anywhere in the paper, and every consequence other than repeat phlebotomy is named without being measured.
