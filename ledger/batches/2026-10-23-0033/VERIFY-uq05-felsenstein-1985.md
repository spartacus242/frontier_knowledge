## CLAIM

support value's nominal target is DATA-SCOPED - "a statement about repeatability under

## REGIME

In the paper's own words:

- Population sampled: "a pool of possible characters" (p. 785); "a distribution of all
  possible configurations of characters" (p. 784); "the underlying pool of characters"
  (p. 786).
- Unit of analysis / resampling unit: characters. Abstract: "the proper method of resampling
  is to keep all ofthe original species while sampling characters with replacement". Species
  are held fixed; characters are drawn with replacement.
- Instrument: bootstrap resampling implemented as character weights ("the weight of a
  character being how many times it was drawn in bootstrap sampling", abstract), summarised
  by "Majority-rule consensus trees" (abstract), over an inference method that may be
  maximum likelihood, parsimony, or clustering by overall similarity (p. 786).
- Threshold: 95% of bootstrap estimates for significance; the complementary rejection rule
  is "less than 5% of the bootstrap estimates" (p. 786); 0.05 in the three-species
  derivation (p. 790).
- Denominator, checked against its near neighbours: the denominator of the 95% is
  **bootstrap replicate trees generated from one dataset** - "95% or more of the bootstrap
  estimates" (p. 786), "at least 95% of the bootstrap estimated trees" (p. 787). It is NOT
  a proportion of independent datasets, NOT a proportion of genes or loci, and NOT a
  proportion of true-tree-containing intervals across repeated studies.
- Operating conditions assumed: characters drawn i.i.d., "a necessary condition for the
  bootstrap method to be valid" (p. 785); independent evolution across characters;
  independent inclusion of characters by the systematist (p. 790); large n for exactness,
  with smaller n "an approximation but frequently is a very good one" (p. 784). Explicitly
  set aside: correlations among characters, which "in practice ... pose the most serious
  challenge to the use of bootstrap methods" (p. 785). Explicitly not corrected for:
  inconsistency of the inference method (p. 786).

**Does the claim sit inside this regime?** The claim is about the inferential scope of a
bootstrap support value, which is exactly what the p. 786 sentence addresses, so yes - but
the fit is not word-for-word, and the mismatch runs in both directions.

NUMERATOR CLASS of the paper's stated target, quoted with its exclusion: "the phylogeny that
would be estimated on repeated sampling of many characters from the underlying pool of
characters", excluding "the true phylogeny". DENOMINATOR: bootstrap replicates drawn from
the observed characters, used as a proxy for sampling from that underlying pool.

OVERLAP, both directions:

- What the paper's class MISSES relative to the claim's wording. The claim says
  "repeatability under resampling of the characters, given the model and the data". The
  paper's target is not a repeatability property of the dataset in hand; it is a limit
  quantity defined by a population - "the underlying pool of characters" - i.e. the estimate
  one would reach from that pool, which is a parameter rather than a feature of the given
  data. The claim's "given the model" has no counterpart in the paper's definition of the
  target; the paper's stated conditions are character-sampling conditions, and where models
  enter it is to say the bootstrap "does not correct for" an inconsistent method rather than
  to fold model correctness into the target. And the claim's gloss "infinite data of the
  same kind" uses a word the paper never uses: "infinite" and "infinitely" appear NOWHERE in
  the paper; what is printed is "many characters".
- What the paper's class INCLUDES that the claim excludes. The claim characterises the
  bootstrap value as "rather than a statement about the true organismal tree". On p. 786 the
  paper itself converts two 95% support values into "as little as 90% confidence in the
  statement that they are both present in the true tree" - a confidence statement about the
  true tree. The claim's characterisation excludes that use; the paper prints it.

PUBLISHED IS NOT PERFORMED. On the specific question the entry poses - whether the paper
prints the data-scoped inferential scope - the answer is yes, but note what kind of
statement it is: the p. 786 sentence DEFINES and ASSERTS the scope. It is an interpretive
definition, not a measurement. No number is attached to it, and within the sections I
transcribed the paper reports no empirical calibration of bootstrap proportions against
known true trees. If the entry's leg needs a sourced DEFINITION, this sentence is one; if it
needed a sourced EVALUATION of coverage, this paper DEFINES rather than measures.

bootstrap's inferential scope, as a definitional assertion, in one sentence on p. 786 with
no scoping opener, and that sentence both asserts the data-scoped target ("the phylogeny

characters") and expressly denies the true-phylogeny target ("not the true phylogeny"). The
paper also, one page's width away, converts two 95% support values into "as little as 90%
confidence in the statement that they are both present in the true tree". Both sentences are
in the paper; the sweep that misses the second is the one I ran first.

Two wording gaps between the entry's leg and the printed text are matters of record rather
than of judgement: the paper's target is a population quantity over an underlying character
pool, not "repeatability ... given the model and the data"; and "infinite" appears nowhere
in the paper.
