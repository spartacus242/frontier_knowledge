Question (canonical) — V1 (as submitted):
At 70–150 K, does the tensile strength of a sintered contact between two water-ice grains depend on whether the ice is stacking-disordered/cubic (Isd/Ic) or hexagonal (Ih) at matched temperature, contact time, load, grain size and surface area, and does the spontaneous Isd-to-Ih transformation change contact strength on its own annealing timescale?

Question (canonical) — V2 (the one reformulation permitted at Gate 1; scored as canonical):
(i) At three or more temperatures in 70–150 K, where the stacking state is quasi-frozen on a laboratory timescale, what is the ratio of mean grain–grain sintered-contact tensile strength for stacking-disordered/cubic (Isd/Ic) ice to that for hexagonal (Ih) ice, at matched temperature, contact time, load, grain radius and specific surface area, with stacking order measured on the same grains by XRD or Raman before and after each contact test? (ii) At one declared anneal temperature in 170–200 K — chosen because the Isd→Ih conversion runs on hours there and because it lies below the ~206–210 K onset at which the diffuse surface layer thickens — does contact strength change through an isothermal anneal that converts Isd to Ih, measured against a paired Ih control held at the same temperature for the same duration?

Significance — consequence:
If YES (ratio outside the pre-declared band, i.e. >=1.5 or <=1/1.5): every ice-sintering parameterisation of the Bahaloo form — force = f(temperature, load, duration, radius) — acquires a fifth state variable, and that variable is not a current condition but a THERMAL HISTORY. Two grain populations at identical present temperature, contact time, load and radius would then have systematically different cohesion because one was deposited recently and the other annealed. For ring, cometary and icy-moon regolith physics that is a qualitative change: cohesion becomes path-dependent, and a freshly vapour-redeposited surface layer (the ordinary state of a sublimating comet or an E-ring-coated moon) is mechanically distinct from annealed bulk at the same temperature. It would also hand the stacking-disorder community a mechanical observable, which it currently lacks.
If NO (the pre-declared null: ratio within 20% of unity and no change through the anneal beyond the paired control): the temperature-only parameterisations are justified against a specifically named and physically plausible alternative, rather than by default. That is a real gain, because the omission is currently an untested assumption: Bahaloo et al. do not mention polytype in the abstract, and the only paper that argues the point at all — Gärtner et al. — argues it from temperature non-coincidence in a different endpoint and confines its own conclusion to its own experiments.

(i) The laboratory ice-collision and ice-sintering community (the Gärtner/Gundlach/Blum/Fraser line, and mechanical sintering groups of the Bahaloo type) would act concretely: add a stacking-order characterisation step to sample preparation and a polytype label to every reported strength, which they do not currently do — Gärtner et al. have the diffraction capability on the same samples and state that stacking order "was not analyzed in depth".
(ii) Comet-nucleus and ring modellers who propagate laboratory tensile-strength parameterisations into aggregate strength would carry a thermal-history term.
(iii) The cubic-ice/stacking-disorder community (the Hondoh and Kuhs lines, and the stacking-order-kinetics work my Q7 returned) gains a mechanical consequence for a structural variable that is currently almost entirely characterised by diffraction and spectroscopy.
Caveat kept explicit: audience (ii) is reached only through P5, the contact-to-aggregate scaling, which this question's estimand explicitly excludes.

Significance — fecundity:
Does the thickness or mobility of the diffuse surface layer depend on the underlying stacking order? Gärtner et al. measure that layer and measure the phase and never cross them, so the crossing is immediately askable. Does the Isd→Ih transformation itself break or heal an existing neck — i.e. is the transformation a mechanical event at a contact, not just a structural one? Does polytype change the sintering ACTIVATION ENERGY (Bahaloo's ~41.4 kJ/mol) or only the prefactor — which would discriminate a surface-diffusion mechanism from a surface-energy mechanism? Does the same polytype–cohesion coupling appear in other polytypic molecular solids relevant to icy bodies?

Significance — anchoring:
(i) The unexplained ~210 K onset of collisional temperature dependence that Gärtner et al. attribute to surface features and from which they explicitly exclude bulk phase — a polytype-dependent SURFACE layer would reopen that attribution without contradicting their quoted sentence, since theirs excludes bulk phase only.
(ii) The standing gap between laboratory sintered-ice strengths and the very low strengths inferred for cometary material.
(iii) The open question on the ice-physics side of what stacking disorder does mechanically, as distinct from structurally and kinetically.

Reason for silence (a mechanism, not "nobody thought of it"):
An instrument-plus-disciplinary-boundary mechanism, and the evidence for it is in Prior 1 itself. The two measurements live in different apparatus: stacking order is read by diffraction or Raman on an ensemble inside a cryostat or a neutron beamline, while contact strength is read by micromanipulation or by collision on individual grains. The one group that got both onto the SAME particles — Gärtner et al., doing neutron diffraction on the very micrometre particles their programme collides — had no mechanical channel inside that beamline, and so had to settle the phase question by comparing phase-change temperatures with a collisional onset reported elsewhere rather than by measuring. Second, the mechanical-parameterisation literature works near the melting point (Bahaloo et al.: liquid bridges on separation, temperatures "closer to the melting point"), where stacking-disordered ice does not exist, so for those workers polytype is not a live variable and never enters the functional form. Third, the planetary community inherits the parameterisation rather than the sample-preparation question, so the omission propagates without being restated. Fourth, the transplant's source frame sits in pharmaceutical and powder physics (Q4), a field with no overlap in venues, vocabulary or conferences with cryogenic ice physics — "polymorph controls adhesion and caking" is routine there and the word "polymorph" does not travel.
