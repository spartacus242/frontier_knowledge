# Daily summary, 2026-10-29

## 08:53 UTC

**Problem:** w*(3;5) from Brown, Landman and Robertson's 2007 table (arXiv:0706.4420): the shortest length n at which every
5-colouring of 1..n has three evenly spaced numbers whose colours are all equal or themselves evenly spaced. The table
prints only "at least 75".

**Label:** PROMISING, not verified. The skeptic gate went 1 of 3.

**Best insight:** a purpose-built search, which crosses colours off each position as neighbours get coloured, is about 30
times faster than the SAT solver here. With it, every one of 97,969 pieces of the length-76 search came back impossible in
4.3 CPU-hours, so w*(3;5) = 76. A SAT solver agreed on 32 sampled pieces, and one skeptic wrote its own search from scratch
and also found no valid colouring of length 76. Two skeptics pointed out that the lower half is old news: colours 1, 2, 4
and 5 never form an evenly spaced triple, so the classical result W(3;4) = 76 (1979) already gives "at least 76", and the
printed "at least 75" was weaker than a known bound. Only the "at most 76" half is new. Side result: a valid 82-long
colouring with six colours, so w*(3;6), printed "?", is at least 83.

**Why it does not count:** two of the three skeptics could not finish their own exhaustive search in the time they had, so
they could not reproduce the upper bound. No skeptic found any earlier publication, but none could open the
Landman-Robertson book, Google Scholar or OEIS search.

**Next step:** a fresh gate on "w*(3;5) is at most 76" whose packet states the classical lower bound and warns that the
check needs a strong search running for several CPU-hours. Then push the six-colour lower bound past 83.
