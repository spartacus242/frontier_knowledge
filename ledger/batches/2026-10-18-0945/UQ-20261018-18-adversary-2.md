## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Museum and occurrence records often carry a hedge written by the person who named the specimen — "cf.", "aff.", "sp.", "?". Readers of aggregated data treat those hedges as if they meant something about how likely the name is to be right: they throw such records away, or keep them as if they were certain. Nobody has checked. The question asks: take qualified determinations that can be shown to have kept their hedge all the way from the original label into the delivered record; fix in advance what counts as an independent later determination and at which rank; then count how often the hedged name survives that later determination, separately for each mark, separately for revision-based and sequence-based resolutions. Three things are then asked of those counts: do they order the marks the way the published usage guidance says they should; does the number move so much between determiners, institutions, clades and decades that it cannot be carried from one dataset to another; and is the movement small enough, against a threshold named before the data were pulled, that an aggregator could publish the mark as a probability. In short: is a taxonomist's hedge a forecast that can be scored, and does it score the same way twice.

Two things I want on the record about the framing. First, the question is not "how often are specimens misidentified" (Goodwin 2015 answered that); it is "how often are specimens misidentified *given that the determiner said so*" — a conditional, and the conditioning variable is itself a published invitation to re-examine the specimen, so the predictor and the resolution mechanism are entangled by construction. Second, the deliverable the record actually promises is not a number but a *licence*: whether an aggregator may rewrite a mark as a probability. That makes the pre-declared tolerance the load-bearing part, and that is where I pushed hardest.

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional** — nothing Unresolved, so no Gate 4 rejection; but novelty is Medium with now **two** close neighbours (Sigovini et al. 2016; TDWG TG2 BISS 4:e58982, a Gate 2 miss in the candidate's own literature), and the Partials on Vagueness and Dissolution both reduce to the same unmet burden. Promotion to Validated on: (a) a demonstration that the frame step clears a stated minimum — a named corpus where qualifier survival is verified end-to-end and selection-free resolutions exist in quantity, given that TG2 found the field's values too unevenly adopted to test at all; and (b) a declared handling of specimen/material-quality covariates alongside the four strata, since the nearest published dataset's between-assemblage spread is driven by one of them.

**Single strongest killing point:** TDWG Task Group 2 (BISS 4:e58982, 2020) reviewed `dwc:identificationQualifier` values in GBIF and dropped both of its planned data-quality tests for this term because "rules of open nomenclature are unevenly adopted across data records" — the candidate's own target community, holding the data, judging the field untestable. It does not kill the question (it computes no rate, and in fact proves the audience is real), but it converts the carried-forward feasibility objection from speculation into evidence, and it is a close prior Gate 2 missed.
