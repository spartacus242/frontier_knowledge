Domain(s): Medicine/biology — biobank and cohort research methods; secondary: statistics (finite-sample robustness, non-ignorable attrition), survey methodology, data-protection scholarship, machine unlearning. Source method: silence mapping.
Question (canonical): In a biobank operating a deletion-on-withdrawal mandate, using dated withdrawal-ID tranches and a pre-fixed panel of headline estimates (k GWAS associations, k' hazard ratios, one polygenic score's discrimination): (a) what is the pre-deletion standardised difference between withdrawn and retained participants on the exposures, covariates and outcomes the cohort is actually used to study; (b) how far does each headline estimate move when recomputed on the frame immediately before and immediately after each tranche, against its Monte Carlo error and pre-declared decision boundaries; and (c) does the cumulative movement accumulate monotonically in the direction (a) predicts — systematic drift rather than noise?
Alternative formulations: What does the right to withdraw do to the scientific record a biobank has already produced? · Are consent-revokers systematically different, on the very variables the cohort studies, from those who stay, and how much do published estimates change when they are removed? · (method-field) How does the realised influence of the actually-withdrawn subset compare with the worst-case influence of an adversarially chosen subset of the same size, and is it signed and cumulative?
Motivating gap: Withdrawal's "how" is densely operational, its "so what" absent. UK Biobank's "Participant withdrawals on UKB-RAP" (opened) mandates that withdrawn data be "no longer accessed, analysed, or retained", asserts removal "supports the accuracy and validity of research outputs", and is silent on existing results, re-analysis, and any withdrawal count. Adjacent literatures are about entry (participation bias) and outcome ascertainment (dropout), not retrospective deletion of already-analysed data.
Presuppositions: P1 withdrawals numerous enough to move anything — ESTABLISHED at one custodian (HUNT2 1,187/65,291 = 1.9%, Ursin 2020, opened), but see A1: that is a sample-withdrawal rate, not a deletion rate, and UKB's lost_to_flup.pdf documents a withdrawal category under which prior data stay usable. P2 earlier/later frames both exist — CONTESTED; the mandate works against it. P3 custodian may lawfully retain a pre-deletion aggregate — CONTESTED (the legal-block outcome is declared an answer). P4 withdrawal is not the informative censoring attrition methods handle — ESTABLISHED structurally, CONTESTED in magnitude. P5 someone acts — CONTESTED. P6 deletion actually executed by every copy-holder — CONTESTED, and sharpened at the late Gate 4: the claim that public repositories hold dated withdrawal-ID lists is refuted (authenticated per-Application download, per-Application ID recoding, repositories exposing only private paths and filtering code), but one opened primary does establish that researchers can hold both frames at once — the ALSPAC/ARIES team held a 2015 and a 2018 frame of the same cohort and could see which participants were in one and not the other. So the irreversibility is de jure rather than de facto, which is itself the finding. P7 withdrawal motives not required — FLAGGED, load-bearing for novelty.
What would count as an answer: (a) a custodian-side analysis run once under governance before destruction, reporting withdrawer-vs-retained distributions on a pre-specified variable set plus the change in a pre-specified estimate panel across each tranche; or (b) a researcher-side equivalent from dated releases and archived derived files with withdrawal-ID lists as the only linkage; benchmarked against the worst-case (AMIP) shift for the same subset size. All within Monte Carlo error → non-issue, practice licensed. Any estimate across a decision boundary → an unquantified, growing, custodian-only-measurable erosion term. Legally unrunnable → the erosion term is unidentifiable to every party (a governance result with the same audience). Limb (c)'s sign test separates drift from noise.
