Question (canonical, Gate 0 as intaken):
For a standardized airborne-eDNA sampling protocol, what is the locality specificity of a detection — the probability that a detected vertebrate taxon was actually present inside the pre-specified survey unit during the sampling window — and how does detection probability for an independently verified point source decay with distance from it?

Question (canonical, Gate 1 reformulation — tightened for well-posedness; both versions retained):
For a named airborne-eDNA protocol (sampler type, filter, flow rate, duration) deployed in a named landscape class, (i) what is the fitted detection-probability-versus-distance function P(detect | taxon present at distance d, meteorology m) for independently verified point sources, reported with a half-distance and CI per taxon group; and (ii) conditional on that function and on an enumerated off-unit source inventory for that landscape, what proportion of a survey's taxon list is attributable to sources outside the pre-specified survey unit, with CI?
Reason for the reformulation: limb (i) of the intake version asks for a "locality specificity" of a *protocol*, but that quantity is a positive predictive value and therefore depends on the base rate of off-unit sources in the surrounding landscape, not on the protocol alone. The reformulation makes the landscape and the source inventory explicit arguments rather than leaving them implicit.

Alternative formulations:
- When an air sample yields a species' DNA, how far away could that animal have been?
- For airborne eDNA surveys, what is the spatial support of a single sample, expressed as a calibrated detection-probability-versus-distance curve against independently verified animal locations, and what share of a survey's taxon list is attributable to off-unit sources?

Motivating gap (as intaken):
Airborne eDNA metabarcoding studies repeatedly recover taxa demonstrably absent from the sampled enclosure or facility — Clare et al. 2022 (Hamerton Zoo) and Lynggaard et al. 2022 (Copenhagen Zoo) both report detections from outside the sampled area plus taxa traceable to animal feed — and were said to treat these as "unexpected" or "possible transport/contamination" without quantifying the rate or source distance, while eDNA is being standardized for regulatory biodiversity work (CEN/TC 230/WG 28 on DNA and eDNA methods; EN 17805:2023; existing regulated use of aquatic eDNA for protected-species presence/absence). Presuppositions (with Gate 1 status, revised against what Gate 2 found):
1. Airborne eDNA detections are reproducible enough for a per-taxon detection probability to be estimable — ESTABLISHED for a single targeted taxon by qPCR (Kroos et al. 2026 fit binomial GLMs and Bayesian logistic models to per-sample detection); CONTESTED for metabarcoded multi-taxon assemblages and in open landscapes.
2. A survey-unit boundary can be pre-specified and in-unit presence independently verified — ESTABLISHED at zoos/captive parks (Hamerton, Copenhagen, Rotterdam, the NZ wallaby park); CONTESTED in open landscape, where verification is the hard part. Note Kroos et al. explicitly could not exclude free-living wallabies outside the park, which is exactly this presupposition failing at the 1 km station.
3. Off-unit sources can be enumerated or at least bounded — CONTESTED, and the binding constraint. Neither Clare et al. 2022 nor Stewart et al. 2026 enumerates an off-unit inventory; Stewart et al. substitute a modelled wind catchment for an inventory.
4. Detections are not dominated by distance-independent field/laboratory contamination — CONTESTED. Westerduin et al. 2023 traced some non-local metabarcoding detections to taxa handled in the same laboratory and concluded the origin of the rest was inconclusive, i.e. a distance-independent component is documented and not yet bounded.
5. Detection decay is a modellable function of distance and meteorology rather than taxon-idiosyncratic beyond use — PARTLY ESTABLISHED and partly FALSIFIED in opposite directions: Kroos et al. 2026 find a significant negative distance effect on detection for a targeted assay, while Clare et al. 2022 find no relationship between read count and distance from the enclosure once in-enclosure samples are excluded (LR = 2.51, p = 0.113). So the signal appears in presence/absence of a targeted assay and not in metabarcoding read depth. The candidate's own stated decisive negative ("a flat curve") has therefore already been observed on one of the two readouts.
6. Regulatory monitoring will in fact use airborne eDNA for presence/absence determinations — CONTESTED; current regulated use is aquatic. CEN/TC 230/WG 28 and EN 17805:2023 are real, but I did not verify any air-specific eDNA standard in draft.
7. Airborne eDNA has a protocol-stable "sample" such that one curve is meaningful across studies — CONTESTED, and Kroos et al. 2026 is direct evidence against it: active samplers detected at 64.4% at 10 m against 11.1% for passive, so the curve is sampler-specific, not protocol-general.

What would count as an answer (as intaken):
A calibration experiment with independently verified source locations (zoo, farm, or translocated enclosure animals) and samplers at pre-specified distances, yielding (i) a fitted detection-probability-versus-distance curve per taxon group with half-distance and CI, and (ii) an estimate with CI of the proportion of a survey's taxon list attributable to off-unit sources; or an existing released dataset from which both can be estimated. A flat, distance-independent curve would be a decisive negative answer.
Gate 2 finding against this criterion: design (i) exists and has been executed and published (Kroos et al. 2026 — verified captive source, samplers at 0/10/100/1000 m, 110 samples, distance coefficient with 95% CI); an estimate answering (ii) exists in two forms (Clare et al. 2022: 17 of 25 detected mammal/bird species were known resident zoo species, i.e. 32% non-resident; Stewart et al. 2026: 100% of detections within 100 m fell inside modelled wind catchments, 62% out to 655 m). Neither reports a half-distance; that is the only element of the stated answer-criterion still missing.

Significance — consequence:
If detection probability decays steeply (Kroos: sharp drop beyond 10 m, residual detections at 1 km), an airborne-eDNA detection supports a locality claim only at fine scale with a long right tail of attributable error, so any regulatory use must attach a distance-conditional probability rather than a presence assertion. If the curve were flat, airborne eDNA could not support locality claims at all. The evidence already in hand splits by readout: presence/absence from a targeted assay decays with distance, metabarcoding read depth does not (Clare: p = 0.113). That split is itself consequential — it says read abundance cannot be used for localisation while targeted detection can be, which is what Clare et al. concluded in words. The marginal consequence of asking the candidate's question on top of this is therefore the quantification of the right tail, not the existence of a decay.
