## What the duplication cost

each other: across the fourteen adversaries the two batches ran, **six executed zero of their eight
required Disguise queries**, every call refused on an exhausted pool. In this batch that was severe —
for UQ-54, *neither* adversary could run a single query, and the candidate was rejected on an attack

a rejection ground of that kind.

The response was not to accept the rate. Every load-bearing kill was checked by a fresh-context
reader; the two Disguise sweeps that had never run were run (12 queries each); and the five
candidates whose kills dissolved were re-attacked by a late strict adversary with a real budget.

## What verification and the late adversaries changed

- **UQ-51: both legs of the disguised-known kill failed.** The kill said Li's Contamination_Detector
  and OpenCompass "already classify every retrieved web copy by whether it carries the gold answer".
  They do carry the three categories clean / input-contaminated / input-and-label-contaminated — but
  "input-and-label" means a question and *an* answer co-occur in the matched pages, with no comparison
  against the gold label anywhere. A real tool, accurately described, doing a different thing: this
  ledger's dominant error mode, found again. The second leg — that MMLU's keys were scraped from the
  very pages that would be the items' occurrences — had no source, and MMLU's own paper
  (arXiv:2009.03300) says most questions came from sources where questions and answers sit on
  *separate* pages. The late adversary (12 queries) could source no kill; the pressure it does apply
  is on identification — wrong-answer contamination and item difficulty are not separable
  observationally, so a signed correction needs an injection arm — and on the reason for silence,
  since infini-gram mini (arXiv:2506.12229) already runs an LLM judge over corpus hits for answer
  presence and Magar & Schwartz (arXiv:2203.08242) explicitly assumed gold labels and scoped the rest
  out. Noticed and set aside, not unseen.
- **UQ-52: the Gate 2 kill was partly refuted, and its routing row withdrawn.** arXiv:2503.20783 is
  "Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training: A Critical Perspective", an RL-for-math paper. It does
  measure template-free answering across six base checkpoints; it only *hypothesises* QA-formatted
  pretraining; it does *not* re-estimate post-training gains against its "nearly pure base model";
  and it does *not* conclude published gains are overstated. Its endpoints are capability-side only —
  no refusal, no persona, no chat-template preference. Two of the four properties the kill quoted are
  false. The late Gate 4 for this candidate could not be run: three launches on two models were each
  killed by a platform safeguard error before the agent could search, and the attempt was not
  reworded a fourth time to get around it. Disguise is therefore recorded Unverified, which blocks
  Validated; well-posedness 2/3 and an Unresolved attack 7 cap it at Provisional independently.
- **UQ-53: the kill was confirmed and narrowed.** Both quoted fragments are in the Deep Learning
  Tuning Playbook, under "Identifying scientific, nuisance, and fixed hyperparameters" — nuisance
  hyperparameters "need to be optimized over in order to fairly compare", and one weight decay across
  model sizes "would not be very insightful". But the playbook says nothing about estimability or
  confounding of a main effect, and no competing ML position was found. So the objection is an
  estimand mismatch — the field states a different comparison target in its own guidance, so coding
  per-arm re-tuning as aliasing selects the headline share rather than measuring it — resting on one
  guidance document plus a bounded absence, which the entry says plainly.
- **UQ-54: the instrument said to have pre-empted it has no behavioural endpoint.** Model Pulse does
  exist (github.com/tardellirs/modelpulse) and does build derivative family trees weighted by
  downloads — but it measures downloads, likes, ranks and milestones only, with no capability, refusal
  or jailbreak measurement, and the three figures the entry quoted (43%, 28%, 37%) appear nowhere in
  the pages opened and have been struck. It instruments the census half and is silent on the half the
  estimand is about. The 12-query sweep found nothing joining a download-weighted derivative
  population to a behavioural endpoint. What now caps the entry is sharper than what rejected it: the
  late adversary found arXiv:2608.15046 reporting, over 1,707 compressed-versus-original cells, that
  median per-item churn is about five times the median net accuracy delta and that the net quantity
  "is least informative when two models are most alike" — so a null D would not vindicate
  reference-weight evaluation, and half the claimed significance goes with that inference. Second, the
  weight itself: Hugging Face counts a download as a request to a query file and every .gguf file
  counts, so weight multiplicity differs systematically between GGUF and single-file classes.
- **UQ-55: the priors assert rather than ask.** arXiv:2508.17076 argues that recsys unlearning
  benchmarks "simulate unrealistically large unlearning requests" and should reflect "the sequential,
  time-sensitive nature of real-world deletion requests" — asserted, not measured, and not posed as a
  question; arXiv:2601.21682 stipulates a predefined stream and concedes in its Limitations that real
  requests may differ. Neither measures a demand stream, so they support the gap claim rather than
  closing it, and attack 2 lands at Partial. What the late adversary found instead is a defect in the
  frame: COPE's retraction guidelines make a retraction close to the *opposite* of a removal demand
  ("the online version of the article should be retained with a clear notice of retraction"), so a
  quarter of the pre-registered sampling frame contributes demands no claimant made. Severable, so it
  caps rather than kills. Two further facts bite on significance: xAI's mandated CCPA disclosure
  already publishes real counts (65 delete requests received, 65 completed, 0 denied, for 2025), and
  TOFU's forget set is drawn from 200 synthetic author profiles, so its corpus footprint is zero by
  construction.
