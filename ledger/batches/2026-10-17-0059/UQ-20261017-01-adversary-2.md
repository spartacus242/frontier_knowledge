## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take adsorption isotherms that someone has already measured, published, and deposited in a machine-readable archive, where you can also find out what surface area the paper itself claimed from that same isotherm. Re-do the BET arithmetic yourself with one fixed, rule-bound program (BETSI-class), decided in advance, including what to do when the rules permit several fitting windows or none. Call that the reference. Now compute the signed fractional gap between what the authors printed and what the rules give. Two questions: (a) is that gap positive on average — do published numbers sit above the rule-bound number; and (b) does the gap get bigger as the material's *rule-bound* area approaches the biggest area anybody had published for that kind of material at that date — i.e. is the slack in a discretionary analysis step spent upward precisely when there is a record at stake? Controls: material class, year, isotherm quality. The claim being tested is not fraud; it is that a free parameter gets pushed in the profitable direction, and that the push is measurable as a regression coefficient on "distance to the record".

My paraphrase deliberately drops the generator's framing of "the degree of freedom is exercised asymmetrically where it pays to be", because that is a conclusion, not a question.

## 3. My own technical objection, sourced and scope-checked

R1's central repair was to compute proximity-to-frontier from the RECOMPUTED reference area rather than the reported one, to kill V0's tautology. That repair introduces a **mechanical negative** bias into the very coefficient that is the primary answer, because the reference area now appears in the denominator of the outcome and the numerator of the regressor.

Sourced general result — Brett (2004), Oikos 105:647-656, read at the URL above. Brett classifies non-independent regressions and reports, verbatim: "spurious correlations of the form X/Z vs Y/Z, X/Z vs Y/Z, and X/Y vs Y are positive," **"while spurious correlations of the form X vs Y/X are negative"**; of that negative form he writes "these regressions are almost always negative" and "regressions of the form X vs Y/X often produce spurious correlations averaging r2 of roughly 0.52-0.63." He also states the dependence: "When the shared term CV was more than 1.5 times larger than the non-shared term CV, spurious correlations were very large," and "Conversely, when the shared term CV was smaller, spurious correlations were generally much smaller." Attribution of the general formula: "where CVZ, CVX, and CVY equal the CVs of variables Z, X, and Y, respectively (Pearson 1897)."

The candidate's primary regression is exactly Brett's negative form. Δ = (A/R) − 1 is Y/X with X = R; proximity P = R/M is X up to the per-class-year constant M. So the regressor is the shared term and the outcome is a ratio with the shared term in its denominator: the artifact's sign is negative.

Scope check, which cuts both ways and which I carry honestly: Brett also writes **"spurious correlations generated by measurement error are generally small."** That matters here, because if the authors' reported area A really was computed from the *same* deposited isotherm as the reference R, then R is deterministic given the pre-registered rules and there is NO error term shared between Δ and P — the artifact vanishes. My own arithmetic (labelled as mine): write log R = t + e and log A = t + b, where t is the material's area on the authors' own measurement basis, e is the mismatch between the deposited isotherm's basis and that basis, and b is the authors' analysis offset. Then log-Δ = b − e and P ∝ t + e, so cov(log-Δ, P) = −var(e), and the OLS slope picks up −var(e)/(var(t)+var(e)) on top of any true effect. So the artifact is proportional to the variance of the *sample/run mismatch*, not to the real spread of areas across materials.

Two facts make var(e) non-zero and the denominator small exactly where the answer lives. First, the record's own presupposition 5 is CONTESTED and cannot be enforced by inspection, because presupposition 7 concedes the authors' window — and therefore which measurement their number came from — is not recoverable; so same-isotherm provenance is assumed, never verified, and residual mismatch is guaranteed rather than optional. The record's own P3 (replicate disagreement in this archive, ~15-16% outlier rates) is the natural floor for var(e), though I did not re-open P3 and so quote that figure as the record's, not as verified by me. Second, var(t) is *restricted by construction* in the frontier stratum: near-record materials cluster in area, which is what makes them near-record. So the ratio var(e)/(var(t)+var(e)) is at its largest precisely in the thin stratum that the record itself says carries the primary answer ("the question's primary answer lives in that stratum").

Net: R1 traded V0's manufactured positive coefficient for a manufactured negative one, concentrated where the estimate matters. A positive finding would survive this (the bias is conservative), but the record's claim that "a coefficient indistinguishable from zero is an equally publishable answer and retires the hypothesis", and the whole null-branch significance case ("the round-robin's spread is exonerated as analyst noise"), are not supported: a null is the expected reading of a true positive effect of modest size under this estimator. One of the question's two answers is not an answer.

A repair exists and is why I grade this Partial rather than Unresolved: define proximity from a quantity independent of both A and R — e.g. the geometric/simulated nitrogen-accessible surface area of the material's crystal structure, which the record's own P2 computes and which exists at scale in structure databases, or the class-year record alone with material identity as the only varying input. But that repair *changes R1's canonical wording*, and R1 was the single permitted reformulation.

Second-strongest objection, from the record's own prior: at the frontier, "material class" and "the structural type where criteria-constrained BET most overestimates" are near-collinear. P2 reports BET overestimates monolayer loading worst in materials combining mesopores with 10-20 Å micropores, and frontier MOFs are disproportionately such materials. Class adjustment cannot separate "frontier materials are where author-convention and BETSI-convention diverge most" from "authors push harder near the frontier", because in the frontier stratum the two covariates are nearly the same variable. The record names P2 as a confound to control; it does not confront that the control is not identified in the stratum of interest.
