## 1. Restatement in my own words (of the REFORMULATED wording, which is the version I judge)

Build synthetic data whose input covariance has eigenvalues falling off as a power law, exponent beta. Pick one network architecture, one initialisation, one token budget, one recipe. Train it 54 times: six beta values crossed with nine (learning rate, batch size) pairs. On every trained network, take each hidden weight matrix with min(m,n) >= 512, look at its eigenvalue spectrum, and fit a tail exponent alpha by the Hill estimator on the top 10% of eigenvalues. Now look at the 54 alpha values as a surface over (beta, T = eta/B). The question is **how many arguments that surface really needs** — does it lie on a curve in beta alone, on a curve in log T alone, on a curve in one combination beta^a T^b, or does it genuinely need both — and what are the two partial derivatives at the centre of the grid, with confidence intervals.

Plainer still: **is the heavy tail of a trained weight matrix's spectrum set by the data's spectrum, by the optimizer's noise, by one product of the two, or by both independently — measured rather than derived.**

## 5. The one new source that matters, with its REGIME written out

**arXiv:2509.24882 — "Scaling Laws and Spectra of Shallow Neural Networks in the Feature Learning Regime" (ICLR 2026).** Read level: full text, `arxiv.org/html/2509.24882v1`.

**REGIME:** population = shallow / quadratic two-layer networks on n i.i.d. samples; unit of analysis = the eigenvalue spectrum of one trained network's weight/feature matrix; operating condition = **the ERM minimiser, explicitly not the learning dynamics**, optimised with LBFGS, with only n, d and the ridge lambda as knobs — **no learning rate, no batch size, no SGD noise anywhere in the frame**; instrument = analytic spectral density plus empirical spectra (Figure 2); threshold = none printed (no fitting range, no estimator, no tail fraction); category = phase classification of the trained spectrum and its tail exponent.

**What it prescribes.** The power law is on the **target**, not the input covariance. Inputs are isotropic: "$\{x_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{n} \stackrel{i.i.d.}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$ denotes the dataset" (Eq. 2). The paper says it adopts "the classical assumption of a target with a power-law spectrum", and for quadratic networks the target eigenvalues are "$\{\sqrt{d}\,i^{-\gamma}\}_{i=1}^d$" (Eq. 9 and surrounding text).

**What it prints — the load-bearing quotation.** Section 2.3, Phase III (Heavy-tail): **"The spectrum is a perturbed version of the target spectrum with a heavy-tail $\rho(x) \sim x^{-1-1/\gamma}$."** Phase VIb repeats it: "As in Phase III, the spectrum is a perturbed version of the target."

*Scope and polarity check on that cut, both things the grammar check does not do.* The sentence's **opening words** — "The spectrum is a perturbed version of the target spectrum" — scope the **trained** spectrum **in Phase III of this paper's own phase diagram**, one phase among several, and they tie the tail to a *perturbation of the target*. Dropping "a perturbed version of the target spectrum" would turn a phase-local statement into a general law, which the paper states nowhere. The clause **ASSERTS** the tail exponent 1 + 1/gamma; it does not deny it and does not record a move away from it.

*Published-is-not-performed, applied.* This paper **DERIVES and PREDICTS** the exponent. It does **not** measure an alpha surface. **I can name no fitted numeric alpha value from it, because none is printed:** the exponent appears as the symbolic form 1 + 1/gamma in a Section 2.3 bullet, not as a numbered equation, table cell or fitted value, with Figure 2 showing the corresponding empirical spectra. And its power-law exponent gamma sits on the **target**, where the candidate's beta sits on the **input covariance** — a different object carrying the law.

*Scope transfer, in writing.* Established in: isotropic Gaussian inputs, power-law target spectrum, the ERM minimiser with no dynamics and no optimizer knob, knobs n/d/lambda, LBFGS. **The candidate is outside this regime on both of its axes** — its beta is the input covariance exponent, and it has a temperature axis this paper does not possess. A result proved under those assumptions is **silent** outside them, not supportive.
