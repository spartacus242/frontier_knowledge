## Restatement in my own words

Take any standard test method whose published precision statement gives a reproducibility limit R. That R came from one interlaboratory study: a handful of laboratories, a few materials, a particular decade. The candidate asks: where the SAME designated method has been round-robined twice independently on matching material, how far apart are the two published R values (distribution of log R2/R1), and is that spread bigger than the spread you would expect anyway from the fact that each study estimated a variance component from a few laboratories? Second, smaller limb: across currently active methods, how old are the studies behind the precision statements and how big were they? In short: is the published reproducibility limit a property of the method, or a souvenir of the labs, materials and era that happened to be in its one study — and the test is realized between-study scatter versus each pair's own design-implied (chi-square) scatter.

REGIME of this evidence: North-American fire test methods with specialised apparatus, 1994-2000, unit = round robin per standard method. It does NOT generalise to high-population methods (petroleum PT schemes, food analysis), so it contests C3 in a subpopulation rather than globally, and the question survives on the remainder.
