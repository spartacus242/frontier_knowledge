## Restatement in my own words

Nearly every published LLM pretraining recipe contains the line "clip the global gradient norm at 1.0", and that number is copied unchanged from recipe to recipe and from one rung of a scaling ladder to the next. Two things nobody has written down. First, descriptively: in a given run, on what share of optimizer steps does that clip actually fire (i.e. the stack's clip coefficient comes out below 1), and does that share rise, fall or hold as the run proceeds? Second, comparatively: is "1.0" the same operation in two different places? It might not be, for two separate reasons — the number is compared against a norm reduced over different sets of parameters depending on the stack (global across DP/TP/PP vs per-tensor vs per-unit), and the typical size of that norm may itself move with model scale. The candidate's V2 wisely stops arguing about gradient magnitudes and instead asks about the *update that actually gets applied*: how far, in relative L2 and cosine, does the applied update sit from the update the optimizer would have applied with clipping switched off? If that deviation and the firing rate are stable across scales and groupings (within a band fixed in advance from seed reruns), the field's copy-paste habit is vindicated; if not, the deliverable is a translation table saying which threshold at scale S under grouping G reproduces the reference run's realized constraint. So: *how often does the clip bind, how much does binding actually change the update, and does the same printed number mean the same squeeze everywhere?*
