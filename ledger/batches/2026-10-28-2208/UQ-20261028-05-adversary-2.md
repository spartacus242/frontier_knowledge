## Restatement (my own words, before any search)

Four odd numbers — 35, 47, 53, 59 — are the only odd orders below 65 at which no quadruple of symmetric circulant ±1 matrices satisfying Williamson's equation exists; every other odd order up to 63 has at least one, and 69 has one. The candidate fixes a list of five functions of the integer n alone — (i) the multiplicative order of 2 mod n together with the number of 2-cyclotomic cosets mod n; (ii) the factorisation shape of n and n mod 4; (iii) the number of four-square representations of 4n meeting the Williamson parity/congruence constraints; (iv) an "orbitwise count of representations of 4n as a sum of four squared moduli at each n-th root of unity"; (v) h+ and the 2-rank of the class group of Q(ζ_n)^+ — and asks: does any one of them take a set of values on the four failing orders that is *disjoint* from the set of values it takes on the realisable ones? And if one does, what does it say about 65 and 67, the smallest odd orders whose Williamson status is unknown?

Stripped of framing: **is there a function on the record's list whose value sets on a 4-element set and a 29-element set of integers happen not to intersect, and does it classify two further integers.**
