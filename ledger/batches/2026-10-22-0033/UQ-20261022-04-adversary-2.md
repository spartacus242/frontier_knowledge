## Restatement in my own words (before attacking)

Take an ordinary, undrugged cell. Its ribosomes put the wrong amino acid in at roughly one residue in a
thousand. That number is a *ratio measured over a population of molecules*. The candidate asks for the
*histogram behind the ratio*: if you could read individual finished protein chains one at a time from end
to end, how many wrong residues would each chain carry — zero, one, two, more — and how often? Then it
asks whether that histogram is wider than it should be if each site erred on its own, where "on its own"
is made precise as a Poisson-binomial built from the position- and codon-specific rates measured in the
same cells (not a single-lambda Poisson). Two sharpened limbs: the reading window must be longer than one
tryptic peptide (out to the whole chain), and the cell must be unstressed. The headline number wanted is
the fraction of molecules carrying two or more errors.

In plainer terms: is mistranslation spread thinly over almost all molecules, or concentrated on a bad
minority — and is the concentration more than site-by-site chance predicts?

## My own arithmetic (labelled; the record's uncertainty carried through)

(i) *The stake.* Taking the record's inherited Mordret figure (~1 misincorporation per 1,000 residues) and a
300-residue chain, λ ≈ 0.3, so under Poisson P(≥1) ≈ 25.9% and **P(≥2) ≈ 3.7%**. The record itself notes
Mordret's per-site rates "span orders of magnitude", so λ is not pinned: at λ = 0.1, P(≥2) = 0.47%; at
λ = 1.0, P(≥2) = 26%. **My arithmetic, range not point estimate.** It confirms the record's own honest bound.

(ii) *Why this estimand is the most reading-error-exposed one there is.* A count-per-chain estimand
accumulates per-residue false calls along the chain: expected spurious calls per molecule = L × f. For
spurious calls not to swamp a 3.7% "2+" fraction over L = 300 you need **f ≲ 10⁻⁴**. From S1's measured
20-way per-read accuracy of 28–42%, f ≈ 0.58–0.72 → **~175–216 spurious "errors" per chain**; from S1's
*simulated* 10-reread 61%, f = 0.39 → ~117. Only S2's **extrapolated** <10⁻⁶ at >30 rereads would clear the
bar (3×10⁻⁴ spurious calls per chain) — and that figure is an extrapolation on a 26-mer at one known site.
**My arithmetic from S1/S2's printed numbers.**

(iii) *The identification problem.* Let N|λ ~ Poisson(λ) with λ varying between molecules. Law of total
variance: Var(N) = E[Var(N|λ)] + Var(E[N|λ]) = E[λ] + Var(λ) ≥ E[N], with equality only if λ is degenerate.
**My own one-line derivation, checkable as written.** So *any* between-molecule variation in the error rate
— ribosome-to-ribosome, cell-to-cell, cell-cycle tRNA charging — produces overdispersion against a
Poisson-binomial built from population-average per-site rates, with **zero within-molecule dependence**.
A positive count-distribution result therefore does not identify the processivity mechanism the
significance section wants to extend from P1. (What would identify it: the *spacing* of co-occurring errors
along the chain — which single-molecule reads would give but the candidate's stated answer criteria never
mention.)

and I read both: S2 reads a 26-residue synthetic peptide ("approximately 25-30 amino acids") and
discriminates three **pre-specified** variants at **one known site**, naming de novo sequencing as future
work; S1 translocates long strands but calls sequence only inside designed variable regions, requires

**no** detection limit, and tops out at 28–42% 20-way per-read accuracy (61% only in a *simulated* 10-reread).
The candidate's estimand is a count per chain, so it integrates per-residue false calls along the chain:
reaching a 3.7% "2+" fraction over 300 residues needs a per-residue false-call rate ≲10⁻⁴ (my arithmetic),
while the measured rates are 0.58–0.72 — the instrument error exceeds the biological signal by 2–3 orders of
magnitude, and the only number that would suffice is an extrapolation on a 26-mer at one known position.
This caps the entry rather than rejecting it, because the record declared the presupposition instead of
smuggling it and the underlying fact about cells is instrument-independent.

**Second leg, named separately as required (it stands alone and would survive the first falling):** by the
law of total variance (derivation (iii)), the count distribution alone cannot distinguish within-molecule
error processivity — the mechanism the significance case wants to extend from P1 — from ordinary
between-molecule variation in the error rate. The candidate's stated answer criteria name no endpoint that
