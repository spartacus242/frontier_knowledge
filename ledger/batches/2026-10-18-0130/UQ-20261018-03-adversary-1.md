## Restatement in my own words

Take one registry where every case carries the operating clinician's identity and date. For each case, measure how long it has been since that clinician last did this procedure, and how many they did in the last 30 and 90 days. Holding fixed who the clinician is, their trailing-year and lifetime volume, the patient mix and the calendar, ask (a) whether a longer gap is associated with worse patient outcome, and (b) if so, how long a gap it takes for the fitted outcome to worsen by a pre-chosen absolute amount relative to the fitted outcome at very short gaps — i.e. the "currency interval" a pilot-style privileging rule would write down — with a confidence interval, or a statement that no such gap exists within the gaps actually observed.

Stripped of the aviation furniture, this is: *does within-operator time-since-last-case predict outcome net of volume, and what is the dose-response curve?* The aviation rule is a use for the answer, not part of the question.
