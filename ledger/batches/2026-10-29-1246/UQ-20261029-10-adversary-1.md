## Restatement (my own words)

Take a JIT-compiled program running under an adaptive VM whose per-iteration times, in Barrett et al.'s sense, never settle. Is the *reason* it never settles that the VM keeps re-deciding what to compile (profile → recompile → new profile → …)? Test: record the VM's per-method compilation plan from a pilot run; run the same program (a) with the loop live, (b) with that plan imposed from the start and adaptive recompilation switched off, (c) with the plan imposed only after the point where the pilot stopped compiling anyway (sham). Count what share of runs a pre-fixed changepoint rule calls non-convergent in each arm; the headline is the (a)−(b) difference, with (c) telling you how much of it is just "any freeze calms things". Pair set: every Barrett VM×benchmark pair whose VM, in a *current* release, can pin a recorded per-method plan.

## DEPTH CHECK (Jace, 2026-10-28) on my restatement

Variation check: the one thing that would have to differ for the answer to differ is whether the runtime recompiles adaptively — a dynamical property, not a practice, harness or convention.
