## Restatement in my own words

Take every positive fundamental discriminant d and the two quadratic fields Q(sqrt d) (real) and Q(sqrt -3d) (imaginary). Scholz's theorem says the 3-rank of the imaginary field equals that of the real field or exceeds it by exactly one. The candidate asks: once that constraint is granted, is there anything MORE that the real field's 3-class group tells you about the imaginary one? Concretely (Gate 1 repair): for each finite abelian 3-group G of order <= 27, does the empirical frequency of the "+1 jump" among pairs with Cl_3(Q(sqrt d)) isomorphic to G equal 3^-(rank(G)+1), the value Dutarte derived from the single-field Cohen-Lenstra laws, or does it depend on G beyond its rank — i.e. is the joint law of the two 3-class groups the product of the single-field laws conditioned on Scholz, or is there residual structure?
