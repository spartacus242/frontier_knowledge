## Restatement (my own words, before any attack)

Take a program running on a managed runtime and record, for every heap object, the moment it was allocated and the moment it became unreachable, both on one clock (bytes allocated so far). Treat those objects the way a demographer treats a population: tabulate deaths and objects-at-risk in cells of (object age, program time), fit a Poisson rate model with age, program-time ("period") and allocation-epoch ("cohort" = period − age) terms, and ask three things: (i) do the second differences of the period effects and of the cohort effects differ from zero (with intervals), since under the exact relation period = cohort + age only second differences are estimable; (ii) does a pre-declared likelihood-ratio test reject the model in which only age matters; (iii) how much deviance does each added axis remove, reported under every ordering of adding the axes because no unique split exists.

## Attack 4 worked first (presupposition failure), as instructed — the numbered conditioning clauses of V2 as it now stands

1. Exact object death times are obtainable on a bytes-allocated clock for whole managed-runtime programs. **TRUE (sourced, F1 full text):** Merlin defines death time as the interval after last reachability, on a bytes-allocated clock, and states generation finer than each allocation is feasible; cost 70–300× slowdown. Caveat recorded: the paper's own experiments are at 1 KB granularity and coarser, so "exact" is demonstrated as a capability, not as the condition of the reported traces. For runtimes other than Java (OCaml, .NET, V8, named in the audience) a Merlin-equivalent is UNVERIFIED by me; the question needs only one runtime, so this kills nothing.
2. With age and death time on ONE clock, cohort c := p − a satisfies p = c + a exactly. TRUE by arithmetic (my own reasoning; it is a definition, not a finding).
3. Under that exact dependence only curvature-type quantities are identifiable, so the quantities V2 restricts itself to are well defined. **TRUE, CONDITIONALLY (sourced at abstract level, F2):** the structural-link identification problem is confirmed; but F2 also states that quantities identifiable under equal intervals "are no longer identifiable for unequal data". V2 declares the clock and NOT the cell widths. An equal-width lattice in bytes across object ages spanning many orders of magnitude is either enormous or destroys the infant-mortality structure (MY OWN REASONING, no source), so a natural analyst would use unequal (e.g. logarithmic) age cells — and then the identifiable set is not the classical one. The clause is true only once an equal-interval lattice is declared, which V2 has not done. Not FALSE; an under-specification handed to attack 5.
4. The at-risk set of each (a, p) cell is recoverable from birth and death times. TRUE by arithmetic (the record's own reasoning, which I checked and agree with: an object born at b dying at d is at risk in cells (a, b+a) for a < d−b; objects alive at program end contribute exposure up to the end and no death — right-censoring that a Poisson exposure model absorbs; my own reasoning).
5. A pre-declared phase rule independent of age exists. **NOT A CLAUSE OF V2.** The record calls this "load-bearing in both versions"; on V2's own wording the period axis is death time p on the bytes clock, cell by cell, and no phase partition enters the estimand anywhere. Phase appears only in the significance text (phase-keyed nursery sizing). So the record's "sharpest presupposition" is a V1 presupposition that V2 removed without saying so, and the record has not re-tested V2 on its own words. This is a defect of the record's self-description, not of the question; it kills nothing and should be corrected.
6. At least five programs with full traces exist. TRUE (F1 names SPECjvm98 programs traced; sourced).
7. V1's "every generational collector's mechanism assumes" — an unenumerated universal over collector implementations; dropped from V2 and marked Unverified by the record itself. Not in the scored wording.
8. Hidden clause the record does not list: that a pooled hazard over all objects is the right object when sites differ strongly in lifetime distribution (Jones & Ryder, read in full by Gate 2). A non-zero period curvature in the pooled hazard can arise purely from drift in the SITE MIX over time while every object's hazard depends only on age given its site — compositional confounding (MY OWN REASONING; no source opened measures this). This does not make the clause false — the pooled marginal is exactly what an age-only collector faces — but it means the finding bears on collector design and not, without a site covariate, on whether an object's own hazard depends on period. Handed to attack 8.
