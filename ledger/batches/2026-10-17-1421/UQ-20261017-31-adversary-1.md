## Restatement (my words)

When you dissociate a tissue and run droplet scRNA-seq, some cells burst and their RNA ends up as free-floating "soup" that is read out in the empty droplets. The candidate asks whether that soup is a usable ledger of the dead: deconvolve it against a fixed cell-type signature matrix, convert RNA-mass fractions to cell counts using separately measured RNA-per-cell and per-type degradation, and ask whether the resulting per-type "missing cells" estimate tracks (slope with interval, tested against 0 and 1) the per-type deficit you get by counting the same tissue block in situ and subtracting the droplet counts. V1 (as submitted) asks the looser question: does the soup tell you which cell types were destroyed and roughly how much.
