## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (canonical, as intaken):
How many people are living with an implanted medical device whose manufacturer has exited, been liquidated or withdrawn support — so that programming, replacement parts, external components or remote monitoring the therapy depends on are no longer available — and what are their measured clinical outcomes (loss of therapy, revision, explant, newly acquired imaging or treatment contraindications) compared with matched recipients of still-supported devices?

Question (canonical, AFTER the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation — this is the version graded):
For a declared jurisdiction and window, and under a support-withdrawal event definition fixed and published before any data pull (minimum constituents of "support" declared per implant class: programming/interrogation capability, firmware and security updates, consumable and external-component supply, replacement-part supply, remote-monitoring service, and a named clinical point of contact): (i) what is the enumerated list of support-withdrawal events and, per event, the estimated number of living recipients with its source and stated uncertainty; and (ii) for those events whose recipients are followable, what is the incidence of therapy loss, revision, explant and newly acquired imaging or treatment contraindication in the post-exit period, compared with the same recipients' pre-exit period and with matched recipients of still-supported devices of the same class — with the estimand declared separately per class and the null permitted for passive classes; the answer to report the share of events whose exposed population could not be counted at all and the share of classes for which no outcome ascertainment was possible.
(Both versions recorded as required. The reformulation changes nothing about the object; it fixes well-posedness only, by (a) stipulating the exposure definition and its pre-registration, (b) making the estimand per-class with a permitted null, and (c) promoting the unenumerable shares from a caveat to a reported quantity.)

Alternative formulations:
1. (Lay) What happens to patients when the company that made their implant goes away: how many such patients are there, and what measurably happens to them afterwards?
2. (Technical) Treating manufacturer exit or support withdrawal as a time-varying exposure on implant cohorts, what is the incidence of support-withdrawal events per implant class and the hazard of therapy loss, revision or explant following exposure?
3. (Method-field wording, pharmacoepidemiology) What is the patient-level effect of product market withdrawal when the product is a permanently implanted device rather than a dispensed drug — i.e. when the withdrawal cannot be answered by switching?

Source method: silence mapping.

## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

What would count as an answer:
For a declared jurisdiction and window: (a) an enumerated list of support-withdrawal events under a definition fixed before any data pull; (b) per event, an exposed-population estimate with its source and stated uncertainty; (c) for events whose recipients are followable, a matched-cohort estimate of therapy loss, revision, explant or contraindication acquisition, post-exit versus pre-exit and versus still-supported comparators, with the estimand stated per class; and (d) the share of events for which the exposed population could not be counted at all, and the share of implant classes for which no outcome ascertainment was possible. (d) is not a limitation section — it is a reported result, and it is the quantity a regulator would act on even if every estimable contrast turns out null.

Is it a question, not a thesis? Yes, with one caveat. The intake framing ("so that programming, replacement parts... are no longer available") embeds the expectation that exit causes deprivation. The reformulation defuses this by declaring the estimand per class and permitting the null for passive classes, and by making the unenumerable share a reported result rather than an excuse. A residual agenda-risk remains and is named as the second-strongest objection below.

Residual well-posedness defects (why this is not 3/3): "support" is stipulated, not discovered, so two honest teams could produce different event lists from the same jurisdiction; "contraindication acquisition" needs an operational rule (e.g. MRI-conditional status becoming unverifiable once the labelling holder is gone) that no source I opened supplies; and the exposure-ascertainment instrument is partly the thing under study, so the enumeration is reflexive. All three are answerable by stipulation-plus-sensitivity-analysis, which is why this scores 2 rather than 1.

## Gate 3 — Significance

Consequence (argued): If the exposed population turns out substantial and post-exit outcomes measurably worse, the finding converts a decade of anecdote and op-ed into an effect size, and makes concrete regulatory instruments arguable on evidence — escrow of programming software and firmware keys at approval, a transfer-of-support duty surviving insolvency, mandatory registry follow-up of withdrawn models, a per-class minimum support specification. If outcomes are null for passive classes and small for active ones, the normative alarm is calibrated down and the useful residue is the unenumerability result. Either way the reported share of events whose exposed population cannot be counted is the actionable number, because it measures a surveillance system's blind spot rather than a clinical effect, and it is the one quantity that cannot come out reassuring by luck.
Audience (specific, and shown to be asking already): device regulators (FDA CDRH's post-market programmes, EU competent authorities and the MDCG now writing supply-continuity guidance under Regulation (EU) 2024/1860 per Q9); implant registry custodians, who would have to decide whether follow-up of a withdrawn model continues; professional societies issuing legacy-device guidance — the CRST letter is a society-adjacent journal publishing exactly this plea in 2023, and Prior 1 records researchers and patient-participants asking for post-trial support in 2022; insolvency practitioners and courts disposing of medtech estates, who currently have no patient-exposure figure to weigh; and payers who fund the revisions and explants.
Fecundity: what minimum artefact set must be escrowed per implant class for therapy to survive a sponsor's exit; should support-continuity risk be priced at approval, and by whom; what is the right surveillance definition of "support", given that the candidate must stipulate one; does registry follow-up lapse differentially for withdrawn products, which is a measurable question about the registries themselves; and what is the explant-versus-abandon-in-situ decision rule for a non-functioning implant.
Anchoring: the post-trial-obligations debate in neuroethics (Prior 1 and the Monash/Penn State records); the Argus II and NeuroControl cases; the UDI-in-claims completeness problem (Pew/NESTcc, Q11); and the EU device supply-continuity rules (Q9). It also touches the recall literature (Q5, Q7) by negation — recall epidemiology exists precisely because a trading manufacturer can be made to act, which is the condition the candidate removes.

Reason for silence (mechanism, not "nobody thought of it"):
Primary mechanism — an incentive and custody gap created by the exposure itself. The party holding the implant count, the model-level records and the support obligation is the party that has ceased to exist; insolvency disposes of assets, not of post-market duties, so the data custodian disappears at the exact moment the exposure begins, and no successor inherits a surveillance duty. There is no sponsor for the study either: the manufacturer cannot fund it, the regulator has no mandate attached to a delisted product, and registries are partly manufacturer-funded and built around the survivorship of marketed implants, so a withdrawn product's follow-up is the first thing to lapse — the same mechanism that produces the silence being mapped (P3).
Secondary mechanism — a disciplinary boundary that split the question in two and gave neither half to epidemiology. The normative half was captured by bioethics and neuroethics (Prior 1, Prior 3), which ask what obligations a sponsor or regulator carries; the enumerative half was captured by investigative journalism (IEEE Spectrum, ICIJ Implant Files), which counts cases story by story. No journal section owns "support withdrawal as a time-varying exposure", and the field that owns the method (pharmacoepidemiology, Prior 2) studies dispensed products where withdrawal is answered by substitution, so the implant case never arrives on its desk.

Scores: Well-posedness 2/3 · Significance 3/3
Well-posedness 2/3 because the exposure definition is stipulated rather than discovered, "contraindication acquisition" lacks an operational rule in anything I opened, and the exposure ascertainment is reflexive with the phenomenon; it would be 3/3 given a published per-class support specification with a dated event rule and a demonstrated sensitivity analysis over definition variants.
Significance 3/3 because two distinct communities are on the record asking for this fact base (Prior 1's researchers and patient-participants, 2022; Prior 3's clinical journal, 2023), a regulator is currently writing rules in the adjacent space (Q9), and the unenumerable-share result is actionable even under a complete null.

Strongest remaining objection (to be handed to Gate 4, not resolved here): significance may be inflated by magnitude. The only exposed-population figure anywhere is >350 persons for one device model, worldwide and cumulative; if per-event populations are routinely in the hundreds and the affected classes are few, no regulator changes a rule and the honest answer is a case series, not an epidemiology. Second-strongest: the question's framing presumes deprivation, and if most exits are accompanied by orderly transfer of support (P6 — which EU Regulation 2024/1860 material I did NOT open may bear on), the exposure is rare by construction. Third: P1 is a claim about how end-of-service and insolvency documents behave, and I opened none of the instances my own searches returned.
