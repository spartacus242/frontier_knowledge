## The one verdict change: UQ-49

UQ-49 asks, for each published model-based pretraining quality filter applied at its
shipped threshold to a dated Common Crawl snapshot, what the prevalence-corrected
enrichment ratio of machine-generated text in the kept set is, and whether it grows
as filtering gets stricter. The batch rejected it as an answered disguised known, with
Disguise Unresolved from **both** in-batch adversaries — independently fatal — on the
ground that arXiv:2609.40295 (30 September 2026, seventeen days before intake) had
already measured and published it.

Because the whole rejection rested on one paper, it got two independent readers, one
searching by identifier and one by subject alone. The paper is real, and both readers
found it to be the on-point paper. Almost everything claimed *of* it needed narrowing:

- It is a **scaling-law paper** — "How Much Is an AI Token Worth? Scaling Laws for
  Wild AI-Generated Web Text" (Russell et al.), fitted over 800 pretrained models.
  The filter-survival audit is a motivating section.
- The survival figures are exact and are in **documents**: 29.3% against 12.8% through
  the FineWeb pipeline (2.3×), 14.5% against 1.5% through DCLM's full pipeline (9.8×),
  over a 10,000-document raw Common Crawl sample. The audit names no labeller.
- **The 27.5% → 31.1% pair is not a before-versus-after-filter contrast.** It is a
  June-to-August 2026 time trend among tokens that have *already* passed FineWeb
  filtering, and the paper reports no pre-filter token share at all. Survival is in
  documents and the shares are in tokens, so the two cannot be combined into an
  enrichment ratio.
- **It names none of the three filters the question asks about.** FineWeb-Edu, DCLM
  fastText and Nemotron-CC do not appear; what is measured is two whole pipelines.
- **No keep-rate ladder and no threshold sweep of any kind**, found independently by
  both readers, so the monotonicity limb is untested in print. No prevalence
  correction either, and none of the words Rogan-Gladen, sensitivity, specificity or
  prevalence correction occurs in the paper.
- The detector operating points (Pangram 3.3.2, FPR 0.05%, FNR 1.99%) are
  vendor-linked: the cited Glickenhaus et al. 2026 shares an author with this paper.

That withdrew the fatal ground, so Gate 4 was re-run cold and separately per
LEDGER.md Gate 5: eight neutral literature questions, one per fresh-context reader,
then two independent judges given the record, the established facts and all eight
answers, neither shown the other's reasoning and both told the Low grade was void.

**No attack came back Unresolved from either judge**, so the fatal rule on attacks 1,
2 and 4 is not triggered and the rejection does not survive. Both judges returned
Provisional independently. Significance 1/3 bars Validated on its own.

The sharpest point either judge made is new and belongs to whoever takes this up:
**neither published survival figure isolates a named model-based filter at its own
operating point.** The FineWeb v1.4.0 arm contains no model-based quality classifier
at all, and the DCLM figure confounds heuristics, deduplication and the fastText
stage. The candidate's actual object is therefore measured nowhere.

Two findings cut the other way and are recorded in the entry. The ladder *design* is
published — Gao 2021 (arXiv:2109.00698) varies its filter's own aggressiveness and
reports kept-set composition along the ladder as source-likeness fractions, with
Table 1 discard fractions from 0.4107 to 0.9315 — so the form is not novel, only the
instantiation. And the distinguishing correction limb is close to inert *and* close
to unexecutable: it moves the published figure by about half a point, the uncorrected
ratio is already a valid lower bound under nondifferential error, and there is no
provenance gold standard for detector positives in a wild dated snapshot.

## Two kills that stand, with their citations corrected

**UQ-46** (does a benchmark carry a fragile stratum shared across quantization, dtype,
kernel and seed perturbations, and what share of the headline score sits in it) stays
Rejected at Gate 2. Its two carrying priors were verified in unusual detail. Prior A
(arXiv:2608.21382) came back confirmed down to all five of the absence claims the
entry rests on — no numerics arm, no concordance statistic, no per-item null, no
margin conditioning, and no stated batch size, the word "batch" not occurring in the
document — and its Section 6 does name a margin-based per-item flip predictor as
future work. Prior B (arXiv:2608.06564) was read in full for the first time by anyone
in this audit, since Gate 2 and both adversaries had only its abstract page; its
figures are exact, with one unit error corrected (the 0.86/0.33/0.00 medians are of
the *surviving margin fraction*, a regression slope, not "median damage"), and it has
no nominally-lossless arm and no concordance statistic, only raw same-item counts.
Hu et al. 2022 holds on substance with two description errors: "In Total" is a row,
not a column, and its 30 settings are model × training strategy rather than
quantization configurations.

The third leg weakened, and the entry now says which. HEAL **does not cross attention
implementation** — all its baselines hold TritonAttention fixed because FlashAttention
lacks 32-bit support — which the record had listed among its arms. So the kernel arm
is unoccupied in all four priors, and the routed residual is one arm larger than
recorded. HEAL's "highly unstable questions" line also turns out to be a two-fragment
manual-inspection remark in an appendix, framed by its authors as a dataset-quality
caveat rather than a finding about flip concentration. A decoration fell; the ground
held, so no late Gate 4 was run, and the entry states that reasoning.

**UQ-47** (what fraction of frontier-model production compute is non-gradient-step
work, and does counting it reorder models) stays Rejected as asked-but-still-open —
but **its strongest kill is misattributed**. arXiv:2605.01158 is not the Olmo 3
technical report: it is "The Hidden Cost of Thinking" by Morrison, Smith and Strubell
(1 May 2026), an environmental-accounting study *of* that family. Its content checks
out, including §4.3's "Development accounts for 82.2% of total GPU hours (6.85M of
8.34M)" verbatim. Three narrowings, all cutting toward the candidate: the denominator
excludes 4,866,064 MI250X GCD-hours of synthetic-data generation in a non-comparable
hardware unit, so the five components are not in one commensurable frame; there is no
explicit non-gradient-to-gradient ratio anywhere, only per-stage totals to combine;
and the paper contains **no FLOP figure at all**. The Epoch essay is confirmed on all
seven attributed claims, with the correction that **discarded and failed runs are not
discussed in it at all**.

## The near-miss, and a promotion clause that was wrong

**UQ-50** (how often a nominal gradient-clipping threshold actually fires, and whether
one nominal number denotes the same intervention across scales and parameter
groupings) was the closest this ledger has come to Validated in twenty-eight batches:
well-posedness 3/3, significance 2/3, no Unresolved attack, and a promotion clause
stating that opening three named DP papers and finding none of them reaches the
composite estimand "reaches Validated".

All three were opened at PDF level. **None reaches it.** arXiv:2212.01539's "fraction
of clipped gradients" is a per-example privacy quantile, not a share of optimizer
steps; arXiv:2006.15429 reports no clip-binding fraction at all; arXiv:2206.07136 has
clipping percentages but per-sample and cited from other work, in DP fine-tuning.
Every item in the clause is discharged.

**And the entry stays Provisional, because the clause was wrong about Gate 5.** Gate 5
names "Medium novelty with a close neighbor" as the Provisional case outright, and
this entry's close neighbours — fairseq's per-window clipped-update percentage and
ZClip's Figure 11 — were confirmed at first hand, not removed. Reading the DP papers
was never going to remove them. The clause has been rewritten to name what a High
grade would actually require.

Three corrections run the entry's own way. fairseq's `clip` field dates to **December
2017**, not 2018 (issue #79 prints "clip 94%"), so the concession is older than
stated. The conceded "0% to 100%" published range is **not real**: issue #250's 100%
was logged on a run with `--clip-norm 0.0`, clipping disabled, which v0.6.0 later
guarded with a `clip_norm > 0` condition. And OLMo's `clipping_rate` counts **parameter
tensors per rank**, not steps, and is dropped from the console log. Against the entry,
arXiv:2206.07136's Theorems 1 and 2 turn out to publish the constant-rescaling
invariance its rows 4 and 8 had treated as the adversary's own unsourced reasoning.

**UQ-48** (is the missingness in the model × benchmark panel behind published AI
capability trends ignorable, and what is the signed bias in the fitted level and
slope) gained the read the batch could not get. Appendix E of arXiv:2512.00193 was
opened: its only inclusion check on the slope is **uniform random dropout** — 100
draws, median slope 0.54 against the main 0.55, across-selection interval 0.43–0.64 —
and the paper contains no Rubin, no "ignorable", no missing-at-random mechanism and no
selection-bias discussion anywhere. So the MNAR question is untouched and limb (b)
sharpens to exactly the MNAR-versus-MCAR distinction. Three of its four promotion
items are discharged; the fourth is a design decision for whoever answers. Two
corrections to its priors: Kang is a NeurIPS 2026 **workshop** poster readable only at
abstract level, and the BenchLM post's 272/170 rescoring describes a **retired**
method, superseded by BenchAlign v5 on 14 July 2026.

## What to carry forward

1. **A third distinct citation failure in three batches.** The 16:19 batch had a
   citation that does not exist; the 18:23 batch had one that was not locatable plus
   one misattributed to the wrong paper by the right author; this batch has an
   identifier that **resolves to an entirely different paper by different authors**
   (2605.01158 as "the Olmo 3 report"). Checking that a cited paper exists is not
   enough — check that the identifier resolves to the paper named.
2. **A promotion clause is not self-validating.** UQ-50's clause promised Validated on
   terms Gate 5 does not offer. Check a clause's promise against Gate 5's text before
   acting on it, and rewrite the clause when it fails.
3. **A headline pair can be a time trend rather than a contrast.** UQ-49's kill read
   27.5% → 31.1% as before-and-after filtering; it is two months apart, both after.
   When a before/after reading is load-bearing, confirm the paper reports a *before*.
4. **A pipeline is not a filter.** The FineWeb v1.4.0 arm of the one on-point paper
   contains no model-based classifier at all. "Measured for two of the three filters"
   became "measured for two whole pipelines, neither isolating a named filter".
5. **The index entry count is usually the line count, not a fabrication.**
   `ledger/INDEX.md` carries nine header lines, so entries + 9 = lines, and the
   figures recorded as invented in earlier batches (134 against 125, 139 against 130)
   are each exactly `wc -l`. This batch: UQ-46's record said "144 lines", which is
   exact; UQ-47 said 145 entries and UQ-49 said 141, which match neither and are
   corrected inline. Earlier batch records described this honestly and are not wrong;
   the project memory that called it fabrication was, and has been corrected.
