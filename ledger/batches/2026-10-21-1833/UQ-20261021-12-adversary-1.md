## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

A pairwise (Bradley-Terry) reward model is trained only on reward differences between responses to the same prompt, so the training loss cannot tell r(x,y) from r(x,y)+f(x) for any function f of the prompt. The candidate asks three things: (i) how much of the variance in a released BT reward model's scalar output is that per-prompt term (a between-prompt / within-prompt variance split, with CIs, for >= 3 RMs on >= 2 corpora); (ii) whether that between-prompt term is the same across re-fits with different seeds (a learned difficulty signal) or changes with the seed (noise the loss never pinned); (iii) for each named practice that compares reward numbers ACROSS prompts — absolute cutoffs for data filtering, cross-prompt best-of-n budget allocation or routing, report cards that average reward over prompts, RL advantages not centred per prompt — how often the practice's own stated decision rule would decide differently if the reward were first centred within prompt.

The candidate's own handoff note says that if Disguise against Prior 1 and Triviality against limb (i) both bite, the leg it stands on is limb (iii) on an evidenced practice: the flip rate of AdaBoN's greedy allocation rule under per-prompt centring. I attacked that leg directly.

## Own arithmetic (labelled): shift-invariance of the two evidenced P2 decision rules

Let the candidate's offset be r'(x_i, y) = r(x_i, y) + c_i for each prompt i. (1) AdaBoN: per the record (and consistent with Source B's summariser read), the allocation rule is the argmax over prompts of the marginal gain V_{i,a_i+1} − V_{i,a_i}, where V_{i,a} is the (KDE-estimated) expected maximum of a samples of r(x_i, ·). Under the shift, every sample for prompt i moves by c_i, the KDE (Scott's-rule bandwidth depends on the sample sd, not its mean) moves rigidly by c_i, so V'_{i,a} = V_{i,a} + c_i for every a, and the marginal gain V'_{i,a+1} − V'_{i,a} = V_{i,a+1} − V_{i,a} is unchanged for every i and a. The greedy argmax is therefore identical at every step; the within-prompt final argmax is identical; and the batch win rate (which compares two allocations' sums of per-prompt maxima over the SAME K prompts) is identical because Σ c_i cancels. Hence the limb (iii) flip rate for AdaBoN's stated rule under within-prompt centring is exactly 0, up to numerical quadrature, with no CI needed. This is derived from the rule as the record itself states it; if AdaBoN anywhere compares LEVELS V_{i,a} across prompts rather than within-prompt differences, this would not hold, and neither the record nor my fetch found such a comparison. (2) Landesberg Appendix C: per the record, routing is "margin-based under a fixed budget". If "margin" is a within-prompt difference between candidate scores (the ordinary meaning in best-of-n routing), it is invariant to c_i by the same algebra and the flip rate is again 0. I did not read Appendix C; this leg rests on the record's own description and is marked as such.
