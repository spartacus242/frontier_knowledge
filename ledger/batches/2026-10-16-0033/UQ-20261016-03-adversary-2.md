## 1. Restatement (my own words, before reading the generator's framing again)

Take the EU's *Xylella fastidiosa* programme in Apulia. The rule is: a plant tests positive, so that plant is destroyed, and so is every host plant within 50 m of it, tested or not. The question asks about the first group only. Of the plants destroyed **because they themselves tested positive**, what share were carrying an infection that, had the plant been left standing, would never have done anything within ten years — never had a spittlebug successfully pick the bacterium up off it (endpoint E1), or never lost the stated amount of yield or died (endpoint E2)? Call that share the overdiagnosis fraction, borrowing the term from the share of screen-detected cancers that would never have surfaced in the patient's life. Then the second half: if you write that share into an explicit loss function that trades destroyed plants against prevented onward infections, what destruction radius falls out of it? And a separate, explicitly non-overdiagnosis quantity: of the untested neighbours felled by the radius rule, what share were uninfected or would likewise never have mattered.

What is genuinely being claimed as new is narrow: not "is felling healthy trees bad" (that is the public argument and is category ii), not "is the test wrong" (category i), but a **counterfactual-progression fraction computed on true positives, with a stated endpoint and horizon, and used to set a legal radius**.
