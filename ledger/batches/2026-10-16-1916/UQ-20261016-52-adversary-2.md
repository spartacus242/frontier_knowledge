## Restatement in my own words

Everyone who studies what post-training does to a language model measures it as "chat model minus its base model". The candidate asks whether the shipped "base model" is actually an untreated artifact at all. Concretely: (i) take released base checkpoints and their chat siblings, and measure how much of the chat model's characteristic behaviour — declining harmful requests, going along with a chat template, doing what a zero-shot instruction says, talking about itself as an assistant — the *base* checkpoint already does, as a share of the base-to-chat gap, and see whether that share grows with release date and with how much instruction-like data the releaser admits to putting in late pretraining; (ii) where the pretraining corpus is public, measure what fraction of its tokens are instruction/chat-shaped and see whether that predicts (i); (iii) take two published "post-training adds X" numbers and recompute them with a cleaner control (a pre-mid-training checkpoint, or a pre-2023 base) to see how far they move relative to their own error bars.

In short: *is the control arm already partly treated, and are the field's published post-training effect sizes therefore shrunken?*

### Findings from the motivating source (F3, read)

- **M1.** Rates confirmed as the record states: bases ~48% refusal on harmful requests vs ~90% for chat siblings; ~3% on harmless (Alpaca) prompts for both. Steering a base with the refusal direction pushes it above 88%.
- **M2.** **Refusal detection is a prefix string match** — "checking whether completions begin with common refusal phrases" — on 100 JailbreakBench prompts under greedy decoding, with the authors' own caveat that "this may miss some refusals", plus some manual checking. No precision/recall reported for base-model text.
- **M3.** The post discusses "incompetent refusals" (the model claims not to understand) and a commenter (Arthur Conmy) notes that refusal-like phrases such as "I don't know how to" are common online. LLaMA-1 completions "often seem a bit dumb" — flagged in the post as a possible confound.
- **M4.** **The post's own limitations section already states the candidate's core worry**: modern pretraining is opaque, which "could blur the line between 'base' and 'chat' models." The record attributes this only to commenters; it is in the post.
