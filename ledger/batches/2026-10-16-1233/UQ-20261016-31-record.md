Question (canonical, Gate 0 as intaken):
Is the unintended electromagnetic radiation (UEMR) of an individual low-Earth-orbit satellite a fixed property of its hardware model, or does it vary over that satellite's operational life — so that a satellite detected emitting in one campaign can be genuinely below detection in a later one, and aggregate sky-noise forecasts built from as-launched snapshots are biased?

Question (canonical, Gate 1 reformulation — the version to carry forward; permitted single reformulation, for well-posedness only):
For a cohort of individually identified LEO satellites (NORAD IDs) of one hardware model, observed with one instrument in fixed frequency bands and a fixed beam/elevation configuration, with multiple passes per satellite per epoch and >=2 epochs >=6 months apart: (a) how does the variance of per-satellite band-limited radiated power (normalised to a fixed range, e.g. dBW or Jy at 1000 km) partition into pass-within-epoch, epoch-within-satellite, between-satellite-within-model and between-model components; (b) with model held fixed, does the epoch-level per-satellite emission change with on-orbit age; and (c) what fraction of previously-detected satellites still in orbit fall below a stated, sensitivity-matched per-satellite upper limit at the later epoch?
(Both versions recorded; the reformulation adds the pass-within-epoch replicate level, the normalisation, and the explicit upper-limit requirement. No change of subject.)

Alternative formulations:
1. Does a satellite's radio-noise leakage drift as it ages on orbit (thermal cycling, degradation, payload operating state, software updates), or is UEMR set per hardware generation at launch?
2. Are UEMR non-detections of previously-detected, still-in-orbit satellites explained by emission variability and duty cycle rather than by differences in survey sensitivity?

Motivating gap:
Anchor anomaly from arXiv:2506.02831 (Grigg et al. 2025, 'The Growing Impact of Unintended Starlink Broadband Emission on Radio Astronomy in the SKA-Low Frequency Range'), fetched by intake this run (quotes are the intake's, re-confirmed as an existing paper in this run's query 6 results but not re-opened by me): 'None of these 68 satellites were detected in our work even though 55 of them were still in orbit during this survey'; 'Currently, the specific component of the satellite that causes this unintended emission is unknown'; the paper offers its numbers as 'a reference to current levels of unintended emissions' for later population-level comparison and runs no per-satellite time series. A second, independent instance of the same anomaly was opened this run: Yang et al. 2026 (arXiv:2603.07631, 21CMA) targeted Starlink-3618 / NORAD 51998 *because* Di Vruno et al. 2023 had detected it, and reports 'no broadband UEMR is found within the frequency range of 110-188 MHz', attributing the null to sensitivity without testing the alternative. So the anomaly is now reported twice and examined zero times. The existing literature's design is snapshot-by-generation (Di Vruno et al. 2023; Bassa et al. 2024, factor-32 v2-Mini result verified by opening the paper this run), and the only within-satellite variability study found (Dong et al. 2026) explicitly declines the variance decomposition.

Presuppositions:
- [established] Individual emitters are identifiable across epochs; surveys attribute detections to NORAD IDs and cross-match. Confirmed by opened sources: Bassa et al. 2024 publishes per-NORAD-ID flux densities (Tables B.1-B.3); Dong et al. 2026 call the Grigg catalogue 'the first statistically large sample in which individual satellites can be tracked across orbits'.
- [established, per the anchor paper's own statement] 55 of 68 previously-detected satellites were in orbit during the later survey, so re-entry does not by itself explain non-detection.
- [contested] Per-epoch sensitivity can be made commensurable so a non-detection becomes a usable upper limit. Supported as a real obstacle: Yang et al. 2026 publish only one generic threshold (146.85 Jy at 150 MHz) rather than per-satellite limits; Bassa et al. 2024 leave two of 29 LBA nulls unnamed. This is why the question demands matched re-observation, not archive re-analysis.
- [contested] Hardware does not change on orbit, so within-satellite change is operating state, degradation or software. Sub-model revisions inside a launch group would break the model-level control; Dong et al. 2026 handle a related confound only by matching launch dates.
- [established] Aggregate UEMR forecasts feed real decisions (IAU CPS / SKAO, ITU-R WP 7D; WRC-27 agenda items 1.16/1.18 surfaced live in query 4). Query 9 additionally surfaced arXiv:2608.11659, 'Towards Genuine Coexistence: Per-Satellite Emission and Radiation Limits...' (not opened) — per-satellite limits are being proposed, which presupposes a per-satellite quantity stable enough to regulate and verify.
- [contested] Operators are not needed to pose or answer it; telemetry would be needed only to attribute a cause, not to measure variance. Partly strained: Dong et al. 2026 state the UEMR 'amplitude can depend on the operational state of the satellite, which is not directly observable from the ground', so an unattributed variance estimate is the ceiling without operator data.
- [flagged] The quantity of interest is per-satellite radiated power in a defined band, not the astronomer-facing aggregate; the two diverge if population composition changes at the same time (Mallama 2026 shows exactly this confound in the optical case).

What would count as an answer:
A matched-sensitivity repeated-epoch campaign: one instrument, fixed bands, fixed elevation/beam configuration, a cohort of individually identified satellites, >=2 epochs months-to-years apart, with multiple passes per satellite per epoch, publishing per-satellite flux densities and stated upper limits for every non-detection. Deliverables: (a) a variance decomposition into pass-within-epoch, epoch-within-satellite, between-satellite-within-model and between-model terms; (b) a test of emission against on-orbit age with model held fixed; (c) the fraction of previously-detected satellites bounded below a stated limit. Either outcome settles it: if epoch-within-satellite variance is large relative to between-model variance, snapshot-derived aggregate forecasts and any mitigation commitment verified against them are invalid as constructed and monitoring must become duty-cycle-aware; if per-unit emission is stable, the reported non-detections are a sensitivity or pointing artefact and the existing forecasting approach stands. A null (epoch-within-satellite variance indistinguishable from zero at stated power) is publishable.

Significance — consequence:
If within-satellite (epoch-level) variance is comparable to or larger than between-model variance: every aggregate UEMR forecast built from as-launched snapshots is estimating the wrong quantity, because it treats a per-unit draw from a state-dependent distribution as a fixed model constant; the factor-32-style generational contrasts become partly confounded with operating-state sampling; monitoring must become duty-cycle-aware (characterising the distribution and its sampling, not a level); and any mitigation commitment verified against a snapshot is unverifiable in principle, which bears directly on the per-satellite limits now being proposed for 1-14 GHz (arXiv:2608.11659, unopened), since a limit on a time-varying quantity must be written as a percentile over operational states rather than a single number. It also redirects the field's acknowledged open problem — 'the specific component ... is unknown' — because a fleet-wide step in emission would implicate software and a per-unit monotone trend would implicate degradation, whereas a flat result implicates fixed hardware layout.
If per-unit emission is stable: the anchor's 68 non-detections are a sensitivity, pointing or beam-geometry artefact, the snapshot design is vindicated, generational comparisons stand as estimates of hardware properties, and per-satellite regulatory limits become the defensible instrument because the regulated quantity is a stable attribute of a unit.

Significance — audience:
IAU CPS and SKAO spectrum-management staff (who publish the aggregate forecasts and negotiate mitigations, and would have to restate commitments as distributional if the answer is yes); ITU-R WP 7D study groups under live WRC-27 agenda items on RAS protection (surfaced in query 4), which codify protection criteria and would need a statistical per-satellite emission specification; observatory RFI groups with the instruments that would run the campaign (MWA/EDA2, LOFAR/ASTRON, DRAO's wideband monitor) — for them the answer decides whether a single impact-assessment campaign is a sufficient deliverable or whether a standing per-emitter monitoring cadence must be funded; and constellation EMC engineers, for whom a per-unit age trend or a fleet-wide step would be an actionable fault-localisation signal.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: what is the duty-cycle distribution of the emitting subsystem, and can UEMR be used as a remote proxy for payload loading or service state; does a software update appear as a synchronised fleet-wide step in emission (the sharpest available handle on the unknown-component problem, obtainable without operator cooperation); does emission trend with thermal-cycle count rather than calendar age; how should protection criteria be written for a time-varying emitter; and, transplanted back, does emitter-signature drift in the RF-fingerprinting sense have a physical, measurable analogue in flown hardware.

Significance — anchoring:
Directly on the anchor anomaly (68 previously-detected, 55 in orbit, none redetected) and its independent recurrence in Yang et al. 2026 for Starlink-3618; on the stated open problem of the unidentified emitting component; on Dong et al. 2026's unresolved illumination dependence and their explicit statement that variance contributions are not separable in their sample; and on Mallama 2026's unexplained optical secular trends, whose root cause is also unknown and whose ageing test was inconclusive for the same level-of-analysis reason.

Reason for silence:
A disciplinary boundary plus an instrument-economics mismatch, not oversight. The data are a by-product of station commissioning and RFI-characterisation observing, funded as one-off impact assessments whose deliverable is the astronomer-facing aggregate level feeding ITU-R and IAU CPS; the anchor paper says as much in offering itself as 'a reference to current levels'. The per-unit-over-time question belongs methodologically to EMC and measurement-system metrology and to emitter-signature-drift work (queries 5 and 8 show both are mature in their own words), whose practitioners have neither the fleet nor a 100-m-class low-frequency array. Two further mechanisms: the constellation is replaced faster than a longitudinal baseline accrues (v1.0 -> v1.5 -> v2-Mini -> DTC inside roughly three years), so generation-level comparison is the design that pays off on a grant timescale and a brighter new generation is a more publishable finding than a null drift in old units; and the enabling data regime is one year old — Dong et al. 2026 call the Grigg et al. 2025 catalogue 'the first statistically large sample in which individual satellites can be tracked across orbits', so the longitudinal question only became askable in 2025 and its second epoch cannot have been observed yet by anyone who started from that catalogue.

Well-posedness note: the reformulation names unit, endpoint, design, estimands and both informative outcomes, and a null is publishable, so it clears the bar in substance. It is held at 2/3 because the intake's estimand ('within-satellite variance across epochs') is not identified by the intake's design ('two or more epochs') — if a satellite is caught in few passes per epoch, epoch-to-epoch difference is indistinguishable from pass-to-pass duty-cycle variance, and Dong et al. 2026 give direct evidence that pass-level variability is large (bootstrap CI [0.441, 1.598] on a within-satellite ratio). The Gate 1 reformulation inserts the pass-within-epoch replicate level, which is what identification requires; it would score 3/3 once a minimum passes-per-satellite-per-epoch and an explicit per-pass detection-efficiency/beam-geometry model are specified, since 'matched sensitivity' across epochs is otherwise not a defined condition. A second, smaller deduction: 'operational life' bundles age, operating state and software version, so the design measures variance it cannot attribute — conceded by the intake, and reinforced by Dong et al.'s observation that operational state 'is not directly observable from the ground'.
