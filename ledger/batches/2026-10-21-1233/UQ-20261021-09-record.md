## Question (canonical)

**V1 (as handed over at intake; recorded, not scored):** Now that a continent-scale, free, regularly updated interferometric ground-motion service (Copernicus EGMS) yields per-structure displacement-rate time series, do the angular-distortion thresholds that geotechnical practice uses to predict settlement damage — the Skempton–MacDonald, Burland–Wroth and Boscardin–Cording family — discriminate damaged from undamaged buildings at population scale, that is, what are the positive predictive value, sensitivity, specificity and ROC of EGMS-derived angular distortion against independently recorded structural damage, with each threshold re-derived at its own author's stated definition?

**V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted, for well-posedness only; scored as canonical):** On a building frame declared before any data pull in one EGMS-covered region, with the damage cut fixed in advance at Burland D≥2 (D≥3 as a pre-specified sensitivity arm) and the predictor declared as *EGMS-derived within-footprint differential vertical velocity over the service observation window* rather than as cumulative angular distortion since construction: (a) what are the sensitivity, specificity and ROC/AUC with intervals, and — on a probabilistically drawn, independently verified sub-sample that fixes damage prevalence in the frame — the positive predictive value, of exceeding each criterion's own limiting value, evaluated only for those criteria whose measurand the service actually supplies (Skempton–MacDonald angular distortion δ/L; Burland–Wroth deflection ratio Δ/L under a declared inflection-point rule), with Boscardin–Cording reported as **not evaluable** because its second input, horizontal tensile strain, is not supplied by a vertical-velocity decomposition; and (b) how much of any discrimination shortfall is attributable to the rate-for-cumulative-distortion substitution, estimated on the subset of the frame that also carries bed-joint levelling?

**What V1 → V2 fixed.** V1 is not well-posed as written, for four reasons, three of which its own presupposition list flags but its canonical sentence then ignores. (i) No damage cut: PPV, sensitivity and specificity are undefined until "damaged" is a declared class, and the choice is consequential — the nearest prior pools Burland D1+ as positive, while the criteria literature treats D3 as the practically meaningful level. (ii) The named "family" is not measurand-homogeneous: Burland–Wroth's criterion is on deflection ratio and Boscardin–Cording's is on distortion *jointly with* horizontal strain, so "re-derived at its own author's stated definition" is unsatisfiable for Boscardin–Cording from a vertical-velocity product — that is a presupposition failure on the candidate's own terms, not merely a contested flag. (iii) PPV is not identified from an opportunistic join: it needs a declared prevalence, hence a verification design, which V1 does not provide. (iv) V1 asks for "ROC of EGMS-derived angular distortion" while its presuppositions concede the service supplies a rate, not a cumulative distortion; V2 makes the predictor a rate by name so a null result is interpretable.

Alternative formulations (from intake, retained): Do the settlement-damage criteria embedded in geotechnical practice work when applied to every building in a region rather than to the few dozen case histories they were fitted on? / Over a declared building population, how well does satellite-measured differential settlement rate separate buildings with recorded damage from those without, at the thresholds practice actually uses?

## Motivating gap (as intaken, and as it stands after Gate 2)

As intaken: the criteria in daily use were fitted to small case-history sets in the 1950s–1980s because measuring differential settlement across a whole building population was impossible; a continent-scale persistent-scatterer service now makes the criterion evaluable as a screening test rather than usable only as an assumption; "the existing InSAR-and-damage literature largely applies the thresholds to draw risk maps, which presupposes the discrimination that this question measures."

**After Gate 2 this last clause is substantially false, and it is the clause the candidate rested on.** The discrimination has been measured and published in the candidate's own field: Prosperi et al. (2023) fit ROC curves for differential settlement, rotation, *relative rotation (angular distortion)* and deflection ratio against surveyed damage over 386 Dutch masonry buildings, report an optimal cut-off per parameter, and compare the result directly to Boscardin–Cording's 3.3‰. Separately, the satellite-instrument-to-surveyed-damage join at building level already exists on 706 buildings (Peduto et al. 2019), and the population-scale application already exists at 269,194 objects nationwide (Drougkas et al. 2020). What remains genuinely unasked is narrower than the question posed: the PPV/prevalence limb, and the same ROC under a *rate* predictor from the free service rather than under levelling.

## Presuppositions (intake list, re-graded against what was opened)

1. EGMS or an equivalent national service supplies persistent-scatterer displacement rates densely enough to estimate a within-footprint differential for a usable share of buildings. **CONTESTED, and now partly informed:** Drougkas et al. 2020 had to interpolate 10 m velocity grids by inverse-distance weighting "to fill gaps where persistent scatterers were absent" before fitting a per-building tilt plane — i.e. at country scale the achieved frame was not the scatterer frame, and a fitted plane on interpolated values is a different predictor from one on real scatterers. The achieved frame remains instrument-defined, not a random sample of the stock.
2. The service's measurand (recent line-of-sight or decomposed vertical velocity at roof-level scatterers over the observation window) maps onto the criteria's measurand (cumulative angular distortion at foundation level since construction). **STRONGLY CONTESTED; the entry's central weak point, and now externally corroborated:** Prosperi et al. 2023 state (Sec. 6, trimmed fragment) "spaceborne-derived are still limited to a fixed timeframe" — the sentence continues, per the summariser, with "leading to lower SRI values for a surveyed building". A source in the field names the direction of the bias the candidate worried about. V2 therefore carries the mapping as a pre-declared sensitivity arm and declares the predictor as a rate.
3. An independent, building-level damage record exists and is joinable. **CONTESTED:** the three priors all used bespoke in-situ façade/levelling campaigns, not an administrative register; none demonstrated a joinable municipal or insurance record at population scale.
4. Damage has causes other than differential settlement, so the test measures discrimination, not attribution. **Established and uncontroversial; both V1 and V2 keep it and avoid causal language.**
5. Each threshold has a single stated definition in its own source. **CONTESTED and now partly refuted for the purpose at hand:** Boscardin–Cording's criterion requires horizontal strain, which the instrument does not supply, so the leg cannot be re-derived at its author's definition at all. Peduto et al. 2019 did compute Burland–Wroth's deflection ratio at the original inflection-point definition (D2/L2) alongside a simplified D1/L1, and concluded the simplified form suffices — evidence that the "re-derive at the author's own definition" limb has already been exercised.
6. The building, not the structural block or the party-wall-joined terrace, is the right unit. **CONTESTED and unresolved by V2.** All three priors are dominated by pre-1950 terraced Dutch/Belgian stock; Peduto et al. 2017 analysed the same area at both units (310 single buildings vs 67 row-house aggregates) and the two units returned different damage ranges (D0–D3 at building level, D0–D2 at aggregate level) without an explanation in the paper. This is a live measurement problem, not a bookkeeping choice.
7. Damage record and ground-motion record cover a common period. **Flagged; V2's rate predictor makes the window explicit.**

## What would count as an answer (V2)

Per-criterion sensitivity, specificity and ROC/AUC with intervals, plus PPV computed on a verification sub-sample that fixes prevalence, on a frame and a measurand mapping both declared before any data pull; the rate-for-cumulative substitution reported as a pre-specified sensitivity analysis against the levelling subset; Boscardin–Cording recorded as not evaluable rather than evaluated on a surrogate. A demonstrated inability to estimate it — scatterer density or the damage record failing — is an admissible answer and is to be reported as such, not as a weak effect. **Note after Gate 2:** for the levelling instrument this answer substantially exists; for the EGMS rate predictor and for PPV it does not.

## Novelty — how the candidate differs from each

- **vs Prior 1 (Prosperi et al. 2023):** the candidate differs on the *instrument* (EGMS roof-level velocity rate over the service window vs bed-joint levelling of cumulative settlement), on *one metric* (PPV, which Prior 1 cannot supply because its sample is non-random, assembled "for post-disaster analyses," with prevalence not even stated), and on *treating the published limiting values themselves as classifiers* (Prior 1 reports its own optimal cut-offs and converts Boscardin–Cording's 3.3‰ into a fragility probability instead). It does **not** differ on the central estimand: ROC discrimination of damaged vs undamaged masonry buildings by angular distortion, in this literature, on a declared Dutch building population, is published. **Scope-transfer check performed in both directions, as required:** Prior 1's regime is 386 non-random pre-1950 Dutch terraced masonry buildings, unit = building, predictor = levelling-derived cumulative SRI, positive class = D1+, metric = ROC/AUC plus an optimal cut-off, no prevalence. The candidate sits *outside* that regime on instrument, on prevalence and on the threshold-as-classifier limb — and *inside* it on the question the canonical sentence actually asks first, namely whether angular distortion discriminates at all.
- **vs Prior 2 (Peduto et al. 2019):** the candidate asks for discrimination statistics where Prior 2 reports fragility parameters, and for a free continent-scale service where Prior 2 used bespoke PSI over four municipalities. But Prior 2 already performs the satellite-to-building-damage join at the candidate's unit of analysis, at n = 706, with the Skempton–MacDonald measurand and with Burland–Wroth's deflection ratio at its *own* inflection-point definition — so the "re-derive each threshold at its author's stated definition from satellite data" move is not new either. A fragility curve plus the frame's own predictor distribution is, in principle, the same information an ROC consumes; nobody reported it that way, which is the residue.
- **vs Prior 3 (Drougkas et al. 2020):** the candidate asks for validation where Prior 3 performs calculation — Prior 3 *is* the risk-map genre the motivating gap names, and it presupposes exactly the discrimination at issue. But it is a 269,194-object country-scale instance of the population-scale application, so the candidate's "now made possible by a new instrument" framing is weaker than intaken: the population-scale computation was already done in 2020 on pre-EGMS processing, and its only validation signal — that its tilt model is "unconservative compared to the average findings" — points at the criteria's performance without measuring it.

## Significance — consequence

Argued, and discounted by what Gate 2 found. If the thresholds discriminate near chance on EGMS rates, code committees learn that a criterion in daily use has no population-scale validity *under that instrument* — but after Prior 1 this would be read as an instrument result, not a criterion result, because the criteria already have a published ROC under levelling with optimal cut-offs (β 4.77 / 3.67 mm/m) of the same order as Boscardin–Cording's 3.3 mm/m. If they discriminate well, population screening is licensed and municipalities and insurers would act. The direction of the answer is therefore partly pre-empted: the consequential unknown has shifted from "do the criteria work" to "does the free service's rate predictor carry the signal the criteria need," which is a narrower claim about an instrument.

## Significance — fecundity

Genuine and mostly in the instrument direction: a measured rate-vs-cumulative transfer function would make every published InSAR risk map interpretable as a screening instrument with a known error rate; it would open the question of what a criterion calibrated *on* population data looks like (as against calibrated on case histories and then applied to populations); and it would force the unit-of-analysis question for party-wall-joined terraces, which Prior 2's companion paper raises implicitly by returning different damage ranges at building and aggregate scale without explaining the difference.

## Significance — anchoring

Touches the scatterer-frame representativeness problem (Prior 3's IDW gap-filling), the Dutch foundation/subsidence-damage liability controversy surfaced at query 10, and the standing mismatch between deterministic serviceability criteria and reliability-based code calibration surfaced at query 9. ## Reason for silence

The honest answer is that it was **not** silent, which is itself the finding, and a reason-for-silence mechanism that fails is a signal to distrust the novelty claim — here it was distrusted and the claim fell. The mechanism that *does* hold is narrower and explains only the residue: the discrimination-metric vocabulary (ROC, POD, PPV) is owned by screening-test and SHM/NDE methodology, while the data are owned by geotechnics, so the metrics arrived in this literature late and incompletely — Prior 1 reaches for ROC but reports no AUC numbers in text, no sensitivity/specificity/PPV values at its optimum, and no prevalence, because its sample was assembled for post-disaster analysis and "may not represent a random sample." PPV in particular is unobtainable without a verification design that nobody has had reason to fund. A secondary mechanism is instrument timing: EGMS became a free, regularly updated continent-scale product only from 2022, after Priors 2 and 3 were processed, so the specific rate predictor V2 names has had little exposure. Neither mechanism supports the stronger claim V1 made.

## Strongest remaining objection (to the residual limb, if anyone re-posed it)

Even V2's residual is confounded by design: with an observation-window rate as predictor and a non-random, instrument-defined frame joined to a reporting-biased damage record, a low AUC is unattributable among (i) the criteria failing, (ii) the rate-for-cumulative substitution, (iii) scatterer-frame selection and (iv) damage under-reporting — and the sensitivity arm that is supposed to separate (i) from (ii) requires a levelling subset of the same buildings, which is precisely the data Prior 1 already has and Prior 1's own authors call non-random and too small to generalise from.
