Question (canonical, Gate 1 reformulation — the one permitted, for well-posedness only):
In published LLM and agent evaluations that release per-item logs carrying error fields, on a frame declared before any data pull: (a) what share of the nominal denominator carries a harness-side failure, by failure class, under a pre-declared taxonomy validated against a hand-coded sample with the unclassifiable share reported; (b) holding the estimand fixed, how far apart are the two ESTIMATORS of the same quantity — drop the affected item (per-protocol) versus score it as a failure (intention-to-treat) — in reported score, in rank correlation, and in counted pairwise rank reversals; (c) separately, and labelled as a DIFFERENT estimand rather than a third estimator, what score does retry-to-completion produce on the same items, and how far is it from each of the two; (d) does harness-side failure probability correlate with model identity, which is what makes the drop estimator biased rather than merely lossy; and (e) what fraction of published evaluations state their convention at all?

Question (Gate 0 original, recorded as required):
In published LLM and agent evaluations with released per-item logs, what share of the nominal denominator is affected by harness-side failures rather than by the model's answer, and how much do reported scores and model rankings move across the three denominator conventions — drop the item, score it as a failure, or retry to completion?

Reason for the reformulation: the Gate 0 version asks for rank correlation "across the three conventions" as if all three estimated one quantity. The intake's own motivating text concedes that retry-to-completion "changes the estimand", so arm 3 is not an alternative estimator of the same target; a rank correlation computed across all three mixes an estimator contrast with an estimand contrast. The reformulation separates them and adds the explicit pre-declaration and validation steps the intake had only in its "what would count as an answer" field. No substantive content is added or removed.

Alternative formulations:
1. What is an evaluation's intention-to-treat score as opposed to its per-protocol score, and do the two rank models differently?
2. How often does a reported benchmark number depend on how the harness handled its own errors?

Significance — consequence:
If convention choice moves rankings beyond resampling noise, then a published leaderboard ordering is partly a measurement of harness error policy, and the specific affected comparisons become unciteable as capability claims until recomputed; operators would have to publish the failure share alongside the score and name the convention. If no reversal survives, the two estimators are interchangeable at current failure rates and the field can keep reporting one number — a null worth having, and the intake is right that even a fraction-of-a-percent answer records something. The deflation, which is real: B has already shipped the actionable form of the "yes" branch (distrust sub-3 pp gaps, match configurations before comparing) without the three-way analysis, so the incremental decision the candidate would change is narrower than the intake claims — it would sharpen a published rule of thumb into a per-comparison reversal list rather than create a new practice.
