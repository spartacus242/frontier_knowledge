Question (canonical): Across genetically identical, identically reared individuals, is the absolute cell number of an organ regulated as a total — with compensatory negative covariance between its constituent regions or cell types — or are the parts regulated independently, so that the variance of the total is the sum of the parts' variances?
(Gate 1 reformulation, the version graded; one reformulation used: in a cohort of n isogenic, identically reared individuals, with a nested technical-replicate layer and a pre-declared partition, does the across-individual covariance matrix of absolute regional cell counts carry negative off-diagonal structure large enough to put Var(total) significantly below SUM Var(parts) once the technical component is subtracted, and does the sign replicate in a second line and under both an atlas and a registration-free marker partition? The reformulation drops the intake's "regulated as a total" mechanism claim, which the intake itself conceded is not identifiable from covariance.)

Alternative formulations:
- When one brain region of an inbred mouse happens to have fewer cells than average, do other regions have more?
- Does the across-individual covariance matrix of whole-organ cell counts carry the negative structure a conserved total implies, and is CV(total) smaller than the CV of any part?

Motivating gap: Intaken as a new-instrument question — classical stereology sampled one or a few regions per animal, so cross-region covariance within individuals was said to be newly measurable. The premise is false and was refuted at Gate 2: the isotropic fractionator counts every dissected structure of one brain, and the covariance was estimated and published in 2015; adversary 1 pushed it back further, finding within-isogenic-strain covariance with a repeat-count technical layer published in 1996.

Three pre-declared outcomes: below the sum = conserved total; indistinguishable = independent parts; positive residual covariance = shared growth factor. Criteria are crisp and pre-declarable — this candidate is NOT rejected for ill-posedness. (Adversary 2: the auxiliary CV(total) < CV(each part) criterion holds automatically under independence and should be struck.)
