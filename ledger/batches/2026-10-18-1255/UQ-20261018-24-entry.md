Question (canonical):
(V2, the Gate 1 reformulation, scored and attacked.) In a record-linkage cohort with individual per-examination machine-reported dose (DICOM RDSR), facility/scanner/protocol identifiers, recorded indication and cancer-registry follow-up — instrumenting a patient's organ dose with the leave-one-out mean dose index for the same examination class at the same facility-scanner-protocol in the same calendar quarter, conditioning on a pre-declared set (indication, age, sex, body region, examination count, quarter, presentation-site stratum) after a declared lag — (a) what is the first stage and its conditional F/partial R²; (b) what is the IV estimate of ERR per unit organ dose; (c) what is the SIGNED DIFFERENCE between (b) and the conventional adjusted estimate on the SAME person-years, against a pre-declared equivalence margin; (d) does the exclusion-restriction battery pass (balance across Z deciles, ≥2 negative-control outcomes, MRI/ultrasound sham instrument)? An interval too wide to separate concordance from discordance is pre-declared an answer.
(V1, as intaken: does an IV estimate using facility-and-scanner protocol dose agree with the conventional observational CT dose–cancer estimate in the same cohort, and by how much does it differ? Not well-posed at 3/3 — "protocol dose", "agree with", cohort and window undefined.)

Alternative formulations:
- Is the CT dose–cancer association confirmed when the only dose contrast used is the one created by WHERE the patient was scanned rather than by how ill they were?
- (Method-owner's words) Does a provider-preference instrument reproduce the conventional dose–response for a continuous iatrogenic exposure, and what is the gap?

Motivating gap:
Per-examination delivered dose with facility/scanner/protocol provenance only became population-scale and individually linkable with RDSR and dose registries (~2011+); before that it was imputed. The paediatric-CT literature (EPI-CT and national cohorts) is contested on essentially one point — confounding by indication / reverse causation — and defends itself with exclusions, lags and mixture models rather than a different source of variation. Same-examination dose varies several-fold between facilities for non-patient reasons. Conventional side has a published number: ERR 0.056/mSv (95% CrI 0.047–0.065), ~11M young Australians (Mathews, ViCBiostat seminar flyer 2015 — grey source, opened).

Presuppositions:
1. RDSR indices convert to organ dose with a declarable error model, largely SHARED within scanner/protocol — conversion machinery established; propagation of shared error into an IV first stage is not.
2. Instrument is strong in-cohort — plausible (documented between-facility/international dose variation), not shown.
3. CONTESTED: exclusion restriction — high-dose facilities may differ in case mix, referral, deprivation, imaging frequency, ascertainment. Falsifiable only.
4. CONTESTED: monotonicity/homogeneity; compliers policy-relevant. Limb (c) partly an ESTIMAND difference, not purely a confounding measure.
5. CONTESTED by registry vintage: latency-limited to leukaemia/brain tumours for a 2011+ cohort.
6. UNVERIFIED: registry linkage permitted; loss to follow-up not differential by facility.
7. LOAD-BEARING, flagged unverified at Gate 2, NOW TESTED AND TRUE (both adversaries, IARC EPI-CT dosimetry 2021 opened): the comparator literature's dose contrast is already facility/machine/period grouped — "PACS dataset was not used at the individual patient level"; individual PACS–RIS linkage for ~30,000 of ~1.43M examinations; "Only 23% of the participating hospitals provided information on machine settings"; shared subgroup parameters "sampled once"; CTDIvol unavailable for "the vast majority of examinations"; RDSR not mentioned.

What would count as an answer:
A pre-registered IV analysis reporting the first-stage strength, the IV ERR per unit organ dose with CI, the conventional estimate on the same person-years, the signed difference with an interval against a pre-declared margin, and the full falsification battery, with dosimetric error propagated. (The record's further claim — that concordance would "largely retire the confounding-by-indication objection" to the paediatric-CT literature — is struck: both adversaries found it contradicted by the opened IARC dosimetry document.)
