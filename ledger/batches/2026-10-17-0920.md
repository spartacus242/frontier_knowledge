## Both reversals are the same failure: a kill stated about a paper nobody had opened

**UQ-24.** Rejected at Gate 2 as a disguised known, on the reading that Gabrielli et al. 2008 had asked and answered the candidate's generic question in the candidate's own tracer vocabulary, with only the contaminant source swapped. The paper is "Siderophile metal fallout to Greenland from the 1991 winter eruption of Hekla (Iceland) and during the global atmospheric perturbation of Pinatubo". It is about volcanic fallout. Its anthropogenic remark is hedged in both directions ("might be also of anthropogenic origin", "One possible anthropogenic source") and it finds the opposite for the second tracer: "the Ir atmospheric budget does not appear clearly perturbed by catalytic converters." It states no bias, no corrected flux and no influx value at all, never mentions spacecraft, rockets or reentry, and gives neither a separation method nor a misattribution rate. The quotation the record carried was accurate; the characterisation of what the paper was about was not, and that characterisation was the whole ground. The second leg — that two published fractions already cover the candidate's bias limb — fails on estimand: neither Murphy et al. 2023 nor Lasue et al. EGU24-14286 propagates a fraction into a bias on an influx constraint, and neither holds a pre-space-age baseline.

**UQ-25.** Rejected at Gate 4 attack 2, on the reading that twenty years of seismic hazard-map testing indicate the candidate's bias-versus-nonstationarity split is not separable. Four papers were opened for that claim — Tasan et al. 2014 (GJI), Stirling & Petersen 2006 (abstract only), Stein, Geller & Liu 2011 (SRL 82(5)) and Rey et al. 2018 — and none of them contains either a decomposition or any statement that one is impossible. The strongest sentence pointing that way, Tasan's "the discrepancies between observations and predictions can generally have more than one explanation", says attribution was *not achieved*, which is not the same claim. Verification also confirmed the nearest prior, Mak & Schorlemmer 2016, on five of seven attributes and found it **absent on the decisive one**: it nowhere separates issue-era estimator bias from post-issue change. So the rate statistic is genuinely prior art and the candidate's limb (a) is genuinely not.

Both kills read a real paper accurately and aimed it at a different thing. That is the single most common reversal on this ledger and it has now produced two in one batch.

## What to carry forward

1. **The Equalise phase works and should not be weakened.** On its first live run it put a second adversary on the two rejections that would otherwise have had one, and two of this batch's three rejections turned out to be wrong. The low-rate rerun on top of it is still the phase most likely to be starved of search budget; it is not the safety net.
2. **A novelty kill is a claim about a paper, and the paper has to be opened.** Both reversals here, and most reversals on this ledger, are a real paper quoted accurately and aimed at a different thing. The Gate 2 requirement to state the prior's unit, endpoint, comparator and estimand does not catch it, because a reviewer who has not opened the paper states those too — wrongly.
3. **"Not achieved" is not "not separable", and "no source says it is possible" is not "a source says it is impossible."** UQ-25 was rejected on that step. When a kill rests on a field's collective failure to do something, the kill needs a sentence saying it cannot be done, not an absence of sentences saying it can.
4. **Verification can find a closer prior than the batch did** (UQ-21's Ingleby talk) without moving the verdict. That is the pass working as intended: the entry is now citing the nearest thing that exists, and a successor will not rediscover it as a surprise.
5. **The fetch cap is still being overrun** — 21-dehaan at 7 of 6, and one cold late adversary at 7 of 6. Both are disclosed in their entries. The explicit discounting warning in the prompt reduces this but does not stop it; the cap needs to be enforced by the runner rather than requested of the reader.
