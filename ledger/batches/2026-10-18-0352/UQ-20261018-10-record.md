## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (canonical, v1 as intaken):
Under the nonlinear, high-crest-factor load mixes that now dominate residential circuits, what is the distribution of registration error across the installed population of static (electronic) electricity meters, and what share of billed energy is misregistered?

Question (canonical, v2 — the ONE Gate 1 reformulation, for well-posedness; this is the version graded):
For one declared national installed base of static residential electricity meters, taking a probability sample stratified by meter model, firmware revision and build year from a distribution utility's asset register, and applying a pre-declared library of residential load mixes synthesised from a published appliance current-waveform set (e.g. the MeterEMI waveform library) against a reference instrument whose immunity to those same stimuli is demonstrated rather than assumed: what is the population-weighted distribution of relative registration error per mix (mean with confidence interval, plus lower and upper tail quantiles), and, under a declared national load-composition model, what fraction of billed residential energy does that distribution imply is misregistered?

Both versions are recorded as required. v2 changes no subject matter; it fixes four things v1 left open: the sampling frame, the status of "realistic" (a pre-declared mix library, so the instrument of the study is declared and not asserted), the reference's own immunity, and the fact that the billed-energy limb is conditional on a load-composition model rather than directly estimable from the asset register.

Alternative formulations:
1. Do the waveforms used in meter type approval and periodic sample testing represent the loads meters actually see, and what does the installed base register under realistic household load mixes?
2. Is there any population-level estimate of static-meter over- and under-registration, as opposed to laboratory demonstrations that large errors can occur?

Motivating gap:
The field is dense with how-to-answer apparatus — type-approval and conformity conditions (IEC 62052/62053, EN 50470), utility periodic sample testing, verification procedures — and appears empty of the corresponding what-is-the-population-value question. The anomaly is on record: Leferink, Keyer and Melentjev reported static meters registering roughly -32% to +582% of true energy on load mixes built from common consumer electronics (IEEE EMC Magazine, 2016); the result was disputed, partly replicated, and followed by standards activity. The intake note flagged this candidate as NOT SEARCH-CHECKED (novelty Unverified) because a 6-query cap had been spent when it replaced a discarded candidate. That has now been executed: 11 searches and 5 fetches, below.

Presuppositions (status after Gate 1):

CONTESTED, but now better than asserted: the VSL/University of Twente deck I opened (Hartman & van den Brom, 2023) reports >70 meters investigated, 24 meters described as representing the Dutch installed base tested against a traceable Yokogawa WT500 on a 140-TMX ideal source, and an in-situ case where a dimming remote switch drove one household meter's reading from about 250 W to negative values. ESTABLISHED in outline; MeterEMI's own appliance list (inverters, SMPS, LED/CFL, induction hobs, speed-controlled pumps) is the field's own statement of the change, but it must be quantified for the sampled population.

## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

What would count as an answer:
An error distribution measured on a probability sample of in-service meters, drawn by model, firmware and build-year strata from a real asset register, against a reference with demonstrated immunity, under a pre-declared mix library — reported as a population-weighted mean relative error with confidence intervals and tail quantiles per mix, plus a conditional energy-weighted estimate of misregistered billed energy under a declared load-composition model. A null is decisive and would close a decade-old dispute: a distribution centred inside the class accuracy limit with small tails under realistic mixes. Either way the acting communities are named (below).

It is a question, not a thesis: the null is publishable, specified in advance, and is the outcome the incumbent parties already assert.

Residual well-posedness defect (the reason this is not 3/3): the billed-energy limb is not a directly estimable population quantity. Misregistered energy depends on the joint distribution of (meter model, that household's actual load composition over the billing period), which an asset register does not carry. v2 therefore makes it a scenario estimand conditional on a declared composition model. That is well-posed but it is no longer a measurement of the real world's misregistered fraction, and a reader could reasonably say the second limb has been answered by assumption. Closing this properly needs a load-composition survey on the same sampled households — an additional study, not a parameter choice.

## Gate 3 — Significance

Consequence (argued): If the distribution is centred outside, or has material mass outside, the class accuracy limit under declared realistic mixes, then three things change mechanically rather than rhetorically. (i) The in-service sampling regime of prior 3 is measuring the wrong thing: its accept/reject decision is taken under conditions that do not include the stimulus, so a conforming population and a misregistering population are indistinguishable under it — and RDI-approved reglements and the annual rejected-population lists are the instrument that would have to change. (ii) The draft test waveforms MeterEMI handed to CENELEC TC13 WG1 / IEC SC77A and the OIML R46 revision stop being precautionary and become calibrated against a measured population harm, which is the usual difference between a draft addendum and a normative test. (iii) A restitution and loss-reallocation question opens across millions of meters, and because the reported error range spans both signs, the aggregate may be a transfer between customers rather than a net overcharge — which is a different regulatory problem with different remedies. If the distribution is centred inside the limit with thin tails, the answer is equally consequential and in the direction the incumbents assert: it closes a decade-old dispute that has run on laboratory demonstrations and press coverage since 2016, lets the committees justify not adding a costly test, and redirects the EMI work from the meter population to the specific appliance classes (dimmers, phase-firing switches, speed-controlled pumps) that doc B implicates. A question whose null is as publishable as its positive is not an agenda.

Audience (specific, and demonstrably already acting without the estimate): national metrology institutes already staffed on this exact object (VSL, PTB, NPL, METAS — doc B is VSL, doc A is an NMI consortium); verification and market-surveillance authorities, with RDI the named decision-maker in the Dutch case since it approves the sampling reglement and publishes rejected populations (doc C); the standards bodies named in doc A (CENELEC TC13 WG1, IEC TC13 WG11, IEC SC77A, WELMEC, OIML R46 revision, under MID 2014/32/EU); energy regulators and DSOs; and consumer bodies. The evidence that this audience is real is that it has already taken positions in the estimate's absence: Netbeheer Nederland published a claim of absence in practice built on tens of complaint cases across >8 million households (doc D), and EMPIR funded three years of work ending in standards drafts (doc A). The decision procedure the answer feeds exists and is running annually.

Fecundity: once a population distribution exists, these become askable and are not now — is registration error socially patterned, i.e. does it correlate with household appliance inventory and therefore with dimmer/LED/heat-pump/EV penetration and with income; can the decades of existing steekproefcontrole data be re-analysed as an error distribution rather than discarded as pass/fail, which is a question for the method field (PTB's sampling-procedures group, OIML G 20) rather than for EMC; what is the sign distribution, since a two-sided error means the aggregate may be a cross-subsidy rather than a loss; how much of reported DSO non-technical loss is metering misregistration (prior 6 made testable); does the design transfer to static gas, water and heat meters, to PV and EV submetering, and to the 2-150 kHz supraharmonic band generally; and how should a mix library be made auditable so that "realistic" is a declared and contestable object rather than a choice of the party running the test.

Anchoring: the 2016 Leferink/Keyer/Melentjev anomaly and its unresolved dispute; the 2-150 kHz supraharmonics emission and immunity gap, which is a live standards problem and not only a metering one; the acceptance-sampling-versus-estimation distinction in legal metrology; MID 2014/32/EU conformity assessment and the OIML R46 revision; and non-technical-loss accounting in DSO reporting.

Significance score: 3/3 — consequence argued in both directions, an audience with an existing annual decision procedure the answer plugs into, real fecundity, and anchoring to a named anomaly and a named standards gap. The honest counter is that the committees may add the test waveforms on precautionary grounds regardless of the population value, in which case the estimate changes the justification rather than the outcome.

## Reason for silence

A three-part mechanism, none of which is "no one thought of it".
1. Disciplinary boundary between the field that owns the DATA and the field that owns the METHOD. The effect was found and is studied by EMC engineers, whose unit of analysis is the specimen under a stimulus and whose deliverable is a test method and an immunity limit; q10 shows the field's own literature producing exactly that (testbeds, waveform libraries, standardisation, compensation) and nothing population-shaped. The machinery for population inference about deployed legal instruments sits one field over and is mature — PTB's "Sampling procedures in legal metrology", OIML TC3/SC4 and G 20, van der Grinten's OIML smart-meter sampling work (q8) — but its estimand is an accept/reject decision on a population, which cannot produce an error distribution no matter how many meters it tests. Neither field's standard output is the candidate's, so the question falls in the gap between them. This is the ledger's own documented failure mode used constructively: the negative was checked in the method field's vocabulary, and the method exists while the question does not.
2. Instrument. Measuring in-service meters under a realistic mix needs a transportable reference with demonstrated immunity to the same stimuli plus a programmable mix source at the meter. The field acquired that only through MeterEMI's testbed and waveform library (2018-2021, doc A); before then the measurement was not makeable at scale, and doc A still describes testing even a supplier-assembled selection as future work.
3. Incentive and access. The only parties holding the asset register and the right of physical access are the DSOs, who are also the parties a positive answer would bill; the verification regime they fund answers a pass/fail question that cannot yield the estimate (doc C), and their public position is an assertion of the null from passive complaint counts (doc D). Nobody whose natural output is the estimate has both the access and the motive, and the party with both has a reason to prefer the regime that cannot produce it.
