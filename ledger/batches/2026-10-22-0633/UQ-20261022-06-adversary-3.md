## Restatement before attacking (the adversary's own words)

Inside real clinical laboratories, a measurement that gets made is not always the measurement that gets released: rules fire (rerun, dilution, delta-check repeat, critical-value repeat, confirmation on a second instrument) and a later run's number goes out on the report in place of an earlier one. The candidate asks two linked things. First, as a bookkeeping fact at the grain of one (specimen, analyte) event, in at least two accredited laboratories on different middleware vendors and different rule sets, under a definition of "first valid measurement" written down before any data is pulled: what fraction of released results is not the first valid measurement of that analyte on that specimen, broken out by which rule caused the substitution — and, reported as a result in its own right, how many candidate laboratories cannot even retrieve a superseded measurement. Second, as a consequence: if you recompute three things that people compute daily from released results — reference-interval limits, the prevalence of results crossing a clinical threshold, and the within-person delta used for monitoring — on first measurements instead, do they move by more than a bound fixed in advance (the analyte's published allowable bias; reclassification across its decision limit; the sign and size of the delta change)? A null answer counts as an answer. The claim to novelty is not the selection mechanism, which Manathunga et al. already name, but the unit (a released patient result inside an accredited laboratory's log), the substrate (released results as the output of a within-laboratory selection rule) and those three downstream estimands.

### 1. Triviality — **Resolved**

**Objection.** Limb (a) is a database query any laboratory could run in an afternoon against its own middleware. Limb (c) is deducible on one page: reruns, dilutions and confirmation repeats fire at extreme values, so substitution pulls extremes inward, reference limits narrow and threshold-crossing prevalence falls. Regression to the mean, written down.

**Best response.** Both halves fail against Starks et al. 2021 — the field's own exemplar of mining middleware audit data, by a team that had the data and published it. On the *data* half: what the middleware yielded was instrument **flag** data, not numeric superseded results; the authors report the flag data "required extensive data cleanup and manual review"; and the paper prints an autoverification rate (87.8% of 16,073 orders before, 93.5% of 91,692 after) and a manual-review rate ("Manual review by a technologist was performed on 10,314 of those specimens (7.8%)", denominator 132,432 specimens) but, as the fetch put it, "No percentage of specimens re-run with PLT-F is printed." The afternoon query was available to them; limb (a) did not come out of it. On the *armchair* half: the paper's biggest rule reflexes PLT-I (impedance platelet count) to PLT-F (fluorescent platelet count) when PLT-I <70 k/mm³, "8.0% of flags that were holding both the CBC and WBC Diff to re-run for PLT-F". That is not a redraw from the same error distribution but a switch of measurement procedure chosen as a function of the value, so the regression-to-the-mean argument does not even deliver the sign, let alone the magnitude — and magnitude against a pre-fixed bound is the question. *My own arithmetic from the paper's printed numbers:* 10,314/132,432 = 7.79%, consistent with the printed 7.8%; and 6.5% held of 91,692 orders ≈ 5,960 orders, a different denominator from the 132,432 specimens — inside the one paper, which is itself evidence the quantity is not a trivial read-off.

### 3. Combinatorial novelty — **Resolved**

**Objection.** Laboratory quality management × selection-bias statistics, with the interface problem solved before anyone arrived: arXiv:2602.14877 §2.3 states the mechanism verbatim — conditional repetition "induces selection on the measurement error" — and supplies the correction. Transport is arithmetic.

**Best response.** There is a real interface problem, exhibited from an opened source rather than asserted. In Starks et al. the dominant rule replaces a PLT-I impedance count with a PLT-F fluorescent count when PLT-I <70 k/mm³. Run that against R1's own exclusion list: the PLT-I run is not QC-failed, not calibration-failed, not clot- or haemolysis-flagged and not out of linearity — so under R1 it **is** the first valid measurement and the released PLT-F value **is** a replacement. But this is not one measurement procedure sampled twice; it is two procedures, the second selected as a function of the first's value. Manathunga's machinery — a truncation-corrected latent-variable variance decomposition on repeated readings from one class of fingerstick device, against one eligibility cutoff — does not cover procedure switching, and dilution raises the same issue in chemistry. So the interface carries a problem neither parent field has solved: selection *plus* procedure substitution, keyed on the value. That is also why R1's rule-wise decomposition, which already separates cross-instrument confirmation and dilution as rule classes, is load-bearing rather than decorative. The "no problem at the interface" charge is defeated; the concession that the mechanism insight is not new stands as Gate 2 made it.

### 4. Presupposition failure — **Partial**

R1's conditioning clauses, listed separately and tested one at a time:

- **(i)** ≥2 accredited laboratories on different middleware vendors with different rule sets are reachable.
- **(ii)** Analyser/middleware logs retain superseded measurements retrievably and exportably.
- **(iii)** A "first valid measurement" rule is declarable pre-pull, excluding QC-failed, calibration-failed, clot/haemolysis-flagged and out-of-linearity runs.
- **(iv)** Published allowable-bias values exist per analyte to bound the reference-limit shift.
- **(v)** Each analyte has its own clinical decision limit, to define a reclassification rate.
- **(vi)** A within-person monitoring delta exists as an estimand computable on both substrates.
- **(vii)** The repeat *replaces* the released result rather than both being released.

**(iv) RESOLVED, with a source.** The Ricós-compiled database prints per-analyte columns "Analyte | Number of papers | Biological Variation (CV<sub>I</sub>, CV<sub>g</sub>) | Desirable specification (I(%), B(%), TE(%))" across roughly 400+ entries — e.g. "S- Albumin 24 3.2 4.75 1.6 1.43 4.07" (allowable bias 1.43%), "S- Calcium … 0.82", "S- Sodium … 0.23" — "calculated from data on within-subject and between-subject biologic variation" and "updated and compiled by Dr. Carmen Ricos and colleagues". Carried caveats from that same page: some entries have no bias value ("---"), and EFLM now hosts a newer database which "covers fewer analytes" — so coverage depends on which database is pre-declared, a choice R1 does not fix.

**(v) PARTIAL, and a defect the ledger should carry.** The page I opened prints **no clinical decision limit for any analyte**. Decision limits plainly exist for some analytes (cholesterol, HbA1c, glucose, eGFR), but for much of routine chemistry — electrolytes, enzymes, proteins — the operative threshold *is* the laboratory's reference limit. For those analytes R1's reclassification component is **circular**: it counts reclassification across a limit that the same limb claims is shifted. R1 says "each analyte's own clinical decision limit" and over-claims; the component is sound only if restricted in advance to analytes with an externally fixed decision limit, which R1 does not say.

**(ii) PARTIAL.** I opened a member of the document class Gate 2 opened zero of. In Starks the extracted middleware data are instrument flags, not numeric results; the paper nowhere states that the original numeric value is retained when a specimen is re-run; and the authors report "extensive data cleanup and manual review". The one class member I read neither supports nor refutes retrievable superseded **numbers**. Unverified by instance, not false — and R1 converts it into a reported primary quantity, so it is not a smuggled falsehood.

**(vii) PARTIAL, with weak positive support.** Starks' rules hold the specimen *before* release ("holding both the CBC and WBC Diff to re-run for PLT-F"), i.e. the first value is not released — which is the candidate's event. Given fact (A) adds that the own field's own definition of a repeat is "a re-analysis … performed before the initial result was reported", explicitly excluding post-report repeats.

**(iii) PARTIAL.** Definable, but the PLT-I case shows the exclusion list does more work than R1 admits: a run can pass all four exclusions and still be one the laboratory regards as inferior in that range. "First valid" is a bookkeeping definition, not a metrological one, and R1's sensitivity arms vary the exclusions but include no arm that varies the procedure-equivalence assumption.

**(i) and (vi) UNVERIFIED.** (i) is a recruitment fact I did not check. (vi) rests on given fact (C), not on a source I opened.

### 5. Vagueness — **Partial**

**Objection.** Answerable whatever the evidence shows. Any share is "the share"; any movement is "a difference"; the materiality bound is the investigator's; and "sensitivity arms that vary each exclusion" guarantee several admissible primary numbers to choose among afterwards.

**Best response.** Half defeated, half conceded. **Defeated:** the reference-limit bound is not the investigator's choice. The Ricós database turns "exceeds the analyte's published allowable bias" into a number computable before any data is seen — 1.43% serum albumin, 0.82% serum calcium, 0.23% serum sodium — so that component is sharp and falsifiable and a null on it is a real null. **Conceded:** the reclassification threshold is not externally fixed for many analytes (same page, no decision limits), so for those it is soft and, per attack 4, circular; and limb (a)'s headline figure is softer than R1 admits. Fact (A) shows how fast this degrades in the field's own hands — the nearest own-field paper prints two rates for one numerator (0.73% and 0.75%), three mutually inconsistent corpus totals, a Methods inclusion list that does not match its Results decomposition, and a numerator whose composition cannot be reconstructed. Starks exhibits the same hazard in miniature, quoting one share against 91,692 orders and another against 132,432 specimens. Sharp on limb (c)'s reference-limit component; soft on limb (a)'s exact number and on reclassification.

### 6. Significance inflation — **Partial**

**Objection.** Four audiences each with "a concrete artefact it would edit" is the sound of padding — above all audience (iv), biological-variation estimators, called "the most direct casualty" because "within-subject CVs are computed on released values".

**Best response.** The objection lands on audience (iv), and I can source the refutation. The EuBIVAS Methods I opened describe a dedicated prospective protocol, not routine production: "Fasting blood samples were drawn weekly for ten consecutive weeks (April–June 2015)"; "resulting in the recruitment of 91 healthy volunteers"; "The samples were stored in a − 80 °C freezer until they were shipped on dry ice to the CHU de Liège (Belgium)"; and decisively "Samples donated from the same individual were measured in duplicate within the same instrument run for a single day", with **no routinely released clinical results used anywhere**. So the flagship modern source feeding the EFLM BV database is immune to the candidate's mechanism by construction: its duplicates are protocol replicates in one run, not production reruns, and no repeat-and-replace rule selects between them. The record's claim that these within-subject CVs are "computed on released values" is wrong for that branch and the "most direct casualty" line should be struck. What survives, and survives well: **(i)** laboratory directors who own the rules — now *evidenced*, since Starks et al. is precisely a laboratory team mining its own middleware to retune these rules and publishing it; **(ii)** reference-interval guidance bodies and indirect-RI authors; **(iii)** EHR/biobank curators. A residual BV branch — within-subject CVs estimated from routine or "big" laboratory data — would still be exposed, but I opened no such source and record that **Unverified**. Three of four audiences stand; the fourth does not; the 2/3 score should not rise on this pass and should carry the EuBIVAS correction.

### 7. Reason for silence — **Resolved**

**Objection.** "A disciplinary boundary with an incentive component" is a narrative, not a mechanism. Middleware audit trails are decades old, the record concedes this is not a new-data story, and laboratory informatics publishes. If nobody asked it with the data sitting there, the likelier explanation is that it is not worth asking — which makes the novelty finding itself suspect.

**Best response.** The mechanism is now exhibited in a document, and the exhibit is the very case the objection posits: a team with the middleware data publishing in a pathology-informatics journal. Starks et al. spend the entire paper in the per-result operational frame. Endpoints: the autoverification rate (87.8% → 93.5%) and the manual-review burden (7.8% of 132,432 specimens). Conclusion: operational — "The biggest impact on autoverification resulted from not needing to perform PLT-F for PLT-I <70 k/mm3." They compute no reference interval, no prevalence of abnormality and no delta on either substrate, and the phrase "delta check" does not appear in the paper at all, only "delta failure" as a rerun trigger. That is the boundary in action: the log is read as workload and rule efficiency, never as a selection acting on a distribution. Given fact (A) shows the same boundary from the other side — the nearest own-field repeat-figure paper prints the share **repeated** and never the share **replaced**. And the record's refusal of the new-instrument story is corroborated: Starks' data are routinely retained hematology flags, so the data existed; what did not exist was anyone whose estimand lived on the other side of the join. A boundary gap persists where an instrument gap closes, which is what we observe.

### 8. Dissolution — **Partial**

**Objection.** It dissolves under its own definition. R1 excludes QC-failed, calibration-failed, clot/haemolysis-flagged and out-of-linearity runs from "valid", which removes the two largest classes from the numerator by fiat: the rerun case is largely runs the laboratory itself voided, the dilution case largely out-of-linearity runs. Fact (A) gives the shape — up to 46.6% of the nearest paper's 701 repeats are voided runs (analyser error flag 37.09%, QC failure, out of linearity 9.55%), and where the first run was voided the released value **is** the first valid measurement. So limb (a)'s primary arm is a near-zero artefact of R1's own bookkeeping and limb (c) has nothing to act on. Worse, where the replacement is real it is often a procedure switch, so "the reference interval on first measurements" is an interval over a value-selected mixture of procedures — not a quantity anyone would adopt, and not comparable to the released-result interval against one analyte's allowable bias.

**Best response.** The sharpest attack, and only half-answered; I grade it honestly rather than reach for a fatal status I cannot source. Three things stop short of dissolution. **First**, R1 pre-declares sensitivity arms that vary each exclusion, so the primary arm's small figure and the widest arm's larger one are both reportable, and the spread between them is itself informative about how much of the released distribution is rule-determined rather than measured. **Second**, the residue is neither empty nor defined away: Starks exhibits a rule holding specimens for re-run at PLT-I <70 k/mm³, "8.0% of flags", where the first run survives every one of R1's four exclusions — a real, non-trivial class of valid-first-run substitutions, and the class on which the extreme-value concentration argument bites hardest. **Third**, the record's MULTI-LEG NOTE already names the surviving leg (limb (c) restricted to threshold-crossing prevalence and the monitoring delta), and R1's rule-wise decomposition is exactly the stratification separating dissolving from surviving cases. **What is not answered, and should be recorded as a standing limitation:** the objection's second half is correct. Where the replacement is a procedure switch (PLT-I→PLT-F, dilution), the first-measurement substrate is a value-selected mixture of measurement procedures, so recomputing a reference interval on it and testing the shift against one analyte's published allowable bias compares *across* procedures rather than detecting bias *within* one. Limb (c)'s reference-limit component survives cleanly only for same-procedure reruns and confirmation repeats. The question narrows to a statable subset; it does not evaporate.
