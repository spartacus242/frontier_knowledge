## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (canonical, as submitted — V1):
Is contamination of the two blood-culture sets drawn in one episode independent between sets, and given the measured within-episode correlation what is the misclassification rate of the standard interpretive convention that one positive set of two indicates contamination while both positive indicates true bacteraemia?

Question (canonical, Gate 1 reformulation — V2; ONE reformulation used, for well-posedness only):
In a frame of blood-culture episodes in which exactly two sets were drawn and draw-level metadata are retrievable, with "commensal isolation" fixed in advance by an organism list and concordance required to be species- (and ideally strain-) matched across sets: (a) what is the ratio of the observed both-sets-concordant commensal proportion to the proportion predicted by between-set independence computed from the same cohort's per-set commensal rate, with a confidence interval (null: ratio = 1); (b) how does that excess partition into variance components by operator, draw structure (single venipuncture / two venipunctures / line-versus-peripheral), prep protocol and antiseptic lot; and (c) under the measured correlation, what is the RELATIVE reclassification fraction of the 1-of-2 / 2-of-2 interpretive rule — the share of 2-of-2 commensal episodes that a pre-specified blinded adjudication standard calls contamination, and of 1-of-2 episodes it calls infection — reported as an interval under stated assumptions about the adjudication standard rather than as an absolute error rate?
(Reformulation rationale: V1's second limb presupposes an absolute reference standard for true bacteraemia, which P5 itself concedes is contested; V2 fixes the estimand as a relative/bounded reclassification fraction and makes the independence benchmark explicit. Both versions recorded, as required.)

Alternative formulations (as submitted):
- How much of what is counted as true bacteraemia on the strength of a commensal organism growing in both of two sets is correlated contamination from a shared draw, operator, skin-prep failure or antiseptic lot rather than bloodstream infection?
- Does the two-set rule for separating contaminants from pathogens rest on an independence assumption that the way blood is actually drawn violates, and by how much?

Motivating gap (as submitted, and its Gate 2 correction):
Submitted gap: the convention's logic is explicitly probabilistic — two contamination events are unlikely to coincide — so it needs between-set independence; contamination is a clustered process attached to operator and technique; the two sets are routinely drawn by the same person, same prep, minutes apart; and the literature reports contamination only as a per-set quality indicator, "never as a correlated process at episode level", so the within-episode correlation "is not reported anywhere I could find".
**CORRECTION FROM GATE 2 (load-bearing): that last claim is false.** Borad et al., *Antimicrobial Stewardship & Healthcare Epidemiology* 5(1), Sept 2025 (opened, full text) analyses paired blood-culture sets AT EPISODE LEVEL, takes the pair as the unit, reports how often BOTH sets grew commensals, and stratifies that both-sets proportion by whether the pair carried a single time stamp (STS; taken as a proxy for one shared single-site draw) or multiple time stamps (MTS; two draws within an hour): 28.1% (STS) vs 18.5% (MTS), P = .11, with per-pair commensal recovery 6.3% vs 7.3%. That is the candidate's mechanism test in weaker form, in the candidate's own field, with the candidate's own unit of analysis, published eight weeks before intake.

## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

What would count as an answer (as submitted, retained):
An episode-level analysis in one or more large microbiology datasets giving (i) within-episode concordance of commensal isolation in excess of what independence predicts from the same cohort's per-set rate, with its confidence interval; (ii) variance components for that excess by operator, prep and draw context; (iii) the implied PPV of the 2-of-2 rule and NPV of the 1-of-2 rule under the measured correlation, against a pre-specified reference standard. Strengthened by a prospective arm in which the two sets are deliberately drawn by different operators with independent prep, making correlation experimentally manipulable. A null — correlation indistinguishable from zero — would be an informative first defence of a decades-old convention.

Assessment: limb (i) is crisply well-posed with a sharp null and is estimable from data that demonstrably exist. Limb (ii) is well-posed conditional on P4, which is site-dependent and only half-verified. Limb (iii) is the weak one: under V1 it asks for an absolute misclassification rate against a reference standard that P5 concedes may not exist independently of the rule being tested, and every adjudication standard found in Gate 2 is unblinded to the very multiplicity it would be used to validate. V2 repairs this by fixing the estimand as relative reclassification under stated assumptions. It is a question, not a thesis, though the submitted motivating gap leaned thesis-ward ("the way blood is actually drawn violates it") and Gate 2 shows the lean was in the right direction.

## Gate 3 — Significance (argued)

Consequence: YES. If the measured excess concordance is large — and Prior 1's numbers plus Prior 2's PPV series both point that way — then the PPV of the 2-of-2 commensal rule is materially below what the independence intuition implies, which means a quantifiable share of central-line removals, transthoracic/transoesophageal echocardiograms and multi-week vancomycin courses are being triggered by correlated contamination, and a quantifiable share of hospital-onset-bacteraemia counts are artefacts of draw practice rather than of infection. If the answer is a null, it retires a live worry about a decades-old convention and, more usefully, licenses the convention's continued use in surveillance definitions that currently rest on it without evidence. Either way the number is actionable because the fix is cheap and already partly deployed: requiring two independently prepped draws, or carrying draw-structure as a covariate in the metric.

Audience: specific and identifiable. (i) Clinical microbiology laboratories setting commenting and workup rules for commensal isolates — they would change the comment attached to a 2-of-2 CoNS result from confirmatory to conditional on draw structure. (ii) Infection-prevention and the bodies specifying hospital-onset bacteraemia metrics — a draw-structure-dependent PPV is a measure-validity problem, and the same group (Kaye, Abdul Azim and colleagues) is already publishing in exactly this space. (iii) Antimicrobial stewardship — vancomycin days attributable to concordant commensal pairs are a named target. (iv) Phlebotomy and vascular-access services, and the diversion-device vendors already citing this literature, for whom "does the intervention reduce the CORRELATION or only the rate?" is a new and answerable product question.

Fecundity: makes askable — should the two-set requirement be re-specified as two independently prepped draws by different operators; should interpretive algorithms weight concordance by draw-structure metadata rather than counting sets; do initial-specimen-diversion devices and dedicated phlebotomy teams reduce the correlation or only the marginal rate; should HOB and CLABSI definitions carry a draw-structure covariate; and does the same correlated-confirmation problem affect every other "confirm on a second specimen" rule in the laboratory (urine, respiratory, surveillance swabs, duplicate critical values).

Anchoring: touches the long-standing CoNS-significance problem; the currently live single-site vs multi-site drawing controversy, which Prior 1 and its IDWeek abstract are part of; the validity of the hospital-onset-bacteraemia measure now being developed as a quality indicator; and, as a transplant target, the reliability-engineering problem of common-cause failure in a 2-out-of-2 redundant confirmatory channel, which query 10 shows is mature and has never been pointed at diagnostic confirmation.

Significance score rationale: consequence real and quantified in adjacent form, audience real and demonstrably active, fecundity good, anchoring concrete — but the headline worry is already in play in the field rather than being opened by this question, which caps it.

## Reason for silence

Credible mechanism, in two parts, and it is a denominator boundary rather than an oversight. First, contamination is institutionalised as a LABORATORY QUALITY INDICATOR whose denominator is the set (or the drawn culture): benchmarks are stated as per-set percentages, and the quantity is owned by laboratory QC and reported to accreditation bodies. The interpretive rule, by contrast, lives in a different document family — commenting and interpretation guidance aimed at the clinician at the bedside. The within-episode correlation is the quantity that links the two, and it therefore belongs to neither committee, appears in neither document's required reporting, and has no constituency that must compute it. Second, the draw-level metadata needed to see the episode as a unit — set identity, time stamps, operator, site — only recently became extractable at scale from laboratory information systems, which is precisely why Prior 1 appeared in 2025 and used a time-stamp proxy rather than operator identity. That is a "new data" mechanism operating on top of a disciplinary-boundary mechanism, and the fact that the question is now being asked, imperfectly, is itself evidence that the mechanism was real and is dissolving. Note the consequence for Gate 2: a reason for silence that is actively dissolving is exactly the situation in which a novelty claim should be presumed stale, which is consistent with the Low grade.
