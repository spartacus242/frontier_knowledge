## Restatement (my own words)

Take frozen clones from the Lenski E. coli lines, revive a set spanning the time series, and make every clone fight every other clone (not just the ancestor), at more than one starting ratio, with replicates. Then ask three things: (a) how often does A beat B, B beat C, and C beat A — and are those cycles bigger than the assay noise; (b) if you split the win/loss matrix into a "ladder" part and a "cycle" part, how much of the signal is cycle; (c) if you fit the famous decelerating fitness curve using the ladder-plus-cycle model instead of the usual ancestor-only scalar, does the fitted curve (exponent, long-run projection) move. The candidate's claim to novelty is that nobody has treated LTEE fitness as a tournament rather than a number.

## Recommended verdict

Rejected — Disguise (attack 2) Unresolved: the canonical question is de Visser & Lenski 2002's title question renamed into tournament vocabulary and re-posed against the 2013 curve, and the 2013 curve's own paper already ran a non-ancestral-reference check at 40,000/50,000 generations; the un-asked residue is estimator machinery (cyclic-triple rate with error null, Hodge share, exponent refit), which the record correctly routes to a separate, narrower candidate. Gate 2 ground confirmed by direct reading of P1 at the publisher route.

Strongest killing point: P1 (opened, publisher route) reports that its ANOVA "tests all forms of non-transitivity", finds the sample x reference-clone interaction "< 2% of the overall variation" with "P > 0.5", and that "the non-transitive model is strongly rejected by the observed cumulative gains"; Wiser et al. 2013 (opened) then competed 40k/50k samples of nine populations "against marked 40,000-generation clones" and states that frequency-dependent interactions "are not expected to amplify gains relative to a distant ancestor, as fitness was measured here" — so the candidate's trajectory limb was asked and answered twice inside the LTEE's own series, once in the hyperbolic era and once in the power-law era.

Repair note for the residue candidate: drop "projected asymptote" for the power-law fit (Wiser: "fitness has no upper limit"); report the cyclic-triple rate per starting frequency; and state up front that archived-clone frequency dependence in Ara-1 (0.9-5.4%, Maddamsetti et al. preprint) is of the same order as P1's ~5% residual error, so the deliverable is a bound.
