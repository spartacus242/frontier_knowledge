Domain(s): Other STEM — electrochemical engineering / battery test metrology and design of experiments. Secondary: data-driven battery prognostics as consumer of the archived datasets.

Question (canonical) — V1 (as intaken):
In long-run cell-cycling experiments, what share of the between-cell variance in cycle life and in degradation-trajectory shape is attributable to the cycling channel and its position (per-channel current/voltage calibration, thermal environment) rather than to the cell, and — for published protocol comparisons and closed-loop protocol optimisations in which channel and protocol were not crossed — would accounting for the channel component change which protocols rank best?

Question (canonical) — V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, one permitted, for well-posedness only; this is the version graded):
For long-run Li-ion cell-cycling experiments: (a) PROSPECTIVE LIMB — in a design in which cells are rotated between channels on a schedule declared before the experiment (>=3 channels x >=3 cells per group, with sham rotations in which a cell is removed and returned to its own channel, traceable per-channel current, voltage and temperature calibration, and logged chamber position), what are the cell, channel and cell-by-channel variance components, with intervals, of the per-interval degradation rate and of pre-declared trajectory features, and what cycle-life variance share do they imply under the pre-declared trajectory model? (b) ARCHIVAL LIMB — in archived protocol-ranking experiments in which the same physical channels hosted cells on different protocols across rounds or batches (Severson et al. 2019; Attia et al. 2020), what is the estimated channel-level variance when channel is entered as a random effect alongside protocol, and does the top-k protocol set change under channel-shrunken protocol estimates?
(Why reformulated: V1's "share of the between-cell variance ... attributable to the channel" is not identifiable in the designs it criticises, because each cell occupies one channel for life; V2 separates the limb that needs a new design from the limb that is identifiable in existing data, where cell is nested in channel but channel is crossed with protocol across rounds. V2 also names the handling/rest confound of rotation and its sham-rotation control, and states that the prospective limb estimates channel effects on degradation RATE, with cycle-life effects propagated through a declared model rather than measured directly.)

Alternative formulations:
- Is what the battery field calls "cell-to-cell variability" partly channel-to-channel variability of the test rig?
- Under a deliberately crossed design in which cells are rotated between channels on a schedule, what are the cell, channel and cell-by-channel variance components of cycle life, and how do protocol effects shrunken for the channel component compare with the published rankings?

Motivating gap:
The settled claim inverted: the spread in cycle life among nominally identical cells cycled under nominally identical conditions is a property of the cells (manufacturing tolerance), and is used to size test matrices, set pack-level and warranty margins, and benchmark lifetime predictors. The untested presupposition is not only that the apparatus contributes little, but that the standard design could separate it at all: one cell per channel for life makes cell identity and channel perfectly confounded, and no amount of within-design replication splits them. Per-channel current/voltage accuracy (order 0.05% of full scale on commercial cyclers) and position-dependent chamber temperature gradients act non-linearly on degradation rate (Arrhenius-like temperature dependence; rate-dependent plating thresholds), which is the mechanism that would make the confound matter. Gate 2 found that the field has named the issue twice without estimating it: Dechent et al. (2021) list "location in a given testing chamber" and thermal inhomogeneity as extrinsic sources and declare their aim to be "intrinsic rather than extrinsic variability"; Devie/Dubarry et al. (2015) assert "calibration across channels" and "neutralization of extrinsic factors" as design controls and report no channel component.

Presuppositions:
1. A cell-versus-channel decomposition is identifiable with a feasible design. CONTESTED, and the hardest point: ageing is destructive and non-repeatable, so a cell cannot be re-run from scratch in a second channel; identification must come from rotation plus a trajectory model, with the schedule pre-declared. A channel effect that is a pure rate multiplier is partly absorbable by the cell's own rate parameter, so the design must declare which contrasts carry the channel term.
2. Rotation does not itself perturb the cell enough to masquerade as a channel effect (handling, rest, re-equilibration, contact resistance). FLAGGED; addressed in V2 by sham rotations, not assumed away.
3. Channel-level metrological differences exist at a magnitude capable of moving degradation rate. TO BE MEASURED by traceable per-channel calibration, not inferred from datasheets. Partially supported for the temperature channel: Wildfeuer & Lienkamp report (summary page) that "extrinsic temperature deviations of only 0.5 degC can lead to resistance variations of more than 1.5 %".
4. Public datasets record channel identity. PARTLY TRUE as intaken: channel IDs are present in the named datasets; chamber position, per-channel calibration history and ambient gradients generally are not, which caps the archival limb at channel identity (and confounds channel with era/batch).
5. Degradation rate is sensitive to small temperature and current offsets. ESTABLISHED.
6. In the published protocol experiments, protocol and channel were not crossed or randomised. NOT VERIFIED — see COULD NOT READ below. Note that V2's archival limb does not need the strong form of this presupposition: it needs only that some channels hosted cells on more than one protocol across rounds/batches, which is what makes a channel random effect estimable. If assignment was in fact randomised, the re-ranking limb weakens to a precision question and the variance-decomposition limb survives intact.
7. Cycle life and the trajectory features are defined consistently enough across channels that a difference is physical and not an artefact of per-channel capacity-check execution. FLAGGED; a per-channel definitional artefact would itself be a channel effect of interest but must be reported separately.

What would count as an answer:
Either (a) a prospective crossed/rotation experiment as specified in V2 — >=3 channels x >=3 cells per group, pre-declared rotation and sham-rotation schedule, traceable per-channel current, voltage and temperature calibration, logged chamber position — reporting cell, channel and cell-by-channel variance components with intervals for degradation rate and for pre-declared trajectory features, and the implied cycle-life variance share; or (b) a re-analysis of an archived protocol-ranking experiment with channel entered as a random effect, stating whether the top-k protocol set changes and whether the estimated channel variance is large enough to have produced the published ranking difference. A null answers it: a channel component indistinguishable from zero at a pre-declared precision leaves the "cell-to-cell variability" reading and the published rankings intact and converts the question into a validated assumption.

Significance — consequence:
If the channel component is material (say an intraclass correlation for channel of 0.1-0.2 or more of between-cell cycle-life variance): (i) replication guidance of the P1 family is structurally wrong, because adding cells inside fixed channel assignments cannot shrink a channel-induced component - test matrices need channel randomisation or crossing, which is a design change, not a sample-size change; (ii) published protocol rankings, including the closed-loop optimiser's top-k set, may be partly apparatus artefacts, and a closed loop that assigned protocols to channels non-randomly was optimising partly over channels; (iii) ML lifetime predictors trained on these datasets may be partly learning channel signatures, and the much-used batch-to-batch generalisation gap acquires a mundane candidate explanation; (iv) pack-level and warranty margins sized from these spreads are mis-sized (too wide if channel variance is being charged to cells); (v) test standards and dataset schemas gain a channel-randomisation, calibration-traceability and position-logging requirement. If the component is indistinguishable from zero at a pre-declared precision: the intrinsic-variability reading and the published rankings stand, the archival datasets become stronger evidence than they currently are entitled to be, and a named confound is converted into a validated assumption - a usable result either way, which is what makes the question well-posed rather than rhetorical.

Battery test laboratories and dataset producers (Stanford/MIT/Toyota, HNEI, Oxford/RWTH, Sandia, NASA, JRC) - would change metadata schemas (channel ID, chamber position, per-channel calibration dates and as-found deviations) and channel-assignment practice; cycler manufacturers (Arbin, Neware, Maccor, BioLogic) - per-channel rather than per-instrument calibration disclosure, and channel-to-channel specifications stated as a between-channel variance rather than a full-scale accuracy bound; the data-driven prognostics community - benchmark construction and train/test splits that are currently made by batch would need to be made by channel as well; standards bodies (IEC 62660, ISO 12405, USABC/DOE test manuals) and the battery-metrology activity already visible in the BIPM CCQM task group on Li-ion batteries and VAMAS; OEM cell-validation and warranty-engineering groups that size margins from reported cell-to-cell spreads; public archives (Battery Archive, BatteryML) that could require the fields immediately.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism — disciplinary boundary plus incentive, with an invisibility-by-construction amplifier):
(1) Disciplinary boundary: the method belongs to variance-component statistics and industrial quality engineering (crossed/nested random effects, gauge R&R, blocking), and to agronomy's and survey methodology's handling of plot and interviewer effects; battery ageing is run by electrochemists and test engineers whose replication culture recognises exactly one random factor, the cell. Gate 2 query 4 shows the method's own field has the vocabulary and no battery instance, which is the signature of a boundary rather than of a solved problem.
(2) The datasheet substitutes for the estimate: a cycler is sold with a 0.05%-of-full-scale accuracy specification, and a specification reads as a bound that makes the question look already answered, so no one estimates the between-channel variance it fails to describe.
(3) Invisibility by construction: with one cell per channel for life, no analysis of any existing single-laboratory dataset can reveal a channel effect, so the anomaly that normally prompts a methodological question cannot appear in the data the field already holds. The confound is not overlooked so much as unobservable within the standard design.
(4) Incentive and throughput: channels are a scarce, fully booked resource; rotation schedules and protocol-by-channel crossing cost throughput, complicate automation scripts, and risk a null result, while a positive result would devalue the asker's own archived datasets and the field's flagship ones. Nobody is rewarded for spending channel-months to discover that their own historical data are partly about their rig.
