false-attribution rate, or an INTER-EXAMINER AGREEMENT statistic for fracture-MODE or

### Abstract, reconstructed verbatim from 18 ordered consecutive summariser segments

> "Despite conventional wisdom, the Charpy fracture appearance transition curve does not
> always coincide with the energy transition curve. Furthermore, unlike Charpy energy,
> fracture appearance tells how a specimen failed. It can therefore be used to
> meaningfully relate the results of Charpy testing to results of other toughness tests
> which may employ different geometries and loading rates. In order to address the
> question of consistency, a set of 20 specimens was prepared and used in a `round robin`
> survey. Results showed that agreement was greatest when operators are experienced,
> samples are close to fracture appearance transition, and simple, two-dimensional
> diagrams are sued for assessment. It was also found that certain inconsistencies exist
> between various assessment techniques for Charpy fracture appearance. As a part of this
> work, fracture appearance curves were compared to energy impact curves for the Charpy
> test and a similar test, the Schnadt K{sub o} test, which uses a sharp pressed notch.
> It was found that energy and fracture appearance transition may differ by as much as
> 50{degrees}C in the Charpy test while the two curves coincided in the Schnadt test. In
> series of toughness tests on 132 steels, the average difference between Charpy energy
> transition and Schnade K{sub o} energy transition was about 27{degrees}C. This is
> believed to represent the difference in toughness between blunt and sharp notches in
> Charpy size specimens."

### What it MEASURES — unit of analysis, endpoint, comparator, estimand, in its own words

- Unit of analysis: Charpy impact specimens of steel. "a set of 20 specimens was prepared
  and used in a `round robin` survey."
- What the operators produced: a **fracture appearance measurement** — i.e. the appearance
  (percent-shear-type) assessment of a Charpy fracture surface, made with "assessment
  techniques" and "simple, two-dimensional diagrams".
- Endpoint 1, the reliability endpoint: **consistency / agreement between operators and
  between assessment techniques**, reported **ordinally only** — "agreement was greatest
  when operators are experienced…". No coefficient, no percentage, no scatter figure in
  the abstract.
- Endpoint 2, the physics endpoint, which carries all the numbers: transition-temperature
  differences. "energy and fracture appearance transition may differ by as much as
  50{degrees}C in the Charpy test"; across "132 steels" the Charpy-vs-Schnadt energy
  transition difference "was about 27{degrees}C".
- Comparator for endpoint 2: the Charpy test vs "the Schnadt K{sub o} test, which uses a
  sharp pressed notch."

### Answer to the single question

**NO, for all three forms, in the regime that matters here.**

- Per-examiner accuracy: **no**. There is no ground truth. Percent-shear / fracture
  appearance has no truth known by construction; the only reference is other operators and
  other assessment techniques.
- False-attribution rate: **no**. Nothing is attributed to a category that could be wrong.
- Inter-examiner agreement statistic **for fracture-MODE or failure-MECHANISM attribution**:
  **no**. There *is* an inter-operator agreement finding, but its object is a **measured
  VALUE** (fracture appearance of a Charpy specimen), and it is stated qualitatively, not
  as a statistic.

This is the "reproduces a measured VALUE" case, explicitly: percent shear area. It is the
nearest thing in this batch to an examiner-reliability study of fracture surfaces, and it
is still the wrong regime for a fracture-mechanism-attribution claim.

### Regime the source establishes its result in

Population: 20 archived Charpy specimens, steel, plus a separate 132-steel toughness
series. Unit: one specimen × one operator. Instrument: visual/diagram-based fracture
appearance assessment, several competing "assessment techniques". Condition: Charpy
impact, blunt notch, compared against Schnadt K{sub o} sharp pressed notch. Threshold:
the fracture appearance transition. Category set: **none** — the output is a continuous
appearance fraction, not a mode or mechanism label. A claim about examiners assigning
*failure mechanisms* (fatigue vs overload vs SCC vs creep) sits **outside** this regime and
this source is **silent** on it, not supportive.

## Defects

- **D1 — (iii) wholly unverified.** OSTI/ORNL 1972570 yielded no front matter and no
  abstract by any route tried. COULD NOT READ, explicitly distinct from NOT FOUND; the
  robots refusal is not absence, since the sibling record 115531 served normally. Two
  plausible routes (the purl, `info.ornl.gov/sites/publications/`) remain untried.
- **D2 — (i) the specified output was NOT FOUND; only a describing paper was found, and
  that could not be read.** No DVM/DGM Fractography round-robin *result* publication
  surfaced in Practical Metallography or a DVM report series. Treating the working group
  as a source of per-examiner accuracy or agreement data is unsupported by anything read
  here.
- **D3 — regime mismatch on (ii), and this is the one that should move a verdict.** OSTI
  115531 measures operator-to-operator consistency in **fracture appearance (percent-shear)
  measurement** on Charpy specimens — a continuous measured VALUE with **no truth known by
  construction**. It is not per-examiner accuracy, not a false-attribution rate, and not an
  agreement statistic for fracture-MODE or failure-MECHANISM attribution. Cited for the
  latter it is outside its regime and therefore silent, not supportive.
- **D4 — no statistic even for what (ii) does measure.** The agreement finding is ordinal:
  "Results showed that agreement was greatest when operators are experienced…". Every
  number in the abstract (50{degrees}C, 27{degrees}C, 132 steels, 20 specimens) belongs to
  transition-temperature comparison or to sample size, not to agreement. Nothing can be
  re-derived at an agreement threshold because no author stated one.
- **D5 — attractive-nuisance citations.** "Results of a DVM round robin on instrumented
  Charpy testing" and "Application of single-specimen methods on instrumented Charpy tests"
  are DVM round robins on measured values; "Classification of fracture characteristics and
  fracture mechanisms using deep learning" reports a *machine* classifier's performance.
  None is a human examiner reliability study. Any of the three could be mis-shelved under
  this question.
- **D6 — text damage in (ii) reproduced, not corrected.** As served: "diagrams are **sued**
  for assessment" (for "used"); "**Schnade** K{sub o}" in one sentence against "Schnadt" in
  the other; TeX-ish markup "K{sub o}", "{degrees}C"; backticks around `round robin`.
  Flagged rather than silently cleaned.
- **D7 — (ii) front matter is thin and has a near-duplicate.** The 115531 record lists no
  authors and no publication date. A same-titled *Welding Journal* record, OSTI 7149335,
  dated 1994-08-01, sits beside it as a "similar record". Do not transplant authors, journal
  or year from one record to the other; if an author/year is needed for 115531, it must come
  from that record's own front matter or from the conference proceedings CONF-9404233.
- **D8 — document type of (ii) stated, not inferred.** OSTI types it "Conference", i.e. a
  conference contribution, not a journal article, not a review, not a guideline. The
  132-steel toughness series is reported inside it as prior/companion work ("In series of
  toughness tests on 132 steels"); the abstract does not say whose series it is, so a finding
  drawn from those 132 steels should not be attributed to this round robin's 20 specimens.
