## WHAT THE SOURCE SAYS (quotations as served)

### Source A, Schuh et al. 2016

The abstract, reconstructed from **eight consecutive segments** returned in order by the summariser at the FIS route and independently returned in the same order at the DOAJ route (labelled a reconstruction; I verified each junction and completed no cut string by inference):

> "Ensuring optimal climate conditions during cultivation is essential for successful and reproducible cell culture based investigation. Being the gold standard, carbon dioxide incubators fulfill this demand in various geometries and sizes to suit diverse cultivation applications. A door opening results in a climate breakdown followed by a restoration period during which optimal conditions cannot be guaranteed. The following paper investigates the influence of incubator door design to optimize climate retainment during the above mentioned event."

**Scope-setting openers, written out as required.**
- "A door opening results in..." - the opening words scope the whole climate claim **to the door-opening event**. The clause **asserts** a breakdown-and-restoration structure and says nothing whatever about any periodicity. It is not a denial of a 24-hour component; it never reaches the question.
- "The following paper investigates the influence of incubator door design to optimize climate retainment **during the above mentioned event**" - the closing words scope the paper's entire contribution to the door-opening event named in the previous sentence. This clause **records a move away** from any ambient or long-run environmental characterisation: the paper is about door geometry, not about what an incubator does when nobody touches it.
- The hedged formulation is the one I rely on. On the only place where the paper gives a figure for how long the atmosphere survives, it is explicitly experience-based, not measured: the gases had "emanated to the external environment" after "a duration of more than 30 s", which the paper attributes to the authors' experience rather than to an instrument. I quote that hedged version and not any bare restatement.

**What is actually in it, by figure.**
- **Figure 4** is the only plot of any quantity against time: CO2 volume fraction over **15 s**, with maximum dotted, mean solid, minimum dashed, for the swing door and the sliding door. Time span: fifteen seconds. Printed y-axis units: **not recoverable at my read level**.
- Figure 3 shows induced air-flow velocity magnitude in the vertical symmetry plane during door opening; the caption carries no units or scale.
- Figures 1, 2 and 5 are a door schematic, a setup sketch and photographs over the 1 s of door movement. Figure 6 is photographs of atmosphere exchange at reference times **10 s, 20 s and 30 s**.
- **Table 1** (boundary conditions) prints: inner volume 590 x 590 x 538 mm; swing aperture 590 x 590 mm; sliding aperture 480 x 480 mm; outer air 20 C; inner air 37 C; and for the simulation 10 Vol-% CO2 and 95 % rh.

**PUBLISHED IS NOT PERFORMED, and here it bites hard.** The 10 Vol-% CO2 and 95 % rh in Table 1 are **simulation boundary conditions** - inputs to ANSYS Fluent with a k-epsilon model, dynamic meshes and user-defined functions for door motion, with three perforated shelves as pressure-jump boundaries - not readings. Figure 4's CO2 curve is therefore a **simulated** quantity, not an instrumented one. The one physical experiment in the paper is a light-section visualisation: a roughly 180 l mock-up regulated at 37 C, filled with **effect fog**, lit by a laser plane and filmed from door opening until 30 s after. Fog is a tracer, not a calibrated environmental channel. The paper names no temperature logger, no CO2 analyser, no humidity sensor, and prints **no measured temperature drop, no measured recovery time and no measured CO2 value**. On the quantity the entry cares about, this paper **simulates and visualises**; it does not measure.

**On circadian content:** none. The summariser, asked directly, reported that the paper "does not mention circadian rhythm, diurnal variation, or 24-hour cycles."

### Source B, Chen et al. 2026

**The chamber is not an incubator, and the incubators were thrown out.** From the Methods: entrainment ran in a greenhouse enclosure, 6 ft L x 6 ft W x 6 ft H (FlowerHouse, Clio, MI). Earlier trials used Percival Scientific and Power Scientific **incubators**, and these were abandoned because vibration from their humidifiers and heating/cooling systems reduced entrainment. Humidity was driven by Honeywell humidifiers, Vremi dehumidifiers and Inkbird IHC200 controllers.

**It is instrumented.** A HOBO MX1104 analog temperature/RH/light logger (Onset, Bourne, MA) plus a ThermoPro TP67-B digital barometer. **Sampling interval and logging duration: not stated** at my read level. Entrainment ran 5 days of humidity cycles per the Fig. 1 legend.

**The amplitude, quoted as served.** The Methods sentence is:

> "The cycles of low (40-50%RH, arid phase, A) and high (>85%RH, humid phase, H) humidity levels."

Its opening words scope the figures **to the designed cycle phases**, and the clause **asserts** the two levels as programmed set-point bands. Note what it is not: it is not a reading, and ">85%RH" is a **bound**, not a measurement - the humid phase has no printed upper value, so no point-to-point amplitude can be computed from this sentence without inventing the top of the range. A second sentence on the page says humidity "varied by 40-50%", which the summariser flagged as ambiguous against the stated ranges; I do not rely on it. Temperature was held **constant at 25 C**, so the paper reports **no** temperature amplitude. Air pressure constant at 780 mm Hg. Period: 24 h, as 12 h arid : 12 h humid.

**Attribution of the variation - sub-claim (c) for this source.** Deliberate programming, flatly. The cycle is applied by controllers on a schedule. The document does not attribute any of its 24-hour structure to HVAC cycling or to scheduled human access, and does not discuss either. The one equipment-origin environmental artefact it does name is **vibration** from incubator humidifiers and heating/cooling systems, which it treats as a confound to be removed by leaving the incubators, not as a periodic component to be characterised.

**It does relate the variation to circadian measurements - sub-claim (d).** Locomotor activity (actograms and free-running rhythmicity across several insect species and *Drosophila* clock and hygrosensation mutants), sugar feeding by CAFE assay in *Drosophila*, and sleep in *D. melanogaster* (Supplementary Fig. S5). No eclosion, no gene expression. **Whole animals throughout; no cell culture and no cell-autonomous circadian measurement anywhere in it.**

## REGIME

**Source A, in its own words.** Population: carbon dioxide incubators as cell-culture equipment, "the gold standard", in "various geometries and sizes". Unit of analysis: a single door-opening event on one 590 x 590 x 538 mm cabinet, two door geometries compared. Operating condition: transient, from door motion (1 s) to 30 s after. Instrument: ANSYS Fluent k-epsilon CFD, plus laser light-section photography of effect fog in a ~180 l mock-up at 37 C. Threshold/category: climate "retainment" during the breakdown-restoration event; DIN 12880 appears as a standard designation in the keywords, not as a measurement. **Does the entry's claim sit inside this regime? No.** The claim is about a periodic component on a 24-hour timescale under undisturbed operation. Source A's longest axis is 30 seconds - **four orders of magnitude** short - and its undisturbed state is a boundary condition, not an observation. A result proved in this regime is silent outside it.

**Source B, in its own words.** Population: multiple insect species, whole organisms, including *Drosophila melanogaster* clock and hygrosensation mutants. Unit of analysis: individual animal behaviour (activity, feeding, sleep) under programmed environmental cycles. Operating condition: constant light, constant 25 C, constant 780 mm Hg, with humidity cycled 12:12 between a 40-50% RH band and a >85% RH band for 5 days, then a 6 h phase shift with a 3-day re-entrainment window and 24 h behavioural assays. Instrument: HOBO MX1104 logger in a 6-ft fabric greenhouse enclosure. **Does the claim sit inside this regime? Partly, and the mismatch is the whole answer.** It is a chamber, it is instrumented, and the periodic component is 24-hourly and tied to circadian readouts. But the enclosure is not an incubator, the cycle is **imposed rather than discovered**, and nothing in it is cell-autonomous.

**Numerator and denominator, both directions, as required.** Source B's 40-50% / >85% RH pair looks like the kind of number the claim asks for, so here is what it actually counts. *Numerator class:* set-point bands of a **deliberately programmed** humidity oscillation in a fabric greenhouse enclosure, explicitly **excluding** the Percival and Power Scientific incubators, which were dropped from the design; also excluding temperature and pressure, both pinned constant by intent. *Denominator:* the designed phase structure of a 12:12 entrainment protocol - not the set of undisturbed operating hours of a culture incubator, and not the variance of an uncontrolled environmental channel, which are the near neighbours it is not. *What Source B's class misses that the claim needs:* an incubator rather than a tent; an **unintended** periodic component arising from the apparatus and its surroundings rather than from a controller set-point; a measured amplitude with both ends printed rather than an open-ended ">85%" bound; and a cell-autonomous preparation. *What Source B's class includes that the claim excludes:* whole-animal behavioural entrainment in multi-species insects under constant light, deliberate 6 h phase shifts, and hygrosensory mutant genetics - none of which is in the claim's scope at all. The two quantities are a word apart and are not the same quantity.

1. **"Unopened" was the entry's state, not the item's nature, and opening it changes the item's standing.** The item is now open and read in full text. 3. **A simulation was about to be treated as an instrumented log.** The only time-axis plot in Source A, Figure 4's CO2 trace, is CFD output. The paper's sole physical measurement is fog visualisation, and its sole duration figure ("more than 30 s") is attributed to author experience. If the entry wants an instrumented incubator channel, this paper **simulates** it and **visualises** a tracer; it does not measure one.

4. **Three query returns are one document.** The entry's "returned by all three adversaries' cell-culture queries" is **three title matches on one paper**, which is corroboration of nothing. I could not and did not verify the adversaries' query logs; I note only that the inference from repeated returns to independent support is unavailable. Separately, in my own searching, Source B surfaced as both a bioRxiv preprint and an npj paper: **two identifiers, one document**. An unopened-prior list that counts those as two is inflated, and this is itself a finding.

5. **Leg (ii) is an unenumerated universal and must be labelled as one.** The claim is of the form "nobody has published X". **Instances I opened:** Schuh et al. 2016 (10.1515/cdbme-2016-0073) and Chen et al. 2026 (10.1038/s44323-026-00082-4). **Instances I did not open:** everything in the list below. 6. **Sub-claim (b) is negative for every document I opened, and I want that stated precisely rather than generously.** Neither opened document prints a time series of an environmental channel over at least 24 hours inside an incubator. Source A's longest time axis is 15 s (Figure 4) with photographs to 30 s. 7. **A lead, declared as a guess and justified by kind before being named.** What kind of document could settle leg (ii)? One whose **purpose** is to characterise the environment of the box over many hours: an instrument-calibration or metrology paper on incubators, a facility-qualification or DIN 12880 / ISO performance study, or a chronobiology methods paper that logs its own incubator while running cell-autonomous bioluminescence. "Studies on Calibration of Medical Carbon Dioxide Incubator" (SciTePress, 2021) **is** that kind - a calibration study of a CO2 incubator is exactly where a multi-hour instrumented temperature and CO2 record would be printed if anywhere. I did not open it; my budget was gone. It is a **lead, not evidence**, and I decline to predict what is in it.

8. **One asymmetry worth flagging for whoever next works limb (a).** Source B's own Methods supply, incidentally, the mechanism the entry's extension would need: incubator humidifiers and heating/cooling systems produce an environmental artefact (there, vibration) strong enough to degrade a circadian entrainment result. That is a published statement that incubator subsystems perturb circadian experiments - but it is about vibration, is not periodic in the paper, carries no 24-hour amplitude, and is not cell-autonomous. It supports the **plausibility** of limb (a) while supplying none of its content.

## BEARING ON THE LEDGER ENTRY

**On leg (i): STRIKE it as an open risk, and correct how the entry carries it.** The TUM item is Schuh, Kirsch, Dillmann, Breuer and Eblenkamp (2016), *Current Directions in Biomedical Engineering* 2(1):329-332, DOI 10.1515/cdbme-2016-0073. It is read in full text. It is a CFD-plus-fog-visualisation study of incubator **door geometry** whose longest time axis is 15 seconds and whose photographs stop at 30 seconds, with no circadian, diurnal or 24-hour content of any kind and no instrumented environmental channel at all. By the entry's own stated test - "if the published work is only about recovery after door opening, with no 24-hour structure, the gap holds" - this item leaves the gap intact. The entry should record it as **opened and non-threatening**, and should fix the description: a DOI-bearing journal paper with five named authors, not "a TUM document", and a **simulation** of CO2 against time, not a log.

**On leg (ii): do not hold it as stated; NARROW it, and CONVERT the remainder into a promotion condition.** As written - "nobody has published an instrumented 24-hour periodic component for an incubator or chamber used in cell-autonomous circadian work" - it is an unenumerated universal resting on two opened documents, and it is additionally **too broad in one word**: "or chamber". On the chamber reading it is already in trouble, because Chen et al. 2026 instrument a chamber, impose a 24-hour periodic component in it, and tie it to circadian measurements. What saves the claim is not the chamber but the three other qualifiers, so the entry should carry them explicitly: **an incubator** (not an enclosure), an **unintended** component arising from the apparatus (not a programmed set-point), and a **cell-autonomous** preparation. Narrowed that way, nothing I opened contradicts it.

**Promotion condition, so this stops being re-litigated.** Promote limb (a) to a held gap only after someone opens the documents of the kind that could actually settle it and reports them negative: (1) "Studies on Calibration of Medical Carbon Dioxide Incubator" (SciTePress, 2021) or another incubator calibration / DIN 12880 / ISO performance study, which is the kind of document where a multi-hour instrumented temperature and CO2 record would be printed; (2) Supplementary Fig. S1 of 10.1038/s44323-026-00082-4, to see whether a logged 24-hour environmental trace with named axes and units is in fact printed there; (3) at least one chronobiology **methods** paper that logs its own incubator while running cell-autonomous bioluminescence. Until those are opened, the entry's correct label for leg (ii) is **unenumerated - true over the two instances opened, not established** - and it should not be cited as an absence result.

**One caution for the drafter.** Nothing in this note licenses the stronger sentence that incubator environments have never been instrumented. Source A names a standard (DIN 12880) and Source B names a logger, a controller and a specific environmental artefact of incubator subsystems; the literature those point into is real and I did not read it. My two reads bound what I checked, not what exists.
