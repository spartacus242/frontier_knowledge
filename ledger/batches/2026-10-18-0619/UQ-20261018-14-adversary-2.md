## Restatement in my own words

Take the public, dated list of accepted records for "train a GPT-2-class model to ≤3.28 FineWeb val loss on 8×H100 as fast as possible" (modded-nanogpt), which has fallen from ~45 min to ~90 s over ~77 accepted records. Each record was accepted on a single timed comparison against the previous best, while the acceptance rule's statistical test sits on the *loss* side, not the *time* side. So: if you went back and actually re-ran every record's own code — five or more times, on at least two different 8×H100 machines, once in the software stack of its own era and once in a single modern stack — how many of the individual "I made it faster" steps would still be distinguishable from machine-to-machine and run-to-run timing jitter at the same p<0.01 the rules demand of loss? Express the answer as a fraction of the series' total speedup, measured multiplicatively (log-seconds, because times compose). Separately: re-apply each accepted change at a bigger scale or a second loss target and report how much of its speedup is left. And count how many of the old records simply will not run any more, grouped by why.

Two things are being asked at once: (a) is the ledger's *time* axis a measurement at all, and (b) are the accepted changes general or selected on one fixed target.

## My own arithmetic (labelled as mine, on published point estimates)

Inputs: 45 min (record 1) and 2.933 min (record 21) from **Table E.1 of arXiv:2506.22419v2, which I opened**; ~1.43 min final and "77 records" from METR as **quoted in the candidate record — I did not open METR**, so those two carry only the record's authority.

- Total multiplicative speedup ln(45/1.43) = 3.449 (31.5×).
- Records 1→21 account for ln(45/2.933) = 2.731 = **79.2% of the total log-speedup**, in 20 step-downs, i.e. a mean of 14.6% per step.
- The remaining ~56 records carry 0.718 = **20.8%**, a mean of **1.29% per step**.
- Sensitivity to the one second-hand input: with a final time of 1.60 min the early block is 81.8% of the log total, with 1.30 min it is 77.0%. The conclusion is insensitive over any plausible value. Caveats carried: per-step distributions inside each block are unknown and heavy-tailed, so these means are not medians; the 77/21 split is the record's number, not mine.

Consequence, which is my strongest attack below: under the candidate's own declared accounting rule (V2(c), log-additive share), the answer to the headline question is bounded below at ≈0.79 by already-published times, *before any machine is booted*, unless the huge early step-downs fail — and if they fail it will be for environment/runnability reasons (arms A1/A2, P1), not because 14.6% hides inside H100 timing jitter. Any plausible run-to-run and node-to-node dispersion on 8×H100 is one to a few percent, which can only touch the ~21% tail.
