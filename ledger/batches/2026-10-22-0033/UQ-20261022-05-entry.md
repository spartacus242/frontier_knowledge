Domain(s): Biology — experimental methods and research-reagent practice (immunoassays in cell and molecular biology). Adjacent: measurement metrology, reporting standards, meta-research.
Question (canonical): For a pre-declared panel of commercial research antibodies, each pinned to a catalogue number and a dated datasheet of record: (i) is the vendor-recommended working dilution for a named application predicted by the product's stated stock concentration, or by a measurable binding constant plus the nominal target abundance in the recommended sample type, under a comparison declared in advance; and (ii) across a pre-declared sample of published immunoassays using that panel, in what fraction is the mass-per-volume or molar antibody concentration in the incubation computable at all from the paper plus that datasheet, and among papers quoting the same nominal dilution for the same catalogue number, what is the spread in computed concentration? [R1, adopted at Gate 1; V0 superseded there for well-posedness — both versions in the record.]
Alternative formulations: (a) Where does "1:1000" come from — the antibody's biophysics, or the bottle's packaging? (b) Biology's most-used reagent class is specified by a dilution rather than a concentration: how often is the dose recoverable from the published record, and is the recommended dilution a property of the antibody at all?
Motivating gap: Methods sections specify antibodies by dilution, and a dilution is a dose only if stock concentration is known — which many products do not state. The titration how-to literature tells users to optimise empirically and is silent on where the vendor's figure came from. Claim driving the candidate: two papers both reporting "1:1000" may differ in dose by orders of magnitude.
Presuppositions: P1 datasheets state a recommended dilution and state/omit stock concentration recordably — not measured here; no datasheet was opened by the record or by any of three adversaries. P2 binding parameters obtainable for enough of the panel — contested, no survey of affinity-data availability found. P3 a polyclonal has a single meaningful affinity — contested, handled by stratification. P4 the selling vendor sets the figure — contested, OEM relabelling; Voskuil 2014 (read by adversary 3) notes the same clone sold "in known mg/ml concentration" and "without any specifications". P5 dose materially affects results — partly established; NordiQC CD8 run 54 (read) shows optimal IHC across a typical 1:25–1:200 range when retrieval/detection are adjusted, which bounds it in that regime. P6 antibodies individuable across papers — bounded by prior A: catalogue numbers missing in 32%/20% of papers, lot numbers in 1%. P7 printed stock concentration accurate — unchecked. P8 stock concentration varies enough for the packaging model to have variance to explain — contested, unchecked. P9 (added by adversaries, not in the record) the recommended dilution is a scalar at all — contradicted in practice: Rockland (read) states manufacturers "often give dilution ranges"; CST (read) states it recommends "a specific dilution or dilution range" and prints no stock concentration.
What would count as an answer: a pre-registered two-part study — (i) regression of log recommended dilution, one row per product x application, on stated stock concentration vs. binding constant plus nominal target abundance, format and vendor as covariates, variance partitioned; (ii) a paper x dated-datasheet join reporting the computability fraction with the identifier-coverage bound, and the spread in computed concentration at fixed nominal dilution.

THIS REJECTION'S OWN VULNERABILITY, recorded as the standing rule requires: Leg A rests on something nobody has measured. It is an ALGEBRAIC identity in the candidate's text — stated stock concentration times quoted dilution, one datasheet per catalogue number, therefore one value — and not a bitwise or empirical finding: no agent has computed concentrations over any paper corpus, no datasheet of record was opened by the record or by any of the three adversaries, and no source reports that such computed values coincide. The identity holds only if a dated datasheet yields a unique scalar stock concentration, and that is precisely what is unmeasured: two opened vendor sources state that recommendations and specifications come as RANGES or are absent (Rockland: manufacturers "often give dilution ranges"; CST prints no concentration), and a datasheet printing a range, a ">" bound, a lot-dependent value, or a lyophilised mass reconstituted at a laboratory-chosen volume would make the computed dose non-unique and the spread empirically nonzero. The single unopened source most likely to settle it: Weller M, "Quality Issues of Research Antibodies", Analytical Chemistry Insights 2016 — the one review squarely on research-antibody documentation quality, unopened by the record and by two adversaries only because opus4.kobv.de serves an Anubis bot interstitial to this reader; try the SAGE journal route rather than that repository PDF.
