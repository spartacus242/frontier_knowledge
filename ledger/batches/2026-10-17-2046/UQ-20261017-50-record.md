Domain(s): AI architecture — training systems and optimization (pretraining recipe engineering; secondary: scaling-law and ablation methodology)

Question (canonical):
V1 (as intaken): Across published large-scale pretraining recipes that all declare the same nominal global gradient-clipping threshold, (a) at what fraction of optimizer steps is the clip actually active, and how does that fraction move over the course of a run, and (b) does the same nominal threshold denote the same intervention across a scaling ladder and across training stacks — given that stacks reduce the clipped norm over different parameter groupings and that gradient-norm scale varies with model size — so that holding 'clip = 1.0' fixed across a ladder in fact varies the constraint?

V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted; scored as canonical): Restricted to runs whose recipe declares clipping of a GLOBAL gradient norm at a nominal threshold c (c = 1.0 as the index case), and defining a step as CLIPPED when the pre-clip reduced norm exceeds c (equivalently, when the stack's clip coefficient is < 1): (a) what is the clipped-step fraction as a function of training progress, per run, estimated from released pre-clip gradient-norm series or from instrumented reproductions; and (b) under a pre-declared invariance band set from seed replicates, are BOTH the clipped-step fraction AND the realized-update deviation — the relative L2 (and cosine) deviation of the update actually applied from the update that would have been applied with clipping disabled, measured at matched checkpoints — invariant to (i) model scale along a ladder at fixed c and (ii) the parameter grouping over which the stack reduces the norm, at fixed c? If not invariant, what per-grouping, per-scale threshold c'(scale, grouping) reproduces a reference run's clipped-step fraction and realized-update deviation?
Reason for the reformulation: well-posedness only. V1 left "actually active" and "same intervention" undefined and let limb (b) be read as a claim about gradient-norm scaling (which Adam-family updates may wash out); V2 fixes the activation predicate on the clip coefficient, moves the endpoint onto the realized UPDATE rather than the gradient, and makes the deliverable an equivalence mapping with a pre-declared invariance band.

Alternative formulations:
- When a recipe says "gradient clipping 1.0", how often does it clip, and does it mean the same thing at 1B parameters and at 70B?
- Is the gradient-clipping threshold a hyperparameter that scaling ladders and architecture ablations hold fixed in name while varying in effect?

Motivating gap:
A dense-how, empty-why region. The clipping threshold appears in essentially every published LLM pretraining recipe, almost always at the nominal 1.0, and the literature is thick with methods for clipping BETTER — ZClip (arXiv:2504.02507), AdaGC (arXiv:2502.11034), SPAMP (arXiv:2510.01578), spectral clipping (arXiv:2605.11838) — while nothing found describes what the shipped constant does in the runs that use it. AdaGC (read at first hand per intake, arxiv.org/html/2502.11034v2) motivates adaptive clipping by two named mismatches of a single global threshold — temporal ("The optimal global clipping threshold typically decreases over the course of training") and spatial (gradient statistics vary across tensors, so "a single global threshold" under-protects some and over-constrains others) — and compares granularities (Table 2: Global / Element / Unit / Tensor), yet reports NO clipping-activation rate for any run, makes NO argument about the threshold's tightness as a function of parameter count, and does NOT treat the same nominal threshold under different groupings as a comparability problem. The activation rate is a quantity large runs compute at every step and nobody publishes — and this run established that it is literally instrumented in shipped open-source training code (see Presuppositions, P1).

Presuppositions:
P1. The clipping-activation rate is recoverable from archives rather than needing fresh runs. Flagged CONTESTED at intake; now LARGELY ESTABLISHED for OLMo-family stacks from code read at first hand (huggingface.co/NousResearch/OLMo-Bitnet-1B/blob/main/optim.py): the optimizer's `clip_grads_and_collect_metrics` computes `total_grad_norm` and derives `clip_coef = max_norm / (total_grad_norm + 1e-6)`, so the logged `total_grad_norm` IS the pre-clip norm and the clipped-step indicator is recoverable by thresholding it; and the code already logs an explicit `clipping_rate = num_grads_clipped / num_eligible_grads`. Two residual limitations, both honest: (i) that metric block runs only when `collect_param_metrics=True`, and whether it was enabled and released for the headline OLMo runs is UNVERIFIED here; (ii) under `_do_global_fixed_clipping` the code sets `num_grads_clipped = len(group["params"])` whenever the clip fires, so `clipping_rate` degenerates to a per-step 0/1 indicator of the global clip — which is exactly the candidate's activation predicate, but is NOT a per-tensor rate, so the published name invites misreading. HF Trainer's logged `grad_norm` is also believed pre-clip (it is the return value of clip_grad_norm_); NOT VERIFIED from source in this run.
P2. Published recipes disclose the threshold and enough of the stack to identify the grouping over which the norm is reduced. Partly CONTESTED (stack VERSION, not just stack name, decides this). Supported in direction by AdaGC's own statement that GlobalGC "needs all-reduce across DP, TP, and PP groups" whereas per-tensor clipping "only needs an all-reduce within each TP group" — i.e. the grouping is a legible property of the stack.
P3. Gradient global norm scales with model size so that a fixed threshold is differentially tight. CONTESTED and central; V2 removes the dependence by making the endpoint the realized UPDATE. For Adam-family optimizers the update is approximately scale-free even where the gradient is not, so if the realized-update deviation is invariant, limb (b)(i) dissolves and the candidate retains only limb (a) and limb (b)(ii) (grouping). This is the candidate's main structural risk and is deliberately left as a measurable rather than an assumption.
P4. Scaling ladders and architecture ablations do hold the threshold nominally fixed across rungs. Checkable from published recipes; ZClip's own baseline practice (c = 1.0, fixed) and the ubiquity of 1.0 in recipes support it; not systematically enumerated here.
P5. If the constraint varies across a ladder, that is a defect for the ladder's purpose — ladders exist to extrapolate or to attribute an effect to the treatment. Checkable; adjacent to ledger entries UQ-20261016-53 and UQ-20261017-28, from which this differs in OBJECT (the clipping operator, not ablation design matrices or batch formation).
P6. Clipping is applied to a norm rather than per-element value clipping in the recipes in frame. Checkable; mixed practice exists (AdaGC's Table 2 includes an Element granularity), and V2 restricts the frame to global-NORM declarations.

What would count as an answer:
Per-run clipped-step fraction as a function of training progress, from pre-clip gradient-norm logs or instrumented reproductions, for at least two stacks at at least three scales under one nominal threshold; a measurement of the realized post-clip update's deviation from the unclipped update (relative L2 and cosine at matched checkpoints), not merely the trigger rate; and an equivalence mapping stating which per-grouping threshold reproduces a given activation rate and update deviation across stacks and scales. "Same intervention" if activation rate and update deviation are invariant to scale and grouping within a pre-declared replicate-noise band — a null that would positively license the field's practice of copying the constant across a ladder. "Not the same intervention" otherwise, with the equivalence mapping as the deliverable that lets a ladder hold the CONSTRAINT, rather than the NUMBER, fixed. A finding that the clip is inactive at almost every step in some scale or stack regime would answer the descriptive limb and make the constant vestigial there.

Significance — consequence:
If the clipped-step fraction is substantial and moves with scale or grouping at fixed c, then every scaling ladder and architecture ablation that copies "clip = 1.0" across rungs has been varying an active constraint along the very axis it extrapolates over, which is a confound in the attribution the ladder exists to produce, and recipes would have to report realized clipping (a free number) and ladders to equalise it rather than the nominal constant. If the clip is inactive at almost every step in some regime, the constant is vestigial there: a line copied into essentially every published recipe can be dropped, and — more consequentially — the stability improvements attributed to it in those runs are misattributed, which bears directly on what the spike-mitigation method family is actually improving on. If both endpoints are invariant within the replicate band, that is a useful null that positively licenses current practice and removes clipping from the list of things hyperparameter-transfer work must handle. All three outcomes change a reporting convention; two change a design practice.

Significance — audience:
(1) Pretraining-recipe engineers who run multi-rung ladders to choose an architecture or a data mixture — they would add one logged scalar per step and, if limb (b) is non-null, equalise realized clipping across rungs instead of the nominal number. (2) The scaling-law and ablation-methodology community, for whom this is another instance of a nominally-held-fixed-but-varying treatment (cf. UQ-20261016-53, UQ-20261017-28). (3) Authors of adaptive-clipping methods — ZClip, AdaGC, SPAMP all baseline against fixed clipping at 1.0 while reporting nothing about how often that baseline binds, so their effect sizes currently sit on an uncharacterised control; A literally lists "clipping frequency" among its stability metrics and prints no value. (4) Maintainers of open runs (OLMo, Marin, LLM360, Apertus), who could publish the series at essentially zero cost — OLMo's optimizer already computes `clipping_rate` and logs the pre-clip `total_grad_norm`. (5) Stack maintainers (Megatron-LM, torchtitan, DeepSpeed, Levanter), for whom an equivalence mapping is the thing that makes a recipe portable between groupings.

Significance — fecundity:
Whether clip activation explains part of the loss-spike literature's unexplained variance; whether the clip is doing work currently attributed to warmup, z-loss or QK-norm (A explicitly groups clipping and warmup as mechanisms "controlling the effective update scale", so disentangling them becomes askable); whether "clip = c" belongs inside the muP-style hyperparameter-transfer framework at all, which prescribes no transfer rule for it; whether the right reporting unit for any saturating training-time operator is its realized activation rate rather than its nominal bound (a convention that would generalise to loss-scaling, update clipping, router capacity factors and RL ratio clipping); and whether a recipe's portability between stacks should be certified by matched realized constraints rather than matched config files.

Significance — anchoring:
The loss-spike and training-instability literature; the adaptive-clipping method family, which is motivated by two named mismatches (AdaGC's temporal and spatial) that it never measures for the shipped constant; hyperparameter transfer across scale (muP and successors) and its silence on clipping; the recurring "ladders hold it fixed in name, not in effect" family in this ledger (UQ-20261016-53 ablation design matrices, UQ-20261017-28 batch-formation design effect), from which this differs in object; and the DP clipping-bias literature, which is both an anchor and the principal novelty risk.

Reason for silence:
An incentive gap crossed with an instrumentation-ownership boundary — not "no one thought of it". The number is a dashboard quantity rather than a result: it is computed inside every large run at every step (OLMo's shipped optimizer computes `clipping_rate` and `total_grad_norm` outright), consulted while firefighting a run, and then never written down, because a stability diagnostic with no headline attached is not publishable and recipe papers publish recipes, not diagnostics. The people who DO publish about clipping are optimizer-method authors, whose incentive is to show their method beats the fixed baseline, not to characterise what the baseline does — which is why A can list "clipping frequency" as a metric and print no value, and why B can assert that the optimal threshold "depends on the particular model" without measuring the dependence. Limb (b) additionally requires reading two codebases' reduction groupings and running a matched ladder: a systems-engineering comparability task that is nobody's paper, sits between the optimization-theory community (which owns the operator) and the training-systems community (which owns the grouping), and has no venue that rewards a null. The RL contrast is the sharpest evidence that this is an incentive artefact rather than a conceptual one: the same community logs and publishes a clip fraction for the policy-RATIO clip as a matter of course, and never for the gradient-norm clip.
