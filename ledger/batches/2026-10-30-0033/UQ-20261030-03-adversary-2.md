## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take one lytic virus and one permissive adherent human cell line. Infect cells one at a time, synchronously, at matched multiplicity, within a fixed window after each cell has been watched dividing; then lyse each cell on its own and count how many *infectious* virions (PFU) it made.

Two numbers are wanted:
- **(i)** How unequal is that per-cell output across cells — expressed as the negative-binomial dispersion parameter *k*, with a confidence interval (and the implied top-decile share).
- **(ii)** How much of that inequality is already fixed before the virus arrives — measured as how much more alike two just-divided sister cells are in log output than matched non-sister cells of the same cycle phase and exposure (an intraclass correlation against that matched null).

The headline claim is that (i) and (ii) together split the spread of per-cell infectious output into a part set by the cell's prior state and a part generated after entry.
