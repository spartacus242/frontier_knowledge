## Verification of the kill at first hand (post-batch, 2026-10-21)

Two fresh-context readers, each handed one source and the claim made of it, neither handed any reviewer's reasoning. **The verdict moved: Rejected to Provisional.**

**Leg 1 falls.** Prosperi et al. 2023 was read in full at the TU Delft repository. It has ROC curves in its title, defines TPR and FPR in equations (2) and (3), plots eight ROC panels with the AUC shaded — and prints **not one absolute discrimination number**. No AUC value, no sensitivity, no specificity, no TPR or FPR, no PPV appears in its text, tables or figure captions; Table 1 is an empty symbolic TN/FP/FN/TP template with no counts; the "best sensitivity-pair" is a black dot whose coordinates are never given; no damage prevalence is stated, which forecloses PPV even in principle. The six percentages in its section 5.3 (14/14/7 shallow, 6/3/1 deep) are relative increases over differential settlement, ambiguous in the source between absolute percentage-point and relative differences, and Table 5's percentages are fragility-curve damage probabilities at the optimised threshold, not discrimination statistics. So the judge's Leg 1 — that the paper *publishes* the candidate's central estimand — does not hold. What holds is that the paper *performs* a ROC analysis and derives its own optimal cut-offs from it.

**And the deciding finding, which no pass had made explicit: the classical limits are never scored as classifiers.** Skempton–MacDonald [46] and Burland–Wroth [26] are cited for DEFINITIONS only and their limiting values are never evaluated, never even stated as numbers; Boscardin–Cording's 3.3 permille is run THROUGH the fragility curve to yield 5% (shallow) and 2% (deep), which is the opposite direction of travel from classification. No TPR or FPR is computed at 3.3 permille. On the question of whether anyone has scored the published settlement limits as damage classifiers on real buildings, this paper is silent rather than supportive of either answer, and it was the closest-looking candidate in the field.

**Leg 2 falls too.** Peduto, Korff, Nicodemo, Marchese & Ferlisi 2019 (Soils and Foundations 59:380–397) was read in full. The factual core the entry claimed holds exactly: 706 buildings, satellite displacement joined to surveyed damage at the individual-building unit, four Dutch municipalities, 180 shallow and 526 mainly-wooden-piled. But it reports fragility parameters and nothing resembling a diagnostic-test evaluation: ROC, AUC, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, false positives and confusion matrices are all absent, and its one use of the word "accuracy" means DInSAR measurement accuracy against levelling. Skempton–MacDonald is cited, not applied — its rotation is a DInSAR footprint profile slope, not an angular distortion between adjacent frame points — and Burland–Wroth is used at their definition only for D2/L2, with D1/L1 the paper's own simplified approach. Its instrument is bespoke TerraSAR-X PSI (2009–2014, SkyGeo), damage surveyed 2015–2016, exterior facades only, D4 and D5 merged, beta constrained by the authors; the European Ground Motion Service postdates it by three years, so it cannot be cited for what a free continent-scale service delivers.

**What the verdict now rests on.** Gate 4 is complete — two independent adversaries, both Disguise sweeps executed, nothing Unresolved on attacks 1, 2 or 4 — and what fell is the JUDGE's Gate 2 call, not an adversary's ground, so no late Gate 4 is owed and the entry is re-graded on the existing record. Novelty is Medium with a close neighbour: Prosperi works the same object, the same buildings and the same predictor, and publishes optimised cut-offs (beta 4.77 and 3.67 mm/m) of the same order as the literature value, which is close even though it scores no published threshold and reports no discrimination number. Provisional, not Validated: adversary 2 left attack 7 (reason for silence) Unresolved, reading the same evidence as a question half-asked in Prosperi's own paper, and well-posedness stands at 2/3.

**Corrections to this entry's own record, none of which changes the verdict.** The claim that 262 Schiedam cases appear in both datasets was already flagged unverified and remains so; Prosperi's 262 Schiedam cases sit adjacent to 264 piled-foundation buildings and the paper never reconciles them, so the two numbers must not be read as the same group. Prosperi's instrument carries a stated directional bias — displacements "of only a few points are gathered at fixed, limited intervals with manual bed-joint levelling readings, thus suggesting that damage appears at higher values of distortion" — and its causal attribution is an assumption: "all the visual damage to the surveyed buildings was assumed to be caused by the settlements". Its bed-joint campaign (2003) predates the damage information by a decade. Satellite data is present in Prosperi only as discussion and as a comparison attributed to Peduto et al., so neither "this paper validates InSAR-derived distortions" nor "satellite data is absent from it" is right. Peduto's served text carries OCR damage in Table 3, so no rotation or deflection-ratio median should be quoted from this read.

The entry's citation was correct throughout — Soils and Foundations 59 (2019), five authors including Ferlisi. The defect was in the brief, not in the ledger. This is the third pass in which a brief written from something other than the entry's full citation manufactured a defect that does not exist.
