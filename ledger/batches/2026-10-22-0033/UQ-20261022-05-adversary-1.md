## Restatement in my own words

Two separate questions wearing one coat. **(i)** Take a fixed list of catalogue-numbered commercial antibodies with a dated datasheet each. For a named application (WB, IHC, ...), is the dilution the vendor prints ("1:1000") better explained by how concentrated the vial is, or by the antibody's binding constant together with how much target is in the recommended sample? **(ii)** Across a fixed sample of papers that used those products: in what share of papers can you actually compute the antibody mass-per-volume in the incubation from the paper plus that datasheet, and — among papers quoting the same dilution of the same catalogue number — how much does the computed concentration vary?

The record's own bet is that (i) comes out "vial", making the dilution a packaging artefact, and (ii) comes out "rarely computable". I do not trust that framing and treat both as open.

## Strongest killing point

The Gate 1 reformulation built a degenerate quantity into limb (ii): one datasheet-of-record concentration per catalogue number times the same nominal dilution gives the same computed concentration for every paper, so the "spread among papers quoting the same dilution for the same catalogue number" is zero by construction wherever it is computable — and the only escape (lot-specific concentration) is closed by prior A's printed 1% lot-reporting rate. Secondary: CST's own account ("provided for a specific number of assays"; "low concentration ... means ... high affinity") makes stock concentration a function of affinity, so the candidate's "packaging vs biophysics" contest may have no two sides.
