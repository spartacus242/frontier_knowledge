Verification (post-batch, three fresh-context checkers, logs UQ-20261017-23-verification-dualgrade.md, -aisc.md and -filter.md): **Rejected stands, and the fatal attack is better sourced than it was — two of its three counts have gone from unopened search-result titles to first-hand sources, while the third stays unsourced.** This entry's verdict turned on a disagreement between its two adversaries: the first graded attack 4 Unresolved, which is fatal, and the second graded the same attack Partial. Verification went at the first adversary's three counts directly.
**Dual certification is real and the field calls it common.** The first adversary's strongest count, which it was careful to label as supported only by unopened titles, now has primary sources. AISC's own FAQ 1.3.7 states that "Certificates of conformance and material test reports for materials that comply completely with more than one standard will indicate those standards", that "These materials are considered 'multi-certified.'", and — decisively for the mass balance — that "The practice is common and an indication of well-controlled processes." AISC Steel Interchange (May 1994) records a producer making "dual grade" A36/A572 Gr 50 material, where "This 'dual grade' capability was achieved by careful selection and control of steel chemistry", and a current producer datasheet offers material that "Can be dual certified as A572 Grade 50/CSA G40.21 50W upon request". So a heat can sit in two grades at once without any downgrade having occurred.
**Conformance is multivariate, also confirmed at first hand.** A992 carries an upper yield limit as well as a lower one, a tensile minimum, "a specified maximum yield-to-tensile ratio of 0.85" and "a specified maximum carbon equivalent of 0.47%", and A572 Gr 50 adds elongation limits (21% in 2 in., 18% in 8 in.). A heat can therefore fail a grade on something other than yield strength, which is enough on its own to open the yield-space balance the Gate 1 reformulation depends on.
**The third count stays unsourced, and so does the candidate's own premise — which is the finding.** Nothing the checker read establishes a leakage rate (scrap, off-grade sale, re-rolling), and nothing establishes that dual certification removes downgrading: as the checker put it, a heat in the overlap of two specifications is a heat that meets both, and a heat meeting only the lower grade is simply certified to the lower grade. That half of the objection rests on inference, not on a source, and is marked so. But the symmetry matters more than the gap: eight queries found **no published rate** of downgrading, re-certification or off-grade sale, and no source at all describing the re-certification of a failed heat into a lower grade. The candidate's own premise is as undocumented as the objection to it. And the fallback the record offered — heat-level retest and re-certification flags, which would make the balance unnecessary — is confirmed absent from the documented certificate contents: AISC FAQ 1.3.1 points to ASTM A6 Section 14, whose required content "includes but is not limited to the heat number, steel grade, and nominal sizes supplied", with no retest or re-certification field anywhere in what was read. A6's own clause text on "Retests", "Test Reports" and "Retreatment" was not obtained, and EN 10204 could not be read, so that negative is incomplete — but the circularity the first adversary charged is intact: the question is well-posed only if data exist that would make it unnecessary.
**P1 is correctly quoted, and the attack-6 arithmetic is vindicated by P1's own wording rather than by the arithmetic.** Both of this entry's quotations from P1 appear as printed, with the reason the entry omitted: the distribution "may be truncated at Fy = 50 ksi, or ln(Fy) = 3.91," because "the sample Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) is nearly vertical at that point". The sample (207 coupons, 38 heats, 8 shapes, 3 producers) and the observed-to-certificate ratio (mean 1.002, CoV 0.044) are confirmed. The citation is completed: Bartlett, Dexter, Graeser, Jelinek, Schmidt & Galambos, Engineering Journal 2003;40(1):2-14. Three corrections. The database size is internally inconsistent in P1 itself — about 25,000 mill test reports in the introduction, 20,259 data points in a figure, 20,295 coupons in Tables 3 and 5 — so no single figure should be cited without that caveat. Ellingwood is cited there as Ellingwood, Galambos, MacGregor & Cornell (1980), not as a sole author. And P1's reliability numbers are: beta 2.52 at L/D = 3 under the original calibration, 2.61-2.77 for phi = 0.9 and 2.37-2.54 for phi = 0.95, with the conclusion that "an increase of resistance factor from 0.90 to 0.95 is not recommended without further study". Confirmed on the decisive point: P1's reliability analysis uses A992 alone, models no adjacent grade jointly, and lets no truncation or selection correction enter beta. On direction, the first adversary's illustrative arithmetic — that neglecting the truncation is conservative, the opposite of this record's claim — is its own, unsourced, and is marked so; but P1's own words carry the same direction, since it calls the neglect "conservatively, neglects any truncation", and that is a first-hand source. The record's claim that the neglect understates lower-tail mass is contradicted by the paper it cites.
**The concrete filter effect exists; the steel analogue does not, and that is the batch's most useful by-product.** Confirmed: Caspeele's HERON 59 paper uses the term, where "this filter effect can be quantified using Bayesian updating techniques" (abstract level only), and Lux, Feiri, Schulze-Ardey, Hegger, Classen & Ricker, Scientific Reports 2026;16:8277 (full text) treats "the filtering effect of conformity control in reliability-based assessments" by updating JCSS Probabilistic Model Code priors with an operating-characteristic likelihood and computing beta by FORM, finding beta up by as much as about 10% and sigma falling from 5.81 to 4.89 N/mm2. Four steel-vocabulary searches found **no steel analogue at all** — a countable negative at title and snippet level, since no steel page was opened. No source states a direction in terms for either material, and no source gives an empirical rejection or reclassification rate. Melcher et al. 2004 is verified verbatim in its full PDF on both strings: the S355 distribution "is asymmetrical, evidently due to the exclusion of values not satisfying the requirements of material standards for that tensile strength class, and therefore they are reclassified to lower tensile strength classes", and "the probability of not maintaining the assumed yield strength nominal value, 355 MPa, is about 8.6%", "this being worse than the supposed 5%". It gives no reclassification rate, never shows how the 8.6% was obtained, and fits S235 and S355 separately.
