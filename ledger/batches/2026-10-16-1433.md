## The rejection rate and what it is allowed to mean

80% (4 of 5), against 60% as the batch returned, and against 0%, 40% and 40% in the three preceding batches. The direction is the opposite of the recent trend and so is the cause: the three preceding rates fell because verification dissolved kills, and this one rose because verification *supplied* one.

That makes it a better number than its predecessors but still a number about the reviewing, not about the candidates. Specifically: UQ-39's rejection was available to the batch and was missed for a mechanical reason — PMC and Europe PMC are hard-blocked for this session, and seven attempts routed through them where one DOI-to-publisher call would have settled it. A ledger whose novelty grade can be set by which hostname answers is not yet measuring novelty. The fix is a routing rule, now recorded: **try the DOI to the publisher first, not last.**

Two further things the rate hides. UQ-39 was within one grade of Validated on everything but novelty, so the batch's most careful work produced a rejection, which is the system working. And UQ-40's rejection is now a much weaker thing than it was six hours ago: not "this has been answered" but "one sentence in one preprint raises it and nobody has run it".
