## Restatement (my own words, before reading the record's framing twice)

When a pretraining run drops its learning rate to zero at the end — the cooldown / annealing / "mid-training" stage — downstream benchmark scores jump. The candidate asks what that jump is *made of*. Specifically: take two or more open runs that released checkpoints densely through the cooldown, score them all on one frozen harness, and split each benchmark's cooldown-phase score change into two parts. Part one is the part you can fake without training: take the checkpoint from *before* the cooldown, leave its weights alone, and apply a fixed output-side correction fitted on held-out items — rescale the temperature, score the options by length-normalised or rank-based likelihood instead of raw label probability, fix the label-emission format. However much of the cooldown's score change that recovers is "sharpening/format." Part two is whatever is left over, checked against a probe panel declared in advance and held identical across checkpoints, and called "knowledge." Then the payoff limb: re-rank at least three published annealing data mixes using only part two, and see whether the ordering that the field used to pick its shipped mix survives.

In one sentence: *did the cooldown teach the model more, or teach it to answer in the shape the scorer rewards — and if it is the latter, were the published mix ablations ranking shape?*

## Pages opened (4 fetches)

1. **https://blog.pebblous.ai/blog/output-format-confounds-data-quality/en/** — first-party executive summary of arXiv:2609.02015, the record's "single most dangerous unread source." Scope now established: **instruction tuning only** ("Instruction tuning rests on two judgments"; Qwen3.5-4B/9B and Mistral-7B-v0.3 "all trained through low-rank adapters"). Pretraining, LR cooldown and annealing are **not mentioned**. It reports **no share or fraction** of a benchmark change. It **does not re-rank candidate datasets or mixes** and **does not re-examine published data-quality ablations**. Its instrument is **fine-tuning on format** (a 4×4 format-transfer map of adapters) — i.e. weight-updating, the thing V2 explicitly disqualifies. Magnitudes it does give: format alone moved an untrained model by up to **70 points on ARC-C**; "the average shift was still 22.5 points" across six tasks under four formats; the gold-label oracle's gain fell from **25.2 points on the training format to 7.4 points on held-out formats**. (*My own arithmetic on those two numbers: ≈71% of that measured gain did not survive a format change. The source states no uncertainty on either figure and the regime is LoRA SFT on 6 tasks, not a cooldown, so this is an order-of-magnitude indication only, not a transferable estimate.*)
2. **https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06826v1** — "Mid-Training of Large Language Models: A Survey", a *different* survey from the one the record cites. Explains the decay-stage improvement by optimization and data quality alone: "lowering the learning rate mitigates gradient variance and stabilizes convergence near favorable minima" and "shifting toward more curated or synthetic corpora enhances the marginal utility of each additional token" (Intro); information-bottleneck and curriculum accounts in §II. On the candidate's question: "The text contains no such sentence. It never raises formatting, calibration, or evaluation-format artifacts as an alternative explanation for annealing-stage gains." Does not mention "microanneal" or name a metric for annealing-mixture ablations. *Caveat recorded:* §VI (Evaluation) and §VII (Future Work) were **not in the retrieved excerpt**, so I cannot certify the question is absent from its future-work list.
3. **https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14893v1** — "LLMs as Signal Detectors: Sensitivity, Bias, and the Temperature-Criterion Analogy". Unit is "three LLMs as observers performing factual discrimination across 168,000 trials" — three *fixed released models*, swept over seven sampling temperatures. "The paper does not analyze training checkpoints or training phases"; pretraining, LR decay, cooldown and annealing are never mentioned. It does not decompose any accuracy *change* into sensitivity and criterion parts and quantifies no share. It states the premise the candidate needs — "aggregate accuracy conflates sensitivity with criterion placement" — and, for a sensitivity rise, "It does not indicate improved factual knowledge." Load-bearing for my strongest attack: "**Accuracy actually decreased with temperature in all conditions**" (Llama-3-Base on TriviaQA 59.2% at T=0.1 → 33.6% at T=2.0), and temperature "**changes the generated answer itself, not only the confidence assigned to it**."
