# UQ-20261016-12 — Adversary #1 log (Gate 4, fresh context)

## Restatement (my own words)
Open-weight instruct models on the Hugging Face hub are git repositories. After the weight files stop changing, maintainers keep committing to the small text files that decide how a prompt is tokenised and formatted and when generation stops (tokenizer files, chat template, generation_config, special-token maps). The candidate asks two things: (a) across the most-downloaded instruct repos, how often does such a post-weight-freeze commit actually change rendered token sequences or stop behaviour (tested by rendering prompts, not by diffing text); and (b) if you evaluate the earliest and latest such revision with byte-identical weights and everything else pinned, how big are the score differences, relative to (i) run-to-run noise measured in the same study and (ii) the size of improvements that model-comparison papers typically present as evidence? The implied claim is that "Model X" without a commit hash does not identify the system under test.
