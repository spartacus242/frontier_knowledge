Question (canonical) — R1, adopted at Gate 1: For a pre-specified set of open-weight releases R, over the population A(R) of publicly available derivative artifacts whose lineage includes R — partitioned in advance into (i) format-only requantizations, (ii) fine-tunes, (iii) merges, (iv) behaviour-modification derivatives (abliterated/"uncensored"), with a declared rule for multi-class artifacts and for merges with parents outside R — and with the PRIMARY weight being each artifact's cumulative hub download count at a pinned census date (per-file where exposed, per-repo with a stated within-repo allocation rule otherwise): how large is D = E_w[m(a)] − m(ref), where m is each of a pre-specified capability suite and a pre-specified refusal/jailbreak-resistance measure, m(ref) is measured on the pinned reference weights through the SAME inference engine and precision path as the class being compared (format-matched control), each component of D is judged against a pre-declared decision margin, and D is reported with CIs, a stated census coverage error, sensitivity across >=3 alternative weightings, and a variance decomposition into between-class and within-class (uploader) components?

Alternative formulations:
- [V0] For a given open-weight release, what is the download-weighted distribution of decision-relevant metrics over the derivative artifacts actually obtained, and how far does that population expectation sit from the value reported on the reference weights?
- What is the gap between the model that is evaluated and the models that are run?

Motivating gap: New-instrument claim — the derivative graph became enumerable (base_model lineage, weight-similarity recovery) and weightable (hub download counts) only recently, so a POPULATION quantity is estimable where the literature reports only reference-weight or author-produced-quantization properties. Release decisions and open-weight safety cases are made about reference weights. Cited as evidence of silence: the ATOM Report (arXiv:2604.07190) uses hub downloads but excludes GGUF/MLX re-uploads from derivative counts and never mentions merges; TMLR 2026 asserts "thousands of open-weight text models have been specifically fine-tuned to disable safeguards" with no measurement and no citation. NOTE: adversary 3 showed the new-instrument half of this gap is overstated (see Adversarial log, attacks 2 and 7).

Presuppositions: P1 downloads proxy use (CONTESTED; R1 defuses the well-posedness half by defining the estimand ON downloads, leaving a significance risk). P2 population enumerable with measured coverage error. P3 hundreds of artifacts evaluable comparably under pinned engines per format. P4 reference weights are a fixed artifact (CONTESTED — inherits UQ-20261016-12's post-release mutation hazard). P5 within-class between-uploader spread exists (partly posed in llama.cpp #13736/#5856 and partly measured by practitioners; subordinate). P6 a population gap would be decision-relevant. P7 suite metrics meaningful on narrow fine-tunes and foreign-parent merges (CONTESTED).

What would count as an answer: (a) a census of A(R) with download weights and measured coverage error; (b) a pinned evaluation of a class- and weight-stratified sample, each artifact in its format's engine against the format-matched reference control; (c) D per metric with CIs; (d) sensitivity across >=3 weightings; (e) a between-class / within-class variance decomposition. YES: some component of D exceeds its pre-declared margin with mass in a nameable class. NO: every component falls inside the reference measurement's own interval and the between-class term is negligible, vindicating reference-weight evaluation as a population proxy. Both branches close the question.

Verification (post-batch, fresh-context checker, log UQ-20261016-54-verification.md): the Model Pulse characterisation that carried the batch's novelty downgrade is PARTLY CONFIRMED, and in the part that mattered it is wrong.
Confirmed: a public tracker called Model Pulse does exist — run by Tardelli Stekel, independent of Hugging Face, Apache-2.0, at github.com/tardellirs/modelpulse with a site at modelpulse.ifsp.dev and a Hugging Face Space. It builds derivative family trees from base_model links and reports how much of a model's reach comes from quantizations, fine-tunes, adapters and merges, over daily snapshots of the hub-stats parquet (rolling 30-day series from 2024-07-29, all-time totals from 2025-02-27).
Not confirmed: the three figures the entry quoted as its evidence — derivatives 43% of hub downloads, quantizations 28%, Qwen3-8B 37% — appear nowhere in the README or repo pages the checker opened, and no census date accompanies them. A report linked from the README was not opened, so they may exist there; until it is read, those numbers are not attributable and have been struck from this entry.
Decisive: Model Pulse measures downloads, likes, ranks and milestones ONLY. It records no capability score, no refusal measure and no jailbreak-resistance measure. So it instruments the census half of this question and has no behavioural endpoint at all, which is the half the candidate's estimand D is about. "A public instrument already does exactly this" was therefore a prior that does a different thing — the dominant error mode on this ledger, found again here.
Also established, and adverse: Hugging Face counts a download as a server-side request (GET or HEAD) to a query file, and for GGUF repositories every .gguf file counts as a query file; per-file counts are not documented as exposed. That is now the entry's second standing objection rather than a footnote.

Scores: Well-posedness 3/3 (contested to 2/3 by adversary 3, unresolved) · Significance 2/3
Strongest remaining objection (restated after verification; the late adversary's, sourced): the headline estimand D is a NET difference of means, and on-regime evidence says a net delta is the least informative summary of exactly the artifact class that carries this population's download mass. arXiv:2608.15046, "Certifying Compressed Language Models: An Audit and a Statistical Toolkit", over 1,707 compressed-versus-original model-by-task cells, reports that median per-item churn is about five times the median net accuracy delta, and that the net quantity "is least informative when two models are most alike". That paper's scope is compression against the original on capability benchmarks at 1.3B-405B — this candidate's class (i) and its capability metric — and does NOT cover fine-tunes, merges, abliteration, the refusal endpoint or any download weighting, so the refusal limb and the magnitude limb survive it. What it kills is the question's NO branch: a null D would not "vindicate reference-weight evaluation as a population proxy", because per-item churn can be large while the means coincide. Half the claimed significance goes with that inference, and the entry no longer rests on it.
Second objection, compounding and absent from the record's P1: the primary weight is a per-file server-side request counter, and for GGUF repositories every .gguf file counts as a query file while per-file counts are not documented as exposed, so weight multiplicity differs systematically between the GGUF class and single-file safetensors classes — contaminating the very comparison that decides where the download mass sits.
