Question (canonical) - V2: For >=2 published cross-day unit matchers at declared operating points, applied to chronic high-density extracellular recordings in which unit identity is labelled by at least one construction that does NOT use the functional response later used to measure tuning, (a) what are the matcher's false-match and split/merge rates, (b) what apparent change do those rates ALONE induce in >=2 published drift statistics under a declared null of zero true drift — the matching-error drift floor — and (c) do published drift magnitudes, matched on brain area and interval, exceed that floor? [This is the Gate 1 reformulation, the single permitted one, and the version Gates 2-5 were run against; the original per-paper decomposition wording is recorded verbatim in ledger/batches/2026-10-23-0033/UQ-20261023-01-record.md.]
Alternative formulations: How much of representational drift is a bookkeeping error about which neuron is which? / What drift floor is implied by cross-day unit-matching error alone, and do published drift magnitudes exceed it?
Motivating gap: cross-day matchers declare operating points and name the confound in their own discussions; nobody prices it into a drift estimate. Confirmed verbatim at full-text read level in both field-standard matchers (UnitMatch: the AUC decline "could be explained either by a decrease in the quality of matching, or changes in functional properties of the units"; EMD/eLife 92495: "since the accuracy is very high (Figure 4), the impact of mismatches is low" — an assertion scoped to a 29% reference subset the same paper says "will not apply to the set of all KSgood pairs"). The record aimed Gate 4 at its own strongest point. (NEW, from adversary 1) direction of bias: misses and splits select a waveform-stable subset and push measured drift DOWN while false matches push it UP, so "floor" presupposes a one-signed bias; an answer must report both components.
What would count as an answer: measured false-match and split/merge rates for >=2 matchers at declared operating points against >=1 declared function-independent identity labelling with its coverage stated; a propagation under a declared zero-drift null giving the apparent change those rates alone produce in >=2 published drift statistics, with CIs; and a comparison against >=2 published drift magnitudes matched on area and interval. A CLEAN NEGATIVE — floor small relative to published drift at all plausible rates and all constructions — settles it the other way and retires the concern. Both branches are informative, which is what keeps it a question rather than an agenda.
