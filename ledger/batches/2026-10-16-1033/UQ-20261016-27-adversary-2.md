## Restatement in my own words

Take one vaccine product and one national registry in which each administered dose carries its manufacturing lot
number and each recipient can be followed for an outcome (infection, or severe disease). Throw away the
unvaccinated entirely. Among recipients only, fit a multiplicative (log-link) risk model with a random intercept
for lot, nested inside the shipment/consignment block the lot went out in, and adjust for vaccination date, time
since dose, dose number, place and person-level covariates. The question is about one number from that fit: the
standard deviation of the lot random intercepts, tau. Two sub-questions: (i) is tau greater than zero by more than
noise — tested against a null built by permuting lot labels inside shipment block and calendar window; and (ii) if
not, how small does the data force tau to be (a one-sided upper bound). The logic for discarding the unvaccinated
is that every lot shares the same unvaccinated denominator, so that denominator is a constant that drops out of any
between-lot contrast. The claim to novelty is that nobody has ever estimated this dispersion parameter on a
clinical endpoint; what has been done is (a) pre-licensure lot-consistency equivalence testing on antibody titres
across 3-4 lots, and (b) batch-level analyses of spontaneously reported adverse events.

Two things I note immediately about the restatement, before attacking. First, the thing the model estimates is the
dispersion of log *risk among the vaccinated*, which equals the dispersion of the log *relative risk*; it is not the
dispersion of log VE (see Attack 4 — this is arithmetic, not a quibble). Second, the question is a question about a
parameter of a model, which makes it immune to several standard attacks (vagueness, dissolution) and exposed to
others (does the parameter mean what the title says it means).

## Novelty, re-graded against the record's own tripwire

The record states: "Medium, and it would fall to Low if an adversary finds any registry analysis with lot as a
covariate on a clinical outcome." That analysis exists and I opened it. Schmeling, Fürst, Manniche, Hansen &
Gilthorpe (preprints.org, posted 27 April 2026, DOI 10.20944/preprints202603.0908.v3) take Czech *nationwide
record-level* data linking each dose's batch to **three-month all-cause mortality**, age- and sex-adjusted, for
four products, and group batches by that rate. All-cause mortality from national records is a clinically
ascertained registry outcome, not a spontaneous report. Two specific claims in the record are therefore false as
written:

- Motivating gap: "The lot-linked analyses that have actually been run on those registries are all on the
  *safety-reporting* side."
- Differentiation from prior 1: the record's sharpest distinction — theirs is voluntary reports, so differential
  reporting is their dominant confounder and not ours — no longer separates the candidate from the prior art, because
  the same group has since moved to a hard registry endpoint on the same substrate.

What survives as un-asked is narrower than the record claims: the *infection/effectiveness* endpoint, the
*variance-component* estimand (rather than cluster analysis of batch-level rates), the within-shipment-block
identification, and the label-permutation null. That is real but it is formulation-and-design novelty one step
removed from an existing design on the same data — and the record itself grades formulation novelty as the weaker
kind. Novelty: **Low-to-Medium, and Low by the record's own stated criterion.** Gate 5's Validated requires
Novelty >= Medium.

The candidate's novelty rests on an axis distinction — "all existing lot-linked registry work is on the
spontaneous-reporting side" — that is false: a batch-level analysis of three-month age/sex-adjusted **all-cause
mortality** in Czech nationwide record-level data across four products was posted in April 2026 by the same author
