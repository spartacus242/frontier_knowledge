# UQ-20261016-45 — Adversary 1 log (Gate 4, run on a Gate 2 rejection; Low grade treated as a prior finding, not inherited)

## Restatement (my words)
Two separate questions wearing one name. (i) Take the published computer-assisted theorems, look up how many core-hours each burned and on what, multiply by a fleet-measured silent-data-corruption rate (carried as an order-of-magnitude band), and report how many undetected hardware faults each result was exposed to and whether it was ever re-checked on different silicon. (ii) Take a formally verified certificate checker (DRAT/LRAT checker, proof-assistant kernel), inject hardware-style faults under a declared model, and measure the share of injections that make it print "verified" on a certificate that is not valid, versus the shares that crash or correctly reject. The conceptual hook is that modern fleet corruptions are often core-local and reproducible, so "run it again" and "check the certificate" may not help.
