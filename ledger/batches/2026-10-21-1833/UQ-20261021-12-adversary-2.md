## 1. Restatement in my own words

A reward model trained the usual way is shown pairs of answers *to the same question* and taught to
put the winner above the loser. Only the gap between two answers to one question ever enters the
loss, so if you added +5 to every answer for question A and -2 to every answer for question B, the
training loss would not notice. The number the model prints is therefore a within-question ranking
score with a per-question zero point that nothing in training pins down. Yet a lot of shipped
machinery reads that number as if it were an absolute quality score that means the same thing on
question A and question B: picking the globally top-scoring samples out of a pool spanning many
prompts, spending more sampling budget on the prompt with the highest raw headroom, averaging scores
across prompts into a leaderboard figure, computing an advantage without a per-prompt baseline.

The candidate asks three measurement questions about that, and nothing else:
(i) empirically, how much of the spread in a released pairwise reward model's output is the
per-prompt term rather than differences between answers to one prompt (share of variance, with CIs,
>= 3 models, >= 2 corpora);
(ii) is that per-prompt term the same thing every time you retrain (k seeds, permuted prompt order) —
i.e. a learned difficulty signal — or is it seed-contingent drift in a direction the loss never
constrained;
(iii) for each named practice that compares reward across prompts, how often does the practice's own
stated decision rule pick differently once you subtract each prompt's mean?

It is not a question about whether BT is identifiable (that is maths), not about reward hacking, and
not about whether reward models agree with humans. A boring answer (small share, or large but
reproducible and monotone in difficulty, with negligible flips) counts as an answer and licenses
current practice.

## 5. Recommended verdict

**Provisional** — nothing Unresolved, so not Rejected; but Medium novelty with an acknowledged close
neighbour, plus a falsified clause in the Motivating gap and a falsified Reason-for-silence mechanism,
which under Gate 4's own rule returns the novelty finding for inspection. Promotion conditions:
(1) strike the claim that the per-prompt shift "was never written down" and cite DPO arXiv:2305.18290
Definition 1 / Lemmas 1-2 as the field's own statement of the premise, reframing the gap as
"written down, proved harmless for policy optimisation, never audited for cross-prompt uses";
(2) drop or re-scope the uncentred-advantage practice, since the canonical recipe zeroes the
per-prompt mean and Lemma 2 covers the idealised case;
(3) keep limb (iii) as the load-bearing limb, now with two sourced practices — AdaBoN's greedy
cross-prompt allocation and the RLHF Book's global top-K selection — and demote limb (i) to a
panel-declared descriptive preliminary;
(4) check the unchecked lead arXiv:2608.23641 before any Validated grade.

**Single strongest killing point:** the candidate's premise is not a gap in its own field's
literature. DPO (arXiv:2305.18290, §5.1) defines reward-function equivalence as
"r(x, y) − r′(x, y) = f(x) for some function f", proves the BT preference distribution invariant to it,
and calls it "a well-known under-specification issue with the Plackett-Luce family of models [32]" —
while §3 reports that prior works already normalize "such that Ex,y∼D [rϕ(x, y)] = 0 for all x".
That does not touch limbs (ii) or (iii), which no source I found measures, but it falsifies the
record's motivating gap and its reason for silence, and it is the leg my verdict rests on. If that
leg fell, my next-strongest is attack 5's panel-elasticity of limb (i), which is my own unsourced
analytic objection and would support Provisional on its own but not a rejection.
