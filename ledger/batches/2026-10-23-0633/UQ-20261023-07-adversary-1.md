## Restatement in my own words

When a hospital transfuses "one unit" of red cells and then re-checks the haemoglobin, how much of the patient-to-patient scatter in how far the haemoglobin actually rose is explained by the particular bag — specifically by how many grams of haemoglobin that bag happened to contain — rather than by the patient (their blood volume, their bleeding, when the sample was drawn)? Second: does whether the patient ends up needing another unit depend on which bag they happened to be handed? Third: if the blood service labelled and issued units by grams of haemoglobin instead of counting bags, how many second units would that save at the same haemoglobin target?

The question's three limbs do not share a fate and I grade them separately throughout: **L1** variance share and slope; **L2** which-unit dependence of the second-unit hazard; **L3** the mass-based-issuing counterfactual.

MEASURAND EXEMPTION — DECLARED AND APPLIED, not merely noted. Whether the slope of increment on grams delivered is non-zero, and how large the unit-mass variance share is, are outputs of the proposed study. I do not grade an attack that says "it assumes units differ consequentially". Likewise "bedside delivered dose is unmeasured" is the question's report of its own gap, not a premise I attack.

## Testing the conditioning clauses (attack 4, run as instructed and given extra weight)

Clauses the question needs TRUE before it can be asked at all, written out literally from the canonical V1 and from Gate 1's single reformulation V2, as distinct from anything a study would measure:

1. **Issued red-cell units differ materially in haemoglobin mass** (there is between-unit variation for L1 to attribute). → **TRUE (sourced).** Epah 2022 abstract: 38.5–79.9 g across 6,058 units. That is better than a two-fold spread. Same unit of analysis as the candidate's exposure.
2. **The haemoglobin mass of the specific unit issued can be known per unit, not merely sampled pass/fail for regulatory QC.** → **TRUE (sourced), and this is the clause Gate 2 left Unverified.** Gate 2 had one instance (a blood-bank information system holding a content field) and wrote that one instance is short of the two or three the document-kind rule asks. Epah 2022 is an **independent second instance from a different group and country**, and it is stronger in kind: adjusted R² > 0.9 on 2,637 held-out units, MAE ≤ 1.43 g, shipped as an open web application, from features a service already records. (MY ARITHMETIC: 1.43 g against the printed 41.4 g span is ≈3.5% of the range — small relative to the between-unit spread the candidate wants to exploit, though the candidate must carry it as exposure measurement error, and I carry Epah's own SD ≤1.13 g with it rather than quoting the MAE as a certainty.) Read level on this instance is abstract only, so what is established is that a validated per-unit estimator is published and distributed; whether it was deployed at issue in any service is not established by what I read.
3. **A population exists that operates a documented single-unit-then-reassess policy in adult inpatients.** → **UNVERIFIED (by me).** I opened nothing on it; Gate 2's query 5 returned UK single-unit audits and Choosing Wisely material, which it also did not open. 4. **The increment is measurable in a declared window without being swamped by bleeding, fluid shifts and sampling time.** → **UNVERIFIED (by me), and live.** My Japanese sweep returned a narrative review on exactly this — early versus delayed post-transfusion haemoglobin measurement in adults (revistamedica.imss.gob.mx 6878) — which I did not open. ## Novelty: checking Gate 2's kill against the candidate's own estimand

I did not open Atilla 2017. On Gate 2's own abstract-level account of it, the prior's printed quantity is a **19.8% reduction, 38/192, denominator = units ORDERED in the study arm**, in a population averaging ~2.1 ordered units per episode, with the endpoint "units transfused and target attainment" under a randomized policy comparison.

- Candidate **L3** asks the pre-declared change in the **48-h second-unit rate**, denominator = **episodes under a single-unit-then-reassess policy**, numerator = **second units triggered by reassessment**. Overlap in both directions: Atilla's class **misses** reassessment-triggered second units entirely (there is no one-unit-then-reassess arm) and misses the un-selected spread of issued content (its intervention **selects** units to hit a target, removing the very variation L1 and L2 are about); it **includes** multi-unit up-front orders, which the candidate excludes, and episodes where no matching unit existed and the original order went out unchanged. Different numerator class on a different denominator — the exact failure mode the ledger flags. **But** the candidate's own second alternative formulation ("issue by mass instead of count; how many fewer units to the same target") is close enough to what Atilla randomized that L3 is fairly called a near-duplicate *of that formulation*, and Gate 2's finding there is a real one.
- Candidate **L1** (variance share attributable to unit Hb mass; slope on grams delivered) — nothing I opened publishes it. The nearest published figure, on Gate 2's abstract-level read of Roubinian 2019, is a joint R² of 0.25 for a covariate set that (unverified at abstract level) does not include measured unit Hb mass. **Qualified absence**: PMC10530070 returned three times in my own sweeps and I opened it none of those times, and JCI Insight 152598 likewise; either could carry a donor/component variance share. I will not write that L1 has no published value.
- Candidate **L2** — unaddressed in everything returned to me; the design family exists (search 6) and has been pointed at donor sex/parity and storage age, not at per-unit haemoglobin mass in anything I saw.

So the Low grade is sound **for L3 and for alternative formulation 2**, and overstated for the canonical question, whose lead limbs are L1 and L2. Net: a close randomized neighbour on one limb — Medium novelty with a close neighbour at best, not High, and not Low for the question as canonically worded.
