## The pattern worth carrying: the author line is as unreliable as the number

Three entries in one batch cited a paper by the wrong author, and twice the error was the same
shape — **the last author printed first**.

- **UQ-08's P1** is Maiworm, Goldner, Mannl & Hammann. The entry had "Hammann et al.".
  Hammann is last.
- **UQ-09's prior 2** is Barnouin et al., with Rivkin **last** of a long author list. The entry
  had "Rivkin, Barnouin, Ballouz, Marchi, Vincent et al." — a plausible-looking order that is
  simply inverted at the front.
- **UQ-08's P2** is Ma & Gernay. The entry had "Gernay & Ma", because the press release it was
  characterised from lists Gernay first.

The third case explains the first two. A citation assembled from a secondary page — an
institutional record, a press release, a repository stub — inherits that page's ordering
conventions, and those pages routinely lead with the senior or local author. A reader who
checks only that the paper exists will pass every one of these. The check that catches them is
reading the **first page of the paper itself**, or Crossref's ordered author list, and that is
now a standing item.

Two more identity failures of the same family, neither about authors:

- **UQ-10's P2 is a regulator's own audit, not an auditor-general report.** The Victorian
  Building Authority audits itself and its licensees; "auditor-general" imported an
  independence the document does not have — and, as it turned out, the document is **better**
  evidence than the entry claimed, because the string it quotes is the audit's own record
  field.
- **UQ-10's "2024" Dutch document is dated 6 July 2021**, and the 2024 in its URL is an upload
  folder. The grid-operators' response dated "2023" from its upload path is almost certainly
  from 2017. **A date taken from a URL path is not a date.**

## arXiv:2601.16612 is a real paper about something else

The sharpest single finding. UQ-10's presupposition P1 was supported in part by "arXiv
2601.16612 finds it across manufacturers". The identifier resolves, and to a genuine
smart-meter metrology paper — Bracale, Janowicz, Kuwałek & Wiczyński, "Challenges in the Proper
Metrological Verification of Smart Energy Meters". But its stimulus is a synthesised
two-component **voltage** signal and its outputs are voltage-based power-quality indicators
(flicker P_st, THD, fundamental frequency). There is no current-channel test, no distorted
current, and no crest factor anywhere — the paper's own "fc" is the fundamental frequency in
hertz, not a crest factor.

So the paper does find manufacturer-to-manufacturer discrepancies under non-ideal conditions,
which is why the mis-citation survived three gates: **the sentence it was cited for is almost
true of it.** What it is not is evidence about error under distorted *current* waveforms, which
is the candidate's entire exposure.

This is the third time in two days that a load-bearing identifier has resolved to the wrong
object (after "Elmore 2022" not existing and arXiv:2605.01158 naming a different paper), and
the first where the wrong object was close enough to the right one to read as support.

## What to carry forward

1. **Check the author line against the paper's own first page or Crossref**, not against a
   record page, a press release or a repository stub. Three defects in one batch, twice with the
   last author printed first.
2. **A real identifier can name a real paper on a different subject, close enough to read as
   support.** Check what the paper measures, not just that it exists and is on topic.
3. **A date in a URL path is not a date.** Two of this batch's four date corrections came from
   upload folders.
4. **A confirmation containing only the string you supplied has confirmed nothing.**
5. **Repeated identical retrieval failures are evidence about the fetcher.** Six failures across
   four agents on one document turned out to be a URL-length limit, not a paywall.
6. **Keep "could not read" distinct from "not found" in promotion conditions.** UQ-08's item
   (ii) is open for the first reason, and only the first reason leaves the entry's exclusion of
   P2 standing.
7. **When a decoration falls, name what holds.** Every entry in this batch lost something on
   verification and none lost its verdict, because each ground was checked against its own
   sources rather than against the adversarial log.
