Domain(s): AI architecture — pretraining schedule design and the annealing/cooldown data layer; secondary: LLM evaluation methodology (format sensitivity, calibration). Source method: silence mapping.

Question (canonical) — V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted; V1 below):
For >=2 open pretraining runs with dense cooldown checkpoints and one pinned harness, decompose each benchmark's cooldown-phase score change into (i) a SHARPENING/FORMAT component, defined as the part of that change reproduced on the PRE-cooldown checkpoint by a fixed-weight output transform fitted on held-out items (temperature/entropy rescaling; length-normalised or rank-based option scoring; a label-emission format correction) with no weight update, and (ii) a KNOWLEDGE residual, measured by a pre-declared panel of probes held fixed across all cooldown checkpoints (length-normalised per-option likelihood, rank scoring, linear probes on frozen features, free-form answers under one fixed grader) — reporting the share per benchmark with uncertainty, a manipulation check validating (i), and the re-ranking of >=3 published annealing data mixes under (ii) alone.

Question (canonical) — V1 (intake, superseded):
Of the downstream benchmark improvement that appears during a pretraining run's final learning-rate decay, what share is attributable to a change in the model's answer-distribution sharpness and output-format conformity rather than to newly acquired task-relevant knowledge, as measured by format-free probes held fixed across the cooldown checkpoints?

Gate 1 note: one reformulation used (permitted). V1 left the decomposition undefined — two components do not partition a score change uniquely without a declared ordering or estimator, and "format-free probe" named a family, not an instrument. V2 pins the sharpening component to a no-weight-update output transform on the pre-cooldown checkpoint (so the component has an estimator and a built-in manipulation check), makes the knowledge component the residual under a pre-declared fixed probe panel reported per probe, and attaches the mix re-ranking as a decision-relevant endpoint. Novelty was graded against both versions; V2 does not escape the neighbours below.

Alternative formulations:
- Does the cooldown teach the model anything, or mostly teach it to answer in the shape the benchmark scores?
- Is the end-of-run benchmark jump a knowledge gain or a calibration/formatting gain — and does the split change which annealing data mixes look good?
- (lay) When a training run cools down and the test scores jump, has the model learned more, or just learned how to take the test?

Motivating gap: The final decay is engineered everywhere and interrogated nowhere. WSD/cooldown-shape work, annealing data mixes and "mid-training" recipes form a dense how-literature; the composition of the gain that phase produces is unasked. Confirmed at Gate 2 from the field's own map: "A Survey on LLM Mid-training" (arXiv:2510.23081, OPENED) offers only an optimization account of the decay-stage improvement — Sec. 8.1: "the rapid convergence in the LR decay stage is due to the first-order directional derivative diminishing with each step" — cites no work measuring what the downstream gain is made of, and does not list the question as open. Meanwhile OLMo 2 (arXiv:2501.00656, OPENED, partial) states mid-training's purpose as "to infuse new knowledge and patch deficiencies in capabilities" (intro) and reports using downstream dev scores to pick the mixes: Sec. 2.4, "we use 200 examples to inform choices (e.g., choices of annealing mixtures)", with "microanneals" so "data sources for mid-training can be independently assessed to reduce experimentation cost" (intro; echoed by the survey Sec. 3.5). So the phase's score movement is a live selection instrument whose content has not been audited.

Presuppositions:
P1. A format-free / sharpness-invariant probe family exists. CONTESTED and load-bearing — but materially STRENGTHENED at Gate 2: "Fine-Tune on the Format" (GEM 2025) already operationalises exactly this separation on intermediate pretraining checkpoints, reporting that "many models struggle to emit the correct label (e.g., 'C'), even when they can select the correct string answer choice" and that its method "disentangles the evaluation of core knowledge from the model's emerging ability to follow formatting instructions". Each probe retains its own confound; V2 requires the panel to be reported per probe rather than pooled.
P2. "Knowledge" and "format/sharpness" are separable at all in a next-token model. CONTESTED — the dissolution risk. V2's manipulation check (does a fixed-weight output transform on the pre-cooldown checkpoint reproduce the component?) is the response, and its failure is an informative answer rather than a broken design.
P3. Cooldown checkpoints are retrievable at sufficient density for >=2 open runs. Asserted for OLMo 2 and Marin-class releases; NOT verified by this agent against a checkpoint listing (no fetch budget left). Flag for Gate 4.
P4. The benchmark jump during cooldown is real rather than a harness artefact. Reported in release reports; OLMo 2's abstract claims the mix "significantly improves model capabilities across many downstream task benchmarks" with no figures in the text read. Still to be re-measured under one pinned harness — which V2 requires.
P5. The decomposition is stable enough across benchmarks to report a share rather than a list of incomparable cases. CONTESTED; V2 retreats to per-benchmark reporting, so the "share" is a vector, not a scalar.
P6. Annealing-mix choices are actually made on cooldown-phase score movement. PARTIALLY SOURCED, not assumed: OLMo 2 Sec. 2.4's GSM8K-dev line plus the microanneal protocol. The survey (Sec. 3.5) describes microanneals as extrapolating "the efficacy of large-scale mixtures" but does NOT state the metric, so the strong form of P6 is still one line of evidence away.

What would count as an answer: For >=2 open runs with dense cooldown checkpoints and one pinned harness, a pre-declared decomposition of the cooldown-phase score change into the sharpening/format component and the knowledge residual, each with its own estimator and a validated manipulation check, reported per benchmark with uncertainty; plus a re-ranking of >=3 published annealing data mixes under the knowledge component alone. Pre-committed bands: (i) sharpening component small AND the mixes re-rank identically -> the worry is retired and current microanneal practice is licensed; (ii) sharpening component large AND the mixes re-rank -> published annealing ablations have been partly selecting format compliance, and mix screening needs a format-corrected endpoint; (iii) component large but mixes do not re-rank -> an evaluation-reporting finding without a curation consequence. A null under band (i) is publishable by construction.

Significance — consequence: If mostly knowledge (band i), a worry that is currently live and unexamined is retired and the microanneal selection practice described in N3 and the survey's Sec. 3.5 is licensed on the record rather than by assumption. If mostly sharpening (band ii), three things change at once: published annealing-mix ablations have been ranking mixes partly on format compliance, so some curation conclusions in the open record are about shape rather than content; cooldown-phase evaluation needs a format-corrected endpoint before it can screen data; and decay-phase scaling/predictive work that fits downstream scores over the cooldown has been fitting a partly format-driven curve. The mechanism-level consequence is sharper still: the survey's only account of the decay gain is an optimization one, so a large sharpening share would mean the field's explanation of the phase and its use of the phase are mismatched.
Significance — audience: (a) Pretraining data teams that run cooldown/annealing ablations and already publish the protocol — AI2 (OLMo 2 microanneals, N3 Sec. 2.4), the Marin community (its "Different Data Ablations on Cooldown Annealing" report), and the FineWeb/Nemotron-CC-style curation lines; they would change the endpoint their screening is scored on, which is a concrete change to a running procedure, not a change of opinion. (b) The evaluation-methodology groups that own format sensitivity and eval noise; they would gain a phase-indexed reason to prefer format-corrected scoring, and N1 shows that community already builds the instrument. (c) WSD/cooldown-schedule researchers (N2's line), whose loss-based account would acquire or lose a downstream interpretation.
Significance — fecundity: Does the sharpening share scale with model size, cooldown length or cooldown shape (directly joinable to N2's shape sweep)? Is it benchmark-format-dependent (MCQ vs generative vs graded free-form), which would predict which suites are safe to screen mixes on? Can a cheap fixed output transform substitute for part of a microanneal, making mix screening cheaper? And if the manipulation check fails, what does that say about the separability of knowledge and format in a next-token model (P2) — a negative with its own research programme.
Significance — anchoring: The cooldown/WSD dynamics literature (N2; "Same Loss, Same Noise, Opposite Schedules" 2607.12360; Haegele et al. on schedule scaling laws); the mid-training survey's stated gaps around decay scaling laws; the emergence/metric-nonlinearity debate ("Mirage"), which this touches but does not share a unit or mechanism with; the format-sensitivity and eval-noise programme; the calibration/sharpening literature; and the RLVR sharpening-vs-capability debate, which is the same shape of question one phase downstream.

Reason for silence: A disciplinary boundary with an incentive gap on one side and an instrument gap on the other, plus a recent instrument change. The cooldown belongs to the optimization/schedule community, which measures the phase in LOSS and landscape geometry (N2) and, where it explains the downstream improvement at all, explains it by optimization (survey Sec. 8.1's directional-derivative line) — in that vocabulary a question about answer formatting is not a question about the phase. The downstream movement belongs to the data-curation community, which uses it as a SELECTION SIGNAL (N3 Sec. 2.4): a mix that moves the score is the deliverable whether the movement is knowledge or format, so asking what it is made of has no payoff inside that workflow and a bad payoff if the answer is unflattering to published ablations. The tooling that would answer it — format-corrected MCQ scoring, length-normalised and rank-based option scoring, fixed probe panels — grew up in the evaluation-methodology community, which works on released final checkpoints and had no dense cooldown series to apply it to; N1 shows that community reaching intermediate checkpoints only in 2025, and the dense public cooldown/annealing checkpoint releases that make the measurement possible are of the same vintage. So the question sits in the gap between the field that owns the phase, the field that spends the phase's output, and the field that owns the method, with the enabling instrument only two years old.
