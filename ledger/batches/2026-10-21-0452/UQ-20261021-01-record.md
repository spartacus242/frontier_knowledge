Question (canonical):
V1 (as intaken) — In chronic-therapy trials and clinics whose participants carry time-stamped electronic dosing records and whose outcome is a biomarker whose pharmacodynamic response time is shorter than the inter-visit interval (blood pressure, LDL, HIV RNA, IOP, INR; HbA1c excluded by design), what is the signed magnitude of the difference between the visit-measured treatment effect and the effect that the same patients' time-averaged (non-visit-entrained) dosing would produce — i.e. how much of estimated efficacy is manufactured by the visit schedule's own entrainment of dosing?

V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted, for well-posedness only; it pins the counterfactual regime and declares the estimand, and is scored as canonical) — In >=2 independent cohorts or trials holding (a) per-patient time-stamped electronic dosing records covering whole inter-visit intervals and (b) a visit-measured biomarker whose pharmacodynamic response time is shorter than that interval (BP, LDL, HIV RNA, IOP, INR), and under one exposure-response model per biomarker declared before any data pull with >=2 model forms as sensitivity: what is the signed difference, computed per randomised arm and in the arm contrast, between (i) the treatment effect as the visit-measured analysis reports it and (ii) the same effect predicted when each patient's own dosing record is replaced by a DOSE-COUNT-PRESERVING regime in which that patient's realised doses are redistributed uniformly across the inter-visit interval — the visit-entrained component removed with total exposure held fixed — expressed as a fraction of the observed arm difference, judged against a margin declared before any data pull, with HbA1c pre-specified as the falsification arm in which the estimated difference must vanish?
(V2 is an ICH E9(R1) hypothetical-strategy estimand on an exposure-mapped scale. V1 is retained because the two differ only in specification, not in object.)

Alternative formulations:
- How much of a drug's measured effect is an artefact of patients taking it more faithfully in the days before the visit at which the effect is measured?
- Treating the visit calendar as a cause of the exposure it is used to evaluate, what is the bias in the ITT estimate induced by visit-entrained adherence cycles, and does it differ between randomised arms?

Motivating gap:
Source method: anomaly mining. The motivating anomaly is now ESTABLISHED on a primary source read at full text, which it was not at intake. Poleon, Sabbagh & Racette (Front Med 2022; 66 POAG patients, MEMS caps, 28-day window bracketing one clinic visit) report adherence rising from the Pre14-4 window to the Pre3-1 window and falling from Post1-3 to Post4-14: overall 85.5 +/- 21.2 -> 88.5 +/- 23.2 (p = 0.01) before, and 87.0 +/- 23.9 -> 84.9 +/- 23.3 (p = 0.02) after; in the optimal-adherence subgroup (>=80%, n = 49) 94.0 +/- 11.7 -> 97.7 +/- 7.4 (p = 0.001) and 95.2 +/- 12.0 -> 95.4 +/- 5.7 (p = 0.007); in the suboptimal subgroup (n = 17) no pattern (p = 0.69, p = 0.32). Wilcoxon signed-rank. So the load-bearing presupposition holds in at least one fast-biomarker indication, with the important qualifier that the effect was carried by the already-adherent and the overall effect size is small (about 3 percentage points).
The gap is that the entrainment is owned as a property of the PATIENT ("whitecoat adherence") and corrected out of the ADHERENCE estimate, and is not posed as a property of the measurement schedule that biases the EFFICACY estimate. The nearest statement of a consequence anywhere in the opened literature is a single uncited sentence in that paper's Introduction, about clinical interpretation rather than about a trial's estimated effect, and it is qualitative. Reconstruction from 3 ordered consecutive fragments with 3-5 word overlaps verified at both junctions, 27 words, matching the summariser's own word count: "IOP readings within the target range may lead clinicians to overestimate treatment effectiveness, which may bias the interpretation of other clinical measures (e.g., visual field imaging results)." Grammar checked on both sides: the preceding sentence fixes the scope as IOP measurements "unrepresentative of those outside of clinical encounters" and the following sentence as possibly precluding "recommendations for indicated adjunctive therapy or surgical intervention" — i.e. the sentence is about a clinician's bedside inference in glaucoma CARE, not about an estimated treatment effect in a trial, and it carries no citation. It is therefore the unhedged, uncited formulation, and I flag it as such rather than leaning on it.

Presuppositions:
1. Cohorts/trials exist holding BOTH time-stamped electronic dosing records and visit-measured fast-responding biomarkers. ESTABLISHED for the dosing-record half (MEMS in glaucoma, verified above; MEMS in hypertension and HIV returned repeatedly across queries). NOT VERIFIED here that any one dataset also holds a randomised arm contrast with the biomarker; this is now the binding feasibility presupposition and the first thing an answer attempt must check.
2. Adherence is non-stationary and entrained to visit timing, not merely noisy. ESTABLISHED (no longer contested as to existence) in one indication at full-text read level; magnitude small (~3 pp overall) and concentrated in the adherent subgroup. Generalisation to HIV/hypertension remains UNVERIFIED in this run.
3. The named biomarkers respond faster than the inter-visit interval. Established background (HIV RNA, BP, LDL, INR); false for HbA1c, which is therefore the pre-specified falsification arm, not a case.
4. Electronic dosing timestamps index ingestion. CONTESTED and load-bearing for the SIGN as well as the magnitude: MEMS logs container openings ("Each container opening was logged as a proxy for an instilled dose", per the opened paper), and curiosity or tidying openings would plausibly also cluster before a visit, inflating the measured entrainment and hence the estimated bias. Needs an ingestion-evidencing device or a validation subsample.
5. A counterfactual "time-averaged adherence" effect is a well-defined estimand. CONTESTED at V1; RESOLVED BY CONSTRUCTION at V2 (dose-count-preserving uniform redistribution, declared exposure-response model), at the price of making the magnitude model-dependent.
6. The entrainment is not already cancelled by the comparator arm. CONTESTED; partial cancellation is likely and the residual is the quantity of interest. Assuming non-cancellation would beg the question, which is why V2 computes the per-arm quantities and the contrast separately.

What would count as an answer:
A signed, interval-estimated difference in >=2 independent datasets between the visit-measured effect and the effect re-estimated with each patient's visit-entrained dosing component removed under the dose-count-preserving regime and a pre-declared exposure-response model, reported per arm and as a fraction of the arm difference, judged against a pre-declared margin; plus the HbA1c falsification arm, in which the estimated bias must vanish. Small and margin-compatible in every dataset with the falsification arm behaving = a negative answer that retires the question. An interval too wide to separate the two answers is declared IN ADVANCE to be an answer about feasibility, not a null.

Significance — consequence:
If the entrained component is material (say a tenth or more of the arm difference) in fast-biomarker indications, then the visit schedule stops being a logistical parameter and becomes an efficacy-relevant design parameter: the quantity a registration trial estimates is partly a property of its own calendar, and no label's dosing instruction reproduces that calendar. Three concrete things move. (i) Non-inferiority margins and dose-ranging slopes calibrated on visit-measured effects are mis-scaled by a factor nobody has estimated — and P2 supplies the relevant order of magnitude for why this is not negligible a priori, since it cites a measurement-reaction SMD of 0.09 against behaviour-change effects of 0.20-0.30. (ii) Part of the efficacy-effectiveness gap, now attributed to real-world non-adherence, would be mechanical and predictable from schedule geometry rather than being a fact about patients. (iii) An ICH E9(R1) estimand would have to state whether it is defined under the trial's visit schedule, which current estimand attributes have no slot for. If the answer is no — small, margin-compatible, falsification arm behaving — a plausible and currently unexamined threat to every fast-biomarker endpoint is retired on evidence instead of by assumption, which is worth the measurement because at present it is retired by silence.

Significance — audience:
Argued, not asserted. (a) Trial methodologists and funders: the MRC commissioned MERIT specifically to develop guidance on measurement-reaction bias in trials, so an institutional appetite for exactly this bias family is documented, not hypothesised — and MERIT's own taxonomy has the empty slot. (b) Regulators reading estimands: ICH E9(R1)'s five attributes include the handling of intercurrent events but not the dependence of the estimand on the assessment calendar; a signed magnitude would be an argument for an additional declaration, and the estimand literature returned by query 11 is where that argument would be made. (c) Pharmacometricians: exposure-response models are routinely fitted on dosing histories treated as exchangeable in time; a visit-locked periodic component is a specification error they can act on immediately. (d) Clinicians in the index indications: glaucoma, hypertension and HIV care already act on the white-coat-adherence idea qualitatively (P1's Introduction), and a magnitude would tell them how much to discount an in-range visit reading.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: optimal visit spacing as a bias-minimising design problem; whether at-home or remote biomarker capture yields a less entrained estimate and by how much; whether scheduling frequency is itself an adherence intervention (and therefore whether the control arm's visit calendar is part of the control treatment); whether arm-asymmetric entrainment is a detectable signature of functional unblinding; and whether the entrained component is a measurable part of the efficacy-effectiveness gap decomposition.

Significance — anchoring:
The efficacy-effectiveness gap; the white-coat family (white-coat hypertension and white-coat compliance/adherence); measurement reactivity in trials (MERIT's unresolved A2 interaction); causal inference with informative observation times (Kant & Krijthe's T -> A arrow); ICH E9(R1) estimand specification; and the standing methodological complaint that electronic adherence monitoring is collected in trials and then used only for compliance screening.

Reason for silence:
A disciplinary boundary with a conceptual-bridge delay, not an absence of thought. The two objects the question needs sit in different communities, and neither community holds both. The adherence community holds the time-stamped dosing records but its estimand IS adherence; for it, a pre-visit surge is contamination of the adherence measurement, to be named as a patient property ("whitecoat adherence") and corrected out — which is exactly what P1 does, down to analysing no outcome at all. The trial-statistics community holds the randomised contrast but under ITT the dosing history is deliberately not in the estimand, so a cyclical structure inside it has nowhere to land; electronic monitoring, where collected, is used to screen compliance, not to construct an estimand. The bridge that would let the question be stated in the owning field's own terms — a legitimate, declarable non-ITT exposure-mapped estimand — arrived with ICH E9(R1) in 2019, and the causal-diagram apparatus for measurement time as a cause of treatment arrived later still (P3, 2025). Before that the question had no sayable form in the field that would act on it. Consistent with this, the one paper that measures the anomaly states the consequence in the vocabulary of bedside inference, uncited, in its Introduction, and never returns to it.

Strongest remaining objection (carried forward to Gate 4):
Partial cancellation plus small entrainment. If the measured surge is ~3 pp, concentrated in patients already near ceiling adherence (where any plausible exposure-response curve is flattest), and present in both arms, the arm-contrast residual may be far below any margin a trialist would pre-declare — in which case the honest answer is the feasibility declaration, not a bias estimate. The candidate's own pre-declaration rule handles this correctly, but it means the expected yield is a bounded null rather than a discovered bias.
