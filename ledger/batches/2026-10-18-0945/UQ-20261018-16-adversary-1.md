## Restatement (my words, not the record's)

Buy the same bacterium twice — once from each of two collections whose catalogues list each other's accession as "equivalent" — sequence what each one actually ships, and do that across a set of such co-held accessions fixed before any sequencing. Then report three things: how far apart the two collections' holdings are, broken out by SNVs, indels, IS elements, plasmids and prophages; what fraction of accession pairs are further apart than two ampoules of a single lot from a single collection are from each other (a yardstick measured in the same study, not borrowed); and whether the distance grows with the recorded number of transfers and years since deposit. Plus a declared rule, written down before the data, for telling "one lineage that drifted apart in two freezers" from "two different isolates that a cross-reference wrongly glued together", with the rate of the latter reported as a result.

In one line: is "the same strain" one organism across the world's collections, and the answer has to be a distribution with an internally measured ruler, not an anecdote.
