## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take the blood tests people actually get in routine care, as they land in the chart. For a given test, the thing being measured is usually a protein, and the patient carries their own DNA spelling of that protein. The question is whether the patient's own spelling of the molecule being measured changes the *number the machine prints* — not the amount of molecule in them — because the test is an antibody that has to grab the molecule and a changed molecule is gripped less well. Three sub-claims have to be separated: (i) does genotype in the analyte's own gene predict the reported value at biobank scale; (ii) does that prediction depend on *which analyzer* ran the sample (if it does, it is the instrument, not the person); (iii) does it shrink to nothing when the same people's same analyte is measured by something that does not depend on antibody grip (mass spec on a peptide away from the variant, or an activity assay). And then the payoff: among people who were actually tested, how many carriers get pushed over a line that triggers a diagnosis or a drug, per 100,000 tests, and does their care actually diverge. Plus a built-in consolation prize: a census of how many routine tests can even be studied this way.

So: "how often does your own genome make somebody else's non-genetic blood test lie about you, across the whole routine test menu, and does it reach the chart?"

## Verdict

**Provisional.** Nothing Unresolved, so not Rejected; but Disguise is only Partial — the nearest-prior set is materially nearer than Gate 2 recorded (FDH/ALB R218H across current clinical FT4 methods already executes limbs (b) and (c) for one analyte, and D-variant serology shows the structural question is routine audit with published discordance rates in a neighbouring branch of laboratory medicine) — and novelty therefore does not hold at the level Validated requires. Novelty confidence should be rewritten as Medium-to-Low pending someone opening PMC7222281 and PMC7408830, the candidate re-sourced as a cross-field transplant rather than a new-instrument question, and the pre-specified fallback extended to cover limb (c).

**Single strongest killing point:** limb (c) — the only limb that distinguishes assay bias from proteoform physiology — rests on a data substrate nobody has shown exists (no biobank with the same people measured for the same analyte by a routine immunoassay and by a variant-agnostic orthogonal method at estimable scale), and the record's pre-specified fallback declares only limb (b) not estimable; if (c) is unavailable, limbs (a)+(d) measure threshold crossings among carriers, which genuine biology produces as readily as binder failure, so the headline "misclassification per 100,000 tests" is not identified. Tellingly, every cell of this table that has actually been filled — FDH, GDF-15 H202D, HbA1c/G6PD — was filled by recruiting and measuring people, not by mining linked records.
