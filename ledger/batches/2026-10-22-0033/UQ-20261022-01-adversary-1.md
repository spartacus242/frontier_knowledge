# Gate 4 — Adversary #1 log — UQ-20261022-01

## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Take every published animal-respirometry paper that prints both an oxygen-consumption and a carbon-dioxide-production figure (or an RQ/RER computed from the pair) for the same animals. Each such pair is a redundant measurement: divide one by the other and the quotient has to sit inside the range that fat/protein/carbohydrate stoichiometry allows (the candidate fixes 0.70-1.00, with a second pre-declared window at the clinical 0.67-1.30 and exemption arms for anaerobiosis, lipogenesis, CO2-storing discontinuous gas exchange, aquatic carbonate buffering and non-steady state). Three things are then asked: (i) what share of those printed pairs lands outside the window, with a CI, cut by taxon, respirometry mode and decade, against a denominator (how many papers print the pair at all) that nobody has counted either; (ii) mode by mode, derived from each mode's own computation, which error classes such a failed quotient can actually finger and which it is structurally blind to, and only then whether it bounds the error in the reported metabolic rate; (iii) whether throwing out or correcting the flagged records shifts the fitted interspecific mass-scaling exponent outside its published interval.

In short: does the comparative-physiology record fail its own internal arithmetic, how often, and does fixing it move the 2/3-vs-3/4 number. It is a forensic audit of printed values (GRIM/statcheck logic) aimed at a corpus whose primary measurements are pooled into scaling datasets.

## Sources I grade on

**S1 — Walsberg GE & Hoffman TCM (2005) "Direct calorimetry reveals large errors in respirometric estimates of energy expenditure", J Exp Biol 208:1035-1043, doi:10.1242/jeb.01477.** Authors from front matter as served. READ LEVEL: FULL TEXT, via WebFetch summariser over the publisher PDF (https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article-pdf/1255121/1035.pdf). Short strings below are as the summariser served them; I did not verify page numbers verbatim, so each is flagged as summariser-served rather than a page-checked quotation.
REGIME: population = 10 individuals each of *Dipodomys merriami*, *Columbina inca*, *Coturnix communis*; unit of analysis = one 30-min to 17-h interval within one animal's trial; operating condition = fasted animals, within-trial, simultaneous measurement; instrument = direct calorimetry run simultaneously with flow-through respirometry (direct calorimetry as the reference standard); threshold = expected RQ/RER window stated by the authors as 0.71-1.00; benefit category = per-cent error in *estimated heat production* relative to directly measured heat.
NUMBERS PRINTED, with where: abstract — respirometric estimates "may incorporate large errors (up to 38%)"; Discussion — mean absolute errors over 17 h of about 21% (kangaroo rats), 15% (quail), 17% (doves), with single-30-min maxima of 38%, 19% and 37%; lipid-only assumption over the final 7 h, 8-18%. RER observations: 1.01-1.20 early in trials; three kangaroo rats at 0.65-0.70 in the final 7 h; four doves at 0.58-0.69 during hours 3-14, described as "below the minimum anticipated for exclusive reliance upon lipids". Attribution: "Candidate explanations for the discrepancies lie in the extensive series of assumptions", with respirometry itself stated as "accurate within 0.4%"; mechanisms offered are anaerobic heat production, bicarbonate CO2 storage and biosynthesis. The paper **surveys the historical validation literature and discusses named published conclusions; it does not audit a corpus and prints no infeasible-RQ share.**

**S2 — Genoud M, Isler K & Martin RD (2018) "Comparative analyses of basal rate of metabolism in mammals: data selection does matter", Biological Reviews 93:404-438.** READ LEVEL: FULL TEXT, via WebFetch summariser over the institutional PDF (https://www.cb.iee.unibe.ch/e58879/e337551/e608359/e749385/Genoud_BioR2018_eng.pdf).

BMR is held as oxygen consumption (ml O2/h). Outliers handled statistically: "Outliers corresponding to a studentized residual >3 were identified and the analyses were repeated after discarding them."

## Single strongest killing point

Walsberg & Hoffman 2005 (S1), the reference-standard study in the candidate's own core taxa: fasted, resting small endotherms held sustained RER values outside the candidate's feasibility window (doves 0.58-0.69 over hours 3-14; three kangaroo rats 0.65-0.70 over the final 7 h; 1.01-1.20 early in trials) in a setup whose authors state respirometry is "accurate within 0.4%" and attribute the discrepancy to "the extensive series of assumptions", offering anaerobic heat production, bicarbonate CO2 storage and biosynthesis. None of those states is an arm the candidate pre-declares for endotherms. So the candidate's redundancy check, in the regime that dominates its constructible frame, flags physiology and conversion-assumption failure rather than measurement error — which means the headline share of limb (i) does not measure what the motivating gap says it measures, and the arm set cannot be enumerated before extraction. This is the leg my verdict stands on if the others fall. It does NOT kill novelty: S1 prints no corpus share, audits no literature, and its numbers (21/15/17% means, 38% max) are a per-cent error in estimated heat production in its own 30 animals, a different quantity at a different unit from anything the candidate asks.

Second leg, sourced separately: Genoud et al. 2018 (S2) holds mammalian BMR as ml O2/h across 1739 entries from 946 publications with no RQ, VCO2 or energy-conversion criterion anywhere in its selection grade, so limb (iii)'s dual-gas subset within the dataset whose exponent (0.729 +/- 0.010 S.E., Table 1, N=549) is at stake may be too small to move anything. These two legs are independent; the first does not depend on the second.
