Question (canonical) [R1, adopted at Gate 1]: In the published enzyme-kinetics literature, restricted to measurement pairs on the same enzyme (same organism, same sequence region, same mutational state) and the same substrate that differ in affinity-tag status (untagged vs tagged, or N- vs C-terminally tagged) and are condition-matched on temperature, pH, buffer and co-substrate saturation: (a) is the pooled within-pair shift in log10 kcat and in log10 KM different from zero and consistent in direction, estimated in a mixed model with tag-type, terminus and laboratory terms; and (b) when a published turnover-number predictor (DLKcat, TurNuP, CatPred or RealKcat) is refitted on re-extracted values with versus without tag/terminus stratification, do held-out error and top-k rankings change beyond resampling noise?
  [V0, intake, superseded: "Across enzyme-substrate pairs measured with both tagged and untagged (or differently tagged) constructs, is there a systematic, direction-consistent shift in reported kcat and KM attributable to the affinity tag and its terminus, and is the shift large enough to change the labels and rankings on which kcat/KM prediction models and genome-scale metabolic models are fitted?" V0 left "large enough to change the labels and rankings" with no threshold, did not say what the pairing unit was, and bundled genome-scale models into the decision rule without naming a measurable model outcome. R1 fixes the pairing unit, names the estimator, and makes part (b) a stated, resampling-referenced comparison on one predictor; the genome-scale-model stake moves to Significance, where it belongs, instead of sitting inside the question as an unmeasured clause.]

Alternative formulations: (1) Is the affinity tag a measurable systematic bias in the enzyme-kinetics literature, or does it only add noise? (2) Do machine-learning kcat predictors trained on tag-unaware databases inherit a construct artefact, and does tag-stratified refitting change their held-out error or their top-ranked predictions?

Motivating gap: The how-literature on affinity-tag expression and purification is enormous, and the STRENDA reporting standard explicitly requires the construct's artificial modification to be reported, so the metadata is sometimes on the page. Isolated case reports say the tag matters for particular enzymes (C-terminal His-tagging changing substrate specificity; the GNAT PA4794 tag acting as a competitive inhibitor; N- vs C-terminal His-tag changing activity of adenylosuccinate synthetase). The corpus-level why-question appears unasked, and the silence is locatable at the point where it would have surfaced: RealKcat (bioRxiv 2025.02.10.637555), the most aggressive re-curation of BRENDA/SABIO-RK-derived kinetics to date, re-read 2,158 source articles and resolved 1,804 inconsistencies in KM, kcat, mutant annotation and substrate assignment, and enumerates data-entry error, assay-protocol and condition heterogeneity, organism/expression-system range, unstable outlier filtering and inconsistent condition metadata as its sources of variation - while the strings "tag", "His-tag", "affinity tag", "fusion", "terminus" and "purification" do not occur in the paper at all ("construct" occurs only in the computational sense, "RealKcat constructs a rich feature representation"). Verified by opening the full PDF in this run. Chen & Nielsen 2021 (PNAS) reaches the neighbouring question - in vitro kcat vs in vivo kmax, R2 = 0.28 in yeast against 0.62 in E. coli - names heterologous expression as the main source of deviation, attributes it to missing post-translational modifications, and likewise never mentions tags or construct design (verified by opening the full text). The 2026 arXiv audit of Haldane consistency in kinetic data requires pairs to match on "the same enzyme form, organism, mutation state, and assay conditions" and carries STRENDA-style condition metadata, but never mentions tags either. Meanwhile these values parameterise enzyme-constrained metabolic models and screen designed enzymes.

Presuppositions:
  P1 Tag identity and terminus are recoverable for enough entries. CONTESTED: they live in methods prose, plasmid names and supplementary maps, not in any database field; STRENDA Level 1A requires the disclosure ("Artificial modification e.g., truncated, His-tagged, fusion protein, lacking native glycosylation") but neither BRENDA nor SABIO-RK exposes it as a queryable field. Full-text extraction at scale is the enabling instrument.
  P2 Enough same-enzyme, same-substrate tagged/untagged (or N- vs C-) pairs exist for a within-pair estimate. CONTESTED and uncounted; power is the principal risk and must be inventoried before the study, not assumed. RealKcat's 2,158 re-read articles is the nearest available indication of how large a re-extractable pool is reachable, and it is not a pair count.
  P3 Assay conditions within a pair can be matched or adjusted. CONTESTED: temperature, pH, buffer, ionic strength and co-substrate saturation differ between papers; Majorek et al. 2014 show buffer (HEPES) and tag occupying the same binding site, so buffer is not merely a nuisance covariate but can interact with the exposure.
  P4 Publication and construct selection are tag-independent. CONTESTED: constructs that behaved badly may never be reported, so any observed shift is a bound of unknown direction.
  P5 Database values are faithful to the source papers. PARTLY FALSE: RealKcat resolved 1,804 discrepancies against the originals, so values must be re-extracted rather than taken from database fields.
  P6 The purification route is separable from the tag itself. CONTESTED: tagged constructs are typically purified by affinity chromatography and untagged ones otherwise, so the estimand is the tag-plus-its-purification package unless same-laboratory pairs isolate the tag. R1 adopts the bundle as the declared estimand.
  P7 A tag effect, if present, is large enough to matter against predictor error. CONTESTED and adverse: published kcat predictors carry errors near an order of magnitude, so part (b) can return "no change" even when part (a) returns a real bias. This is why the two parts are reported separately rather than as one verdict.

What would count as an answer: Build a paired dataset by full-text extraction (same organism, same sequence region and mutational state, same substrate; tagged vs untagged or N- vs C-terminal; condition-matched), with the pair inventory reported first as a feasibility result in its own right. Estimate within-pair log-ratio shifts for kcat and KM in a mixed model with tag-type, terminus and laboratory random terms, reporting CIs; then refit one published predictor (DLKcat, TurNuP, CatPred or RealKcat) with and without tag/terminus stratification on identically re-extracted labels and report change in held-out error and in top-k rankings against a resampling null.
  Pre-registered bands: YES-bias = pooled |delta log10 kcat| >= 0.2 with a CI excluding zero and the same sign in at least two tag classes (and the same, separately, for KM) AND refitting changes held-out error or top-k rankings beyond resampling noise. NO = both CIs lie inside +/-0.1 and refitting changes nothing, which positively licenses databases, reporting standards and model builders to continue ignoring construct metadata. NOISE-NOT-BIAS = within-pair CI spans zero while within-pair variance is comparable to or larger than between-enzyme variance, which argues for construct metadata as a weighting variable rather than a correction. Part (a) YES with part (b) NO is a fourth closed outcome: a real artefact that current predictors are too coarse to feel, which bears on curators and modellers but not on predictor builders.

The candidate turns a title-level suspicion with n = 3 into a corpus-level, direction-consistency estimand with a pre-registered equivalence band, and adds a second part (predictor refitting) that N1 does not touch.   vs N2: Chen & Nielsen run the corpus-level design the candidate needs - pooling database-derived in vitro kcat, stratifying by how the protein was obtained, and reporting how badly the strata agree - but their exposure is the expression host and their estimand is agreement with in vivo kmax, not a within-pair tagged-vs-untagged shift. Significance - consequence: Part (a) YES with a consistent direction makes construct metadata a correction term: BRENDA and SABIO-RK would have a reason to add a tag/terminus field (the STRENDA checklist already demands the information, so the standard exists without a schema to hold it), re-curation efforts such as RealKcat would have a named covariate they currently do not record, and tag-blind pooling of literature kcat becomes a known-biased operation. Part (a) NOISE-NOT-BIAS makes it a weighting variable instead, which is a cheaper and different intervention. Part (a) NO inside the equivalence band is the outcome that positively licenses current practice: curators, the reporting-standards committee and model builders could cite it to justify ignoring construct metadata, and the case-report literature would be recast as a collection of genuine but idiosyncratic exceptions rather than the visible tip of a bias. Part (b) is what decides whether predictor builders act: given that published kcat predictors carry errors near an order of magnitude while the pre-registered bias threshold is 0.2 log10 units, the honest expectation is that held-out error may be untouched while top-k rankings - which is what enzyme-engineering users actually consume - may still move. A part (a) YES with part (b) NO would say the artefact is real but below current model resolution, which bears on curators and on enzyme-constrained metabolic modellers (who take maximum or selected kcat values, where a directional bias does not average out) rather than on model benchmarks.
Significance - audience: BRENDA and SABIO-RK curators (a new field and an extraction rule); the STRENDA/Beilstein reporting-standards committee (whether Level 1A's tag item is being complied with and whether it should move into database schemas); builders of kcat/KM predictors (DLKcat, TurNuP, CatPred, RealKcat groups - the RealKcat team in particular, having re-read 2,158 articles, is the group for whom the marginal cost of capturing tag metadata is lowest); enzyme-constrained genome-scale modellers using database kcat as parameters; enzyme-engineering groups that rank designed variants against literature baselines. The first and third are the audiences whose behaviour a YES would change most cheaply; the reaction risk is that curators regard a 1.6-fold systematic shift as negligible against the 6-8 order-of-magnitude spread they already live with.
Significance - fecundity: whether the same construct covariate biases other curated biochemical corpora (protein stability ddG sets, binding-affinity databases, solubility sets - all assembled from tagged constructs); whether the tag effect is predictable from structure (distance from active site to terminus, terminal secondary structure), which would let a model carry a correction without the metadata; whether tag effects and the heterologous-expression anomaly of N2 are the same phenomenon measured twice; whether reported-construct metadata predicts other reproducibility outcomes (an audit of STRENDA Level 1A compliance as a data-quality instrument in its own right); and whether full-text construct extraction is accurate enough to serve as a general curation covariate.
Significance - anchoring: Chen & Nielsen's unexplained in vitro/in vivo gap and its uncorrelated heterologous-expression subset; the 6-8 order-of-magnitude spreads and 1,804 corrections documented by RealKcat; Kroll et al.'s finding that DLKcat fails on mutants and unfamiliar enzymes (a label-quality anomaly whose construct component has never been examined); the standard-without-schema gap between STRENDA Level 1A and database fields; the mechanistic anchor of Majorek et al., where the tag occupies the substrate site and inhibits competitively, which predicts a KM-biased rather than kcat-biased signature and therefore gives the question a falsifiable fine structure.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism): a metadata boundary plus a division of ownership. The tag is recorded in methods prose, plasmid names and supplementary maps and in no queryable field of any kinetics database, so for as long as curation and modelling worked from database fields the covariate was not merely unexamined but unqueryable; full-text extraction at scale, which RealKcat has just demonstrated on 2,158 articles, is the instrument that makes the question askable, and it arrived in 2025. Crossing that boundary is a second problem: the tag is owned by protein-expression methodologists, who treat it as bench craft and publish per-protein case reports ending in "assess case by case" (N1, N3, Stulajterova 2025), while kinetic-data quality is owned by curators and ML groups who stratify on what their schemas hold - organism, host, temperature, pH, mutational state - and whose vocabulary for construct variation stops at "expression system" (N2's phrasing exactly). Neither side's publishable unit is a corpus-level tag audit: the case report is cheap and publishable, the audit requires re-reading thousands of methods sections and, if it returns the equivalence band, produces a null that only licenses existing practice. The incentive gap is therefore real but one-sided, and it is the weakest part of the silence story: a determined reviewer could say the field has already decided the answer is "sometimes, so check your own enzyme", which is a defensible resolution of the weak form and would leave only the predictor-refitting half genuinely unasked.

Scores: Well-posedness 2/3 - the pairing unit, the estimator, the exposure and the decision bands are explicit and all four outcomes are closed by data, but presupposition P2 (that enough condition-matched pairs exist) is uncounted, so it is not yet shown that the described evidence can be assembled, and the declared estimand is the tag-plus-purification bundle rather than the tag, identified only where same-laboratory pairs exist. Significance 2/3 - consequence, audience and fecundity are concrete in both directions and the NO outcome is genuinely useful, but the magnitude at issue (0.2 log10) sits an order of magnitude below current predictor error and well inside the spread curators already tolerate, so part (b) may well return nothing and the audience's likely reaction to a modest YES is to note it and carry on.
