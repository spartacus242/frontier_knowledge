Domain(s): Medicine — clinical-trial methodology; research ethics; pharmacoepidemiological / health-services measurement

Question (canonical):
For completed randomised trials of a chronic therapy whose participants are linkable to routine dispensing and clinical records, with each participant's own dated last visit taken as the exposure: (i) what is the distribution over pre-declared treatment states (trial drug continued / pre-trial therapy resumed / third agent / nothing dispensed) at 3, 6 and 12 months after closeout, against a 12-month pre-randomisation look-back; and (ii) against a declared control series, what are the level and slope changes at the closeout date in the trial's primary outcome or declared routine proxy, pooled and by randomised arm?

Alternative formulations:
- What is the measured clinical cost of a trial ending, to the people who were in it?
- Treating trial closeout as an exposure, is there a detectable treatment and outcome discontinuity in participants' routine records after their last visit, and is it arm-dependent?

Motivating gap:
Intake read the field as dense with how-answers about closing trials (closeout operations, OLE design, audits of whether post-trial access is *stated* under Helsinki para 34 / CIOMS 6) and silent on what participants' records actually show afterwards. Gate 2 falsified the central factual claim: SPRINT's extended follow-up read post-closeout outpatient BP from routine EHR by arm, and UKPDS post-trial monitoring reports between-group HbA1c differences "lost after the first year". Full record: batches/2026-10-18-1255/UQ-20261018-25-record.md.

Presuppositions:
1. Participants of enough completed trials are identifiable in routine data with consent/regulatory permission — CONTESTED, partly established (113 linkage-extended trials; SPRINT EHR subset n=2,944; van Onzenoort 2011 pharmacy refills n=182).
2. Per-participant last-visit dates are obtainable, so closeout is a dated person-level exposure — UNVERIFIED by anything opened.
3. Pre-randomisation therapy observable in the same data — evidenced only by van Onzenoort 2011 (adversary 1).
4. Arm assignment releasable for blinded trials — CONTESTED; without it only the pooled limb is estimable.
5. Any discontinuity is attributable to closeout rather than to the end of monitoring or secular change — CONTESTED and unresolved; closeout and end-of-monitoring are the same event, and no named comparator separates them.
6. Primary outcome or proxy measurable in routine data at comparable intervals — established for surrogates (BP, HbA1c), not for event or patient-reported primaries.
7. (Added by adversary 2) Closeout is a single discrete person-level event — undercut by protocol-specified OLEs, rollover studies and continued-access programmes, in which the discontinuity is protocol-assigned and the placebo arm may *start* active drug.

What would count as an answer:
Per-participant dispensing and outcome series from 12 months pre-randomisation to 12 months post-closeout with pre-declared estimands: state fractions at 3/6/12 months; level and slope change at closeout against the declared control series; arm differences; intervals and attribution design stated. A near-zero discontinuity would be a substantive answer. An answer of shape (ii) already exists for index trials.

Significance — consequence: Real but thin as formulated. A large discontinuity would attach a magnitude to Helsinki para 34 / CIOMS 6 and give HTA modellers an empirical prior for treatment-waning; near-zero would retire a standing worry. But for surrogate outcomes the answer is already partly in hand and points one way (SPRINT, UKPDS-PTM, DPP washout), so the decision-relevant increment sits almost entirely in the treatment-state limb.
Significance — audience: Real and partly self-declared — ethics committees and sponsors; HTA agencies and modellers; trialists designing extensions; registry-holding pharmacoepidemiologists; a post-trial-care research community that states the gap in print ("To date, there is no research on the post-trial experience in relation to HIV care in low income settings", medRxiv 2020.11.14.20231712). Counter-pressure: where routine dispensing can see the trial drug (marketed agents), the ethical obligation bites least; where it bites most (unlicensed investigational product), dispensing records cannot see it.
Significance — fecundity: Does reversion magnitude predict legacy-effect decay across trials? Is reversion larger where the drug was unreimbursed at closeout? Can the monitoring effect be separately sized? Does arm-dependent reversion bias extension estimates in a signed direction?
Significance — anchoring: The legacy-effect / post-trial-monitoring anomaly; the HTA treatment-waning assumption; the stated-versus-delivered post-trial access gap.
