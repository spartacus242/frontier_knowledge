## Question (canonical)

[V0, intake] When a hospital activates an automated alert or quality measure whose trigger is a threshold on a manually charted clinical value (e.g. respiratory rate >= 22, SpO2 < 92%), does the distribution of subsequently charted values develop a density deficit immediately above the threshold and an excess immediately below it, beyond the pre-deployment digit-heaping pattern, and is any such distortion large enough to account for the documented post-deployment decay in the alert's measured positive predictive value?

[R1, adopted at Gate 1 — the one permitted reformulation, for well-posedness only] At sites with staggered go-live dates for a real-time automated EHR alert whose trigger is a threshold on a discretionary (non-device-captured) charted vital sign, and where threshold awareness among charting staff is documented per site (training material, order-set or flowsheet text):
(a) does the charted value's *discrete* mass function change at go-live in a pre-specified direction — a fall in the share of observations in the first k integer values at or above the trigger and a rise in the k values immediately below it, estimated on integer support (counterfactual-mass / bunching estimator built from the site's own pre-period heaping pattern, not a continuous-density McCrary test), with a pre-registered equivalence band on the excess-mass ratio (CI containing 1 = no distortion);
(b) is the same change absent in two negative-control series — device-auto-populated values at the same numeric threshold, and discretionary values at thresholds no live alert uses; and
(c) conditional on (a) being outside the band, what fraction of the *same site's own* measured change in that alert's PPV and sensitivity from its first to its later post-deployment year is reproduced by re-scoring the later cohort under the site's pre-go-live charting mass function, holding the label definition and case mix fixed?
Three pre-committed verdicts: distortion present and sufficient; present but quantitatively insufficient; absent within the equivalence band.
Reason for reformulation: V0 was not decidable as written on three counts — "the documented post-deployment decay" referred to decay reported for other systems at other sites rather than an estimand at the site being measured; "beyond the pre-deployment digit-heaping pattern" named no estimator, and the standard density-discontinuity machinery it gestured at (McCrary 2008; Cattaneo-Jansson-Ma) is defined for continuous running variables while charted respiratory rate is integer-valued with enormous atoms at 18 and 20 (Badawy et al. 2017), so "density deficit above the threshold" has no well-defined meaning at RR >= 22 without a discrete-support counterfactual; and threshold awareness was listed as a contested presupposition while the question's identification requires it, so it was moved into the inclusion criteria.

## Alternative formulations

1. Do the numbers clinicians type move away from an alert's threshold once the alert goes live, and does that movement explain why deployed early-warning scores get worse with age?
2. Is Goodhart's law measurable in the *input* layer of EHR decision support: does threshold-based triggering change the measurand that feeds it?

## Motivating gap

Intake joined three reported observations: (a) manually charted vital signs heap at round numbers, respiratory rate overwhelmingly at 18 and 20 (Badawy et al., BMJ Qual Saf 2017), and respiratory rate is a trigger term in qSOFA, NEWS and most sepsis alerts; (b) deployed early-warning systems underperform development estimates and drift downward after go-live (Epic Sepsis Model external validation, Wong et al., JAMA Intern Med 2021, AUC 0.63 vs vendor-claimed 0.76-0.83 — cited from the generator's intake; *not re-verified by an opened source in this run*, see Novelty notes); (c) threshold-linked quality measurement provokes documented behavioural response at the *coding* level after CMS SEP-1 (Rhee et al., JAMA 2017 — also not re-verified in this run).

Gate 2 refutes the gap as stated. The intake asserted "no published test asks whether the charted numbers themselves shift away from a live threshold". Such a test is published: Carey et al. (2009) compared the distribution of recorded systolic blood pressure in UK general practice before (2000-01) and after (2004-05) the QOF ≤150/90 target went live across ~3.16M readings, and found exactly the predicted asymmetry — the share recorded at 148-149 mmHg rose 2.3% -> 4.2% while 151-152 mmHg fell 1.8% -> 1.3%, with the true proportion above 150 estimated higher than the recorded one. The value-level question was therefore asked and answered, in primary-care BP under a payment target. What survives Gate 2 is narrower than the intake claimed: whether the same distortion occurs for *real-time automated alerts* (seconds-scale, patient-specific, no payment at stake) on *discretionary device-free* variables with device-captured negative controls, and limb (c) — the sufficiency decomposition against a deployed model's own measured performance change — which no opened source asks.

## Presuppositions

1. The trigger value is known, formally or informally, to charting staff. CONTESTED at intake; moved into R1's inclusion criteria (documented per site), so the study is defined only where it holds.
2. The charted value is partly discretionary rather than device-captured. ESTABLISHED for respiratory rate (Badawy et al. 2017: RR clustered at 18/20, kurtosis 23.9, while heart rate was approximately normal); contested for SpO2 and HR, which R1 uses as negative controls rather than exposures.
3. Pre- and post-go-live charting distributions are comparable apart from the alert. CONTESTED (device refresh, staffing, COVID-era documentation templates, flowsheet redesign — note that EWS go-live is itself known to change charting *behaviour*: McBride et al. 2005 found RR recording rose from ~30% to ~69% to ~91% of occupied beds after a new chart plus MEWS). Requires contemporaneous control wards and staggered multi-site go-live.
4. Deployed alert performance actually decays rather than appearing to decay through label or case-mix drift. CONTESTED; R1 makes this a dependent limb (c) measured on the site's own PPV series, not an imported premise.
5. Go-live dates, exact thresholds and per-observation charting metadata are recoverable at usable N. Feasibility presupposition; plausible in multi-site EHR research networks, unverified here.
6. A post-go-live discontinuity in a discretionary variable implies behavioural distortion rather than physiology. CONTESTED and the inferential crux: physiology does not know where a threshold is, so appearance only after go-live, only in discretionary variables, and only at the live threshold is the identifying argument. Must be pre-registered.
7. (Added at Gate 1) The charted value's support permits a well-defined "immediately above/below" contrast. PARTLY FALSE as intake assumed: RR mass sits at 18 and 20, the common trigger is 22, and the ordinary heaping pattern already puts near-zero mass at 21 and 23 — so the first-limb estimand must be defined on integer support against the site's own pre-period mass function, and a "null" can be produced by heaping alone.

## What would count as an answer

A pre-registered multi-site study using staggered go-live dates, delivering: (1) the limb-(a) excess/missing-mass estimates on integer support with the pre-specified equivalence band; (2) both negative controls from limb (b) — device-auto-populated values at the same numeric threshold and discretionary values at no-alert thresholds; (3) if and only if (a) falls outside the band, the limb-(c) decomposition: the share of the site's own measured PPV/sensitivity change reproduced by re-scoring the later cohort under the pre-go-live charting mass function, with the label definition and case mix held fixed. Each of the three pre-committed verdicts is reportable; the null (absent within band) kills a widely assumed folk mechanism and is as publishable as the positive. Minimum disconfirmation: distortion inside the equivalence band at every site with documented threshold awareness, or distortion present in the device-captured control, which would indict the design rather than the clinicians.

## Novelty — how this differs from each

vs Carey 2009: this is the candidate's weak point, not its strength. Carey already answers the candidate's first limb in substance — charted values move away from a threshold once the threshold is live — so "does the distribution develop an excess below and a deficit above" is asked and answered for a clinical measurement under a known cut-off. Remaining differences are the setting and the second limb, not the phenomenon: Carey's trigger is an annual, practice-level, financial, retrospectively audited target (no patient-specific consequence at the moment of charting), whereas R1's trigger fires in seconds and summons a colleague to the bedside; Carey's variable is device-assisted BP with no device-captured control series, whereas R1 requires discretionary RR with auto-populated SpO2/HR as negative controls on the same numeric threshold; Carey estimates recording bias as an epidemiological correction, whereas limb (c) asks whether the distortion is quantitatively sufficient to reproduce a deployed model's own measured PPV/sensitivity change. Carey also had no alert and therefore no model performance to decompose.
vs Kämpfen 2023: the same statistical object (density discontinuity in a recorded clinical value at a threshold) used for the opposite purpose. There it is an RDD validity check on research-protocol measurements by study fieldworkers with no stake in the cut-off, at a single cross-section, and the null finding is the desired result; here it is the estimand itself, measured on clinician-charted operational data across a go-live, with distortion as the hypothesis and an equivalence band specified so the null is also informative. No pre/post contrast, no negative controls, no performance limb there.
vs Badawy 2017: Badawy supplies the candidate's premise (RR is discretionary and heaps; HR does not) and is explicitly silent on thresholds — single-centre, one period, no go-live, no alert, no threshold-referenced estimand. Its heaping at 18/20 is also the main nuisance R1 must model, because the ordinary heaping pattern alone leaves near-zero mass at 21-23 regardless of any alert.
vs McBride 2005 and Gravelle 2010 (secondary): both establish that threshold-linked measurement provokes behavioural response at adjacent margins — *whether* a value is recorded, and *who* is counted — while leaving the recorded number itself untested. Together with Carey they bracket the candidate: the margin above (coding/denominators) and the margin below (recording completeness) are studied, and the value margin is studied for payment targets but not for real-time alerts.

## Significance — consequence

If yes, and sufficient: every post-deployment recalibration, monitoring statistic and external-validation estimate computed on charted inputs downstream of a live threshold is contaminated by an endogenous measurand, so the standard remedy for drift (retrain on recent local data) encodes the distortion instead of correcting it; threshold-based triggering is then self-undermining by construction and design shifts toward device-captured inputs, continuous scores whose cut-points are not visible to charting staff, or triggers held in software the charting user cannot anticipate. It also reframes a share of the reported post-deployment decay of early-warning systems from a property of models to a property of measurement, with a safety reading that is genuinely ambiguous: suppressed triggers could mean missed deterioration or an appropriate local correction of an over-sensitive alert, and distinguishing those becomes the next question. If present but insufficient, the field gets a bounded nuisance term and the decay literature keeps its case-mix and label-drift explanations. If absent within the band, a widely repeated folk mechanism ("staff chart around the alert") is killed with a number, and the Carey result is shown not to transport from payment targets to real-time alerts — which is itself informative about what kind of incentive distorts a measurand.

## Significance — audience

Health-system model-governance and assurance teams, who currently monitor deployed-model outputs (AUC, PPV, alert volume) and would have to add input-distribution surveillance at the trigger value with go-live as the event; quality-measure designers at CMS/NQF, whose SEP-1-style threshold measures have documented coding-level response and would face evidence of value-level response; EWS and CDS vendors choosing between threshold triggers and continuous scores, and deciding how much of the trigger to expose in training material and flowsheet text; regulators of adaptive clinical software (predetermined change-control plans), for whom an endogenous input distribution breaks the assumption that post-market data can validate a locked or updating model. Honest discount: the actionable change for the first group is modest and cheap (one added monitoring chart), and a positive finding is organisationally awkward for exactly the institutions holding the data, which bears on whether they would publish rather than on whether they would care.

## Significance — fecundity

Opens: is there an optimal visibility policy for a trigger value (does concealing the cut-point preserve the measurand, and at what cost to clinician trust and to the escalation the alert exists to produce)? Can a distortion-robust trigger be built on the invariant part of a heaped distribution, or on device-captured surrogates of a discretionary variable? Is threshold-induced value distortion a harm, a benign re-anchoring, or an implicit clinician override carrying prognostic information the model lacks (testable: do suppressed-trigger patients deteriorate less than threshold-crossers)? Does the distortion decay, persist or grow with alert tenure, and does it transfer when the threshold moves (a within-site falsification test the staggered design already supports)? And generally, which incentive structures distort a measurand: Carey's annual payment target did, so does a seconds-scale patient-specific alert do more or less?

## Significance — anchoring

Goodhart's law and performative prediction (arXiv 2601.04447; Mendler-Dünner et al., NeurIPS 2022) — strategic-feature behaviour theorised in ML and, as far as the opened sources show, not measured in clinical charting; the post-deployment performance-decay literature for early-warning systems (medRxiv 2025.07.31.25332542 and the Epic-sepsis critiques, where decay is attributed to case mix, label and site shift); measurement reactivity, digit heaping and digit preference in vital signs (Badawy 2017); the manipulation-testing toolkit from public economics (McCrary density tests, bunching estimators, manipulation-robust RDD, arXiv 2009.07551) and its reported-value analogues at regulatory compliance limits; P4P gaming at the register and exception margins (Gravelle 2010).

## Reason for silence

Not fully silent, which is the finding: the question was asked and answered one disciplinary step away (Carey 2009, payment target, primary care, BP) and has not crossed into inpatient real-time alerting. The credible mechanism is a disciplinary boundary reinforced by an incentive gap. The estimator belongs to health economics and public finance, where bunching and density-discontinuity tests were built for tax kinks, test-score cut-offs and reported compliance data; the artefact belongs to clinical informatics, which measures model outputs and classes charted inputs under "data quality" — a nuisance to be imputed or cleaned, not an endogenous measurand with a response function. The two literatures share almost no venues or reviewers, and the informatics framing supplies no reason to put the go-live date on the x-axis of a value histogram. The incentive gap sharpens it: the parties holding go-live dates, exact thresholds and per-observation charting metadata are the parties a positive result would show gaming their own safety alerts, and charting accuracy is institutionally framed as individual documentation quality (a disciplinary matter) rather than as predictable system behaviour, so the natural data holder has little reason to run the test and some reason not to publish it. "No one thought of it" is not available here and is not claimed — Carey's group thought of it in 2009.
