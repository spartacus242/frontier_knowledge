# UQ-20261021-11 — Adversary 1 log

## Restatement (my own words)

Train the same language-model recipe several times, changing only the random seed. Each run develops a few residual-stream channels with enormous activations. The candidate asks whether those channels sit at the SAME coordinate indices from run to run — scored as Jaccard overlap of the top-m index sets per layer (and per token-position class, at several thresholds, at two model sizes) against a uniform shuffle null and an "at-initialisation ranking" null — and whether the full per-channel magnitude ranking correlates across runs (Spearman). Framed for quantization/kernel people as: can an outlier-channel layout be fixed once per recipe, or must it be re-measured for every checkpoint?

## The key argument (MY OWN DERIVATION, written out so every step can be checked; not quoted from any source)

Claim: for any pretraining recipe whose architecture, initialisation law and optimiser are equivariant under relabelling of the residual-stream coordinates, the law of the trained network is invariant under that relabelling, so ANY statistic of coordinate identity computed between two INDEPENDENT seeds has exactly the uniform-permutation-null distribution. In particular the per-layer Jaccard overlap of top-m index sets between independent seeds is hypergeometric (= the candidate's null (a)), and the Spearman rho between two seeds' full per-channel magnitude vectors has the independent-rankings null distribution (mean 0). This holds for every layer, every token-position class, every threshold rule and every model size, and also for the "different-corpus run" null and for the at-init ranking of a DIFFERENT seed.

Steps. (1) Let π be a permutation of the d_model residual indices. Apply it simultaneously to the columns of the embedding, the rows of W_q, W_k, W_v, W_up, W_gate, the columns of W_o, W_down, every residual LayerNorm/RMSNorm gain and bias vector, and the rows of the unembedding. LayerNorm's mean and variance are sums over the residual dimension (permutation-invariant); gains and biases act elementwise and are permuted with the activations; rotary embeddings act on head dimensions AFTER projection; SwiGLU/GELU act on MLP hidden units, not on residual coordinates; the residual add is coordinatewise. Hence the permuted network computes the identical function on every input: an exact symmetry of the loss. (2) Standard initialisations draw every weight entry i.i.d. (Pythia config: small_init / wang_init; Llama: scaled normal), so the init law is invariant under π. (3) Adam/AdamW, weight decay, global-norm gradient clipping and dropout masks are all coordinatewise or norm-based, hence equivariant; the data stream indexes embedding ROWS (vocabulary) and never touches residual coordinates. (4) Therefore if seed s yields trained weights θ with top-m set S, the relabelled init yields π(θ) with top-m set π(S), with equal probability: the law of S is invariant under all π. S_d acts transitively on m-subsets, so S is UNIFORM over m-subsets. (5) Two independent seeds give two independent uniform m-subsets: |S_A ∩ S_B| is hypergeometric, which is exactly the uniform index-permutation null the candidate names. For the full magnitude vector, the rank vector is uniform over permutations (ties have probability zero), independent across seeds, so Spearman rho is distributed exactly as under independence. (6) The only structural breaker of exact equivariance in practice is floating-point reduction ORDER over the residual (contraction) dimension in matmuls and norm sums, a roundoff-level effect tied to hardware tiling; everything else in the Pythia-160M config above (rotary on head dims, iid init, Adam, zero dropout, model-parallel 1) is exactly equivariant.

Scope check, written down: the argument needs (i) iid or exchangeable init over residual coordinates, (ii) no coordinate-specific hyperparameter (no per-coordinate learning rate, no block-structured training format over residual dims, no designated sink channel), (iii) independent seeds, (iv) an optimiser that is coordinatewise. The candidate's own regime (PolyPythias seeds, Pythia architecture, Adam) satisfies all four by the served config. A recipe that deliberately designates a channel, trains in a block-scaled format along d_model, or re-uses part of the init across seeds, would escape it and would be a DIFFERENT question. A measured overlap above null would therefore be evidence of roundoff-order symmetry breaking or of a shared-init leak, not of a recipe-level layout.

Why this is not the "privileged basis" point the record relies on: elementwise operations (RMSNorm gains, per-channel scales) break the ROTATION symmetry O(d) of the residual stream down to the permutation-and-sign group; they do not break the permutation symmetry. The record's anchoring sentence — "permutation symmetry and linear mode connectivity, where the residual stream is the one place the symmetry is already broken elementwise" — conflates the two, and its P1 inference that "the candidate needs NO cross-run matching step" because "the basis is literally shared" rests on that conflation. Both opened cross-seed studies behave as the symmetry predicts: Gurnee et al. (record's F3) match MLP neurons by activation correlation, and Brown/Vyas/Bansal define convergence "up to permutation" and maximise over permutations. Neither states the theorem for residual coordinates; the derivation above is mine, and per the ledger rule a kill resting on an unsourced derivation is graded accordingly below (see attack 1 and 4 statuses and the verdict line).
