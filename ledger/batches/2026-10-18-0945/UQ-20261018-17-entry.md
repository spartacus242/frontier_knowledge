Question (canonical): Across the published in vitro reconstitution literature for a declared process family, what is the distribution of the ratio between each component's concentration in the reconstitution and its measured concentration in the cell where the process is claimed to occur, and in what fraction of reconstitutions does the demonstrated sufficiency fail when every component is held at its cellular concentration?
Alternative formulations: Does the reconstitution literature show that purified components are sufficient for a cellular process, or only that they are sufficient at concentrations the cell never reaches? / For each published reconstitution, how far above cellular concentration was each component pushed to make the process run, and does that fold-excess predict whether additional cellular factors were later found to be required? (One Gate 1 reformulation, V2, recorded in the record: one pre-declared process family and registry-defined frame, a declared comparator ladder — cell-average, compartment-average, bounded local-enrichment allowance of ≤10^2 — log10 ratios with two-sided uncertainty, thresholds at 10^0.5 / 10^1 / 10^2, and a bench subset re-run at rung (a) against a pre-declared detection limit with buffer/crowding/temperature as declared arms.)
Motivating gap: Intake held that the sufficiency claim of a reconstitution rests on an untested concentration presupposition, that absolute proteomics (PaxDb) now makes the comparison checkable at literature scale, and — load-bearing — that the worry is argued only qualitatively inside the condensate subfield and "nowhere quantified as a distribution over a frame". That central negative is false as stated: the fold-excess distribution was published in 2021, over a declared frame, with PaxDb as the comparator, in the candidate's own declared secondary subfield (Prior 1).
Presuppositions: (1) reconstitution papers report component concentrations in extractable units — contested and evidenced biased (Prior 1 could form the ratio for 75/89 frame proteins and 30 of those 75 had no usable Csat); (2) a matched cellular concentration exists per component — contested (absolute proteomics covers a minority well; copy-number→concentration needs an uncertain volume); (3) cell-average total concentration is the right comparator — contested and against the candidate's interest (Prior 1's authors call PaxDb values "theoretical lower limits"); (4) "sufficiency fails at cellular concentration" is decidable with a declared detection limit — must be designed in, and Prior 2's conditions were chosen post hoc against the in-cell target with no held-out test; (5) the in vitro process is the same process as the cellular one — contested case by case (Prior 2's endpoint is partitioning and FRAP, not function); (6) later discovery of a required factor is codable without hindsight bias — no precedent found anywhere; (7) buffer, crowding and temperature are secondary to concentration — contested and probably false (Prior 2's best match needed pH 5.8, 30 °C, 300 mM KOAc, ATP).
What would count as an answer: a pre-declared frame; per (paper, component) in-vitro concentration paired with a matched cellular concentration carrying its own uncertainty; the log-fold-excess distribution and the fraction of reconstitutions above pre-declared thresholds; for a pre-specified subset, a bench re-run with all components at cellular concentration against a declared detection limit with buffer/crowding as declared arms; and a pre-registered test of association between maximum fold-excess and subsequent identification of additional required factors. Small fold-excesses with sufficiency surviving the drop would be an equally decisive answer. Gate 2 finding: for the condensate family that answer is already partly on the record and it is mixed.
