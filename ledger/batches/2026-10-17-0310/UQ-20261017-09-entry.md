**The original kill does not stand.** A checker opened both papers. arXiv:2512.02393 (Liu et al., "Process-Centric Analysis of Agentic Software Systems") does build Graphectory graphs over 4,000 trajectories (500 SWE-bench Verified issues x 8 agent-model pairs) and its solved-run sentence is verbatim — "An important observation is that inefficiencies, although to a lesser extent, are prevalent in resolved instances as well." But its measures are counts of ACTIONS: Loop Count is the "Number of times the agent decides to repeat a previously executed action", Average Loop Length "The average number of actions involved before an agent decides to repeat". It defines no single inefficiency rate, its anti-patterns are prevalence percentages over trajectories, and **the words token, tokens, dollar and waste do not appear in it**. arXiv:2607.22585 (Vats & Golev) reports tokens — not dollars — per solved task across 2 models x 3 harnesses on 50 tasks, with 95% paired-task bootstrap CIs, amortised as "the cell-wide sum of tokens_total divided by the cell's solved count" with "Failed and infrastructure-error trials ... included in the numerator"; it is a descriptive side-by-side, not a variance decomposition, and it presents no re-ranking, only a recommendation "that coding-agent leaderboards adopt harness-model pairs as the unit of evaluation". Asked directly whether either reports the fraction of token or dollar spend that was discarded rework, and whether either re-ranks a leaderboard net of rework, the checker answered NO to both for both. So the attack-2 Unresolved is not sourceable and **falls to Partial**, and the Gate 2 regrade to Low that rested on it is withdrawn; novelty returns to Medium.
**The region is nonetheless much more crowded than the entry knew.** A second sweep of nine queries in cost, observability and context-engineering vocabularies found four 2026 papers the batch never surfaced, each nearer than the two it killed on. arXiv:2509.23586 (Trajectory Reduction, Trae Agent) says waste is "widespread in almost all trajectories" from a manual review of 100, gives a worked case where "the waste costs 1714 tokens out of 1995 tokens", and reports that its reflection module "removes 69.2%-77.4% of the content it processes", cutting input tokens 39.9-59.7% and net cost 21.1-35.9%. arXiv:2607.12161 (Token Reduction Is Not Cost Reduction) bounds an addressable cost share at about 4% conservative, 12% central and 25-30% upper, and reports an unattributed billing residual of 8.7% [7.2, 10.1] which it does not call waste. arXiv:2608.01347 (Prompt-Induced Waste in Coding Agents) defines waste operationally — a paired median reasoning ratio above 1.5 with a CI lower bound above 1.1 and no success gain — and reports about 1.9x median cost for runs carrying one unused branch. arXiv:2604.22750 reports that "around 50% of them are repeated actions on the same file" for three models. None is the candidate's estimand: removal rates by an LLM judge, scenario bounds, prompt-variant ratios and action shares are each a different quantity from the share of a run's consumed tokens that is rework under a counterfactual rule, and none re-ranks a leaderboard net of rework. **The new rejection ground: the rework rule names no well-defined quantity on the instrument the question proposes.** The canonical question deliberately replaced the decidable rule ("no later step reads it") with counterfactual non-use — removal from every later step's context would not change any later action — and proposes to apply it to released HAL traces. Both halves of that fail against opened sources. First, the counterfactual is not decidable from a recorded trace: every published step-attribution method obtains it by re-executing the agent. Causal Agent Replay (arXiv:2606.08275) "re-executes the trajectory forward under the same stochastic policy" with K forward rollouts per intervention and Shapley coalitions exponential in the worst case; CausalFlow (arXiv:2605.25338) replaces each candidate step with K=3 proposals and recomputes downstream steps; Credit Without Ground Truth (arXiv:2608.19760) audits credit signals against executed-replay ground truth. Second, and worse, the rule's predicate is almost never literally true even with nothing removed. "On Randomness in Agentic Evals" (arXiv:2602.07150, ICLR 2026, about 60,000 SWE-Bench-Verified trajectories, 10 runs per configuration) finds that single-run pass@1 estimates "vary by 2.2 to 6.0 percentage points depending on which run is selected", with SDs above 1.5 points at temperature 0 (Devstral-2/r2e-gym 35.4% +/- 1.7, range 32.0-37.8), and that trajectories diverge within tens of tokens at temperature 0 — median first divergence at token 56 for DeepSWE-preview and token 32 for Qwen3-32B, with two temperature-0 runs on django-9296 matching for 93 tokens, diverging at token 94, and ending in opposite outcomes. So an ablated re-run shows changed later actions whatever the removed step contributed, and the rule's yes/no has no fixed value unless the continuation is deterministic. Credit Without Ground Truth adds that the replay target is undefined at 13.1%-26.8% of decision points across two policies and that only 30.5% of points with defined ground truth show a nonzero replay contrast at achievable resolution.
That is a presupposition failure on attack 4 — the question needs counterfactual non-use to be determinable from the released traces it names, and it is not — compounded by vagueness and dissolution on attacks 5 and 8: the quantity becomes well defined only if restated as a distributional contrast against a noise floor, estimated from K rollouts per arm with a stated equivalence margin, or under a fixed-seed local model where replay is exact. Every one of those requires re-executing the agent, which the released-trace instrument cannot do, and none of the three published methods implements removal from later context, the exact intervention the rule names. **Unresolved on attack 4 is fatal, so the Rejection stands on that ground alone**, with Low-to-Medium novelty no longer load-bearing and well-posedness 2/3 independently barring Validated as the batch already recorded.
