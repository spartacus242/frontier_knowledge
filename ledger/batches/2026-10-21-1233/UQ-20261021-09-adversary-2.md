# UQ-20261021-09 — Adversary 2 log (independent; first log not read)

## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take every building in a region covered by the free European satellite ground-motion service. For each one, work out from the satellite data how fast one side of the building is sinking relative to the other, and raise a flag whenever that exceeds the limiting value some textbook settlement-damage criterion prints. Question: how often is the flag right? Concretely — on a building frame and a damage cut (Burland D>=2) both fixed before any data is pulled, what are the sensitivity, specificity and ROC/AUC of each criterion's own limiting value, and, on a verification sub-sample that pins the damage prevalence down, its positive predictive value; counting only criteria whose input the satellite product actually supplies (so Boscardin-Cording is declared not evaluable for want of horizontal strain); and how much of any failure comes from using a recent *rate* where the criteria want *cumulative* distortion since construction.

In short: the candidate wants the error rates of a geotechnical rule of thumb used as a population screening test, with a satellite as the instrument.
