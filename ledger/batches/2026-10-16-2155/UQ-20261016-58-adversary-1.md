## Restatement (my own words, before reading the generator's framing twice)

Most protostome animals carry a natural scission in their large ribosomal subunit, so their 28S rRNA runs as two sub-28S fragments instead of one tall peak. The RIN-type score that sequencing labs use to decide whether RNA is "good enough" was built on animals whose 28S is intact. The candidate asks two measurement questions and one exploratory one:
(a) did scoring these animals on that instrument actually cost them anything countable — lower recorded integrity scores, and more samples rejected or sent back for re-extraction — relative to break-free taxa, holding preservation/tissue/protocol/input fixed;
(b) in matched RNA from one break-bearing and one break-free species degraded in the same graded series, what RIN in the break-bearing species corresponds to the same real degradation as a given RIN in the break-free one (so: by how many RIN points should a "RIN >= 7" gate be relaxed for break taxa);
(c) net of research effort, body size, collection difficulty and preservation, does break status predict whether a taxon has any transcriptome at all.
In short: has a vertebrate-calibrated ribosome ruler been quietly taxing the animals whose ribosomes are built differently, how much, and what is the conversion factor?
