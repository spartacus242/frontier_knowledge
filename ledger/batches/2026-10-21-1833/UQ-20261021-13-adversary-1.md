## Restatement (my own words)

Take preference datasets where each pairwise label carries the identity of the person who gave it (or at least their vendor / shift / guideline version). Fit a logistic (Bradley–Terry) model of the label with crossed random effects for item, annotator and annotator-group, and a linear-probability companion. Ask three things: (a) how much of the label variance sits at the annotator and group levels (ICC on both scales, with CIs); (b) given the labels-per-annotator distribution actually present, what Kish design effect and effective N follow, and how that moves with the cluster definition; (c) at a fixed number of labels, does reward-model accuracy depend on how many distinct annotators contributed, in the way N_eff rather than N predicts. In short: when a dataset says one million preference pairs, how many independent judgments is that, measured rather than assumed, and does the reward model care.

My own arithmetic (labelled as such): from S2, 33,000 conversations / 13,000+ users gives a MEAN cluster size of at most ~2.5 votes per user. Under Kish's equal-cluster formula Deff = 1 + (n-1)rho, a mean n of 2.5 bounds Deff above by ~2.5 even at rho = 1. The unequal-cluster form (Deff built on sum n_i^2 / N) can be much larger if a few users cast thousands of votes; the page prints no per-user distribution, so I cannot carry that through, and the bound is stated for the equal-cluster case only.
