Domain(s): Medicine — infusion therapy and vascular access; hospital pharmacy (injectable admixture characterisation); nursing/patient safety. Method owner: survival biostatistics (nested/shared frailty, variance partition) and prediction-model calibration.
Question (canonical): In hospital systems where infusate history is attributable to a SPECIFIC peripheral IV catheter (line-level administration documentation or validated pump-to-line association), fitting a cause-specific nested frailty survival model for catheter failure with random effects for patient, inserter, ward and device lot plus a per-admixture infusate term: (a) on the log-hazard scale, what is the variance partition coefficient of the infusate component for each failure mode (phlebitis, infiltration/extravasation, occlusion, dislodgement, infection), with an interval — a declared DESCRIPTIVE decomposition, any causal reading of "attributable" demoted to a secondary analysis under a stated identification strategy for confounding by acuity; and (b) does the incumbent binary access rule (osmolarity above ~600 mOsm/L, or pH outside ~5–9, implying central access) discriminate and calibrate against (i) the fitted infusate-attributable hazard and (ii) extravasation injury as a co-primary endpoint, as well as an index estimated from the same data under internal–external cross-validation across systems? (V2; the one Gate 1 reformulation. V1 and the reason for reformulation are in batches/2026-10-18-0130/UQ-20261018-05-record.md.)
Alternative formulations: (1) Is the peripheral-versus-central access decision rule calibrated against the catheter failures it exists to prevent? (2) How much of peripheral IV failure is caused by what was put through the line rather than by the line, the patient, the inserter or the ward?
Motivating gap: Silence mapping. PIVC insertion is the commonest invasive hospital procedure and ~a third to a half of catheters fail before therapy completes; the literature is dense with "how" (securement, dressings, replacement policy, insertion technique) and thin on the EXPOSURE, while the rule gating which drugs may enter a peripheral vein is a pair of fixed numeric thresholds reproduced across standards without field calibration against failure. Manrique-Rodríguez et al. 2021 (read in full) apply those thresholds to 307 admixtures, place 40% in the high-risk tier, and state that "human tolerance of pH and osmolarity has not been well studied" and the osmolarity threshold is "not well defined". CORRECTION at Gate 4: the record's claim that the field is "close to silent on what the exposure is" is false — Ryder et al. 2020 ran a randomised, blinded ovine trial on exactly that exposure. The gap is narrower than the record states and concerns the estimand, not the topic.
Presuppositions: (1) line-level infusate attribution — was CONTESTED, now ESTABLISHED in at least the regime V2 conditions on (Yasuda et al. 2025 attribute noradrenaline dose to specific PIVCs, 3,410 catheters, 23 ICUs); generalisation beyond critical care unevidenced. (2) failure modes recorded and distinguishable — CONTESTED; subjective phlebitis grading and reason-less removals push ascertainment error into the residual and bias the infusate VPC downward. (3) per-product pH/osmolarity retrievable — ESTABLISHED (307 measured admixtures; values move with diluent and concentration). (4) thresholds intended as failure predictions rather than conventions encoding extravasation-injury severity — was the strongest reframing available to a critic, now CORROBORATED twice from opened text (Bahl 2022: the INS list is "based on infusate osmolarity thresholds that increase the likelihood of these complications"; Ryder 2020 frames osmolarity and pH as "risk factors for midline catheter failure"); but the rule actually operationalised in practice is the INS vesicant/irritant LIST plus MAGIC compatibility, not the numeric pair, so limb (b) is partly mis-aimed as written. (5) lot and inserter recoverable as variance components — CONTESTED for lot. (6) catheter failure patient-relevant — ESTABLISHED. (7) infusate assignment not wholly confounded by acuity — CONTESTED; handled by making the primary estimand descriptive.
What would count as an answer: Pre-registered analysis in ≥2 systems with line-level attribution reporting (a) the log-hazard variance partition with the infusate share and interval, by failure mode; (b) discrimination and calibration of the incumbent rule against the infusate component and, separately, against extravasation injury; (c) an empirically derived per-admixture peripheral-vein-burden index under internal–external cross-validation; (d) the count and direction of access decisions that would flip. A small infusate share, or thresholds that already capture what there is to capture, answers it negatively — the more useful direction for standards panels.

Significance — consequence: If the infusate share is substantial and the incumbent thresholds discriminate it poorly, the access rule is miscalibrated in a measurable direction and the deliverable is a replacement index with a counted number of decisions flipping both ways — drugs now barred from peripheral veins with little measured failure burden would stop generating central lines (each carrying its own thrombosis and bloodstream-infection hazard), drugs now passed would be flagged. The rule binds 40% of 307 standard adult admixtures, so recalibration moves a large traffic. A small share, or thresholds that already capture what there is, retires a recurring criticism and redirects effort to the patient, inserter and ward components. The negative is live rather than rhetorical: three cohorts (Bahl aHR 1.17; Yasuda HR 1.06; Kia's medication type below rate and care unit) all point weakly.
