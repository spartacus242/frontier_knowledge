# UQ-20261015-07 — Gate 4, adversary #1

## Restatement (my words, not the generator's)

When the frozen (~100 K) and the warm (≥270 K) crystal structure of one protein, grown in the same lattice, disagree about which rotamer or backbone state a residue mostly occupies, which of the two does AlphaFold 2/3 (and the open AF3 clones) produce when you sample it several times — and does any lean toward the frozen state survive once you separate proteins the model saw in training from those it did not? Secondary: is the lean larger than what a plain PDB-derived rotamer library would already give you?

Stripped of framing: "Does a model trained on a frozen-structure database reproduce frozen-structure side chains?" — with a memorisation control bolted on.

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional at best** — no attack among 1, 2, 4 is Unresolved, but attack 7 is Unresolved: the record's disciplinary-boundary mechanism is contradicted by verified author overlap (Mobley; Keedy; Lindorff-Larsen) across the RT-crystallography benchmark, cryo-artefact, and AF side-chain literatures, and the only credible remaining reason for silence is that the answer is assumed ("AF learns the PDB mode; the PDB is cryo"), which caps significance. Novelty of the explicit formulation holds on the searches that ran (Medium, with the caveat that non-English and full-text bioRxiv coverage is missing and the Bradford-2021 citer list was not read). To promote: evidence that a specialist in both camps regards the result as open (not assumed), and a power estimate showing the same-crystal-form, major-conformer-switch, exposure-stratified residue set is non-trivially populated.

**Single strongest killing point:** the question is unasked because its headline answer is already assumed by the people positioned to ask it — AF2 is documented to favour PDB-modal rotamers and the PDB mode is the cryo mode (Fraser 2011: databases biased "toward smaller, overpacked, and unrealistically unique models") — so the record's reason-for-silence collapses into expected triviality, and the residual that is genuinely open (lean beyond a cryo-derived rotamer prior, in an exposure-stratified residue set) may be too small and too sparsely populated to measure.
