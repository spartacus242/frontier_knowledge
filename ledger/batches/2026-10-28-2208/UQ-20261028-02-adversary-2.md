## Restatement (my own words, before any search)

Take a real quadratic field Q(sqrt d), d > 0 a fundamental discriminant, and its Scholz reflection
partner Q(sqrt -3d), which is imaginary. Scholz's theorem pins the 3-ranks together to within one:
r_3(d) <= r_3(-3d) <= r_3(d) + 1, so for each d exactly one of two things happens — the imaginary
partner's 3-rank either equals the real field's ("+0") or exceeds it by exactly one ("+1"). The
question is: what decides which, statistically? Specifically, condition on the WHOLE 3-class group of
the real field being isomorphic to a named finite abelian 3-group G, and ask for the probability of the
"+1" case as d runs over fundamental discriminants up to X. Two sub-questions: (i) does that measured
probability equal 3^-(a+1), where a = rank(G), which is what the published rank-level prediction says;
and (ii) does it depend on anything about G BEYOND its 3-rank — i.e. is the pair's joint 3-class-group
law a function of ranks only, or does the finer module structure of one side carry information about the
other that Scholz's inequality does not already force?

Note the shape: for G of order <= 27, "beyond rank" has content only in two cells — rank 1
(Z/3 vs Z/9 vs Z/27) and rank 2 ((Z/3)^2 vs Z/9 x Z/3). That observation is MY OWN REASONING about the
enumeration the repair declares, not a source finding.
