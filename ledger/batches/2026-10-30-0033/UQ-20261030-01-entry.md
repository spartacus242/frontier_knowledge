Question (canonical): In two or more symmetrically dividing mammalian cell lines, over >=150 live-tracked divisions per line, with three damage species labelled simultaneously in the same cell — (i) aged/depolarised mitochondria, (ii) cytosolic misfolded-protein inclusions, (iii) lipofuscin-autofluorescent lysosomes — and the daughter order in every division fixed by one species-independent rule (the daughter inheriting the older mother centriole), is the matrix of within-division Spearman rank correlations between the volume-normalised signed asymmetries a_s = (x1 − x2)/(x1 + x2) all-positive (one sorting axis), indistinguishable from a species-label-permutation null (independent per-species mechanisms), or negative (active routing of different junk to different daughters)?

Alternative formulations: (1) When a mammalian cell dumps damage on one daughter, is that one dumping decision or three independent ones? (2) Is "the damaged daughter" one thing, three things, or a category error?

Motivating gap: Asymmetric partitioning at division is reported separately, by different groups and in different organisms, for each damage species alone. Nothing any of the four readers opened measures whether two or more distinct damage species share a daughter in symmetrically dividing mammalian cells — the difference between one sorting mechanism and several. The closest existing work measures within-division co-inheritance for one autophagic/lysosomal family in mouse HSC divisions its own abstract calls asymmetric, as a thresholded frequency rather than a correlation of signed normalised asymmetries.
