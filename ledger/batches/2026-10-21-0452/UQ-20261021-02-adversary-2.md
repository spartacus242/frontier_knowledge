## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

In a health system whose electronic order records separately store who typed an order, who authorised it and who cosigned it, and whose audit logs timestamp those actions: (a) how often is the clinician named as prescriber not the clinician who actually chose the drug, measured against a declared gold standard (observation or blinded record review) and broken out by drug class, with the share of orders having no single chooser reported separately; (b) how well do the order/audit-log fields work as a proxy for "who chose" (sensitivity/specificity against that gold standard); and (c) on one cohort, if you re-run a prescriber-preference instrumental-variable analysis and a prescriber-targeted audit-and-feedback evaluation with exposure moved from the named prescriber to the person credited with the decision, how much and in which direction do the IV point estimate, its first-stage strength, and the feedback effect move, judged against a pre-declared equivalence margin?

Put bluntly: *is the name in the data the person whose behaviour we think we are estimating, and what does getting it wrong cost the estimate?*
