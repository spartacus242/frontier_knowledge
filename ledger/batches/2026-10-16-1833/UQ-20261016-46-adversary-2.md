## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Some big multimodal training sets are not shipped as data. They are shipped as a spreadsheet of links plus
per-row metadata, and you build the actual dataset by going out and fetching the links yourself. Two claims
follow and the candidate wants both tested.

First: when you fetch the links some years after publication, a fraction of them no longer give you a usable
image. Is the set you end up with a *random* subset of the original list, or does it lean — more hosts of one
kind, more of one language, more of one licence, more of one quality-score bucket — in a way you could predict
from the columns already in the spreadsheet? Measure that as retention ratios per column with confidence
intervals, plus how well a model fitted on those columns alone can tell a survivor from a casualty.

Second, and this is the part the candidate cares about: does it matter to the model? Train several seeds on
"what still downloads today" and several seeds on a same-size random draw from the original list, same compute,
and see whether the benchmark gap exceeds the seed-to-seed noise.

Shorter: has "trained on LAION-2B" quietly become a date-stamped claim rather than a dataset name, and does the
date change the model? I grant that is a sharp question. My job is to break it.

## The missed prior (sourced)

**D. Carlini et al., "Poisoning Web-Scale Training Datasets is Practical", arXiv:2302.10149 (v1 2023-02-20,
v2 2024-05-06).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10149 — opened, body read.

Quoted verbatim from the paper:
- Table 1 rows, column "Data from expired domains": **"LAION-2B-en [83] 2323 2022 ✗ 0.29%"**, "LAION-400M [84]
  408 2021 ✗ 0.71%", "LAION-2B-multi 0.55%", "LAION-1B-nolang 0.37%", "CC-3M [90] 3.3 2018 ✗ 1.04%",
  "Conceptual 12M 1.19%", "COYO-700M 1.51%", "VGG Face 3.70%", "FaceScrub 4.51%", "PubFig 6.48%",
  **"OBELISC [49] 353 2023+ ✗ 0.09%"**, "Falcon RefinedWeb 0.24%", "MMC4-FF 0.14%".
- On CC-3M: **"Of the 3.3 million original images, 2.9 million images are still hosted online, but just 1.1
  million"** … **"the other 1.8 million images have changed since the initial dataset."**
- On LAION: **"we found 175M and 183M modified images, respectively."** (CLIP-similarity criterion; the snippet's
  "respectively" leaves the two denominators unresolved — I did not confirm them, so I rest nothing on this one.)
- Mechanism: the attack **"exploits the mutable nature of internet content"**; **"Because data served by these
  URLs changes over time, the curator will frequently"** …; **"the data seen by the dataset curator at collection
  time might differ"**.
- Defence: **"distributing cryptographic hashes for all content"**; maintainers **"can prevent split-view attacks
  by attaching a cryptographic hash"**; caveat that integrity checks **"can significantly degrade the utility of a
  dataset"** when content is re-encoded or resized.
- Disclosure: **"we notify the maintainers of each affected dataset and recommended several low-overhead defenses."**

My own arithmetic on Carlini's CC-3M counts, carrying their rounding (each count stated to 0.1M, so ±0.05M):
still-hosted 2.9/3.3 = **88%** (86-90%); of still-hosted URLs, changed 1.8/2.9 = **62%** (59-65%); serving the
originally annotated image 1.1/3.3 = **33%** (31-35%). These are my figures derived from their stated counts, not
conclusions they state as percentages.

What this prior does and does not do, stated precisely so it is not overclaimed:
- It **falsifies two specific sentences in the record.** (a) "not done for any corpus on manifest covariates …
  and not done at all for LAION, YFCC100M or OBELICS" — Carlini reports a per-corpus, domain-level
  non-retrievability figure for **LAION-2B-en (0.29%) and OBELISC/OBELICS (0.09%)**, i.e. for two of the five
  named corpora, on the candidate's own first covariate (registrable domain), across a ten-corpus family.
  (b) Gate 2 search 9's finding "no published figure" for LAION availability — there is one, in a 2023 paper.
- It **pre-empts the headline fecundity item.** The record offers as new: "attrition as an adversarial surface —
  a host that knows its images are in a manifest can decide which ones stay up, making manifest-distributed
  corpora steerable after publication — a data-poisoning channel that requires no poisoning." That is
  split-view poisoning, published in 2023, costed ($60 for 0.01% of LAION-400M), disclosed to the maintainers,
  with the defence the record proposes (content hashes) already recommended and its utility cost already noted.
  The record's distinction (steering by *withdrawal* rather than *substitution*) is real but thin, and is a
  strictly weaker attack than the published one.
- It **does not answer limb (i).** 0.29%-1.51% is the expired-*domain* channel only, far below Hong's 21.4%
  download-failure rate; no retention ratios by language, licence, caption length, aesthetic or NSFW decile or
  shard; no CIs; no fitted retention model; no discrimination or calibration.
- It **does not touch limb (ii).** No model is trained on a vintage subset; the accuracy numbers quoted above are
  poisoning success rates, a different estimand.
