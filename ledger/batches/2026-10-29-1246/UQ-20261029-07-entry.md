Question (canonical): For data drawn from a generating process whose entropy rate H is known exactly by construction, does the loss achievable by a transformer family converge to H as parameters and tokens grow, or to a strictly positive floor H + Delta, and is Delta a property of the architecture rather than of the data? [Reformulation note: Gate 1's one permitted reformulation (Version B) declares byte-level bijective tokenization, two process families (a fixed 8-state HMM, H by the mixed-state/Blackwell integral; a CNF PCFG, H in closed form), two architecture families (pre-LN decoder-only transformer; one gated-linear-recurrent/SSM family), a matched-FLOP ladder, the estimator E in L(N,D) = E + A/N^alpha + B/D^beta with a bootstrap interval, and a +/-0.01 nats/symbol equivalence band. Both versions stand in the record; Version A is judged as the canonical wording.]
Alternative formulations: (1) Is the "irreducible loss" term of a neural scaling law the entropy of the data, or the sum of the data's entropy and an architecture-specific approximation floor? — judged on its own words this fails the object test (its blank is the interpretation of a published fit) and is not the headline wording, as the record itself concedes. (2) What is the asymptotic excess loss Delta = lim(achieved loss) − H on a process of exactly computable entropy rate, and how does Delta differ across architecture families at matched compute?
Motivating gap: parametric scaling laws in universal use split loss into a reducible power-law part and an irreducible constant, and the standard reading identifies that constant with the entropy of the data.
