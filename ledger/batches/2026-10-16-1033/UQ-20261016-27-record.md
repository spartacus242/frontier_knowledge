Question (canonical — Gate 1 version B, reformulated; see "Gate 1 reformulation" below):
Among recipients of a single vaccine product in a registry that records manufacturing lot number and links to outcome ascertainment, fit the outcome (infection, or severe outcome) on lot-level random intercepts nested within shipment block, conditioning on vaccination calendar date, time since dose, dose number, geography and recipient characteristics: is the between-lot standard deviation of the lot random effect — which equals the between-lot SD of log VE, the common unvaccinated reference cancelling — distinguishable from zero, and what one-sided upper bound do the data place on it?

Question (Gate 1 version A — as intaken, retained per Gate 1 record):
In immunization registries that record manufacturing lot numbers alongside linked outcome data, is the between-lot variance component of vaccine effectiveness distinguishable from zero after conditioning on calendar time, geography and recipient characteristics, and what upper bound do the data place on it?

Gate 1 reformulation (one permitted; used, for well-posedness only):
Version A leaves two things unpinned that decide whether the question is answerable. (i) *Estimand.* "Between-lot variance of VE" appears to require a per-lot unvaccinated comparator; it does not. The unvaccinated reference is common to all lots, so under a multiplicative model the between-lot variance of log VE is identified from the vaccinated stratum alone, as the variance of lot-level random intercepts on outcome risk among recipients. Version B states this, which converts an apparently infeasible design (thousands of lot-specific VE estimates) into a feasible one (one hierarchical model on the vaccinated). (ii) *Identification.* The central threat — lots ship in geographic/temporal blocks — is answered by nesting lot within shipment block and taking within-block contrasts, which Version B makes part of the estimand rather than leaving to analysis discretion. Version B is the canonical form; Version A is preserved because it is the form a reader will recognise.

Alternative formulations:
- Lay: Do different manufactured batches of the same vaccine protect people to measurably different degrees? (NOTE: this lay form is *not* un-asked — see Novelty.)
- Technical: Treating manufacturing lot as a random effect in a test-negative or cohort VE analysis, what is the estimated between-lot standard deviation of log VE, and its upper confidence limit?
- Regulatory: Does national lot release, which certifies identity, potency and sterility against in-vitro specifications, bound between-lot variation in field effectiveness, and by how much?

Motivating gap:
Every published VE estimate pools all lots, i.e. treats lots as exchangeable with respect to effectiveness. Lot release certifies against in-vitro specifications; no release criterion is an effectiveness measure, and the one paper that asks whether official batch control is still worth doing (Kretzschmar et al. 2018, *Vaccine*) answers it entirely from quality-specification failures with no clinical outcome data of any kind. The exchangeability presupposition was historically untestable because lot numbers were not linkable to outcomes — MMWR documents this failure verbatim in a 1984 measles outbreak investigation, where a faulty-lot hypothesis was raised and abandoned because "detailed lot information was not available from the providers." COVID-19 programmes were the first to record lot at scale in linkable national registries, which makes the variance component estimable for the first time. The lot-linked analyses that have actually been run on those registries are all on the *safety-reporting* side (Schmeling et al. 2023; Manniche et al. 2024; PEI's own SafeVac 2.0 analysis). The effectiveness side has, as far as I can establish, never been decomposed by lot.

Declared priors (declared at intake, not discovered by this Gate 2):
Schmeling et al. 2023 (*Eur J Clin Invest*) — batch-dependent rates of *suspected adverse events* for BNT162b2 in Danish data; a passive-surveillance analysis open to reporting-rate and batch-era confounding and criticised on those grounds. Also declared: the "hot lot" claims in VAERS and the standard regulatory reply that they are reporting artifacts. This candidate is the design that would settle that dispute on the effectiveness axis rather than restate it.

Presuppositions (established / CONTESTED):
1. Lot numbers are recorded with sufficient completeness and accuracy in at least one outcome-linkable registry. CONTESTED. Partially supported: PEI's app study tied 1,179,877 vaccinations to 401 batches with batch numbers validated against authorised batches; Danish/Swedish national systems hold doses-per-batch. But dose-level lot capture linked to the *individual* (not aggregate doses shipped per batch) is the requirement, and is unaudited.
2. Lots are spread across enough geography and calendar time to be separable from era and place effects. CONTESTED — central identification threat. Version B partially internalises it (lot nested in shipment block); residual threat is that within-block lot variation may be near-degenerate, in which case the design yields only a wide upper bound.
3. Per-lot VE is estimable with usable precision, or at least the variance component is. CONTESTED; may only yield an upper bound via hierarchical shrinkage. The question is written to accept that outcome.
4. In-vitro potency release assays are not already a sufficient surrogate for effectiveness. CONTESTED, and now partly sharpened against evidence: pre-licensure lot-consistency testing bounds the *immune surrogate* across a handful of lots (BNT162b2 SAP: 4 lots, S-binding IgG GMR, 95% CI required within 0.67–1.5). That bounds central tendency across 4 manufacturing-era lots on a surrogate, not the variance across thousands of commercial lots that have since passed through distribution. See "Strongest objection foreseen".
5. Post-release cold-chain handling is recorded or exchangeable across lots. CONTESTED; it is a candidate mechanism, not merely a nuisance. If handling is the driver, the variance component is real but its referent is distribution, not manufacture — which changes the action (cold-chain policy, not release criteria) without dissolving the question.
6. The estimand is a variance component, not an allegation about particular lots. Established by construction in Version B, and load-bearing: the question must not be readable as a thesis that specific lots are defective.
7. Outcome ascertainment does not itself vary with lot. CONTESTED — violated if lots correlate with setting (care-home versus community rollout); partly absorbed by shipment-block nesting, since setting travels with shipment.

What would count as an answer:
A hierarchical analysis in at least one lot-linked registry reporting the between-lot SD of log VE with an interval, plus a pre-registered falsification check: permuting lot labels within shipment block and calendar window must reproduce the null distribution of the variance estimate. Two informative outcomes, not one: (a) a bounded null — "between-lot VE variation is below X percentage points" — retires the hot-lot effectiveness hypothesis by design rather than by appeal to reporting artifact, and for the first time supplies evidence for the exchangeability assumption every pooled VE estimate makes; (b) a positive finding obliges lot release to add an effectiveness-correlated criterion, and makes release-assay values candidate predictors of lot-level deviation. A null that is merely imprecise (upper bound wider than the VE estimate itself) is a negative result about the registries, and must be reported as such rather than as a null.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
1 (Manniche/Schmeling): same unit of analysis (manufacturing batch), same registries, opposite outcome axis. Theirs is spontaneous-report *counts* per batch, with reporting rate as the dependent variable and the known confounders (media attention, dose number, batch era) acknowledged but unmodelled. The candidate's outcome is ascertained clinically rather than reported voluntarily, so the dominant confounder of their design — differential reporting — does not apply; the estimand is a variance component rather than a per-batch rate; and the identification strategy (within-shipment-block contrasts plus a label-permutation null) is the thing their critics said was missing. The candidate is not an extension of this paper to effectiveness; it is a different design on the same data substrate.
2 (lot-consistency SAP): this is the closest thing in the candidate's own field to an answer, and the reason novelty is Medium rather than High. It asks "are lots equivalent?" and answers yes within a 1.5-fold margin — but on 4 lots, pre-licensure, from the manufacturing-era inventory, with an immune surrogate as endpoint and a central-tendency equivalence test rather than a variance component. The candidate asks about the dispersion of the full commercial lot population after distribution, on the clinical endpoint, with no surrogate step. A 1.5-fold IgG GMR bound on 4 lots places no bound on the SD across thousands.
3 (Kretzschmar): asks the regulatory half of the candidate's question — is lot release earning its keep — and answers from in-vitro specification failures alone. The candidate supplies the outcome-side evidence that paper did not have and could not have had; the two together are the argument that "no release criterion is an effectiveness measure" is a gap and not a design choice.
4 (PEI): a regulator's own batch-level analysis with validated batch numbers, confirming that the data linkage exists and that the institutional response to lot questions stops at adverse-event reporting. Its methodological critique of Schmeling is substantially the candidate's own identification strategy, applied to the other outcome.
5 (MMWR 1984): the historical form of the question, asked and abandoned for want of lot records. It establishes the reason-for-silence mechanism from a primary source rather than by speculation, and it shows that the *allegation* form ("was this lot faulty?") has been asked for forty years while the *variance-component* form has not.

Significance — consequence:
If yes (SD distinguishable from zero): lot release acquires a gap it must close — an effectiveness-correlated release criterion, or at minimum a validated bridge from release assays to the lot-level deviation, which is a concrete regulatory action the OCABR network and FDA CBER are the bodies that take. Published VE estimates acquire an extra variance component they currently omit, so their intervals are too narrow by an amount this study would quantify; cross-setting VE discrepancies now attributed to design, population and variant get a fourth candidate explanation. The hot-lot dispute moves from assertion-versus-artifact to a measured quantity, on the axis (protection) where it has never been measured. And the mechanism hunt — mRNA integrity, LNP size distribution, cold-chain excursion — gets an outcome-anchored target rather than a specification-anchored one.
If no (a usefully tight upper bound): the exchangeability assumption underlying every pooled VE estimate in the literature gains its first direct empirical support, which it has never had. That is not a null result for the pooling convention; it is the missing validation of it. It also retires the hot-lot hypothesis on the effectiveness axis by design rather than by appeal to reporting artifact — the appeal PEI currently has to make, and which persuades nobody who is not already persuaded. And it supplies Kretzschmar et al.'s 2018 question with the outcome-side answer they had to do without: release testing, whatever it does or does not measure, is accompanied by field protection that varies by less than X points.
If no but imprecise: a negative result about registry lot capture, which is itself actionable for the bodies that specify immunisation-registry data standards.

Significance — audience:
(i) OMCLs and the EDQM OCABR network, and FDA CBER lot release — they write and apply release criteria, and a positive finding names a criterion they lack. (ii) National immunisation programme epidemiologists holding the lot-linked data (SSI Denmark, Folkhälsomyndigheten, UKHSA, Israeli HMOs) — they would add a lot-mix term to routine VE monitoring, and they are the only people who can run this. (iii) VE methodology networks (I-MOVE, VISION, EU multi-country VE platforms) — a variance component they currently set to zero by assumption. (iv) Manufacturers' process-capability and CMC teams — a positive finding converts an in-process specification question into an outcome-validated one, and that is the specification they would tighten. (v) Pharmacovigilance bodies that have had to answer hot-lot claims with a methodological argument rather than a measurement.

Significance — fecundity:
Once a lot-level deviation exists as an estimated quantity, a series of questions become askable that currently cannot be: which release-assay values (potency, RNA integrity, LNP polydispersity) predict a lot's deviation — which is the validation study that would turn release assays into genuine surrogates; how much of the deviation cold-chain excursion records explain, separating manufacturing from distribution; whether between-lot variance differs by platform (mRNA versus adjuvanted protein versus live attenuated, where thermal lability differs by orders of magnitude); whether lot variance was larger in the scale-up era than at steady state, which is testable within the COVID-19 programmes themselves and bears directly on Manniche's early-batch finding; and whether lot-level deviations on the safety and effectiveness axes are correlated, since a shared potency mechanism predicts a specific sign and an immune-response mechanism predicts the opposite.

Significance — anchoring:
Touches (a) the unexplained between-study heterogeneity in VE estimates, conventionally attributed to design, population, variant and waning, with no manufacturing term; (b) the primary-versus-secondary vaccine-failure literature, which has no lot-level stratum; (c) the hot-lot dispute, live in oversight correspondence and unresolved on the effectiveness axis; (d) the correlate-of-protection and immunobridging programme, since a lot-level deviation that release assays fail to predict is a surrogate-validity failure; (e) the general and larger question of whether manufacturing-process controls on biologics are validated against clinical outcomes at all, of which Kretzschmar et al. 2018 is the one published instance of anyone asking.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism, four strands, three with primary-source support):
1. **New data.** Individual-level lot capture linked to outcome ascertainment did not exist before the COVID-19 national registries. MMWR 1984 documents the earlier impossibility in the regulator's own words — the faulty-lot hypothesis was raised and dropped because "detailed lot information was not available from the providers." A question cannot be posed as an estimable variance component while its grouping variable is unrecorded; it survives instead as an allegation about individual lots, which is exactly the form it took for forty years.
2. **Disciplinary boundary.** Lot-to-lot variability is owned by CMC and quality (process capability, in-vitro specification, nested variance components) and VE by field epidemiology. The two do not share journals, reviewers or funders. The quality side treats the release specification as the terminus of inference; the epidemiology side treats the vial as an undifferentiated exposure. Neither field's standard output requires the other's data, so the gap is nobody's omission.
3. **Institutional pre-emption by the lot-consistency paradigm.** The question looks already answered to the field, because a named regulatory design (lot-consistency testing: 3–4 lots, surrogate endpoint, 1.5-fold equivalence margin) is understood to settle it. That the design bounds the central tendency of a surrogate across a handful of pre-licensure lots rather than the dispersion of protection across thousands of distributed ones is a distinction nobody has had cause to draw. This is the strand that makes the silence *reasonable* rather than merely accidental, and it is also the strand that makes the candidate vulnerable at Gate 4.
4. **Incentive and taboo gap.** Lot-stratified analysis has been pushed almost exclusively by the vaccine-critical community on the safety axis, which has made the whole class of analysis reputationally costly for mainstream epidemiology; regulators' standing reply frames lot questions as reporting artifacts (PEI 2023), which closes the inquiry rather than prompting the effectiveness-side design. A mainstream group faces an asymmetric payoff: little credit for a null, substantial risk from a positive finding read as vindication of hot-lot claims. This strand is inference from the documented record, not from a source that states it.
Not offered: "no one thought of it." The record shows people did think of it, repeatedly, in the allegation form.

Strongest objection foreseen (recorded for Gate 4, not a substitute for it):
Triviality via the surrogate route. If an accepted correlate of protection exists for the product, and lot release plus lot-consistency testing bound between-lot variation in the immune surrogate, then an upper bound on between-lot VE variation may be derivable analytically from existing published numbers — in a page, without touching a registry. The defence is that the 1.5-fold GMR margin is an equivalence claim on the mean of 4 pre-licensure lots rather than a bound on the SD across the commercial population, that it is an *acceptance* criterion rather than an *estimate*, and that it says nothing about post-release handling; but this defence needs the actual lot-release potency distributions to be quantitative, and I did not obtain them. An adversary should attack here.
