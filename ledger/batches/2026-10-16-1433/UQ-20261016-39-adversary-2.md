## Restatement in my own words

A ~2 m axial-FOV PET scanner sees every organ and the blood input function in one dynamic acquisition. Current practice fits each organ (or each voxel) its own compartmental model, independently, and never adds the pieces back up. The candidate asks: if you take those independently fitted per-organ models, sum the activity they predict, add measured excreted activity and a remainder term that is *bounded in advance rather than fitted*, does the total equal the decay-corrected injected activity to within propagated uncertainty? And for any tracer where it does not, if you refit all organs jointly under a conservation constraint, which organ's parameters move, which way, and by how much in Ki, Vt, K1 and organ absorbed dose?

In short: (a) an accounting audit of injected activity against independently fitted organ models, and (b) a claim that the audit's residual can be attributed to a named organ's kinetics.

## Single strongest killing point (and its honest limit)

A compartmental model fitted to an organ's own time-activity curve reproduces that curve, so the sum of fitted organ activities is, to within fit residuals, the sum of *measured* organ activities. The closure residual R(t) is therefore an audit of scanner calibration, segmentation, excretion accounting and the asserted remainder bound — not of the kinetic parameters — and because a global activity-conservation constraint is a functional of the predicted TACs, it carries no information about the parameter directions that leave TACs invariant (precisely where k2/k3/k4 are badly determined). Limb (b) consequently has no identification strategy, and the record's own cited numbers put instrument-side error at or above any kinetics-induced residual. **Limit, stated plainly: this is my own theoretical argument; I opened no primary source for it and it is scope-limited to the well-specified regime, so it is graded Partial and not a kill.** It is the claim a verifier should test first, because if it holds it moves attacks 4, 6 and 8 together.
