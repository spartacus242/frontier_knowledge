## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take papers that report results run on somebody else's quantum computer over the internet (IBM, Google, IonQ, Rigetti, Quantinuum). Each of those machines has a per-day record of how good it was: T1/T2, one- and two-qubit gate errors, readout fidelity. The question: if you look up how good the machine was on the day a paper's run happened, and ask where that day sits in the machine's own recent distribution of goodness (its percentile over, say, the surrounding 60 days), do published runs cluster in the good tail? Two sub-questions: (a) for what share of papers can you even pin the run to a date the archive still covers, and (b) for those you can, are the percentiles uniform or skewed high — and, where the headline number moves monotonically with device quality, how much of the headline number is that skew worth? "Uniform" is a real answer. No claim of misconduct: a user re-running when the machine looks bad, a vendor scheduling reserved time into good weeks, and a vendor tuning before a demo all produce the same skew.

Two things I notice in the restatement that the record's framing softens. First, the whole object depends on a factual claim about an engineering artefact (by-date retrievability), not on anything conceptual. Second, the thing being measured is partly the *designed* behaviour of the platform (error-aware transpilation exists to exploit the current calibration state), so a positive finding is not obviously news.

## Fetches (4/4) — pages opened

- F-A https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29872 — the record's flagged unopened displacer, REAL: Köster & Mauerer, "Claim against Measurement: Statistical Artefacts in Quantum Error Mitigation Benchmarks" (v2, 24 Jul 2026). Reviews **81 QEM papers** on eight criteria (25% use inferential methods; 42% report uncertainty only descriptively); in a 132-configuration ZNE sweep, scale factors, extrapolation method and "hardware calibration are not merely incidental but active"; a **72-hour real-hardware study** found the same ZNE configuration's effect size more than 3x larger "depending solely on when it is executed", with a sharp reduction in effective independent observations; proposes minimum reporting standards including **longitudinal drift assessment** and inferential testing with effect sizes. No calibration-archive join, no publication-corpus run dates, no percentile/rank of device state.

## Novelty judgement (mine)

Medium stands, barely, and for a narrower reason than the record gives. Nothing in nine fresh vocabularies or in the four papers I opened joins a vendor calibration archive to published run dates or tests uniformity of run-time state percentiles. But F-A materially closes the gap the record left open: in the candidate's own field, a 2026 paper reviews a publication corpus, demonstrates that execution epoch alone flips published-grade conclusions, names hardware calibration as an active factor, and prescribes longitudinal drift reporting. The normative half and the sensitivity half are both in print from one group; only the retrospective-archive measurement is missing, and F-D indicates it is missing because it is not buildable through the vendor's supported API. Un-asked, in this case, is close to unaskable-of-the-archive. Unopened potential neighbour I had no budget for: arXiv:2508.21231 ("Qubit Health Analytics ... for HPC-Integrated Quantum Processors").
