Source method: anomaly mining.

Question (canonical, Gate 1 version B — the one that stands):
For a pre-declared set of shipped open-weight LLM releases, a pre-declared prompt set, and a detector-defined set U of under-trained vocabulary items (reported SEPARATELY from the set R of tokenizer-unreachable items, since the two sets and their hazard meanings differ), under free self-conditioned generation: (i) what is the mean and upper-tail step-wise pre-truncation probability mass on U and on R; (ii) at what share of generation steps does at least one member of U (resp. R) remain inside the sampling support after the release's own declared default truncation rule is applied — with the arm defined per artifact from `generation_config.json`/model card, and an explicitly declared substitute arm recorded as such for releases that ship none; and (iii) what is the realised emission rate of members of U and of R per 10^6 generated tokens, against a null of a vocabulary subset matched on unigram frequency profile?

Question (Gate 1 version A, as intaken — recorded, superseded):
"In ordinary, non-adversarial generation from shipped open-weight LLMs, how much step-wise probability mass does the model place on its own under-trained or unreachable vocabulary items, what share of that mass survives the truncation sampler at the release's own default decoding settings, and what is the resulting emission rate per million generated tokens?"
Reason for reformulation (well-posedness only, one reformulation used): version A (a) fuses "under-trained" and "unreachable" into one object although the literature defines them separately and by different tests (see P1 evidence below), (b) leaves the shipped-default arm undefined for releases that ship no config, and (c) states limb (iii) with no null, so a non-zero rate would have no reference. No scope was widened.

Alternative formulations: (1) Are glitch tokens a live inference-side hazard or only an input-side one — how often do shipped default sampling configurations actually let an under-trained token through into output? (2) What is the measured leakage rate of the untrained region of an LLM's output vocabulary under the decoding settings the model is actually served with?

Motivating gap: The detection literature establishes that shipped tokenizers carry items the model was effectively never trained on, and tests them by FEEDING THEM IN. The symmetric output-side arm — whether the model puts mass on them unprompted, and whether the shipped truncation rule already removes it — is measured by none of the five sources opened here, and the one 2026 paper that builds exactly the per-step support-membership instrument it needs points it at the opposite end of the distribution (legitimate human words being cut off). Intake's gap claim was title-level only; Gate 2 re-established it at first hand from full-text reads and narrowed it (see "nearest priors").

Presuppositions:
P1 — ESTABLISHED AT FIRST HAND (not inherited from a title). Shipped tokenizers contain a non-trivial set of under-trained and of unreachable items. Source: Land & Bartolo, arXiv:2405.05417 (READ LEVEL: full text). Definitions quoted from the paper: "UNREACHABLE TOKENS are those that are never produced as a result of tokenizing text."; and on the other term, "We refer to these as 'under-trained' or 'untrained' tokens, reserving the latter term only for cases in which we have clear indication" (Sec. 1) — note the hedge: "untrained" is the narrower claim, and this is the formulation to quote, not a bare "untrained tokens exist". Printed figures: Sec. 3 — verified tokens "typically ranges between 5–50% of tested candidate tokens, corresponding to 0.1–1% of the total vocabulary size"; Table 1 carries 24 open-weight model rows with confirmed/tested counts (e.g. Qwen1.5 32B 2450/2966; Gemma 2B 3161/5117; GPT-2 Medium 49/999; Llama2 7B 20/639; Jamba v0.1 6/1280). Unreachable items are NOT counted in Table 1; Sec. 3.2 prints "over 1,400 manually added tokens of emojis are categorized as unreachable" (Command R/R+), "over 200 unreachable tokens" (OLMo), and 1,542 untrained special tokens (Jamba). INTERNAL-CONSISTENCY FLAG, recorded rather than smoothed: for OLMo v1.7 the paper gives Table 1 = 178/993 while Sec. 2.4 gives "191 out of 49,575 tokens pass our verification step" and 175 of 993 for the top 2%; three mutually inconsistent values for one model, so no single OLMo count is load-bearing here. Independent second instance of the kind: Li et al., arXiv:2404.09894 (READ LEVEL: full text) prints 7,895 glitch tokens "from all tokenizers" out of 182,517 tokens tested.
P2 — CONTESTED, definitional, unchanged. "Under-trained" is operationalised from the checkpoint alone. Confirmed at first hand: Land & Bartolo's indicator uses weights only, no forward passes — cosine distance of output-embedding rows to a reference vector built from known untrained IDs, C(A,x)_i = 1 − (A_i·x)/(||A_i|| ||x||), with the L2 norm of E_in for untied models; their Limitations note the tied-model method needs "manually specifying a set of reference under-trained tokens". Li et al. instead define a glitch token behaviourally — "A glitch token t is distinctly characterized by its capacity to disrupt an LLM's comprehension of its semantic essence" — thresholded by failure of any of three proxy tasks (repetition, spelling, length). Two detectors, two different sets: the answer must therefore be reported per detector, which the reformulation requires.
P3 — MOSTLY ESTABLISHED, flagged; now evidenced rather than assumed, and this is the document-kind premise that gets checked by opening instances. The premise that releases ship declared defaults is NOT established by my own reads of three release artifacts (I opened none; fetch budget went to papers). It is established at one remove by Awad et al., arXiv:2605.27268 (READ LEVEL: full text), whose Sec. 5 draws "documented default or recommended decoding settings" from Hugging Face generation configs and model cards for 7 base/instruct pairs — i.e. a third party successfully harvested this field for 14 artifacts. The same paper also shows the premise is lossy at the edges: its text gives Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as T=0.6 while its Table 2 lists T=0.7 as the "nearest evaluated temperature", so a declared default does not always survive into an evaluation grid unchanged. STATUS: premise holds in kind on third-party evidence; direct instance-opening of release configs is an OPEN CHECK for Gate 4, and is cheap.
P4 — CONTESTED, and deliberately not assumed. Emitting such a token is behaviourally consequential. The question measures the consequence; a harmless emission is still an answer. Note the only consequence evidence opened is input-side: Li et al.'s Table 3 symptom distribution (e.g. repetition-task "Random Characters" 53.16% for Text-Davinci-003, 80.22% for GPT-3.5-Turbo) is measured on tokens FED IN, and is silent on what an emitted token does.
P5 — MEASURAND EXEMPTION DECLARED, not merely considered. That this hazard rate is unquantified is the question's measurand. An attack that the rate may turn out to be zero, or that nobody has needed it, is a prediction about the answer and not a false premise; limb (iii)'s null and the stated negative reading make a zero a result.

What would count as an answer: A per-model table over the pre-declared prompt set and the pre-declared per-detector sets U and R giving (i) mean and upper-tail step-wise pre-truncation mass; (ii) share of steps with any member inside the post-truncation support under the artifact's declared default rule; (iii) emission rate per 10^6 generated tokens against the frequency-matched null; plus sensitivity arms over >=2 detectors (e.g. an unembedding-geometry detector and a behavioural proxy-task detector, which P2 shows disagree) and >=2 (temperature, top-p/top-k/min-p) settings. Decision rule: post-truncation support membership at or below round-off across models and settings establishes the pathology as input-side only; a non-zero rate names the models and prompt classes where it is live and implies the logit-mask fix. NOT an answer: a re-run of detection with no decoding stage; an adversarial-input study; a count of glitch tokens occurring in an INPUT corpus (see the near-miss denominator flagged below).

Significance — consequence: If post-truncation support membership and emission are non-negligible for some shipped release/default pair, the fix is a logit mask over U ∪ R applied in the serving stack — machinery that already exists (vLLM ships a documented "sampling mask"; bad-words/logit-processor hooks are standard) — and the deliverable is a shippable list per tokenizer release rather than a research programme. It would also reclassify the glitch-token hazard from input-only to bidirectional, which matters because the mitigations currently proposed are all training-side: Li et al.'s Sec. 8.3 offers dataset curation, architecture changes, post-training evaluation and feedback loops, and no serving-side control. If the answer is at or below round-off across models and settings, the output-side arm closes: the detection literature's input-side scoping is vindicated, serving defaults are licensed explicitly, and the on-device vocabulary-pruning line (Ivanov et al.) gains a direct argument that the under-trained region is free to drop. A negative is therefore informative and publishable, which is what keeps this a question rather than an agenda.
Significance — audience: (a) serving-engine maintainers (vLLM/SGLang/TGI/HF `generate`), who own the truncation step and already expose the mask — they would act by adding or declining to add a default mask; (b) model release teams writing `generation_config.json`, who would ship the mask list alongside `tokenizer.json`, and whose declared defaults Awad et al. show are already being harvested by third parties; (c) the SE glitch-token line (Li et al. FSE 2024; GlitchProber; GlitchMiner; GlitchCleaner at AAAI), whose remit is detection and repair and whose cheapest mitigation would be one they have not evaluated; (d) red-team/jailbreak-surface analysts, for whom a self-emitted anomalous token is a different threat object from an injected one. The audience claim is discounted honestly: (a) and (b) will act only if the number is non-trivial, so for them a null is a licence rather than a change.
Significance — fecundity: Does emission concentrate in identifiable contexts (immediately after special tokens, in code, in non-Latin scripts, at high temperature)? Is emission rate a cheap behavioural detector of under-training, inverting Prior 2's weights-only indicator and giving P2's detector disagreement an external referee? Does instruction tuning or RLHF suppress it — Prior 1's base→instruct shift (0.740→0.728 mean reachability) shows the alignment stage measurably moves support membership, so the same contrast is immediately askable here? Does speculative decoding change the hazard, since the draft model's U differs from the target's? Does quantization inflate mass on near-zero-logit rows? Should a shipped tokenizer carry a declared emission-safe mask as part of its release contract?
Significance — anchoring: the detection literature's untested symmetric arm (Priors 2 and the whole SE cluster); the non-canonical-mass problem (Prior 3), where the candidate's R-emission is a subset nobody has isolated and whose number is only shaded in a figure; the sampler-adapter literature's open question of what truncation actually removes (Meister et al., returned not opened); the on-device vocabulary-pruning line, which prunes by log-mass and would be told whether the under-trained region costs anything; and Prior 1's own stated limitation that it audits membership and not "soft probabilistic erosion".

Reason for silence: An instrument/discipline asymmetry, not inattention. The two halves of this question are owned by communities with non-overlapping apparatus. The under-trained/glitch-token side sits in software-engineering dependability and safety venues (FSE 2024, AAAI, the Cohere/EMNLP detection line) and its instrument is a prompt harness: every opened source in that family places the token in the INPUT and reads a response, at temperature zero precisely to remove the sampler from the picture (Land & Bartolo App. B: "By using a temperature of zero ... we minimize the effect of random sampling"; Li et al.: "we set the temperature to 0. We keep all other parameters at default values"). Removing the sampler is methodologically correct for detection and makes the candidate's question invisible from inside that apparatus. The per-step truncation-support audit lives in the NLP generation/decoding literature, and the first paper to build it against documented shipped defaults appears to be 2026 (Prior 1) — pointed at legitimate words, because that community's motivating concern is diversity and coverage loss, not hazard leakage. Secondary mechanisms: an expectation-of-null incentive gap (serving engineers assume truncation handles the tail, and a near-zero result reads as a non-finding), and a data-availability one (the "release's own default decoding settings" arm only became reliably machine-readable with `generation_config.json`, which is why Prior 1's harvest of it is recent).
