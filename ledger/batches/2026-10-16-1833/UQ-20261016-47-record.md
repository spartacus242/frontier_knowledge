## Question (canonical) — Gate 1 reformulation, version B (used from here on)

Holding corpus, algorithm, pre-tokenizer and vocabulary size fixed, draw k ≥ 5 independent fitting samples of size S from one pretraining corpus and fit one tokenizer per draw: (a) what is the between-draw divergence of the resulting tokenizers — vocabulary Jaccard, merge-sequence agreement, and per-domain fertility SD on held-out text — as a function of S; and (b) when one model per draw is pretrained on identical text at a **matched byte budget and matched FLOP budget**, how large is the between-draw SD of validation bits-per-byte and of downstream scores, relative to two reference quantities fixed in advance — the seed-only SD under a single fixed tokenizer, and the published algorithm-choice / vocabulary-size gap?

### Question (canonical) — version A as intaked (recorded per Gate 1 rule)

"Holding algorithm, vocabulary size and pretraining mixture fixed, how much of a tokenizer is determined by the particular sample of text it was fitted on — measured as vocabulary and merge-sequence divergence between tokenizers fitted to independent samples of the same corpus, and as fertility divergence on held-out text — and does that sampling variance propagate into a spread in loss-per-FLOP and downstream accuracy comparable to the gaps the literature attributes to tokenizer algorithm and vocabulary-size choices?"

**Why reformulated (well-posedness only, one reformulation used):** version A specified "identical token budgets", which is not a comparable budget across tokenizers that differ in fertility — equal token counts mean unequal text seen, which is exactly the confound that makes cross-tokenizer loss comparison contested. Version B fixes matched **byte** budget plus matched FLOP budget and names **bits-per-byte** as the loss endpoint, which is the normalisation under which the comparison is defined. Version A's "loss-per-FLOP" is also replaced by "BPB at matched FLOPs", a measurable rather than a ratio of two moving quantities. No change to scope, limbs or thresholds.

## Alternative formulations

1. Is a tokenizer a reproducible artifact of a recipe, or a sample statistic with a standard error that nobody has ever reported?
2. When a paper reports that tokenizer A beats tokenizer B, how much of that gap lies inside the noise of refitting either tokenizer on a fresh sample of the same data?

## Motivating gap

Pretraining recipes treat the tokenizer as a fixed preprocessing component with two named design dimensions (algorithm, vocabulary size) and report neither the corpus the tokenizer was fitted on nor how large that sample was — yet BPE/Unigram fitting is a greedy, sample-dependent estimation procedure, routinely run on a small arbitrary slice chosen by a pipeline default rather than a decision (SentencePiece exposes `input_sentence_size` and `shuffle_input_sentence` precisely to subsample; verified at https://google.github.io/sentencepiece/doc/options/). The tokenizer literature varies the things the field already names as choices — algorithm, vocabulary size, library, pre-tokenization, language/mixture composition, domain adaptation — and the two large recent suites hold the fitting sample constant and unexamined. Gate 2 confirmed this in the sources themselves: TokEval states "All custom tokenizers are trained on 10 GB of text" with no sampling, replicate or seed detail; TokSuite explicitly puts "training tokenizers from scratch while varying one design factor at a time" out of scope.

## Presuppositions

1. Tokenizer fitting is sample-dependent. TRUE BY CONSTRUCTION for BPE/Unigram; the open quantity is magnitude. **Partly measured already**: arXiv:2502.20273 reports BPE shared vocabulary with a 900GB reference rising from ~58% (1GB fit) to ~97% (900GB fit) at vocab 40,960, and 40%→97% for UnigramLM, 4%→92% for WordPiece. Those are bias-against-a-reference numbers under a *nested* sampling scheme, not variance across independent draws.
2. The published algorithm-choice and vocabulary-size gaps were each measured from a single tokenizer fit per condition. **VERIFIED at Gate 2, and verified strongly.** Ali et al., arXiv:2310.08754 (NAACL 2024): one tokenizer per design cell, 24 tokenizers / 26 models at 2.6B, **one seed per condition**, no SDs or CIs anywhere; its Limitations state the authors "did not investigate the effect of different random seeds", and all 26 models cost ~59,000 GPU-hours. TokEval (arXiv:2608.18062): 44 custom tokenizers, 46 models at 1.27B, apparently one model per condition, no seed replicates; its "±" values are tokenizer-family-clustered standard errors across *configurations*, not retraining variance. TokSuite (arXiv:2512.20757): 14 off-the-shelf tokenizers, no fits of its own, one run per condition, noise estimated by 10,000-trial bootstrap over benchmark *items*. The comparison limb therefore stands and is not weakened.
3. Models with different tokenizers can be compared on a common loss scale. CONTESTED; handled by fixing bits-per-byte plus matched byte and FLOP budgets, with downstream accuracy as the fallback. TokEval does report validation and FLORES+ BPB across 46 differently-tokenized models, which is precedent that the normalisation is usable in practice.
4. Compute suffices for k refits × ≥2 scales. FEASIBILITY, and the binding constraint: Ali et al.'s single-seed grid of 26 × 2.6B models cost ~59k GPU-hours, so k = 5 draws × 2 scales × seed replicates is a large ask. The design degrades gracefully to limb (a), which is nearly free.
5. Merge-sequence edit distance and vocabulary Jaccard are meaningful divergence measures. CONTESTED and now sharpened by two opened sources: arXiv:2508.06621 ("Merge Lists are Unnecessary for BPE Inference") argues the merge list is dispensable at inference, and arXiv:2305.03029 shows that *replacing the merge-selection rule outright* changes downstream BLEU by ≲1–1.5 points in high-resource pairs. So merge-order divergence can be large while segmentation behaviour is nearly identical. This is why per-domain fertility on held-out text, and not merge distance, must carry limb (a)'s claim; merge divergence is reported as a descriptive upper bound only.
6. The fitting sample is drawn from the same corpus as the pretraining data in the compared conditions. DESIGN REQUIREMENT; it is also the point of difference from the tokenizer-transfer, domain-adaptation and mixture-composition studies, all of which vary the fitting *distribution*.

## What would count as an answer

A variance-component decomposition with confidence intervals.
- Limb (a): fit k ≥ 5 tokenizers per cell on **independent (disjoint) samples** of sizes S ∈ {0.1, 1, 10} GB from one corpus, identical algorithm, pre-tokenizer and vocabulary size; report between-draw vocabulary Jaccard, merge-sequence agreement, and per-domain fertility SD as functions of S, each against a within-draw control (same bytes, shuffled order / different library seed).
- Limb (b): train one model per draw at ≥2 scales on identical text at matched byte and FLOP budgets, plus seed replicates under one fixed tokenizer; report between-draw SD of validation BPB and of downstream scores against two pre-fixed references: (i) the seed-only SD, (ii) the published algorithm-choice gap (e.g. Ali et al.'s ~1.7-point monolingual average-accuracy gap between BPE-SP-33 and BPE-HF-33, and the ~9% best-vs-worst spread on ARC-Easy).
- Decision rule: "the sample matters" = between-draw SD of the same order as the algorithm-choice gap; "it does not" = between-draw SD at or below seed SD at the smallest S tested. A monotone decay of the variance with S, with the knee located, is the most useful form — and would sit alongside, not inside, the *bias* knee that 2502.20273 already places at 120–150GB for English BPE intrinsic metrics.

## Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, all opened)

**Prior 1 — "How Much is Enough? The Diminishing Returns of Tokenization Training Data", arXiv:2502.20273, 2025 (ICML 2025). Opened: https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20273v2**
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS: one trained tokenizer per (algorithm, vocabulary size, fitting-data size) cell; 396 vocabularies. English 1–900GB (Pile+RedPajama de-duplicated), Russian 30–600GB (OSCAR); BPE / UnigramLM / WordPiece; vocab 40,960 / 64k / 128k / 256k; GPT-4 pre-tokenizer regex fixed.
- ENDPOINT: intrinsic only — share of vocabulary shared with the reference tokenizer, (weighted) Jaccard of token usage over 7 held-out domains, morphological alignment, a cognitive score, Rényi efficiency, pre-token coverage. No fertility, no merge-sequence divergence, no model-based endpoint.
- REFERENCE / COMPARATOR: the single tokenizer fit on the full 900GB corpus (plus pairwise overlaps in App. B).
- ESTIMAND: the saturation point of tokenizer agreement and intrinsic quality as fitting data grows — i.e. E[divergence(S)] against a fixed asymptote, under an explicitly **nested** sampling scheme ("The size increase is augmentative, meaning that each corpus subset properly includes all those with smaller size").
- Verified absences (read, not inferred): no SDs, no CIs, no replicate fits, no stated seeds, **no language models trained at all** ("to evaluate the trained tokenizers without the additional computational overhead of training dozens of full LLMs"). The paper notes qualitatively that rare tokens are "susceptible to variation between tokenizers trained on slightly different subsets of data drawn from the same overall distribution" — it names the candidate's phenomenon in one sentence and does not measure it.

**Prior 2 — "Tokenizer Choice For LLM Training: Negligible or Crucial?" (Ali et al.), arXiv:2310.08754, NAACL 2024. Opened: https://arxiv.org/html/2310.08754v4**
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS: one (tokenizer, 2.6B GPT-3-style model) pair per design cell; 24 tokenizers, 26 models, ≤52.6B tokens each, ~59k GPU-hours total.
- ENDPOINT: average downstream accuracy over task suites, fertility on a 10,000-document held-out set, and training cost.
- REFERENCE / COMPARATOR: other tokenizer *design* cells — BPE vs Unigram, HuggingFace vs SentencePiece, vocab 33k/50k/82k/100k, English-only vs EN/DE/FR/IT/ES, plus a GPT-2 baseline.
- ESTIMAND: the mean downstream effect of a named tokenizer *design choice*, from a single fit and a single training run per cell.
- Relation to candidate: this is the source of the comparison constant (≈1.7-point monolingual average-accuracy gap BPE-SP-33 vs BPE-HF-33; ~9% best-vs-worst on ARC-Easy; up to 68% higher multilingual training cost with an English-centric tokenizer) **and** the verification of presupposition 2. It describes dedicated ~70B-word tokenizer corpora built to match the model corpus's domain proportions, but never states whether a tokenizer saw all 70B words or a subsample, and never varies the fitting corpus.

**Prior 3 — "What changes when you randomly choose BPE merge operations? Not much", arXiv:2305.03029. Opened: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.03029**
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS: an NMT system (Transformer-base, fairseq, 10k updates) per (language pair, merge count ∈ {2k, 5k, 32k}, merge-selection rule ∈ {greedy, softmax-sampled, count-proportional, uniform}); **10 seeded replications per cell**, EN→FI/ET/DE/UZ, 0.53M–4.2M sentence pairs.
- ENDPOINT: BLEU and chrF (sacrebleu). No vocabulary overlap, no formal fertility/compression, no perplexity.
- REFERENCE / COMPARATOR: standard frequency-greedy BPE, with seed-level standard errors (≈0.02–0.2 BLEU typically; 0.9–1.5 for Uzbek at 2k–5k merges) and a Bayesian model with 94% HDIs.
- ESTIMAND: the mean effect on translation quality of randomising the *merge-selection rule* on a **fixed, unresampled** corpus. Average variant effects span −0.98 (uniform) to +0.56 (greedy); uniform is credibly negative only for German (−1.72) and Uzbek (−6.92).
- Relation to candidate: it is the only opened source that puts a tokenizer-fitting perturbation *against seed noise* on a downstream endpoint. Its perturbation is strictly coarser than resampling the corpus, so it bounds the expected size of the candidate's limb (b) from above in high-resource settings — and that is simultaneously the nearest partial answer and the strongest objection to the candidate's cost.

**Also opened, not counted among the three (adjacent-object suites):** TokEval, arXiv:2608.18062 — 44 custom tokenizers all fitted on 10GB with no sampling detail, 46 nanochat models at 1.27B, BPB on FLORES+/validation plus BLiMP/GSM8K/HumanEval/MBPP, one model per condition, no refits, no seed comparison; varies algorithm, pre-tokenization and training *mixture* (English / balanced multilingual / code-heavy), i.e. distribution, not sample; separately observes that cross-tokenizer rankings for math and code are "unstable" under continued training, which is the candidate's phenomenon showing up as an unexplained nuisance. TokSuite, arXiv:2512.20757 — 14 off-the-shelf tokenizers, fits none, 1B models at ~100B tokens, one run per condition; its 0.17–0.27 spread in average relative robustness drop across tokenizers versus a 0.04 spread across 300M/1B/7B scale is the kind of effect size the candidate's variance term would have to be compared against, and the paper states it cannot compare to training-run variance.

## Novelty — how this differs from each

- **vs Prior 1 (2502.20273):** same object (the fitted tokenizer), different estimand. Prior 1 measures **bias toward an asymptote** under nested subsets; the candidate measures **variance across independent draws at fixed S**, with a within-draw control, which Prior 1's design forecloses by construction. The candidate adds two endpoints Prior 1 omits (merge-sequence divergence, per-domain fertility SD) and the entire model-level limb Prior 1 explicitly declines to run. Honest statement of overlap: Prior 1 substantially answers "does a 1GB fitting slice give you a different vocabulary than a 900GB one" (yes — ~42% of the vocabulary differs for BPE), so the candidate's limb (a) is **narrowed, not untouched**: what remains new there is the variance decomposition, the fertility and merge endpoints, and the variance knee as distinct from the bias knee.
- **vs Prior 2 (2310.08754):** Prior 2 varies the dimensions the field names as choices and holds the fitting sample fixed and undescribed; the candidate holds every named dimension fixed and varies **only** the fitting sample. Prior 2's estimand is a design effect; the candidate's is a nuisance variance against which that design effect must be read. Prior 2 cannot answer the candidate even in principle: one fit, one seed, no error bars, seeds explicitly dropped.
- **vs Prior 3 (2305.03029):** the intervention differs in kind — merge-*rule* randomisation on a fixed corpus versus corpus *resampling* under a fixed rule — and the regime differs (sub-5M-pair NMT, BLEU, 10k updates versus LLM pretraining, bits-per-byte, ≥2 scales, code/math/multilingual domains). Prior 3 also reports nothing at the tokenizer level, so it cannot locate where in the pipeline its null comes from. It is nonetheless the closest thing to a published answer to limb (b) and must be treated as a prior probability on the result, not as an unrelated paper.

## Novelty confidence: Medium

Distinct across all eleven vocabularies tried — no source anywhere fits k tokenizers on independent same-size samples with everything else fixed, and none reports a between-fit SD of any tokenizer or model quantity — but one neighbour (Prior 1) is close enough that it already publishes numbers for the candidate's cheap limb in bias form, and one (Prior 3) already publishes a partial null for the candidate's expensive limb in a different regime. Not High for those two reasons. Not Low: no near-duplicate design exists, and the grade rests on five papers read in full rather than on titles.

## Significance — consequence

**If yes** (between-draw SD of the same order as the algorithm-choice gap): every single-fit tokenizer ablation in the literature is reporting a design effect confounded with an unestimated variance term — concretely, Ali et al.'s ~1.7-point averages, TokEval's 98 intrinsic-metric/BPB correlations over 46 one-model conditions, and TokSuite's 0.10-wide robustness spread would all need k refits per arm before any ranking survives. Tokenizer comparisons acquire a mandatory replicate count; the fitting corpus and its size become reportable hyperparameters; and tokenizer-fit variance enters the error budget of tokenizer-related scaling-law claims. TokEval's own unexplained observation that math/code tokenizer rankings are unstable under continued training would get a candidate mechanism.
**If no** (between-draw SD at or below seed SD even at S = 0.1GB): the field's practice is licensed explicitly rather than by assumption — one fit is enough, a small slice suffices, and inheriting another model's tokenizer is defensible on this axis. That is a cheap reusable negative that retires a standing reviewer objection and tells practitioners not to spend compute here. Prior 3 makes this the more likely outcome for high-resource English, which lowers the expected surprise but not the usefulness.
**Either way**, the S-dependence with the knee located is directly actionable: it answers "how many bytes should I fit my tokenizer on?" in variance terms, complementing Prior 1's bias answer of 120–150GB for English.

## Significance — audience

(1) Pretraining data/infrastructure teams who currently pick the fitting slice by pipeline default — they would start recording it, and would size it by the measured knee. (2) The comparative-tokenizer research line that is visibly active right now (Ali et al. 2024; Dagan et al. 2024; TokSuite 2025; TokEval 2026; "Objective vs. Search" 2026) — it would gain the replicate requirement and the denominator its effect sizes currently lack; TokSuite and TokEval both state the comparison is beyond their scope, which is an audience naming its own need. (3) Evaluation-methodology and reproducibility researchers who already publish variance budgets for seeds, data order and benchmark sampling (Dodge et al. 2020; Quantifying Variance in Evaluation Benchmarks 2024) — tokenizer fitting is a missing term in those budgets. (4) Tooling maintainers (SentencePiece, HF `tokenizers`) who expose `input_sentence_size` with no documented consequence.

## Significance — fecundity

How many tokenizer refits must an ablation average over before a claimed gap is publishable? Does fitting-sample variance scale with morphological richness or low-resource status (Prior 1's slower Russian convergence and Prior 3's Uzbek sensitivity both point that way, from opposite directions)? Can the variance be engineered away — a bagged, averaged or regularised fitting objective, giving a deliberately stabilised tokenizer? Is between-draw divergence a cheap predictor of a tokenizer's brittleness under domain shift, letting tokenizers be screened without training models? Should a shipped tokenizer carry a stated uncertainty the way a calibration artifact or an International Standard does? And does the variance term explain any part of the known instability of cross-tokenizer rankings?

## Significance — anchoring

Touches: (i) the unreported-fitting-corpus gap, verified inside the sources themselves (TokEval: "All custom tokenizers are trained on 10 GB of text", no sampling detail; Ali et al.: dedicated ~70B-word tokenizer corpora, no statement of what each fit actually saw); (ii) the contested cross-tokenizer loss-comparison problem, which the candidate must solve operationally to answer limb (b); (iii) the ML variance-reporting programme, which has covered seeds, data order and benchmark item sampling but not preprocessing-artifact fitting; (iv) TokEval's open observation of unstable math/code tokenizer rankings; (v) the mature standard-error-of-equating and finite-training-set-codebook literatures, which have the structural answer and no connection to this object.

## Reason for silence

Three mechanisms, compounding.
1. **Determinism illusion plus artifact framing (conceptual bridge missing).** Tokenizer fitting is deterministic given its input, and it ships as a hash-pinned file (`tokenizer.json`, a `.model`) that is inherited and version-controlled. A file with a checksum does not look like an estimate with a standard error. The randomness lives one level up — in *which slice of the corpus was handed to the trainer* — and that slice is typically selected by a library default (`input_sentence_size`, `shuffle_input_sentence`) rather than by a recorded decision, so it is neither logged nor varied. Prior 1 states the phenomenon in a single sentence about rare tokens and then does not measure it, which is what a missing bridge looks like in print.
2. **Disciplinary boundary.** The method that would own this question — variance-component decomposition / multiverse analysis of a preprocessing artifact — belongs to statistics and evaluation methodology, while the tokenizer belongs to the data-engineering layer of a pretraining stack. Query 6 found that exact method applied to neuroimaging, EEG decoding, LLM annotation pipelines and autointerpretability scoring, and to no tokenizer. Nobody owns "the uncertainty of a preprocessing artifact" in this field.
3. **Incentive and cost gap.** Limb (a) is nearly free but publishes as a negative result whose main effect is to license *other people's* single runs; limb (b) costs k× a full ablation grid, and the field's own benchmark for that grid is ~59k GPU-hours for 26 single-seed models, with seeds explicitly dropped for cost. There is no reward for buying the error bar on someone else's comparison — the classic structure under which a cheap, obvious measurement goes unmade.

## Scores

**Well-posedness: 3/3.** After the Gate 1 reformulation the endpoints are measurable (vocabulary Jaccard, merge agreement, per-domain fertility SD, validation BPB, downstream scores), the budget confound is pinned (matched bytes and FLOPs, bits-per-byte), the two reference quantities are fixed in advance and one of them is a published number verified by reading the source, and the decision rule discriminates in both directions. The contested presuppositions are each flagged and each carried by a different endpoint (merge divergence is descriptive; fertility is load-bearing). Residual flaw, recorded rather than fatal: the published algorithm-choice gap used as reference quantity is itself a single-run point estimate with no error bar, so the comparison is against a number, not against an interval.

**Significance: 2/3.** Real, named, currently active audience that states the gap in its own papers; both answers change practice; good fecundity. Held below 3 because Prior 3 makes a near-null downstream result the more likely outcome in the regime most teams care about, and because the expensive limb would then be a confirmation; and because limb (a) is partly pre-answered in bias form by Prior 1, so the cheap half of the contribution is narrower than the intake assumed.

## Strongest remaining objection (pre-Gate 4)

Prior 3 randomised BPE's merge selection outright — a much coarser perturbation than resampling the fitting corpus — and found high-resource effects of ~1 BLEU against seed SEs of 0.02–0.2. If a strictly larger perturbation is nearly null, the candidate's limb (b) is predictably near-null for English at scale, and the expensive half of the design buys a confirmation. The available response (regime differs: pretraining, BPB, ≥2 scales, 100B-token budgets, code/math/multilingual domains where TokEval already observed unstable rankings; and a null is the answer the field needs to license its practice) is plausible but currently unevidenced in the candidate's own regime — Partial at best until someone runs limb (a).

## Suggested first step toward an answer (pointer only — not taken)

Limb (a) alone, at near-zero cost: fit k = 10 BPE and k = 10 Unigram tokenizers at fixed vocabulary 64k and fixed pre-tokenizer on **disjoint** 0.1 / 1 / 10 GB draws from one FineWeb-class corpus; report between-draw vocabulary Jaccard, longest common merge prefix, and per-domain fertility SD on held-out text, each against a within-draw control (same bytes, different shuffle). Position every number explicitly against arXiv:2502.20273's nested bias curve. That fixes the design constant — the number of refits limb (b) would need to detect an effect of the size of Ali et al.'s ~1.7-point gap — before any GPU is spent on models.
