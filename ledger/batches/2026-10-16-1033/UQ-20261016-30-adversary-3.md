## 1. Restatement (my own words, written before re-reading the record's framing section)

Take a real pretraining run that crashed and resumed one or more times. The run, or its release, in effect asserts: "this model was trained on these documents, in this order, each once." The question has two limbs.

(a) **How wrong is that assertion, and in what currency?** For runs where you can actually see the restart history and rebuild the data order — open releases, or a replication you instrument yourself using the recovery code that shipped frameworks really use — compute, per restart and in total: which documents were never fed in at all; which were fed in more times than claimed (both as a fraction of the nominal total); and how far documents moved from their claimed position in the stream.

(b) **Does it leave a mark on the model?** Under a test fixed in advance, with a stated smallest-detectable effect, ask whether documents sitting next to a restart boundary are memorized differently — or carry different trajectory-dependent influence — than matched documents far from any boundary.

The hoped-for payoff is a tolerance number: either "the exactness assumption behind attribution/memorization/provenance claims is good to X" or "it is off by Y, and here is the dated confound".

## 4. The eight attacks

### Attack 1 — Triviality (can it be answered in a page from existing knowledge?)
**Objection.** A large core of limb (a) is derivable without any new run, and the strongest form of the check has already been executed and published. Three steps.
(i) *The resume rule is code, and the code is public.* Megatron-LM checkpoints `consumed_train_samples` (and separately `skipped_train_samples`) in its training state, and `MegatronPretrainingSampler` is constructed with `consumed_samples`; so for that stack the restart offset is a restored integer and the skip/duplicate accounting is a subtraction, not an experiment.
(ii) *The dominant divergence term is a rollback artifact that never touched the weights.* On a crash at step K with the last checkpoint at C, both the weights and the loader counter are restored to C. The samples consumed in (C, K] were delivered twice, but their first delivery updated weight states that were then **discarded**. The sequence that produced the released weights is therefore unchanged by that duplication. OPEN-1B is the published demonstration: across seven restart segments it could "validate the restart using hash comparisons on the steps which were repeated against the prior segment" (§3.4) — i.e. the repeated steps reproduced the prior segment's state. That is a bitwise-stronger check than any multiset or ordering divergence, on exactly the auditable-run class the candidate scopes itself to, and it came out matching.
(iii) Consequently limb (a)'s headline, for state-checkpointed loaders at fixed world size, is *zero* for the weight-shaping sequence and *(replayed steps x global batch)* for the delivered sequence — a paragraph plus a table of loader versions.
I also *attempted* an arithmetic triviality kill from published frontier numbers and it failed: Llama 3 §3.3.4 gives 466 interruptions in 54 days and ">90% effective training time" but, in the text I read, no checkpoint interval and no quantified lost work. **My own arithmetic, with the source's own looseness carried through:** if "effective training time" means wall-clock not lost to failure and recovery, then all time spent re-executing rolled-back steps lies inside the <=10% remainder, so re-delivered tokens are **at most ~10%** of nominal — but that remainder also absorbs detection, requeue and checkpoint-load time (the 504-GPU analysis the record cites reports an 11-minute median retry interval, which is pure overhead, not recomputation), so the true figure is strictly and unknowably smaller. Published numbers give a ceiling, not an answer. I report this as a failed attack, not a result.
**Best response.** What survives (i)-(iii) is not nothing. Resharding under *per-rank sharded* streaming loaders (not Megatron's global-index design, and not OPEN-1B's purpose-built rank-independent stream) is where sample-level continuity is genuinely unclear, and no code-reading settles it without a run. Loaders that do not checkpoint loader state produce a *never-consumed* term, which is a real, non-zero, weight-relevant divergence and is not a rollback artifact. Operator-driven interventions — the OPT-175B logbook's data-position adjustments and rollbacks — change the effective sequence by hand, and no amount of code inspection recovers what an engineer typed. And the candidate's likeliest actual finding, that for most public releases the claimed sequence cannot be reconstructed at all, is a provenance result that no page of arithmetic delivers.
**Status: Partial.** (Not Unresolved: the residue above is genuine research, and my one quantitative triviality route — frontier-run arithmetic — provably does not close, because the needed checkpoint interval is unpublished.)

### Attack 2 — Disguise (is it a known question renamed?)
**Objection.** Translated out of AI-infrastructure vocabulary, the question is a standard, named exercise in at least four fields, and has been posed once in-field under a different name. Clinical trials: this is per-protocol / as-treated accounting — measure the delivered exposure instead of assuming the assigned one; the methodology is textbook (search 2). Survey methodology: limb (a) is frame **coverage error**, decomposed exactly as the candidate decomposes it into undercoverage (never consumed) and overcoverage/duplicate listings (consumed more than claimed), inside the Total Survey Error framework (search 3). Regulated manufacturing: planned-versus-actual **reconciliation** against preset limits with mandatory deviation investigation is routine GMP batch-record practice (search 9). And in-field, arXiv:2406.05546 is literally titled "Effects of Data Consistency in Parallel Machine Learning Training" and asks whether relaxing data consistency across failure recovery hurts the model — the candidate's own question, posed in 2024, which the generator's eleven queries missed.
**Best response.** Borrowed vocabulary is not a borrowed answer. ITT/per-protocol, coverage error and GMP reconciliation supply *estimand templates* the candidate should adopt (and its V1 largely has), not values; none of those fields has ever applied them to a training run, and the candidate's limb (b) — a model-side signature of the deviation — has no analogue in them at all. The one in-field posing is weak where it matters: 2406.05546 is a single CNN on FashionMNIST on one laptop-class machine, its endpoint is accuracy over time, its reference is checkpointing, its nearest accounting figure counts *gradients processed*, and by my reading it never counts a skipped or duplicated sample or looks at any individual example. Asking a question badly at toy scale leaves the question open, and its existence actually corroborates the candidate's silence claim: even the paper named after data consistency under failure does not do the accounting.
**Status: Partial.** (The objection does not land as "already answered", but it does land as "already named, and once already asked" — which the record's novelty section does not know.)

### Attack 3 — Combinatorial novelty (Field A x Field B with no problem at the interface?)
**Objection.** The pairing is "distributed-systems recovery" x "data attribution/memorization", and at the interface the problem is thinner than advertised, because the systems side has already closed it by construction where anyone cares. OPEN-1B defines a canonical stream from seed and manifests whose "complete position ... is captured by a small, rank-independent record" that "does not depend on world size, rank, number of nodes, or any other property of the machine executing it" (§2.3) — which dissolves the record's sharpest presupposition (#6, that resharding changes what "resume" means) as a design choice rather than a research finding. Megatron's global `consumed_train_samples` does the same at coarser granularity. So the interface reduces to: badly engineered loaders have bugs; well engineered ones do not; here is a bug catalogue.
**Best response.** There is one genuine interface object, and neither parent field owns it: the distinction between the **delivered** sequence (what the loader handed to accelerators, an at-least-once quantity the systems side measures in goodput) and the **effective** sequence (what survived into the released weights, the exactly-once quantity attribution and provenance claims actually rest on). No systems paper I opened states which of the two its guarantees cover; no attribution paper states which of the two it assumes. OPEN-1B's hash check is evidence about the effective sequence, and it says nothing about the delivered one; ElasWave's loss-deviation measurement conflates them. Naming and measuring that gap is interface work, not a cross-product.
**Status: Partial.**

### Attack 4 — Presupposition failure
**Objection.** The question smuggles in an unflagged assumption that does most of its work: that the restart-induced divergence enters the trajectory that produced the released weights, so that a per-document model-side signature at a boundary is possible in principle. For the dominant shipped configuration this is false. Under rollback-replay with loader state in the checkpoint, the duplicated deliveries belong to a discarded branch of the optimisation; the effective sequence is the nominal one. OPEN-1B's §3.4 hash comparison on repeated steps is a published instance of this exactness holding across seven restarts. The word "realized" in the canonical question does not distinguish delivered from effective, so limb (a) can return a headline percentage that is, mechanically, an accounting of wasted compute, while limb (b) is then null **by construction** rather than by evidence — and a bounded null obtained where no mechanism exists is not information about the floor of anything. The record's presupposition list has seven entries and this is not among them; presupposition 4 concerns *sensitivity* to a perturbation, taking for granted that a perturbation reached the weights at all.
**Best response.** The presupposition is unstated, not false across the question's stated scope. The question's own answer criteria require, at minimum, "a loader that checkpoints its state, one that does not, and one world-size change": in the second configuration the never-consumed term is real and does shape the weights (documents the restarted shuffle never reaches in that epoch are simply absent), and in the third, per-rank sharded loaders can lose sample-level continuity in ways no one has measured. Operator-driven rollbacks of the OPT-logbook kind also move the effective sequence by hand. And discovering that the delivered-versus-effective distinction is where the whole question lives would itself be the finding — it is the thing neither parent literature states.
**Status: Partial.** (I deliberately do not grade this Unresolved, which would be fatal: I can show the assumption fails in the main case, not across the question's scope. But this is the strongest surviving objection, and the question should not be recorded without the amendment that splits the two sequences.)

### Attack 5 — Vagueness (answerable whatever the evidence shows, or never?)
**Objection.** Two soft spots. "Ordering divergence (displacement from claimed position)" names no statistic — mean absolute displacement in samples, in steps, Kendall's tau, fraction displaced beyond a threshold — and those can be made to look large or negligible on the same data. And the deliverable is version-relative: the answer is a property of a loader build, not a fact about training runs, so "the divergence" has no stable referent and the number expires with the next release.
**Best response.** Pre-registration is exactly the instrument that closes the first hole: the question as written requires a test fixed in advance with a stated detection floor, and a pre-registered protocol must name its displacement statistic before it sees data, so the answerer cannot shop for a flattering one. The second point is a feature of the design, not vagueness: the question requires that "the recovery semantics in force at each restart [be] named (and resharding events separated out)", so the output is an explicitly labelled table of (loader, version, world-size change) -> divergence, which is how any metrological claim about an instrument is reported. A null is bounded by the floor; a non-null is attributed to a named configuration. Neither branch is unfalsifiable.
**Status: Resolved.** (Weakest of the eight objections. One amendment is still required: name the ordering statistic in the pre-registration.)

### Attack 6 — Significance inflation
**Objection.** The audience list is inflated, and I can source that for its most impressive member. The record names "EU AI Act training-data summaries" and provenance/standards work as an audience that would "set a tolerance in the protocol". The Commission's own explanatory notice and template say the opposite: the summary should be "generally comprehensive in its scope instead of technically detailed", it "does not require disclosure of the details for the specific data and works used to train the model" (para. 16) and "does not require disclosure of the exact mix and composition of data sources" (para. 22), scraped content is reported at the granularity of the "top 10 % of all domain names", and the AI Office supervises without "performing a work-by-work assessment" (para. 26). A regime that declines work-by-work assessment cannot act on a per-document consumption divergence either way. The ML-BOM/attestation literature I sampled (TAIBOM, AIBoMGen, OWASP) attests datasets and artifacts, not consumption sequences. The infrastructure limb is admitted weak by the record and is further undercut by OPEN-1B, which solves the property by design and verifies it by hash. That leaves one audience: attribution and memorization researchers — and on the likeliest branch (zero effective divergence for state-checkpointed loaders) their "license" is already derivable from the code, so the publication tells them to carry on.
**Best response.** One real audience is enough if it would genuinely change practice, and this one would on the branch that matters: if per-rank sharded or state-less loaders produce a non-zero never-consumed term, then every "document D was seen exactly once at position p" claim built on such a run — which includes work on open runs whose loaders are not Megatron-class — needs an error bar it currently does not report. Litigation-side analysis also does not inherit the AI Office's self-restraint: a court asking whether a work was actually consumed is not bound by a disclosure template's granularity, and "the manifest does not establish consumption" is a technical claim with or without the AI Act. The fecundity items (should a realized data-consumption log be a release artifact? can restart history be read out of weights?) are live regardless.
**Status: Partial.** (The record's Significance 2/3 is defensible only after the regulatory limb is struck; on my evidence the audience argument is one-and-a-half limbs, not five.)

### Attack 7 — Reason for silence
**Objection.** The record's mechanism (1) — a disciplinary boundary in which the infrastructure community instruments only time, so data-sequence integrity is on nobody's dashboard — is partly overturned by evidence the record itself cites but did not read. OPEN-1B makes the batch stream an explicit, seed-derived, machine-independent claim, checkpoints document-level consumption counters per source, and validates its own seven restarts by hash comparison on repeated steps. That is the infrastructure community treating claimed-versus-realized data position as a first-class, audited property. The boundary is thinner than claimed. A simpler mechanism fits the silence better, and it is unflattering to the candidate: in the configurations that matter the answer is zero by construction and known to be zero by the people who build the loaders, so there is nothing publishable; where it is not zero, the runs are proprietary or the loader is simply buggy and gets fixed rather than measured.
**Best response.** "The answer is zero and everyone knows" is itself only an assertion until someone measures it, and it is exactly the assertion a vendor has an incentive to make (the record's mechanism (2): Cerebras "deterministic checkpoints", MosaicML "Elastic Determinism" are feature claims, and OPEN-1B sets "no tolerance" for mismatch while shipping its reduction library as "a proprietary library", by the paper's own conclusion). OPEN-1B is a purpose-built 1B-parameter demonstration whose stream design is explicitly *not* what shipped stacks do; generalising from it to frontier practice is the error the candidate exists to prevent. Mechanism (2) — the only parties who can audit a frontier run's realized sequence are the labs whose provenance claims the audit would qualify, under live regulatory and litigation pressure — survives my evidence intact, and Llama 3's §3.3.4 is the exhibit: 466 interruptions reported, effective-training-time reported, and not one word about the data loader, consumed samples, or data repeated on restart.
**Status: Partial.**

### Attack 8 — Dissolution
**Objection.** Under analysis the question separates into four, three of which are not research. (i) *What are each shipped loader's resume semantics?* — read the code; Megatron's checkpointed `consumed_train_samples`/`skipped_train_samples` answers it for the most-used stack. (ii) *How much compute is wasted re-executing rolled-back steps?* — already instrumented by infrastructure teams as goodput and effective training time; this is the delivered-sequence number wearing a provenance costume. (iii) *Is the claimed sequence reconstructable at all for public releases?* — a real provenance question, and the one with teeth, but it is a survey of release practice, not a measurement of divergence. (iv) *Does a restart leave a per-document model signature?* — null by construction wherever (i) returns "state is checkpointed", so the live version of limb (b) is confined to a small set of configurations that must be named in advance. Once split, the candidate's headline — "the divergence between claimed and realized token sequence" — turns out to name a quantity that is either zero, or wasted compute, or unavailable.
**Best response.** A question that decomposes into a code question, a telemetry question, a survey and a bounded experiment has not evaporated; it has acquired a work plan, and the decomposition is itself the contribution, because no existing source states which of the four it is answering. The delivered/effective split is not available in the literature today: ElasWave measures loss deviation (both sequences conflated), OPEN-1B measures state hashes (effective only), goodput papers measure time (delivered only), and attribution papers assume exactness (neither). Limb (iii) remains an unanswered question with consequences for every "was this document in the training set" claim, and limb (iv) remains genuinely open in the per-rank-sharded and state-less configurations.
**Status: Partial.**

## 5. Closing: the single strongest surviving objection

The strongest objection that survives my own best defence of the candidate is the **delivered-versus-effective collapse** (attack 4, with attacks 1 and 8 as its consequences). The canonical question's "realized token sequence" is ambiguous between the tokens a loader handed to accelerators and the tokens that shaped the released weights, and under the dominant shipped recovery path — rollback to a checkpoint that carries the loader's own position, as in Megatron's checkpointed `consumed_train_samples` and in OPEN-1B's rank-independent stream record — those two differ by exactly the discarded branch of the optimisation. The duplicated deliveries that limb (a) would score as "consumed more times than claimed" updated weights that were thrown away. OPEN-1B has already published the stronger check on this, in the auditable-run class the candidate scopes itself to: across its seven restart segments it validated each restart "using hash comparisons on the steps which were repeated against the prior segment" (§3.4). So on the main branch limb (a) measures wasted compute under a provenance name, and limb (b)'s null is a null without a mechanism, which licenses nothing. The candidate is not dead — the never-consumed term in loaders that do not checkpoint their state, sample-level continuity under per-rank resharding, operator-driven data-position edits of the OPT-logbook kind, and above all whether any public release's claimed sequence is reconstructable at all, are open and consequential — but it should not be recorded in its present wording. It needs the two sequences separated, limb (b) scoped to the configurations where the effective sequence actually moves, the EU-AI-Act audience limb struck on the Commission template's own words, and arXiv:2406.05546 and arXiv:2505.22042 added to its prior art.
