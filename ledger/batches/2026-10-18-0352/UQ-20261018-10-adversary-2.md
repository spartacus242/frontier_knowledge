## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take one country's fleet of in-service electronic household electricity meters. Draw a proper random sample from the distribution utility's asset register, stratified by meter model, firmware and build year. Feed each sampled meter a fixed, published set of realistic household current waveforms — dimmers, switch-mode supplies, LED lamps, PV inverters, speed-controlled motors: high crest factor, fast di/dt — while measuring the true energy with a reference instrument that has been *shown*, not assumed, to be immune to those same waveforms. Two numbers are wanted: (a) how the relative registration error is distributed across the fleet, weighted to the population, with a confidence interval and the tails; and (b) given an assumed national picture of how much of household load is of that kind, what fraction of household electricity billing is therefore wrong.

In short: the lab says some of these meters can read wildly wrong on real appliance waveforms; nobody has measured what the *fleet* does, and nobody has priced it in energy.

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional** — nothing Unresolved on attacks 1, 2 or 4, so not a rejection; Unresolved on attack 7 caps it at Provisional, and novelty cannot exceed Medium because limb 1's estimand turns out to be routine practice in an untried vocabulary and because the MeterEMI Final Publishable Report is still unread (three routes failed). Promotion conditions: open the 17NRM02 Final Publishable Report and Summary and confirm no per-model comparison table over a representative EU meter selection; re-cut the motivating gap and the reason for silence so neither rests on the false claim that in-service sampling cannot yield an error distribution; and declare a misregistration tolerance for limb 2.
