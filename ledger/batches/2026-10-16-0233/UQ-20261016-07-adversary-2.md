## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take a corpus where chest-X-ray labels were produced by running NLP over the attached free-text report (MIMIC-CXR, CheXpert and friends), and where you can tell which of a patient's studies came first. Look only at cases where a report says "finding F is present" and the same patient's *previous* report also said F was present, and where F is the sort of thing you could in principle see on one film (so "stable", "improving", "resolved" on their own don't count). Question: (a) how often will three or more radiologists, shown only the current image and nothing else, say F is not there — once you subtract how often they say that about first-time ("index") exams read the same blinded way; (b) are report-derived positive labels wrong more often on follow-up exams than on index exams for the same finding classes, and is the extra error piled up in findings that typically clear (consolidation, effusion, oedema, pneumothorax, lines/tubes) rather than in findings that persist (cardiomegaly, fibrosis); and (c) does a standard classifier's measured score move when you drop or re-label the follow-up exams, compared with dropping an equally large random slice?

In one line: *how much of the "ground truth" in report-labelled imaging corpora is a restatement of the previous exam rather than a description of this one, and does that bias benchmark scores in a known direction?*

### Pages actually opened (4 fetches)

- https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15635 → PDF has no machine-readable text (empty extraction). Re-opened via abs page.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15635 → Cajas Ordóñez et al., "ModaLens: Measuring Image Sensitivity in Report-Conditioned Medical VLMs", submitted 2026-09-14. Image-swap audit of MedGemma-27B on 3,199 paired MIMIC-CXR cases; answers change on ~4% of trials when the report is present vs ~21% without it, i.e. the report substitutes for the image. Uses same-patient other-study images as the swap source and explicitly flags that its own labels are report-derived. **Adjacent but distinct**: it measures a model's insensitivity to the image, not whether a follow-up report's assertions are supported by its own image; no series-order/index-vs-follow-up analysis, no reader adjudication.
- https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11467 → VisualCheXbert verified. The record's quotations are accurate and if anything understated: §4.2.2 contains "radiologists labeling reports have access to clinical report history, which biases their diagnoses", "diagnoses were carried forward from prior studies", "clinical context and prior imaging played a role in these discrepancies", the "decreased"/"trace residual" effusion observation, and a note that in the ICU "multiple consecutive radiographs are taken". It reports Cohen's kappa ~0.312–0.430 and F1 ~0.470–0.561 against image labels, and performs **no** stratification by series order or index-vs-follow-up. Prior is real, correctly cited, and genuinely the nearest neighbour.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10084 → Agudelo-Londoño et al., "When Repository Labels Are Not Image-Level Truth", submitted 2026-08-10. Verified real; cardiomegaly-only MIMIC-CXR audit, near-zero agreement, ~1% of expert-confirmed cases recovered, disagreement attributed to non-mention; abstract mentions no prior reports, no carry-forward, no index-vs-follow-up. Record's characterisation is accurate, including that it points the opposite direction (false negatives).

Spot-check of cited priors: VisualCheXbert (2102.11467), 2608.10084, 2609.15635 confirmed real with content as described. 2210.06340 and 2602.23676 were not re-opened within budget; the record already flags that the ">76% of reports reference priors" figure is a second-hand citation it did not verify at source, which is the correct handling. No fabricated prior detected.

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional** — nothing Unresolved, novelty holds at Medium after 9 new vocabularies found no duplicate, but the close neighbour (VisualCheXbert, which names carry-forward in print and notes consecutive ICU radiographs) plus the unfixed conspicuity confound in attacks 4/5 are exactly Gate 5's Provisional triggers; promotion to Validated requires the question text itself to carry a severity/conspicuity-matching clause (or a non-radiograph anchor) and a pre-registered policy for hybrid change-plus-finding phrasings.

## Single strongest killing point

The blinded single-image panel cannot distinguish "restated but no longer there" from "still there but now below the detection threshold of a portable AP film", and the index-exam noise floor does not fix it because index positives are systematically more conspicuous than follow-up residual positives — so the question's own predicted signature (excess report-label false positives in the finding classes that resolve) is generated just as well by declining conspicuity with no inheritance at all. The design matches on finding class and never on severity, and conspicuity appears nowhere in the question.
