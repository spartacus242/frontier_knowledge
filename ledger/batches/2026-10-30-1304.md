## The cadence, corrected

The biology batch twelve hours earlier reported a problem worth repeating: it ran about six hours against a six-hour cron, so every second firing was absorbed and the rotation advanced one area per twelve hours rather than per six. Three options were put to Jace — leave it, move the cron to twelve hours, or cut a post-Gate-5 layer, which the evidence said was the wrong trade. Nothing was changed; it is his call and the card is still open.

**This batch ran in about four hours from its 12:33 firing — three and a half from the start of candidate generation at 13:04, which is what the batch directory is named for — with every layer intact, so the absorption turns out to be a property of the pipeline's shape rather than of its depth.** What changed is only how the rounds were sequenced. The biology batch ran its verification pass, then one agent per entry that both audited the judge and amended the entry, then a correction pass — each round waiting for the whole of the previous one. This batch split the audit from the application and launched each agent the moment its own input existed: the nineteen readers first, then a dedicated auditor per entry as each write-up was finished, then an applier per entry as each audit landed. The audits and the write-ups overlapped; four of the five appliers were running before the last audit returned.

That fits inside a six-hour cron with about two hours to spare, and the 18:33 firing should not be absorbed. **No layer was cut.** If anything the pipeline got deeper: separating the auditor from the applier added a sixth and seventh agent and produced two of this batch's most useful corrections, both of which overturned the judge. So the recommendation on the open card changes: the honest answer is now "leave the cron alone", and the twelve-hour proposal should be read as a fallback for a batch that genuinely needs six hours rather than as the fix for this one.

## The mix clause, and how this batch handled it

LEDGER.md's Parameters require all five candidates of a batch to come from the one rotating area; the Depth requirement's in-scope-fields paragraph requires that at least two of the five be in mathematics, physics or computer science and at most two in medicine. For a medicine batch both bounds fail at once, and trimming to two still leaves zero — so read per batch, a medicine batch could only return nothing. This is the batch where that conflict actually bit.

The reading taken, and the one the runner enforced, is that the mix clause is a distribution constraint over the **cycle** rather than over the batch: one full cycle is 25 candidates, 15 of them in mathematics, physics and computer science and 5 in medicine, which satisfies both bounds. Nothing in LEDGER.md was changed to accommodate it, and nothing in the runner script was changed either — the reading was passed in as part of the run's area parameter, so both stored files stay as Jace wrote them while the question is his to settle. A decision card went to him earlier today; until he answers, a later medicine batch should be run the same way.

### What moved, entry by entry

**UQ-20261030-06 — Rejected, unchanged; the ground demoted in degree, not in kind.** The whole verdict stands on Roychoudhuri, Putcha and Møller 2006 (doi 10.1007/s10552-005-0615-9, PMID 16633912) having asked the candidate's question across its exact five paired organs, and the entry already records that both adversaries read it at abstract level only. Five readers and about eighteen fetches later the body is still unread, and the pass established that it is unreachable by every route tried from here rather than merely untried: OpenAlex reports the work closed with all four locations serving no PDF, both named institutional deposits hold metadata and no file, and `rd.springer.com` serves the abstract, the section headings and the complete reference list and no body. Three routes remain genuinely open and are recorded as open, not exhausted — a print or interlibrary copy, `scholar.archive.org` once its rate limit clears, and the publisher PDF. Unpaywall was declined on policy, because it asks for a contact email address and this pipeline does not send one; that is a route declined, never a route that failed.

What the pass did find is a refutation of the entry's own routing claim. The entry said that only lung and testis left-right size ratios are served in print. They are not the only ones: Perven et al. 2014 (DOI 10.3329/medtoday.v26i1.21304) prints right and left ovarian mass at three age bands over 70 bodies, Ahmed et al. 2011 disagrees with it in sign at reproductive age, Pongsatha et al. 2002 prints ovarian volumes, and Scutt 2006 prints a mean left:right breast size ratio of 1.04 (SD 0.18). So a mass-grain account of the breast and ovary asymmetries is constructible from print today, which is what the routed row now says.

**UQ-20261030-07 — the batch's one verdict change, Rejected to Provisional.** The entry staked its verdict in terms on one clause of one adversary: that the measurand is degenerate at the candidate's own pinned 2 per cent variant-calling threshold, so there is no between-donor contrast left to decompose. A reader at full text across eight corpora established that this is a property of that corpus and that pipeline rather than of the measurand — Braun 2021 prints combined bottlenecks of 38 at 1 per cent and 15 at 3 per cent on the same pairs, Martin and Koelle print 38 of 39 pairs at 10 or above at 1 per cent, Lythgoe prints 1 to 8 across 14 pairs, and the one corpus calling at exactly 2 per cent prints a fitted mean of 1.68 with a 95th percentile of 11 and one pair above 200.

The ground that actually carries the re-grade, though, was already in the adversary's own log and nobody had used it: adversary 1 had labelled the step from printed degeneracy to non-estimability as "MY OWN REASONING, NOT A SOURCE FINDING" and reported that its grounding search returned nothing, and LEDGER.md already makes such a kill Partial at best and never Unresolved. That lifts the rejection trigger on the record's own terms rather than on anything the judge inferred — which matters, because the judge's first draft cleared the attack on its own reading of how one paper enumerates its transmission pairs, and the audit refused that: by the same standard the ledger applies to an adversary, a judge's inference can hold a clause short of fatal but cannot clear it.

**No late Gate 4 is owed here, and the entry says so and says why.** LEDGER.md's late-Gate-4 rule exists because a candidate rejected at Gate 2 "has no Gate 4 at all". This candidate was rejected at Gate 4, where all eight attacks had already run against the full Gate 0–3 record with two independent adversaries, so what is owed is a re-grade and not a gate. Writing that onto the face stops a later reader hunting for a pass that was correctly not run.

**UQ-20261030-08 — Rejected, unchanged; one leg loses a group and one gains a candidate member.** The disguise leg now rests on two author-disjoint groups rather than three: on a full-text read of the same mirror both adversaries used, Noble et al. 2022 does not contrast boundary growth with a front that carries bulk diversity, and the words "pulled" and "pushed" are absent from both the published version and the preprint. ## What the auditors found in the judge's own rulings

The judge's write-up of a verification pass is the least reliable document in the chain, so this batch gave each write-up a dedicated auditor whose only job was to check it against the raw reader returns — separate from the agent that then applied the corrections. Six auditors ran over the five entries, two of them independently on UQ-10.

**They filed 83 defects: 19 on UQ-06, 16 on UQ-07, 13 on UQ-08, 11 and 15 on UQ-10's two audits, and 9 on UQ-09.** All six endorsed the status the write-up recommended. Two dissented on substance, and both dissents were upheld against the judge:

- On **UQ-08** the judge restated the three-organ-site rule upward from "at most one member" to "at least two", on a cross-reader inference it had labelled as its own in one section and then used as established in another. The auditor refused it: the kidney member was never graded against the rule by the reader that found the paper, the liver member rests on a reanalysis plus a reader's flagged inference with the primary methods unread, and the one reader actually asked the question answered "at most one". The entry now carries the honest count instead.
- On **UQ-07** the auditor accepted the status change to Provisional and replaced the ground for it, as described above. It also found the sentence "no clause of attack 4 comes back FALSE on a source any reader opened" to be false: the kill's clause about transmission direction being established was *reinforced* by six corpora, and only its consequence was rescoped. A clause and its consequence fall separately, and an entry that blurs them cannot be re-read later.

The recurring defect from the previous batch recurred, though less widely: a write-up quoting "the entry" for a phrase that lives only in its Gate 0–3 record or an adversary log. Five instances, spread over three of the five entries, each caught by grep of the entry file; the auditors on UQ-07 and UQ-08 extracted every attribution to "the entry" in their write-ups, grepped each one, and returned that family clean. One new family appeared and is worth naming — **an inverted decisive test**: UQ-06's write-up stated backwards what its single named decisive check would do, so the next reader would have been handed reversed polarity on the one thing the pass called decisive.

The two independent audits of UQ-10 overlapped only partly and corrected each other once, over whether the entry had invented a quotation or relayed one its adversary was served. Two auditors on one document is worth the cost.

**One defect was the judge's own and is recorded as such.** The brief sent to read Li et al. 2024 carried a guessed DOI and a guessed PII, neither of which exists; the reader spent calls discovering that and then found the real identifiers. The entry prints no DOI for that paper at all, so the guess was the judge's, not the entry's. The rule it breaks was already written down after an earlier batch: if a brief must ask a reader to *find* a source, say so and attach no guesses, because a guess reads as the claim.

## What this batch adds to the process notes

1. **A Gate 4 kill whose attack falls on verification is re-graded, not re-gated.** LEDGER.md's late-Gate-4 rule is written for a Gate 2 kill and says why — such a candidate "has no Gate 4 at all". A candidate rejected at Gate 4 already had all eight attacks run by two independent adversaries, so nothing was skipped and no late pass is owed. The entry has to say that in terms, or a later reader goes looking for a pass that was correctly not run. This is the ledger's first reversal of a Gate 4 kill.
2. **The ground for such a re-grade has to be the record's own, not the judge's.** The judge's first draft cleared the attack on its own reading of one paper's sampling structure, against two readers' sourced conclusions. By the same standard the ledger applies to an adversary, a judge's inference can hold a clause short of fatal but cannot clear it. The ground that works was in the adversary's own log all along: it had labelled its load-bearing step as its own reasoning and reported that its grounding search returned nothing.
3. **A kill pinned to the candidate's own threshold is a one-corpus kill until somebody surveys the threshold.** And the strongest support for rescoping it was not the survey of other corpora but a printed sentence in the killing paper itself: half its specimens carried no variants above threshold at all, which makes its degeneracy a property of its own donors' diversity.
4. **A clause and its consequence fall separately.** Writing "no clause came back false" when a clause was in fact reinforced and only its consequence rescoped makes an entry unre-readable later.
5. **A dedicated auditor, separate from the agent that applies the corrections, pays for itself.** Six auditors filed 83 defects, endorsed all five statuses, and overturned the judge on substance twice. Two auditors on one entry overlapped only partly and corrected each other once.
6. **A brief must never attach a guessed identifier.** This rule was already written down and was broken again this batch, with a guessed DOI and a guessed PII for a paper the entry cites without one.
