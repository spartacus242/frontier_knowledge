## Restatement in my own words

Organs for transplant are currently charged for preservation injury by the clock: cold ischemia
time (CIT), the elapsed hours between cross-clamp and reperfusion. That variable assumes the
organ sat at a known, essentially constant cold temperature. The candidate asks: if you actually
have the temperature trace (container or perfusate, sampled over the storage interval), and you
convert that trace into a chemical-kinetics exposure — the time integral of an Arrhenius rate
factor, i.e. "how much degradation could have happened", equivalently duration paired with the
pharmaceutical mean kinetic temperature of the interval, plus an explicit extra penalty for
sub-zero excursions, with the activation energy and penalty shape fixed before looking at data —
then (a) does adding that single number to a risk model that already contains CIT buy you real
discrimination and calibration for delayed graft function and 1-year graft function in a nested
comparison, and (b) does CIT's own coefficient shrink when the dose is in the model, i.e. is part
of what we have been calling a time effect actually an uncontrolled-temperature effect?

Two things worth saying about that restatement. The first limb is a prediction-increment question
about a particular hand-built summary statistic of a temperature trace. The second limb is a
causal/mediation claim that CIT is partly a proxy. They have very different evidential standing,
and the record's significance case leans on the second while its feasibility case leans on the
first.
