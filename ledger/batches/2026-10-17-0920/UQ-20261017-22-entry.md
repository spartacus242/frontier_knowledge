Question (canonical): For long-run Li-ion cell-cycling experiments — (a) PROSPECTIVE: under a design in which cells are rotated between channels on a pre-declared schedule (>=3 channels x >=3 cells per group, sham rotations, traceable per-channel current/voltage/temperature calibration, logged chamber position), what are the cell, channel and cell-by-channel variance components, with intervals, of per-interval degradation rate and of pre-declared trajectory features, and what cycle-life variance share do they imply under the declared trajectory model; and (b) ARCHIVAL: in archived protocol-ranking experiments where the same physical channels hosted cells on different protocols across rounds (Severson 2019; Attia 2020), what is the channel-level variance when channel enters as a random effect alongside protocol, and does the top-k protocol set change under channel-shrunken protocol estimates?
(Graded version is V2; V1 — "what share of between-cell variance in cycle life is attributable to the channel" — failed Gate 1 as unidentifiable in the designs it criticises. Both versions in the record.)
Alternative formulations:
- Is what the battery field calls "cell-to-cell variability" partly channel-to-channel variability of the test rig?
- Under a rotation design, what are the cell/channel/cell-by-channel variance components of cycle life, and how do channel-shrunken protocol effects compare with the published rankings?
Motivating gap: Presupposition inversion on a settled claim. Cycle-life spread among nominally identical cells is read as manufacturing tolerance and used to size test matrices, warranty margins and ML benchmarks — but one cell per channel for life makes cell and channel perfectly confounded, so the standard design cannot separate them at all. Per-channel current/voltage accuracy and position-dependent chamber gradients act non-linearly on degradation rate (Arrhenius; plating thresholds). The field names the issue twice without estimating it: Dechent et al. 2021 list "location in a given testing chamber" as an extrinsic source and declare intrinsic-only scope; Devie/Dubarry et al. 2015 assert "calibration across channels" as a control and report no channel component.
Presuppositions: (1) a cell-vs-channel decomposition is identifiable with a feasible design — CONTESTED and hardest (ageing is destructive, so identification needs rotation plus a trajectory model; a pure rate-multiplier channel effect is partly absorbable by the cell's rate parameter); (2) rotation does not itself perturb the cell — FLAGGED, addressed by sham rotations; (3) channel metrological differences large enough to move rate — PARTLY SUPPORTED (P2 summary: 0.5 degC extrinsic deviation -> >1.5% resistance variation; Attia SI: "single temperature chamber for all channels", "cell temperature increase during cycling is significant"); (4) public datasets record channel identity — VERIFIED TRUE at Gate 4 (two independent repos read a per-cell `channel_id` out of the Severson/MATR files); chamber position and calibration history are not recorded; (5) rate sensitive to small T/current offsets — ESTABLISHED; (6) protocol and channel not crossed/randomised in the published experiments — UNVERIFIED (Attia SI describes no assignment scheme; V2's archival limb needs only the weak form); (7) cycle life defined consistently across channels — FLAGGED.
What would count as an answer: (a) the prospective rotation experiment above reporting the three variance components with intervals and the implied cycle-life share; or (b) a re-analysis of an archived protocol-ranking experiment with channel as a random effect, stating whether top-k changes and whether channel variance could have produced the published ranking difference. A null answers it: a channel component indistinguishable from zero at pre-declared precision converts a named confound into a validated assumption and strengthens the archives.
