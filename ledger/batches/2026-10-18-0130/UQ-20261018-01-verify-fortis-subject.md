## Findings
Q1 Main paper: Fortis S, Corazalla EO, Jacobs DR Jr, Kim HJ. Persistent empiric COPD diagnosis and treatment after pulmonary function test showed no obstruction. Respir Care 2016;61(9):1192-1200. DOI 10.4187/respcare.04647. PMID 27165420.
Q2 Numbers (abstract): 1,805 subjects with one PFT session (spirometry + plethysmography); 855 included = 473 normal PFT + 382 restrictive defect. Persistent empiric COPD diagnosis 60/855 (7%). Persistent empiric treatment (bronchodilators and/or ICS) 208/855 (24%). ORs: dx persistence - current smoker 44.7, former 17.3, age 1.03/yr; treatment - empiric dx 24.6, etc. Denominator for both is all 855 (not only those with a prior COPD label; the abstract does not give how many carried a COPD label before the PFT). Not verified against full text. Abstract also says ~1/4 of FEV1/FVC >= LLN subjects had nonspecific PFT abnormalities, denominator not given.
Q3 Design: retrospective record review of single PFT sessions. Abstract gives no follow-up period or time window; no stated period after the test. Reads as cross-sectional/one-time ascertainment. Caveat: methods not read (full text unreadable).
Q4 Comparator: none described in abstract; ORs are within-cohort (factor vs no factor). Normal vs restrictive PFT subgroups exist but no external control. Caveat: abstract only.
Q5 NOT FOUND. No paper found measuring rate of removal/resolution/inactivation of a chronic-disease code after a contradicting test as time-to-event with comparator. Searches 2 and 6 above.
