## Restatement (my own words)

The headline "rare-disease patients wait ~5 years for a diagnosis, and the wait is falling" is computed from people who already have a diagnosis. Anyone whose symptoms began recently can only be in the sample if they were diagnosed quickly, so recent onset cohorts look fast by construction (right truncation); advocacy-panel recruitment may push the other way by over-sampling long odysseys. The candidate asks: pick one data source that lists people by when symptoms started (not by whether they were diagnosed), estimate the onset-to-diagnosis distribution per onset cohort with truncation and delay-dependent inclusion modelled, put the naive diagnosed-only estimate beside it on the same records, and see whether the published downward trend keeps its sign and size, splitting any difference into truncation artefact, case-mix drift and real change in the diagnosis hazard.

- F3. https://ard.bmj.com/content/74/3/e12 — returned the ORIGINAL Sørensen & Hetland DANBIO article (contributors "JS" and "MLH"), not a reply letter. The record describes 74(3):e12 as "the other half of the correspondence"; that is a mislabel — e12 is the target paper, e77 in vol 73 is the comment. Content: authors report "a significant and consistent decrease in the time between onset of symptoms and the time of diagnosis", attribute it to "a stronger awareness of the importance of early diagnosis", and acknowledge that censoring, channelling and immortal time bias "may have resulted in relatively more patients with milder disease being registered during the early years." Sensitivity analyses: biologic-treated subgroup, registered after 2005, and symptom onset within 2 or 5 years before DANBIO entry — "similar to those on the whole data set (not shown)". Delays over 240 months "were truncated at 240 months in order to reduce variance" (a cap, not statistical truncation handling). So the target paper itself names the selection problem and does not correct it; none of its sensitivity analyses addresses right truncation (restricting to recent onset makes it worse).

Different object (pre-diagnosis visits conditional on diagnosis). Not a prior.
