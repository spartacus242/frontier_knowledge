Question (canonical) — V2, the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation, scored as canonical: Under a forensic-style black-box design — specimens failed in the laboratory by mechanisms known by construction, a mechanism label set and an explicit inconclusive option fixed in advance, examiners working independently and blind both to the answer and to one another, and each specimen randomised across context arms declared in advance as either NON-DIAGNOSTIC (identity of the commissioning party; which side retained the examiner) or DIAGNOSTIC-BUT-MANIPULATED-IN-VERIDICALITY (a service-history narrative that is true, false, or withheld) — what are the false-attribution rate, the inconclusive rate and the inter-examiner reproducibility of expert fracture-mode and failure-mode determination in engineering failure analysis, and by how much does each context arm shift them relative to the no-context arm?

Question (V1, as submitted at intake; recorded per Gate 1): Under a forensic-style black-box design — specimens failed in the laboratory by a mechanism known by construction, examiners blinded to one another and to the answer, and task-irrelevant context (claimed service history, identity of the commissioning party) randomised between arms — what are the false-attribution rate, the inconclusive rate and the inter-examiner reproducibility of expert fracture-mode and failure-mode determination in engineering failure analysis, and does supplied context shift them?

Why the reformulation was needed (well-posedness only): V1 imports the phrase "task-irrelevant context" wholesale from forensic pattern comparison, where the case narrative is non-diagnostic by professional consensus. In failure analysis it is not: service history (loading, environment, temperature, duty cycle) is a legitimate and normally required input to mechanism determination, so an arm that varies it is not manipulating an irrelevance and a finding that it shifts conclusions would not be evidence of bias. V1 therefore smuggles in a contested presupposition (new P8 below) and would have been answerable in favour of "bias" no matter what the evidence showed. V2 splits the manipulandum into a genuinely non-diagnostic component (who retained the examiner) and a diagnostic component whose VERIDICALITY rather than relevance is varied (true / false / withheld history), which makes each arm's null interpretable. No other change; the estimands are the same three rates plus arm differences.

Motivating gap: Forensic pattern disciplines were pushed into measuring examiner error with black-box studies and now argue over how to analyse them. Engineering failure analysis performs a task of the same epistemic structure — an expert assigning a category from pattern features on a physical specimen — and its determinations decide insurance claims, recalls, airworthiness directives and litigation outcomes. The field's own reliability work measures DETECTION (probability of detection and false-call rate in non-destructive evaluation), not ATTRIBUTION. Confirmed from both sides at Gate 2: the method-owning field's current methodology paper enumerates the disciplines black-box studies have been run in and no engineering field appears among them (P3', full text); and the nearest engineering neighbour measures hit/miss POD and explicitly does not measure flaw-type or mechanism classification (P1', full text). What would count as an answer: A rate with a confidence interval in each cell of the design — false attribution, inconclusive, and a reproducibility statistic across examiners — together with the context effect expressed as a difference in rates between each context arm and the no-context arm, from a pre-registered protocol in which the specimen ground truth is adjudicated independently of the examiners and the inconclusive treatment is pre-committed. A pre-registered null (low false-attribution rate, no detectable context effect) is an equally complete answer and would be immediately usable by courts and by the bodies that accredit failure-analysis laboratories; the symmetry of usefulness between the positive and the null is a mark in the design's favour. A demonstration that single-mechanism ground truth cannot be constructed for the label set is a third admissible answer and would falsify P2 — it must be reported as a falsification of the design's presupposition, not as a finding about examiners, which is the discipline that keeps the third branch from functioning as rhetorical insurance.

Significance — consequence: If the false-attribution rate is material and context-sensitive, then every failure-analysis conclusion entering litigation, a recall decision or an airworthiness directive carries an error term that is currently unquantified and, worse, signed — a context effect biases rather than merely adds noise, so it cannot be dismissed as attenuation. The remedies would then be specifiable and cheap because another discipline has already built them: blind verification, linear sequential unmasking of instrumental evidence, and case-manager control of non-diagnostic context, none of which currently appear in failure-analysis practice or accreditation. If the answer is a null, the field acquires something it does not now have — a published rate with an interval, measured under a named design, that an expert can offer when asked what his discipline's error rate is. The null and the positive are both immediately usable, by different audiences, which is unusual and is the main argument for the design. A third outcome, that single-mechanism ground truth cannot be constructed across the label set, would say something sharp about the determinations themselves: that the categories are not cleanly instantiable even in the laboratory, which bears on whether they can carry the evidentiary weight placed on them.
