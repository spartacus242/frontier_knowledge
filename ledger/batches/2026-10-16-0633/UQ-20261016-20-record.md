Question (canonical, V1 — scored version, reformulated once at Gate 1 for well-posedness):
For a dated, versioned manufacturability rule set implemented separately per synthesis platform (oligo-array pool, column, enzymatic) and calibrated against at least two publicly queryable vendor feasibility interfaces, applied as a classifier to fixed-length windows tiling a named reference assembly (GRCh38 plus one non-human control genome):
(a) what fraction of bases is classified reject-or-reprice, per platform and per date;
(b) for each class in a pre-specified annotation set (protein-coding exons, promoters and CpG islands, structured-RNA genes, low-complexity sequence and each repeat family, enhancer catalogues), what is the log2 enrichment of excluded bases against a null of composition-matched shuffled windows, with CIs and pre-committed bands; and
(c) for a pre-specified set of published synthesis-based "genome-wide" screens, what share of each study's nominal target space falls in the excluded set, and how does that share compare with the dropout that study itself reports in its library-representation QC?
Pre-committed bands: MANUFACTURING BLIND SPOT (excluded >= 5% of bases AND >= 2-fold enrichment with CI excluding 1 in >= 2 functional annotation classes) / HONEST GENOME-WIDE (excluded fraction non-trivial but enrichment CI spans 1 in every functional class) / NEGLIGIBLE (excluded < 1% of bases on all platforms).

Alternative formulations:
- (V0, intake) What fraction of a reference genome's functional annotation classes falls in sequence space that current commercial DNA synthesis platforms reject or silently rewrite, and are the classes most often excluded (structured RNAs, CpG islands, high-GC promoters, low-complexity and repeat regions) thereby systematically absent from the synthesis-based libraries that functional screens describe as genome-wide?
- (lay) Which parts of the genome can we not even manufacture, and does that blind spot coincide with the parts we most want to test?
- (framing) Is "genome-wide" in a synthesis-based MPRA or library screen a claim about biology or about vendor manufacturability filters?

Gate 1 — what changed from V0 to V1, and why:
(i) "Silently rewrite" is demoted from a measured limb to declared out-of-scope. Rewriting is observable only by ordering and re-sequencing, so no classifier-plus-annotation study can settle it; and P5 itself concedes that for non-coding library members the rewriting limb most likely collapses into refusal. V1 scores "reject-or-reprice" and states the rewriting question as a separate, vendor-cooperation-dependent study.
(ii) Platform index and date stamp move into the estimand, because P2 is right that the property is per-platform and perishable.
(iii) A null (composition-matched shuffled windows) and pre-committed bands are added, so that "excluded but not enriched" is a reportable answer rather than a failed study.
(iv) A non-human control genome is added so annotation-class enrichment is not confounded with GRCh38's own repeat-representation problems (P6).

Motivating gap: SILENCE MAPPED. The how-literature on difficult DNA is dense and almost entirely commercial: vendors publicly score, reject, reprice and "optimise" sequences. The on-point own-field query (Q1 below) returned MPRA statistical-method papers and two vendor pages, one of them specifically about MPRA oligo synthesis — i.e. the nearest hit in the candidate's own field and own words is marketing, which reproduces the claimed silence rather than refuting it. The proposed mechanism is that the filter acts at ordering, before any data exist, so the exclusion appears in no screen's methods, results or negative findings, and a manufacturing boundary is read as biological silence. Gate 2 PARTIALLY FALSIFIED the gap: the classifier half has been built at least three times (Synthesis Success Calculator 2020; SCP4ssd 2023; DNA Scanner as a cross-vendor feasibility web app), and SCP4ssd already ran a difficulty classifier over 4,318 sequences drawn from the E. coli MG1655 genome and reported 13% difficult (3,777 easy / 541 difficult). What remains unlocated is the annotation-class enrichment against a composition-matched null, the per-platform dated framing, and the screen-level audit.

Presuppositions:
- P1 (CONTESTED, load-bearing): vendor manufacturability rules are specific enough to implement as a classifier over genomic windows. Gate 2 finding: PARTLY RELIEVED — published difficulty classifiers exist and DNA Scanner exists as a cross-vendor feasibility interface, so a rule set is obtainable without reconstruction alone. Residual contest: those classifiers are trained on gene-synthesis turnaround outcomes from provider order histories (SCP4ssd reuses 1,076 labelled sequences from the Synthesis Success Calculator), which is not the same decision surface as an oligo-pool order's accept/reject. V1 mitigates by reporting the spread across >= 2 interfaces plus one reconstructed rule set as part of the result.
- P2 (CONTESTED): "rejected or rewritten" is a property of sequence rather than of order length, platform and price tier. Accepted as contested; V1 indexes the estimand to platform and date rather than defusing it.
- P3 (CONTESTED, weakest limb): published genome-wide screens dropped these regions rather than recovering them from native DNA by PCR, capture or cloning. Untested in Gate 2 and materially uncertain — genomic-fragment STARR-seq and capture-based libraries are not synthesis-limited at all, so limb (c) must first classify each study by library provenance and will exclude a large share of nominally genome-wide screens; the unclassifiable share must be reported.
- P4 (ESTABLISHED): annotation classes are defined independently of synthesisability, so a GC/repeat-to-annotation correlation is the finding, not a confounder.
- P5 (CONTESTED; now out of scope): "silently rewrite" happens often enough to matter and is detectable. Nothing found in Gate 2 supports detectability from outside the vendor; demoted at Gate 1.
- P6 (CONTESTED): reference-genome coordinates are the right frame. Partly mitigated by the control genome and by reporting repeat families separately; overlap between the synthesis-excluded set and the reference's own representation problems remains a real double-counting risk, and must be checked against the dark/camouflaged-genome literature.
- NOT presupposed: that the excluded set is enriched in functional classes. HONEST GENOME-WIDE is a real outcome.

What would count as an answer: Two measurable outputs, dated and versioned. (i) The per-platform classifier applied genome-wide, giving the excluded base fraction and its log2 enrichment or depletion per annotation class against composition-matched shuffles with CIs, scored by the pre-committed bands — so that "excluded but not enriched in functional classes" is a real negative answer meaning "genome-wide" is honest. (ii) For the pre-specified screen set, restricted to studies with de novo synthesised libraries, the measured share of nominal target space in the excluded set compared against that study's own reported representation dropout; agreement validates the classifier, and a gap in either direction localises whether the loss happens at ordering or at assembly. Rule-set sensitivity is a reported quantity, not a caveat.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
vs P1 (SCP4ssd, 2023) — the closest prior, and it executes a weak form of limb (a). Differences: (1) its unit is a gene-length sequence from a bacterial genome, not tiled windows of a reference assembly, and E. coli carries almost none of the annotation classes the candidate is about (no mammalian-style CpG-island promoters, negligible mammalian repeat content, no enhancer catalogue); (2) its label is gene-synthesis turnaround success inherited from one 2020 order-history dataset, not a per-platform accept/reject surface, so it cannot be stratified by platform and carries no date semantics; (3) no annotation-class breakdown and no null, so it cannot distinguish "13% difficult" from "13% difficult and that 13% is

vs P2 (Riolo and Steckl, 2022) — shares the candidate's framing move exactly: importing manufacturing-engineering benchmarks to ask whether genomes are manufacturable. This weakens any claim that the lens is absent from biology. But the resolution is wrong by several orders of magnitude: P2 compares whole-genome sizes and synthesis throughput against software and chip benchmarks, and asks whether a genome is manufacturable at all; the candidate asks which windows inside one genome are not, and whether those windows are where the annotation is. P2 has no classifier, no windows, no annotation, no null, no platform stratification and no screens.
vs P3 (dark/camouflaged genes, attribution unverified) — the same question structure (technology-excluded territory, censused against annotation, enrichment in classes people care about) at the sequencing boundary rather than the synthesis boundary. Three real differences: (a) sequencing-side exclusion leaves a residual in the data (reads exist and fail to map), so it is detectable post hoc from any deep dataset, whereas synthesis-side exclusion leaves nothing because the molecule was never ordered; (b) the sequencing boundary is characterised by public read-level evidence, the synthesis boundary by proprietary order-validation rules, which is why P1 of this candidate is contested and P3's analogue was not; (c) P3 has no second limb auditing published studies' claimed coverage. The risk P3 poses is not duplication but double-counting: the two excluded sets probably overlap heavily in repeat and segmental-duplication territory, and limb (b) must report the intersection.

Significance — consequence: If MANUFACTURING BLIND SPOT: "genome-wide" in the synthesis-based assay literature is partly a statement about vendor manufacturability, and every pooled reanalysis of MPRA-class data inherits a composition-correlated missingness that is currently modelled, if at all, as missing-at-random. Three concrete changes follow — a reported synthesis-accessible fraction alongside nominal coverage; reinterpretation of null results in high-GC promoter and structured-RNA territory as partly non-results rather than negative results; and a defined target list for alternative chemistries. If HONEST GENOME-WIDE (excluded but not enriched in functional classes): that is a genuinely informative negative, because it licenses the coverage claim, retires a plausible systematic worry, and reassigns observed library dropout to assembly, cloning or sequencing rather than ordering — which is actionable in the opposite direction, since those steps are inside the experimenter's control. If NEGLIGIBLE: the question dissolves for current platforms and becomes a historical measurement, with the date stamp as its only lasting content.
Significance — audience: Four groups, with the mechanism by which each would act differently. (1) MPRA / STARR-seq / saturation-mutagenesis library designers: they would pre-screen designs against the classifier and report the excluded share, instead of discovering it as an unexplained representation skew after sequencing. (2) The consortium QA layer that already exists: data.igvf.org publishes an MPRA quality-metric profile, so there is a live, named standards surface that could absorb a synthesis-accessibility field — this is the most concrete evidence that the audience is real rather than rhetorical. (3) Groups training sequence-to-function models on pooled reporter data, who would have to treat the training distribution as censored by a known, computable rule rather than as a random sample of regulatory space — this changes model evaluation, not just data description. (4) Enzymatic / alternative-chemistry synthesis vendors, whose central public claim is that they move this boundary: a dated census is the benchmark their claim is currently assessed against only anecdotally, and it would let a capability claim be scored rather than asserted. Honest discount: group (1) largely knows that GC-rich and repetitive designs fail, and routinely routes around them, so for them the answer would change reporting and meta-analysis more than bench practice.
Significance — fecundity: Four questions become askable. (i) Do sequence-to-function models degrade specifically on the synthesis-excluded complement — a held-out set that no synthesis-derived training corpus can cover by construction, and therefore a cleaner generalisation test than any random split? (ii) Does the synthesis-excluded set coincide with the sequencing-side dark genome (a compounded blind spot where neither reading nor writing reaches) or is it complementary (so the two technologies cover for each other)? That is a well-defined set intersection and nobody has computed it. (iii) Does the boundary's documented movement across vendor generations show up as a datable expansion in what reporter-assay papers claim to cover — i.e. is the literature's notion of "genome-wide" a lagging indicator of synthesis chemistry? (iv) Can a curated benchmark of synthesis-hard functional elements serve as the standing target set against which new chemistries are scored?
Significance — anchoring: The dark/camouflaged-genome census (same question at the reading boundary, already executed, and the obvious methodological template); the GC- and length-dependent oligo representation bias quantified in DNA data storage (bioRxiv 566554); the constrained-coding literature, which formalises precisely these constraints and computes capacity but never asks what share of natural sequence violates them; Riolo and Steckl's manufacturability-benchmark framing; the inverse-direction Sc2.0 result that synthesis can be used to demonstrate annotation completeness, which shows the synthesis/annotation link is already drawn — but only in the direction where synthesis tests annotation, never where synthesisability filters it.

Reason for silence: A disciplinary-and-incentive boundary with a specific structural feature — censoring at the design stage. The mechanism has three parts, and the third is the one that does real work. (1) Ownership: the constraint set lives in vendor order-validation code and, in formal form, in coding theory, where it is studied over designed codewords; neither holder has reason to compute its footprint on a reference genome. (2) Incentive: the party holding the rules is the party whose capability claim the census would bound, so publishing it is a commercial liability — the same pattern as UQ-20261015-20's reagent vendors. (3) NO RESIDUAL IN THE DATA: unlike the sequencing-side analogue, where excluded territory announces itself as reads that fail to map and so produced a visible artefact that prompted a census, a sequence that was never ordered leaves nothing behind. The designer removes it before submission, the methods section describes what was built, and the absence is invisible to every downstream reader. A missingness that generates no artefact generates no question. Honest weakening, which Gate 4 should press: this is not a "could not have been asked" mechanism. The classifier half has been built three times (2020, 2023, plus DNA Scanner), and SCP4ssd already pointed one at a genome. So the silence is about the join being unglamorous and the data being someone else's asset, not about the question being unavailable — closer to "nobody's job" than to an instrument or conceptual barrier, which is a weaker mechanism than the intake claims.
