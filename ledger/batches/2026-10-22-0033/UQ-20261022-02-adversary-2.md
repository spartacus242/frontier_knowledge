## Restatement in my own words

Take some enzymes that you can actually count the working molecules of (because somebody has already built a stoichiometric titrant or a single-turnover burst assay for them), pick the enzymes and the purification recipes and the counting rules before you touch a pipette, and then ask two things.

(a) Of the protein you weigh out, what share is actually catalytically competent — and is that share a property of the purification recipe (so it repeats across preparations made the same way) or just luck of the batch? Report it as a distribution with per-enzyme and per-route variance, with the total-protein number cross-checked on a second, unrelated quantitation method.

(b) Published turnover numbers for one and the same enzyme disagree between papers. If the active share really varies as much as (a) says it does, how much of that disagreement could it possibly account for — the ceiling, not the actual split — assuming today's preparations are interchangeable with the long-gone preparations behind the old papers, with whatever is left over blamed on assay conditions once you have adjusted for the temperature, pH, buffer, salt and substrate each paper reported?

In short: biochemistry divides a rate by "how much enzyme" when it means "how much working enzyme", and nobody has measured the gap across a declared panel or priced what the gap could be worth in the literature's own scatter. Limb (a) is a wet measurement; limb (b) is a bookkeeping ceiling that leans on an assumption no experiment in the design can test.

## Single strongest killing point

Limb (b)'s denominator does not exist in print and the record quietly substitutes the wrong one. The candidate defines limb (b) on the **between-report** log10 kcat variance for the same enzyme, but the record's "printed bound on the total spread (log10 SD 1.4) that limb (b)'s budget would have to live inside" is Prior 2's spread **across roughly 130 different E. coli enzymes** — a quantity spanning six orders of magnitude, which no single enzyme's inter-laboratory disagreement does. That is scope transfer inside the novelty record itself. If the three other legs of this point fell, this is the leg the objection stands on, and it rests on the record's own printed characterisation of Prior 2 plus the arithmetic impossibility of a six-order within-enzyme spread (READ LEVEL: I did not open Prior 2 myself). The supporting legs, each sourced separately: SOURCE A prints no within-enzyme between-study variance and only its own predictor r.m.s.e. of 1.06 log10 (Fig. 2a); SOURCE B prints only agreement statistics (R² 0.28 / 0.41 / 0.12); SOURCE D varies 13 laboratories under one fixed protocol and prints confidence intervals with no variance components. So limb (b) would have to manufacture its own denominator before it could bound anything — which does not kill the question, but does confirm that the question the ledger is admitting is really limb (a) with limb (b) as a conditional appendix.
