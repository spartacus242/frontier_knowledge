## Restatement (my own words)

When you chop up a tissue and run it through a droplet machine, some cells die on the way and never get counted, and their RNA ends up floating in the buffer, so it gets packaged into every droplet as background ("soup"). The field treats that background as dirt to be subtracted. The candidate asks whether the dirt is instead a receipt for what was destroyed: if you take the RNA from the empty droplets, break it down against a signature matrix declared in advance, then divide by how much RNA a cell of each type carries and by how badly each type's RNA has degraded, do you get a per-type count of missing cells that actually tracks the shortfall you see when the same tissue block is counted a second way (in situ or snRNA-seq)? Concretely (V2): is the calibration slope of soup-derived missing counts on the orthogonal count deficit distinguishable both from 0 (soup is uninformative) and from 1 (soup is a complete receipt), with an interval? The record retains V1 — "is the soup a quantitative record of the cell types destroyed" — as a canonical formulation alongside V2.

### Fetches (4/4)

- F1 `https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.13.699237.full.pdf` — ambient-decontamination benchmark. Gene-level only. Ambient load per feature "strongly and positively correlated (average R = 0.857) with the average expression level of the same feature in 'empty' droplets"; concludes "ubiquitously expressed mature cytoplasmic messenger RNAs is the major source of ambient RNA" and that results suggest "ambient RNA load is a stochastic sampling process". No cell-type decomposition of the ambient profile, no composition/proportion correction, no external (snRNA/in situ/spatial) composition reference, no lost-cell estimation.
- F2 `https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13059-023-02978-x` — **prior P1 spot-check: real and quoted accurately.** Janssen P, Kliesmete Z, Vieth B, et al., *Genome Biology* 24:140 (2023). Confirms the record's quotes "the contamination profiles do not always reflect the cell type proportions of the sample" and "a clear shift in cell type composition with a decreased PT fraction". Confirms the record's defence of the limb: SCDC reference was the run's **own** cells — Methods: "the endogenous single cell allele counts from the respective replicate were used as reference (qcthreshold = 0.6)" — no external signature matrix, no mass-to-count conversion, no per-type RNA content, no degradation model, no orthogonal in-situ calibration. **But also a fact the record does not report:** P1 draws the dissociation-destruction inference itself, concluding PT cells may be "more vulnerable to dissociation procedure than other cell types" from the ambient composition shift.
- F3 `https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41467-018-07466-6` — Moss, Magenheim, Neiman et al., *Nat Commun* 9:5068 (2018). Deconvolves plasma cfDNA against "a reference methylation atlas of 25 human tissues and cell types"; converts fractions to absolute genome equivalents per ml assuming "the mass of a haploid genome is 3.3 pg"; and explicitly warns the signal "reflects the sum of multiple parameters: total cell number in these organs, the degree of baseline turnover", with absent signals "likely reflect[ing] alternative clearance routes".
