## Question (canonical) — R1, adopted at Gate 1

Scope declared up front: platforms whose parameter tables are openly published (PK-Sim / Open Systems Pharmacology; openly published system-parameter compilations and the EMA/FDA submission annexes that cite them). Proprietary libraries are out of scope and the resulting coverage gap is itself reportable.

For a pre-specified list of PBPK *system* parameters — microsomal protein per gram of liver, cytosolic protein per gram of liver, hepatocellularity per gram of liver, hepatic and renal blood-flow fractions of cardiac output, organ volumes, and the tissue-composition inputs (neutral lipid, phospholipid, water, acidic phospholipid, albumin/lipoprotein fractions) that feed Rodgers–Rowland / Poulin–Theriault tissue:plasma partition prediction:

(a) **Provenance graph.** Under a pre-registered operational rule for "mutually independent primary human measurement" — distinct subject sample AND distinct assay AND distinct laboratory, with a stated adjudication procedure for borderline cases (re-analysis of a prior cohort, multi-site series, review-of-a-review citation chains) — how many independent primary human sources, and what pooled subject N, year range, and demographic composition (age strata, sex, ancestry where recorded, procurement route: autopsy vs surgical resection vs perfused transplant-reject tissue), underlie each default value and each default uncertainty distribution; and what is the *shared-source incidence matrix* across the parameter list (which parameter pairs descend wholly or partly from the same subject series)?

(b) **Decision robustness, under a stated inference rule.** For at least five published PBPK analyses that bore on a regulatory decision and are reproducible in an open platform (OSP qualification-framework models and published case studies), Monte Carlo propagation under (i) vendor/default uncertainty, treating system parameters as independent, and (ii) provenance-corrected uncertainty — where the correction is defined in advance as: replacing each parameter's sampling-uncertainty-on-the-mean with the standard error implied by its *effective* independent-source count and pooled N rather than its nominal N, and imposing the shared-source correlation from (a) — does the decision metric cross its pre-specified boundary? Boundaries fixed in advance: a predicted victim-drug AUC ratio leaving the 80–125% no-interaction zone or crossing the ICH M12 no-effect boundary used in the original analysis; a paediatric predicted-exposure point estimate or interval leaving the adult exposure-matching band the original analysis relied on.

Pre-committed verdicts, all three informative: **provenance thin AND at least one decision flips**; **thin but all five decisions robust** (a defence of current practice, not a null); **provenance adequate as documented**.

## Alternative formulations

1. (Lay) How many people's bodies are actually inside the physiology defaults of the PBPK models used to justify doses and interaction labelling without running a trial — and if that number is small and the same bodies recur across several parameters, does the justification still hold?
2. (Technical) Are PBPK system-parameter uncertainties traceable to mutually independent primary data, or do they share so few primary subject series that propagated confidence intervals computed under independence are too narrow to support the decisions built on them?
3. (Neighbouring-field framing, metrology) Treating a PBPK platform as a measurement model in the GUM sense, what are the covariances between its physiological input quantities induced by shared primary sources, and what is the effect on the combined standard uncertainty of its decision-bearing output?

## Motivating gap

The settled claim being inverted: *PBPK model uncertainty is dominated by drug-specific parameters, because system parameters are "known physiology."* That claim licenses a regulatory practice — FDA and EMA accept PBPK analyses in place of clinical drug-interaction and paediatric studies (FDA, "Physiologically Based Pharmacokinetic Analyses — Format and Content," 2018; EMA PBPK reporting guideline, 2018, whose Annex 6 is specifically "additional supporting documentation: system parameters / compound files"). Uncertainty and sensitivity analysis for PBPK is an established subfield, and single parameters have been meta-analysed (Barter et al. 2007 for MPPGL/HPGL; Leeder et al. 2022 for paediatric MPPGL). What Gate 2 did not locate is any provenance audit of the parameter *set* — independent-source counts, effective subject N, demographic composition, and the correlation induced when several parameters descend from the same autopsy or surgical series — nor any re-run of decision-bearing analyses under such a corrected uncertainty structure. The field discusses parameter uncertainty while treating the defaults' pedigree as settled, and the pedigree-analysis machinery that would do this (NUSAP in environmental modelling; GUM correlation treatment in metrology) sits in fields PBPK does not borrow from on this point.

Gate 2 also found that the *correlation* element is explicitly named and deferred in a current EU regulatory-science deliverable (CONFIRMS, Nov 2025 — see prior P2), which makes that sub-limb asked-and-open rather than un-asked, and that the decision limb is being approached from a different direction entirely (empirical observed-vs-predicted calibration — prior P1). Both findings are recorded against the candidate, not around it.

## Presuppositions

1. Platform defaults are documented well enough to trace to primary sources. **CONTESTED, and handled by scope restriction:** Simcyp and some commercial libraries are partly proprietary. R1 declares the answerable scope as PK-Sim/OSP plus openly published parameter tables and submission annexes, and treats the proprietary coverage gap as a reportable result rather than an obstacle.
2. "Mutually independent primary measurement" can be defined operationally. **Established as feasible by precedent:** Leeder et al. 2022 stratified MPPGL by tissue source and procurement route and estimated a source effect, so source adjudication is demonstrably doable; R1 pre-registers the rule.
3. Published regulatory PBPK analyses are reproducible outside the sponsor. **CONTESTED:** most submission models are not public. Mitigation is the OSP qualification-framework models and published case studies, which narrows the five case studies to a convenience set — a real external-validity limit, flagged not dissolved.
4. Current workflows propagate system-parameter uncertainty as if independent. **CONTESTED and now partly resolved against the naive version:** some population modules already encode covariances (correlated organ volumes, allometric and demographic covariate structures), and CONFIRMS treats parameter correlation as a known limitation of standard sensitivity analysis. The audit must therefore establish what each platform actually does. Note the distinction the audit must keep: existing covariance structures encode *between-subject physiological covariation*, which is not the same object as *shared-primary-source epistemic correlation*. Nothing found suggests the latter is encoded anywhere.
5. Regulatory conclusions are sensitive to system-parameter uncertainty at all. **UNKNOWN — this is limb (b), not an assumption.** A robust-decisions finding is a pre-committed legitimate answer. Partial evidence exists in the opposite direction (local/global sensitivity analyses that rank drug-specific parameters above system parameters), which is why a "thin but robust" verdict is live.
6. Provenance thinness, if found, is a defect rather than appropriate parsimony. **CONTESTED:** a parameter measured once but tightly constrained by physiology (mass balance, conservation of cardiac output) may need no replication. R1 therefore reports decision impact, not source counts alone, and limb (a) is deliberately descriptive.
7. (Added at Gate 1) A source count can be mapped to a corrected uncertainty distribution. **CONTESTED and the question's weakest joint.** R1 pre-commits to one mapping (effective-source standard error on the mean + shared-source correlation; population variability untouched). Any such mapping is a modelling choice, so limb (b)'s result is conditional on it, and a sensitivity analysis over the mapping is part of the deliverable.

## What would count as an answer

Two deliverables with pre-committed verdicts.

1. **Provenance graph:** per parameter, independent primary human source count under the registered rule, pooled subject N, year range, demographic composition, procurement route, and the shared-source incidence/correlation matrix across the parameter set. Machine-readable, with every citation chain traced to the primary report (or marked untraceable, which is itself a result).
2. **Decision propagation:** for five reproducible decision-bearing analyses, Monte Carlo under default-independent vs provenance-corrected-correlated uncertainty, reporting for each whether the decision metric crosses its pre-specified boundary, plus the sensitivity of that answer to the provenance-to-distribution mapping.

Verdicts: provenance thin AND ≥1 decision flips / thin but all robust / provenance adequate as documented. A fourth outcome must be declarable in advance: **not traceable** — if a pre-specified majority of parameters cannot be traced to primary sources within the open scope, the reportable answer is the bounded traceability census plus the shared-source matrix for the traceable subset, with limb (b) declared not estimable.

## Novelty — how this differs from each

**vs P1 (Bogdanovich 2026).** P1 is the most serious challenge, because it reaches the candidate's limb (b) by a route that may make limb (a) unnecessary. It measures *total* platform imprecision empirically, observed-vs-predicted, and converts it directly into decision boundaries (acceptable predicted GMR limits). Any error contributed by thin or shared-source system parameters is already inside that 18–31% CV, in aggregate. The candidate differs in three ways, and only the third is a strong difference. (i) Direction: P1 is top-down and anonymous about causes; the candidate is bottom-up and attributive, asking *which* inputs and *which* source structure produce the imprecision — needed to know what to measure next, which P1 cannot say. (ii) Object: P1 says nothing about source counts, subject N, demographics, or cross-parameter correlation, and nothing about the provenance of defaults. (iii) **Decisive: scope of applicability.** P1's method requires a corpus of clinical observations of the very quantity being predicted. That exists for adult CYP DDI. It does not exist for the extrapolations where PBPK actually substitutes for a trial — first-in-paediatric exposure matching, organ impairment, rare-genotype and pregnancy scenarios — because there, the clinical study the model replaces is the one that was not done. In exactly that regime the empirical calibration route is unavailable and parameter provenance is the only handle. The candidate is therefore distinct and non-redundant *in the paediatric and organ-impairment limb*, and largely redundant with P1 in the adult-DDI limb. This is a reason to narrow limb (b) toward exposure-matching case studies, and Gate 4 should press it.

**vs P2 (CONFIRMS Deliverable 3, Nov 2025).** P2 asks a methods-performance question — how well do UQ methods behave on complex models — and explicitly flags parameter correlation as a known neglected element, deferring even a two-parameter correlated case to "if time allows." The candidate differs in asking an *empirical question about the actual parameter set* rather than about estimator performance: where the correlation comes from (shared primary subject series), how large the shared-source structure actually is across a named parameter list, and what it does to real published decision-bearing analyses rather than to a hypothetical drug. P2 also treats correlation as a generic statistical property; the candidate identifies a specific, auditable physical cause. **Honest consequence: because P2 names and defers the correlation element in a dated regulatory-science deliverable, the correlated-propagation sub-limb is asked-and-open, not un-asked.** It is the provenance census and the shared-source causal structure that P2 does not pose.

**vs P3 (Leeder 2022).** P3 is the candidate's method, executed for one parameter, and it demonstrates both feasibility and that the finding can be non-null: effective paediatric N for MPPGL was one sample in the original meta-analysis, and procurement route (autopsy vs perfused) measurably shifts the value. It even gestures at the cross-parameter issue by noting that the same liver samples drive source-dependent effects on CYP3A4 activity and cytosolic naltrexol formation — i.e. the shared-source correlation the candidate wants to quantify, observed in passing and not quantified. The candidate differs in three respects: parameter *set* rather than one parameter; explicit cross-parameter shared-source incidence matrix rather than a within-parameter source covariate; and propagation to named regulatory decision boundaries rather than to a refitted age equation. P3 is strong evidence that limb (a) is tractable and worth doing, and simultaneously the strongest argument that the field would regard it as normal-science extension rather than a new question — the generalisation from one parameter to a set is a labour increase, not obviously a conceptual one. That tension is the main thing for Gate 4 to adjudicate.

## Significance — consequence

**If provenance is thin and at least one decision flips:** the warrant for a specific, named class of trial waivers is weakened in a way regulators can act on without new clinical data — by requiring provenance documentation in submissions (the EMA guideline's Annex 6 already defines the slot where it would go), by widening default system-parameter uncertainty for extrapolations outside the clinically anchored regime, or by targeting a small number of replication measurements where the shared-source matrix shows concentrated leverage. The last is the cheapest and most actionable output: a ranked list of which one or two primary measurements, if independently replicated, would most narrow decision-relevant uncertainty.

**If provenance is thin but all five decisions are robust:** a positive, publishable defence of current practice, and a stronger one than currently exists, because today's defence is an untested presupposition rather than a demonstration. It would also justify *not* spending on replication — a real saving, since autopsy and surgical liver series are expensive and ethically constrained. This is a live outcome: sensitivity analyses consistently rank drug-specific parameters above system parameters, and P1's empirically bounded total imprecision caps how much limb (b) can move the adult-DDI cases.

**If provenance is adequate as documented:** the presupposition is vindicated and the audit becomes a reusable traceability artefact (a machine-readable provenance graph for the open-platform defaults) that sponsors and reviewers can cite instead of re-deriving.

**If untraceable:** arguably the most consequential outcome, and one no current framework reports. A finding that a pre-specified majority of open-platform system parameters cannot be traced to a primary human measurement within the open literature is a direct, checkable claim about the evidentiary base of model-informed drug development, and it is the kind of claim that moves documentation requirements rather than model structure.

## Significance — audience

Specific and identifiable, with the action each would take:

- **FDA Office of Clinical Pharmacology and EMA's Modelling and Simulation Working Party / PBPK assessors.** They already require a system-parameter annex (EMA PBPK guideline Annex 6) and have no criterion for what makes its contents adequate. A provenance graph supplies one. They would act by amending reporting expectations — the cheapest possible regulatory response, needing no change to any platform.
- **The Open Systems Pharmacology consortium.** They own an open, versioned physiology database and a public qualification framework. They are the only party able to attach provenance metadata to defaults at source, and the only one for whom this is a routine repository change rather than a disclosure.
- **Paediatric and organ-impairment extrapolation groups** (the EU and US paediatric-PBPK networks, and sponsors seeking paediatric study waivers). This is the regime where P1's empirical calibration is structurally unavailable, so it is where a provenance finding would bear directly on whether a specific waiver is defensible.
- **ICH M15 / model-credibility framework drafters, and the ASME V&V 40-derived credibility community.** Credibility frameworks currently score verification, validation and applicability. Parameter pedigree is not a scored dimension. A worked demonstration that pedigree moves (or does not move) a decision metric is exactly the evidence a framework needs to add or decline to add a dimension.
- **Certara and Simulations Plus (adversarially).** They hold the proprietary libraries this study cannot reach, and P1 shows they are already investing in the empirical-calibration route. A credible open-platform provenance result creates pressure to answer in kind, which is how the proprietary coverage gap would plausibly close.

Honest deflation: the regulatory audiences would act on documentation, not on model structure, and documentation changes are low-cost and low-impact. The strong action — widening default uncertainties for unanchored extrapolations — requires limb (b) to flip a decision, which is uncertain, and in the adult-DDI regime is bounded above by P1's measured total imprecision.

## Significance — fecundity

Questions that become askable only after an answer: (1) **Optimal replication design** — given a shared-source matrix, which single additional primary measurement most reduces decision-relevant uncertainty per unit cost? This is a value-of-information question that cannot even be posed without limb (a)'s correlation structure. (2) **Transfer of the audit to the other model classes that substitute for trials** — QSP models, in-silico trials, physiologically based biopharmaceutics models, ICRP biokinetic models, and the digital-twin literature all rest on reference-physiology defaults with the same unaudited pedigree; a demonstrated protocol makes those audits routine. (3) **Demographic reach of the defaults** — if the provenance graph records ancestry and age composition, it becomes answerable whether the "virtual populations" used for global regulatory decisions descend from subject series that represent the populations being dosed, which is the representativeness critique made precise and quantitative rather than rhetorical. (4) **Reconciliation of the two routes** — does bottom-up provenance-propagated uncertainty predict P1's top-down measured imprecision, and if not, what accounts for the residual? That is a sharp, falsifiable comparison, and it is unaskable until both halves exist. (5) **Whether pedigree belongs in credibility scoring at all** — answerable empirically, once pedigree and decision impact have been measured on the same cases.

## Reason for silence

Four mechanisms, compounding; none is "no one thought of it."

1. **Disciplinary boundary, in a specific and checkable form.** The machinery for this question lives in two fields PBPK does not borrow from on this point. Provenance/pedigree analysis of model parameters is mature in environmental modelling (NUSAP; query 5), and correlated-input uncertainty propagation is standard in metrology (GUM, IMEKO; query 9). PBPK's uncertainty subfield imported *sensitivity* analysis from the same environmental-modelling tradition and left pedigree analysis behind. The one pharmacometric artefact that uses the word "pedigree" (Novartis xGx, opened) is a qualitative checklist with no source counting — evidence that the concept arrived without its quantitative content.

2. **Incentive gap with a wrong-pocket structure.** The cost of the audit falls on platform vendors (disclosure of library derivations) and on whoever does the archival labour; the benefit accrues to regulators and to sponsors in a regime (paediatric, organ impairment) where no commercial actor is seeking a competitive edge. Tracing a 1970s autopsy series through decades of review-citing-review is unrewarded work: not a new result, not a new method, publishable at best as a database paper.

3. **A substitute route that is better rewarded and crowds this one out.** Qualification frameworks grade platforms on demonstrated predictive performance against observed clinical data, not on parameter pedigree. P1 is exactly that route, executed well, by the vendor, in support of a 2025 EMA qualification. For the clinically anchored regime it genuinely is the better instrument — which both explains the silence and limits the candidate's scope. The silence is therefore rational rather than negligent in adult DDI, and unexplained in the unanchored extrapolations where the substitute does not work. That asymmetry is the sharpest form of the reason-for-silence claim, and the most vulnerable point if an adversary can show the paediatric community has in fact posed it.

4. **The settled-claim mechanism proper.** "System parameters are known physiology" is doing load-bearing work as a *background assumption* rather than as a claim under test. Background assumptions are not searched for; they are what makes other searches possible. The sensitivity-analysis results that rank drug-specific parameters first are read as confirming it, when they only show that *within assumed uncertainty ranges* system parameters matter less — the ranges themselves being the unexamined object.

Scores: Well-posedness 2/3 · Significance 2/3

Well-posedness 2/3 — R1 fixes scope, the independence rule, the case-study count and the decision boundaries, and adds a fourth declarable outcome. It loses a point on presupposition 7: the mapping from source structure to a corrected uncertainty distribution is a modelling choice, not a measurement, so limb (b)'s verdict is conditional on a rule the investigator chooses. The uncertainty/variability conflation this invites is a genuine trap (a thin source base widens the confidence interval on a mean, not the between-subject spread that drives a population simulation's percentiles), and while R1 pre-commits to one mapping and a sensitivity analysis over it, that is mitigation rather than resolution. Secondary deduction: the five case studies are a convenience set constrained by what is reproducible outside sponsors (presupposition 3), so limb (b)'s external validity is bounded by availability.

Significance 2/3 — real, named audiences with concrete actions; limb (a) is independently valuable and limb (b) non-redundant in exactly the regime where PBPK most substitutes for trials. Not 3/3 because P1 shows the decision limb is already being answered by a better-instrumented route wherever clinical anchoring exists, which confines the candidate's unique contribution to the unanchored extrapolations and caps the plausible size of a decision flip; and because the most likely regulatory response is a documentation requirement, which is cheap to adopt and modest in effect.

Strongest remaining objection (for Gate 4 to press): P3 executed this candidate's limb (a) for one parameter and treated the generalisation as a matter of more labour, not a new question; P1 reaches limb (b)'s payoff — decision boundaries — without any provenance work at all. An adversary should ask whether what remains is a database-building exercise with a pre-announced conditional result rather than a question. The defence, which must be evidenced and not asserted, is the paediatric/organ-impairment asymmetry: P1's instrument structurally cannot operate where the clinical comparator was never collected, and that is precisely where the waivers are granted.
