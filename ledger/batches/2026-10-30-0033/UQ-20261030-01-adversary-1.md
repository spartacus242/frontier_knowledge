# UQ-20261030-01 — Gate 4, adversary #1

## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Take two or more ordinary mammalian cell lines that divide into two apparently equivalent daughters. Watch at least 150 individual divisions per line, with three kinds of cellular junk tagged at once in the SAME cell: worn-out/depolarised mitochondria, clumps of misfolded protein in the cytosol, and lysosomes carrying autofluorescent lipofuscin. In each division, decide which daughter is "daughter 1" by one rule that has nothing to do with the junk (the one that gets the older mother centriole), and for each junk type compute how lopsidedly it was split, as a signed fraction corrected for the daughters' volumes. Question: within a single division, do the three lopsidednesses move together (all pairwise rank correlations positive — one junk-sorting axis), look like nothing (indistinguishable from a null built by shuffling which junk type is which across divisions — each junk type sorted by its own machinery), or move oppositely (negative — the cell deliberately sends different junk to different daughters)?

In short: when a mammalian cell dumps damage on one daughter, is it one dumping decision or three independent ones?
