# VERIFY - ASTM D6300 current edition: is the re-study trigger design-based or outcome-based?
Checker: fresh context. Budget used: 6 WebFetch (3 of which retrieved nothing), 2 WebSearch. Date 2026-10-22.

## CLAIM

UQ-20261022-12 asks whether the CURRENT edition of ASTM D6300 contains a clause comparing
newly determined precision with previously published precision, and so whether a precision
re-study is triggered by DESIGN criteria (minimum laboratories p, replicates n, degrees of
freedom) or by an OUTCOME comparison (achieved precision measured against published precision).
The entry records that only the 2003/04 text was read, and that in that edition there is no
such comparison clause. The entry's stated stake: if the trigger is outcome-based, the
candidate's headline distribution is a selection artefact.

Sources as the entry carries them: "ASTM D6300, current edition (-20a, -23a or later), via the
ASTM store page, any open summary, a national adoption (IP 367 / EN ISO 4259 family), or an
openly posted copy," plus the ASTM work items WK89836 (D5442) and WK67628.

## WHAT THE SOURCE SAYS (quotations as served)

### (a) Read level

Stated above: **store page / scope only** for D6300-23a; full Rationale text for the two work
items. No full text of any current D6300 edition was obtained.

### (b) Is there a clause comparing new precision with previously published precision?

**COULD NOT CHECK on the current edition.** The edition I reached is **ASTM D6300-23a**, and I
reached only its Scope and Significance and Use. Within those two sections the answer is that
no such clause appears, and I can say that at the word level: the summariser reported that
across the whole of section 1 and section 5 as served, none of the strings "previously
published", "published precision", "minimum", "laboratories", "degrees of freedom",
"re-study", "restudy" or "compare" occurs. ("interlaboratory" occurs in 1.1 and is a different
string.)

But that is a fact about two sections, not about the standard. The scope of D6300-23a as served
is purely a programme-planning scope - reconstruction from 13 consecutive segments:

> "This practice covers the necessary preparations and planning for the conduct of
> interlaboratory programs for the development of estimates of precision (determinability,
> repeatability, and reproducibility) and of bias (absolute and relative), and further presents
> the standard phraseology for incorporating such information into standard test methods."

(Reconstruction, segments 1-4 of 13 consecutive returns; junction checked - "programs" /
"for the development", "bias (absolute and relative)" / "and further presents".)

The nearest the served text comes to an adequacy criterion is the Significance and Use
sentence, which is a purpose statement and not a trigger:

> "ASTM test methods are frequently intended for use in the manufacture, selling, and buying of
> materials in accordance with specifications and therefore should provide such precision that
> when the test is properly performed by a competent operator, the results will be found
> satisfactory for judging the compliance of the material with the specification."

(Reconstruction, segments 14-17 of the same ordered set; junctions checked.) Note what this
sentence benchmarks precision against: **the specification the material is judged against**,
not a previously published precision figure. It is the hedged, purpose-level formulation, and I
quote it in preference to any bare paraphrase. It does not establish a re-study trigger.

Also served, and relevant to where any such clause would be inherited from:

> "This practice is intended for use by D02 subcommittees in determining precision estimates and
> bias statements to be used in D02 test methods. Its procedures correspond with ISO 4259"

(Reconstruction, segments 31-32; junction checked.) So the ISO 4259 family is the live route for
anyone continuing this check, exactly as the entry guessed - and it is the route I did not reach.

### (d) The stated reason for the re-study, verbatim

**WK89836** - Rationale in full, as served (two consecutive segments, assembled; junction
"ASTM D6300." / "It is necessary" checked; labelled a 2-fragment reconstruction):

> "In a previous revision of D5442, it was found that the precision was unsatisfactory according
> to requirements of ASTM D6300. It is necessary to develop a new interlaboratory study."

The entry's quoted fragment "unsatisfactory according to requirements of ASTM D6300" is
**accurate as far as it goes, but it is cut on the left in a way that changes its referent.**
The grammar on both sides of the cut matters here. The full subject is "the precision" in "a
previous revision of D5442" - that is, what failed is a precision statement produced by an
earlier study, assessed against **"requirements of ASTM D6300"**. The sentence does not say the
precision was compared with a previously published precision value. It says it failed D6300's
own requirements. Those requirements are what sub-claim (c) asks for, and I could not read them.

**WK67628** - Rationale in full, as served (two consecutive segments, assembled; junction
"inter-laboratory study" / " utilizing differing material types" checked; labelled a 2-fragment
reconstruction):

> "This change is to incorporate the precision and bias statement to be determined by an
> inter-laboratory study utilizing differing material types replacing the previous statement
> determined using a single material."

This rationale contains **no outcome claim at all.** Nothing failed. The stated defect is in the
DESIGN of the earlier study - one material - and the remedy is a design change: differing
material types. No achieved-precision figure, no published-precision figure, no comparison.

### (e) Bearing - is the trigger outcome-based, design-based, or undetermined?

**Split, and on the documents' own words it leans design/validity-based, not outcome-based:**

- WK67628: **design-based, unambiguously**, on its own words ("replacing the previous statement
  determined using a single material").
- WK89836: **undetermined, but anchored to D6300's requirements rather than to a published
  precision value.** "unsatisfactory according to requirements of ASTM D6300" routes the
  judgement into D6300's own conformance criteria. Whether those criteria are design minima
  (p, n, degrees of freedom, outlier handling) or an outcome threshold is precisely what I could
  not read. What the sentence does NOT say is that new precision was compared with previously
  published precision - that reading is not in the text.

## REGIME

**D6300-23a, in its own words.** Unit of analysis: an **interlaboratory programme** run by an
ASTM Committee D02 subcommittee, producing "estimates of precision (determinability,
repeatability, and reproducibility) and of bias (absolute and relative)" for insertion into a
test method's precision statement. Population: "generally limited to homogeneous petroleum
products, liquid fuels, and lubricants with which serious sampling problems (such as
heterogeneity or instability) do not normally arise." Explicitly excluded by the document:

> "solid or semisolid products such as petroleum coke, industrial pitches, paraffin waxes,
> greases, or solid lubricants when the heterogeneous properties of the substances create
> sampling problems."

(Reconstruction, segments 8-9; junction checked.) Operating condition: test "properly performed
by a competent operator." Instrument: none - this is a statistical practice, not a measurement
method.

**Does the ledger's claim sit inside this regime?** Only partly, and the gap is worth naming.
D6300 is a practice for **how to run and compute a precision study**, addressed to
subcommittees. It is not, on the evidence of its own scope, a standard that adjudicates whether
an existing published precision statement must be replaced. A re-study "trigger" is therefore
not obviously a thing D6300 has at all - it may live in D02's procedural rules rather than in
D6300. That possibility is not resolved by what I read, and the entry should not assume the
clause it is hunting for exists in D6300 to be found or missed.

One regime note that cuts against the entry's framing of WK89836: D5442 is a wax method
(petroleum waxes), and **paraffin waxes are named in D6300-23a's own 1.3 exclusion list.** If
D5442's earlier precision study was judged "unsatisfactory according to requirements of ASTM
D6300", the document being applied is one whose scope flags that very material class as
sampling-problematic and directs the user to "consult a trained statistician." That is a
design/applicability failure mode, not an achieved-precision-too-wide failure mode. I flag this
as an inference, not a quotation.

**Numerator/denominator check.** There is no number here to check in either direction. Neither
work item prints a precision value, a repeatability figure, a reproducibility figure, a count of
laboratories, or a degrees-of-freedom count. Neither names a previously published precision
value against which anything was measured. The current-edition text I read prints no number
relevant to the claim. So the near-neighbour trap does not arise - but nor does any supporting
measurement. Under published-is-not-performed: WK89836 **ANNOUNCES** a judgement of
unsatisfactoriness without printing the quantity judged, and **ANNOUNCES** a future study
("It is necessary to develop a new interlaboratory study"); WK67628 **ANNOUNCES** an intended
replacement. Neither **measures** anything, and neither is an evaluation of a precision figure.

## DEFECTS FOUND

1. **The entry's premise that the current edition would settle this is not yet tested.** I read
   D6300-23a at store-page level only. The entry's "only the 2003/04 text was read" remains
   true in substance: no body text of a current edition has been read by anyone in this chain.
   Sub-claims (b) and (c) are **COULD NOT CHECK**, not confirmed-absent. An unread section's
   silence is not evidence either way.
2. **The entry's quotation of WK89836 is cut on the left.** "unsatisfactory according to
   requirements of ASTM D6300" is verbatim, but omits "In a previous revision of D5442, it was
   found that the precision was" - which supplies the subject and makes clear the comparator is
   D6300's requirements, not a published precision value. As the entry carries it, the fragment
   reads as though it supports an outcome comparison. It does not.
3. **WK67628 does not support an outcome-based trigger and should stop being paired with
   WK89836 as if it did.** Its stated reason is a single-material design defect. On the
   evidence, the entry's two-work-item pairing is one design-based case and one unresolved case.
4. **Neither work item page served a Title or a Status.** The entry attributes WK89836 to D5442
   (supported, from the rationale's own body) and WK67628 to a named standard (**not supported
   by anything I could read**). That attribution needs its own check.
5. **D6300-23a is itself listed as "Standard Historical"** on the ASTM store, so it is not the
   current edition. Anyone finishing this check should identify the live designation first; the
   entry's "-20a, -23a or later" does not name it.
6. **A framing defect in the entry, independent of the reading:** it treats "design-based" and
   "outcome-based" as exhausting the options. The D6300-23a scope as served suggests a third
   possibility - that D6300 specifies no re-study trigger at all, being a how-to for running a
   study, with the decision to commission one sitting with the subcommittee. If so, the question
   as posed has no answer in this document and the selection worry has to be settled elsewhere.

## BEARING ON THE LEDGER ENTRY

**NARROW the attack, and strike one of its two legs.**

The entry's strongest remaining objection is outcome-based selection into the frame. On the two
documents that are supposed to carry it:

- **Strike the WK67628 leg.** Its own words give a design reason - a single-material study being
  replaced by one with "differing material types." It is affirmative evidence for a design-based
  commissioning, and it should be moved from the objection's support to the claim's support.
- **Narrow the WK89836 leg to what it says.** It says a prior D5442 precision statement "was
  unsatisfactory according to requirements of ASTM D6300." That points at D6300's conformance
  requirements, not at a comparison with previously published precision. The outcome-based
  reading the entry builds on it is an interpretation the sentence does not carry, and the
  entry's left-cut quotation is what makes the interpretation look textual.
- **Convert sub-claims (b) and (c) into a promotion condition, not a finding.** The decisive
  read - the body of the live D6300 edition, specifically its programme-planning sections and
  its annexes, checked for (i) any clause comparing new precision with previously published
  precision and (ii) stated minima for p, n and degrees of freedom - **has still not been done
  by anyone in this chain.** The entry must not record "no such clause in the current edition":
  that would convert my store-page read into a full-text finding. The honest state is: no clause
  in Scope or Significance and Use of D6300-23a; body unread.

Net: the objection is weaker than the entry has it, because its clearer leg (WK67628) actually
runs the other way and its stronger-sounding leg (WK89836) rests on a mis-cut quotation. But it
is not refuted, because the document that would refute it is still unopened. Promotion condition:
identify the live D6300 designation, obtain the body text by a legitimate route (a national
adoption of the ISO 4259 family is the cheapest, since D6300-23a states its "procedures
correspond with ISO 4259"), and answer (b) and (c) from printed text with section numbers.
