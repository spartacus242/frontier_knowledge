## Restatement (my words)

Several independent methods estimate the mass of extraterrestrial dust reaching Earth each year, and the estimates disagree by about an order of magnitude. Some of those methods infer the flux from the abundance of "meteoric" metals (Na, Fe, Mg, Ni, Ir, Pt, Os) in stratospheric particles, mesospheric metal layers, meteoric-smoke extinction, or polar snow and ice. Spacecraft reentry now injects some metals (Li, Al, Cu, Pb, Nb, Hf, Ag) at rates comparable to or larger than cosmic dust. The candidate asks, method by method: how far does the reentry input bias each inferred flux; at what reentry mass or calendar date does the bias exceed each method's own quoted precision; and can a pre-declared meteoric-vs-spacecraft separation rule, validated on pre-space-age archived material, keep each constraint usable as reentry mass grows? A null (every method's tracers are elements where reentry is small, or every method already excludes modern material) would close it in the negative.

## What the opened sources say (quoted where load-bearing)

- Schulz & Glassmeier 2020 (arXiv:2008.13032, Adv. Space Res.): "we compare the mass influx of human-made (anthropogenic) objects to the natural mass flux into Earth's atmosphere"; "Today, anthropogenic material does make up about 2.8 % compared to the annual injected mass of natural origin"; megaconstellation scenario raises this toward ~40%, with metal injection greatly exceeding natural. The only implication sentence: "All this can have yet unknown effects on Earth's atmosphere and the terrestrial habitat." Nothing on bias to influx estimates — but this is the ABSTRACT PAGE ONLY; full text not read. So the comparison the candidate's V1 opens with ("now that reentry input is comparable") was posed in 2020, five years before the record's P2.
- Carrillo-Sánchez et al. 2016 (GRL; White Rose abstract): total input 43 ± 14 t/d; constraints are "lidar observations of the vertical Na and Fe fluxes above 87.5 km", "the measured cosmic spherule accretion rate at South Pole", and COBE/Planck zodiacal-cloud observations. Abstract only. So the current canonical metal-tracer constraint rests on Na and Fe, not on Li/Al/Cu/Pb.
- Gabrielli et al. 2008 (Chem. Geol. 255, 78-86; full text): recent (1991-1995) Greenland snow Ir and Pt medians are ~5x and ~8x Holocene values; a Pt-vs-Ir regression intercept of 3.6 ± 0.6 fg/g is read as an additional non-cosmic Pt input; Pt spikes in 1994-95 are linked to vehicle catalytic converters, while "the Ir atmospheric budget does not appear clearly perturbed by catalytic converters"; the authors conclude recent Ir and Pt "do not reflect only the cosmic signal caused by the fallout of MSP", challenging their use as meteoric-smoke markers in recent Northern Hemisphere snow. The extraterrestrial flux from ice (Gabrielli 2004a) is drawn from pre-industrial Holocene ice. The paper derives no flux number itself.

## Verdict

No attack Unresolved; Partial on 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8; Resolved on 5 and 7. By the task's mapping this is not a fresh Gate 4 rejection. But novelty does not hold for the candidate as written: the generic question is a known, solved problem in each sub-field (restrict to pre-industrial material; curate a contaminant class), the opening comparison dates to 2020, and the unoccupied residue is a near-null arithmetic note whose one live input (Schulz 2025's Fe/Mg/Si figures) neither Gate 2 nor I could read.
