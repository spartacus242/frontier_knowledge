## Restatement (my own words)

A vendor switches off a closed model snapshot and tells users to move to a named replacement. Some papers that used the old snapshot published the model's answer to every item they tested. Two things are asked. (a) If you feed the replacement those same items under the paper's recorded settings, how often does it give a different answer than the archived old answers — judged against how often the OLD model disagreed with itself when run twice? (b) Over the whole set of such papers, what fraction of their stated conclusions would come out the other way if redone on the replacement — with a confidence interval, a pre-declared rule for what "the other way" means per paper, and the fraction of papers that had to be thrown out (and why) reported alongside?
