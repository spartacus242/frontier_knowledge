## Restatement in my own words

Freeze a release of ProteinGym's 217-assay missense benchmark. For every assay whose separately distributed pre-preprocessing ("raw") file or source deposit actually holds two or more independently selected replicate measurements of the same variants, measure how well that assay agrees with itself — a reliability coefficient on the merged-score scale, Spearman–Brown-stepped-up to however many replicates were merged into the released single `DMS_score`, with bootstrap CIs. Then take the published zero-shot leaderboard and, for each predictor-assay pair, divide the reported rank correlation by the square root of that assay's reliability (Spearman's 1904 correction for attenuation; three alternative estimator arms declared up front). Ask three things: (a) what each eligible assay's reliability is; (b) of the gap between a predictor's published correlation and a perfect 1.0, how much is the assay disagreeing with itself rather than the model being wrong; (c) whether removing the assay-error part reorders the leaderboard (Kendall tau between published and corrected orderings, plus pairwise reversals in a pre-set top-k). And, as a result in its own right, what fraction of the 217 assays can be audited this way at all.

Put plainly: *is the protein-variant-effect leaderboard partly a ranking of which assays happened to be measured precisely, and how far below 1.0 is each assay's true ceiling?*
