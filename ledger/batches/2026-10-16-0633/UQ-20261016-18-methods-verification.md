Run by the judge after adversary #1 rejected the candidate on a signal-processing argument. Fresh context, neutral framing, 20 queries, 0 refusals, with numerical checks the agent wrote and ran itself.

## Verdict on the claim under test: **PARTLY_CONFIRMED**

## 1. Does a block-shuffled surrogate preserve the amplitude spectrum?

NO — a block-shuffled / block-bootstrap surrogate does NOT preserve the amplitude (Fourier power) spectrum, and this is the standard result.

WHAT IT PRESERVES EXACTLY: (a) the empirical marginal distribution — a block permutation is a permutation of the same sample values; (b) therefore the sample variance and, by Parseval, the TOTAL integrated power (my check: total periodogram power ratio = 1.000 for every block length tested); (c) the joint/multivariate structure inside a block, i.e. the autocovariance at lags k << L (L = block length).

WHAT IT DESTROYS: all dependence across block joins. Künsch (1989, Ann. Statist. 17:1217, "The Jackknife and the Bootstrap for General Stationary Observations") and Liu & Singh (1992) define the blockwise bootstrap precisely so that dependence is retained within a block and the resampled blocks are i.i.d.; Lahiri (1999, Ann. Statist. 27:386, "Theoretical comparisons of block bootstrap methods") analyses the resulting O(1/L) bias. Spectrally this means: the resampled series' autocovariance is effectively tapered (Bartlett-type for the moving block bootstrap; exactly geometric, (1-p)^|k|, for Politis & Romano's 1994 stationary bootstrap), which in the frequency domain is a CONVOLUTION of the spectral density with a kernel of bandwidth ~1/L. Consequences, in frequency terms relative to the block length:
  - f << 1/L (periods longer than a block): power is whitened/flattened — long-range and low-frequency power is destroyed. The dedicated result is Zhang et al., "How complete random permutations affect the dependence structure of stationary sequences with long-range dependence" and "Applying bucket random permutations to stationary sequences with long-range dependence" (Fractals, 2006/2007): block ("bucket") permutation converts a long-range-dependent sequence into a short-range one.
  - features narrower than ~1/L in bandwidth are smeared by the 1/L-wide kernel.
  - f >> 1/L: only the SMOOTHED/expected spectrum is approximately retained, with O(1/L) bias; the realized periodogram is not retained at all, and block-boundary discontinuities inject broadband power.

NUMERICAL CONFIRMATION (scripts /tmp/claude-0/-home-claude-frontier-knowledge/a6b26651-5b07-5d11-a078-d31795831aa4/scratchpad/blk.py and blk2.py). AR(1), phi=0.98, N=8192: block shuffle with L=256 cut power in the band f < 1/(2L) to 0.525 of the original and raised the band 1/(2L)–2/L to 1.263; per-bin relative deviations exceeded 10^3 even at f > 4/L. Narrowband case (sine at f0=0.05, L=512, so f0 is 25x above 1/L): the periodogram at the signal bin fell to 0.074 of the original, because the block joins break phase continuity and scatter the line into sidebands, while power below 1/(2L) ROSE by 2.35x. So the spectrum is not preserved either below or above 1/L; only the total is.

CONTRAST WITH THE FT SURROGATE: Theiler, Eubank, Longtin, Galdrikian & Farmer, "Testing for nonlinearity in time series: the method of surrogate data," Physica D 58 (1992) 77-94, Sec. 2.2.3 p.81: "The alternative algorithm generates surrogate data which by construction has the same Fourier spectrum as the original data"; Sec. 2.4.1 p.84: "The surrogate data are constructed to have the same Fourier spectra as the raw data," and they declined to window precisely because they wanted "the real and surrogate data to have the same power spectrum." Schreiber & Schmitz, "Surrogate time series," Physica D 142 (2000) 346 (arXiv chao-dyn/9909037), Sec. 3.2: "the surrogate time series have the same Fourier amplitudes as the data." My check: the FT surrogate reproduced the periodogram to a maximum relative deviation of 4.46e-13 (machine precision) — exact by construction. (Caveats in Theiler Sec. 2.4.1-2.4.3: endpoint mismatch, the windowed WFT variant, and AAFT do NOT preserve the spectrum exactly — AAFT biases toward "a slightly flatter spectrum," which is why Schreiber & Schmitz 1996 PRL 77:635 introduced the iterative IAAFT.)

## 2. Does a coherence test against independently phase-randomised surrogates have power?

YES. Independently phase-randomised FT surrogates preserve each series' amplitude spectrum exactly while driving the cross-spectral coherence to its no-coupling null, so a magnitude-squared-coherence (MSC) test against them has power even though an amplitude-spectrum test against the same surrogates has none.

MECHANISM (primary source): Prichard & Theiler, "Generating surrogate data for time series with several simultaneously measured variables," Phys. Rev. Lett. 73 (1994) 951 (arXiv comp-gas/9405002). By the extended Wiener-Khinchin theorem the cross-spectrum is their Eq. (4), X_j*(f)X_k(f) = A_j(f)A_k(f)·exp{i[phi_k(f) - phi_j(f)]} — it depends on the PHASE DIFFERENCE, not on either amplitude spectrum. Hence their Eq. (5) prescription for PRESERVING cross-correlations: x~_j(t) = F^-1{X_j(f)e^{i phi(f)}} "where phi(f) is the same for all j", justified by "Since Eq. (4) only involves differences of phases, this is readily achieved by adding the same random sequence phi(f) to phi_j(f) for all j." The converse is immediate and is the standard construction of an uncoupled null: give each channel its OWN independent random phases and the phase differences are randomised, so E[S_xy(f)] = 0 at every frequency while |X_j(f)| is untouched.

STATED EXPLICITLY IN THE SURROGATE LITERATURE: Schreiber & Schmitz, "Surrogate time series," Physica D 142 (2000) 346, Sec. 6.2 (Multivariate data): "In other cases, we can decide either to keep or to destroy cross-correlations between channels. The former can be achieved by applying the same permutations to all channels." Sec. 4.6 (iterative multivariate surrogates) notes that the earlier scheme produced surrogates by "multiplying the Fourier phases of each of the channels by the same set of random phases," and that cross-structure is fixed by requiring "the new phases must implement the same phase differences exhibited by the corresponding phases"; in their worked heart-rate/breath-rate example the non-cross-preserving surrogate breath rate "lost the coherence present in the data." Pereda, Quian Quiroga & Bhattacharya, "Nonlinear multivariate analysis of neurophysiological signals," Prog. Neurobiol. 77 (2005) 1 (arXiv nlin/0510077), describes exactly this null: significance is assessed against "an ensemble of signals with the same autocorrelation than the original ones but completely independent from each other." Zhao et al. (IEEE EMBC 2008) states the general premise: surrogates "are designed to destroy any coupling present in the signal."

THE DEDICATED PRIMARY SOURCE FOR COHERENCE: Faes, Pinna, Porta, Maestri & Nollo, "Surrogate data analysis for assessing the significance of the coherence function," IEEE Trans. Biomed. Eng. 51(7) (2004) 1156-1166, doi:10.1109/TBME.2004.827271. They compare IID, FT and AR surrogates as coherence-threshold generators: IID preserves only the distribution, while "FT and AR surrogates preserve the power spectrum"; the IID threshold depends only on record length and estimator settings whereas the FT/AR thresholds are FREQUENCY-DEPENDENT, which is what prevents false coupling detections from independent oscillations at nearby frequencies in short, narrow-band records. They RECOMMEND the power-spectrum-preserving surrogates for this purpose — i.e. the very property the objection calls fatal is the reason FT surrogates are the preferred coherence floor.

WHY THE AUTO-SPECTRA CANNOT CARRY THE TEST: MSC is normalised by both auto-spectra, |S_xy|^2/(S_xx S_yy), so under H0 its sampling density depends only on the degrees of freedom. Thomson & Chave (1991, Eq. 2.53) and Hannan (1970, p. 259), as quoted in the multitaper-MSC paper arXiv:2505.06368: under "a null hypothesis of no linear relationship between the two random processes," p(C^2) ∝ (1-C^2)^{K-2} with K the number of tapers/segments — giving the familiar 1-alpha level C^2 = 1 - alpha^{1/(K-1)} (Carter 1987; Halliday et al. 1995). No term in either series' spectrum appears. The complementary climate-side primary source is Ebisuzaki (1997, J. Climate 10:2147), "A method to estimate the statistical significance of a correlation when the data are serially correlated" — random-phase surrogates of each series, 95th percentile of the surrogate coherence as the threshold; Traversi et al. (2012) randomise both series and take the per-frequency maximum.

NUMERICAL CONFIRMATION (/tmp/claude-0/-home-claude-frontier-knowledge/a6b26651-5b07-5d11-a078-d31795831aa4/scratchpad/blk2.py): coupled pair (y = 8-point moving average of x plus noise), Welch MSC with 32 segments. Data MSC at f0 = 0.985. Across 200 INDEPENDENTLY phase-randomised FT surrogate pairs: mean MSC at f0 = 0.040 (≈ 1/K = 0.031, the analytic no-coupling level), 95th percentile = 0.727. The test discriminates decisively; meanwhile the surrogates' own periodograms are identical to the originals to 4e-13.

CAVEATS worth pre-specifying: the null level depends on the EFFECTIVE degrees of freedom, so overlapping Welch windows, tapering and leakage must be accounted for (the analytic white-noise threshold is anti-conservative for strongly coloured or narrowband series — see arXiv:1504.01611, which is why the surrogate floor is preferred); and a loop-consistent-phase requirement (phase of S_xy stable across the band / consistent around the dose->pressure loop) is an ADDITIONAL constraint beyond MSC magnitude that independent phase randomisation also destroys, since surrogate phase differences are uniform by construction.

## 3. Where does a zero-order-hold staircase put spectral nulls and peaks relative to f = 1/T?

TRANSFER FUNCTION. For a hold of length T, h(t) = rect(t/T) = 1 for 0 <= t < T, so H(s) = (1 - e^{-sT})/s, and with s = i*2*pi*f, H(f) = T·sinc(fT)·e^{-i*pi*f*T}, i.e. |H(f)| = |sin(pi f T)/(pi f)| = T·|sinc(f/fs)| with fs = 1/T (Wikipedia "Zero-order hold"; standard sampled-data texts, e.g. Franklin, Powell & Workman, "Digital Control of Dynamic Systems"; Oppenheim & Schafer; Iqbal, "Introduction to Control Systems," Sec. 7.2 "Pulse Transfer Function"; NPTEL Digital Control module 1 lec 4).

NULLS AND MAXIMA. The sinc envelope has its global MAXIMUM at f = 0 (DC, gain T) and exact NULLS at f = k/T = k·fs for every nonzero integer k. It decreases monotonically from DC to the first null: at Nyquist, f = 1/(2T), the gain is sinc(1/2) = 2/pi, "a 3.9224 dB loss at the Nyquist frequency" (Wikipedia, Zero-order hold). The Analog Devices technical article "Equalizing techniques flatten DAC frequency response" gives the same response, "H(f) = sin(pi f/fS)/(pi f/fS)", and states directly: "As the DAC output frequency approaches its update frequency fS, the frequency response approaches zero or null" (and 2.42 dB attenuation at 80% of Nyquist; 0.1 dB flatness only out to ~0.17·f_Nyquist). So for a stationary held level the claim is right: f = 1/T is a spectral NULL, not a peak — the nulls are exactly where the ZOH suppresses the sampling images.

BUT THE ANSWER DOES CHANGE IF THE HELD LEVEL DRIFTS. If the updates track a monotonic ramp, x(t) = a·t, the ZOH output is the staircase y(t) = a·T·floor(t/T) and the hold ERROR e(t) = y(t) - x(t) = -a·(t mod T) + const is a SAWTOOTH of period T. Its Fourier series has lines at exactly f = k/T, k = 1, 2, 3, ..., with amplitudes falling as 1/k. This does not contradict the sinc: the envelope has only a SIMPLE zero at f = k/T, whereas a ramp's spectrum is a dipole (proportional to delta'(f)), so each image at k/T is delta'(f - k/T); the product of a simple zero with a derivative-of-delta is a nonzero delta, of weight -H'(k/T). A simple zero cannot kill a dipole. Hence a drifting ZOH puts a genuine line comb AT k/T, including a line at the update frequency itself. (This is the familiar DDS/phase-accumulator-truncation and sample-and-hold "sawtooth spur" mechanism; cf. "First-order correction for zero-order hold distortion in sampled-data filters," Electron. Lett. 1969.)

NUMERICAL CONFIRMATION (/tmp/claude-0/-home-claude-frontier-knowledge/a6b26651-5b07-5d11-a078-d31795831aa4/scratchpad/zoh.py; T = 100 fine samples, 100 hold intervals, Hann window, comb power compared with the local off-comb background at k = 1, 2, 3):
  - stationary random held levels: comb/background = 0.099, 0.052, 0.043 — i.e. power at k/T is SUPPRESSED below the neighbouring background, consistent with the null;
  - drifting staircase (ramp): comb/background = 6.8e9, 1.4e10, 3.2e10;
  - the isolated sawtooth hold error: comb/background = 1.3e14, 7.4e13, 6.0e13.
So the sign of the effect at f = 1/T flips entirely with drift.

PRACTICAL UPSHOT for a dose-to-pressure analysis with a T-periodic update: "ZOH implies a null at 1/T" is a safe prediction only when the held quantity is stationary around a fixed level over the analysed record. Any monotonic trend, ramp, or slow drift in the held level — or any other non-stationarity that makes successive held values systematically different — regenerates a line comb at k/T. A pre-specified analysis should therefore detrend/high-pass before leaning on the null, or treat k/T as a potentially contaminated frequency rather than as a built-in negative control.

## Effect on the rejection

The surrogate/coherence half of claim UQ-18-surrogate-and-comb is CONFIRMED and the "rendered powerless" objection is REFUTED as applied to the full pre-specified analysis. The objection is locally valid for exactly one of the four pairings: a band-limited-power statistic tested against a Fourier-phase-randomised surrogate has essentially zero power, because the FT surrogate reproduces the periodogram by construction (Theiler et al. 1992, Secs. 2.2.3 and 2.4.1; verified to 4.5e-13 relative error). It does not generalise. (i) The block-shuffled surrogate preserves only the marginal distribution and the total power exactly, and the spectral SHAPE only as a ~1/L-smoothed, O(1/L)-biased version; low-frequency power below ~1/L is whitened (Kuensch 1989; Lahiri 1999; Politis & Romano 1994; Zhang et al. 2006/2007 on bucket permutations). Measured: at L=256 the band f<1/(2L) retained 0.525 of the original power, and a narrowband line 25x above 1/L retained only 0.074 of its bin power. So band-limited power DOES have power against the block floor. (ii) Magnitude-squared coherence is normalised by both auto-spectra, so its no-coupling density depends only on the degrees of freedom, p(C^2) ∝ (1-C^2)^{K-2}, giving C^2 = 1 - alpha^{1/(K-1)} (Hannan 1970; Thomson & Chave 1991; Carter 1987; Halliday et al. 1995) - no term in either series' spectrum appears. Independently randomising each series' phases randomises the phase differences that ARE the cross-spectrum (Prichard & Theiler 1994, Eq. 4), driving coherence to that null while leaving both amplitude spectra exact; Schreiber & Schmitz (2000, Sec. 6.2) state the preserve-vs-destroy choice directly, and Faes et al. (IEEE TBME 2004) is the dedicated primary source, recommending power-spectrum-preserving surrogates precisely as the coherence floor. Measured: data MSC 0.985 vs independent-FT-surrogate mean 0.040 (~1/K) and 95th percentile 0.727. A loop-consistent-phase requirement is a further constraint these surrogates also destroy. Naming both floors and both statistics is therefore a well-founded design, not a self-defeating one.

The ZOH half is CONFIRMED ONLY CONDITIONALLY, and the claim as written is too strong. The ZOH transfer function H(s) = (1 - e^{-sT})/s, |H(f)| = |sin(pi f T)/(pi f)|, peaks at DC, loses 3.9224 dB at Nyquist, and has exact nulls at f = k/T - so a stationary staircase does carry a null, not a peak, at f = 1/T (Analog Devices: "As the DAC output frequency approaches its update frequency fS, the frequency response approaches zero or null"; verified, comb/background 0.099 at k=1). But if the held level drifts monotonically the hold error is a sawtooth of period T whose Fourier series puts lines at exactly k/T with 1/k amplitudes; the sinc's simple zero cannot cancel the ramp's dipole image there. Verified: comb/background 6.8e9 at k=1 for a drifting staircase, 1.3e14 for the isolated sawtooth error. So the "null rather than a peak at 1/T" claim holds for a stationary held level and REVERSES under drift. Any analysis using f = 1/T as a built-in negative control must detrend or high-pass the held quantity first, or treat k/T as potentially contaminated.
