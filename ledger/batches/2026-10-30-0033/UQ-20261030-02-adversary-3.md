# Gate 4 — Adversary #3 (fresh context, harder rerun). UQ-20261030-02

## Restatement (my own words, before reading the record's framing claims)

Build human cell lines that are identical except that each carries a single, tagged, switch-on-able LINE-1 element sitting at one known spot on a chromosome. Switch it on, let it copy itself into new places, and collect the new insertions without using the tag's expression to find them. Then ask: do the new insertions pile up in stretches of genome that physically sit close to the switch-on-able element's own chromosomal home — close in the sense of a Hi-C/Micro-C contact map, measured mainly on OTHER chromosomes, after you have already accounted for where the L1 enzyme likes to cut, how open the chromatin is, when the region replicates, and its base composition? And if you move the donor element to a locus in the opposite compartment (A to B or B to A), does that dependence move with it? In short: does a LINE-1 copy land near where it came from, or is landing independent of origin?
