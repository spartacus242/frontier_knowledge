## Verification of the kill at first hand (post-batch, 2026-10-21)

Two fresh-context readers: one handed the Rochman paper and the four strings this entry quotes from it, one sent on an independent sweep for the released-library and USL limb. **The verdict does not move — Rejected — and the ground it rests on is narrower than the entry stated.**

**The kill holds where it was read.** The paper's front matter is exactly as cited (all seven authors in order, Eur. Phys. J. Plus 133:537, 2018, DOI 10.1140/epjp/i2018-12361-x), section 8.3 genuinely is a held-out predictive test, and all four quoted strings are in it. The exercise: 12 benchmarks supplying 14 integral quantities enter the adjustment (Table 1), and the caption of Table 7 reads verbatim "51 benchmarks are used: 18 hmf, 18 pmf, 7 imf, 3 mmf, 4 mcf and 1 icf". A single in-set/out-set split, not a leave-one-out. So the candidate's primary limb — the shift when benchmarks are held out of a library's own integral-data adjustment, in the direction the candidate hypothesised — is asked and answered in the candidate's own field, which is what the Gate 2 grade turned on.

**Three scope corrections, and they matter for what this paper may be cited for.** (1) The unit is five individually named ENDF-6 files — the paper's own prior nominal file 0, its BMC, BFMC and Mocaba posteriors, and file 1834, the best member of its own random ensemble. **No released evaluated library is scored on the held-out set.** JEFF, JENDL and ENDF/B appear only in the introduction, in section 8.2.2 and in a remark about one ENDF/B-VIII reaction; the paper never says file 0 derives from a released library, only that it resembles what "one can find in a nuclear data library". Any claim of the form "adjusted data generalise worse than released libraries" is outside this paper's regime, and the paper is silent there rather than supportive. (2) The endpoint is an unthresholded mean bias in pcm — absolute 311 (file 0) against 627, 493, 470 and 423 — plus chi-squared per benchmark, with **no sampling uncertainty, no interval, no bias uncertainty, no area of applicability and no tolerance limit**. The strings "bias uncertainty", "upper subcritical limit", "USL", "subcritical", "ANSI", "ANS-8" and "8.24" do not occur, and no criticality-safety standard is cited; "criticality-safety" appears once, as one item in a list of application areas. So the paper cannot be cited for a criticality-safety bias in the ANSI/ANS-8.24 sense, and its silence on the USL is an absence, not a flag. (3) ICSBEP is named in sections 1 and 2.2 and Table 1 cites the handbook for the measured values, but section 8.3 says only "a new set of criticality benchmarks" and the Table 7 caption names no source — so the ICSBEP provenance of the 51 is an inference from the class codes, exactly as this entry already recorded.

**Two quotation defects in this entry, corrected above.** "larger chi2 and biases" was a transliteration rather than a quotation: the paper prints the chi-squared glyph, and the sentence's subject is "The adjustment methods as well as the best random file", which the fragment dropped. And the file-0 string was quoted away from its hedges — its own sentence opens "As observed," tying it to Table 7, the next sentence makes the conclusion dependent on the benchmark selection and the file type, and the sentence after that explicitly refuses the strong reading: "Such observations do not discard the advantages of the presented methods for the uncertainty reduction". The entry now carries the surrounding sentences. One further distinction worth keeping: the paper's own "no predictive power" remark is about the linear-combination method, which it excluded from the test, and is not a caution about validation.

**The residual limb is UNVERIFIED, not established as un-asked, and that is load-bearing.** The independent sweep could not settle whether the overlap question is posed for released libraries, because the two documents most likely to carry it — WPEC SG-22's "First conclusions" and the ENDF/B-VIII.0 release paper — were both robots-refused, and eleven further USL and bias-uncertainty documents (WPEC SG-33, SG-44, UACSA Phase IV, CSSG Response 2014-02, LA-UR-16-23533 among them) were identified and never opened.
