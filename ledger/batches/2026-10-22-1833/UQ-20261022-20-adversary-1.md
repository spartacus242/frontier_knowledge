## Restatement (my words, not the generator's)

Read literally, the reformulated wording needs four things to be true of the world before anything is measured: that such family/checkpoint pairs exist; that a public, dated, class-labelled request-length corpus exists; that the SAME corpus C supplies the exceeding requests in a form that can be re-served at two window settings (limb (b) is "on the exceeding stratum only" of C); and that request lengths logged in C reflect demand rather than the cap of whatever model served them.

C3. Limb (b) is stated "on the exceeding stratum only" of C: this presupposes that C's exceeding requests can be RE-SERVED at L_new and at L_prev-with-truncation and graded. That requires prompt content and a gradable task in C. TESTED on three opened instances of the kind: FALSE on all three. Mooncake: "Raw text and tokens are not included for privacy protection." Content-bearing public corpora exist (WildChat, LMSYS-Chat-1M returned by query 9, NOT OPENED), but they are chat logs of one closed product, not the tool-trace/coding classes where the record says the limit binds, and using them splits (a) and (b) across two populations — the exact estimand/instrument mismatch the Gate 1 reformulation was invoked to remove. The question does NOT convert this clause into a reported quantity (it reports an outcome difference, not the share of corpora that permit the experiment), so the measurand exemption does not reach it. Grade: FALSE (sourced, three instances), with the repair path noted.

C4. Lengths logged in C reflect demand, so that P(input > L_prev) is the share of traffic the doubling served. TESTED on S1 and R1-R3: CONTESTED (sourced). S1 shows that in the agentic-coding class the harness compacts prompts at "near 50%, 65–66% (the overall median), 80%, and near-100% utilization" of the model's limit, so logged prompt lengths are shaped by the serving limit before they can exceed it — a served request cannot exceed the limit of the model that served it, and in the class most likely to bind it is cut well before. R1-R3 carry no per-model context-limit field (Mooncake and Azure do not even name the model), so the censoring cannot be corrected from the corpus. Exceedance of L_prev is therefore observable on C only for requests served by models with limit > L_prev, and the demand that L_new would have unlocked (the quantity "marginal value of the last doubling" actually needs) is unobservable on any served-request trace. The record's P1 names model-mixing but not censoring; the reformulation's "declared external-validity limitation" covers representativeness, not truncation of the measurand's own support. Grade: CONTESTED (sourced one way by S1; no opened source shows an uncensored public corpus).

C5. "announced context-length increases" are the binding engineering variable (P4). S1 bears on this: in agentic coding the binding variable is the harness's compaction threshold, not the announced window; see attack 8. Grade: CONTESTED (sourced, S1 Fig. 17).

## What would settle attack 4 (kind of document first, then nominee)

Kind: a public, dated corpus of production or production-like requests that (i) carries the PROMPT CONTENT or a re-executable equivalent, (ii) carries a gradable task outcome per request, (iii) has a workload-class label including a coding-agent or tool-trace class, and (iv) prints or allows computing per-request input lengths with a known serving limit per request (so censoring can be bounded). Nominees and whether they are that kind: the Copilot team's promised "sanitized traces" (S1 Sec. 12) — unknown; "sanitized" in this literature has meant length-and-hash only (R1), so this is a guess, not a lead.
