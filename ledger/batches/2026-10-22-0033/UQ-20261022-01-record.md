Alternative formulations: Do the published metabolic rates that comparative physiology's scaling datasets rest on fail their own internal arithmetic check, and by how much? · Treating simultaneously reported VO2 and VCO2 as a redundant measurement pair, what is the internal-inconsistency rate of the comparative respirometry literature, and what error floor does it place on interspecific metabolic-rate comparison?

Motivating gap: RQ is bounded by substrate stoichiometry (~0.70 fat to 1.00 carbohydrate), so a reported pair outside that window is a redundancy check that fails and flags that at least one of the two rates is wrong. Comparative databases and the 2/3-versus-3/4 exponent dispute pool exactly these primary measurements, and no audit found here computes the corpus's infeasible-RQ share or propagates it into pooled values. CORRECTION carried out of Gate 1, which the intake had the wrong way round: the intake's mechanism was that VO2 and VCO2 "share flow, water-vapour and analyser-calibration terms", so a shared-term error shows up as an infeasible RQ. The opposite holds for the dominant shared term. In flow-through respirometry both rates are computed linearly in the STP-corrected flow rate, so a multiplicative flow error scales numerator and denominator alike and cancels in the ratio to first order; water-vapour dilution acts on both gas fractions and partly cancels; single-analyser span/zero error acts on one channel and does not cancel. The redundancy check is therefore BLIND to the shared terms the intake named and SENSITIVE to per-channel calibration and water-vapour-asymmetry error. (THIS IS MY OWN DERIVATION from the structure of the Haldane-transformed equations, with NO source opened for it — flagged for the adversary to check, and not relied on for any novelty grade.) It does not kill the question: per-channel calibration failure is a real and consequential error class, and in the CO2-unscrubbed single-channel mode a wrong RQ propagates into VO2 by a known route, which is exactly why limb (ii) must be derived per mode. It does mean the intake's error-bounding claim is inverted for the shared terms and that the question's reach is narrower than V1 implies.

Presuppositions:

So the frame is constructible but is a terrestrial/dual-channel frame, not the whole corpus.
2. NOTE from a source opened here: the window professional practice actually screens on is WIDER than the candidate's. AARC's clinical practice guideline lists as a validity criterion that "RQ rests in the normal physiologic range (0.67 to 1.3)". The headline share in limb (i) is therefore highly sensitive to a window choice on which an authoritative document disagrees with the candidate, which is why the 0.67-1.30 arm is promoted into the operative formulation rather than left as a sensitivity note.
3. An infeasible RQ in the pre-declared strata indicates measurement error rather than real physiology — CONTESTED; this is what the arms are for.
4. Limb (ii) of V2 asks for the identifiability before the magnitude.

If mode-and-calibration classification in the dual-gas literature is reported at rates like these, a large share of the frame will be unclassifiable and must be reported as such rather than imputed.
6. The interspecific allometric fit is sensitive enough to a minority of mis-measured points for the test to be informative — CONTESTED, and testable by simulation before extraction. Argued below (Significance — consequence) rather than assumed: the bias path runs through mass-dependence of the error, not through its mere presence.

What would count as an answer: a pre-registered audit in which frame, feasibility window, unit and sensitivity arms are fixed before extraction, reporting (i) the infeasible share with a CI, by taxon, mode and decade, with the dual-gas denominator and the unclassifiable share reported separately; (ii) a per-mode derivation — not an assumption — of which error classes the RQ check identifies, and only then what it bounds in the reported rate, carrying the sources' own stated uncertainties; (iii) exponents refitted under pre-declared exclusion and correction rules against the published interval. A share near zero with exponents unmoved answers it negatively and settles it just as well: that result would be a positive licence, an explicit measurement-error floor for pooled respirometry, which nobody can currently issue.

Significance — consequence: If the infeasible share is non-trivial AND mass-dependent, comparative physiology acquires a measurement-error floor it does not currently quote, and the exponent dispute changes character: that dispute is decided on interval width, and this ledger's own verified arithmetic for UQ-20261017-32 has Genoud et al. 2018's SELECT fit at 0.729 +/- 0.010 S.E. with a published claim that the interval excludes both 2/3 and 3/4, so a bias of a few per cent concentrated in part of the mass range is decisive rather than cosmetic. The argued mechanism, not asserted: a random minority of mis-measured points widens intervals and leaves the slope alone, so the consequence hangs on non-randomness in mass — and there is a reason to expect it, since analyser resolution is a fixed quantity while the signal scales with animal size, so per-channel calibration failure should bite hardest at the small end, which is where a slope is most leveraged. That is a falsifiable prediction the audit tests rather than assumes, and if it fails the consequence collapses to wider intervals. If the share is near zero and exponents are unmoved, the field can for the first time issue an explicit, quantified licence for pooling primary respirometry values, and the RQ redundancy check can be retired as a screening tool for this corpus.

Reason for silence: a disciplinary boundary with an instrument fact underneath it, plus an incentive gap — and notably NOT "nobody thought of it", since the check is written down in another field's guidelines. The RQ-plausibility screen is owned by clinical and nutritional metrology, where it is applied PROSPECTIVELY to one's own test and then discarded: an out-of-range RQ means repeat the measurement, so no one accumulates the out-of-range values, and AARC's section 9.1 gives the criterion with no interpretation of failures. The corpus is owned by comparative physiology, whose audit culture has matured in the adjacent dimension (PRIOR 1 scores 202 papers on 53 criteria) but scores TEXT, because documentation can be scored by reading and needs no re-computation, whereas a feasibility audit needs paired numbers that most papers do not print together. The instrument fact that keeps the two apart: in aquatic respirometry, the branch with the strongest current methods literature, CO2 production is essentially never measured — verified in full text, PRIOR 1 never mentions it — so for the authors best placed to run this audit the redundant pair does not exist, and the audit is possible only in the scattered terrestrial dual-channel literature whose denominator looks too small to be worth counting until someone counts it. Finally an incentive asymmetry: the finding has no positive deliverable for an individual lab (it indicts the shared record, not a rival's hypothesis) and its negative outcome — a licence to pool — is unpublishable-looking in advance.
