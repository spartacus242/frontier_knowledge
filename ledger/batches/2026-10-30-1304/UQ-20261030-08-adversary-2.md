## Restatement in my own words

Take a resected human carcinoma that has a grossly identifiable outer boundary. Slice it by distance
from that boundary and ask what clones are present in each shell. Two possibilities. (A) The outermost
shell holds only a few of the clones present deeper in — the rim is a thinned-out sample of the bulk,
because the cells that happen to be at the leading surface at any moment are the only ones that found
the next layer, and most lineages are lost. (B) The outermost shell holds roughly the clonal mix the
bulk holds, because cells from behind keep arriving at the rim faster than the rim loses lineages. The
question asks which, with two numbers attached — how much lower diversity is at the rim than in the
bulk, and over what distance the deficit decays — and then asks whether different organ sites answer
differently. The imported names for (A) and (B) are "pulled" and "pushed" expansion fronts.
