## Restatement (my words, not the generator's)

Take an off-the-shelf sparse-MoE checkpoint and run it at inference with a hard per-expert token budget, so some tokens are thrown away before the expert FFN. Two things are asked. (a) Are the thrown-away tokens a non-random slice of the input — piling up in particular languages/scripts, document domains, rare-vs-common vocabulary, or late sequence positions — and does the answer change between the two named rules for choosing victims (first-come-first-served overflow vs. drop-the-lowest-gate-score)? (b) Run the identical model on the identical inputs with the budget switched off; for each slice, does its share of dropped tokens predict how much worse it does, well enough that an operator could price a capacity setting per slice before shipping it?

In short: is the capacity factor a throughput knob with a hidden, forecastable distributional bill, and who gets the bill.
