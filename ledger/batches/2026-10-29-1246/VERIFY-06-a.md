## 4. What the paper derives, exactly (brief item 2)

Object: the spectrum nu of the trained first-layer Gram S-hat = W-hat^T W-hat / sqrt(pd) at the ERM minimiser (quadratic network); the diagonal network's learned weights for the diagonal case. So YES, it is the TRAINED WEIGHT matrices' spectral density (of W^T W), not the NTK, not the data covariance, not a feature-map Gram — with the qualification that the "trained" matrix is the global ERM minimiser reached by LBFGS, not an SGD endpoint.

Result 2, Eq. (16), as read: nu(x) = F_{mu_delta}(lambda eps) delta_0(x) + mu_delta(x + lambda eps) 1_{x > 0}, where "mu_delta represents the spectrum of S* + delta Z" with Z ~ GOE(d), and "there exists constants delta(n,d,lambda) and eps(n,d,lambda) (that are obtained from (22))". Status: a "Result" built on state evolution whose fixed point is Conjecture 1 (Eq. 22); the Figure 3 caption says state evolution is "rigorous only in the asymptotic limit n_eff/d = Theta(1) with d >> 1". In words (my gloss, labelled): the learned spectrum is the target spectrum, additively perturbed by a GOE of width delta (free additive convolution with a semicircle), shifted left by lambda*eps and truncated at zero with the truncated mass placed at zero.

The heavy-tail statement, Section 2.3 ("Interpretability, and a 'universal' error decomposition"; introduced by "Focusing now on the quadratic network, the result provides an interpretation of the risk in terms of the weights spectrum."):
- v1, Phase III bullet, verbatim: "III (Heavy-tail): The spectrum is a perturbed version of the target spectrum with a heavy-tail rho(x) ~ x^{-1-1/gamma}."
- camera-ready continues the same bullet: "Regularization shifts the bulk leftward by ≈ lambda d^2 / 4n, yielding R = Theta((lambda d^2/n)^2)."
- Phase VIb (camera-ready): "VIb (Heavy-tail): As in Phase III, the spectrum is a perturbed version of the target (a heavy-tailed bulk) with perturbations Theta(sqrt(Delta) d^2/n). The risk decays with the perturbation as R = Theta(d^2/n)."
- "rho(x)" and "x^{-1-1/gamma}" appear ONLY in the Phase III bullet (both versions; summariser's sweep). The paper counts "eight distinct spectral phases" while listing nine labels (Ia, Ib, II, III, IV, V, interpolation peak, VIa, VIb) — the interpolation peak is presumably not counted as a phase; noted, not adjudicated.

Grammar on both sides of the cut: the sentence's opening words "The spectrum is a perturbed version of the target spectrum" scope the trained spectrum IN PHASE III of the paper's own phase diagram and tie the tail to the target; the clause ASSERTS the exponent 1 + 1/gamma; it does not deny it or record a move away from it. It is a phase-local statement, not a general law. It is not a numbered equation, theorem or table; no fitted number accompanies it.

The self-description, reconstructed from THREE consecutive summariser segments (camera-ready, Section 2.3): "This provides a mathematical theory from first principles for the observations of" + "(Martin & Mahoney, 2021a; Martin et al., 2021), whose terminology" + "(e.g. "bleed-out", "rank collapse", . . . ) we borrow in the following." PUBLISHED IS NOT PERFORMED: the paper DERIVES and PREDICTS the exponent; it prints NO fitted tail exponent. MY OWN REASONING, labelled: if one identified the GOE width delta with an SGD-noise-induced fluctuation, Eq. 16 would predict the tail exponent to be INSENSITIVE to it (a perturbation of the target leaves the tail exponent at 1 + 1/gamma), which would be a "beta alone" prediction for the candidate's heavy-tail cells. The computation that would ground it: an SDE/stationary-distribution model of SGD on the quadratic network giving the law of S-hat - S*.

## 10. Bearing on the entry (no verdict recommended)

Question answered by the paper, limb by limb:
- "beta alone": PARTLY and ADJACENTLY. The paper derives (does not measure) tail exponent = 1 + 1/gamma, a function of the data-side exponent alone, at the ERM minimiser, with the power law on the TARGET and inputs isotropic. Transferring gamma to the candidate's input-covariance beta needs a model-dependent mapping the paper does not supply (the entry's gamma_eff = beta/2 is adversary 3's labelled arithmetic for a linear model; MY check: under that mapping the predicted density exponent is 1 + 2/beta, i.e. 5.0, 3.0, 2.33, 2.0, 1.8, 1.67 for beta = 0.5...3.0, and the paper's gamma > 1/2 condition would exclude the beta = 0.5 and 1.0 cells; a Hill estimator returns the survival index 1/gamma_eff = 2/beta, one less than the density exponent, so the candidate's protocol must declare its convention — all of this is my reasoning, not a finding).
- "T alone": UNTOUCHED. No temperature, learning rate or batch size exists in the model; the authors list "the implicit biases of SGD towards heavy tails" as future work.
- "single combination beta^a T^b" and "both irreducibly": UNTOUCHED, for the same reason.
- The crossed surface: not measured; not measurable in this frame.

Easier, not occupied: the paper's existence makes the candidate's question EASIER to answer and sharper to pose, which is a different relationship from prior art. It supplies (i) a null prediction for the beta limb — a tail exponent set by the data-side exponent and insensitive to the estimation-noise perturbation — that the candidate's grid would test cell by cell; (ii) a phase diagram implying that a power-law tail exists only in some (n, d, lambda) phases (III, VIb), which bears on the candidate's contested presupposition 1 and says where on the n/d^2 axis to look; (iii) a concrete object (S-hat = W^T W/sqrt(pd)) matching the HT-SR ESD. What it takes away is the "no mechanism" premise and the record's universal that the scaling-law line "never forms a trained weight matrix" — both already struck in the entry, correctly on my read.
