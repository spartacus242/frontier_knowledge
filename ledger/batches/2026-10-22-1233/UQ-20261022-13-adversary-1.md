## Restatement in my own words

Take the magnitude a named agency (USGS NEIC, JMA, GeoNet) had on its public record at some fixed time after an earthquake — the time at which a parametric contract or an automated response reads the number — and treat that number as a prediction of the magnitude the same agency's catalogue will carry at a declared later date. Limb (a): does the later change average zero, and is it unpredictable from what was already known when the number was read (depth, region, how many stations, which magnitude type, how big the event, local time, the number itself)? Limb (b): take every publicly known contract or operational threshold that keys on that agency's number; how many of those yes/no decisions would have gone the other way on the later number, which way, and for how much?

The question therefore has two regimes buried in it, which I separate deliberately because the kill attempts turn on it: (i) short-latency vintages (minutes to an hour, rapid magnitude types such as Mwp, mb, Ms, Mj) and (ii) contractual-latency vintages (days to weeks, the agency's reviewed Mww). The record's "fixed settlement latency" is silent on which, and the Gate 1 version lets tau be anything.
