## Restatement in my own words

Take one pile of Common Crawl. Hire two or three different AI models as graders, each with roughly the same measured ability but from different model families, and have each one pick out the same *fraction* of the pile as "good". You now have two or three corpora that differ only in who graded. Train the same three-to-five candidate model designs (dense vs MoE, attention variant, depth/width, LR rule) on each corpus, same FLOPs, same tokens, two or more random seeds per combination, one benchmark suite fixed in advance. Two numbers come out: (i) how big the grader x design interaction is compared with the seed-to-seed wobble inside a single cell, and (ii) how many of the pairwise "design X beats design Y" verdicts actually *flip direction* when you switch graders, counting only flips bigger than a declared minimum. The underlying worry: a paper saying "architecture X beats Y" may really be saying "X beats Y on a corpus curated to the taste of Llama 3" — and nobody has reported the number that would tell us which.

## My own arithmetic (labelled as such)

(a) **Degrees of freedom at the declared minimum design.** T=2 teachers, A=3 arms, S=2 seeds gives interaction df = (T-1)(A-1) = 2 and within-cell error df = T*A*(S-1) = 6. The exact 95% interval for the ratio of expected mean squares is [F/F_.975(d1,d2), F/F_.025(d1,d2)]. I computed the F quantiles myself by bisection on the closed-form F CDF (regularized incomplete beta with integer parameters; my F_.975(2,6) = 7.2599 reproduces the standard table value 7.26):
  - df (2,6): 7.2599 / 0.02542 -> the interval spans a factor of **285x**
  - df (2,12) [T=2,A=3,S=3]: span **201x**
  - df (8,15) [T=3,A=5,S=2, the record's maximal declared design]: span > 10x
  - df (8,30) [T=3,A=5,S=3, i.e. 45 pretraining runs]: span **10.3x**
  So limb (i) "ratio with CI" yields a CI spanning an order of magnitude even at 45 matched-FLOP pretraining runs. This bounds the *precision* of limb (i); it does not make the directional decision rule ("does the interval exclude 1?") undecidable.
(b) **The 10.1% keep-set overlap is keep-rate confounded.** The record's own quoted Nemotron-CC Table 9 keep shares are 8% / 11% / 25%. For any sets, intersection <= smallest and union >= largest, so Jaccard <= 0.08/0.25 = **0.32**. The observed 0.1015 (I reproduce the record's division: 1,152,821 / 11,359,655 = 0.1015) is therefore ~32% of its structural ceiling, not the near-total disagreement the bare "10.1% overlap" suggests. Under the candidate's own matched-keep-rate condition the ceiling rises to 1.0 and the realised overlap is unknown and plausibly much higher — i.e. the design condition attenuates the corpus difference the motivation leans on. Uncertainty carried: the record read these counts from the PDF; I did not re-open Table 8, so the counts are its reading, and the bound is a ceiling argument, not an estimate of matched-rate overlap.
(c) **Scope check on my strongest prior, in the candidate's regime.** Liu et al.'s teachers are *not* capability-matched: Table 2 spans 78.31–84.4 top-1, a ~6-point range. Its reversals are small and unguarded: under T(A) the five students span 65.8–66.6 (0.8 pt); S2 moves 66.6 (rank 1, T(A)) to 65.4 (rank 5, T(B)), 1.2 pt; the GT column contains two exact ties (65.6/65.6 and 65.5/65.5), so the ranking resolution is at or below plausible run-to-run variance, and the paper reports no replicates. So the prior is in the candidate's interaction class but *inside* exactly the two defects (capability confound, no noise floor) the candidate's reformulation exists to remove.
