Question (canonical — Gate 0 intake version, recorded):
In corpora of repeated high-precision measurements of the same physical quantity, is a measurement's blinding status associated with the calibration of its STATED UNCERTAINTY — the ratio of quoted uncertainty to the realized scatter of same-quantity measurements — after conditioning on quantity, era and precision?

Question (canonical — Gate 1 reformulation, ONE permitted, for well-posedness only; this is the operative version):
Over a declared frame of published measurements of quantities each having >=K independent same-quantity determinations and a pre-declared non-circular reference value, with each measurement hand-coded for blinding status (hidden signal region / hidden offset or scale factor / salted data / none / non-ascertainable), what is the ratio of the uncertainty-calibration factor — the multiplier c that makes the per-measurement pulls (x_i - x_ref)/u_i unit-variance — between the blinded and unblinded strata, estimated with quantity fixed effects and era and precision controls, with a tail index estimated separately per stratum; or is the design non-identifiable in this frame?
(Reformulation changes: unit of analysis fixed to the published measurement; "realized scatter" replaced by an explicit pre-declared reference and a named estimand; the estimand made a CONTRAST of calibration factors rather than an association; non-identifiability admitted in advance as an answer.)

Alternative formulations:
- Does blinding change how well physicists' error bars predict their own later disagreement, as distinct from changing their central values?
- Are blinded analyses' uncertainty budgets systematically more or less realistic than unblinded ones, and by how much?

Motivating gap:
The blinding-in-physics literature is dense with HOW and empty of measurement. Confirmed by reading, not recalled: Klein & Roodman (2005) is a techniques review whose only quantitative meta-measurement is a central-value herding test (Fig. 2; chi2 = 131.2/83 dof around prior published averages vs 249.7/~82 dof around the final average) and which, read directly, "never says whether blinded error bars are larger, smaller, or better calibrated"; its stated conclusion is "we cannot say conclusively whether bias has influenced measurements in nuclear and particle physics." arXiv:2311.13542 (read at intake) is explicitly argumentative, costs-of-blinding from three anecdotes. So the CENTRAL-VALUE limb is asked-and-unanswered — an open problem belonging in the routing file, not this ledger — and is deliberately excluded here. The UNCERTAINTY limb is what is unasked. Mechanism with a sign: an unblinded analyst whose value already sits near expectation has less reason to keep hunting systematics (quoted uncertainty too small); a blinded analyst, unable to calibrate against expectation, may inflate the systematic budget defensively (too large). The limb is measurable because dated measurement histories with split statistical/systematic uncertainties are published for many quantities, and the marginal calibration statistic is already a mature published quantity (see the "already computed" finding below) — only the blinding-stratified contrast is missing.

Presuppositions (established / contested, as flagged):
1. Blinding status is ascertainable from the publications for a usable fraction of the frame and gradable (hidden signal region, hidden offset/scale factor, salted data, none). CONTESTED; leading feasibility risk. Reporting is inconsistent, especially pre-~2000; a non-ascertainable stratum must be reported, never imputed.
2. Published measurement histories supply enough same-quantity repeats with dates and stated uncertainties. Largely ESTABLISHED — the Review of Particle Physics subset alone holds 8,469 measurements of 864 quantities (Bailey 2017, read). Usable count after the blinding-coding and reference restrictions must still be reported.
3. A non-circular reference for realized scatter exists (a later far-more-precise determination, or leave-one-out). CONTESTED where no decisive later value exists; such quantities excluded in advance. Note: Bailey's pairwise z = |x_i - x_j|/sqrt(u_i^2+u_j^2) is an alternative that needs NO reference and should be carried as the sensitivity analysis.
4. Blinding is not perfectly confounded with era, collaboration size and intrinsic precision. CONTESTED and central. Blinding adoption tracks recent decades and large collaborations, so the design is within-quantity with era controls, and if separation fails the honest answer is non-identifiability in this frame.
5. Quoted uncertainties are intended as roughly 1-sigma coverage and are comparable across experiments. APPROXIMATELY ESTABLISHED by convention; systematics conventions differ enough that a sensitivity analysis is required.
6. The central-value-bias question is already asked and unanswered and is excluded here. ESTABLISHED by direct reading of Klein & Roodman 2005 and arXiv:2311.13542. The exclusion is what keeps this from being a known open problem in new clothes.
7. NEW, added at Gate 1 — the reference-availability asymmetry. Blinded measurements cluster in recent decades and are therefore the ones LEAST likely to have a decisive later more-precise value, so a naive design evaluates the blinded stratum against systematically weaker references. CONTESTED and must be pre-handled by restricting to quantities with a decisive later value available to BOTH strata, or by using the reference-free pairwise statistic.
8. NOT presupposed: that blinding has any effect on uncertainties, or that either direction is expected.

What would count as an answer:
A declared frame of measurement histories; blinding status coded from the publications under a pre-specified scheme with the non-ascertainable stratum reported; per-measurement pulls against the pre-chosen reference (later high-precision value, or leave-one-out), with Bailey-style reference-free pairwise z as sensitivity; then the uncertainty-calibration factor c and a tail index estimated separately for blinded and unblinded strata with quantity fixed effects and era and precision controls. The answer is a ratio of calibration factors with a confidence interval and a sign, or a demonstration that the strata cannot be separated in this frame. A null is informative: it removes the "blinding distorts the error budget" half of the cost argument that the 2023 critique makes from anecdote.

MANDATORY "already computed" check (the candidate's calibration-factor limb): the MARGINAL quantity has been computed and published repeatedly, and I report the numbers. Bailey 2017 fits a Student-t to pairwise normalized differences over ~41,000 measurements of ~3,200 quantities and gets tail index nu ~ 2-3 ("almost-Cauchy"), with observed P(z>5) = 0.12 for interlaboratory data against ~6e-7 Gaussian expectation. PDG publishes per-quantity scale factors as routine practice. Klein & Roodman's own Fig. 2 gives chi2 = 131.2 for 83 dof around prior published averages, i.e. chi2/dof = 1.58, implying an error-inflation factor of about 1.26 (MY OWN ARITHMETIC from their stated chi2 and dof, carrying no uncertainty they did not state). So the claim "nobody has measured the calibration of physicists' error bars" would be FALSE and is not claimed here. What is unmeasured is the contrast between blinding strata.

PRIOR A. Bailey, "Not Normal: the uncertainties of scientific measurements", R. Soc. Open Sci. 2017 — read at https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1612.00778 (arXiv:1612.00778).
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS: the pair of independent measurements of the same quantity (99,433 pairs over 5,580 Cochrane measurements/310 quantities; 53,988 pairs over 8,469 Review of Particle Physics measurements/864 quantities; 66,677 over 12,380 radionuclide measurements/1,437 quantities; 965,416 over 14,097 interlaboratory measurements/617 quantities).
- ENDPOINT: the distribution of z_ij = |x_i - x_j| / sqrt(u_i^2 + u_j^2), fitted as a non-standardized Student-t with degrees of freedom nu and scale sigma.
- REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: none for the quantity — the paper states z "does not require a reference value". The comparator is the Normal distribution, and secondarily the four research areas against each other.
- ESTIMAND: the tail index nu (~2-3) and core width sigma of the pull-pair distribution; the frequency of >5-sigma disagreements (0.12 for interlab vs ~6e-7 Gaussian; "up to five orders of magnitude more frequent than naively expected"). Levy-stable alternatives fit 4-30x worse in chi2.
- Stratified by: research area; an era subset ("newer measurements made since the year 2000"); quantities with >=10 measurements; BIPM key comparisons and stable particle properties as a quality tier. NOT by collaboration. Direct reading: "blind", "blinding", "mask", "masked", "salt" and "hidden signal region" do not appear; expectation bias is mentioned only as a narrative explanation ("prior experimental or theoretical expectations which may influence results"), never as a stratifying covariate; and for interlab data the authors argue "there should be no expectation, selection, or publication biases."

PRIOR B. Klein & Roodman, "Blind Analysis in Nuclear and Particle Physics", Annu. Rev. Nucl. Part. Sci. 55 (2005) 141-163 — read at https://www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/bias/2005-klein.pdf.
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS: the individual historical measurement, in four measurement histories (neutron lifetime, K0S lifetime, a particle mass, gA/gV); the dof (83) imply ~85 measurements.
- ENDPOINT: deviation of each measurement from the published average existing BEFORE it, and from the final average; summarised as a chi2 for normality around each.
- REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: the prior published average versus the current/final average — a comparator pair, not a blinding contrast.
- ESTIMAND: central-value herding — whether measurements cluster nearer the prior average than the final value. chi2 = 131.2/83 dof around prior averages vs 249.7/~82 dof around the final average; the authors' own verdict is "Although the effect is not striking, the measurements do tend to cluster nearer the prior published averages than the final value", caveated with "that would not necessarily mean that the experimental results were biased by experimenters' concerns" and "we cannot say conclusively whether bias has influenced measurements in nuclear and particle physics."
- Contains NO dataset or statistical analysis comparing blinded with unblinded outcomes. Its only blinded-vs-prior comparison is the single SNO anecdote (4.90 vs 5.09 x10^6 cm^-2 s^-1, "in excellent agreement"). On uncertainties it says only "we are conservative in our estimates of systematic uncertainties" (as a motivation for open analysis) and "the size of an experimenter's bias cannot be estimated"; it never addresses error-bar growth or shrinkage after unblinding.

PRIOR C. Moustgaard et al., "Impact of blinding on estimated treatment effects in randomised clinical trials: meta-epidemiological study", BMJ 2020;368:l6802 — read at https://jameslindlibrary.org/wp-data/uploads/2024/03/Moustgaard-2020.pdf.
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS: the Cochrane meta-analysis (142 meta-analyses containing 1,153 trials), with blinded and non-blinded trials contrasted WITHIN each meta-analysis and combined across them by a Bayesian hierarchical model.
- ENDPOINT: the log odds ratio (continuous outcomes as SMDs re-expressed as ORs); secondarily kappa, the SD increase in between-trial heterogeneity, and phi, the SD of mean bias across meta-analyses.
- REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: blinded trials are the reference group; non-blinded and unclear-status trials are compared against them.
- ESTIMAND: ROR = OR(non-blinded)/OR(blinded) — e.g. analysis Ia, ROR 0.91 (95% CrI 0.61 to 1.34); plus kappa by analysis (Ia 0.22, 0.02-0.60; Ib 0.10; IIa 0.06; IIb 0.10; III 0.05).
- Reading confirms it does NOT compare standard errors, CI widths or coverage between strata, does not use tau^2 or I^2, and does not assess calibration of reported uncertainty. Its heterogeneity verdict is explicitly null-for-lack-of-information: "Information was insufficient to determine whether lack of blinding was associated with increased heterogeneity between trials", with "In all instances the credible intervals were wide, including both considerable difference and no difference." The additive model's statement that heterogeneity among non-blinded trials "can only increase (or remain unchanged)" is a model constraint, not a finding.

Significance — consequence:
If YES with a sign, it converts a contested cost claim into a measured one. The 2023 critique argues from three anecdotes that blinding is expensive partly because it distorts the error budget; a measured ratio of calibration factors either supplies that argument with a number or removes half of it. If blinded uncertainties are measurably more conservative, review bodies can price blinding's cost in units of lost precision instead of arguing it; if they are better calibrated, blinding gains a second, independent justification that does not depend on the unresolved central-value question. If NO (ratio indistinguishable from 1), the error-budget half of the cost argument is retired and the blinding debate collapses back onto the central-value limb alone, which is where the evidence is already known to be absent. If the strata cannot be separated from era and collaboration size, that itself is a publishable constraint on what the historical record can ever settle about analysis practice — and it tells the field that the question is only answerable prospectively, by recording blinding protocol as structured metadata from now on.
Honest discount: the usable N is the weak point. The intersection of (quantity with a decisive later reference) x (measurement with ascertainable blinding status) x (both strata present within quantity) may leave a frame small enough that the confidence interval includes both directions — the same failure Moustgaard hit on kappa with 1,153 trials. A wide null would be much less informative than a sharp one.

Significance — audience:
(i) Particle and nuclear collaborations and their internal review bodies deciding whether and how deeply to blind: they currently choose between protocols on the strength of a techniques review and anecdote, and would act on a signed calibration contrast by adjusting how much systematic-budget conservatism they accept as blinding's price. (ii) The compilers of measurement averages — the PDG specifically, which already publishes per-quantity scale factors and whose error-inflation practice is an explicit live choice (arXiv:2004.01219 proposes an alternative to it). A blinding-stratified calibration factor is directly actionable there: it says whether the inflation should depend on analysis protocol, which is a change to a published number millions of downstream citations use. This is the audience that makes the candidate more than trivia, because the decision already exists and is already parameterised. (iii) Metrology, where the same statistic is already named (Birge ratio, "dark uncertainty") and where blinded inter-laboratory evaluation is an available but unpriced option. (iv) By transfer, any field now debating blinded evaluation whose current evidence base, like Moustgaard's, is central-value-only.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: does the effect, if present, live in the statistical or the systematic component of the budget (the split is published, so this is immediately estimable)? Does calibration scale with blinding DEPTH (hidden offset vs salted data vs hidden signal region), which would make the exposure a dose rather than a binary? Does blinding status predict the magnitude of a measurement's own LATER revision, a different and sharper endpoint than scatter? Does the tail index, as distinct from the core width, move with blinding — i.e. does blinding change the rate of gross outliers rather than the typical error bar, which is the component Bailey shows dominates the surprise? And prospectively: should blinding protocol be recorded as structured metadata in compilations such as the Review of Particle Physics so that this question becomes answerable without hand-coding?
Anchoring: Bailey's unexplained almost-Cauchy tails (nu ~ 2-3) across all four corpora, for which no mechanism is established; the PDG scale-factor practice and the live proposal to replace it; the neutron-lifetime beam-bottle discrepancy of 4-8 sigma, a standing anomaly that is by construction a statement about quoted uncertainties; Klein & Roodman's unresolved herding chi2; and the explicit standing claim, in the 2023 critique, that "there is surprisingly little evidence for experimenter bias in physics research."

Reason for silence:
Two mechanisms, both structural, neither "no one thought of it".
(1) Disciplinary boundary with an inverted framing. Physics's own meta-measurement tradition treats quoted-uncertainty understatement as a NUISANCE TO BE CORRECTED, not an outcome to be EXPLAINED: the PDG's response to excess scatter is to multiply the error by a scale factor and move on, and Bailey's response is to fit a heavier-tailed distribution. In that framing there is no reason to regress the calibration factor on anything, so no exposure variable is ever sought. The machinery for regressing a calibration statistic on a methodological covariate within strata is owned by clinical meta-epidemiology (Moustgaard's within-meta-analysis contrast is exactly it), and that community has no reason to look at particle data. Bailey is the one author who did touch the physics corpora with a statistician's tools, and read directly, the word "blind" does not occur in the paper.
(2) The exposure variable does not exist in machine-readable form, and nobody owns the cost of creating it. Compilations record values, uncertainties, dates and references, but not analysis protocol; blinding status would have to be hand-coded from hundreds of primary papers with inconsistent reporting, especially pre-2000. A collaboration has no incentive to fund a study whose output is a judgement on its own community's error bars, and an outside metascientist lacks the domain access to code the protocols. This is an incentive-plus-missing-metadata gap, and it predicts exactly the asymmetry observed: the HOW literature, written by practitioners, is dense; the measurement, which needs an outsider's method and an insider's coding, is absent.
Secondary: the normative load. Blinding is now close to a professional virtue in precision physics, and a finding that blinded uncertainty budgets are systematically inflated would read as an argument against a practice the field has adopted for good independent reasons — a mild taboo that makes the question unattractive to ask from inside even where the data sit.
