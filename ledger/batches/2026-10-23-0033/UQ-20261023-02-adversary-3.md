## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Are the boxes we call "constant conditions" actually running on a daily schedule of their own? Concretely: inside a light-tight DD or LL circadian recording enclosure, is there a real once-per-day wobble in heat, shaking, noise, CO2, moisture or human traffic; if there is, is the thermal part of it big enough to tug or mask the oscillator for the preparation being recorded; and does that wobble explain why the same genotype's free-running period comes out at different values in different cabinets and different buildings? Limb (a) is a measurement of the room's own day. Limb (b) is an apportionment: how much of the chamber-to-chamber spread in tau is the building rather than the animal.

## Single strongest killing point

**Milligan, Sales & Khirnykh (1993), *Physiology & Behavior* 53:1067–1076, "Sound levels in rooms housing laboratory animals: An uncontrolled daily variable"** — a primary measurement paper that monitored the sound channel of laboratory animal rooms over periods of up to 24 hours and concluded, in its abstract as served, that "the acoustic environment of laboratory animals is a daily variable that is usually uncontrolled". That is limb (a) for one channel, named in the candidate's own terms, 33 years before the candidate, in a behavioural-physiology journal, and the record's sweep did not return it. **The leg this verdict would stand on if the others fell:** not that Milligan measures tau (it does not) and not that it prints a 24-h amplitude (at my read level it does not, and I say so), but that the candidate's framing — that this worry survives only as a rhetorical question in outreach prose and a "can" in non-peer-reviewed methods guidance — is false: it has a measured, published, titled precedent for one channel that no adversary or gate in this record had seen.

## What I could not close, and what kind of document would close it

The open attack is **Disguise**. The kind of document that would settle it: a **primary measurement** paper that logs at least one of the six channels at or inside a circadian recording enclosure (or, at minimum, an animal room) over >=24 h at a resolution that resolves a daily cycle, and that **prints** a 24-hour amplitude or a 24-hour profile from which one can be read, with units — ideally alongside any period measurement. **Milligan 1993 is that kind of document** for the sound channel at the room site, and I know that from the abstract's own account of what it monitored and concluded, not from its title: ten pages, monitoring "up to 24 hours", the daily profile as its stated object. What I do not know is whether it prints a fitted amplitude — which is precisely the published-is-not-performed question — so this is a **nomination, not a finding**. The promotion condition for this entry is therefore specific: obtain Milligan 1993 at the publisher (no ScienceDirect route appeared in any of my nine queries and neither aggregator served a DOI, so this needs a route I did not have) and determine whether it prints a 24-h sound amplitude; then open the two Springer legacy records from query 1, which are the likeliest home of a classical German-language prior, and Czeisler 1999, which is the nearest answer-side precedent in any field.
