Domain(s): Medicine — diagnostic safety; measurement of diagnostic error; chart-review methodology. Method-owning neighbours: diagnostic-test-accuracy methodology (review/incorporation bias, QUADAS-2), psychometrics (criterion contamination), patient-safety record-review methodology (hindsight bias), clinical informatics (copy-forward propagation), and — found only by the adversaries — radiology/pathology peer-review QA and forensic blind verification.

Question (canonical): On one case set reviewed twice under a pre-registered protocol, what is the signed difference in the classified diagnostic-error rate between (A) standard unblinded chart review with the full record available, and (B) review in which the index encounter's own diagnostic assertions and every downstream repetition traceable to them are redacted, while subsequently generated findings, test results and diagnoses not traceable to the index assertion are retained — reported with its uncertainty, a measured index of blinding success, and the same contrast computed on a subset whose diagnosis is anchored to an index-independent reference (autopsy, mandated external re-adjudication, or a registry with independent ascertainment) to fix the bias direction?

Alternative formulations:
- (lay) Does diagnostic momentum hide diagnostic error from the very instruments built to count it?
- (counterfactual) If the same cases were re-adjudicated with the initial diagnosis redacted, how far would the published diagnostic-error rate move, and in which direction?
- (method field's words) Is the reference standard of diagnostic-error chart review conditionally dependent on the index assertion it is used to judge, and what is the magnitude of the resulting review/incorporation bias in the estimated error rate?
(Gate 1 reformulation used once, for well-posedness only; intake version and rationale in the record.)

Motivating gap: The field is dense with instruments for measuring diagnostic error (Safer Dx and Revised Safer Dx, e-triggers, Global Trigger Tool, SPADE look-back/look-forward, missed-opportunity and care-transition reviews) and thin on whether the measurement is identified. All take later recorded diagnoses as truth. The field names the mechanism that would corrupt that truth in its own journal (Blume, "The inherited diagnosis and diagnostic error", Diagnosis 2026) without drawing the measurement consequence. The Safer Dx validation paper (opened) names hindsight bias in its limitations, does not estimate it, and blinds its reviewer to the error outcome while supplying up to a year of subsequent EHR — blinded to the wrong variable for this purpose. No validation paper found reports a redaction arm.

Presuppositions:
- P1. Diagnostic labels propagate, so the later diagnosis is not independent of the index one. ESTABLISHED as a named phenomenon in the candidate's own literature (Blume 2026, abstract opened); magnitude unmeasured and is the thing at issue.
- P2. Chart-review instruments use subsequent documentation as their reference standard. PARTLY ESTABLISHED, and narrowed at Gate 4: holds by construction for look-back/look-forward and coded-diagnosis e-trigger designs; overstated for Safer Dx, whose items judge the appropriateness of the index diagnostic process and use later EHR "to help determine the clinical context" (adversary 1, opened).
- P3. A redaction protocol can remove index assertions and their inheritance while leaving the presenting features reviewers need. MOST CONTESTED and conceded circular: tagging text as "inherited" presupposes knowing what was independently ascertained, the quantity at issue. If the later diagnosis IS the inheritance, arm B has no reference. Bracketed, not solved, by pre-registered tagging rules, reported inter-annotator agreement, and arm B with and without borderline text.
- P4. Some subset has an index-independent reference to fix direction. PARTLY ESTABLISHED; such subsets are small and selected, so direction is fixed only locally.
- P5. Reviewers can be kept blind in practice. CONTESTED; style, test ordering and referral patterns leak the index impression, so blinding success must be measured, not asserted.
- P6. The classified error rate is the quantity consumers of this literature use. PLAUSIBLE; burden estimates, dashboards and instrument-validation accuracy are built from per-record classifications.
- P7 (added at Gate 4, adversary 2). That a "true" error rate exists for the arms to be biased relative to. CONTESTED: the field's own position is that there is no gold standard for diagnostic error, so the arm difference may be a difference between two conventions. Answered only by the anchored subset.

What would count as an answer: A paired re-adjudication on one case set with two blinded arms — full chart versus chart with index assertions and traceable repetitions redacted — reporting the difference in classified error rate with uncertainty, a measured blinding-success index, inter-annotator agreement on the inheritance tagging, and the same contrast on an index-independent anchored subset; plus restatement of at least one published rate as a bound with stated tightness. A null licenses existing rates against this channel. A large difference converts every published rate into a bound. A sign reversal between anchored subset and full set indicates redaction removes diagnostic evidence rather than contamination — it falsifies P3 rather than answering the question (flagged by both adversaries as rhetorical insurance; see attack 5).

Significance — consequence: If the difference is large, every chart-review-derived diagnostic-error rate becomes a bound of newly estimated looseness in a known direction, and burden estimates and institutional dashboards inherit the correction; the usual defence that reviewer noise attenuates rather than biases fails, because contamination by the index assertion is signed. Remedies are then cheap and specifiable: a redaction arm as a required validation element and a reported blinding domain in diagnostic-safety measurement reporting. A null licenses existing rates against this channel and replaces a ritual caveat with an estimate. A sign reversal indicates the instruments are not identifiable from available records at all and must be anchored externally.
Significance — audience: AHRQ's diagnostic-safety measurement programme; the Safer Dx / Revised Safer Dx developers, whose own limitations section names the worry and who already hold a suitable case set; SPADE and e-trigger developers, whose designs take the later coded diagnosis as truth by construction; Global Trigger Tool and missed-opportunity reviewers, who could add a blinding-success measure at near-zero cost; editors applying STARD/QUADAS-style appraisal, who have no signalling question for this instrument class. Evidenced rather than asserted at Gate 4: a leading measurement group ran exactly this methodological experiment on its own instrument in another substrate and placed it in a top specialty journal (Elmore 2022 [CITATION NOT LOCATABLE — see Verification V2]).
Significance — fecundity: Is contamination larger where label propagation is heaviest (primary care, psychiatry, chronic-disease labels) than where each encounter re-derives its assessment (emergency medicine, pathology)? Does ambient AI documentation increase or decrease inheritance, making pre- and post-ambient rates incomparable? Can inheritance be tagged programmatically, turning the redaction arm into a corpus-scale instrument? Is the reviewer's noise floor itself index-dependent, which would recast this literature's inter-rater-reliability results? Does the anchored-subset contrast yield a transportable correction factor or only a local one?
