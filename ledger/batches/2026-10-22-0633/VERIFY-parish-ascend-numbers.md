### (e) SAMPLING FRAME — confirmed, with one correction and one two-stage nuance

Confirmed numbers, from Methods and Abstract as served:

- **15,480** randomised in ASCEND: "The ASCEND 2x2 factorial randomized trial, in 15,480 people with
  diabetes but no occlusive arterial disease".
- **Random sample of 152**: "A random subgroup of 152 participants (balanced by treatment
  allocation) with urine samples at both baseline and" / "follow-up was selected for U-TXM assay."
  Abstract: "a randomly selected sample of 152 participants (76 aspirin arm, 74 placebo arm)".
- **Additional purposive adherent sample of 198**: "U-TXM at follow-up was later assayed in a further
  198 participants who reported being adherent to their study" / "tablets (the 98 who reported taking
  their tablet more than 12 hours before their sample and 100 selected at random)." Abstract: "plus
  198 (93 aspirin arm, 105 placebo arm) adherent to study drugs" / "and selected to maximize the
  numbers ingesting their last tablet 12-24 hours before urine sampling."

So: 152 random (74 placebo) + 198 purposive-adherent (105 placebo), out of 15,480 randomised — the
sub-claim's arithmetic is right.

Two things the sub-claim's framing gets wrong or leaves out:

1. **The 7% rests on the RANDOM sample, not the purposive one.** 5/74 is the Random-sample ITT
   placebo cell. The purposive sample's placebo positivity is the separate **4/105 (4%)**, and the
   combined **5/168 (3%)** is the one that mixes random with purposive. The caution "a purposive
   sample's positivity rate is not a trial-wide prevalence" therefore attaches to the 4% and the 3%,
   **not** to the 7%.
2. **The 152/150 discrepancy.** 152 were *selected*; Table 1's random-sample row reports **N = 150**
   (76 aspirin, 74 placebo), the Methods stating two participants were excluded because baseline
   values were above the assay range. So "74 placebo" is the post-exclusion assayed number and the
   two excluded both came from the placebo arm (the selection was "balanced by treatment allocation",
   i.e. 76/76 selected). That is my inference from the served numbers; the paper as served does not
   state which arm the two exclusions fell in.
3. **The randomisation is two-stage.** The 152 were a random subgroup *of participants with urine at
   both baseline and follow-up*, and follow-up mail collection itself went to "a random sample of
   around 10% participants". So the frame is: random ~10% resample → those returning both samples →
   random 152 → 150 assayed. Each stage is random or near-random, but the final 74 placebo is not a
   simple random draw from 7,740 placebo-allocated participants, and non-return of a mailed sample is
   not addressed in anything I was served.

### (f) CONCORDANCE against the participant's own form — stratifies and tests, never measures agreement

The honest answer is neither "yes" nor "neither". It does something in between, and the distinction
decides whether the gap is open.

What it **does**: it classifies participants by what their own sampling form said, and reports the
assay result within those classes. Methods, as served, record "adherence to study aspirin/placebo
tablets (reported on the sampling form) and non-study aspirin use", and define the non-adherent class
as "non-adherent (last tablet date earlier than the day before sampling, or known to be taking
non-study aspirin)". Table 1 then prints the assay outcome in each class — placebo arm: adherent
1/63 (2%) versus non-adherent 4/9 (44%) — and the ‡ footnote attaches **P<0.0001 for heterogeneity
in the difference by adherence to randomized treatment.** So a stratification by the form, and a
formal test that the assay result differs across the form's classes, are both **performed and
printed**.

What it **does not do**: it never frames any of this as agreement between assay and form, and prints
**no agreement statistic of any kind** — no kappa, no sensitivity or specificity, no concordance or
percentage-agreement figure, and no count of participants whose assay contradicts their own form
presented as such. The closest sentence in the paper runs the other way round, reporting suppression
within a self-reported class rather than agreement: "Among participants reporting adherence to
aspirin, 86% in the random sample and 71% in the additional adherent sample".

So, in the ledger's own vocabulary: the paper **MEASURES** suppression by form-declared class and
**TESTS** heterogeneity across those classes; it **neither DEFINES nor MEASURES** agreement between
the assay and the form. A reader can compute a discordance count from Table 1 — in the random sample,
4 of the 5 suppressed placebo participants were already flagged non-adherent by the form, leaving
**1/63** form-declared-adherent placebo participants contradicted by the assay — but **that
subtraction is the reader's arithmetic; the source does not print it as a discordance or as a measure
of the form's accuracy.**

Also absent: **"non-study aspirin" never appears with a count or a percentage.** It appears only in
the Methods definition, "known to be taking non-study aspirin". How many of the 15,480, or of the 74,
or of the 4/9, were taking non-study aspirin is not printed anywhere I was served, and the Discussion
does not mention non-study aspirin use in the placebo group at all.

### (g) RE-ESTIMATION of the treatment effect — No

No. The paper does not re-estimate, adjust or correct ASCEND's treatment effect for the measured
placebo-arm positivity or for non-adherence. Its statistical analysis section estimates effects on
the **biomarker** only: "Effective suppression and differences in log U-TXM by treatment allocation
were analyzed..." (logistic and linear regression). The cardiovascular effect enters only as a cited
result from the main paper, used to support an inference:

> "the finding of a statistically significant 12% proportional reduction in the primary" /
> "cardiovascular outcome with aspirin in ASCEND, shows that the suppression achieved was sufficient
> to be beneficial."

(two consecutive fragments; reconstruction)

That is an argument from the published effect to the adequacy of suppression. It is not an adjusted
analysis, and no adjusted or compliance-corrected estimate appears. The Abstract's Conclusions, as
served, make no claim about the trial's effect size at all: "Daily aspirin significantly reduces
U-TXM in participants with diabetes, including at 12-24 hours after ingestion."

The Discussion as served also does **not** say the samples were small, non-random or
unrepresentative, does **not** discuss non-adherence or its implications for the trial, and states
one limitation only: "However, a limitation of the present study was that it did not include
different dosing schedules."

## REGIME

What the source establishes, and where its numbers are and are not valid.

- **Population**: ASCEND participants — adults with diabetes and no history of occlusive arterial
  disease / cardiovascular disease, UK, within a 2x2 factorial randomised double-blind trial of
  aspirin 100 mg once daily versus placebo.
- **Unit of analysis**: one participant, one mailed spot urine, at **one** follow-up occasion a mean
  of 2 years after randomisation. Not person-time, not repeated measures, not clinic visits.
- **Instrument**: competitive ELISA for 11-dehydro-thromboxane B2, expressed per mg creatinine.
- **Threshold**: U-TXM < 1500 pg/mg creatinine = "effective suppression". This is the authors' own
  cut-point and every percentage above is at that threshold.
- **Endpoints**: (i) binary effective suppression, analysed by logistic regression; (ii) percentage
  reduction in U-TXM, from linear regression on log U-TXM. **Comparator**: randomised allocation
  (aspirin vs placebo). **Estimand for the headline 7%**: the proportion of *allocated-placebo*
  participants in the random sample whose single follow-up urine fell below 1500 pg/mg creatinine —
  an intention-to-treat proportion in a biomarker sub-sample.
- **Scope of the 7%**: n = 74 placebo, random sample, ITT, single occasion. It is the positivity rate
  of one assay at one threshold on one mailed sample in a randomly drawn sub-sample, with **no
  printed confidence interval**. It is **not** a rate of concealed non-adherence (4 of its 5
  positives were form-declared non-adherent), not a rate of non-study aspirin use (never counted),
  and not a trial-wide prevalence over 15,480 or over person-time.
- **Scope of the 3%** (5/168): adherent participants only, **random plus purposive samples
  combined**. Conditional on a form-declared adherence class, and its denominator is mostly
  purposive (105 of 168).
- **Scope of the 4%** (4/105): purposive-adherent sample only. Selected to load the 12–24 h window.
  Not a prevalence in any population.
- **Silent outside this**: the paper says nothing about other aspirin doses or dosing schedules (its
  stated limitation), nothing about non-diabetic populations, nothing about any occasion other than
  the ~2-year mailed one, and nothing about how the biomarker relates to the trial's clinical effect
  beyond the one inferential sentence citing the main paper.

## DEFECTS FOUND

1. **(a) Wrong location, and a quotation cut before its own qualifier.** The string "82% allocated
   aspirin versus 7% allocated placebo achieved effective suppression of U-TXM (Table 1)" is verbatim
   in the paper but it is in **Results paragraph 2**, not the Results first paragraph (which is about
   baseline balance). More consequentially, the printed sentence opens **"During follow-up, in the
   intention-to-treat analysis of the random sample,"** — the quotation begins exactly after the
   clause that names the regime. The grammar on the left of the cut is where the sample and the
   estimand live. Quoted as handed to me, the 82%/7% reads as a trial-wide ITT result; in the source
   it is scoped to the random sub-sample's ITT analysis.
2. **(b) Verbatim prefix, cut mid-predicate.** "Only 3% of participants adherent to placebo had
   effective suppression" is a true prefix of "...had effective suppression **of U-TXM at
   follow-up**." The completion is innocuous. The material omission is elsewhere: "adherent to
   placebo" is the **combined** 168, 105 of whom came from the purposive sample.
3. **(e) The random/purposive attribution is inverted in the sub-claim's framing.** The warning "a
   purposive sample's positivity rate is not a trial-wide prevalence" was aimed at the 7%. The 7% is
   the **random** sample's figure. The purposive figure is 4% (4/105); the mixed figure is 3%
   (5/168). Any argument that discounts the 7% as purposive is reading the wrong row.
4. **(c) 152 is not 150, and not 74+76.** 152 were selected balanced by allocation; Table 1 reports
   N=150 with 74 placebo, two excluded for baseline above the assay range. Citing "a random sample of
   152 (74 placebo)" merges the selected and the assayed counts. (Also, by my arithmetic, two placebo
   participants in that sample appear in no adherence row: 9 + 63 = 72, not 74.)
5. **(f) Stratification is not concordance — but "neither" is also wrong.** The paper does print the
   assay outcome cross-classified by the participant's own form (placebo: adherent 1/63 vs
   non-adherent 4/9) and does print a heterogeneity test on it (P<0.0001). It prints **no** agreement
   statistic. Reporting this source as having "no comparison at all" against the form understates it;
   reporting it as having concorded assay against form overstates it. The precise gap is an
   **agreement/accuracy measure**, not a cross-tabulation.
6. **Published is not performed, in the direction that matters here.** The paper **MEASURES** the
   placebo-arm positivity and **PRINTS** the number (5/74, 7%, Table 1, Random sample / All row,
   placebo "Percentage achieving effective suppression" column). So a claim that the quantity is
   unpublished fails. But it **never prints** a discordance count, a kappa, a count of non-study
   aspirin users, or an adjusted treatment effect — those it does not even define.
7. **No uncertainty on the suppression percentages.** Table 1 gives 95% CIs for geometric means and
   percentage reductions only. The 7% and the 3% are printed bare. Any interval downstream is the
   downstream author's arithmetic.
8. **Front matter not fully established.** The ORA PDF as served carries no journal/volume/article
   line and no dates; the publisher page served those date fields blank and no article-type label. So
   "Trials 2023;24:166" is confirmed at the publisher page, but the Received/Accepted/Published dates
   and the masthead type label are not confirmed at either route. Two smaller front-matter points:
   the real title contains "(A Study of Cardiovascular Events iN Diabetes)", which the short form
   drops; and the title page gives "Michael R. Hill", not "Hill M".
9. **A served-text variance I did not resolve.** The Table 1 caption came back as "11-dehydro
   thromboxane" from the PDF and "11-dehydro-thromboxane" from the publisher page. Reported as
   served, not corrected.
10. **Silence noted as silence.** The Discussion does not mention non-study aspirin in the placebo
    group, does not discuss non-adherence's implications for the trial, and does not claim the samples
    were unrepresentative. Its only stated limitation is the absence of other dosing schedules. That
    silence is not a prohibition on anyone else doing the concordance or the adjustment, and the
    dosing-schedule limitation is the absence of a **different** thing — it is not a flag of the
    concordance or re-estimation gap.

## BEARING ON THE LEDGER ENTRY

The attack on the Provisional entry rests on this paper's numbers, so what it decides is narrow and
can be stated exactly.

**What the paper does close.** It publishes a measured placebo-arm positivity for aspirin-pattern
thromboxane suppression in a randomised trial, with its denominator, at a stated threshold: **5/74
(7%)** intention-to-treat in a **random** sub-sample, Table 1, "Random sample / All" row, placebo
"Percentage achieving effective suppression" column, at U-TXM < 1500 pg/mg creatinine. Any version of
the entry that says no such quantity has been printed is refuted by this source. The figure is from a
random sample, so the purposive-sample objection does not reach it.

**What the paper does not close, and this is where the attack fails.** Two things the ledger entry's
gap turns on are absent:

- **(f) No agreement measure.** The paper stratifies the assay result by the participant's own
  sampling form and tests heterogeneity across those strata (P<0.0001), but prints no kappa, no
  sensitivity/specificity, no concordance figure, and no count of participants whose assay
  contradicts their own form framed as such. The discordance a reader wants — form-declared-adherent
  placebo participants whom the assay contradicts — is computable from Table 1 as **1/63** in the
  random sample and **5/168** combined, but **that is the reader's subtraction, not the paper's
  printed figure**, and the paper never evaluates the form's accuracy. A cross-tabulation at a
  coarser grain than the question is a granularity gap, not a closure.
- **(g) No re-estimation.** The paper never re-estimates ASCEND's treatment effect adjusted for the
  measured positivity. It cites the published 12% proportional reduction — a figure it **attributes
  to the main ASCEND paper** — and infers that "the suppression achieved was sufficient to be
  beneficial". An inference from a published effect is not an adjusted analysis. Nothing in the paper
  defines, bounds or performs one.

**What decides the cap.** If the cap is set from the 7%, note what that number is and is not. It is
one mailed spot urine per person at a single occasion a mean of 2 years after randomisation, n=74
placebo, ITT, **with no printed confidence interval** — so any interval around it is downstream
arithmetic, not the source's. And **4 of its 5 positives were already flagged non-adherent by the
participant's own form** (4/9, 44%), so the 7% is overwhelmingly accounted for by declared
non-adherence rather than by concealed exposure. The residual that a cap on *undetected* placebo-arm
aspirin exposure should rest on is the adherent-stratum cell: **1/63 (2%)** in the random sample, or
**5/168 (3%)** if the purposive sample is pooled in — and that pooled 3% is mostly purposive and is
not a prevalence. A cap built on 7% as a rate of hidden exposure would be using a number outside the
regime its authors proved it in; a cap built on 2–3% is inside the regime but rests on 1 and 5
events respectively, with no uncertainty supplied by the source.

**Recommended disposition.** The attack is **not sustained** on (f) or (g): this source neither
concords the assay against the participant's form as an agreement measure nor re-estimates the
treatment effect, so the Provisional entry's gap survives in those two respects. The attack **is
sustained** against any formulation of the entry claiming that placebo-arm positivity has never been
measured and printed with a denominator — it has, here, in a random sample. Two of the quotations
carried into the record need correcting before the entry is finalised: the 82%/7% string is in
Results paragraph 2 and must carry its own opening clause "During follow-up, in the intention-to-treat
analysis of the random sample,"; and the "Only 3%" figure must carry its denominator of 168 adherent
placebo participants, 105 of them purposively selected.
