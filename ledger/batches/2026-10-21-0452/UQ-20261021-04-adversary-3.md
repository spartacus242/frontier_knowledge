## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Since 2026 payers must publish prior-authorization approval, denial and appeal percentages, all computed on requests that actually reached (or were adjudicated by) the payer. The candidate asks: of the clinical decisions that *would* have needed a prior authorization, what share never becomes a request at all because the provider gives up, substitutes or does not bother; and, payer by payer, is that pre-submission attrition higher where the published denial rate is lower (so that a "good" denial rate partly means "nobody asks")? Third limb: if you fold attrition back in, in what share of payer×service cells does the ordering of payers by "obstruction" reverse, against a flip threshold fixed in advance? Plainer: is a low published PA denial rate partly a selection artifact of who bothers to apply, and does that scramble the payer rankings already being published?

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional.** No attack is Unresolved on 1, 2 or 4, so no Rejection; Validated is barred by (i) a close neighbour the record missed — Brot-Goldberg et al. 2023 occupies the framing limb and limb 1 for drugs, making novelty "Medium with a close neighbour" — and (ii) the record's own well-posedness of 2/3, which Gate 5 requires at 3/3. The record's demotion condition on the unread KFF 13 Aug 2026 analysis is NOT triggered (read via republished copy; it says nothing about care never requested).

**Single strongest killing point:** the candidate's own field has already asked and answered the framing question and executed the P5 classification, in a randomized design — "These effects are quite large, and refute claims that high prior authorization approval rates mean that prior authorization has little significance for actual utilization" (Brot-Goldberg et al., NBER w30878, Sec. 3.3, reconstruction from two consecutive segments) — while naming exactly the gap the candidate proposes to fill ("nor can we compute rejection rates"). Scope limit stated: Part D drugs, which the CMS-0057-F metric excludes, and no cross-plan comparison by approval rate; so it reduces novelty rather than extinguishing it.

**What would promote it:** (1) open the services-side sentinel-effect literature surfaced in query 10 (ACR "Cost Saving/Shifting"; Radiology Business "Non-denial RBM program still effective…"; RSNA 2009 abstract 8003072) and confirm none pairs deterrence with cross-payer denial rates; (2) read Brot-Goldberg Sections 6–7 (truncated in my read) for any statement on denial-rate transparency; (3) pre-declare the three sign outcomes and a stringency-vs-friction decomposition; (4) either drop the "obstruction ranking" limb or define obstruction descriptively; (5) resolve the cross-service limb so well-posedness reaches 3/3; (6) record the novelty grade as Medium-with-close-neighbour, naming F-A, and correct the record's "nothing on the provider side" and "no one asked" statements.
