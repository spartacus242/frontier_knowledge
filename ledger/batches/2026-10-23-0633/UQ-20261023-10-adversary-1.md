## Restatement (my own words)

When an inpatient's kidney-cleared drug is being given below its labelled full dose because their clearance sat under the label's renal threshold, and their clearance then climbs back over that threshold and stays there a day: how long until the full dose is actually reinstated (cumulative incidence, with drug stop / discharge / death as competing events), how often it never is, how much cumulative labelled dose is forfeited in the meantime, was the omission charted as a deliberate choice or not, and does each site's renal-dosing alert logic contain any rule that fires on a too-LOW dose at a recovered clearance?

## Sources graded on

SOURCE A — Gupta and colleagues (Cardinal Health), "Epidemiology and outcomes of patients with changes in renal function during hospitalization that may require drug dosage adjustment", ISPOR 13th Annual International Meeting, 2008; Value in Health 11(3) (abstract). Author list beyond the first name was not returned by the summariser; I do not reconstruct it. READ LEVEL: abstract-is-the-document, received as a SUMMARISER SUMMARY, not verbatim — the summariser declined a verbatim segment-by-segment reproduction, and I did not have a fetch left to ask for short fragments. Every number below is as the summariser reported it.
REGIME: population = 1,011,055 non-dialysis admissions with >= 2 serum creatinine values, 74 US hospitals, 2003-2006; unit = admissions; instrument = modified Cockcroft-Gault CrCl in four strata (normal/mild/moderate/advanced); threshold = movement of >= 1 stratum; category = share of admissions whose CrCl improved / worsened / was unchanged, plus unadjusted mortality and median LOS by group. NOT a measurement of any dosing action.
Numbers as reported: unchanged 79.2%, worsened 11.6%, IMPROVED 9.2% of admissions; improved group mortality 1.8% (95% CI 1.7-1.9), median LOS 4 days. Summariser's answers to my direct questions: time to dose adjustment — not reported; doses increased — not stated, the abstract refers only to "potential dosage adjustment" (summariser-reported fragment) for both directions.
WHAT IT DOES AND DOES NOT DO FOR THE CANDIDATE: it establishes, at scale, that the candidate's triggering event (within-admission clearance improvement across a dosing band) is common — roughly one admission in eleven — which is a denominator fact the record did not have. It does not define, bound, predict or measure restoration, time to restoration, or exposure deficit; its medication limb is counterfactual need ("may require"), the same object as Eppenga's. Scope: strata are not label thresholds, so 9.2% is a neighbour of the candidate's inclusion rate, not the rate itself.

UNVERIFIED: I opened zero labels (curl to DailyMed was refused; no fetch remained). My own reasoning, not a finding: this is a SELECTION clause, so it needs only a non-empty list, and if most labels print indication-conditional ranges the harm is to limb (c)'s breadth, not to its existence. The record already flags this; it stays the promotion condition for limb (c).
C3. "Measured clearance" is routinely available. TRUE in substance, misnamed in wording: Source A computed clearance from serum creatinine by Cockcroft-Gault across 1.01M admissions, i.e. ESTIMATED clearance is routine; the clause says "measured" while its own sensitivity arm offers "a cystatin-C or measured-clearance estimate where available", which concedes the primary instrument is an estimate. A wording defect for attack 5, not a false premise.
C4. Clearance crossings back above a dosing threshold occur within admission often enough to populate a cohort. TRUE (sourced, summary-level): Source A, 9.2% of admissions improved by >= 1 CrCl stratum.
C5. The same threshold and the same estimating equation apply before and after the dip. CONTESTED, declared as such by the record; handled by stipulation and sensitivity arms. No source I opened bears on equation switching. My own reasoning: a 24 h persistence rule additionally presupposes at least daily creatinine after the crossing, and V2 does not say how episodes with sparser labs are handled — a well-posedness gap, small.
C6. A measurable window exists between recovery and the first competing event. NOT A PRESUPPOSITION: limb (b) reports the share never restored before a competing event, so this is a MEASURAND and the exemption is declared. It does bear on significance (attack 6/8): Source A's improved group has a median LOS of 4 days (my inference from that number: many windows will be short).
C7. Charts record a rationale that lets non-restoration be classed deliberate vs undocumented. MEASURAND (limb d reports the undocumented share). Exemption declared.
C8. Each site has a deployed renal dosing rule set. Weak presupposition of limb (e); a site with none reports none. Record's Boussadi instance (which I did not open) shows one exists. Not a kill.
C9. V2's inclusion wording "administered dose fell below the labelled full dose while clearance was below threshold" captures renal-attributed reductions. CONTESTED (declared by the record as residual confounding). My own observation, not a finding: V2 dropped V1's word "reduced", so it also admits episodes that started at a low dose and never had a full dose; for those "restoration" is first escalation. Same clinical question, slightly different object; should be stated.

No clause came back FALSE or CONTESTED on a source I opened that the question cannot survive without. C2 is the live risk and it is UNVERIFIED, which kills nothing.
