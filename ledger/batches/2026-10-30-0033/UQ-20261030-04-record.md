Question (canonical):

What the reformulation changed, and why (well-posedness only):

(iii) the pre-declared cell-type set is enumerated rather than described (Gate 1's enumerate-or-state-the-rule requirement): the two required types are **skeletal-muscle fibre segments** (post-mitotic) and **cultured primary dermal fibroblasts** (dividing), with cardiomyocytes admissible as a substitute post-mitotic type.

WHAT THE REPAIR ITSELF PRESUPPOSES (tested as hard as the clause it replaced):
- That a post-mitotic cell type yields ≥10^3 individually barcoded near-full-length molecules. mtDNA copy number in a muscle fibre segment is in the thousands, so copy number is sufficient in principle, but **recovery** at full length is not demonstrated for any post-mitotic somatic cell: in the one published instance I opened, 293T gave 424 UMI groups from ~5 cells, i.e. ~85 molecules/cell (MY ARITHMETIC from the paper's Table 1 and Methods, not a figure it prints) — an order of magnitude below copy number and an order of magnitude below what V2 asks. The repair therefore presupposes a recovery efficiency nobody has shown in a post-mitotic cell.
- That "single cell" is definable for a multinucleated skeletal-muscle fibre. V2 asks for a fibre **segment**, which is a stipulation, not a cell; it must be declared as a fixed-length laser-captured segment or the unit is ill-defined.
- That fibroblast passage number is fixed, since a dividing cell type's replication history is the exposure the question is about.

Motivating gap:
Per-cell/per-tissue somatic mtDNA mutation burden is reported as a per-base frequency pooled over the hundreds to thousands of genome copies in a cell, with the molecule's identity discarded in the denominator. A per-molecule instrument now exists (see Novelty), so the joint distribution of mutations along one physical molecule is obtainable. Whether mtDNA replication is uniform across copies or hierarchical — a few templates copied repeatedly while most are rarely copied — is a long-standing unknown that a pooled per-base frequency cannot address in either direction, because a hierarchy leaves its signature only in the joint distribution along one molecule: repeated copying of the same template compounds errors on that lineage.

**CORRECTION TO THE INTAKE GAP, established in this pass and load-bearing.** The intake claimed the instrument "reports, for the first time, the SET of mutations carried by one physical molecule" and that this is "a case where a quantity was previously unmeasurable rather than merely unmeasured." BOTH CLAUSES ARE FALSE as of March 2023. Bi et al., *Nucleic Acids Research* 2023;51(8):e48, doi:10.1093/nar/gkad208, publish iMiGseq, which UMI-tags and sequences individual full-length mtDNA molecules from single cells, and which explicitly reports per-molecule haplotypes, genetic linkage of de-novo mutations, and phylogenetic trees of individual molecules from one oocyte. The set of mutations on one physical molecule has been reportable for three years. What remains unmeasured is the **multiplicity distribution and its dispersion** — the instrument's output has not been reduced to that statistic. The question survives as *unmeasured*, not as *previously unmeasurable*, and its significance argument must rest on the former.

Presuppositions:
1. **CONTESTED and load-bearing (the feasibility clause).** Individual near-full-length mtDNA molecules can be sequenced at an accuracy such that two or more true mutations on ONE molecule are callable above error. STATUS AFTER THIS PASS: partially supported, not established. Bi et al. 2023 do not use duplex chemistry; they use UMI-grouped consensus with group depth ≥3 and in-group read-consistency filtering. Their molecule-level error statement is a polymerase-fidelity calculation — the UMI-labelling step "introduces one error in ∼1600 unique 16 556-bp molecules", with ~0.03 SNVs attributed to it in their Nanopore data — not a demonstration on a known-sequence spike-in at matched length. No spike-in control appears in the portion of that paper I could read. The candidate's own requirement (a molecule-level error model estimated from a matched-length spike-in) is therefore unmet by the only instrument found, and it is the single clause most likely to route this question rather than answer it.
2. **Labelled prerequisite, not the finding.** Single cells can be isolated with their mtDNA complement intact and without molecules mixing between cells — enforced by per-cell barcoding with a cross-contamination control.
3. Observed variants are de-novo somatic rather than inherited. OPERATIONAL RULE (added at reformulation): exclude (i) any variant at a position differing from rCRS in the individual's own consensus haplogroup assignment, and (ii) any variant recurring in cells of the same individual above a pre-fixed cell-recurrence threshold. This rule is not cosmetic: in Bi et al.'s 293T data, 424 UMI groups carry 7766 high-confidence SNVs, ≈18 per molecule by MY division — a rate absurd for de-novo somatic mutation and consistent with domination by fixed haplogroup variants, since those cells are assigned to haplogroup U5a1. **THIS IS MY OWN REASONING ABOUT THE NUMERATOR CLASS, NOT A SOURCE FINDING**; the paper's composition of that figure is not decomposable from the text I read, and I did not reach its Discussion.
4. **A Poisson null is the right null. THIS IS MY OWN REASONING ABOUT THE STATISTICS, NOT A SOURCE FINDING.** Poisson follows if every molecule experiences the same per-replication error rate and the same number of replications; a negative-binomial alternative corresponds to heterogeneity in replication counts. I searched for a published mtDNA-population-genetics model stating this correspondence (query 9) and found none that I opened; the relaxed-replication / random-intracellular-drift literature returned by that query models copy-number and heteroplasmy dynamics, not per-molecule mutation multiplicity. The correspondence remains my inference and is Unverified.
5. **CONTESTED:** that enough mtDNA replication occurs in the chosen cell types for a hierarchy to leave a detectable signature — which is why one post-mitotic and one dividing type are both required. Note the adverse interaction found in this pass: the cell types with demonstrated 10^3–10^4 molecule yields in Bi et al. are oocytes (3911, 1137, 17942 and 2493, 1849, 615 molecules for single NSG and B6 oocytes), and oocytes are the type where this presupposition is weakest.
6. Molecule fragmentation during extraction does not split multi-mutation molecules into apparently single-mutation fragments — a nuisance biased towards the Poisson null, bounded by read-length reporting and by restricting the numerator to molecules above a pre-fixed called length.

What would count as an answer:
At least ~50 single cells per cell type, in the two enumerated cell types (skeletal-muscle fibre segments; cultured primary dermal fibroblasts), each cell yielding at least ~10^3 individually sequenced near-full-length molecules, with a per-molecule error model estimated from a spike-in of known sequence at matched length.
HEADLINE NUMBER: the index of dispersion (variance/mean) of de-novo somatic mutations per molecule within each cell, with a confidence interval, against the Poisson expectation computed from that same cell's per-base frequency × called molecule length, plus the fitted negative-binomial shape parameter.
Overdispersion beyond the error model, reproduced in the second cell type and robust to read-length stratification, establishes that mutation is concentrated on a subset of genomes and makes a replication hierarchy the leading explanation. Agreement with Poisson rules the hierarchy out at the measured depth and is equally an answer.

Significance — consequence:
If overdispersed beyond the molecule-level error model: mtDNA replication within a cell is not uniform across copies, and a minority of templates is copied repeatedly. That changes the arithmetic of every pooled mtDNA burden figure in the literature — the same per-base frequency would correspond to far more mutation-free genomes and a small heavily-mutated subpopulation, which bears directly on heteroplasmy threshold effects in mitochondrial disease, where what matters is the number of functionally compromised genomes rather than the average. It would also give the clonal-expansion literature a mechanism other than random intracellular drift, and would predict that the expanding lineages are identifiable as molecules before they are visible as cell-level heteroplasmy. If Poisson at the measured depth: replication is effectively uniform across copies, which is a direct positive result for the relaxed-replication / random-drift family of models that currently underpins clonal-expansion and bottleneck theory, and it bounds how much of the age-related burden can be explained by template reuse. Both answers are informative, which is the mark of a well-set null.

Significance — audience:
(i) mtDNA population geneticists and stochastic modellers of heteroplasmy, who would parameterise replication-count heterogeneity from a measured dispersion instead of assuming relaxed replication — this is the community that would change its equations the day the number appeared; (ii) mitochondrial-disease researchers working on heteroplasmy thresholds, who would re-express threshold arguments in terms of the number of compromised genomes rather than a mean frequency; (iii) ageing-biology groups studying clonal expansion of mtDNA mutations in post-mitotic tissue; (iv) the single-molecule method developers themselves (the iMiGseq group and the full-length multiplexed-mtDNA groups), for whom this is a new readout from data they already generate and whose immediate action would be to add a matched-length spike-in to the protocol.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: is the dispersion nucleoid-structured (do co-mutated molecules co-localise)? Does a POLG or TWNK maintenance mutant change the dispersion as well as the rate? Do deletion-bearing molecules sit on the high-multiplicity lineages, which would link the hierarchy to the best-documented case of mtDNA replicative advantage? Does the germline bottleneck sample molecules or lineages? And methodologically: once per-molecule multiplicity is a reported statistic, within-cell molecular phylogenies become a lineage-tracing substrate at a resolution below the cell.

Significance — anchoring:
Touches four standing problems: the mtDNA clonal-expansion problem (random drift vs selection vs template reuse); the mitochondrial genetic bottleneck and what its unit of sampling is; the non-random-segregation / immortal-strand debate, which query 11's no-date sweep confirms is still live and still argued without molecule-level data; and the "replicating subpopulation" question of whether only part of a cell's mtDNA pool turns over.

Reason for silence:
Three mechanisms compound. First, the field's canonical summary statistic for mtDNA mutation burden is mutations per sequenced nucleotide, and that denominator discards molecule identity by construction — P2's Figure 4 caption computes it as mutations over (region length × mean DCS depth) — so the dispersion statistic has no slot in the standard pipeline's output and would not be produced incidentally by anyone using it. Second, the instrument that makes the statistic obtainable was published in 2023 and its demonstrations were aimed where its funding pointed: ultra-sensitive heteroplasmy quantitation, oocyte haplotyping, and validation of mitoTALEN and DdCBE editing. Per-molecule linkage appears there as a capability claim, not as an estimand. Third, and most specifically, there is an adverse coupling between yield and biological interest: the cell type where the published method reaches 10^3–10^4 molecules per cell is the oocyte, which is replication-quiescent at the stage sampled and therefore the type where a replication hierarchy is least expected to have left a signature; the somatic types where the hierarchy should be visible are the ones where the published yield (~10^2 molecules per cell, my arithmetic from P1) falls short of what a dispersion estimate with a usable confidence interval needs. A question whose natural first instrument is best in the least informative tissue tends not to get asked.

Scores: Well-posedness 2/3 · Significance 3/3

Well-posedness 2/3, argued: the headline estimand is a single named number with a stated null, a stated alternative, a confidence interval, pre-fixed robustness checks and an enumerated cell-type set after reformulation, which is 3/3 material. It loses a point because the load-bearing feasibility presupposition is not merely flagged but unmet by the only instrument found: no source I opened bounds the molecule-level false-positive rate with a matched-length known-sequence spike-in, and without that bound "overdispersion beyond the error model" has no numerical content. The ~10^3-molecules-per-post-mitotic-cell requirement is also unmet in the one published somatic instance by about an order of magnitude. Both are gaps in what would count as an answer, not merely in whether anyone has done it.

Significance 3/3, argued: a specific identifiable community (mtDNA stochastic modellers) would change a model parameter on either answer; the yes-answer re-interprets the entire pooled-burden literature without contradicting any of it; the no-answer is a positive result for the dominant model rather than a null; and it anchors to four named standing problems. It is not novel trivia on any reading.
