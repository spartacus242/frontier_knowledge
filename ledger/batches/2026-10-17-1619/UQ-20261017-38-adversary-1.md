## Restatement (my own words)

Take a fixed list of published clinical scores that each come with an action cut-off. For each score, collect several independent, inspectable implementations (web calculators, apps, packages, appendix code). Feed them the same patients, including awkward ones (boundary values, odd units, missing predictors). Ask three things: how often do the implementations return different numbers; how often does the difference put the patient on the other side of the cut-off; and, where the publication actually pins down the answer, how much of the cut-off flipping is coder error as opposed to the publication itself leaving the answer open. V1 adds that the "publication leaves it open" region is sized by a rubric before any implementation is run.

In one sentence: is a published risk score a well-defined function in practice, and how much does the choice of calculator rather than the patient decide the treatment category?

## New priors found by this adversary

**N1. Seiberth S, Terstegen T, Strobach D, Czock D et al. Eur J Clin Pharmacol 2020;76:1465-70 (opened).** One published equation (CKD-EPI creatinine); 49 freely available online calculators identified, 47 producing results, from 11 countries; five hypothetical reference cases varying age, sex, creatinine and ethnicity, with the two Caucasian-female cases placed on either side of the equation's own sex-specific creatinine cut-off (i.e., the awkward-region design the candidate claims as its own); reference values "computed independently in two Excel sheets". Findings as quoted by the publisher page: "Only 36 of 47 calculators (76.6%) produced accurate eGFR results for all reference cases"; "Eight of 47 (17.0%) calculators were considered as faulty"; "Erroneous calculations that might lead to inappropriate clinical decision-making were found in 8 of 47 calculators." Error causes adjudicated per calculator: ethnicity handling (4), inverse BSA de-indexing (2), wrong equation branch applied below the 0.9 mg/dl cut-off (1), creatinine unit conversion (1), mislabelled unit (1), unexplained (2); deviations under 1 ml/min treated as rounding. Threshold relevance: one worked example (eGFR 42.7 indexed converted to 51.6 instead of 35.2 ml/min, "could lead to quite different clinical decisions in therapy with metformin"); CKD-stage changes NOT systematically counted. Attributes errors to implementations, not to the CKD-EPI publication, but notes "no published reference cases existed for evaluating CKD-EPI calculators" — the executable-specification / conformance-suite point the candidate lists under fecundity. Unit of analysis = (calculator, reference case); comparator = specification oracle; estimand = proportion of calculators correct on all cases. This matches the candidate's limb (i) design at I = 47 and limb (iii) adjudication qualitatively, in a fourth register (clinical pharmacology), on a score whose action thresholds (CKD stage, renal dose adjustment) are among the most consequential in medicine. My own arithmetic from the source's numbers: 11/47 = 23.4% of calculators were wrong on at least one of five cases (source reports the complement, 76.6%); the source gives no uncertainty for these proportions.

**N2. Yang L et al. Arch Osteoporos 2023;18:43 (opened).** Two implementations of one model (GE Lunar DXA-FRAX, Irish calibration v3.8, vs Web-FRAX v4 Ireland) on 1,254 real patients; agreement examined by Bland-Altman within subject. When both implementations receive the same inputs (with BMD), "there is excellent agreement between the Web-FRAX and DXA-FRAX tools", limits of agreement under 1%. The paper's headline discordance is with- vs without-BMD (a different input set, not an implementation difference). Reclassification across treatment thresholds NOT reported. This is a direct pairwise-agreement measurement of two independent implementations of one score on a real input distribution — the candidate's limb (i) pairwise estimand — and it comes out near-zero. It does not use an oracle. It does not do limb (ii) or (iii).

Neither N1 nor N2 reports a threshold-crossing RATE over a pre-declared input distribution, nor an inter-rater statistic for adjudication, nor a pre-run spec-determined/undetermined partition. Both, like P1 and P2, answer the question in the direction "implementations mostly agree/are mostly right; a minority have gross, nameable errors (units, ethnicity branch, equation branch, BSA de-indexing, boundary cells)".

## Presupposition test

Presupposition 1 ("the published description is what everyone believes they are using, so divergence is defect") was actively tested and is weaker than the record states. (a) Seiberth 2020 classified "the same value returned regardless of ethnicity" as a FAULT; after the 2021 race-free CKD-EPI refit, the identical behaviour is the correct implementation of the current equation. So "faithful to the publication" is version-indexed, and the set of implementations in the wild spans versions. (b) Yang 2023 compared v3.8 and v4 of FRAX, with country calibrations — again a plurality of legitimate "the published score" referents. (c) The MDCalc FAQ title "Why does a score appear different from what my hospital uses" (not opened) indicates the vendor treats inter-implementation difference as partly legitimate local variation. V1 handles this by pre-declaring the publication per score, which is a workable fix, but the candidate's record did not name version plurality as a confound anywhere, and it bears directly on the spec-determined/undetermined partition: an implementation of a later version is not "unfaithful" and not "under-specified" — it is a third category V1 lacks.
