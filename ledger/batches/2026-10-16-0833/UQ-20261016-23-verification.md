## Claim under test: `UQ-23-fomina`

## Verdict: **PARTLY_CONFIRMED**

## What the sources actually say

1) FOMINA 2023 EXISTS AS DESCRIBED; FIGURES ARE RIGHT. Fomina YY, Byrne JJ, Spong CY, "Evaluating strength of recommendations for commonly administered medications in lactating women," Journal of Maternal-Fetal and Neonatal Medicine 2023;36(1):2163626, DOI 10.1080/14767058.2022.2163626, PMID 36597824 (https://utsouthwestern.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/evaluating-strength-of-recommendations-for-commonly-administered-). Retrospective review of all LactMed entries as of December 2020. "Of 1,408 medications, 714 had no associated data, 664 had minimal-to-moderate data, and 30 had strong data." My own arithmetic: 714/1408 = 50.7% (~51%), 30/1408 = 2.1% (~2%). The reported figures are therefore accurate.

CRITERIA FOMINA ACTUALLY USED (this is the load-bearing point): a three-tier evidence-PRESENCE scheme keyed to LactMed's own four content categories (maternal drug levels, infant drug levels, effects on infants, effects on lactation). "No data" = no research studies associated with the drug. "Minimal-to-moderate" = research missing in one or more of the four categories, or evidence limited to case reports/observational studies. "Strong" = research in all four categories from PK/PD, cohort, case-control or RCT designs. Nothing in this scheme interrogates the DESIGN of a milk concentration-time dataset: no criterion on sampling schedule across a dosing interval, steady state, number of subjects, completeness of milk collection, assay LOQ, or whether a depletion curve can be fitted. It counts whether studies exist and of what broad type, not whether their data meet design requirements.

2) MEER 2015 EXISTS AND IS THE CLOSER PRIOR. Meer DH, Wieringa A, Wegner I, Wilffert B, Horst PGJ, "Lactation studies of anticonvulsants: a quality review," Br J Clin Pharmacol 2015;79(4):558-565, DOI 10.1111/bcp.12524 (https://www.bps.ac.uk/publishing/our-journals/british-journal-of-clinical-pharmacology/volume-79/issue-4/lactation-studies-of-anticonvulsants-a-quality-re). The standard applied is NAMED and EXTERNAL: "guidelines from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the International Lactation Consultant Association (ILCA)." Scope: 19 anticonvulsants, 62 studies. Faults found: only one lamotrigine study and one gabapentin case report correctly assessed both absolute infant dose and milk-to-plasma ratio; only one carbamazepine, phenytoin or vigabatrin study correctly assessed absolute infant dose; 25 of 62 publications were case reports, letters or abstracts; sampling methods often unreported; "the number of samples within a given dose interval was often insufficient"; participant numbers generally low. So the specific design defects a residue-depletion lens would flag (sampling density within a dosing interval, n) are already documented — for one drug class.

3) EMA MILK GUIDELINE, EMA/CVMP/SWP/735418/2012 Rev.1, dated 25 March 2022, effective 1 August 2022 (https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/adopted-guideline-determination-withdrawal-periods-milk-revision-1_en.pdf). Method: "The withdrawal period is calculated as the 95/95 tolerance limit" (upper 95% confidence limit of the 95th percentile), from n >= 20 animals, rounded up to whole milkings. Depletion phase: not given a standalone definition; it is encoded as a monotonicity assumption plus a dosing anchor. "during the depletion phase we expect concentrations decreasing with time" (Step 3) and "This step incorporates the prior knowledge that during the depletion period residue concentrations are decreasing" (s.4.2) — enforced by per-animal monotonic regression pooling adjacent increasing values. The anchor: "the last administration of the compound shall be 12 hours (one milking period) before milking 1," and if that is not met "the data from milking 1 should be discarded" (s.4.1.8). LOQ handling: "Measured concentrations cijk < LOQ are temporarily replaced by LOQ" (Step 1); a sample is "considered to be below the LOQ only if all measurements on that sample are below the LOQ" (Step 2); censoring indicator set to 0 "if a value yij=LOQ is replaced by a higher value in the monotonic regression" (Step 3); the plotting substitution of LOQ/2 is "done for graphical purposes only" and "has no influence on the estimation of the withdrawal period" (Step 4); and in Annex I, "a data point below the LOQ just counts as a point below MRL1."

DEPENDENCE ON A PRE-FIXED LIMIT: yes, decisively. The per-animal endpoint is defined only relative to MRL1 — Step 5: "identify the first time point tj (in milkings) with cij<=MRL1 and cik<=MRL1 for all k>j"; and "if the last concentration in the data set is still above MRL1, TTSCi cannot be calculated." Step 9 recomputes across a range of MRL1 values and Step 10 reads off "the real MRL1." The sub-LOQ rule is likewise stated in MRL terms. So the time-to-safe-concentration endpoint, the LOQ rule and the tolerance limit all presuppose a numerical limit fixed in advance. One hedge matters for the proposed study: a footnote states "In the absence of a numerical MRL, another reference value may be used" — i.e. a prespecified reference value is required, but it need not be a formal MRL. (MRL2 is referenced in the Annex wording but the document does not define it; I could not resolve that.)

4) THE TWO DIRECT QUESTIONS. Does published work already assess whether human milk PK studies meet a named external design standard? YES, but narrowly: Meer 2015, FDA + ILCA criteria, anticonvulsants only. I found no such audit over LactMed as a whole or any population, and none against veterinary residue-depletion design requirements (VICH GL48 / the EMA milk guideline). The nearest recent work, Monfort A, Macente J, Van Neste M et al., "Pragmatic and contextualized methods selection for safety assessment of infant systemic exposure through human milk: the Milk4baby decision tree approach," Front Pharmacol 2025;16, DOI 10.3389/fphar.2025.1602018, is a method-selection decision tree verified on 50 medications from LactMed and Le CRAT; it cites the LactMed census figures secondhand ("54% of drugs in the LactMed database lack lactation safety data, and only 2% have robust evidence") and warns that "poorly designed case reports/case studies may yield limited or misleading information," but it does not extract or grade published milk concentration-time profiles against design criteria. Does anything compute breastfeeding-interruption intervals by a statistical tolerance-limit rule against a stated infant-exposure limit? I found NOTHING. Searches surfaced only half-life rules of thumb, NRC radiopharmaceutical milk-discard tables, and relative-infant-dose thresholds (the 10% convention, RID calculators) — point estimates and heuristics, not an upper one-sided tolerance limit on a population of depletion curves against a prespecified exposure limit. The 95/95 tolerance-limit machinery appears only in the veterinary residue literature.

## Effect on the ledger verdict

The "disguised known" verdict should NOT stand as stated, but the novelty claim has to be narrowed and must now cite both priors.

Why it does not stand: Fomina 2023 is not the same question over the same source. It is a presence-of-evidence census — does any study exist, in which of LactMed's four content categories, and is it a case report or a PK/cohort/RCT. It contains no design criterion at all: no sampling-schedule, steady-state, n, collection-completeness or LOQ requirement. A study asking whether published milk concentration-time data could survive residue-depletion design requirements is asking something Fomina's instrument cannot answer, even though both run over LactMed. Treating 51%/2% as having settled design adequacy conflates "no study exists" with "the existing study's data are unfittable."

Why it partly stands: the claim is right that the design-adequacy question is not virgin ground. Meer 2015 is a genuine audit of human lactation PK studies against a named external standard (FDA + ILCA), and it already reports the two defects a residue-depletion lens would most want to claim as its own finding — insufficient samples within a dosing interval, and unreported sampling methods. So "no one has audited published human milk PK data against an external design standard" would be FALSE, and the proposal cannot be written as if the design-adequacy question were unasked. What is unoccupied is (a) the scope — Meer covers 19 anticonvulsants and 62 studies, not LactMed-wide; and (b) the standard — nobody has applied veterinary residue-depletion requirements (VICH GL48, EMA/CVMP/SWP/735418/2012 Rev.1) to human milk data.

Suggested verdict: downgrade from "disguised known" to "known-adjacent, scope-and-standard novel, with one structural caveat." The caveat is substantive and should be recorded against the proposal rather than hidden: the EMA machinery the proposal borrows is limit-dependent all the way down. Time-to-safe-concentration, the sub-LOQ rule and the tolerance limit are all defined relative to a pre-fixed MRL1, and human lactation has no agreed numerical infant-exposure limit analogous to an MRL. The guideline's own footnote ("In the absence of a numerical MRL, another reference value may be used") leaves room, but it means the proposed study must stipulate its own reference value, and any adequacy verdict it reaches is conditional on that stipulation rather than on a regulatory fact. That is an assumption to defend in the design, not a reason to kill the study. The second question — tolerance-limit interruption intervals against a stated infant-exposure limit — is, as far as I can find, genuinely unoccupied, and is the stronger novelty claim of the two.
