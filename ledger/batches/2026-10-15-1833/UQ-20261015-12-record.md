Question (canonical): **[R1, adopted at Gate 1]** In a within-donor randomised processing-delay series from a single venipuncture (K2EDTA and a stabilising tube; plasma separated at 0, 2, 6, 24 and 48 h, at room temperature and at 4 °C), with deep WGS plus methylation-based deconvolution on every aliquot:
  (i) **Phase question.** Does delay-associated loss of coverage at nucleosome-phase positions differ between tissue-marker sets — i.e. does the fall in the amplitude of ~190 bp coverage periodicity (and in phase-resolved coverage at the inferred dyad vs linker offsets) within tissue-specific marker windows differ in magnitude across those windows by more than the within-donor, within-timepoint reproducibility interval?
  (ii) **Outcome question.** Do (a) deconvolved non-haematopoietic tissue fractions and (b) an openly pre-registered re-implementation of an arm-level DELFI-type fragmentation score plus the 100–150 bp proportion move outside pre-specified equivalence bands with delay, **after** adjustment for a measured leukocyte-lysis covariate (neutrophil/NK methylation-marker fraction and the burden of long, nucleosome-multiple-exceeding fragments) and with an exogenous tracer present to identify degradation of pre-existing cfDNA separately from newly released genomic DNA?

  **[V0, as submitted]** Does ex vivo nuclease degradation of plasma cell-free DNA between venipuncture and plasma separation act preferentially at nucleosome-phase positions that differ between tissues, so that time-to-processing systematically shifts inferred tissue-of-origin fractions and cancer-like fragmentomic scores rather than only shortening fragments?

  Reason for the (single, well-posedness-only) reformulation:
  (a) V0 bundles a mechanism claim and an outcome claim under one yes/no. The likeliest real result — scores move, phase-loss differences do not reach significance, or vice versa — has no verdict under V0. R1 splits them into two independently answerable questions, each with its own band.
  (b) "preferentially at nucleosome-phase positions that differ between tissues" names no measurable quantity. R1 fixes the estimand: periodicity amplitude and phase-resolved coverage within named marker windows, compared against a within-donor reproducibility interval.

  (d) V0 leaves the central confounder (delay-induced leukocyte lysis adding white-cell genomic DNA) as a caveat. Nearest prior P2 shows this is not a caveat but the leading alternative explanation of the very shift V0 predicts. R1 moves it into the question as a measured covariate **plus** a tracer, so that a contamination-only result is a defined, reportable outcome rather than an unresolved confound.
  R1 does not narrow the question to dodge prior work: the outcome half is kept, and is graded against P2 below.

Motivating gap (as corrected):
- Fragmentomics rests on the claim that fragment size, end motifs and nucleosome footprints report the chromatin state of the releasing tissue. The claim presupposes that post-collection degradation is tissue-nonspecific: a nuisance that shortens fragments without changing tissue attribution.
- The preanalytical fragmentomics literature measures size, integrity, 5-Mb/arm profiles and end-motif entropy. The study closest to the candidate (P1) treats processing time **observationally** in 30 healthy EDTA donors, reports no significant effect on size distribution or end-motif entropy, and reports — without statistics — "a moderate impact of the processing time on the genome-wide cfDNA fragmentation patterns", i.e. at exactly the chromosome-arm feature level a DELFI-type score is built from. It performs no nucleosome-positioning analysis and no tissue-of-origin deconvolution, and is confined to double-stranded library preparation (all verified by opening the preprint).
- The standards layer is silent on the relevant outcomes: the NCI Biospecimen Evidence-Based Practices document for cfDNA (P3) governs delay as a protocol window (EDTA centrifuged within 2 h; up to 4 h RT; up to 24 h at 4 °C) and never mentions fragmentomics, nucleosome footprints, end motifs or tissue-of-origin/cell-type deconvolution.
- What is **not** a gap, contrary to the intake: that nobody has looked at deconvolved fractions under delay. P2 did, in blood cell types, and found movement. What remains un-asked is (i) the nucleosome-phase mechanism, (ii) non-haematopoietic tissue fractions and a fragmentomic score as the outcomes, and (iii) any design that separates differential degradation of pre-existing cfDNA from newly released leukocyte DNA.

Presuppositions (each marked, per Gate 1):
  P-1 Tissue-specific nucleosome phasing is resolvable at the depth used. ESTABLISHED at high-depth WGS; CONTESTED at the shallow depths of deployed tests. R1 specifies deep WGS for the phase question and treats the shallow-depth case as a separate, downstream question.
  P-2 Deconvolution references (methylation or nucleosome atlases) remain valid under partial degradation. CONTESTED and now partly undermined: P2's own authors read their 72 h EDTA shift as a change in cell-type composition, which presumes the reference stays valid while the input changes; the reverse reading (reference invalidity under degradation) is untested. This is a live threat to interpreting (ii)(a) and must be addressed by in-silico degradation of the reference.
  P-3 Processing delay can be randomised within donor from a single draw. ESTABLISHED (P2 did it at 3 timepoints; aliquot volume limits the number of deep-WGS timepoints).
  P-4 Nuclease activity in standard EDTA tubes is non-zero within clinically realistic windows. ESTABLISHED from yield and size changes; P3 reports delay effects after as little as 4 h, and notes that wet ice did not prevent change after a 2 h delay. FLAGGED in the other direction: EDTA partly inhibits DNases (a Clin Biochem 2015 title to that effect was surfaced by query 13 but not opened), so the effect size may be small in EDTA and the stabilising-tube arm may show less still.
  P-5 Delay-induced leukocyte lysis can be separated from differential degradation of pre-existing cfDNA. CONTESTED; this is the central confounder and the hardest design element. P2's result is the paradigm case of the confound: NK fraction rose sharply and neutrophil fraction fell at 72 h in EDTA (mean absolute change 13.81% by CelFiE), which is consistent with added white-cell DNA alone. Covariate adjustment alone will not identify the two; R1 therefore requires an exogenous tracer.
  P-6 Published fragmentomic scores can be recomputed faithfully outside their originating laboratories. CONTESTED; some are proprietary, so an open re-implementation must be pre-specified and cannot be claimed to be the commercial score.
  P-7 The field has not already answered the outcome half. **PARTLY CONTRADICTED** by P2 (blood cell types, EDTA, 72 h) and, historically, by the prenatal observation that fetal fraction falls after delayed centrifugation (reported in P3's evidence section). The surviving un-asked content is the phase mechanism, the non-haematopoietic/score outcomes, and the lysis/degradation discrimination.
  Gate 1 verdict: P-3 and P-4 established; P-1, P-2, P-5, P-6 flagged contested; P-7 explicitly downgraded. R1 is a question, not a thesis: "all shifts inside the bands once contamination is adjusted" is a stated, informative, publishable outcome that licenses current protocol windows, and "contamination only" is a third defined outcome.

What would count as an answer: Within-donor randomised time course from one venipuncture per donor (0, 2, 6, 24, 48 h; room temperature and 4 °C; K2EDTA paired with one stabilising tube), n chosen for the equivalence bands, in healthy donors and in donors with known tumour burden. Per aliquot: deep WGS (phase analysis), methylation sequencing (deconvolution), and an exogenous nucleosomal tracer spiked at draw to identify degradation of pre-existing material.
  Outcomes and decision table:
  - (i) phase: amplitude of ~190 bp coverage periodicity and dyad-vs-linker phase-resolved coverage within tissue-specific marker windows; the test is whether the delay slope differs **between** marker sets, against within-donor reproducibility.
  - (ii)(a) deconvolved non-haematopoietic tissue fractions; (ii)(b) openly re-implemented arm-level DELFI-type score and 100–150 bp proportion.
  - covariate: neutrophil/NK methylation-marker fraction plus long-fragment burden, as the lysis index.
  - YES = fractions or score leave the pre-registered bands with delay, the departure survives adjustment for the lysis index **and** is corroborated by the tracer, and phase-resolved coverage loss differs between marker sets.
  - NO = all departures inside the bands after adjustment; licenses current protocol windows and confirms the field's presupposition.
  - CONTAMINATION-ONLY = departures explained by the lysis index and the tracer shows no differential degradation; closed and informative, and the expected result if P2's reading generalises.
  - Sensitivity: in-silico degradation of the deconvolution reference (tests P-2); repeat the score at the shallow depth deployed tests use (tests P-1).

Significance — consequence: If YES, a measurable part of the fragmentomic tissue-of-origin and cancer-detection signal is manufactured between venipuncture and centrifugation, which makes processing delay an effect modifier rather than a quality metric. Multi-site trials in which delay correlates with site, case/control status (symptomatic patients bled in clinics with different courier schedules) or screening arm would carry a bias that no downstream statistical adjustment currently models, and specimen-handling windows would have to be set from fragmentomic outcomes rather than from yield and integrity. If NO (all shifts inside the bands after adjustment), the field's central presupposition is positively confirmed for the first time, current windows are licensed, and the published tissue-of-origin literature gains a robustness result it does not have. If CONTAMINATION-ONLY, the practical remedy is different and cheap: measure and adjust for the lysis index rather than tighten the clock. The three outcomes imply three different protocol actions, which is what makes it worth asking. Marginal consequence is reduced by P2: that fractions move at all is no longer news, so the value lies in the mechanism, the non-haematopoietic/score outcomes, and the discrimination.

Significance — audience: (a) Multi-cancer-detection developers, who would have to add a delay arm to analytical validation and, under a YES, declare a handling window tied to score stability. The FDA review materials surfaced for Galleri-class tests (query 11) show a regulator actively evaluating such claims in 2026, and the robustness analyses on record address population differences, not specimen handling. (b) Preanalytical standards bodies — the NCI BEBP document (P3) is the concrete instance: it exists, it is maintained (revised Feb 2025), it sets the window, and it contains no fragmentomic outcome, so a result here would change a document that already governs practice. BloodPAC/CLSI/IFCC occupy the same position. (c) Designers of multi-site screening trials, who would need delay recorded per sample as a covariate rather than as an inclusion criterion. (d) Biobank custodians holding legacy plasma with heterogeneous processing times, for whom the answer determines whether fragmentomic reanalysis of those collections is interpretable at all.

Significance — fecundity: Makes askable: whether phase-resolved degradation can be inverted into a *clock* that estimates each sample's effective delay from the sequencing data, turning the confounder into a QC covariate; whether the same phase-selectivity operates in vivo, so that circulating nuclease levels (DNASE1L3 and DNASE1 genotype or activity) modulate apparent tissue fractions between individuals as well as between timepoints; whether deconvolution references must themselves be degradation-matched to the sample (P-2); whether end-motif and tissue-of-origin signals have different delay sensitivities, which would rank feature families by robustness for assay design; and whether the fetal-fraction decline long known in prenatal testing shares this mechanism, which would unify two literatures that currently share only a protocol warning.

Significance — anchoring: Touches (a) the open question of what fraction of cfDNA fragmentation is nuclease-driven versus cell-death-driven; (b) the known-but-unquantified MNase/DNase digestion-level artefact in chromatin footprinting (query 3), which is the same physics in a different vessel; (c) the reproducibility gap between fragmentomic classifiers trained in one laboratory and validated in another, where handling differences are a standing unexplained candidate; (d) the prenatal precedent in P3 of a tissue fraction that demonstrably moves with delay; and (e) P2's unexplained 72 h leukocyte-subtype shift, which is currently attributed to composition change without any test against differential degradation.

Reason for silence: Three mechanisms, none of them "nobody thought of it".
  1. Disciplinary boundary with mismatched outcome vocabularies. Preanalytical work is published by biobank and laboratory-medicine groups in clinical-chemistry venues, whose established endpoints are yield, integrity index, size distribution and mutant allele fraction (visible in P1's and P3's outcome lists). Fragmentomic methods are built by genomics and oncology groups on frozen biobank plasma collected under one fixed protocol, where delay is held constant by design and variable-delay samples are excluded rather than studied. Neither side owns the conjunction: the first lacks the deconvolution and phase machinery, the second lacks variation in the exposure. P2 is the exception that proves the pattern — a preanalytical group did reach for deconvolution, and stopped at blood cell types without touching nucleosome phase or any clinical score.
  2. Incentive gap. The YES answer is costly to exactly the parties who could run the experiment best: it says a validated test's score depends partly on courier time. The deployed scores are proprietary, so an outside group cannot compute the clinically relevant endpoint and must pre-register a re-implementation that the developer can disown (P-6). The result is a question that is unattractive to insiders and only partly answerable by outsiders.
  3. Cost and instrument threshold. The phase question needs deep WGS on every timepoint of every donor plus matched methylation sequencing, i.e. roughly an order of magnitude more sequencing per donor than any preanalytical study on record (P2 used RRBS on 3 donors; P1 used shallow dsDNA libraries), and phase-resolved coverage analysis within marker windows became routine only with the post-2016 nucleosome-footprint toolchain. The experiment was not affordable at the time the protocol windows were written, and the windows have not been revisited since.
