## Restatement (my own words, before reading the generator's framing again)

When a patient has two blood-culture sets drawn for one febrile episode — in practice usually by the same person, with one prep, within minutes — is skin-flora growth in set B statistically independent of growth in set A? And if it is not, if contamination clusters within an episode because the two sets share an operator, a prep, an antiseptic lot or a single needle-stick, then how wrong is the bedside heuristic that reads one-of-two commensal-positive as "contaminant" and two-of-two as "real bacteraemia", given that the heuristic's whole force comes from the coincidence of two supposedly independent accidents being unlikely?

Three separable limbs: (a) an empirical excess-concordance ratio against an independence benchmark computed from the same cohort's per-set rate; (b) variance components of that excess by operator / draw structure / prep / antiseptic lot; (c) the reclassification fraction of the 1-of-2 / 2-of-2 rule against an adjudication standard (V2 softens this to a bounded relative fraction).

## My own arithmetic (flagged as mine, not any source's)

Replicating the record's triviality note independently from Borad's published figures. If the per-pair "at least one commensal-positive set" proportion is 6.3-7.3%, then under independence with per-set rate p, P(at least one of two) = 1-(1-p)^2 ≈ 2p, so p ≈ 3.2-3.7%; P(both) = p^2 ≈ 0.10-0.14% of all pairs; and conditional on at least one set being commensal-positive, independence predicts both-positive = p/(2-p) ≈ 1.6-1.9%. Observed in Borad: 18.5% (MTS) to 28.1% (STS). Ratio ≈ 10-18x depending on which end of each published range is taken. Carrying the source's own uncertainty: Borad's STS-vs-MTS contrast is itself P = .11, i.e. not significant, single-centre and retrospective, and the record is right that the 6.3/7.3% and 28.1/18.5% denominators may not share a base (one counts pairs with at least one commensal-only-positive set, the other is a proportion among positives) — so this ratio is an order-of-magnitude indication, not an estimate, and it is mine, not Borad's.
