# UQ-20261021-05 — Adversary #1 log

## Restatement (my own words)
In hospitals that already telemeter oxygen pipeline flow and pressure per network leg at ~1-minute resolution and hold per-patient-hour oxygen prescriptions in the EHR: does the oxygen actually reaching a patient's wall outlet fall below the set flow as a continuous function of how much oxygen *other* patients on the same pipeline leg are drawing at that moment (expressed as a fraction of the leg's engineering design flow, breakpoint pre-declared), and does that shortfall explain a measurable share of within-patient variation in a pre-declared oxygenation endpoint (SpO2 time-in-target, FiO2 shortfall, device escalation) — estimated with within-patient contrasts and a negative-control leg? Either a sloped load-response curve or a flat curve with a stated detection bound is a reportable answer.

## My own arithmetic (labelled)
Flowmeter calibration 50 psi = 50 x 0.0689476 = **3.45 bar**. HSSIB measured the HDU falling 4.2 -> **3.1 bar** (record P1), i.e. **below** the 50 psi calibration/reducing-valve setpoint. Normal nominal is ~4 bar (~58 psi) with alarm limits 3.75 bar (~54 psi) and 4.90 bar. So in normal operation the meter stays above its 50 psi inlet, but the one measured surge incident drove inlet below it — the regime where delivered flow would degrade is demonstrably reachable. This defeats the "flat by construction" dissolution. (Source numbers: record P1; 50 psi from Davidson F2. Conversion is mine.)
