## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Model vendors keep announcing bigger context windows in roughly doubling steps. The question asks, for each announced step in a family that ships its weights: how often does real traffic actually need the extra room (what share of requests have inputs longer than the OLD limit, split by the kind of workload); on just those too-long requests, does serving them at the new limit actually produce a better result than chopping or compressing them down to the old limit plus retrieval; what does the bigger window cost to serve per request; and did the trick used to extend the window make the model worse at ordinary short prompts. In one line: **was the last doubling worth it, scored against traffic rather than against synthetic long-context benchmarks** — with a pre-declared negative answer (tiny exceedance share + no outcome gain over truncation+retrieval + a real short-context cost = the doubling destroyed value).

## Strongest killing point (one paragraph)

The reformulation was adopted so that "the family supplying the thresholds also supplies the checkpoints", and limb (d) is written as the delta between "the pre- and post-extension checkpoints of that family". On the Qwen2.5-1M Technical Report (arXiv:2501.15383, read in full via the PDF), that pair does not exist at either of Qwen's announced steps: Table 4 places Qwen2.5-7B/14B-Instruct at a claimed 32K **extended to 128K with DCA+YaRN**, a training-free inference-time method on one set of weights, so the 32K→128K step has no second checkpoint and its short-context delta is zero by construction rather than by measurement; and the 1M models were "developed based on Qwen2.5 models" from "an intermediate version from Qwen2.5 Base models", not from the released 128K checkpoint, so the comparison the report already prints (Table 6 / Sec. 6.2: MMLU-Pro 56.3 vs 54.3, GPQA 36.4 vs 41.4, MATH 75.5 vs 72.9, HumanEval 84.8 vs 86.0, non-monotone in both model sizes) is a sibling-model difference and not a cost attributable to the extension. So limb (d) is, for the candidate's own flagship example, simultaneously **already published** and **unattributable** — and the clause that it is a measurable per-step quantity is assumed by the wording, never reported by it, so the measurand exemption does not reach it.

## What would reverse this, and what kind of document it would take
