Question (canonical):
V1 (intake): Is the near-equal partition of total cell biomass across logarithmic cell-size classes reported for the human body a general feature of animal bodies, and does the slope of that within-body cell-size spectrum shift with species body mass?
V2 (the single permitted Gate 1 reformulation, for well-posedness only; NOT the version novelty was graded on — both were searched): For S >= 3 animal species spanning >= 10^4-fold body mass, with "one cell" fixed in advance by a written rule that disposes of the four hard cases (anucleate erythrocytes counted as cells; a multinucleate skeletal myofibre counted as one cell with its mass as measured, and also, as a declared sensitivity, as one cell per myonuclear domain; an adipocyte at its in-situ lipid-filled volume; a neuron including its processes), and with a declared volume-estimation protocol and propagated uncertainty: (a) what is the fitted log-log slope b_s of cell count on cell mass within each body, with 95% CI, estimated under a pre-declared size-spectrum fitting method; (b) does b_s differ from -1 (the equal-biomass-per-log-class value) and from the published human value -0.97 (CI -1.09 to -0.87); (c) is b_s independent of species body mass, tested as a slope-equality / regression-of-slopes test rather than against an unspecified "predicted direction"; (d) how much of each species' |b_s + 1| survives dropping that species' two or three dominant tissues by biomass; and (e) are equal-biomass classes also equal-energy classes under measured or interval-bounded per-cell metabolic rates?
Scored as canonical for the record: V1, because the Gate 2 ground attaches to V1's own words and V2 does not escape it.

Alternative formulations:
- Do animals other than humans show roughly equal total biomass per logarithmic cell-size class, and is the exponent conserved across a wide range of body mass?
- Is the within-organism distribution of cell biomass over cell size a conserved allometric property, or an incidental consequence of the particular tissue composition of the human body?

Motivating gap (as at intake, then corrected by the read):
Intake held that Hatton et al., "The human cell count and size distribution" (PNAS 2023), report a whole-body census in which cell count and cell size are near-inversely related so that each logarithmic size class holds roughly similar total biomass; that this is numerically reminiscent of the Sheldon spectrum in pelagic ecosystems, whose size-structured predation cascade has no counterpart inside a body; and that the anomaly is reported for one species, at one life stage, by one census, with no comparative measurement and no mechanistic test. READ LEVEL at intake: title/abstract only, NOT OPENED.
CORRECTION after opening the paper at the publisher (see Gate 2, F3/F5): two of the three intake claims do not survive.
(1) The comparative question is POSED IN PRINT by the source paper, in a section headed "A Recurrent Pattern", in these verbatim sentences: "A similar pattern may hold across the bodies of mammal species."; "we should expect a similar inverse cell size-count relation to hold in other mammals"; "the ratios of different cell types in different tissues are constant across species"; "From mouse to elephant, the mean size of most cell types is similar"; "predictable patterns between cell mass and count may be established across mammalian developmental programs". Presuppositions (status after Gate 2):
- The reported near-invariance is real rather than an artefact of pooling heterogeneous literature estimates of cell volume and count. CONTESTED, and the paper concedes the ingredients: a synthesis of >1,500 sources for ~400 cell types (1,264 cell groups), mass "often inferred from microscopy and assumed densities rather than measured directly", source-to-source spread up to 4-fold in size and up to 20-fold in count, and one component (lymphocytes) flagged as possibly needing a 4-fold upward revision.
- Cell number and cell size can be measured for >= 2 further animal species to comparable accuracy. CONTESTED and now quantified as the binding constraint: the human census exists only because a century of reference-man dosimetry and clinical morphometry accumulated the inputs; nothing comparable exists for a second species. The human number is a synthesis, not a measurement, so "comparable accuracy" is a low bar in precision and an extremely high bar in sources.
- "Cell size class" is usable across cell types with extreme geometry. CONTESTED at both tails; V2 exists only to discharge this, and the paper's own flag that "it is not yet clear how these relations might extend to the myonuclear domain" shows the ambiguity is live in the source literature, not invented here.
- A whole-body spectrum is a meaningful object, not a statement about three tissues. CONTESTED and partly falsified in the direction that hurts: the paper reports biomass "dominated by muscle and fat, specifically myocytes and adipocytes", with "myocytes make up about half of cell biomass", and reports the two visible departures from flatness as a break near platelets and haploid sperm (~1e-11 g) and a low-biomass gap between adipocytes (~1e-6 g) and myocytes (~1e-4 g) — i.e. the irregularities are located at exactly the dominant-tissue boundaries.
- Ecosystem size-spectrum theory yields a determinate body-mass prediction that survives transfer once the predation cascade is removed. CONTESTED at intake and, on the read, false as stated: the source paper derives its -1 expectation from balanced growth with a lower size bound, not from any spectrum theory, and offers no body-mass dependence at all. V1's "predicted direction" therefore has no referent; V2 removes it.
- Tractable species span >= 10^4-fold body mass. Mouse-to-elephant spans ~1e5-fold and Hatton's own 33-cell-type dataset already covers that span for mean cell size, so the span is available for means; it is NOT available for whole-body size-class censuses, for which the available N is one.
- Cell-level metabolic rate scales with cell size in a measurable or boundable way. Supported as boundable, not as measured: Savage et al. 2007 infer cellular metabolic rate indirectly (part of it from in vitro data on nine species) rather than measuring it in vivo, and argue from mass-specific metabolic rate falling as ~M^-1/4 that "Bc and mc cannot both remain constant simultaneously".

What would count as an answer:
A cell census by size class for >= 2 further animal species spanning >= 10^4-fold body mass, under a counting rule and volume protocol declared before data collection with propagated uncertainty, delivering (i) a fitted spectrum slope with CI per species; (ii) a test of slope equality across species and of slope-vs-body-mass dependence (under V2, as a slope-equality test against -1 and against the human -0.97, not against an unspecified predicted direction); (iii) a hold-out decomposition showing how much of the human near-invariance survives excluding the two or three dominant tissues; (iv) a check of whether equal-biomass classes are equal-energy classes under measured or bounded per-cell metabolic rates. A clean negative settles it as well as a positive.

Significance — consequence: Argued, and discounted twice. If the answer is yes with a slope statistically indistinguishable from -1 across S species, the within-body cell-size spectrum becomes a comparative trait: a one-parameter summary of body composition that constrains any model of tissue allocation, and PRIOR 1's Zipf-law framing gains the generality it currently only conjectures. If no — slopes differ, or track body mass — then the human result is demoted to a fact about human tissue composition, and since PRIOR 1 explicitly derives its expectation from constant cross-species cell-type ratios, a no would falsify that premise, which is a real and load-bearing claim in the comparative-physiology literature. That is a genuine consequence. But the discounts are heavy. First, the yes answer is close to foregone and therefore low-information: constant cell-type ratios (P1) times body-mass-invariant mean cell sizes (P2, 14 of 18 cell types with CIs including 0) mechanically delivers a similar spectrum, so the experiment largely re-measures its own premises. Second, the informative limbs are the ones the candidate treats as secondary — the dominant-tissue hold-out, which P1's own reported irregularities (the adipocyte-myocyte gap, myocytes at ~half of cell biomass) suggest would bite hard, and the equal-energy check, which Q9 shows nobody has posed for cells in a body.
