# UQ-20261016-07 — Adversary #1 log

## Restatement (my own words)
In a serial chest-radiograph corpus whose labels come from NLP over the attached reports (MIMIC-CXR-type), take every follow-up report that positively re-asserts a finding the patient's immediately preceding report also asserted, where the finding is one a reader could call present/absent on a single film. (a) What share of those re-assertions does a blinded >=3-reader panel, seeing only the current film, call absent — after subtracting how often the same panel calls absent a positively-asserted finding on first-in-series exams? (b) Are report-label false positives higher on follow-ups than on index exams at equal case-mix, and does the excess sit in findings that resolve (effusion, consolidation, oedema, pneumothorax, lines) rather than in ones that persist (cardiomegaly, fibrosis)? (c) Does dropping or re-labelling the follow-ups move a standard classifier's measured performance more than dropping a random subset of equal size? In short: how much of the "ground truth" in report-labelled CXR datasets is copied from the previous report rather than seen on this film, and is that error signed rather than symmetric?

## Recommended verdict
**Provisional.** Nothing Unresolved, but Disguise (2), Presupposition (4), Significance (6) and Reason for silence (7) are Partial; novelty is weakened because the phenomenon has a 50-year-old name in radiology's own error taxonomy ("alliterative error" / "satisfaction of report", Brady 2017 Insights Imaging Table 2) and Olatunji et al. 2019 (arXiv 1905.02283) already quantified report-positive/image-negative at 20% — so the candidate is a signed, exam-position decomposition of a known discrepancy rather than a new category. Promote to Validated if: (i) Kim & Mansfield AJR 2014 and the alliterative-error literature are opened and shown not to report positive-label-on-negative-image rates stratified by follow-up status; (ii) the design adds view/technique matching to the index-exam noise floor; (iii) the motivating gap is reworded to drop "not a category in the field's own error taxonomy".
