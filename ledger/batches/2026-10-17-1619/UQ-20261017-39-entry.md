Domain(s): Medicine — ward care of the deteriorating patient, clinical monitoring and early-warning scores. Method-owning neighbours: clinical prediction-model methodology (calibration/transportability) and biostatistics of informative observation processes.
Question (canonical): In a ward cohort carrying, on the same patients and hours, both continuous device-derived vital signs and concurrent routine clinician-scheduled observation sets, with outcomes ascertained independently of both streams: under a pre-declared aggregation rule (primary worst-value per 1-hour window; sensitivity median / time-above-threshold and 5/15/60/240-min windows) and restricted to the parameters common to both streams, what are the realised calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, discrimination, alert rate per patient-day, PPV and lead time of unchanged published NEWS2 thresholds on each stream; what recalibration restores the intermittent stream's operating point on the continuous stream; and — tested within the intermittent stream alone — does observation intensity carry prognostic information beyond the recorded values, i.e. how much of the score's apparent prognostic value is carried by the timing of the observations rather than the physiology they recorded? (V1; reformulated once at Gate 1, V0 in the record.)
Alternative formulations:
- Is an early-warning score a measurement of the patient, or partly a measurement of how often somebody decided to measure the patient?
- Under continuous ward monitoring, what are the realised alert rate, PPV and lead time of unchanged early-warning thresholds, and what recalibration restores the published operating point?
Motivating gap: Ward observation frequency is clinician-chosen and rises with concern, so every ward EWS was fitted to values recorded at moments selected partly by prognosis; continuous wearables remove that selection while hospitals feed the new stream into the same scores. Anchoring anomaly (postoperative-hypoxaemia continuous-monitoring literature, Sun et al. 2015) recalled only, never opened — unverified.
Presuppositions: P1 observation times informative given values — ESTABLISHED (Jarvis 2015; Agniel 2018). P3 the score needs a declared aggregation rule — CONTESTED and consequential (alert rate 1.2–2/patient-day at 1 h vs 70 at 1 min). P4 unchanged thresholds are applied in service — NOT DOCUMENTED; one feasibility study deliberately ran continuous monitoring without alarms. P5 a paired cohort exists — ESTABLISHED (van Rossum n=46; Wilson n=41 of 48). P6 a published calibration exists to survive — FALSE (Gerry 2020: calibration assessed in 8/22 development models, 36%); and Gerry p12 verbatim, "Calibration of an integer scoring system cannot be assessed because it relies on the model producing predicted probabilities", so NEWS2 as published has no intercept or slope to transport at all.
What would count as an answer: Both streams scored on the same patients under the pre-declared rule, reporting the six estimands per stream; a within-intermittent-stream test of whether measurement intensity (time since last set, sets/24 h, clinician-initiated escalation) adds prognostic information beyond the values; the intercept/slope/threshold map restoring the intermittent operating point; and a declared bound on how much of the cross-stream gap is measurand mismatch rather than timing. Equal calibration and operating point answers in the negative and licenses reusing thresholds.

Significance — consequence: If unchanged thresholds transported, hospitals could pipe continuous streams into existing escalation rules.
