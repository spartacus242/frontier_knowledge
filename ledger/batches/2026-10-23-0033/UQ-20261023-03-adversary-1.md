## Restatement (my words, not the record's)

Does a bacterium's outer shell wear out from being loaded repeatedly, instead of simply breaking when loaded hard enough once? Operationally: trap single *E. coli*, hit each one over and over with the same dilution shock — one small enough that a naive cell survives it — spaced at a fixed recovery interval, and ask whether the chance of bursting on the next shock climbs with the number of shocks already taken, once you account for the biggest shock that cell has seen. If it does: (a) does the typical shock-count-to-bursting fall off as a straight line in log-log against shock size over at least four sizes; (b) is there a shock size so small that nobody bursts however many you give within the achievable budget; (c) if you give two blocks of two different sizes, do the two fractional-life doses add to 1 at bursting. All of it twice: once in growing cells, once with wall synthesis blocked.

Plain version of what is really being asked: is bursting a strength problem (set by the single largest load) or a wear problem (set by how many loads)?
