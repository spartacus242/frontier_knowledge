Domain(s): Other STEM — atmospheric chemistry and aerosol science; cosmochemistry (extraterrestrial mass influx); space-environment science.

Question (canonical): Now that spacecraft-reentry metal input to the stratosphere is comparable to or larger than the cosmic-dust input for several elements, by how much does it bias the tracer-based constraints on cosmic-dust influx to Earth, and can meteoric and spacecraft material still be separated per particle and per snow/ice layer well enough to preserve those constraints as reentry mass grows?
(Gate 1 reformulation V2, used for Gates 2-4: for each published method that constrains extraterrestrial mass influx from metal abundances — per-particle stratospheric aerosol, mesospheric metal layers, meteoric smoke, polar snow/firn/ice — state its tracer elements and compute (a) the fractional bias implied by the published per-element anthropogenic reentry flux and its uncertainty and (b) the reentry mass or date at which that bias exceeds the method's own quoted precision, with the meteoric/spacecraft separation rule declared in advance and its misattribution rate estimated on pre-space-age archived material.)

Alternative formulations:
- Lay: Has space hardware broken the instrument we use to weigh cosmic dust?
- Technical: What is the anthropogenic reentry component of the post-1990 metal-tracer record in stratospheric aerosol, mesospheric layers and polar snow, and what bias does it impose on the inferred extraterrestrial influx?

Motivating gap: Murphy et al., PNAS 120(43) 2023, doi:10.1073/pnas.2313374120 (read in full via the White Rose deposit) finds >20 elements in spacecraft-alloy proportions inside stratospheric sulfuric-acid particles, ~10% (+/-7%) of particles >120 nm carrying reentry metals against ~20% carrying meteoric iron, and reentry Li, Al, Cu and Pb already exceeding the cosmic-dust influx of those metals. Its own stated open items are aerosol properties, oxide persistence, nucleation, optics and novel chemistry; it does not raise the influx-bias limb, and comes nearest with the remark that reentry copper "could possibly double the concentration of copper in Antarctic snow" — framed as deposition, not as bias to a constraint.

Presuppositions: (1) at least one published influx constraint rests on metal abundances in stratospheric aerosol, mesospheric layers, meteoric smoke or polar snow — ESTABLISHED in kind, inventory not assembled. (2) meteoric and spacecraft material are separable per particle by element ratios — ESTABLISHED operationally (Murphy's Al/Fe > 0.1 with Na as discriminator; NASA JSC's C/TCN/TCA/AOS classes) with no published error rate. (3) reentry flux is estimable per element with usable uncertainty — ESTABLISHED (Schulz et al. 2025, 43 elements) but the uncertainty is large and partly judgemental by the authors' own account. (4) pre-space-age archived material exists for the validation the question needs — NOT VERIFIED. (5) FALSIFYING CONDITION: the disputed influx range is sensitive at the magnitude of the anthropogenic input for at least one tracer method — PARTLY ADVERSE: the anthropogenic-dominated elements are the alloy ultratraces (Li, Al, Cu, Pb, Nb, Hf, Ag) while the dominant constraints run on Na, Fe, Mg, Si, Ni, Ir, Pt, Os. Adversary 2 opened one new prior (Egan et al., EGU25-4460) whose element list, as paraphrased, puts Ni and Mn in the already-exceeds-natural set, so (5) is not safe in either direction. (6) tracer bias propagates to influx bias through each method's transport/removal model — NOT VERIFIED, flagged contested; limb (a) is not computable from a flux ratio without it.

What would count as an answer: (i) an enumerated method inventory with tracer elements and a propagated per-method bias; (ii) a measured spacecraft-attributable fraction of the tracer signal under a pre-declared separation rule validated on pre-1960 material, with a stated misattribution rate; (iii) a dated projection of when each method can no longer constrain the natural flux at its own precision. A null — all tracer methods resting on elements where reentry input is small, plus a flat pre/post-1990 contrast — closes it in the negative.
