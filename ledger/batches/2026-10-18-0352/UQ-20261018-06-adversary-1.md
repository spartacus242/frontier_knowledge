## Restatement (my own words)

Pick one monitoring network and one variable it measures (stream stage, coastal water level, or gust speed). Before touching any data, fix a list of station x storm pairs, where the storms come from an outside catalogue, not from the station's own record. For each pair, work out how big the peak really was at that station from evidence that does not depend on the station's instrument (survey marks, neighbours, a hindcast), with an error bar. Then ask two things. (a) Does the chance that the instrument stops reporting during the storm go up with the true peak size, and by how much, with confidence intervals; or is it flat? (b) If you refit the extreme-value model treating the dark peaks as non-ignorably censored (either as a selection model or as "at least the last value recorded"), how far do the 50- and 100-year return levels move versus the usual fit to the published annual-maxima series?

The candidate's claim to being un-asked rests, after Gate 2, on limb (a) alone: the record states "no opened source estimates the probability of instrument loss as a function of the realized magnitude being measured, in the measurand's own units, for any environmental network."
