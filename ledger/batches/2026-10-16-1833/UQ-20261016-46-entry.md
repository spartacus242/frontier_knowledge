Question (canonical): For corpora distributed as URL manifests (LAION-2B-en/Re-LAION, DataComp CommonPool, YFCC100M, OBELICS), pinned to an archived manifest revision and a resolution date T: (i) is the still-retrievable subset a selective rather than random thinning, selective on covariates the manifest itself records (registrable domain, TLD, language, caption length, licence, aesthetic and NSFW deciles, dimensions, shard), measured as per-covariate retention ratios with CIs plus out-of-fold AUC and calibration of a retention model against a pre-registered null band on N = 200k rows per corpus; and (ii) at fixed compute, does a model trained on the retrievable-at-T subset differ from one trained on a size-matched random subsample of the same archived manifest by more than a ±2 SD within-arm seed-to-seed equivalence band on the dataset's own suite (k ≥ 5 seeds per arm)?
Alternative formulations: (1) Has "trained on LAION-2B" stopped naming a fixed distribution — do two labs that train on it years apart train on measurably different data and get measurably different models? (2) Is link attrition in URL-distributed corpora ignorable given what the manifest observes? (3) Do URL-distributed training corpora suffer persistence bias in Elmas's sense, and does it propagate to a trained model rather than only to a descriptive statistic?
Motivating gap: The artifact a lab obtains is whatever the web still serves on download day, while papers, model cards and licences cite the dataset as a fixed object. Attrition is large and dated (Hong et al.: 21.4% of 12.8M CommonPool URLs failed, April 2025; mlfoundations/datacomp #3 ~91% success May 2023, #41 0.884 July 2023); manifests are edited (Re-LAION, 2024-08-30). The intake claim that the silence is total was withdrawn at Gate 2: Hong et al. compared 1,000 failed vs 1,000 successful URLs on three image-derived attributes and called the differences "slight". Distinct from UQ-20261016-38 (robots.txt publisher-side exclusion in Common Crawl text) and UQ-20261015-04 (Wikipedia citation hazard by topic), both read in full.

Verification (post-batch, fresh-context checker, log UQ-20261016-46-verification.md): the Gate 4 kill is REFUTED at its load-bearing figure.
The rejection rested on “substitution is 59-65% of surviving URLs (adversary arithmetic on Carlini et al.'s stated CC-3M counts, arXiv:2302.10149)”. That paper states no such counts. A checker handed only the claim and the identifier reports: it gives about 1.04% of CC-3M images on expired domains (Table I, DNS record absent), at least 0.08% buyable for $1K, and in Section IV-D a domain-level content check — “Among all domains hosting more than 10 images, we find just one domain has our attack signature” “when comparing images with perceptual similarity”, and “If we instead compare cryptographic hashes of images, we find two additional domains that have been repurchased”, both judged benign (lapsed DNS, re-encoded images). The unit is the domain, not the URL; the estimand is a count of domains, not a fraction of surviving URLs; and no date is attached to the content-change analysis (DNS lookups ran May and August 2022). There is therefore no published majority-substitution rate to derive 59-65% from, in this paper or, as far as the checker could establish, anywhere.
What survives from the kill: the paper does treat dead links and content substitution as two different measurements, so the confound the adversary pointed at is real — its magnitude is simply unknown, and on the only published measurement it is small rather than dominant. The entry is re-based accordingly.
