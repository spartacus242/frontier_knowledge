## The mix clause and the one-area rotation are in conflict, and this is the first batch where it bites

LEDGER.md carries two rules that cannot both hold for a biology or a medicine batch.

- **Parameters, "ONE AREA PER BATCH, ROTATING"** (Jace 2026-10-16, cycle set 2026-10-28): "all five candidates in a batch come from a single area … A candidate outside the batch's area is a failed candidate however good it looks — replace it rather than stretch the area to fit."
- **Depth requirement, in-scope fields** (written 2026-10-28): "Of the five candidates in a batch, at least two must be in mathematics, physics or computer science and at most two in medicine; a batch that cannot manage that returns fewer than five rather than filling the gap."

Taken per batch, the second clause is satisfiable for three of the five areas and unsatisfiable for two:

|---|---|---|
| mathematics | satisfied (5) | satisfied (0) |
| physics | satisfied (5) | satisfied (0) |
| computer science | satisfied (5) | satisfied (0) |
| **biology** | **violated (0)** | satisfied (0) |

A medicine batch is impossible under the literal per-batch reading: trimming to two candidates satisfies the medicine cap and still leaves zero in mathematics, physics or computer science, so the batch could only return nothing. A rule that forbids two of the five areas outright cannot be what was meant, since the same document sets the rotation that requires those two areas to take their turns.

**The reading used here, and why.** The mix clause is a distribution constraint over the cycle, not over the batch. The rotation already delivers it: one full cycle is 25 candidates, 15 in mathematics, physics and computer science and 5 in medicine, which satisfies both bounds at 3 of 5 and 1 of 5. The one-area rule is the one with an authoritative state file, the stronger wording, and the one the runner enforces — `unasked-ledger-batch-sequential-v17.js` tells the generator the area "is not negotiable". Since the runner is what actually runs, the conflict was already resolved in favour of the rotation before this batch; what was missing was a record of it.

## An operational fact that changes the effective cadence, and is Jace's to decide

**The 06:33 UTC firing of 2026-10-30 landed mid-batch and was absorbed.** That is the established handling — LEDGER.md forbids a second concurrent batch, and two firings were absorbed the same way on 2026-10-29 — and an absorbed firing does not spend an area's turn, so the next batch still takes medicine.

What is new is the duration. This batch started at 00:33 and was still in its verification and amendment layers at 06:33: **six hours, against the three the process notes assume.** The extra time is not slack. It is the three layers that now run after Gate 5 — a 44-check verification pass, a per-entry amendment agent that audits the judge's compression, and a correction pass that applies those audits to the write-ups. They are what moved two verdicts on this batch and what found 119 defects in the judge's own rulings, so they are earning their time.

But at six hours per batch on a six-hour cron, **every second firing is absorbed, and the ledger advances one area per twelve hours rather than per six.** The rotation still turns in order; it turns at half the rate the cadence implies. Three things could be done about it, and the choice is Jace's rather than a session's:

1. **Leave it.** The cadence becomes effectively twelve-hourly and the rotation takes two and a half days per cycle instead of one and a quarter. Nothing is lost but tempo, and the absorbed firings cost nothing.
2. **Move the cron to every twelve hours** so the schedule states what actually happens. This is the honest version of option 1 and makes the absorbed firings stop appearing as skipped runs.
3. **Shorten the batch** by cutting one of the post-Gate-5 layers. This is the only option that trades quality for tempo, and on this batch's evidence it would be the wrong trade: without the verification pass this batch would have shipped 60% rejected with two kills that do not survive contact with their own sources, and without the amendment audit it would have shipped 119 misstatements in the permanent record.

No change was made. The cron, the trigger prompt and LEDGER.md all still say six hours.

### What moved, entry by entry

**UQ-20261030-03 reverses, Rejected to Provisional.** The kill stood on Leg B, which its adversary presented as carrying "no inference of mine": the printed sibling-8-versus-cousin-7 odds ratios in the lineage-memory preprint (bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.06.27.661951). A reader at full text found those two numbers printed only inside an illustrative parenthetical — "(e.g., with values of 8 and 7, respectively, for a threshold of 15 VSV mRNA)" — read off a plotted curve and supporting only the claim "far above 1". There is **no n and no denominator** behind them (the single n = 2 in the figure belongs to panel B's clone-CV bars), **no CI, SE, SEM, error bar, plus-or-minus or bootstrap anywhere in the document** on a complete string search, and **no sibling-versus-cousin test**: the only inferential machinery is a 500-shuffle permutation null testing each of the two against no association, so 8 and 7 are each contrasted with 1 and never with each other. The ~35-division half-life the kill leaned on is doubly derived, from fitted k_on = k_off = 0.02/day through ln2/0.02 = 34.657 days and then into divisions by an uncited "roughly divide once a day". A presupposition failure cannot rest on a contrast the source never drew.

The entry's own named unopened residue then answered the question the kill had decided by inference. arXiv:2004.06029, read at full text by two routes (arXiv v3 HTML and the eLife version of record at par.nsf.gov), finds that a non-genetic shared state **does decay with relatedness**: 0.86 at generation 1 falling to 0.00 by generation 9, fitted as e^(−0.23g). Three independent re-derivations put that fit's half-life at 3.0–3.6 generations against the caption's asserted 4.5 — a discrepancy in the source, recorded as the readers' arithmetic and not as the paper's figure.

**UQ-20261030-04 reverses, Rejected to Provisional, on its own pre-declared terms.** Its adversary had written the reversal condition into the record: an opened human source printing a de-novo somatic mtDNA frequency at or above 10⁻⁵/bp. A subject-only reader, given no paper, found Kennedy SR et al., PLoS Genetics 2013;9(9):e1003794, doi 10.1371/journal.pgen.1003794, printing **1.9 × 10⁻⁵ per bp for aged human prefrontal cortex, interval 1.7–2.1 × 10⁻⁵** — sixteenfold the mouse figure the kill used. Re-derived at L = 16,569 bp, λ moves 0.0199 → 0.3148 and multi-mutation molecules per 1,000 move **0.2 → 40.3** (186 at the caudate copy number of ~4,617), easing headroom clause C1 from about 150% of signal to about 10%. The kill's premise was "at the only per-base frequency anyone opened", and that premise is simply false now.

Separately, and independent of the reversal: **adversary #1's two quoted strings are not in the document the entry attributes them to.** They are verbatim in bioRxiv 2023.04.14.536897, where they form one sentence rather than two findings, and describe sequencing errors the authors themselves call "a non-specific proxy" for damage.

**UQ-20261030-05 stays Rejected, and is now over-determined rather than merely upheld.** The kill's ground survived first-hand verification of its source — quotation verbatim, own-voice Results, within-line scope, no pore count and no absolute flux — so under LEDGER.md's late-Gate-4 rule no late pass is owed, Gate 4 having already run. One decoration is struck: the clause "does not present a direct, designed comparison of baseline transport between cell types" is **not in the paper**, verbatim or otherwise. And the entry's *headline novelty claim is false*: Ribbeck & Görlich 2001 (doi 10.1093/emboj/20.6.1320), which the entry itself named as unopened residue, reports absolute per-pore flux as its native unit — "a single NPC is capable of translocating 2500 NTF2 homodimers or ~800 transportin molecules per second". A subject sweep confirmed this is not one document but a literature. Novelty on the headline quantity falls to **Low**, so the entry is now rejected at a second and better-sourced gate as well as on its attack-4 failure. What survives as genuinely unposed is narrower and is stated as such in the entry: a per-pore flux whose denominator is a *counted* pore number in the same cells, for one declared cargo across several cell types, with a between-cell-type variance decomposition in mammalian cells.

**UQ-20261030-01 stays Provisional, with novelty narrowed against it.** Ogrodnik et al., PNAS 2014 — named in the entry's *own* promotion condition — scores JUNQ and IPOD within the same division (Fig 2G, n = 29, 80% together against "roughly a fifth" apart, chi² P ≤ 0.0001). The qualitative limb of the candidate's question is therefore answered and novelty on it falls to **Low**; what survives is the signed rank-correlation matrix under a label-permutation null across three damage classes, which is a finer object than an 80/20 binary concordance over two aggregate species. The entry now says so, and says that a future reader may reasonably re-reject it as a disguised known at the qualitative grain. Of its three promotion conditions, one is discharged (the unrun eighth Disguise query was run), one is answered in the negative (no published instance of live mother-centriole-age ordering alongside two or more damage channels exists; the strongest support for that negative is that a 2024 review by the author of the nearest papers surveys exactly this literature and never mentions centriole age), and one is **withdrawn as unreachable rather than carried as undischarged** — no open copy of Blood 2022;139(13):2011–2023 exists, and a condition nobody here can discharge is not a condition.

**UQ-20261030-02 stays Provisional, and its own central concession is withdrawn.** The entry's Gate 5 line claimed three adversaries had each found a nearer prior, falsifying two of Gate 2's "no such framing" negatives, the load-bearing one being that Bousios et al. 2020 (Mobile DNA) "derives a TE re-integration contact-probability law and announces the gap". Read at full text by two routes, **both halves of that sentence fail**: the expression P_c(s) ~ s^(−3ν) is imported from the polymer-physics literature with citations at the formula and at every exponent regime, the one new step is self-labelled by its authors "a crude calculation", it is fitted to no transposon data; and the word "donor" does not occur anywhere in the paper, which is in any case a review-and-perspective rather than primary data. So Gate 2's negative was not falsified by that document, and novelty is **better** supported than the entry granted.

The danger moved to the other 1994 paper. The entry's promotion condition made its fate turn on Tower & Kurapati 1994, whose model is homology-dependent on its abstract — the safe branch — while **Dooner et al. 1994** (Genetics 136(1):261–279) proposes a *general spatial* mechanism in the abstract's own words ("a physical association between the donor site and nearby receptor sites", "an ordering of the chromosomes in the interphase nucleus") and **prints a donor-relative statistic with a denominator**: 10 of 37 and 7 of 37 unlinked insertions on the donor chromosome. The candidate's own hypothesis was therefore posed, with a general mechanism and a quantified donor-chromosome statistic, in 1994 for maize *Ac*. The condition is re-set against Dooner rather than Tower. It does not reject the entry — both reads are abstract-only, an unread body grounds no novelty-Low grade, and the mechanisms differ materially since *Ac* reinserts as DNA at its excision site while L1 leaves the nucleus as RNA — but the entry no longer claims the question is unposed at chromosome or cytological grain.

Two further priors proved *further* from the candidate than the entry claimed, and one deferral was discharged negative. Engreitz et al. 2012, reached at full text where the record had only an abstract, turns out to have its authors **explicitly disclaiming** the analysis the entry treated as a prior ("the datasets analyzed here were not sufficient to directly test whether the formation frequencies of these translocations are correlated with the contact frequencies between genomic partners"), with the only statistic in the passage, p = 0.532, belonging to the correlation that *failed*. Servant et al. 2017's "No chromosome was specifically targeted by L1 de novo insertions" is verbatim and resolved, but its donor is **episomal** — a construct cloned in pCEP4 with no mapped chromosomal coordinate anywhere in the paper — so a sentence about chromosomes cannot answer a donor-proximity question when the donor has no genomic position to be proximal to. And Babushok et al. 2006, the one prior the entry had deferred to an unopened supplemental count table, has **no such table**: the supplement is a single file of Methods, three Supplemental Discussions and two reference lists, the word "Table" does not occur in it, and the article never cites a "Supplemental Table" anywhere. That deferral is discharged negative on a read that reached the whole supplement — "not in the document", not "could not read it".

That paper carried a lead worth more than the negative. **The one chromosome with a significant integration excess is itself one of the donor loci, and the paper never says so**: the H27 founder carries transgenes at chromosome 11, chromosome 18 and one unmapped site, and the single chromosome-level result is an excess on chromosome 18 at P = 0.017, which the authors explain by gene density and AT-rich endonuclease target sites. It cannot be settled from the published record — the chromosome-18 count is not printed, each insertion's donor is never assigned, and no coordinates were deposited — but it is the closest thing in the literature to the candidate's question lying latent in a dataset and left unasked. The paper's own chromosome-level denominator is also internally inconsistent: 48 uniquely mapped, 47 plotted, and "n = 49 insertions with assigned chromosome number" in the supplement's test, unreconciled, and 49 is the denominator the P value actually uses.

## What the amending agents found in the judge's own rulings

Every amending agent was asked to report the judge's errors, as team memory requires, and all five did. **They found 119, against 67 in the computer-science batch of 2026-10-29: 28 on UQ-01, 26 on UQ-02, 24 on UQ-03, 19 on UQ-04 and 22 on UQ-05.** A second round of five agents then applied those audits to the write-ups, and a third round applied the corrections the audits had located in files outside the write-ups — entries, Gate 0–3 records and adversary logs already committed.

The write-ups grew from 87 KB to 139 KB under correction, **a 60% increase with no sentence deleted.** That is the shape of the defect: almost every correction added back a scope, a read level, an attribution or a hedge that the compression had removed. The judge's compression of a verification note remains the least reliable document in the chain, and it is less reliable than this ledger had so far recorded.

**The recurring defect is new, and it is the one worth carrying forward: AN ENTRY'S FACE BLAMED FOR A PHRASE THAT ONLY ITS WORKING PAPERS USED.** It landed at least eight times across four entries, and in the worst instance it set a verdict. On UQ-05 the write-up opened by declaring "THE HEADLINE NOVELTY CLAIM IS FALSE", quoting the entry as asserting that the quantity is "genuinely unprinted (no reader can name a published molecules·pore⁻¹·s⁻¹ figure for any cell type)". Neither string is in the entry. Both are adversary 3's own words in its working log, and the entry's two actual formulations are narrower in the way that decides the point: one is scoped to *two or more cell types*, which the finding never touched, and the other to a *printed* value. Ribbeck & Görlich is one cell type, so what falls is the adversary's formulation and the any-cell-type half of the entry's — not the claim the write-up declared false. The same error ran on UQ-03, where the write-up quoted the entry as saying its leg carried "no inference of mine", a phrase that occurs zero times in the entry and once in adversary #3's log.

The consequence is a rule this ledger did not have: **an entry is answerable for its own face, and a verdict that quotes an entry must be checked against the entry file, not against the record or the adversary log that fed it.** Four more instances were a date conflation, a "back-of-envelope" characterisation, a Spearman-coefficient claim with its read-level scope stripped, and a page range — each belonging to a record or a brief and each attributed to the entry.

The other defects fell into five families, all of them failures of the same kind — a reader's own epistemic care discarded in compression.

- **Read level overstated, and COULD NOT READ rendered as NOT FOUND.** The commonest single fault. Negatives a reader had scoped explicitly to the sections it was served ("not printed anywhere in the Abstract, Introduction, Results, Figure 1–4 legends or Discussion as served — not absent from the paper as a whole") were written as flat statements about whole papers. On UQ-03 this happened twice on the two negatives that matter most, where the Materials and Methods — the one place the source itself says the computation lives — were reached by no route.
- **Reconstructions presented as printed quotations.** The fetcher caps quoted material near 125 characters, so readers returned long passages as ordered consecutive segments and labelled them reconstructions. At least nine quotations across four write-ups were offered as the paper's printed words, including both of the Heldt figures and the Snijder percentages on UQ-03 and the "nearly 100 MDa/s" headline on UQ-05 — which is not only a reconstruction but a claim the paper's own arithmetic never reaches, its body computing 80.
- **A reader's labelled inference promoted to a finding.** Readers were instructed to separate their own reasoning into an inference field, and did. The compression then used the most load-bearing of those as though the paper had said it: on UQ-03 the cousin mapping the reader called "my own inference, and it is the load-bearing one", and the panel assignment behind a fitted decay constant the reader said plainly "is not printed"; on UQ-05 the whole measured-versus-divided-by-a-pore-count distinction, which its author flagged as "MY OWN FRAMING, not a claim any paper makes" and which is nevertheless the most useful thing the pass produced.
- **A reader's own grade upgraded.** "Not satisfied anywhere I read" became "satisfied nowhere". "Durrieu et al. is the one I found", with an instruction attached to report it as "not found, search incomplete" and never as "does not exist", became "only one variance decomposition exists at all". A negative a reader rated good evidence of absence from a summariser's term search became a verified absence.
- **Counts.** Every reader count in the batch was wrong. UQ-01's write-up said ten readers where twelve returned across thirteen launches, and one subject sweep where there were two. UQ-03's said five in one paragraph and six in another, against nine. UQ-05's said five against seven. Route and document counts were wrong too, in both directions.

Two findings are worse than a misstatement. **On UQ-01 the write-up omitted two whole checks of that entry** — Hill et al. 2016 and Zhou et al. 2014, both COULD NOT READ — and the second of them is the source behind a mechanistic claim the entry's own adversarial log cites as published, which nobody in this pipeline has read past its Summary and title. And **on UQ-01 the write-up "withdrew" a sentence of the entry that is true**, on a misquotation of it; the correction reinstates it.

**The judge's briefs were defective too, and three are recorded in the write-ups rather than left out:** the Bojang brief named two of Crossref's four authors, the Bousios brief asked whether the paper "derives" a law and so embedded the claim under test, and a brief on the nuclear-transport range presupposed it was built from two figures whose endpoints in fact come from two other studies.
