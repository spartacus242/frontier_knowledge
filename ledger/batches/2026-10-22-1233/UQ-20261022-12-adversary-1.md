## 1. Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Standards bodies publish a reproducibility limit R for each test method, derived from one interlaboratory study (ILS). Some methods have been put through a second, independent ILS on matching materials. The candidate asks: over a pre-declared frame of such (method, material-class) pairs, how far apart are the two published R values (distribution of log(R2/R1)), and is that scatter wider than the pure sampling scatter you would predict from each study's own laboratory count, material count and replicate structure? A second limb asks for a census of how old and how small the studies behind currently active precision statements are. Pairs whose design fields cannot be recovered are to be counted and reported, not dropped.

What the question CONDITIONS ON (written out before testing; see attack 4): (C1) that such twice-studied method pairs exist in non-trivial number; (C2) that (p, m, n) are recoverable — converted by R1 into a reported coverage gap; (C3) that the design-implied spread of s_R is given by "the chi-square sampling distribution" at (p, m, n); (C4) that the published R IS the study's s_R-derived output, so that a ratio of published Rs is a ratio of two statistics; (C5) that the two studies are INDEPENDENT (distinct laboratory populations); (C6) that "comparable materials" can be declared by rule — R1 makes this a declared rule, so the result is conditional on it; (C7) second limb: that the study date and counts are recoverable for active methods — R1 again reports failures as a gap.
