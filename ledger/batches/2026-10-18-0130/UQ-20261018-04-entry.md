Domain(s): Medicine — perioperative care, infection prevention, sterile processing. Method-owning neighbours: reliability engineering (usage-based service life), hospital asset/clinical engineering.
Question (canonical): In hospital systems whose instrument-tracking system records per-ITEM cumulative reprocessing-cycle counts and per-item-to-case linkage, what are the three separately-estimated associations between an item's cycle count (and calendar age) at the moment of use and (i) intraoperative malfunction or exchange of that item (item-use level); (ii) sterilisation-process failure of the load or tray containing it (load/tray level); and (iii) NHSN-defined surgical-site infection at 30 and 90 days in the case it served (case level, exposure = case max or mean item cycle count) — each adjusted for service line, surgeon, case complexity and tray type, each with a pre-declared negative-control exposure and stated minimum detectable effect? (V2; the one Gate 1 reformulation. V1 asked whether cycle count and age "predict" malfunction, sterilisation failure and SSI — one verb over three units of analysis. Novelty finding applies to both.)
Alternative formulations:
- Do reusable surgical instruments have a measurable service life in clinical use, and is current practice — retire on visible damage, or at a bench-validated cycle limit — consistent with it?
- Is the number of times an instrument set has been through decontamination a risk factor for anything that happens to the patient it is used on?
Motivating gap: Per-item cycle histories linked to cases are a by-product of barcode/RFID tracking claimed to be unused as an exposure, with contamination research concentrated on flexible endoscopes by outbreak pressure and rigid-instrument life governed by bench cycle simulations. LOAD-BEARING CORRECTION (Gate 2, extended by both adversaries): the premise is false. Yoshikawa 2019 took per-item cumulative use counts out of an RFID instrument-management-system log (34,390 operations, 2013–2017) and used them as the exposure against instrument failure; Homma 2023 compared cleaning counts and age across 69 in-use endoscopic devices; Pitschner 2007 read each catheter's reprocessing count off its barcode, joined it to the patient and blinded it to the operator.
Presuppositions: (1) item-level cycle data with per-case linkage retrievable for research — CONTESTED; item-to-operation linkage exists in at least one deployment, item-to-patient-outcome linkage unevidenced (P1's team held the key and used no patient data). (2) cycles are a meaningful wear exposure — CONTESTED and evidenced against: positive but negligible (P1, OR 1.026/use, R² 0.032), null (Homma 2023: cleanings 281 vs 261 P=0.27; age 7 vs 7 y P=0.90), inverted in the scope literature (P2, aOR 0.80). (3) intraoperative malfunction usably recorded — CONTESTED, and adversary 2 found direct evidence against: Nichol 2024 put 24 observers into 7 ORs because instrument errors are logged by manual database entry not linked to the EMR; both device-side priors used proxies (repair request; IF detector). (4) confounding by tray type, service line, surgeon addressable — CONTESTED, unresolved. (5) manufacturers' limits bench-derived — VERIFIED (reprocessing-validation lab's own end-of-life cycle-series simulation under ISO 10993-1/-15). (6) SSI attributable at individual-item level — WEAK; no prior attempts it, and limb (ii)'s wear-to-load-failure mechanism has no source on the record.
What would count as an answer: A pre-registered cohort in one or more item-level-tracking systems, cases linked to the specific trays and items used, estimating pre-declared exposures (cycle count at use; item age; cycles since inspection) against three pre-declared endpoints — OR-recorded malfunction/exchange; load or tray sterilisation-process failure (BI failure, wet pack, load recall); NHSN SSI at 30/90 days — adjusted as above, with a negative-control exposure (cycle count of an instrument type not used in that procedure) and a stated MDE per limb. Gate 1: well-posed in V2 (3/3); the OR-record endpoint is a feasibility defect in the answer-criterion, not an ill-posedness.
