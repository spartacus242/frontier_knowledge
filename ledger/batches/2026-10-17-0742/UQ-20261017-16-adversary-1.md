## Restatement (my own words)

A cardiac-arrest, stroke or STEMI case timeline in a registry is stitched together from timestamps written by several machines that each keep their own clock (dispatch computer, defibrillator/monitor, ambulance ePCR, ED triage system, inpatient EHR, lab analyser). The candidate asks three things: (a) how far each of those clocks is from true time, expressed as a distribution per pair of sources that share a timeline; (b) what fraction of those clocks are already kept on a traceable reference; and (c) if you take a published "outcome falls X% per minute of delay" estimate and re-fit it treating the timestamps as mismeasured by the distribution found in (a), how much does the per-minute slope, its CI, and the implied value of a one-minute gain move. The claimed payoff: response-time targets may rest on an attenuated slope.

## Recommended verdict

No attack is Unresolved, so Gate 4 alone would allow Provisional at best (attacks 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 Partial). But novelty does not hold: my Disguise sweep confirms the Gate 2 finding and sharpens it — the offset limb was named, quantified as "errors of many minutes" and engineered around in a 1996 Survivalink patent (US5749902) and measured against NIST by Brabrand 2012; only the slope re-fit is un-done, and it is asked-but-open (Brabrand asserts it; Goodwin recommends it). Recommended: **Rejected (Gate 2 ground stands — limb (a) already asked/partly answered; slope limb routed to routed-open-problems as an unperformed quantitative-bias analysis, restricted to genuinely multi-clock intervals such as call-to-first-shock and EMS-to-ED hand-off).**

Single strongest killing point: the per-minute slope that underwrites the response-time targets the candidate wants to re-examine (Utstein core "Response time", PSAP call-connect to arrival-at-scene) is written by one dispatch-domain clock, so inter-source clock offset cannot bias it; the intervals that do cross clocks were already identified as offset-corrupted ("errors of many minutes") and corrected by sync-at-download in a 1996 patent, and are dominated by a different, unmodelled error (bystander collapse-time estimation).
