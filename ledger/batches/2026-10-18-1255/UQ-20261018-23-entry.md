Domain(s): Medicine — clinical informatics; real-world-evidence methods; deployed clinical decision support. Source method: cross-field transplant (migration-fidelity auditing from digital preservation into medicine).

Question (canonical):
For a pre-declared frame of K health-system EHR replacements, under an element-class taxonomy declared before any data pull (structured observation, free-text note, waveform, image metadata, order, medication-administration record, scanned/imported document, device or interface feed) and with "queryable" pre-defined as retrievable through the receiving system's own research query surface (CDW or OMOP/i2b2-style extract) WITHOUT recourse to a read-only legacy viewer: (i) what is the per-class survival fraction at departmental go-live; (ii) in >= 2 systems, does a before/after element-density audit with the go-live as breakpoint, plus one negative and one positive control class, reproduce the specification-derived fractions; (iii) for one pre-declared published EHR-based analysis and one deployed risk model whose look-back crosses that boundary, what is the sign and size of the induced change in feature availability and realised calibration? (V2, the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation; V1 as intaken is in the record.)

Alternative formulations:
- What does a hospital lose, as evidence, when it changes EHR vendor — and has anyone audited it?
- Treating an EHR replacement as an archival migration, what is its fidelity by data class, and how far back is the record actually usable afterwards?

Motivating gap:
Digital preservation treats a system migration as an epistemic event — significant properties declared in advance, fidelity audited, losses published. Medicine performs the same operation constantly and treats it as an IT and cost decision: a declared subset converts, while notes, waveforms, order and administration detail, device feeds and older results go to a read-only legacy viewer or a scanned-document store. Meanwhile RWE studies claim decade-long look-back and deployed models carry 1-5 year features across those dates, and no reporting checklist (STROBE/RECORD/RECORD-PE, TRIPOD+AI, CODE-EHR) requires a migration-history statement.

Presuppositions:
1. CONTESTED, likely binding — per-transition conversion specifications are documented and retrievable (if not, limb (i) falls back to limb (ii)). Supported in one public case by GAO-22-103718 (A1).
2. ESTABLISHED by construction — "survival as a queryable element" is definable per class; V2 pins it to the research query surface.
3. CONTESTED — loss is patterned by element class rather than idiosyncratic per site. CONTRADICTED IN PRINT at Gate 4: Miake-Lye et al. 2023, "no two sites used the same approach to data migration"; Schreiber & Garber 2020, "no two sites have reported the same approaches."
4. CONTESTED, must be demonstrated on a named sample — published RWE analyses and deployed models whose windows cross migration dates exist.
5. DEFENSIBLE — a read-only legacy archive is not equivalent to migrated data for research or CDS. Supported by GAO (non-migrated ECG/blood-bank served via the JLV viewer).
6. CONTESTED but tractable — migration dates are retrievable and sharp; staggered go-lives modelled per department. WEAKENED: GAO shows VA scoped conversion by patient-activity status and date range as well as class, so a single breakpoint sees a ramp, not a step.
7. FLAGGED — the legacy denominator is countable at all, in a decommissioned system.
8. NEWLY FALSIFIED at Gate 4 — that a post-migration queryable element implies a converted element. At least one Schreiber & Garber site "relied on manual abstraction and data entry," so the numerator pools conversion fidelity with human re-entry effort.

What would count as an answer:
Three separately reportable limbs exactly as V2 states them: a per-class survival table over >= K transitions; a density audit in >= 2 systems with go-live breakpoints and both controls; and a demonstration limb reporting change in feature availability and realised calibration. "Nothing is lost that matters" is a legitimate answer and would close it.
