The three rejections got one adversary each; the two survivors got two. In the 05:22 batch, where the first pass rejected two and the rerun did fire, the rerun gave a *third* adversary to each of the three survivors. So under both branches of the rule the rejections are the least-attacked entries in the batch — and a rejection is the verdict that ends the inquiry, while a Provisional keeps its promotion condition and comes back. The rule allocates its scrutiny to the entries that are least costly to get wrong.

Nothing here is a reason to doubt the three rejections: each was checked at first hand afterwards and each held. It is a reason to doubt the rule. The cheap repair is to stop conditioning the extra adversary on the rate at all and spend it on whichever candidate's kill rests on the fewest independently read sources — in this batch that would have been UQ-16 and UQ-20, each killed on a single adversary's reading of sources the batch had not opened.

## One bad habit, four misattributed figures

UQ-20's entry carried five figures for its decisive prior, Karcher & Lehman 2014, that the paper does not contain: 21% abandonment (the paper says 11.2%), roughly 60 and 90 minutes for stat and non-stat specimens (the paper says 54 and 88), an "arbitrary six-hour window" for abandonment (the paper sets no window at all; its criterion is "failure to recollect or relabel a rejected specimen"), and a list of named harms — haematoma, phlebotomy-associated anaemia, nerve injury — that appears nowhere in it. All of them came from one source: a *CAP TODAY* trade-press review of July 2012, which the batch could open when the 2014 paper itself was behind a captcha, and which therefore describes an earlier Q-Probes cycle or preliminary data. The paper was opened on the second verification attempt at the publisher's own article page, and every figure was corrected in the entry.

The general rule this earns: **a trade-press or review account of a study is a pointer to the study, never a substitute for its numbers.** When the primary source cannot be opened, the honest move is to record the figures as unread rather than to take a secondary account's version and attribute it to the primary. Two of this batch's other entries now carry the same discipline explicitly — every Haviari figure in UQ-18 is marked abstract-level, and Kato's v2 title and Appendix D.6 are marked as resting on a third-party summary.

## What to carry forward

1. **Fix the rerun condition.** Conditioning the extra adversary on the batch's rejection rate guarantees that in every batch the rejections are the least-attacked entries. Spend it instead on whichever kill rests on the fewest independently read sources.
2. **A review is not the paper.** Four wrong figures in one entry came from treating a trade-press summary as the primary source's numbers. When the primary cannot be opened, the figures are unread.
3. **Say which level you read at, in the entry.** The checkers that reported `read_level` explicitly (FULL TEXT / ABSTRACT ONLY / THIRD-PARTY SUMMARY / NOT READ) produced findings that could be acted on; the ones that did not forced a second pass. That field is worth keeping in every verification prompt.
4. **An unverifiable claim still has a direction.** UQ-18's unread body would, if read, more likely deepen the rejection than lift it. Marking an unverified claim is necessary; saying which way it would cut if checked is what tells a later reader whether to spend a fetch on it.
5. **Three checkers overran their fetch caps** (17-watkinson 7/6, 19-ghana 8/6, and one in the 05:22 batch). The residual pass's prompt added an explicit warning that an overrun discounts the findings, and all three of its readers stayed inside the cap. Keep the warning.
