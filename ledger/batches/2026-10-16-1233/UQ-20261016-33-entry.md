Question (canonical): Computed with safeguards material-balance discipline — an inventory-difference statistic, a propagated measurement-and-documentation variance, and a stated detection probability at a stated false-alarm rate — what is the smallest sustained controlled-substance diversion rate a hospital's reconciliation and analytics can detect per dispensing unit per month, and how does that minimum detectable rate compare with quantities in adjudicated diversion cases?
Question (Gate 1 reformulation, one reformulation used, both versions on the record): For a named ADC-served unit and drug-formulation class, with ID = (beginning inventory + receipts) − (recorded administered + documented waste + returns + ending inventory) and a propagated sigma_ID in which every term is declared either a measured variance or an interval bound (witnessed waste unmeasured by construction), what is the smallest sustained monthly removal M at which each of three named tests — (a) the unit's current discrepancy-review practice, (b) a reproducible vendor alarm rule, (c) a safeguards-style sequential test on cumulative ID (CUMUF/Page-type) — attains Pd = 95% at Pfa = 1 per unit-year; and how does M compare with per-month rates reconstructed from adjudicated cases? (Reformulated because the intake version fixed no test, no (Pd, Pfa) pair and no units.)

Alternative formulations:
- What is the detection limit of hospital drug-diversion surveillance, stated as the smallest diversion detectable at a fixed false-alarm rate, rather than as a classifier's performance on cases already found?

Motivating gap: Published and marketed diversion detection is classifier performance against already-identified cases. The AJHP 2022 ML study reports 96.6% sensitivity and a 160-day mean earlier-detection gain on 22 known incidents — all of them nursing staff, and the sensitivity measured against 98th-percentile heuristic labels rather than confirmed diversion — and states "Fourth, we tested sensitivity, not specificity"; it reports no alert rate, no diverted quantities and no Pd as a function of quantity. The only published "how much is missed" figures are executive opinion surveys. Hospitals are legally required to keep these accounts with no detection performance specified for them. The interface is not decorative: safeguards assumes bulk material with quantified instrument uncertainty, whereas the hospital balance's dominant error term is documentation error of unknown distribution under partial adversarial control (Epstein 2016: reconciliation error 15.8% baseline, 8.8% -> 5.2%; "Most reconciliation errors are clerical").

A demonstration that the attested outflow fraction makes sigma_ID unboundable below some large M is admissible and informative.

(i) CONFIRMED. On the Gate 1 identity a removal concealed inside documented waste or charted administration leaves every term satisfied, so E[ID] = 0, Pd = Pfa at every M, and the declaration-only balance has no minimum detectable rate; Fan 2019's verbatim naming of falsified administered and wasted amounts makes that path real rather than hypothetical.
(ii) CORRECTED, and this is the load-bearing error. A safeguards-style material-balance test is not the balance standing alone. Burr T, Bonner E, Michalak S, Norman C, "An Overview of Statistical Methods used in Nuclear Safeguards" (JSM proceedings 2019, pp. 553-579, read at https://ww2.amstat.org/meetings/proceedings/2019/data/assets/pdf/1199480.pdf) states that "the material balance (MB) is defined as MB = Ibegin + Tin - Tout - Iend", that "these paired data are declarations usually based on measurements by the operator", that falsification "would cause a mean shift between the operator declaration and the corresponding inspector measurement", and that "the D statistic and the one-item-at-a-time tests rely on estimates of operator and inspector measurement error RSDs". Safeguards' answer to exactly this candidate's problem is therefore an independent inspector verification measurement — the MUF+D structure — which the candidate's identity does not contain. So the kill does not void the question, it redirects it: the declaration-only branch has no finite limit, which is the degenerate outcome presupposition 8 pre-declares admissible, and the non-degenerate branch is a verification-augmented balance in which an assay's measurement uncertainty and a verification sampling fraction enter sigma_ID and set a finite limit. The candidate does not say which branch it means; that is added to the promotion conditions below. In the hospital case the inflow side is already instrumented (ADC dispense events, receipts, counts are machine records) and the outflow side is declaration only, so the asymmetry the record locates is real; a verification counterpart for the outflow side exists as an active engineering target (syringe-residual assay, returned-PCA-bag verification) but the checker found only a capstone title and opened no peer-reviewed study of its detection performance, so it is located, not quantified.
(iii) A second obstacle from the same primary, absent from the whole record and arguably more informative than the identity argument: "It is important to recognize that large throughput facilities cannot typically achieve DP >= 0.95", against a stated goal of DP >= 0.95 at a significant quantity of 8 kg Pu. What defeats these tests in the method-owning field is the throughput-to-variance ratio, not the accounting identity — and a dispensing unit is a very high-throughput, high-relative-variance facility. This strengthens the anchoring argument and makes the degenerate answer the prior expectation rather than a surprise.
