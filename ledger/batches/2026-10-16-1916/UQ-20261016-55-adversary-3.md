## Restatement (my own words)

Has anyone measured what real demands to delete content from LLM training data actually look like — who sends them, under which legal basis, how often, how many identifiable items each names, and how large and how scattered those items are inside a named open corpus (Dolma, peS2o) — and then checked whether the forget sets stipulated by TOFU, MUSE, WMDP and DUSK resemble that measured demand stream on those axes, with the confidential (unobserved) share of demands bounded rather than ignored?

Plainer: "Do unlearning benchmarks' forget sets look like real deletion requests, and has anyone counted the real requests to find out?"

## Recommended verdict

**Rejected** — Unresolved on attack 2 (Disguise: the question is posed in recsys, arXiv:2508.17076, and presupposed in LLM continual unlearning, arXiv:2601.21682; web-scale Disguise search could not be run and may not be marked passed) and on attack 4 (Presupposition failure: two of the four named benchmarks are not removal-demand proxies and have undefined or zero values on the comparison axes; MUSE spans request sizes already; the public demand population is near-empty on the one complete archive queryable, and the largest in-frame demand is a destruction order, not an unlearning request).

Routing if rejected as already-asked: the benchmark-realism line — Lubitzsch et al. 2025 (recsys), FIT/PCH 2026, MUSE C5/C6 (2024/ICLR 2025), CleanSlate (2026); census method — Bertram et al. CCS 2019, Urban/Karaganis/Schofield 2016.

What would promote it (if the judge disagrees on Disguise): read arXiv:2508.17076's research-question list and Cooper §7 to confirm neither poses the census; drop TOFU and WMDP from the comparison; restate the test as quantile envelopes; and show from Lumen (not GitHub alone) that the dataset-host notice stratum has at least hundreds of members, so that "distribution" is not a courtesy word for a handful of case studies.

## Single strongest killing point

The comparison is ill-posed for half its targets and near-empty on its demand side: WMDP's own README defines it as a hazardous-knowledge safety proxy and TOFU as fictitious (zero corpus footprint by construction), so the token-share/entanglement axes are undefined or trivially zero for two of the four named benchmarks; meanwhile the one complete public notice archive I could query (github/dmca, 2011–2026) holds single-digit notices naming ML training datasets, and the largest in-frame real demand (Bartz v. Anthropic, 482,460 works) is an order to destroy dataset copies, not a request that any model unlearn — so the "real forget-set distribution" collapses to a few litigation events with non-unlearning remedies plus a sliver of case studies, while the question itself ("benchmarks simulate unrealistically large requests; real deletion requests are many, small and sequential") is already posed in recsys (arXiv:2508.17076) and presupposed in LLM continual unlearning (arXiv:2601.21682).
