Domain(s): Biology — protein engineering / directed evolution, deep mutational scanning; AI for science (surrogate-guided design). Source field for the transplant: quantitative genetics / animal and plant breeding. Source method: cross-field transplant.

Question (canonical): [R1, adopted at Gate 1] Restricted to published multi-round protein-engineering campaigns (classical directed evolution or ML-guided design) in which (i) round r's screened variants carry >=2 independent replicate measurements, (ii) the number screened and the number carried forward are reported, and (iii) round r+1's library is derived from round r's selected variants (mutagenesis, recombination, or surrogate-guided sampling in the same sequence neighbourhood; de novo generative redesign is out of scope): (a) on a pre-registered scale (log activity or log enrichment, with saturated/censored wells handled by a stated Tobit or rank-based rule), does the realized round-over-round change in library mean trait value agree with the prediction R-hat = i x rho x sigma_g, where i is the standardized selection differential implied by the reported selection fraction with a finite-library correction, rho is the accuracy of selection — sqrt of variant-level repeatability t = sigma_g^2 / (sigma_g^2 + sigma_e^2/m) when selection acts on the assay itself, or the estimated prediction-vs-measurement correlation when selection acts on an ML surrogate (selection-index / indirect-selection accuracy) — and sigma_g is the among-variant standard deviation estimated from round-r data alone; reported as calibration slope with 95% CI, mean bias, and 80%/95% prediction-interval coverage, every input taken from round r only; and (b) at each campaign's own fixed well budget N = n x m (n variants, m replicates each), does the (n, m) split that maximizes predicted response under that campaign's own estimated sigma_g^2 and sigma_e^2 differ materially from the split the campaign actually used, "materially" being >=20% of predicted per-round gain forgone?

Reformulation record (one permitted at Gate 1, for well-posedness only):
- V0 (intake): "In protein-engineering campaigns that screen a library and carry the best variants forward, does the realized per-round gain match the breeder's-equation prediction — selection intensity x variant-level assay repeatability x library genetic standard deviation, with surrogate-model correlation entering as indirect-selection accuracy — and would the breeding-theory-optimal split of a fixed assay budget between number of variants and replicates per variant differ materially from the splits campaigns actually use?"
- Why reformulated: V0 (1) left "gain" ambiguous between the change in library mean and the improvement of the single best variant (the quantity the field actually reports), whereas the breeder's equation predicts only the former; (2) wrote repeatability as a multiplier rather than as sqrt(t), i.e. did not distinguish repeatability from accuracy of selection, so its prediction formula was not the breeding-theory one; (3) left "materially" undefined; (4) did not restrict scope, so it swept in generative-design rounds where "offspring of the selected parents" has no referent and the equation does not apply. R1 fixes all four and makes both arms decidable.
- R1 is adopted. V0 is retained above verbatim.

Alternative formulations:
- A1: Is the response to selection in directed-evolution and ML-guided design rounds predictable in advance from assay noise, library diversity and selection fraction, the way breeders predict genetic gain per cycle?
- A2: At a fixed number of assay wells, are protein-engineering campaigns screening too many variants too noisily (or too few too precisely) relative to the allocation that maximizes expected gain per round?

Motivating gap: Quantitative genetics has had a predictive theory for a century for exactly the decision these campaigns make every round — expected gain per cycle from heritability, selection intensity and genetic variance; optimal allocation of test capacity between candidates and replication; and selection-index theory for selecting on a correlated predictor instead of the trait itself, which is what an ML surrogate is. Protein engineering instead reports hit rates and best-variant fold-improvements; the ML side frames the same loop as Bayesian optimization measured by regret; and the field's own library-design theory concerns completeness and diversity rather than predicted gain under a noisy assay. Searching across four vocabularies returned the selection-stringency, monitoring, benchmark-validity and batch-BO literatures and nothing applying response-to-selection prediction or optimal-allocation theory to these campaigns. The interface has a real problem in it, not just a vocabulary mismatch: selection acts on a surrogate's prediction rather than the trait, assay repeatability is almost never reported as a variance component, and epistasis violates the additivity the theory leans on — so the transplant has a genuine chance of failing in a diagnostic way.

Presuppositions:
1. Enough published campaigns retain replicate structure to estimate variant-level repeatability from round-1 data. Partly established (MaveDB / ProteinGym / SSMuLA entries and DMS papers commonly report replicate correlations); contested for industrial campaigns, and contested as to whether >=20 campaigns additionally satisfy (ii) and (iii) of R1. Flagged contested; the corpus-existence check is the first step, not an assumption.
2. Library phenotype variance can be decomposed into variant-attributable and assay-noise components. Established in principle given replicates; Enrich2-style random-effects treatments of DMS data already do a version of this.
3. Breeder's-equation assumptions hold approximately within a round: selection on a continuous (possibly predicted) trait, roughly additive variation among the selected parents' descendants, an unselected baseline. Contested — epistasis in protein fitness landscapes is strong and well documented. This is the premise most likely to break, and its breaking is informative (it localizes the failure to non-persistence of sigma_g across rounds).
4. Selection intensity is recoverable: number screened and number carried forward are reported. Often but not always true; R1 makes it an inclusion criterion rather than an assumption.
5. Gain is measurable on a scale where means and variances are comparable across rounds. Contested — assays saturate and are censored; R1 requires a pre-stated transformation and censoring rule.
6. For ML-guided rounds, surrogate accuracy rho estimated on round-r data is a usable stand-in for realized accuracy on the selected tail. Contested, and one of the two named mechanisms by which a "no" can arise.
7. Not presupposed: that the theory fits. A mismatch localized to epistasis, to surrogate-selection accuracy, or to censoring answers the question.

What would count as an answer:
(a) A retrospective test across >=20 qualifying published multi-round campaigns, comparing predicted with realized round-over-round change in library mean where the prediction uses round-r quantities only; reporting calibration slope with CI, mean bias, and prediction-interval coverage. "Yes" = slope CI inside a pre-specified band (e.g. 0.75–1.33) with coverage within 10 points of nominal; "no" = biased prediction with the bias attributable to a named mechanism (loss of sigma_g between rounds => epistasis; shortfall concentrated in surrogate-selected rounds => selection accuracy; shortfall tracking the share of censored wells => saturation).
(b) An allocation analysis computing, under each campaign's own measured sigma_g^2 and sigma_e^2 at its own budget N, the expected-gain-maximizing (n, m) split and the gain forgone by the actual split; "small" = <10% forgone, "material" = >=20%.
(c) Ideally one prospective round run at the theory-optimal allocation against the campaign's default allocation, pre-registered, with the realized difference in round gain as the outcome.
Communities that would act: academic protein-engineering groups and platform companies allocating screening budget; DMS/MAVE consortia designing replicate structure; benchmark builders (ProteinGym, FLIP, SSMuLA) who rank models without accounting for assay repeatability.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
1. vs Alpay & Desai 2024: this is the closest prior on the selection half. It varies selection stringency k (how many parents are kept) and asks how that changes outcomes — the same lever as selection intensity i — but it is a forward theory/simulation study of landscape-driven outcomes, with the number of mutants n held fixed. The paper has no selection differential, no response-to-selection formula and no breeding heritability; its outcomes are the expected log maximum fitness of the next round and the average maximum after 5/10/20 rounds, i.e. extreme order statistics, not the change in library mean that the breeder's equation predicts. It contains no assay-noise or replicate term, so no repeatability and no accuracy of selection, and its introduction notes the screening-capacity/population-size tradeoff without modelling it. The candidate differs by (i) predicting mean response from round-r quantities and testing calibration against realized gain in published campaigns, (ii) putting assay repeatability and surrogate accuracy into the prediction as rho, and (iii) solving the (n, m) budget split that the prior explicitly leaves unmodelled.
2. vs Subramanian 2026: closest prior on the surrogate half. It shows empirically that rank correlation degrades as a predictor of "selection value" at stricter cutoffs (R^2 of 0.50 at top-10% falling to 0.16 at top-1%; 3 of 41 assays worse than random at top-1%), and that the correlation-to-selection mapping does not transfer across benchmark families. That is an empirical audit of correlation as a proxy for selection outcome; the candidate instead asks whether a specific closed-form prediction (i x rho x sigma_g) is calibrated, with rho defined as indirect-selection accuracy and sigma_g estimated as a variance component. The prior has no assay-repeatability term, no library-variance term, no per-round gain quantity and no allocation analysis, and is scored on static benchmark assays rather than on realized round-over-round gain in multi-round campaigns. Its finding is, however, a live reason the candidate's "no" branch could trigger, which is why it also appears under anchoring rather than as a duplicate.
3. vs Dai et al. 2023 (BTS-RED): closest prior on the allocation half. It is the candidate-vs-replicate budget tradeoff stated exactly — more unique inputs with fewer replicates versus fewer with more, under input-dependent noise — but it is an algorithm (batch Thompson sampling with adaptive per-input replicate counts n = ceil(sigma^2(x)/R^2)) with no-regret guarantees, evaluated on precision agriculture and AutoML. Its objective is cumulative or mean-variance regret, not expected response to selection; it prescribes a sequential policy rather than computing the gain forgone by an allocation a real campaign already used; it never mentions protein engineering; and it makes no contact with breeding theory (no breeder's equation, heritability, selection intensity or genetic gain). The candidate differs by being retrospective and diagnostic on real campaigns, by using the breeding-theory optimum as the benchmark allocation, and by reporting gain forgone in the campaigns' own units.

Significance — consequence: If yes — per-round gain becomes a quantity campaigns can forecast and budget against before spending wells, rather than a result reported after the fact. Screening budgets become sizeable from two measurable numbers (assay repeatability and library variance) plus a chosen selection fraction; a campaign could pre-register expected gain and detect an underperforming round as a deviation rather than as disappointment; and assay repeatability becomes a reportable property of an assay, the way Z' is in HTS. If no — the direction of the miss is itself the finding, and each direction has a named owner: sigma_g that does not persist across rounds indicts additivity and tells campaign designers that single-round theory cannot be chained, pointing to a protein analogue of the Bulmer effect and a persistence coefficient; a shortfall confined to surrogate-selected rounds says cross-validated model correlation overstates accuracy on the selected tail, which is an argument for reporting accuracy at the acted-on cutoff (converging on Subramanian's recommendation from an independent direction); a shortfall tracking censored wells says the field's assay scales, not its theory, are the obstacle. On the allocation arm, a material gap would mean money is currently being spent on the wrong margin — most plausibly too many variants measured too noisily, since variant count is the visible number in a paper and replicate count is not — and a small gap would be a genuine vindication of the field's accumulated practice, which is worth knowing because it is currently asserted rather than checked.

Significance — audience: (1) Platform companies and academic groups sizing screens: the decision is literally how many wells per variant at a fixed plate budget, taken every round, currently by convention and historical protocol. (2) DMS/MAVE consortia (Atlas of Variant Effects-type efforts) designing replicate structure: they currently choose replicates to clear a QC correlation threshold; if repeatability is the multiplier on achievable gain, replicate structure becomes a design parameter with a target value rather than a hurdle. (3) Benchmark builders (ProteinGym, FLIP, SSMuLA): if measured model-vs-assay correlation is bounded by sqrt(repeatability), model rankings on low-repeatability assays are partly rankings of assay noise; the action is to publish per-assay repeatability alongside the leaderboard and to report performance at the cutoff users act on. (4) Quantitative geneticists and breeders, for whom a failure mode of their own theory in a system with strong epistasis, near-unlimited "offspring" and an unusually noisy phenotype is a stress test they cannot get from livestock or crops.

Significance — fecundity: Answering it makes askable: whether an effective heritability of a protein assay is a stable, reportable assay property; whether a persistence-of-variance coefficient across rounds can be estimated and used as the shrink factor, giving a protein analogue of the Bulmer effect and of long-term selection-limit theory for libraries; whether ML surrogate retraining is better modelled as an increase in selection accuracy rho than as a change of acquisition function (which would make model-improvement and screening-budget decisions commensurable on one scale); how many rounds versus how deep per round, the protein analogue of breeding-cycle-time optimization; and whether the best-variant fold-improvement the field reports can be reconstructed from mean response plus library variance, which would reconcile the field's reporting convention with the theory's predicted quantity.

Significance — anchoring: Touches (i) the standing question of whether protein fitness landscapes are locally additive enough for short-horizon prediction, by converting it into a calibration measurement rather than a landscape-geometry argument; (ii) the winner's curse / regression-to-the-mean problem in screening hits, which is the same shrinkage the rho term encodes; (iii) the benchmark-validity problem in ML for proteins, where correlation is known to decouple from top-k selection value (Subramanian 2026); (iv) the DMS reproducibility literature's unused variance decompositions (Enrich2-era random-effects modelling), which already compute the ingredient nobody reports; and (v) the Bulmer effect and selection-induced variance reduction from quantitative genetics, which predicts a specific direction of over-prediction if sigma_g is taken from round r.

Reason for silence: A disciplinary boundary plus a reporting-convention/incentive gap, with a data-availability component.
- Boundary: the breeder's equation is taught in animal- and plant-breeding curricula and essentially never in biochemistry, protein engineering or ML-for-biology; the two literatures share almost no venues, reviewers or training path. The ML side that does think about the loop inherited its framing from Bayesian optimization, where the natural objective is regret, not response to selection — so the obvious formalism was already occupied, and an occupied formalism is rarely displaced by an unfamiliar one.
- Reporting convention: protein campaigns are judged by the best variant's fold-improvement, an extreme order statistic. The breeder's equation predicts the change in the mean. The field therefore does not publish the quantity the theory predicts, which makes the comparison invisible and makes anyone who tried it look as though they were measuring the wrong thing. Symmetrically, replicate-level variance is treated as QC to be passed (drop assays below r = 0.8) rather than as a parameter to report, so the prediction's main input is routinely computed and then discarded.
- Incentive: a prospective allocation test (arm c) spends real screening budget on a comparison whose likeliest publishable outcome, if current practice is near-optimal, is a null; and the retrospective arm's payoff accrues to the field's methodology rather than to any one campaign's protein.
- Data: aggregated multi-round, replicate-retaining campaign data at the scale arm (a) needs (MaveDB, ProteinGym, SSMuLA and similar) has only recently existed, so the retrospective was not previously attemptable even by someone who had the idea.
This is not "no one thought of it": adjacent work shows people repeatedly arriving next to the question from the stringency side, the monitoring side, the benchmark side and the BO side without crossing into breeding theory.

Strongest remaining objection (for Gate 4's benefit, not a Gate 4 output): that the "no" branch may not be diagnostic after all. Epistasis, surrogate-accuracy decay at the selected tail, censoring and change-of-library-design all push predicted gain the same way (over-prediction), and in a retrospective corpus they are not separately identified — campaigns that use surrogates also tend to change library construction, and the censoring share correlates with how much improvement was achieved. If the mechanisms cannot be told apart, a miscalibration finding degrades to "the transplant does not work," which is much weaker than the record claims.
