# UQ-20261016-51 — Gate 4 adversary #1 log

## Restatement (my own words)
When a benchmark test item turns up in an open pretraining corpus, does the leaked copy actually carry the benchmark's answer? Two limbs: (a) of all corpus copies of an item, what share assert the gold answer, a non-gold answer, no answer, or expose a wrong gold key; (b) on items whose answer-bearing copies mostly assert a non-gold answer, does an open-data model score below equally hard items with zero copies, so that "contamination" can push a reported score DOWN and a decontamination filter can remove a penalty as well as a bonus? The record's own fallback: if non-gold copies are under ~2% of occurrences, the answer is NO by low exposure.

Secondhand, from PhishBencher (OPENED): "leading LLMs sometimes scored as well as (or even better on) questions whose original label was wrong ... This implies potential data contamination on the original MMLU benchmark."

## New facts bearing on the grade
- F1 (PhishBencher, OPENED): a peer-reviewed line already treats the ANSWER CARRIED by a published benchmark copy as the lever: it publishes one of several correct answers so that memorization of the published copy shows up as above-Bayes-ceiling accuracy. This is designed, not natural, contamination and it never retrieves corpus copies — but it shows the "what does the leaked copy say" variable is on the table in the literature, not just on the Lacuna page.
- F2 (Gema et al. via PhishBencher, secondhand): the inference "model agrees with an erroneous gold key -> contamination" is already in a peer-reviewed paper. Neighbour D's premise is therefore in the literature, not only in public discourse. This is the mirror case of the candidate's limb (b) (gold wrong, copies carry gold), which the record itself lists under fecundity.
- F3 (Magar & Schwartz footnote, OPENED): the community explicitly saw that copies may "appear unlabeled" and scoped it out as "beyond the scope". Supports the reason-for-silence story (assumption baked into the standard design), and shows no one has since filled the slot with a polarity measurement that I could find.
- F4 (Soft Contamination, OPENED): corpus-wide retrieval of benchmark duplicates in an OLMo-class corpus at scale exists (embedding search over Olmo3 data), so the instrument for limb (a) exists; it was used for counts, not content.
