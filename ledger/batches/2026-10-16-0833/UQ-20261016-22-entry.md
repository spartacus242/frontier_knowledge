Question (canonical): In laboratory rodent experiments, is the order in which animals are removed from a cage a repeatable individual trait that predicts behavioural and physiological phenotype, such that assigning animals to groups in the order they are caught induces between-group differences comparable in size to the treatment effects such experiments report?
Alternative formulations:
- Is "easy to catch" a phenotype, and does allocating mice to arms in catch order quietly un-randomise preclinical experiments?
- How much of a rodent study's between-group difference is explained by the order animals were lifted out of the cage rather than by the treatment?
- (Gate 1 reformulation, retained) For individually marked cage-mates caught by a standard protocol on >=5 days: (i) ICC of catch rank within cage; (ii) per-rank coefficient for open-field distance, corticosterone, weight slope, glucose AUC with cage as random effect; (iii) does catch-order allocation shift group means by >=0.5x the median standardised published effect on those endpoints?
Motivating gap: Submitted as: randomisation is treated as satisfied once a random sequence is applied to IDs, presupposing that reaching into the cage is itself random, a step said to be unreported and outside ARRIVE's scope. Gate 2 falsified the qualitative premise: ARRIVE 2.0's Item 4 Explanation and Elaboration states that selecting an animal "at random (i.e., haphazardly or arbitrarily) from a cage is not statistically random", names researcher judgement as the bias and allocation concealment as the remedy. What is missing is quantification, not the question. Full record: batches/2026-10-16-0833/UQ-20261016-22-record.md
Presuppositions:
1. Catch order is recordable without perturbing phenotype — low risk (marking + log).
2. LOAD-BEARING AND CONTESTED: catch rank has non-zero within-individual repeatability. Nearest evidence is negative — Jolly et al. 2019 (BES 73:129): repeatable boldness, no detectable between-individual variation in detection probability. If ICC ~ 0 the question collapses.
3. CONTESTED: catch-order allocation is common practice — undocumented; ARRIVE warns against it (weaker than evidence); careful labs already randomise within-cage order (Varholick et al. 2019).
4. Endpoints carry exploitable between-individual variance — partly supported (within-cage R^2 0.01-0.17), but null for faecal glucocorticoid metabolites and EPM.
5. Order effects reflect a stable trait, not transient cohort-removal stress — a transient channel is independently documented.
6. Not presupposed: that the effect is large (criterion is comparative).
What would count as an answer: Pre-registered three-part study — (i) ICC with CI for catch rank over >=5 days, cage and day random effects, rank analysed ordinally and as first-vs-last; (ii) endpoint regressions on catch rank with a design arm separating stable trait from removal-order stress; (iii) simulation over fitted coefficients, or a direct catch-ordered vs catch-randomised allocation contrast, against a pre-specified published effect-size distribution. "Yes" = ICC lower bound > 0 AND criterion (iii) met; "no" = a >=90%-powered null on (i) or (iii). A practice survey is not an answer.

The first half of the record's reasoning is CONFIRMED: the hazard is on the record in the candidate's own field. ARRIVE 2.0 Item 4a states that selecting an animal "at random (i.e., haphazardly or arbitrarily)" from a cage is not statistically random, names researcher judgement as the mechanism and prescribes allocation concealment, citing no measurement; NC3Rs guidance names the easier-to-catch mechanism in so many words. The second half — that the magnitude has never been measured — is REFUTED, and this is a correction to the entry, not a flourish. **Takao et al. 2016, Front Behav Neurosci 10:99** measured removal-order effects on rectal temperature, hot-plate latency, elevated-plus-maze open-arm time and plasma corticosterone in roughly 1,700 control C57BL/6J mice, nine years before the record's headline prior. Schipper et al. 2025 is therefore neither the first nor the strongest measurement and must not be cited as if it were; its Methods could not be read, so whether its "testing order" is physical catch order or a pre-assigned order is unverified. What survives as genuinely unmeasured is narrower and is the only thing a successor should claim: (i) no study measures the **repeatability** of catch order as a stable individual trait across days (ICC or equivalent) — the only repeatability work is on wild-rodent trappability, a different construct, and it concluded trappability is not a personality proxy; and (ii) no study benchmarks an order-induced shift in group means against **published treatment effect sizes**. Restated as those two gaps the claim is confirmed; as the record wrote it, it is not.
