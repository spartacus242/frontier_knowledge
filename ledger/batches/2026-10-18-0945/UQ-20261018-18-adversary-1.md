## Restatement (my words, not the generator's)

Take every museum or occurrence record whose identification was hedged with one of taxonomy's doubt marks — "cf." (probably this species), "aff." (close to, but apparently not, this species), "sp." (genus known, species not named), "?" (doubt attached somewhere) — and treat the hedge as a forecast. When a later, independent re-identification arrives (a monographic revision of the whole collection, or a DNA sequence from the same specimen), how often does it agree with the hedged name at a stated rank, per mark? Do the marks' agreement rates fall in the same order as the usage guides' "confidence" ranking? And is each mark's rate consistent enough across who determined it, which institution holds it, which higher taxon it belongs to and which decade it was written in that a data aggregator (GBIF, OBIS, PBDB) could publish the mark as a number rather than a flag?

Three things the framing invites me to accept that I did not: (i) that the four marks are graded strengths of one proposition; (ii) that an agreement rate conditioned on a mark measures the mark rather than the specimen; (iii) that no evaluative tradition inside identification practice has ever scored hedged identifications.
