Domain(s): Medicine / STEM — biological standardisation and metrology in laboratory medicine; pharmacopoeial science. Neighbours engaged: physical metrology (BIPM/JCTLM), index-number economics (chain drift), haemostasis standardisation.
Question (canonical): For WHO International Standards now on their third or later generation with publicly retrievable collaborative-study reports, (a) what is the product across replacements of the discrepancy between each study's estimated potency ratio to the outgoing standard and the unitage actually assigned to the incoming one, (b) what is the convolution across replacements of the published per-study dispersions that the continuity convention sets to zero, and (c) do (a)+(b) exceed the tolerance of the named IU-denominated clinical cut-offs and pharmacopoeial potency limits for those analytes, and the routine reproducibility of the assays applying them? (Gate 1 reformulation; the Gate 0 wording — "how far has the realised IU drifted from its original definition" — was superseded because the continuity convention makes that point estimate identically zero by construction.)
Alternative formulations: (1) Is the IU of a biological metrologically continuous across standard generations, or is today's IU an undocumented random walk from the first standard? (2) What is the total uncertainty of "1 IU" today if every replacement's calibration uncertainty is carried forward instead of zeroed? (3) Is WHO's mandated per-replacement continuity evaluation sufficient to bound the cumulative deviation of the realised IU, or does its per-standard scope make portfolio-level deviation unobservable in principle?
Motivating gap: Presupposition inversion. Replacements are value-assigned against the predecessor in a collaborative study; the assigned unitage is then treated as exact to keep the unit "continuous" and the study's dispersion is not propagated. Standards for hormones, coagulation factors and heparin are on their 2nd–6th generation while every IU-denominated threshold (anti-HBs 10 IU/L, FVIII potency labelling, hCG, anti-D) behaves as if the unit were fixed. The field has a per-replacement check and no cumulative account — so the claim is "never aggregated", not "never noticed". Gate 4 found the motivating anomaly to be misread (see below).
Presuppositions: P1 replacement calibrated against predecessor with a published ratio + dispersion — partly confirmed (Gray 2017), contested for oldest generations. P2 assigned unitage stated without uncertainty — historically true, now policy-false (Bristow ID-909: WHO recommendations revised 2004 require an uncertainty statement and a continuity evaluation, per replacement). P3 chain is linear and documented — partly falsified: multi-parent bridging (Poller 2005), re-anchoring (HPLC-assigned GH) and chain splitting (1988 FSH) are documented. P4 IU-denominated thresholds would move with the unit — supported for named cases, but adversary 1 showed reagent/platform calibration and commutability dominate for anti-HBs. P5 drift is confounded with artefact degradation — confirmed live and acknowledged in-field. P6 published dispersion estimates bridging error — contested and unresolved: NIBSC's own account makes GCV a mixture of assay imprecision and method bias. **New at Gate 4 (fatal):** the rounding premise behind estimand (a) ("500 IU/ampoule, 10 IU/ampoule") is uncited and is contradicted by the record's own assignments (0.86 U/ampoule; 130 and 173 IU/mg, i.e. 2–3 significant figures); and across the candidate's flagship chain the source material changes (bovine lung → porcine mucosa), so the ratio across that step has no common referent.
What would count as an answer: Per-analyte chain reconstruction from the public collaborative-study record — product of assigned-versus-estimated residuals as cumulative systematic deviation, convolution of consensus-potency standard errors as cumulative uncertainty, both compared to each IU-denominated decision threshold's tolerance and to routine assay CV. Three outcomes: small relative to assay CV (convention vindicated with numbers); large for named standards (generation-tag or re-anchor them); record too incomplete for the oldest chains (documented coverage gap addressed to WHO ECBS and custodian laboratories).
