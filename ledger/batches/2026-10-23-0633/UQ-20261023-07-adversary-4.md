## Restatement in my own words

Hospitals are told to give one red-cell unit and then re-check the patient. But a "unit" is not a measured amount of
haemoglobin: regulators only require each unit to clear a MINIMUM content, so what is actually hung at the bedside is an
unlabelled quantity of grams. The question asks three things in the frame of V2 (the repair, which is what now stands):
(a) if you took non-bleeding adult inpatients at a service that both runs one-unit-then-reassess AND records the
haemoglobin mass of each unit as it is issued, how much of the spread in the post-transfusion haemoglobin rise is
traceable to the grams in that particular bag rather than to the patient; (b) using the fact that which compatible bag
comes off the shelf next is essentially an accident of inventory, does the chance of needing a second unit inside 48 h
track the grams in the first bag; (c) if the service issued by grams instead of by count at the same haemoglobin target,
how much would the 48-h second-unit rate move. Put colloquially: is "one unit" a dose, is the bag or the body the source
of the variability, and would labelling the bag save second units.

**(C) The real nearest neighbour, which was sitting in the record's own search log unopened: Roubinian et al., JCI Insight 2022;7(1):e152598 (FULL TEXT).** Its regime and its printed numbers:
- **REGIME:** adult patients who received a **single RBC unit** at 12 US hospitals, 2013-01-01 to 2016-12-31; **102,043 evaluable transfusion episodes in 46,705 patients** (genotype subset 6,168 episodes in 5,354 patients); unit of analysis the **episode**, defined as one unit from one donor with usable pre- and post-transfusion labs and **no other RBC unit in between**; routine care; instrument = linked donor/component/recipient metadata plus RBC-Omics genotypes; endpoint the haemoglobin increment, with a secondary within-48-h additional-transfusion endpoint; category explained variation and odds.

## Attack 4 in full, run the way this gate requires

**The measurand exemption is APPLIED, not merely noted.** V2 reports rather than assumes: the magnitude of the variance share, the sign and size of the slope on grams delivered, and whether the second-unit hazard depends on unit mass. I do not attack any of those, and the record's statement that bedside delivered dose is currently unmeasured is a report of the gap, not a premise. Attack 4 does not reach them.

**Clauses V2 needs TRUE before it can be asked at all, written out literally, then graded against documents.**

1. **Services operating a documented single-unit-then-reassess policy for adult inpatients exist.** → **TRUE in substance, UNVERIFIED as a policy document.** Roubinian 2022 (full text) is built on **102,043 adult single-unit episodes** at 12 hospitals, each defined as one unit with no other RBC unit in between — single-unit practice at scale is instantiated. I opened no guideline or audit document, so "documented policy" itself is Unverified by me. Kills nothing.
2. **Each issued unit's haemoglobin mass is recorded at issue.** → **FALSE of every instance anyone in this pipeline has opened, and enumerated rather than asserted.** Instances opened: Atilla 2017 (full text, verification pass) — the figure is *calculated* as "donor Hb level × 450 mL", lives in a Hemosoft database field, with **no labelling of units**, so it is a pre-donation capillary concentration scaled by a constant and is not the issued unit's mass; Roubinian 2022 (my full text) — not measured, not modelled; Roubinian 2019 (verification pass) — a constant ~89 g assumed; Saqlain 2022 (my full text) — unit volume not even a variable; Epah 2022 (abstract) — *predicted* by machine learning with MAE ≈1.43 g, which is a prediction and not a record at issue. Instances **not** opened: the nine German red-cell-concentrate product information leaflets from query 2, and every blood-service standard (JPAC 6.2, Lifeblood). **Why this is still not a kill:** the clause is a design requirement that a prospective study can create — nothing I opened says per-unit assay at issue is impossible, and Atilla shows an issuing system can carry a per-unit field at all.
