## CLAIM

The ledger entry's position, as handed to me: this source is the reason a candidate's
novelty is capped at Medium, and it **DEFINES without EVALUATING**. Specifically:

- (a) front matter and author order as printed;
- (b) what the consensus definition is, in full, and whether manufacturer exit /

## WHAT THE SOURCE SAYS (quotations as served)

Byline in printed order, with degrees as printed:

The author order in the ledger's source line matches the front matter exactly, all

### (b) The consensus definition, in full

**RECONSTRUCTION — assembled from 16 ordered consecutive served fragments, each ≤100
characters, each overlapping the previous one by one word at the junction. Junctions
verified by overlap. Approx. 200 words. Nothing inferred or completed.**

> "Apropos to the previously mentioned facts, factors, considerations, and concerns
> provided in this systematic review and consensus statement, we propose the adoption
> of a standard definition of abandonment of active implantable neurotechnological
> devices, which constitutes 1 of the following[†] 1. Failure to provide information
> relevant to (the existence or absence of) plans for medical, technical, and/or
> financial responsibility as fundamental aspects of patient consent during and after a
> clinical trial. 2. Failure to fulfill reasonable responsibility for medical,
> technical, and/or financial support prior to the end of an implantable device's
> labeled lifetime. 3. Failure to address any immediate needs (eg, infection or device
> programming) of the individual using the implanted device, which may result in safety
> concerns and/or the deterioration of device effectiveness. 4. Failure of a clinical
> research trial if or when (1) informed consent has failed to address ongoing access
> to and management of the implanted device (per 1) and/or such other devices that may
> be demonstrated as having equal or greater therapeutic value in the future and (2)
> individuals responsible for the trial have not made a reasonable effort to facilitate
> continued access to device and support for patients who benefit from the device."

[†] The punctuation at the junction after "1 of the following" was **not served** —
fragment 4 ended "1 of the following" and fragment 5 began "following 1. Failure". A
colon almost certainly sits there, but I did not supply it. The apostrophe in
"device's" is served as a curly apostrophe (device’s).

Two structural facts about this definition that matter more than its wording:

- It is **disjunctive**: "constitutes **1 of the following**". Any one of the four
  failures is abandonment. It is not a four-part conjunctive test.
- The caption of Figure 2, read as served, is: "This figure addresses the 4 failures,
  any of which may constitute device abandonment" — consistent with the disjunction.

**Manufacturer exit / liquidation / support withdrawal as a trigger: NO, not in those
words, and not as a named trigger.** The words "manufacturer", "company",
"bankruptcy", "liquidation", "insolvency", "exit", "discontinu-", "withdraw", "out of
business", "ceases" appear **nowhere inside the definition passage or the sentences
immediately around it**. The definition is agnostic as to *who* fails and *why*:
clause 2's "Failure to fulfill reasonable responsibility for medical, technical, and/or
financial support prior to the end of an implantable device's labeled lifetime" would
*cover* a manufacturer that stopped supporting a device, but the definition does not
name manufacturer exit as a trigger.

Insolvency is, however, squarely present **elsewhere in the body**, served verbatim in
short strings:

> "Finally, there are numerous examples of neurotechnology companies becoming
> insolvent."

> "the use of other investigational devices when companies dissolve or cease
> manufacturing or providing services for a particular product."

> NeuroVista: "the commercial entity Neurovista (date of insolvency, August 2013),
> which was developing" / "a first-in-human brain implant, declared bankruptcy, and
> patients who received implants" / "with the technology felt betrayed." (three
> consecutive served fragments)

So: a ledger sentence saying the definition *includes* manufacturer exit as a trigger
would be an over-read of the definition; a ledger sentence saying the paper is *silent*
on manufacturer insolvency would be wrong about the body. The accurate statement is
that insolvency is the paper's motivating case and is handled by a general
support-failure clause, not by a named corporate-exit trigger.

On the discipline that published ≠ performed: the paper does not *define*, *bound*,

### (d) The systematic review component: 7 included articles, no findings reported

Served:

> "of 734 articles identified, 7 articles" were "related to or addressed neurological
> device abandonment."

The review **does not report what those 7 articles found.** They are cited as
references 3 and 5–10 and their findings are not described. **No included article is
described as giving a count or a rate.** What the paper says about the included
literature instead, served in fragments:

> Limitations: "Second, the literature was sparse on this topic, and thus it will be
> likely that" …

> Conclusion: "a comprehensive literature review on neurological device abandonment
> revealed that this ethical" / "issue was largely buried within case reports, case
> series, and clinical trials."

The pacemaker literature (232 searched, 41 relevant) is a **separate comparative
search reported in Limitations**, explicitly not among the 7 and not used to extend the
definition.

So the review relays no rate from its inputs. On this sub-claim the source is not
merely silent about its own numbers; it characterises the whole underlying literature
as sparse and case-report-shaped.

### (e) A call for enumeration or outcome measurement — not present

Keyword sweep of the full text. **Absent entirely**: "registry", "register",
"enumerate", "enumeration", "track", "tracking", "surveillance", "database",
"incidence", "prevalence".

**Present, but in other senses** — reproducing the containing strings as served:

- Clinical/device monitoring, not epidemiological counting: "as well as processes,
  guidelines, and laws required for sound commercialization, provision, access," /
  "monitoring, and economic support of extant and emerging devices."
- Clinical monitoring of the patient: "which would require ongoing" / "monitoring and

- Device maintenance: "Third, failure to monitor and maintain the implanted technology
  could lead to recidivistic and perhaps" / "rebound signs, symptoms, and effects in

- "outcome measures" occurs once, about **trial design**: "Accomplishing these goals

  "power analyses, variability in research participant characteristics…"
- "outcomes" twice in the **consent** paragraphs: "Ensuring patient and participant

The paper's forward-looking ask is normative, not metrological. The Key Points

### (f) Scope — neurological, and narrower than that

Served scope strings:

- Abstract: the review concerns devices **"in the setting of deep brain, vagal nerve,
  and spinal cord stimulation."**
- Key Points, Findings: the definition is **"inclusive of devices used in deep brain
  stimulation, vagal nerve stimulation, and spinal cord stimulation."**
- The definition itself is worded for **"active implantable neurotechnological
  devices"** (see (b)).
- Noninvasive: the issues **"may also be applicable to noninvasive neurological
  technologies,"** with the authors noting that nonindwelling devices raise fewer of
  the same concerns.
- Non-neurological implants: cardiac pacemakers were **not** in the main review; the
  232/41 pacemaker search is comparative illustration in Limitations only, and is not
  used to extend the definition.

It does **not** generalise to implanted devices broadly.

## REGIME

The regime in which this source establishes what it establishes:

- **Object**: active implantable *neurotechnological* devices, concretely DBS, VNS and
  SCS; indwelling and electrically active. Not cardiac rhythm devices (comparator
  only), not noninvasive neurotech (gestured at as possibly analogous), not implanted
  devices in general.
- **Unit of analysis**: for the review component, the **article** — 734 screened,
  7 included, over a PubMed search with data collection 13 Sep 2023 – 1 Feb 2024
  following a Royal Society summit of 13–14 Sep 2023. For the consensus component, the
  unit is the **expert panel's agreed proposition**; there is no patient-level unit of
  analysis anywhere in the paper.
- **Endpoint / estimand**: there is none. The output is a *definitional* object — a
  disjunctive four-clause criterion for whether a given situation counts as
  abandonment. No comparator, no effect measure, no uncertainty, no threshold on any
  measured quantity.
- **What it can therefore support**: that a consensus definition exists, who agreed it,
  when, for which device classes, and in what words; that the literature it searched
  was sparse and case-report-shaped.
- **What it is silent on**: how many people are affected; how often; with what
  consequences; and whether any such measurement should be undertaken. Silence here is
  silence, not a prohibition and not an endorsement of the gap.

A claim handed to this source about **implanted devices broadly**, or about
**non-neurological implants**, sits **outside** this regime and the source is silent
there — it is not a prior and not a refutation. A claim about **DBS/VNS/SCS
abandonment definitions** sits squarely inside it.

## DEFECTS FOUND

1. **The definition has three published formulations with different counts, and the
   entry must say which it quotes.** The Discussion and Figure 2 give **four** failures
   ("constitutes 1 of the following"; caption: "the 4 failures"). The Abstract
   "Conclusions and Relevance" gives **three** — consent, support before the end of
   labeled lifetime, immediate needs — and then treats trial failure separately and
   **hedged**: abandonment arising from a failed research trial "should be contingent
   on specific circumstances." The Key Points "Findings" gives the same **three**
   themes. None of the three formulations carries a citation; all are the panel's own.
   Quoting "the definition" as a three-part test, or as a four-part conjunctive test,
   both misrepresent it.
2. **Disjunction, not conjunction.** "constitutes 1 of the following" means any single
   failure suffices. An entry that paraphrases it as a cumulative test inverts the
   definition's strength.
3. **Manufacturer exit is not a named trigger in the definition, but insolvency is the
   paper's motivating case.** Both the over-claim ("the definition names manufacturer
   liquidation") and the under-claim ("the paper does not discuss company insolvency")
   are wrong. See (b) for the precise split.
4. **Article type.** It is a *Consensus Statement* in *Health Policy* whose review
   component yielded 7 articles and reports none of their findings. Treating it as a
   systematic review that synthesised evidence about abandonment over-reads it; the
   review established that the literature was sparse, not what the literature measured.
5. **The 41-of-232 pacemaker figure is a count of comparator articles.** If anything
   downstream has converted it, or the 7-of-734, into a statement about devices or
   patients, that is a unit-of-analysis error; the source prints these only as
   literature counts.
6. **My own read limit, not a defect of the source**: Figure 1 and Figure 2 interiors
   were not readable. The "prints no numbers about people" finding covers text,
   abstract, Key Points and captions only.
7. **Diacritics dropped** in the short form: Lázaro-Muñoz.
8. **One route limitation to record rather than paper over**: on my final fetch the
   summariser **refused** segmented transcription of the Abstract "Conclusions and
   Relevance", the Key Points "Findings" and the Abstract "Findings" paragraphs, having
   accepted the same segmentation technique for the Discussion definition on an earlier
   call. Those three paragraphs are therefore reported here as short served quotes plus
   summary, **not** as verbatim reconstructions, and are labelled as such above. They
   are readable; I ran out of budget, not routes.

## BEARING ON THE LEDGER ENTRY

**The entry's load-bearing claim holds.** On the full text I could read, Okun 2024
prints **no** count of affected patients, **no** prevalence, **no** incidence and
**no** outcome rate. The words "prevalence" and "incidence" do not occur. The only
person-numbers are one undenominated NeuroVista anecdote and the composition of the
consensus panel. The "DEFINES without EVALUATING" characterisation is accurate and, if
anything, understated: the paper also does not relay any count or rate from its 7
included articles, and it does **not call for** enumeration or outcome measurement —
"registry", "track", "surveillance", "incidence" and "prevalence" are all absent, and
its forward-looking ask is for "guidelines, policies, and laws."

**Two qualifications that should go into the entry, both of which bear on the Medium
cap.**

First, **regime**. This source's regime is active implantable *neurotechnological*
devices — DBS, VNS, SCS. If the candidate it caps is pitched at implanted devices
broadly, or at non-neurological implants, then Okun 2024 is **silent** in that regime
rather than prior art in it, and the cap would need another source to carry it. Inside
DBS/VNS/SCS the cap is well-founded: a consensus definition for exactly these device
classes is published, and a candidate that mainly offers a definition there is
genuinely anticipated.

Second, **what kind of anticipation this is**. A definition citation is not an
evaluation, and this is a definition citation: a Consensus Statement in Health Policy
whose review component established that the literature was "sparse" and "largely buried
within case reports, case series, and clinical trials." So it anticipates *defining*
abandonment and anticipates nothing about *measuring* it. If the candidate's
contribution is enumeration, prevalence, or an outcome rate, this source does not touch
it — and the entry should not let a definition paper cap a measurement claim beyond
the definitional part.

One stray hazard for whoever writes the entry: do not let the surrounding insolvency
discussion migrate into the definition. The definition's support clause is actor- and
cause-agnostic ("Failure to fulfill reasonable responsibility for medical, technical,
and/or financial support prior to the end of an implantable device's labeled
lifetime"); manufacturer exit is the paper's motivating example, handled in the body,
not a trigger enumerated in the definition.
