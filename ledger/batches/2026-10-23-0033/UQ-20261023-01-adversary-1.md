## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take at least two published algorithms that decide "this spike-sorted unit on day N is the same cell as that unit on day N+k" in chronic Neuropixels-class recordings (UnitMatch, the EMD matcher), each run at a stated decision threshold. Score them for identity errors — pairs wrongly declared the same (false matches) and single cells wrongly split into several or several merged into one — against a labelling of cell identity that does not itself use the tuning curve you later measure. Then ask: if nothing about the cells' tuning actually changed between days, how much apparent "representational drift" would those identity errors alone produce in the drift statistics the field publishes (e.g. tuning-curve correlation across days, population-vector correlation)? Finally, are the drift magnitudes the literature reports for the same brain area and inter-session interval bigger than that error-only number?

The question as it now stands is the Gate 1 reformulation (floor-plus-comparison), not the original per-paper decomposition. I tested the reformulation and its repair presuppositions R1–R3, as instructed.

C3 (= repair R2). Published ephys drift statistics can be recomputed or analytically perturbed under an injected error rate. — UNVERIFIED: I opened no drift paper's data-availability statement. Note, however, that an analytic perturbation needs only the error rates and the ONE-DAY distribution of tuning across units, not released matched-unit tables, so the clause is weaker than the record states.

C4 (= repair R3). A null of zero true drift is realisable. — NOT A WORLD-FACT CLAUSE. The null is a modelling assumption (set day-2 tuning equal to day-1 tuning for true pairs; pair false matches with the day-1 tuning of a different unit), of the same kind Stevenson 2011 uses for its finite-sample "upper bound". It does not require a within-session split-half surrogate to EXIST; the record's R3 conflates measuring the ERROR RATE (which needs a labelling, C2) with declaring the NULL (which needs only a model). The record aimed Gate 4 at R3; on inspection R3 is not where the risk is.

C5. False matches, under zero true drift, move the drift statistic in the direction the field reads as drift (i.e. the induced quantity is a "floor"). — PARTIAL, with a sourced caveat: Dhawale 2017 notes that activity-metric tracking "may bias datasets toward units whose waveforms do not change", i.e. the misses (false negatives / splits) select a stable-looking subset, which pushes measured drift DOWN while false matches push it UP. The net sign of matcher-induced bias is therefore not guaranteed positive, so "floor" is a presupposition about direction that the candidate's own presupposition 5 ("errors approximately independent of tuning") is doing the work of securing. Not FALSE; the question should report both components rather than a one-sided floor.

C6. Published drift magnitudes exist, matched on brain area and interval, for areas and intervals at which the two matchers have been run. — UNVERIFIED by me (opened no drift paper); plausible from the record's returns.

C7. "Same neuron across days" is a well-defined object. — MEASURAND EXEMPTION APPLIES, declared here as the record declared it: the question scores a decision against a declared labelling and reports failure of the labelling as a result. Attack 4 does not reach it.
