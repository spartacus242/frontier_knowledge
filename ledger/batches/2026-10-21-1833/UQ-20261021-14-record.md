Domain(s): AI architecture — model design and evaluation for non-autoregressive (masked-diffusion) language models. Method home for the measurement: variational inference and generative-model likelihood estimation (importance-weighted bounds, bidirectional Monte Carlo sandwich bounds).

Question (canonical, Gate 0 as intaken):
For the open-weight masked-diffusion language models now released at 7–8B scale, how loose is the variational bound their reported perplexities are computed from — what is the gap, in nats per token, between the published bound and an unbiased or sandwiched estimate of the model's actual log-likelihood — and does closing that gap change the diffusion-versus-autoregressive comparisons those perplexities are used to support?

Question (Gate 1 reformulation — the one permitted reformulation, for well-posedness only; recorded alongside the original, NOT substituted for it at Gate 2):
For shipped masked-diffusion LMs at 7–8B, decompose the difference between the published ELBO-derived perplexity and an exactly-evaluable likelihood into (a) variational slack against the model's own uniform-order marginal and (b) the change of scored distribution induced by replacing uniform-order marginalisation with a deterministic test-time unmasking policy, reporting each separately in nats per token with Monte Carlo standard errors.
Why the reformulation was needed: the original asks for the gap against "the model's actual log-likelihood" as if that were a single number. The candidate's own P1 flags that any-order conditionals need not be mutually consistent, so "the actual log-likelihood" is order-dependent and the headline estimand is ambiguous until the target marginal is named. The reformulation names it. Note that the reformulation does not rescue the candidate: the original is what Gate 2 was run against, and the reformulation's residue is narrower than a novel question (see "Residue" below).

Alternative formulations:
- When a diffusion language model reports a perplexity, how much of that number is the model and how much is the looseness of the bound?
- Are the likelihood figures in diffusion-LM release tables comparable with the exact likelihoods of autoregressive baselines, and if not, by how much is the diffusion model being penalised?

Motivating gap (as intaken):
Masked-diffusion LMs crossed into open weights at 7–8B in 2025 (LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B) and their release tables place a bound-derived likelihood beside autoregressive baselines whose likelihood is exact by the chain rule. A one-sided bound compared against an exact value is a biased comparison of unknown magnitude with a known direction — it penalises the diffusion model — and the magnitude decides whether the architecture comparison stands.

PRIOR 1 — DUEL: Exact Likelihood for Masked Diffusion via Deterministic Unmasking. Gilad Turok, Chris De Sa, Volodymyr Kuleshov. arXiv:2603.01367v2, 10 Mar 2026 (cs.LG); ICML 2026 virtual pages 70439 / 74392. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.01367v2 — READ LEVEL: FULL TEXT (HTML + PDF, experiments and tables).
  REGIME: population = masked-diffusion checkpoints, both author-trained 110M models (SEDD, MDLM, BD3-LM at L'∈{4,8,16}; "All 110M models use 12-layer DiT, hidden dim 768, 12 heads") and one shipped 8B model, "LLaDA-8B-Base (Nie et al., 2025) with sequence length L=2048"; unit of analysis = per-corpus test perplexity per checkpoint; operating condition = evaluation-time deterministic unmasking policy (left-to-right / greedy / margin / threshold / oracle), ELBO taken as a K-sample Monte Carlo estimate with 1–128 samples; instrument = exact likelihood of the policy-induced distribution (Thm 4.3) against the K-sample ELBO; threshold = none, reported as perplexity; category = likelihood/perplexity benefit, not downstream accuracy. CANDIDATE SITS INSIDE IT for the headline question and for the 7–8B scale limb, but outside it on the estimand (see below).
  UNIT OF ANALYSIS: checkpoint × corpus perplexity. ENDPOINT: test perplexity (bound-derived vs exactly computed). COMPARATOR: an autoregressive model's exact perplexity on the same corpus (ARM baseline 17.54 on OWT, 22.83 on LM1B, Llama3-8B-Base at 8B). ESTIMAND: the exact log-likelihood of the distribution induced by a chosen deterministic unmasking policy — NOT the uniform-order marginal that the ELBO bounds.
  NUMBERS PRINTED, WITH LOCATION (per the published-is-not-performed rule): Table 5, caption "Zero-shot perplexity (↓) for 8B models.   UNITS CAVEAT, stated rather than silently converted: the paper prints PERPLEXITY and, in the text read, "does not state a log base or per-token normalization." The candidate asked for nats per token. Treating the printed numbers as exp of a mean natural-log per-token loss — AN ASSUMPTION THE PAPER DOES NOT STATE — MY OWN ARITHMETIC gives an 8B ELBO-minus-exact gap of ln(15.29)−ln(14.50)=0.053, ln(39.04)−ln(36.00)=0.081 and ln(85.17)−ln(78.91)=0.077 nats/token on Wikitext, Lambada and AG News. That conversion is mine and is labelled as such; the PRINTED quantity is perplexity.
  NOT PRINTED: no Monte Carlo standard errors or confidence intervals anywhere in the text read; no nats/token; no variance or range of implied log-likelihood across orders (Table 7's four rules plus an oracle is a comparison of five policies, not a measured spread over sampled orders); Dream-7B cited in references but never evaluated.
  HOW THE CANDIDATE DIFFERS: DUEL's exact number scores a DIFFERENT distribution from the one the ELBO bounds. The paper says so itself — "the ELBO measures p_θ^{π^unif}, not the test-time distribution", "it does not yield proper likelihood under the test-time distribution", and "Choosing a rule is not merely a computational convenience; it determines which distribution we sample from" — and then writes "The gains come entirely from evaluation, not model changes" without separating bound slack from the change of scored distribution. So DUEL's ELBO-minus-DUEL difference is NOT the candidate's "variational gap"; it is variational slack plus a policy substitution, unresolved into its parts. That residue is real but narrow.
