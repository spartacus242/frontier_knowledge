Alternative formulations:
- Does blinding change how well physicists' error bars predict their own later disagreement, as distinct from changing their central values?

Motivating gap: The blinding-in-physics literature is dense with HOW and empty of measurement. The record therefore conceded the central-value limb as asked-and-open and claimed only the uncertainty limb as unasked.

Output: a ratio of calibration factors with an interval and a sign, or a demonstration of non-identifiability.

Verification (post-batch, fresh-context checker, log UQ-20261017-05-verification.md): the Disguise kill is CONFIRMED on the point that decides the verdict, the second kill's attribution is wrong while its substance survives as the ledger's own algebra, and the routed row is corrected on two counts.
Henrion & Fischhoff 1986 (Am. J. Phys. 54(9):791-798) poses the candidate's uncertainty limb, verbatim: "Having a preexisting recommended value may particularly encourage investigators to discard or adjust unexpected results, and so induce correlated errors in apparently independent experiments", and "The result would be initially small Birge ratios, unduly tight confidence intervals, and the exclusion of discrepant data." And it does not answer it: the mechanism is hypothesised and illustrated qualitatively (the speed-of-light bandwagon, Millikan's notebooks), with no exposed-versus-unexposed or blinded-versus-unblinded arm anywhere. What it measures is pooled calibration — Birge ratio, interquartile index and surprise index over the speed of light (27 values, 1875-1958), G (14 values), the proton magnetic moment, the inverse fine-structure constant, the ohm ratio and 306 Particle Data Group lifetimes and meson masses — concluding "Reported uncertainties are too small." So the candidate's question was asked in a physics journal in 1986 and is still unanswered, which is what makes this an already-asked rejection and what makes the row routable.
One correction to the kill, in the candidate's favour and recorded because it matters for the row rather than for the verdict: the remedy Henrion & Fischhoff propose is NOT analysis blinding. It is referee-blind review — "referees might be asked to scrutinize the account of an experiment 'blind,' before knowing its outcome and whether it affirmed prior expectations." Blinding the analyst to the recommended value is the candidate's operationalisation, not theirs, and the entry's earlier phrasing that they proposed the candidate's remedy is struck.
The second kill's attribution is wrong. Klein & Roodman (2005) report no comparison of uncertainty calibration between blinded and unblinded analyses, and no statement that a central-value bias contributes to the variance of normalised residuals — the terms "pull" and "normalized residual" do not appear in what could be read. What is there is a chi-square check on four historical unblinded series: "the measurements do tend to cluster nearer the prior published averages than the final value", with "the chi2 for the hypothesis that the measurements are normally distributed around the prior averages is 131.2 for 83 degrees of freedom, whereas the chi2 for the hypothesis that they are normally distributed about the final average is 249.7". The substance of the attack survives on those two printed numbers, but as this ledger's own arithmetic and algebra rather than as a cited result: E[p^2] carries the squared bias, so a calibration factor estimated on normalised residuals cannot exclude the central-value limb, and the factor implied by the two printed chi-squares moves from sqrt(131.2/83) = 1.26 to sqrt(249.7/83) = 1.73 on the choice of reference alone. The entry's figure of 1.75 is corrected to 1.73, and the claim is relabelled as the ledger's derivation. Presupposition 6 is still false, so attack 4 stands — on arithmetic the ledger owns rather than on a sentence misattributed to Klein & Roodman.
