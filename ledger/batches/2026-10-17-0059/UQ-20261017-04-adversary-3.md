## Presupposition tests against the literature

- P2 ("universal-MLIP effective-harmonic phonons are a usable instrument in THIS regime — unrelaxed deposited geometries"): CONTRADICTED by the method's own prerequisites. The imaginary-modes review states the structure must be "relaxed to a stationary point on the potential-energy surface (PES) and the forces are equal to zero" and that "a well-converged geometry optimisation is a prerequisite"; its Table I lists "(I) Inadequately relaxed structure" as an origin of imaginary modes with the remedy "Tighten force convergence criteria" [S1]. phonopy instructs: "Relax the crystal structure with the potential before the phonon calculation so that the residual forces, and the residual stress on the lattice, vanish" [S2]. A deposited geometry is a stationary point of an MLIP's PES only by coincidence (the MLIP would have to reproduce the thermal-average experimental cell and coordinates exactly). The candidate's frame therefore places EVERY entry, correct or wrong, in the review's first artefact class by construction. The `--fz` residual-force subtraction [S2] removes first-order contamination of the finite-difference Hessian but does not make the Hessian at a non-stationary point the dynamical matrix of any equilibrium state; the review's own example shows the flag's sign depends on where on the PES it is evaluated: "the imaginary modes in RS SnS harden under compression and eventually become real" [S1].
- P4 ("a deposited model can be evaluated at its reported temperature under a defined finite-temperature effective-harmonic treatment", without relaxation): CONTRADICTED for both canonical effective-harmonic methods. SSCHA's free-energy minimization relaxes the atomic (centroid) positions — the guide explains that for Au "the atomic positions have no degrees of freedom, thus the relaxation will end before even start", i.e. for any structure WITH free positional parameters the minimization moves them [S3]. TDEP defines displacements "from their equilibrium positions" and uses first-order force constants "to find the finite temperature equilibrium structure", with a `relax` option and the source comment "I wouldn't have used the first order if I did not need to relax?" [S4]. So a finite-temperature effective-harmonic evaluation relaxes the reference to the POTENTIAL's thermal equilibrium; it cannot be performed "WITHOUT relaxation" of the deposited model. The only non-relaxing alternative is the bare 0 K harmonic Hessian, to which temperature contributes nothing at the level of instability (harmonic force constants are temperature-independent by construction; cf. [S5]). P4 and "without relaxation" cannot both hold.
- P3 (artefacts separable): the review's classification supports the record's flag, but in this frame the dominant artefact class (I) is produced by the frame itself, and the record's remedy — DFT on a stratified subsample — cannot detect it, because DFT evaluated at the same non-stationary point shares it (my inference from [S1]'s classification, which is engine-independent).
- P1 (adjudicated-wrong set enumerable): plausible; untested here (budget). The record's conditioning caveat stands.
- P5: fine as a design choice.
- P6: unverified by me (see above).
- The "averaged model of a superstructure" outcome class: the review documents exactly this phenomenon for correct experimental structures — "the Cmcm phase has intrinsic dynamical instabilities and, like the cubic perovskite structure, is an average" [S1] — but the statement is about the stationary point of the symmetric structure. At an arbitrary non-stationary deposited point the "average-structure" reading is not available.

## Scope check on my technical claim (required by the brief)

Claim: harmonic phonon frequencies computed at a non-stationary point of the PES are, by the field's own classification, an artefact source rather than a measure of dynamical stability, and the finite-temperature effective-harmonic machinery relaxes the reference. Regime check: the candidate evaluates deposited geometries on an MLIP PES. (a) Cell: an MLIP inherits its training functional's equilibrium-lattice bias and has no thermal expansion at the Hessian level, so the deposited cell is not the MLIP's equilibrium cell — residual stress ≠ 0 (phonopy names "residual stress on the lattice" alongside residual forces [S2]). (b) Internal coordinates: free positional parameters differ from the MLIP minimum by the model's and the experiment's errors — residual forces ≠ 0. Exception: structures with all atoms on fixed special positions have zero internal residual forces by symmetry; for them only (a) applies, and [S1]'s SnS passage shows (a) alone flips the flag. So the claim holds across the candidate's frame; the magnitude of the artefact varies by structure and is largest exactly along the soft directions where the "superstructure" signal is supposed to live. I did not quantify the typical MLIP lattice bias (no source opened) and do not rely on a number for it.
