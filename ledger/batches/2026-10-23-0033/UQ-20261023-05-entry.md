Domain(s): Biology — systematics and phylogenomics; every comparative-methods analysis conditioning on a published topology. Method home: forecast verification / calibration and the mutual-disagreement bound of measurement science.

Question (canonical): For a pre-declared frame of pairs of published phylogenetic studies sharing at least k taxa after a declared taxonomic-name reconciliation, what is the realized rate at which a clade published at >=95% support is incompatible, on the shared taxon set and under a declared bipartition-conflict test, with a clade published at >=95% support in the other study of the pair — reported by support type, stratified by a declared study-to-study data-overlap measure and by era, as a lower bound on the joint error of the two >=95% labels — and how does that rate compare with a per-method nominal benchmark declared in advance?
Reformulation note: this is V2, the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation; V1 ("a later independent study", "consistent with what the label nominally means") was undecidable on independence and had no per-method referent. The single reformulation is therefore spent.

Alternative formulations:
- Have phylogenetic support values ever been verified against outcomes, the way a weather forecast is verified?
- How often do two papers both claim >=95% certainty for incompatible trees of the same taxa?

Motivating gap: silence mapping — support values are computed on nearly every published tree and have a large theory/simulation literature, but their realized reliability as published has never been checked against the published record. The field validates support against a known or reference truth; this design's comparator is another published study and needs no truth.

Presuppositions: (1) published clades carry machine-extractable support with topology at scale — evidenced-contested (support numbers in 1 of 5 opened Open Tree studies, 2 of 16 trees; support type machine-declared in 0 of 5; adversary #3's free GitHub search found ot:bootstrapValues in 238 phylesystem-1 files, a numerator with no denominator), pre-registered as an answer branch; (2) "same taxa" and "contradicted" decidable under declared name reconciliation and bipartition test — TRUE, implemented at corpus scale by prior 3; (3) two studies of the same taxa are not independent — handled as a stratifier; (4) support types not interchangeable — flagged, crosswalk inferred from range; (5) mutual incompatibility lower-bounds the joint error of the two labels — the record grades this "arithmetic, not an assumption"; this is the clause that failed (see adversarial log, attack 4); (6) re-study is non-random, biasing upward; (7) labels are intended to carry a frequentist or posterior meaning — partly answered against for the bootstrap (Huang et al. 2020: no point limit in the equally-right/equally-wrong regime). Repair-introduced premise: a study-to-study data-overlap measure computable from what papers report — UNVERIFIED by the record and by both adversaries who tried to instance-check it.

What would count as an answer: an extracted corpus with explicit counts (incompatible >=95% clade pairs over comparable clade pairs), a reliability curve of contradiction rate against the label stratified by support type, overlap and era, and sensitivity analysis over the name and conflict rules; a negative answer is a rate at or below the few-percent level the label implies; if the corpus premise fails, the availability/granularity finding is the answer and must be reported as such and not as a small contradiction rate.

Significance — audience: support-method developers (two full-text papers five years apart motivated by "strong support for wrong trees"; a whole Syst Biol 71(4) special issue on new support measures, whose introduction states the frequency premise and never grades the printed label); comparative-methods users needing a defensible uncertainty prior; Open Tree / TreeBASE curators, for whom the documented granularity gap is a cheap, specific curation ask.

Significance — fecundity: does the rate fall as data scale grows; does it differ by support type once a crosswalk is declared (an empirical test of presupposition 4); Brier decomposition of published labels into reliability and resolution; whether overlap stratification separates two looks at one dataset from genuine replication; whether contradiction rate predicts later taxonomic revision.

Significance — anchoring: the "well-supported but conflicting topologies" anomaly; Huang et al. 2020's asymptotic non-convergence; the phylogenetic data-availability problem (Drew et al. 2013, title level only); the realized-error-rate-of-stated-uncertainty tradition in physics and reliability engineering.

Reason for silence: disciplinary boundary plus an instrument/incentive gap — the method's home field (forecast verification, metrology) does not own the data, and support is largely not deposited with topology, so the audit was expensive to impossible over much of the record it would audit, while effort flows to proposing new measures rather than grading the printed one.
