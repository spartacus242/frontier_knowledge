What that paragraph contains (verbatim fragments as returned by the fetch of
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-64918-6):
- the room-temperature conformation of the K+-binding loop "is not observed in any of the available
  cryo structures including ours";
- it "is also not predicted by AlphaFold3" (cited to Supplementary Fig. 8);
- "presumably hinting at a current strong prediction bias of the algorithm towards cryogenic
  temperatures";

  structures collected at temperatures below 220 K" (as of July 2025);

0.832 Å, i.e. the global agreement is good and the disagreement is local — exactly the regime the
candidate proposed to study.

## Recommendation

**Novelty FALLS: Medium → Low. Reject UQ-20261015-07 at Gate 4 as already asked and answered.** The
prior is Günther et al. 2025, *Nature Communications* 16, 9089 (doi 10.1038/s41467-025-64918-6),
Discussion plus Supplementary Fig. 8 and Methods: AlphaFold3 fails to predict the room-temperature
K+-binding-loop conformation of FosA^KP (residues 92–101) that is absent from all cryogenic
structures, which the authors read as "a current strong prediction bias of the algorithm towards
cryogenic temperatures… likely caused by the training data from the PDB" (~94% of entries collected
below 220 K), while flagging that FosA^KP cryo structures were probably in the training set. If the
ledger wants to keep anything, the only survivor is a narrower, quantitative successor — a
multi-protein, multi-seed, exposure-stratified measurement of the effect size against a prior-only
rotamer comparator — which query 14 suggests nobody has run, but which no source poses as an open
question either, so it is a research proposal rather than an un-asked question.
