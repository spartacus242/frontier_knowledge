## Restatement (my words)

Shipped libm accuracy is reported as a maximum ulp error found by exhaustive (binary32) or adversarial black-box (binary64) search over the whole encoding space. The candidate asks: if you instead record the arguments that real HPC/ML programs actually pass to exp, log, pow, sin, cos, tan, erf, exp2, rsqrt, tanh (by interposing on an instrumented corpus), then (a) what fraction of those calls hit arguments at which glibc, LLVM-libc, a vendor CPU library, CUDA libdevice and its fast-math variant disagree in the last bit, (b) what fraction is not correctly rounded per library and what does the ulp-error histogram look like, and (c) how do those usage-weighted figures compare with the same figures under uniform weighting over the representable domain? In short: are the published worst cases reachable by production argument streams, and what is "usage-weighted" libm accuracy relative to "domain-weighted" accuracy?

## My own arithmetic (labelled; elementary, uncertainty ~±1 exponent value per boundary)

Uniform weighting over the representable domain is uniform over (sign, biased exponent, mantissa). binary64 normals span 2046 exponent values; binary32 normals span 254.
- exp, binary64, RN: |x| < 2^-54 rounds to 1.0 (unbiased exponent <= -55: 968 values); x >= 2^10 overflows to +inf and x <= -2^10 underflows to 0 (exponent >= 10: 1014 values); overflow actually starts at 709.78 and underflow-to-zero at about -745.13, so exponent 9 is partly trivial too. Non-trivial share <= 64/2046 ~ 3.1%, i.e. >= ~97% of the domain-uniform mass sits where every implementation returns the exact correctly rounded answer.
- expf, binary32: |x| < 2^-25 -> 1.0; overflow at 88.72, zero below about -103.97. Non-trivial exponents -25..6 (6 partial) ~ 31.5/254 ~ 12%, so ~88% of the uniform mass is trivial. Dividing RLIBM-32's glibc expf count by the non-trivial band: 1.7E5 / (0.12 * 2^32) ~ 3.3e-4 misrounded within the band versus 4.0e-5 over all encodings (counts quoted to two significant figures, so ~±5%).
- sin/cos, binary64: |x| < 2^-26 gives sin(x)=x exactly (~49% of exponent values); |x| >= 2^53 (~47%) are integers no production code passes to sin; the "ordinary" range 2^-26 <= |x| < 2^53 holds ~79/2046 ~ 3.9% of the uniform mass.
- log and rsqrt are scale-covariant (log(2^k m) = k ln 2 + log m), so uniform-over-encodings is roughly uniform over exponent x mantissa and, barring the x~1 cancellation band, a near-constant misrounding rate across exponents is expected; the (i)/(ii) factor is then predicted ~1 regardless of corpus.
Consequence: the candidate's "by what factor do (i) and (ii) differ" clause is largely fixed a priori per function (factor >> 1 for saturating/trivial-tail functions exp, exp2, erf, tanh, sin, cos, tan; ~1 for log, rsqrt) before any corpus is instrumented. The premises "implementations are exact in the trivial regions" and "usage mass sits in the non-trivial band" are not sourced here, so this is an argument, not a cited result.
