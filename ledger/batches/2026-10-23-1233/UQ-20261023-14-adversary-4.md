## 0. Restatement in my own words (not the generator's)

Fix a list of clock installations before looking at any data: one row per (operator, clock-or-receiver-model) pair. Keep a row only if (i) somebody can get hold of a per-unit record of that unit's time error, phase offset, holdover state **or** alarms, sampled at least every 15 minutes for at least 30 straight days covering a GNSS disturbance, (ii) that record says which model the unit is (or at least what class of holdover oscillator it has), and (iii) the thing the unit feeds is covered by one of a named list of standards. The disturbance window must be dated from something other than those units' own output — regulator bulletins, NOTAMs, aircraft-derived interference maps, NAGUs, constellation or ground-segment anomaly notices. Then: how big was the worst time error those units actually handed to the thing they feed (middle and upper quantiles, per disturbance and per model, with unit counts), and what share of units went past the number its governing standard prints (with a Clopper–Pearson interval)? Also report how much of the nominal denominator was lost to records that turned out to be missing.

In one line: *nobody has ever scored the deployed clock fleet against its own standards' numbers during real GNSS trouble — what would the scoreboard say?*
