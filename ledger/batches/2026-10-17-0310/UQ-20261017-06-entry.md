Domain(s): AI architecture — sparse mixture-of-experts LM design and pretraining dynamics. Neighbours searched: permutation symmetry / linear mode connectivity and model merging; clustering stability and cluster validity (statistics); label switching and latent-class identifiability (finite mixtures); interpretability universality / convergent learning; vision-MoE representational analysis; expert offloading and serving.

Question (canonical): Train k >= 3 sparse-MoE language models under one recipe differing only in seed (router/expert init and data order), at a scale at which OLMoE's router-saturation curve reproduces; with a pre-declared probe corpus, layer set, top-k and expert matching rule M (Hungarian matching on per-expert activation profiles), measure with CIs (i) A_cross, cross-run agreement of the token-to-expert partition after M (ARI/NMI) at the 1%-of-pretraining checkpoint and at the end; (ii) S, the same agreement within a run between its 1% and final checkpoints (OLMoE's ~60% top-8 saturation); (iii) A_0, nulls from expert label permutation and from matching one run to an unrelated run; and (iv) the share of between-seed variance in terminal held-out loss and downstream scores predictable from 1%-checkpoint routing statistics — reporting A_cross - A_0 and A_cross/S per layer and the sensitivity of all four to the load-balancing auxiliary-loss weight.
(Gate 1 version A, as intaken, recorded not graded: is the token-to-expert partition that saturates in the first ~1% of MoE pretraining canonical — reproduced up to expert relabelling by independent runs of one recipe — or an arbitrary early lottery, and does it predict the run's terminal quality? One reformulation used; Gate 1 budget spent.)

Alternative formulations: (1) Do two MoE runs of one recipe differing only in seed converge, after optimal expert matching, on the same expert specialization, or does each freeze a different arbitrary partition in its first ~20B tokens? (2) How much of the run-to-run variance in a sparse MoE's final quality is already determined by routing fixed in the first 1% of pretraining? (3) Is the router's induced clustering of tokens over experts stable across resamplings of training stochasticity, or a stability-zero partition that only looks structured because it is frozen early?

Motivating gap: Anomaly mining on a quantified, never-cross-checked observation. OLMoE §5.1 (arxiv.org/html/2409.02060v2, read): after 1% of pretraining (5000 steps / 20B tokens) "up to ~60% of routing to the top-8 activated experts has already saturated", rising to ~80% at 40%, against a 8/64 = 12.5% random baseline — but measured WITHIN one run, against its own final checkpoint, with no cross-seed comparison. FLAME-MoE (arXiv:2505.20225, opened) independently reports that "routing behavior stabilizes early in training", likewise within-run and with no seed replicates. So the field holds two reports that MoE routing is effectively decided in the first 1% of training and, on everything opened at Gate 2, no measurement of whether what is decided is the same thing twice. Gate 4 complicated this: OpenMoE (arXiv:2402.01739, title/phrase level only) named "early routing learning" and a token-ID mechanism before OLMoE, so the anchor was interrogated — just not across seeds; and Equifinality (arXiv:2604.14419, abstract + §1-4.3 read) states in its introduction "Cross-seed analysis reveals 500x-above-random functional overlap through entirely different weight parameterizations", method unread. Verification (post-batch, three fresh-context checkers, logs UQ-20261017-06-verification.md, -verification-companion.md, -verification-threeseed.md): all three Unverified Disguise checks are now discharged, and the strongest objection is substantially refuted — the 500x overlap is a different object from limb (i).
**The cap.** arXiv:2604.14419 ("Equifinality in Mixture of Experts: Routing Topology Does Not Determine Language Modeling Quality", Ternovtsii & Bilak) was opened through to its cross-seed section. The architecture is an ST-MoE decoder-only transformer with cosine routing to learned 64-dimensional centroids, top-k=4, 3 hops, 1024 singleton experts, 37.6M and 76-84M parameters, WikiText-103 with a 32K BPE vocabulary, seeds 42, 137 and 7. The "500x-above-random functional overlap" is a **Jaccard similarity between the top-20 projected-vocabulary word sets** of an expert and its best match in the other seed: "Jaccard similarity of top-20 projected vocabulary words (best-match across 1024 experts)", averaging "0.15 (Wide) and 0.13 (Deep)" against "the random baseline of ~0.0003". Weight space shows nothing: "best-match cosine similarity averages 0.107 for both architectures".
That is an output-side **function** overlap, not an agreement between the two runs' token-to-expert partitions. Four further differences, each from the opened text: matching is "best-match across 1024 experts", with no Hungarian, assignment or one-to-one constraint found, so many experts may share a best match and the statistic is a per-expert maximum; the comparison is one seed pair (42 vs 137) at **converged checkpoints only** — nothing at 1% of training, where limbs (i) and (iv) are posed; no cross-seed quantity is related to held-out loss or downstream score; and the ~0.0003 null is not derived in the paper and looks like a single-pair expectation rather than a max-over-1024 null, which would make the 500x ratio an overstatement in the paper's own favour. So limb (i) is not "in substance" measured there, the Gate 2 miss is a real miss of a relevant neighbour rather than of a prior, and novelty is not reduced to Low. The entry's attack-1 leg that Equifinality "predicts limb (iv) near-null" is correspondingly weakened and restated: its cross-seed measure is never connected to quality, so it forecasts nothing about limb (iv).
**The two residuals.** Companion arXiv:2604.14434 ("Geometric Routing Enables Causal Expert Control in Mixture of Experts") is an intervention study on single converged checkpoints — Semantic Dictionary projections, knockout, steering, output-vector rewriting — on Wide 1x12 (PPL 33.93) and Deep 3x4 (PPL 34.62). It carries **no cross-seed overlap statistic at all**; its only permutation null is 200 label permutations for a silhouette score, and its Jaccard figures (14.3-33.3%) compare contexts within one model. The "15 runs across 3 seeds" it mentions belong to 2604.14419. REFUTED as a prior. arXiv:2607.28308 ("Beyond Geometric Complementarity: Coherent Overlap in Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Routing") has three seeds only for a matched-compute Top-1 versus Top-2 validation-loss comparison in a six-layer MoE (delta = 0.1016 +/- 0.0025), and its "expert subspaces overlap substantially" is a **within-model** measurement: each expert gets its own centred rank-128 subspace and the index compares inter-expert separation against within-expert dispersion inside one model.
