Question (canonical) — V1 (the single permitted Gate 1 reformulation; V0 below):
On a pre-declared frame F of preference corpora that ship stable per-annotator identifiers (or a usable proxy: vendor, shift, guideline revision), under a pre-declared cross-classified model of the pairwise outcome — a Bradley-Terry / logistic specification with crossed random effects for annotator, annotator-cluster (vendor x shift x guideline revision) and item, plus a linear-probability companion — (a) what are the variance components and the resulting annotator-level and cluster-level intraclass correlations, on BOTH declared scales (latent-logistic and linear-probability), with CIs; (b) what Kish design effect Deff and effective sample size N_eff do they imply when computed with the UNEQUAL cluster sizes actually present rather than an equal-cluster formula, with sensitivity to the cluster definition; and (c) holding total labels fixed and varying the number of distinct annotators, does reward-model accuracy (or a declared RLHF endpoint) track N_eff rather than N, against pre-registered bands (NULL Deff <= 1.1 / MODEST 1.1-2 / MATERIAL > 2)?

Question (V0, as intaken): In the human preference datasets used to train reward models and to adjudicate model comparisons, what is the annotator-attributable share of label variance — the intraclass correlation among labels from the same annotator and among annotators sharing a vendor, shift or guideline revision — and what effective sample size does the resulting design effect leave for a corpus quoted as N preference pairs?

Reason for the reformulation (well-posedness only): V0 leaves the ICC scale undeclared, and for a BINARY pairwise outcome the ICC is scale-dependent — a latent-logistic ICC and a linear-probability ICC are different numbers from the same fit, and a Deff built from one is not the Deff built from the other. V0 also leaves the frame undeclared, which matters because P1 is a binding feasibility risk. V1 fixes the scale, the frame, the model and the bands and changes nothing about what is being asked.

Alternative formulations:
1. If a dataset says it holds a million preference labels, how many independent judgments is that really?
2. Treat the annotator as the interviewer and the preference label as the interview: what is the intra-interviewer correlation and the interviewer design effect of a preference corpus?

Motivating gap: Official statistics will not publish a survey estimate without an intra-interviewer correlation and a design effect converting nominal N to N_eff; preference corpora are quoted in raw label counts and reward-model sample-size reasoning treats labels as independent. The quantity is DEFINED and ILLUSTRATED in the candidate's own field (Kopteva & Hlynianyi-Zhuk below) and never MEASURED — the "published is not performed" pattern rather than a known. Cluster sizes in production pipelines are large, so the inflation is driven as much by n as by rho: at n = 2,000 labels per annotator, even rho = 0.01 gives Deff ~ 21 under equal clusters, which is why the number, not the direction, is the question.

Presuppositions:
P1 Annotator identifiers, or a usable proxy, are recoverable for a non-trivial frame. UPGRADED FROM BINDING RISK TO PARTLY ESTABLISHED at Gate 2: Raj (arXiv:2606.27578, opened) reports PRISM as having "stable per-annotator IDs" and uses 1,394 users with n_j >= 6, and PluriHarms as 15,000 harm ratings from 100 annotators on 150 prompts with "a stable per-annotator ID"; the same source states HelpSteer2 annotator "individual identities are not released", and that OASST2 (author) and SHP (subreddit) groupings are not rater ids. So the frame exists but is narrow, and must still be declared before any pull. READ LEVEL for this fact: summariser-mediated targeted read of the PDF, not a full read.
P2 A crossed or partially crossed design exists (same item labelled by >= 2 annotators AND same annotator labelling >= 2 items). PARTLY ESTABLISHED, still CONTESTED: PluriHarms is 100 annotators x 150 prompts with "Each prompt-response pair is rated by multiple annotators" (Raj, opened), which implies crossing, but Raj does not print the rater-by-item cross-tabulation, and for PRISM he states the analysis is nested rather than crossed. Production pipelines label most items once, so the estimable frame may be only an overlap sample.
P3 For the design-effect limb, "noise" and legitimate preference heterogeneity need not be separated, because both reduce the effective N for a SINGLE-reward-function target. CONTESTED and explicitly flagged: on a pluralistic-alignment reading annotator variance is signal, not error. V1 states the single-target estimand so the question does not equivocate. (Note the irony surfaced at Gate 2: the nearest measurement neighbour, Raj, is a Pluralistic Alignment workshop paper that treats the same variance as structure to preserve.)
P4 Deff governs downstream behaviour, i.e. reward-model error tracks N_eff rather than N. CONTESTED; limb (c) tests it by subsampling rather than assuming the survey analogy.
P5 Cluster structure is non-trivial (annotators label many items each, within vendor-level clusters). Likely in production, MEASURED not assumed in V1; the PRISM n_j >= 6 filter shows that for public corpora cluster sizes can be small, which cuts the other way.
P6 Someone would act: teams buying annotation decide how many labels and from how many distinct annotators. The decision is real; demand for the statistic is inferred, not evidenced.

What would count as an answer:
(i) Variance components (annotator, annotator-cluster, item, residual) from a crossed or partially crossed preference-labelling design on >= 2 corpora in F, or one purpose-collected replication, under the pre-declared cross-classified model, with CIs, on both declared scales.
(ii) rho and Kish Deff computed with the clustering ACTUALLY present (unequal cluster sizes), with sensitivity to the cluster definition (annotator / vendor / shift / guideline version), and N_eff reported beside the corpus's advertised N.
(iii) A downstream validation holding total labels fixed and varying the number of distinct annotators, reporting reward-model accuracy or an RLHF endpoint as a function of N versus N_eff.
A clean negative — rho near zero, Deff near 1, no difference in the subsampling arm — counts and retires the concern.
NOT answers: pairwise inter-annotator agreement rates alone; annotator demographic-bias studies; a Deff computed from an ASSUMED rho; prompt-clustered power calculations; a variance partition whose facet is the LLM judge rather than the human annotator.

Significance — consequence: If the annotator ICC is non-negligible, the arithmetic is unforgiving because Deff = 1 + (n-1)rho is driven by cluster size, and production annotators label thousands of items each: rho = 0.01 at n = 2,000 gives Deff ~ 21, so a corpus advertised as a million preference pairs is worth order 50,000 independent judgments, every binomial CI on a reward-model evaluation is too narrow by roughly sqrt(Deff), and the marginal label from an existing annotator is worth a small fraction of the marginal label from a new one — which inverts the cheapest way to buy data. Procurement would shift from depth to breadth, and dataset cards would owe an N_eff beside their N. If instead rho is near zero, Deff near 1 and the subsampling arm flat, the field's unstated independence assumption is licensed for the first time and the concern retires — a publishable null. The asymmetry is the point: the two outcomes prescribe opposite purchasing behaviour, and nobody has the number.
Significance — audience: (1) Teams buying annotation, who already decide labels-per-annotator versus number-of-annotators — the one decision here that is documented rather than inferred, since PEBS's own frame shows corpora being built with per-annotator identifiers and minimum per-rater counts (PRISM's n_j >= 6, PluriHarms' 100 x 150). (2) Leaderboard operators who publish CIs on preference votes; the "Leaderboard Illusion" exchange shows this community already argues about the standard errors of preference aggregates, and voter-level clustering is a correction none of them applies. (3) Reward-model researchers reporting accuracy with independence-assuming intervals. (4) Dataset publishers deciding whether to ship annotator ids — a decision whose cost is zero and whose value this question prices. Honestly discounted: for audience (3) demand is inferred; and the pluralistic-alignment community (PEBS's own venue) would read the same variance as signal to preserve rather than noise to divide out, so for them the quantity is interesting but the N_eff framing is not their decision.
Significance — fecundity: whether Deff explains the observed flattening of reward-model accuracy against preference-data scale; whether vendor-level clusters align with identifiable reward directions (a bridge to reward-hacking work); whether annotator-stratified purchasing beats random purchasing at fixed budget; whether DPO/GRPO and other direct-alignment pipelines inherit the same deflation or are differently exposed; whether guideline revisions act as period effects in a time series of corpora; and whether Messing's judge-facet partition and this annotator-facet partition compose, giving a total design effect for a pipeline that uses human preference data to train and LLM judges to evaluate.
Significance — anchoring: the reward-model data-scaling plateau; the leaderboard-CI dispute (Chatbot Arena arXiv:2403.04132 and LMArena's response to "The Leaderboard Illusion"); the pluralistic-alignment debate about whether annotator variance is error or signal; Kopteva & Hlynianyi-Zhuk's explicitly unmade measurement; Kish's deff and the official-statistics convention of never publishing an estimate without one. Reason for silence: a new-data mechanism plus a disciplinary boundary, with an incentive gap on top. (1) New instrument: the quantity is unestimable without stable per-annotator identifiers, and the corpora that carry them are recent — PEBS's own frame shows the field splitting into corpora with stable ids (PRISM 2024, PluriHarms 2026) and the widely used ones without (HelpSteer2 "individual identities are not released"; OASST2 and SHP offer only author and subreddit groupings). The most-cited preference corpora made the measurement impossible, so the absence of the number is partly an absence of the frame. (2) Disciplinary boundary: the method is owned by survey statistics and official statistics (intra-interviewer correlation, Kish deff, ESS) while the data is owned by ML annotation vendors; where the method has crossed into this field it crossed through educational measurement into the LLM-JUDGE facet (Messing's D-study), not into the human-annotator facet, so the bridge was built next door. (3) Incentive gap: a published design effect divides the headline number that dataset papers and annotation vendors sell, and nobody in the supply chain benefits from reporting that their million labels are worth fifty thousand. Competing mechanism to hold against this, flagged rather than suppressed: the pluralistic-alignment reading means some of the field has already decided annotator variance is signal, in which case the silence is not an oversight but a different research programme — and that would also weaken the significance case (P3, audience limb 4).
