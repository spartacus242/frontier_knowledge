## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take one training run. At the end of it there are many saved checkpoints that are, on the thing the run was optimising (held-out validation loss), indistinguishable from the one the lab actually released — "indistinguishable" fixed in advance by a tolerance band. Call those the siblings of the shipped file. The question is three-part:
(a) How much do the numbers people actually care about — benchmark accuracy, how often the model refuses harmful requests, how often jailbreaks get through, how often it finishes long agentic tasks — move as you walk across those siblings, with checkpoint-to-checkpoint movement separated from eval-item sampling noise?
(b) Is that movement bigger or smaller than the error bar that is currently printed next to such a number for a single checkpoint (item-level / paired bootstrap)?
(c) If the lab picks the sibling that looks best (on loss, or on a dev suite it then reports), how inflated is the published figure relative to a randomly chosen in-band sibling scored on a fresh eval draw?
Underneath: does a published capability or safety number describe the recipe, or does it describe which file happened to be uploaded — and by how many error-bar widths?
