## Restatement (my own words)

When a high-throughput DFT pipeline drops the calculations that fail to converge, are the dropped materials a random subset of what was attempted, with respect to the properties the database is used for (formation energy, gap, magnetic state, dynamical stability), once composition and structure are accounted for? Or does the numerics filter preferentially remove certain kinds of materials, so that hull counts, screening hit rates and MLIP errors computed on the survivors are biased — and by how much? The design: enumerate an attempt frame, recover a sample of failures by escalated settings or a second code, compare property distributions of recovered-failed vs retained under a pre-declared featurization, hand-sort failures into numerics-driven vs physics-driven, and recount a published downstream statistic.

## What the opened documents say (quotes verbatim from the fetched text)

**MC3D — Huber, Minotakis, Bercx, Reents, Eimre, Paulish, Hörmann, Uhrin, Marzari, Pizzi, arXiv:2508.19223 (dated 2025-09-06 in the PDF).**
- "Out of the 38 739 structures that have been processed, the relaxation workflow successfully completed 33 142 structures, corresponding to a success rate of 85.5%." Fig. 3 caption: "About 14.5% of workflows fail to complete successfully despite the error handling mechanism." (My arithmetic: 5,597 terminal failures.)
- "The vast majority of errors come from the failure to converge to the required precision" (geometry optimisation or SCF). Fig. 3 caption: "The most common observed errors are the failure of the pw.x simulation code to achieve ionic or electronic convergence". Error handling "manages to recover from these errors in over half of the cases in which a single pw.x execution fails." Restart distribution: 67.4% finished first time; 14.5%, 3.0%, 0.6% needed 1, 2, 3+ restarts.
- Failures ARE broken down by covariates: Fig. 2 shows outcomes "as a function of (a) the number of species (i.e., distinct chemical elements) in the structure" and "(b) the number of atoms in the primitive cell" with "red indicates workflows that failed with an unrecoverable error."
- Failures are NOT broken down by physical property: "all structures are considered to be magnetic and metallic in behavior in the initial geometry optimization"; the paper does not compare properties of failed vs successful structures.
- Provenance: Abstract: "The database includes the full provenance graph of all the calculations driven by the automated workflows." Data availability: for COD structures the archives "contain the complete provenance of all calculations and data." (The paper does not state in so many words that failed nodes are in the public archive; the failure statistics are computed from the provenance.)
- A numerics-driven exclusion at the FRAME-definition stage: lanthanide and actinide structures were excluded because the available pseudopotentials "often lead to numerical instabilities and a significant failure rate."

**Kingsbury et al., PRM 6, 013801 (2022), full text.**
- Sec. III C: of roughly 11,000 SCAN and 25,000 r2SCAN calculations attempted, "we observed a completion rate of 96% for r2SCAN, but only 69% for SCAN." "Among the incomplete SCAN calculations, we estimate that at least 25% failed due to unrecoverable errors, whereas the remaining calculations may have simply run out of wall time, which was limited to 48 hours in our work." (My arithmetic, carrying the paper's "at least": unrecoverable-error share of attempted SCAN is between ~8% and 31%; r2SCAN incomplete share ~4%, composition unstated.)
- "the data in Figure 4a reflect only successful calculations" — acknowledged, not analysed. No statement about which kinds of materials failed; no property comparison of failed vs converged.

**Chan, Plumlee & Wild, "Constructing a simulation surrogate with partially observed output", arXiv:2304.09372 (statistics of computer experiments / UQ).**
- Frames failed-simulation outputs with the missing-data taxonomy: "missing completely at random (MCAR), missing at random (MAR), and missing not at random (MNAR)"; "in many cases it is MAR because run time depends on the input"; Sec. 5.1: "Under MNAR, the response missingness depends on unobserved quantities, for example, the value of the response." "Our proposed method does not address the source of missingness in the data."
- Prior practice: "these works assume no missing data (or simply remove output with missing data)"; Higdon et al. (2015): "9% of the model outputs were discarded due to such types of failures"; Bollapragada et al. (2021), on failure modes of the nuclear Fayans energy density functional, "show that it is not likely the output is MCAR"; "However, no systematic missingness mechanism is proposed."
- Application fields named: climate, fluid dynamics, nuclear physics (Fayans EDF), weather, storm surge, influenza. "Materials, chemistry, and DFT are not mentioned."
