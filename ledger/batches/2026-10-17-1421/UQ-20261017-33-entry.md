Question (canonical): Over a pre-declared frame of published non-clinical biological analyses that re-run end-to-end from archived code and data and reproduce their published headline numbers to a declared tolerance, and over a pre-registered frequency-weighted catalogue of single-point faults, what is the per-fault probability that an injected fault is both SILENT (the run completes and trips no hard failure, flagged threshold or visibly anomalous sanity plot among those the pipeline itself emits, judged by a blinded rubric with inter-rater agreement reported) and CONSEQUENTIAL (the paper's pre-extracted headline claim changes past that paper's own stated threshold), and does that probability partition more into analysis class than into the authors' software practices?
(Gate 1 reformulation V1 adopted; V0 intake text and the full V1 wording in batches/2026-10-17-1421/UQ-20261017-33-record.md.)

Alternative formulations:
(1) If a published biological analysis had one plausible bug — off-by-one coordinate, swapped strand, mis-sorted join, unit mismatch, mis-specified contrast — how often would nothing in the pipeline notice and the conclusion still change?
(2) What is the mutant-kill rate of biology's de facto test suite (its own QC plots and logs), scored against the biological conclusion rather than against the code?

Motivating gap: Software engineering and reliability engineering measure fault sensitivity by injection; computational biology imported only the dual technique (metamorphic testing), which scores a TOOL against invariants the testers authored. The field's evidence on faults in specific published analyses is anecdotal (column swaps, coordinate conventions, Excel gene-symbol corruption, one-line-script retractions) and supports no rate, no fault-class ranking, and no specification for the assertions that would catch them.
