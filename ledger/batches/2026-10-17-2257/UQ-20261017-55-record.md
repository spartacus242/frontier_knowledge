Question (canonical) — V2, the one Gate 1 reformulation, used for grading:
For K >= 3 cell types pre-declared from tissues whose in vivo temperature is distinctly non-37 °C (at least one cooler and one warmer), in a 2x2 factorial crossing tissue-matched vs 37 °C temperature with physioxic vs atmospheric oxygen, with proliferation-rate-matched controls and a distance metric to a freshly isolated in vivo reference profile both declared before any data are taken: what fraction of the baseline in vitro-to-in vivo distance is closed by temperature correction, reported per cell type with confidence intervals alongside the fraction closed by oxygen correction measured on the same scale and in the same experiment, and the temperature x oxygen interaction?

Question (canonical) — V1, as received at intake:
For cell types native to tissues whose in vivo temperature differs materially from 37 °C, how much of the phenotypic discordance between cultured cells and their in vivo counterparts is attributable to the universal 37 °C culture default?

Why reformulated (well-posedness only): V1's "how much ... is attributable" names no estimand, no reference and no metric, and so could be answered by any effect size in either direction; V2 fixes the comparator (tissue-matched vs 37 °C), the reference (freshly isolated in vivo profile), the metric (declared in advance), the rate-matching that separates temperature from the growth-rate change temperature itself causes, and the oxygen arm that puts the two defaults on one scale. No change of subject matter.

Alternative formulations:
- Is the standard 37 °C incubator a mis-specified condition for cells from cooler and warmer tissues, and how far do their transcriptomes, proliferation and differentiation move toward in vivo profiles at tissue-matched temperature?
- Does cell culture have a temperature problem of the same size as its documented oxygen problem?

Motivating gap:
A silence map, not an anomaly. The oxygen default carries an explicit, named critique literature (physioxia; supraphysiological oxygen), whereas the temperature default is dense in how-answers (incubator specification, calibration tolerance, QC logging) and nearly empty of why-questions, though in vivo tissue temperature spans roughly 32-40 °C. VERIFIED IN THIS TASK, from sources opened: (i) the oxygen critique exists and argues mis-specification — Alva et al. 2022 (Cells 11:3123) calls standard incubator headspace ~18% O2 a mismatch with 2-6% in most tissues and the commonly cited 20-21% figures "incorrect" — and that same review mentions temperature only in passing (as something incubators regulate) and never raises the parallel setpoint question; (ii) the best-practices review that owns the condition-specification agenda in this field, Klein et al. 2022, gives tissue-matched O2 values and recommends considering in vivo niche conditions, gives NO equivalent temperature ranges and makes NO tissue-matched temperature recommendation, writing instead "It is implicitly assumed that culture temperature is controlled at 37°C" and "Although thermal regimes of cell cultures may be reliably inferred from calibrated incubators"; (iii) the standards document that owns the method, GCCP 2.0, files temperature under calibration, monitoring and records (Table 3) with thresholds framed as heat shock above 39 °C and slowed replication below 35 °C, and describes room air as "hyperoxic, hypocapnic, and cold for cells in culture" — taking 37 °C as the reference against which other temperatures are deviations. Presuppositions:
1. In vivo temperature of each source tissue is known to ~1 °C in the relevant physiological state — CONTESTED for deep organs and across diurnal/activity variation. Partially supported for reproductive tract by measurements the Cochrane embryo-temperature review cites (follicular fluid vs ovarian stroma differing up to 2.3 °C near ovulation, Grinsted 1985; oviduct caudal isthmus 1-2 °C cooler than the ampulla in pig and cow, Bahat 2003/Hunter 1986).
2. 37 °C really is the near-universal default in the relevant literature — UNVERIFIED as a number. Treated here as a checkable claim, not a fact. Query 9 returned no protocol-corpus survey; the strongest support found is indirect (GCCP 2.0 and Klein et al. both describing 37 °C as the implicit assumption). GCCP 2.0 cuts the other way in one line — "The optimal culture temperature depends on the type of cells involved" — so the default may be softer in guidance than in practice. This presupposition must be measured before the factorial is worth running.
3. Cultured cells retain temperature-responsive programmes — CONTESTED at intake, now supported: Metzger et al. 2022 found genome-wide transcript and bioenergetic responses in porcine myoblasts at 35/39/41 vs 37 °C.
4. An in vivo reference profile exists for the same cell type and species against which discordance can be measured — LOAD-BEARING and the weakest link. Freshly isolated cells carry dissociation artefacts and in vivo atlases carry their own; no source opened here establishes an artefact-free reference.
5. Temperature effects are separable from the proliferation-rate change temperature causes — MAJOR CONFOUNDER; V2 requires rate-matched controls. GCCP 2.0's own note that "growth below 35°C typically slows replication" makes this unavoidable.
6. Incubators hold alternative setpoints with equal precision without altering pH or dissolved-gas conditions — FEASIBILITY, and contested: the CO2/bicarbonate pH coupling is temperature-dependent, and the Klein/KAUST group's own 2025 gas-only bioreactor work shows moderate pH deviations alone trigger transcriptome-wide responses, so pH must be instrumented, not assumed.
7. "Discordance attributable to temperature" is estimable as a variance share or distance reduction under a metric declared in advance — accepted only as a convention; see well-posedness score.
8. Media formulations developed at 37 °C do not themselves become limiting at other temperatures — CONFOUNDER, unchecked. Relevant neighbour: physiological-media x oxygen factorials already exist (see query 11), so media is a known-live third factor.

What would count as an answer:
Per cell type, the fraction of baseline in vitro-to-in vivo distance closed by tissue-matched temperature, with CIs, reported beside the fraction closed by physioxia in the same experiment, plus the interaction. A negligible temperature share beside a large oxygen share retires the question. A comparable or larger share, or a strong interaction, makes culture temperature a condition the standards and reporting-checklist community (GCCP/ALTEX, PRINCE, repository datasheets) would have to specify per cell type rather than inherit. NOTE A GENUINE ASYMMETRY FOUND IN THIS TASK: the oxygen side of that comparison does not yet exist either — Alva et al. 2022 is a narrative review with no pooled effect size, no meta-analysis and no attribution estimate, so the scale the candidate wants to place temperature on would have to be built by the same experiment. This is a cost, and also part of the candidate's fecundity.

Significance — consequence:
If the temperature share is comparable to or larger than oxygen's, or if there is a strong interaction, then culture temperature becomes a per-cell-type specification rather than an inherited constant: GCCP 2.0, PRINCE and repository datasheets would each have to carry a justified temperature, and the published phenotype of every cell type from a cool or warm tissue acquires a named, estimable bias term. If the share is negligible beside oxygen's, the field's inheritance is vindicated at low cost and the question retires — which is a useful negative precisely because the same experiment would produce the first attribution estimate for oxygen, which Alva et al. 2022 shows does not exist. The asymmetry cuts both ways: a null result here is not a wasted experiment.

Significance — audience:
(i) The cell-culture standards community — the GCCP 2.0 authorship (Pamies, Leist, Hartung, Stacey et al.) and the ALTEX/3Rs constituency — who would have to add a justification requirement to a parameter currently filed under calibration. (ii) The physioxia constituency (Stuart lab; Klein/Duarte group), who have built the argument for one condition and would be handed a second on their own scale. (iii) Organoid, MPS and organ-on-chip developers, whose selling point is in vivo fidelity and who already engineer non-default O2, pH and flow. (iv) Cell repositories (ATCC, ECACC), whose datasheets currently print 37 °C for essentially everything. (v) The tissue-specific communities already departing from 37 °C (testis 32-34 °C, skin models, cultivated meat at species temperature), who would gain a shared frame.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens the joint question — a condition budget across temperature, O2, CO2/pH and media, of which the Klein/KAUST pH work and the 2025 physiological-media x oxygen factorials are the first two cells. Makes askable: does tissue-matched temperature move drug-response IC50s and toxicology endpoints enough to matter for in vitro safety testing; do the per-tissue departures already in use generalise or are they local accidents; is there a thermal analogue of the HIF axis that gives temperature a mechanistic constituency; and what is the right in vivo reference when every candidate reference carries its own artefact.

Significance — anchoring:
The in vitro/in vivo transcriptome gap and loss of tissue identity in culture; the physioxia/supraphysiological-oxygen critique; cell-line drift and the irreproducibility literature; the reporting-checklist movement (PRINCE, GCCP 2.0, ARRIVE-analogues for in vitro work); and the open methodological question of what counts as a reference profile for a cell type.

Reason for silence:
A disciplinary-boundary and framing mechanism, with an instrument history and an incentive term — not "no one thought of it".
(1) Instrument history: temperature was solved at the equipment level before it was ever posed as a biological specification question. The water-jacketed 37 °C incubator made temperature the one culture parameter you could trust, and the literature that owns the method inherited that framing — GCCP 2.0 files temperature in a table of equipment parameters to monitor, and Klein et al. 2022 write that thermal regimes "may be reliably inferred from calibrated incubators" while insisting O2, CO2 and pH must be measured in the medium. A parameter that is already reliable does not look like a parameter that might be wrong.
(2) Framing capture: non-37 °C temperatures were claimed early by heat-shock and hypothermia biology, which read them as a STRESS or STIMULUS against a 37 °C baseline. GCCP 2.0's thresholds are stated in exactly those terms (heat shock above 39 °C, slowed replication below 35 °C), and its description of room air as "cold for cells in culture" takes 37 °C as the zero point. Febrile-range and mild-hypothermia work therefore accumulates as perturbation biology and never aggregates into a question about the default. Oxygen escaped this because HIF and ROS gave physioxia a mechanistic hook and a molecular-biology constituency.
(3) Disciplinary scatter: the per-tissue departures sit in communities with no shared forum — testis and spermatogenesis, skin models, cultivated meat, embryology — and embryology, the one that did pose the setpoint question, posed it in clinical vocabulary with live birth as the endpoint, which makes it illegible as a cell-biology methods question.
(4) Incentive: changing temperature changes proliferation rate, which breaks the comparability every protocol, passage schedule and historical control depends on. The confounder is not merely a design nuisance; it is a reason the experiment is unattractive to run and a reason the question is unattractive to ask.
