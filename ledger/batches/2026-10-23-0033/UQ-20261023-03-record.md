## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (V1, as received): Does the bacterial cell envelope fail by fatigue — that is, for repeated sub-lytic osmotic loading cycles, is the probability of lysis governed by the NUMBER of cycles at a given amplitude in the way an S-N stress-life relation describes, and does damage from mixed-amplitude sequences add linearly as Miner's rule predicts?

Question (canonical, V2 — the one reformulation permitted at Gate 1, for well-posedness only): For single *Escherichia coli* cells held in a microfluidic trap and subjected to repeated hypo-osmotic downshocks of fixed amplitude DPi (each below the single-shock lytic threshold) at a fixed recovery interval, does the per-cell hazard of lysis depend on the cycle index n after conditioning on the largest amplitude the cell has experienced — and if so, (a) is median cycles-to-lysis N(DPi) describable by a stress-life power law log N = a - b log DPi across >= 4 amplitudes, (b) is there an amplitude below which no lysis occurs within the attainable cycle budget, and (c) in a two-block mixed-amplitude sequence, does SUM(n_i / N_i) = 1 at failure as Palmgren-Miner linear damage summation predicts — each tested in a growing arm and in a wall-synthesis-inhibited arm?

Both versions recorded. Why V1 was reformulated, three well-posedness defects, none of them cosmetic:
1. **Loading sign unspecified.** V1 says "osmotic loading cycles" without sign. The bacterial lysis literature I opened loads the envelope to failure by hypo-osmotic DOWNshock (Reuter et al. 2014, P2; Hegde 2020, P4), whereas the intake's motivating source (Sun, Shi & Huang 2021) applies HYPERosmotic shock, which deflates turgor rather than raising wall tension. A question about tensile failure of the sacculus that does not say which way the water moves can be answered either way. V2 fixes the sign to downshock.
2. **"In the way an S-N relation describes" is unfalsifiable as written.** V2 names the functional form (log-log linear), the number of amplitudes, and the nested test (does n carry information after conditioning on max amplitude).
3. **V1 conjoins two sub-questions** (S-N form, Miner additivity) that can receive different answers; V2 separates them as (a)/(b)/(c) so each is independently settleable.

WHAT THE REPAIR ITSELF NOW PRESUPPOSES (tested as hard as the clause it replaced, per the rule that a reformulation carries its own presuppositions):
- **R1.** That a sub-lytic downshock band exists which is reproducibly non-lytic at cycle 1 yet lytic within the cycle budget. If single-shock lysis probability is steep in DPi, that band may be narrower than the deliverable amplitude resolution. Hegde 2020 prints only one point relevant here ("For a large 960 mOsmol hyposmotic shock, ~ 36 % of the hepta mutant cells survive the challenge") and that is a mutant at a large amplitude, so the WT sub-lytic band is NOT established by anything I opened. FLAGGED, not assumed.
- **R2.** That wall-synthesis inhibition can be imposed without itself lysing cells over the cycle budget. The obvious reagents (beta-lactams) are themselves lytic, so the "synthesis-inhibited arm" the candidate calls a required control is confounded by construction with the endpoint. This is a NEW problem introduced by the repair, not inherited from V1. FLAGGED as the second-strongest objection.
- **R3.** That a single identified cell can be held for the full cycle budget. See Gate 1 arithmetic below: at the recovery timescale printed in Hegde 2020 this fails at 10^3 cycles, which is why V2 says "the attainable cycle budget" rather than inheriting the intake's "10^2-10^3".

Alternative formulations:
- (lay) Do bacteria have a fatigue limit — an osmotic shock amplitude they survive indefinitely but not ten thousand times?
- (sharpest single limb) Is envelope failure a strength problem set by peak turgor, or a damage-accumulation problem set by cycle count?
- (method field's own words) For the component "the peptidoglycan sacculus under cyclic tensile loading", what is the stress-life relation, is there an endurance limit, and does linear damage summation hold — with the complication that the component is repaired in service.

Motivating gap: Envelope mechanics is framed as a strength / peak-stress problem, so the cycle-count axis has not been traversed in a bacterial cell wall. **This gap statement is NARROWED by Gate 2 and the intake's universal must not stand as written.** The cycle-count axis HAS been traversed in a single living cell (Qiang et al. 2019, human red blood cells, up to ~900 electrodeformation cycles, with a Wohler-like stress-life curve reported in SI), and cycle-count-dependent killing HAS been measured in a walled microbe (Palou et al. 1998, *Zygosaccharomyces bailii* under oscillatory hydrostatic pressure). What is unoccupied is the intersection: per-cell lysis hazard versus osmotic cycle number in a walled bacterium whose wall is being resynthesised during the test. READ LEVEL on the intake's motivating source (Sun, Shi & Huang, Front Microbiol 12:718600, 2021): summariser-mediated read of the Frontiers full-text page, abstract returned as ordered short excerpts, inherited from intake and NOT re-opened in this Gate 2 (fetch budget); it applies a single hyperosmotic shock, infers stiffness from population-averaged length change, and does not vary shock number.

Presuppositions:
- P1. Sub-lytic loading can be delivered repeatedly to the same identified cells at controlled amplitude and recovery interval. FLAGGED CONTESTED and partially FALSIFIED in arithmetic at 10^3 cycles (Gate 1).
- P2. Failure is an observable per-cell event distinguishable from growth arrest and from mechanosensitive-channel-mediated solute release. CONTESTED at intake ("bang or whimper"); now PARTLY ESTABLISHED by a source opened at abstract level: Reuter et al. 2014 observed individual *E. coli* under one downshock and report that "lysis occurs with a high probability, but the precise path differs between individual cells." So the event is per-cell observable, on a millisecond timescale, but heterogeneous in path — the endpoint must therefore be a pre-declared path-agnostic criterion, validated, and NOT "loss of granularity" alone, since that same abstract records granularity loss "but with the retention of DNA".
- P3. The envelope has a damage state that can change without being fully repaired on the experiment's timescale. CONTESTED and the crux. Nothing I opened establishes or refutes it for peptidoglycan. Fatigue competes with synthesis; the growing and synthesis-inhibited arms are required controls, and R2 above says the inhibited arm is itself hard to build.
- P4. Cells are mechanically comparable at cycle 1. Not assumed; handled by stratification on cell-cycle stage and length and by randomised amplitude order.
- P5. Mixed-amplitude sequences are deliverable, so Miner's rule is testable. The rule is a hypothesis under test; its failure is an informative answer.
- P6. The term mapping is a DECLARED CONSTRUCTION, not a discovered identity (stress amplitude -> osmotic step; cycles-to-failure -> shocks-to-lysis; endurance limit -> sub-threshold amplitude survivable without bound). FLAGGED as the place to attack first. Note one mapping defect found at Gate 2: hydrostatic pressure (the Palou neighbour's load) is isotropic and imposes no differential wall tension, so that prior's cycle effect cannot be read as wall fatigue, and the candidate's mapping must not borrow its support.
- P7. Lysis probability is the right failure metric; sub-lethal consequences (growth-rate loss, morphology, antibiotic susceptibility) are declared secondary endpoints, not substitutes. NOTE: the nearest method prior (Qiang 2019) measured exactly the sub-lethal axis and NOT failure, which is why it does not answer this question.
- **MEASURAND EXEMPTION DECLARED, not merely considered.** The question's object is a measurement programme whose central quantity (per-cell lysis hazard as a function of cycle index) is by construction unmeasured at the time of asking. An attack that the quantity is unknown, or that the damage state is not yet demonstrated, is an attack on the measurand and not a false premise; P3's contestedness is reported, not smuggled.

## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

What would count as an answer: Per-cell survival as a function of (amplitude, cycle index) at >= 4 downshock amplitudes spanning the single-shock lytic threshold, in growing and synthesis-inhibited arms: fitted stress-life curves with CIs; a nested test of whether cycle index carries information beyond the largest amplitude experienced; a test for an amplitude with no lysis inside the cycle budget; and a two-block mixed-amplitude test of SUM(n_i/N_i) = 1 with a stated alternative (e.g. sequence-dependent, load-order-sensitive damage). A clean negative — hazard depends only on peak amplitude, no cycle-index term in either arm — answers the question as "strength-governed, transplant fails". That is a result, not a null.

THE CYCLE-BUDGET ARITHMETIC, which is where V1's feasibility claim breaks (labelled as MY OWN arithmetic on one quoted figure):
Hegde 2020 (PhD thesis, Edinburgh, read at ABSTRACT LEVEL ONLY) prints, for WT *E. coli* under a single hyposmotic shock: "The fast volume expansion is on the order of 0.5-2s whereas the recovery phase lasts few minutes." Scope of that sentence: WT *E. coli*, single cell, single hyposmotic shock, volume as the observable; it ASSERTS the recovery timescale, it does not deny anything about repeated shocks. My arithmetic: if a cycle requires full recovery before the next load, a cycle costs minutes, so 10^3 cycles costs ~10^1 hours and 10^2 cycles costs several hours. At *E. coli* doubling times of 20-60 min that is many generations, so the "same identified cell" becomes a LINEAGE and the per-cell unit of analysis dissolves well before 10^3 cycles. The intake's "10^2-10^3 controlled cycles per identified cell is routine" is therefore NOT supported by anything I opened and is contradicted in arithmetic by the only recovery-timescale figure I have. Two escapes, both changing the question: cut the budget to ~10^1-10^2 cycles per cell (which weakens any endurance-limit claim and may put R1's band out of reach), or shorten the interval below full recovery (which changes the load case from "repeated discrete cycles" to "sustained partial loading" and makes the cycle count ambiguous). V2 therefore says "the attainable cycle budget" and leaves the budget to be established, rather than asserting 10^3.

It is a question, not a thesis: the Miner limb and the endurance-limit limb both have pre-stated alternatives, and the negative ("strength-governed") is written as an answer rather than a failure.

**Well-posedness score: 2/3.** Full marks are blocked not by vagueness — V2 names form, amplitudes, arms, nested test and estimand — but by the unresolved unit-of-analysis problem at high cycle counts (R3 / the arithmetic above) and by R2, the synthesis-inhibited arm being confounded with the endpoint by the pharmacology of the obvious reagents.

Not High: two neighbours are genuinely close and both were opened — P1 supplies the fatigue method inside a single living cell including a Wohler-like stress-life construction (in SI, unopened), and P5 supplies a published cycle-count-dependent killing result in a walled microbe — so the candidate is an intersection of two occupied positions rather than virgin ground, and the intake's motivating universal was overstated. Not Low: no opened prior varies osmotic cycle number in a bacterium, none prints the candidate quantity (per-cell lysis probability versus cycle index), none tests Miner's rule or an endurance limit in any cell, and the repair-competition crux (P3 of the presuppositions) is absent from every source opened — P1's object cannot repair at all and P5's unit cannot see repair. The grade would move to Low if item 1 (the *P. aeruginosa* nanoindentation paper) turns out to repeat loads on identified cells and report a loading-history effect on failure.

## Gate 3 — Significance

**Consequence (argued).** If YES — cycle index carries information after conditioning on peak amplitude — then envelope failure is a damage-accumulation problem and three things change. First, the mechanical model of the sacculus acquires a state variable it does not currently have: current envelope mechanics treats the wall as an elastic/viscoelastic shell with a strength, and a measured stress-life relation would require a damage term and would bound how fast repair can erase it. Second, "sub-lethal" becomes a conditional category: an amplitude certified harmless from single-shock data would be lethal in repetition, which directly touches any process that cycles osmolarity — osmotic-shock lysis in bioprocess product release, hurdle processing in food, and the osmotic excursions bacteria actually meet in soil wet-dry cycling and in host niches. Third, it supplies the mechanism the oscillatory-processing literature lacks: P5 has had the empirical result that counts fall with cycle number at equal holding time since 1998 and offers no fatigue account of it, so a per-cell stress-life relation would convert an empirical processing trick into a model with a dial. If NO — survival depends only on peak amplitude in both arms — the result is also consequential and is not a null: it would establish that active resynthesis resets the damage state faster than it accumulates, which is a quantitative statement about repair capacity that nobody has made, and it would mark the boundary of a transplant that has already succeeded in a non-repairing cell (P1). The asymmetry matters: in materials with no repair, fatigue is universal; a clean negative in a repairing structure would be a genuine mechanobiological finding about repair, not an absence of signal.

**Audience (specific, with the action).** (i) Bacterial cell biophysics — the groups doing single-cell osmotic shock in microfluidics (Pilizota at Edinburgh, per P4; Phillips at Caltech, per P3; Huang, per the intake's Sun et al.) already own the instrument and the single-shock protocols; the action is adding a cycle-index arm to assays they run already, which is why the question is cheap for exactly the people best placed to answer it. (ii) Food and bioprocess engineers in the oscillatory/hurdle-processing line (P5's community) — the action is choosing cycle count against amplitude on a mechanistic basis instead of empirically, and the existence of a published cycle-count effect in their own literature is evidence this community is real and already acts on the variable. (iii) Cell-wall-active antibiotic pharmacodynamics — if lysis is history-dependent, dose-schedule modelling acquires a cycle-count axis, and the candidate's synthesis-inhibited arm sits directly on the beta-lactam mechanism. (iv) Cell-mechanics methodologists who have built the RBC fatigue platform (P1) — the action is porting a validated fatigue protocol to a repairing cell, which is their stated methodological frontier.

**Fecundity.** Does repair rate SET the endurance limit, making the limit a physiological rather than a material parameter? Is damage localised at the division site, which would couple fatigue to the cell cycle and to the constriction-rate effect the intake's Sun et al. reports? Does cycling sensitise cells to beta-lactams (a fatigue-antibiotic interaction, directly testable)? Do mechanosensitive channels function as fatigue mitigation rather than only as emergency valves, which reframes P2's gating question? Is there a load-order effect (high-then-low vs low-then-high), which is where Miner's rule fails in metals and would be the first mechanistic discriminator? Does the stress-life exponent differ between Gram-positive and Gram-negative envelopes, giving a mechanical reading of wall architecture?

**Anchoring.** It attaches to three live items, each sourced to something opened: the bang-or-whimper failure-mode question (P2, where path heterogeneity under ONE shock is already the reported finding and would have to be classified before a cycle count means anything); the rate-dependence result (P3, which already shows single-shock outcome is not a pure amplitude threshold and is the strongest existing reason to expect a loading-history axis); and the unexplained cycle-count effect in oscillatory microbial processing (P5, an empirical regularity 28 years old with no mechanistic account).

**Significance score: 2/3.** Held below 3 because the strongest consequence branch (YES) is conditional on an effect that the Gate 1 arithmetic says may only be reachable at ~10^1-10^2 cycles, where an endurance-limit claim is weak; and because the antibiotic-pharmacodynamics audience is argued from mechanism rather than from any source opened here.

## Reason for silence

A disciplinary boundary with an instrument gap on each side, not an oversight. Fatigue is owned by mechanical and reliability engineering, and its only successful crossing into single cells came through blood rheology (P1, 2019), where the clinical motivation — circulatory support devices, repeated capillary transit, sickle disease — handed the investigators a cycle count as the natural exposure variable. Bacterial envelope mechanics acquired its questions from growth, shape determination and antibiotic action, where the loading of interest is quasi-static turgor; its canonical osmotic experiment is a single shock delivered to a population, and its single-cell refinements have gone into magnitude (P4), rate (P3) and failure path (P2) because those are the variables the MS-channel question needs. Nobody in that field had a reason to count cycles. Meanwhile the one community that DID count cycles and found they matter (P5, food-process engineering, 1998) works in CFU/ml under hydrostatic pressure, has no per-cell failure endpoint and no mechanical model of the wall, and publishes in journals the biophysics literature does not cite. So the two halves of the question — the cycle-count axis and the per-cell lytic endpoint — have existed for decades in fields with no citation traffic between them, and the bridging instrument (microfluidic media exchange with per-cell imaging, which makes repeated amplitude-specified loading of an identified cell deliverable at all) is recent. Additionally, the crux presupposition supplies its own suppressor: a biologist's prior is that peptidoglycan is continuously resynthesised, so "fatigue" sounds category-mistaken for a self-repairing structure, which is a reason not to ask rather than a reason to test — and whether it is correct is precisely limb (c) of the question.

Scores: **Well-posedness 2/3 · Significance 2/3**
