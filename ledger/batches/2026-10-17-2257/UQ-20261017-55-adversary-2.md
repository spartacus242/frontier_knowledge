## Restatement in my own words

Strip the apparatus and the question is: *cell culture runs almost everything at 37 °C, but plenty of tissues do not sit at 37 °C in the body — so how much of the well-known gap between a cultured cell and the same cell in the animal is just the wrong thermostat setting, and is that share big or small next to the gap caused by the other famous wrong setting, atmospheric oxygen?*

The operational version (V2) pins that down as an experiment-shaped quantity: pick at least three cell types in advance whose home tissue is clearly not 37 °C, with at least one cooler (e.g. testis ~34 °C, skin ~33 °C) and one warmer; run a 2×2 of tissue-matched-vs-37 °C temperature against physioxic-vs-atmospheric oxygen; keep proliferation rate matched so you are not just measuring "cells grew slower"; declare in advance a distance metric to a freshly isolated in vivo profile; then report, per cell type with confidence intervals, what fraction of the baseline in-vitro-to-in-vivo distance each correction closes, and whether the two corrections interact.

So the candidate is really two claims bundled: (a) a **silence claim** — the field never asks whether the temperature setpoint is itself mis-specified, the way it does ask about oxygen; and (b) a **quantification claim** — nobody has ever put temperature and oxygen on one attribution scale against an in vivo reference. My attacks went after (a), because that is where the record's Medium grade is anchored and where it admits two vocabularies were swept only at title level.
