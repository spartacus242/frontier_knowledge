## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Every drug package now carries a serial number and a logged custody trail. The candidate asks three things of that trail, in order:
1. When the patient actually receives a package, how old is it (time since manufacture), how much of its labelled life is left, how many custody hops did it make and how long did it sit at each — and are those quantities spread out *within* the same product-and-manufacturer, or only *between* products? A pre-declared within-product ICC threshold is the gate on the next two limbs.
2. Where that within-product spread exists: if you retrieve dispensed or returned units and assay them (potency, dissolution, monograph limits), does potency slope with remaining shelf life and cumulative dwell time *within a batch* (batch as random effect), i.e. net of between-batch manufacturing quality?
3. For some product with a routinely measured potency-sensitive endpoint, does that provenance vector track the endpoint in a cohort?

In short: is "within expiry" a lossy summary of a per-package quantity that serialization has just made measurable — and first, is that quantity even variable enough within product and batch to be an exposure at all?
