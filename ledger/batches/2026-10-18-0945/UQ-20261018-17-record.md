## Question (canonical)

**V1 (as intake, scored):** Across the published in vitro reconstitution literature for a declared process family, what is the distribution of the ratio between each component's concentration in the reconstitution and its measured concentration in the cell where the process is claimed to occur, and in what fraction of reconstitutions does the demonstrated sufficiency fail when every component is held at its cellular concentration?

**V2 (the one reformulation permitted at Gate 1, for well-posedness only; recorded, not scored as a separate candidate):** For ONE named process family declared before extraction (frame = papers indexed by a pre-existing reconstitution registry for that family, date window and inclusion rule fixed in advance), and with the comparator declared as a ladder — (a) cell-average concentration from one named absolute-proteomics source, (b) compartment-average under a declared volume model, (c) a bounded local-enrichment allowance of ≤10^2 — what is the distribution of log10(in-vitro concentration / matched cellular concentration) per (paper, component), with per-pair uncertainty propagated from both sides; what fraction of papers have a maximum component excess above 10^0.5, 10^1 and 10^2 on each rung of the ladder; and, for a pre-specified subset re-run at the bench with every component on rung (a), does the paper's own primary readout survive against a pre-declared detection limit, with buffer, crowding and temperature as declared arms rather than as free variables?

Alternative formulations (from intake, retained): Does the reconstitution literature show that purified components are sufficient for a cellular process, or only that they are sufficient at concentrations the cell never reaches? / For each published reconstitution, how far above cellular concentration was each component pushed to make the process run, and does that fold-excess predict whether additional cellular factors were later found to be required?

## Motivating gap (as intake, with the Gate 2 correction)

Intake argued that a reconstitution's sufficiency claim rests on an untested presupposition about concentration; that absolute-proteomics copy numbers (PaxDb) now make the comparison checkable at literature scale; and — the load-bearing claim — that the worry is "argued qualitatively inside one subfield (biomolecular condensates) and nowhere quantified as a distribution over a frame: no fold-excess distribution, and no relation between fold-excess and later-discovered missing factors."

**That central negative is false as stated.** The fold-excess distribution exists, published in 2021, over a declared frame, with PaxDb as the comparator, in the candidate's own declared secondary subfield (Prior 1 below). Intake's READ LEVEL note said nothing had been read beyond titles and that the condensate-subfield priors were the first thing Gate 2 must open; opening them is what killed the candidate.

## Presuppositions (intake list, with Gate 2 status)

1. Reconstitution papers report component concentrations in units recoverable by extraction. **CONTESTED, and now evidenced as biased:** Prior 1 could form the ratio for only 75 of 89 frame proteins, and 30 of those 75 had no usable saturation concentration at all — i.e. the extractable subset is roughly half the frame even in the one family that has a curated concentration registry (LLPSDB/PhaSePro/DrLLPS/PhaSepDB). For families with no such registry the extraction is per-paper and the selection bias is uncharacterised.
2. A measured cellular concentration exists for each component in a relevant cell type. CONTESTED (absolute proteomics covers a minority of proteins well; copy-number→concentration needs an uncertain volume).
3. The cell-average total concentration is the right comparator. CONTESTED, and against the candidate's interest. Prior 1 reached exactly this objection and resolved it *against* the critique, stating that PaxDb values "should … be considered as theoretical lower limits" because the operative concentration is local. V2's declared ladder is the minimum repair; it does not dissolve the objection.
4. "Sufficiency fails at cellular concentration" is experimentally decidable with a declared detection limit. Must be designed in; Prior 2 shows the positive direction is decidable in practice, and shows the trap — Prior 2 *chose* its conditions by comparing against the in-cell target, so the agreement is partly post hoc and no held-out test is reported.
5. The in vitro process is the same process as the cellular one. Contested case by case; needs a declared adjudication rule, not the original paper's claim. Prior 2 is the live example: its endpoint is partition coefficient and FRAP dynamics, not the cellular function (Dcp1 partitioning misses, and the decapping activity that is Dcp1's in-cell job is absent from the reconstitution).
6. Later discovery of an additional required factor is recordable without hindsight bias. Pre-declared rule and direction needed; nothing found in the literature that attempts this coding.
7. Buffer, crowding and temperature are secondary to concentration. CONTESTED and, on the read evidence, probably false in the regime that matters: Prior 2's best match to cellular P bodies required pH 5.8, 30 °C, 300 mM KOAc and ATP, so these are co-determining rather than secondary.

## What would count as an answer

As intake: a pre-declared frame; per (paper, component) in-vitro concentration paired with a matched cellular concentration carrying its own uncertainty; a reported log-fold-excess distribution and the fraction of reconstitutions above pre-declared thresholds; for a pre-specified subset, a bench re-run with all components at cellular concentration against a declared detection limit with buffer/crowding as declared arms; and a pre-registered test of association between maximum fold-excess and subsequent identification of additional required factors. Intake correctly noted that "fold-excesses mostly within an order of magnitude, sufficiency surviving the drop" is an equally decisive answer. **Gate 2 finding: for the condensate family that answer is already partly on the record and it is mixed** — 33.3% of proteins phase separate in vitro inside their cellular range, 22.7% within one order of magnitude above it, 44% more than an order of magnitude above (Prior 1); and one eight-component reconstitution run with every protein at its estimated cellular concentration reproduced in-cell partitioning and dynamics to within ~2-fold (Prior 2).

## Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, all opened and read)

**Prior 1 — Farahi N, Lazar T, Wodak SJ, Tompa P, Pancsa R. "Integration of Data from Liquid–Liquid Phase Separation Databases Highlights Concentration and Dosage Sensitivity of LLPS Drivers." Int J Mol Sci 2021;22(6):3017.** https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/22/6/3017 (doi 10.3390/ijms22063017; preprint bioRxiv 2021.02.19.430946). READ.
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS: one human LLPS driver protein (89 high-confidence drivers integrated from PhaSePro, DrLLPS, LLPSDB, PhaSepDB; 78 with in vitro data; 75 with PaxDb values).
- ENDPOINT: lowest in vitro concentration at which LLPS was observed (saturation concentration where available), in µM.
- REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: the same protein's **highest PaxDb-derived cellular concentration** — the candidate's own primary comparator, in the candidate's own units.
- ESTIMAND: descriptive — how often and by how much the in vitro concentration exceeds the cellular one, reported as a three-bin fold-excess breakdown: **25/75 (33.3%) phase separate in vitro within the cellular range; 17 (22.7%) above by less than an order of magnitude; 33 (44%) above by more than an order of magnitude**; 30 proteins unanalysable for want of a Csat. Summary statement: in vitro concentrations are "considerably higher, often by one or two orders of magnitude, than cellular concentrations", with the authors' own reading that PaxDb values "should, therefore, be considered as theoretical lower limits."

**Prior 2 — "Quantitative reconstitution of yeast RNA processing bodies" (Rosen lab; PNAS 2023; read as bioRxiv 2022.08.13.503854).** https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.08.13.503854.full.pdf READ (this is the intake's COULD NOT READ title; it is now read).
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS: individual in vitro condensates and micrographs (concentrations measured in condensates >2.5 µm; six micrographs over two replicates) in an eight-component reconstitution.
- ENDPOINT: partition coefficient (condensate/bulk), FRAP fractional recovery and exchange rate, fractional overlap between components.
- REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: previously published **in vivo** partition coefficients and dynamics for yeast P bodies (the lab's own quantitative in-cell inventory).
- ESTIMAND: agreement between in vitro and in vivo partitioning/dynamics, judged against a ~2-fold threshold, **with every protein set to its estimated cellular concentration**. Verbatim: the best match came from "Using the estimated cellular protein concentrations (Fig. S5), at pH 5.8, and incubating at 30 °C"; setting all components equal at 150 nM "resulted in greater deviation from cellular values"; the single-protein assays, by contrast, were "roughly two- to tenfold higher than estimated cellular concentrations of these molecules." On missing factors: additional lower-expressed components, PTMs and chemical or mechanical energy "are not necessary" for a native-like compartment. Only Dcp1's partitioning missed.

**Prior 3 — "Cytosolic concentrations of actin binding proteins and the implications for in vivo F-actin turnover." J Cell Biol 2023;222(12):e202306036.** https://rupress.org/jcb/article-pdf/222/12/e202306036/1919319/jcb_202306036.pdf READ.
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS: individual *S. cerevisiae* cells (n = 92–137 per protein, three experiments), aggregated to a per-protein population mean; actin plus 14 ABPs.
- ENDPOINT: cytosolic (free) concentration in µM (0.6–11 µM) and cytosolic fraction (0.6–0.9).
- REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: internal cross-method (Western vs GFP intensity vs MS), *S. pombe* values from Sirotkin et al. 2010, and cell-cycle stage. **Not** in vitro reconstitution concentrations.
- ESTIMAND: population-mean cytosolic concentration and cytosolic fraction per protein, plus the ABP-to-F-actin-binding-site ratio (summed F-actin-binding ABPs 42.5 µM vs 8.8 µM F-actin, ~5-fold). Runs no reconstitution and reports no in vitro concentration comparison.

## Novelty — how this differs from each

- **vs Prior 1:** this is the near-duplicate, and it duplicates the limb the candidate leads with. Same comparator (PaxDb cell-average), same ratio, same "fraction above a threshold" shape, on a declared frame, in one of the candidate's own declared domains. The candidate's residual over Prior 1 is real but thin: a log-ratio distribution with uncertainty propagated from both sides instead of three bins; an extraction from reconstitution methods sections rather than from LLPS-database curation; process families beyond condensates; "sufficiency for a cellular process" rather than "LLPS observed" as the endpoint; and the bench and association limbs. None of that rescues the motivating gap, which asserted the distribution did not exist.
- **vs Prior 2:** Prior 2 executes the candidate's bench limb for n = 1 system and gets the answer intake called "equally decisive" — sufficiency survived with all components at cellular concentration. It is not the candidate: no literature frame, no declared detection limit for a negative, conditions tuned post hoc against the in-cell target, and buffer/pH/temperature/ATP treated as free variables rather than declared arms. But it removes the candidate's claim to be opening an untried question, and it supplies a counterexample to the inverted presupposition in the very family where the critique is loudest.
- **vs Prior 3:** genuinely different object — it measures the comparator, not the ratio, and it never looks at reconstitution concentrations. This is where the candidate's negative survives: outside the condensate family nobody has formed the ratio, because no registry of reconstitution concentrations exists for those families. A real but narrow opening, and the method that would own it already exists under another name (regulatory toxicology's in-vitro-to-in-vivo concentration-ratio analysis, query 7).

## Significance — consequence

If fold-excesses are large and sufficiency fails at cellular concentration: a large tranche of "purified components are sufficient" claims is downgraded to "sufficient under supraphysiological driving", and whole-cell and systems models that inherit in vitro parameters inherit a bias of known sign. If they are small, or sufficiency survives: the criticism retires as a general claim and localises to the condensate field. Prior 1 and Prior 2 together already deliver a mixed verdict for condensates — 44% of drivers more than an order of magnitude above cell-average, yet an eight-component reconstitution matching in-cell partitioning at cellular concentrations — which is itself the informative outcome, and it blunts the candidate's consequence by showing the two limbs can both be answered without the general claim moving.

## Significance — audience

(i) Reconstitution biochemists and the journals that review them — a reporting norm (state each component's concentration beside a cited cellular value) is cheap and actionable, and Prior 1's existence shows the ask is already live in one field; (ii) whole-cell/kinetic modellers, who consume in vitro parameters and currently have no provenance flag for concentration regime; (iii) the condensate community, where the critique was raised and where Prior 1's "PaxDb is a lower limit" rebuttal is the standing answer; (iv) regulatory-toxicology methodologists, who own the ratio method and would recognise the transplant. Audience (i) is the one with an evidenced unmet ask; audience (iii) has already absorbed the finding and moved on, which is evidence against inflation of this candidate specifically.

## Significance — fecundity

Whether local enrichment (condensate partitioning, scaffold recruitment, membrane binding) quantitatively closes the measured fold-excess rather than being invoked to; whether a declared detection-limit protocol can distinguish "fails at cellular concentration" from "below the assay's sensitivity" (the design hole Prior 2 leaves open); whether buffer/crowding/temperature or concentration is the larger lever when a reconstitution fails (Prior 2 says the former is not secondary); whether fold-excess predicts later discovery of required factors (untouched); and whether the toxicology ratio machinery transfers to endogenous components.

## Significance — anchoring

The macromolecular-crowding critique of dilute-solution biochemistry (Ellis, NOT OPENED here); the condensate field's physiological-concentration criterion (Alberti/Gladfelter/Mittag-type considerations papers, surfaced at query 11); parameter provenance in whole-cell models (adjacent to ledger entries UQ-20261017-54 and UQ-20261017-15, both on in-cell versus in vitro discrepancy, neither duplicating this); quantitative in vitro–in vivo extrapolation in toxicology.

## Reason for silence

For limb 1 there is **no silence to explain** — the question was asked and answered in 2021 where the data infrastructure existed. That is itself the mechanism for the remaining silence, and it is a data-infrastructure asymmetry rather than a conceptual one: the condensate field built curated registries of in vitro phase-separation concentrations (LLPSDB, PhaSePro, DrLLPS, PhaSepDB), so forming the ratio against PaxDb cost one analyst a few months; no comparable registry exists for cytoskeletal, membrane-traffic, replication or translation reconstitutions, so the same analysis there is a per-paper extraction from methods sections whose reporting is incomplete (Prior 1 lost ~half its own frame even with a registry). Added to that is an incentive gap: a reconstitution paper's deliverable is the demonstration, and no journal requires a cellular-concentration comparison, so the numbers are reported in whatever units the assay used. Both mechanisms are credible, and both point at a narrower candidate than the one filed — which is the Gate 2 outcome, not a rescue of it.
