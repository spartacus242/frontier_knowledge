## Question (canonical)

**V1 (Gate 1 reformulation, the assessed version).** For a declared frame of published training runs and the K training stacks named in them at the named versions, define a run's **realized per-token weight** w_i as the coefficient multiplying token i's next-token NLL inside the single scalar whose gradient the optimizer consumes at one optimizer step, after padding, truncation remainders, sequence packing, prompt/label masking, micro-batch loss reduction, gradient accumulation and cross-rank averaging, normalized so that Σ_i w_i = 1 per step and per run; and take as the **stated objective** the declared reference of uniform weight over gradient-bearing tokens together with the run's configured per-source mixture weights. Then:

(a) **Code-level audit.** For each stack at each named version, what is the implied w_i as a closed-form function of sequence length, masked-token count, micro-batch composition, accumulation-window size and world size?
(b) **Realized distribution.** For runs whose config and data order are public (or for an instrumented replication under shipped semantics, with the frame declared), what is the realized weight distribution, reported as (i) the zero-weight share — forward-pass token slots with w_i = 0, i.e. padded, truncated-away and masked tokens — against the run's reported token count; (ii) the effective-to-nominal token ratio, Kish effective sample size (Σw)²/Σw² as a fraction of the gradient-bearing token count; and (iii) the induced per-source mixture shift, total-variation distance between realized source weights Σ_{i∈s} w_i and the configured mixture weights?
(c) **Commensurability.** In a controlled pair of runs differing only in loss-reduction semantics at matched FLOPs, is the held-out loss and benchmark gap inside or outside a band pre-registered from (1) the published effect sizes of recipe and data-mixture changes and (2) a same-configuration seed-variance estimate?
Limbs (a)–(c) are reported separately for the packing-dominated pretraining regime and the post-training (SFT/RL) regime, because the length-heterogeneity presupposition holds in one and is contested in the other. For runs whose releases state only a token count and a mixture, the finding is recorded as a measured **unspecifiability share** — the fraction of the frame for which no gap is definable — and that outcome is an answer, not a failure of the frame.

**V0 (intake, superseded).** "Across published training runs and the frameworks that produced them, what is the realized per-token weight distribution of the objective that was actually optimized — after padding, truncation remainders, sequence packing, prompt-token loss masking and micro-batch loss normalization — and does its gap from the stated objective exceed the effect sizes that recipe and data-mixture papers claim?"

Why V0 failed well-posedness: "the objective that was actually optimized" names no estimand; "gap from the stated objective" has no metric and no declared reference (the stated objective is usually written in notation that elides the denominator); "exceeds the effect sizes that recipe papers claim" has no band; and the pretraining/post-training split was asserted inside the frame rather than reported out of it. One reformulation used, as permitted.

## Alternative formulations

1. What fraction of a run's reported tokens carried gradient signal, and with what weight — is "tokens seen" a comparable unit across training frameworks at all?
2. If two labs report the same mixture weights and the same token count, were they optimizing the same objective?
3. (neighbour vocabulary) What are the design weights of a training run, and what is its design effect? A per-sequence-mean loss over a length-heterogeneous batch is an unequal-probability reweighting of the corpus; what is the induced shift relative to the sampling design the run claims?
4. (lay) When a paper says "we trained on 15T tokens of this mixture," which tokens actually counted, and how much did each one count for?

## Motivating gap

A region dense with how-answers and empty of why-questions. Implementation guidance on padding, truncation, packing, loss masking, gradient accumulation and loss reduction is everywhere, and at least one vendor documentation page exists specifically to make the user choose a normalization — Cerebras, "Training with number of tokens loss scaling" (multiple versions, training-api.cerebras.ai and docs.cerebras.net, confirmed live in query 10, 2026-10-17).

**The anchor's primary source is now located**, clearing the intake flag "PRIMARY SOURCE NOT LOCATED." The non-equivalence of gradient accumulation to large-batch training under per-micro-batch loss normalization with unequal token counts is a documented, repeatedly re-opened defect family in the most-used fine-tuning stacks, found by GitHub issue search (free route, 3 queries):
- huggingface/transformers#14638 (2021-12-06) "Gradient accumulation causing different training curves" — the family is five years old.
- huggingface/transformers#37474 (2025-04-13) "Trainer.training_step incorrectly normalizes mean token loss when n_gpu > 1"; #41842 (2025-10-24) "Incorrect usage of `num_items_in_batch`?"; #43604 (2026-01-29) "Revisit the condition for scaling the loss"; #46731 (2026-06-17) LabelSmoother ignores `num_items_in_batch`, "inflating gradients by ~gradient_accumulation_steps"; #47688 (2026-07-31) "Heads that declare **kwargs but drop num_items_in_batch train with an inflated loss under gradient accumulation" — this last one quotes the live code showing the normalization decision is taken by an `inspect.signature` VAR_KEYWORD check, so which objective a run optimizes depends on a model class's signature.
- huggingface/trl#2604 (2025-01-22) loss scales with accumulation steps in SFTTrainer; #5619 (2026-04-22) DAPO loss not normalized by accumulation steps; #7047 (2026-09-04) uneven accumulation windows weighted by size; #7569 (2026-10-08) "MoE aux loss is scaled by num_processes × gradient_accumulation_steps," measured at ×2.19 and ×4.88.
- huggingface/trl#7206 (2026-09-14, open) states the candidate's mechanism verbatim: "independently normalizing each microbatch makes effective token weighting depend on packing," with a worked two-micro-batch example (100 tokens at mean loss 2.0, 900 at 0.5) giving 1.25 under micro-batch averaging versus 0.65 under a token-mean denominator over the whole window.

Direct source evidence that the per-micro-batch denominator is the shipped default in a pretraining stack, not only a fine-tuning bug: GitHub code search returned `megatron/core/pipeline_parallel/schedules.py` with `loss = torch.sum(losses.view(-1) * loss_mask) / loss_mask.sum()` — a token-mean whose denominator is that micro-batch's own unmasked token count, so the realized weight of a token depends on which micro-batch it landed in.

What is absent, in everything opened: no published run reports its realized per-token weight distribution, its zero-weight share against its reported token count, or its effective-to-nominal token ratio; no scaling-law, recipe or data-mixture paper carries loss-reduction semantics as a covariate; and the governance literature that defines what training compute means never reaches tokens at all — the Frontier Model Forum's "Issue Brief: Measuring Training Compute" (opened, query 11) enumerates precision, recomputation, discarded branches, model boundaries and a <5% approximation tolerance, and **never mentions tokens**.

## Presuppositions

1. **Framework defaults for loss reduction differ across the stacks used by published runs.** PARTLY ESTABLISHED IN SOURCE, previously CONTESTED. Megatron-LM's pipeline schedule computes a per-micro-batch token-mean (code quoted above); HF Trainer's choice is decided by a signature check with a fallback to `loss / gradient_accumulation_steps` (quoted in transformers#47688); TRL normalizes per micro-batch on at least one path (trl#7206). torchtitan, Levanter and Axolotl were NOT verified in source (one GitHub code-search probe for torchtitan returned no match), so "differ across K stacks" is established for three stacks and unverified for the rest.
2. **The realized weight distribution is reconstructable from a run's released config plus data.** Established where pipeline and data order are public; otherwise only under replication, which bounds the frame and is declared in V1.
3. **Sequence lengths are heterogeneous enough for the reweighting to be non-negligible.** Established for SFT/RL and long-document regimes; CONTESTED for fully packed pretraining, where packing makes lengths near-uniform. V1 handles this by reporting the two regimes separately rather than claiming pretraining.
4. **Published effect sizes in recipe and mixture papers are small enough to be commensurable with this perturbation.** CONTESTED; measurable from the papers themselves before any experiment, and limb (c) pre-registers the band from them.
5. **FLOPs and gradient-bearing tokens diverge because padded and masked tokens still consume compute.** Established arithmetic, and the share is already published for some corpora: Krell et al., arXiv:2107.02027 (opened) reports "up to 50% of all tokens can be padding" for common NLP datasets and "up to 89%" for GLUE-cola at sequence length 128, with a 2× speedup from packing. Material to compute-threshold accounting only if padding shares are large in the audited frame, which limb (b)(i) measures rather than assumes.
6. **The stated objective in papers is specific enough for a gap to be defined.** CONTESTED, and V1 converts it into the reported unspecifiability share rather than assuming it away.
7. **(added at Gate 1) A single scalar per optimizer step exists whose gradient the optimizer consumes, so w_i is well defined.** Established for the stacks examined; it fails for objectives with non-linear per-sequence terms (e.g. a sequence-level mean inside a clip, or a group-normalized RL advantage), where w_i is state-dependent rather than fixed — so limb (a) must report the RL regime's weights as functions of the sampled group, not constants. This is a real limitation on the RL arm.

## What would count as an answer

(i) A code-level audit of the loss-reduction semantics of the K most-used training stacks at the versions named in published runs, each expressed as the implied per-token weight as a function of sequence length and micro-batch composition. (ii) For runs whose data and pipeline are public, the realized weight distribution computed and reported as the zero-weight share, the effective-to-nominal token ratio (Kish ESS) and the induced per-source mixture shift (TV distance). (iii) A controlled pair of runs differing only in loss-reduction semantics at matched FLOPs, with the held-out loss and benchmark gap placed against the pre-registered band from published recipe effect sizes and same-config seed variance. The question is answered by those three together. YES: ESS ratio well below 1, mixture TV shift comparable to the deltas mixture papers optimize over, and a loss/benchmark gap outside the band. NO (a null): induced mixture shift and loss gap both inside seed noise, which retires the concern and establishes "tokens seen" as comparable across stacks. A high unspecifiability share answers the accounting limb directly.

## Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, with sources)

**P1. huggingface/trl issue #7206, "AsyncGRPOTrainer: loss normalization across gradient accumulation and complete-rollout admission," opened 2026-09-14, open** — https://github.com/huggingface/trl/issues/7206 (body read verbatim).
- Unit of analysis: one trainer's `compute_loss` code path in one library at one commit.
- Endpoint: whether the per-micro-batch normalizer makes effective token weighting depend on packing; a correctness property, with a two-row synthetic example (micro-batch means 2.0 at 100 tokens and 0.5 at 900 tokens → 1.25 vs 0.65).
- Reference/comparator: the token-mean objective whose "denominator should cover the entire accumulation window and all ranks."
- Estimand: an invariance property — "repacking identical supervised tokens and causal contexts should preserve their loss weighting."

**P2. "Balanced Aggregation: Understanding and Fixing Aggregation Bias in GRPO," arXiv:2605.04077v1** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04077 (opened).
- Unit: the aggregation rule applied to token-level policy-gradient terms within each sampled group.
- Endpoint: training stability and final performance on six reasoning and coding benchmarks (Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Qwen3-1.7B; DAPO-17k, Polaris).
- Comparator: standard GRPO sequence aggregation and token aggregation.
- Estimand: not formally stated; implicitly the performance/stability difference between Balanced Aggregation and the two standard rules, with the relative ranking of those rules claimed to depend on response-length variation and the positive–negative length gap.

**P3. "Instruction Fine-Tuning: Does Prompt Loss Matter?", arXiv:2401.13586v3** — https://arxiv.org/html/2401.13586v3 (opened).
- Unit: fine-tuned models from a factorial design of 60 runs (10 prompt-loss-weight levels × 2 base models × 3 datasets), scored on 13 benchmarks.
- Endpoint: a min-max-scaled "relative aggregate" of benchmark scores; secondary mechanism measures are training-loss RSD, weight distance from the base model, training-data BLEU and generation length.
- Comparator: PLW = 0 (the original Alpaca prompt masking) and PLW = 1.0 (unmasked).
- Estimand: the shape of the PLW–performance curve, headline a negative quadratic in w_p = PLW^0.30103 with a predicted critical value λ = 0.242 for AlpacaDataShort.

Recorded but not graded as one of the three: Krell et al., "Efficient Sequence Packing without Cross-contamination," arXiv:2107.02027 (opened) — publishes the padding share for reference corpora, treats packing as objective-preserving ("mathematically equivalent to the original models") and never asks about per-token weighting; Frontier Model Forum, "Issue Brief: Measuring Training Compute" (opened) — compute accounting without tokens; Kahan et al. on estimands in cluster trials (query 9) — the method-owning field's apparatus, never transplanted; Cerebras "Training with number of tokens loss scaling" — the how-answer that motivated the silence mapping.

## Novelty — how this differs from each

**vs P1 (trl#7206).** Closest of the three, and it states the candidate's mechanism in the candidate's own field in almost the candidate's own words — so the mechanism is not un-noticed. It differs on all four attributes: its unit is one code path at one commit, the candidate's is (published run × stack version); its endpoint is an invariance property demonstrated on a synthetic two-micro-batch example, the candidate's is the realized weight distribution over a real run's corpus and its functionals (zero-weight share, Kish ESS ratio, mixture TV shift); its comparator is the within-library correct denominator, the candidate's is the published run's own stated objective and configured mixture; its estimand is correctness of an implementation, the candidate's is the size of the gap between a paper's claim and its run, and whether that gap is commensurable with the recipe literature's effect sizes. P1 is the existence proof that the perturbation is real; it is not a measurement of it on any run, and it makes no contact with mixture weights, token-count reporting or cross-stack comparability.

**vs P2 (Balanced Aggregation).** This paper does something close to limb (c), restricted to RL: it compares aggregation rules under controlled training and ties the ranking to response-length variation. The candidate must concede that for the RL post-training arm, "does the aggregation rule change outcomes?" is asked and answered in-field. It differs on unit (a group-level rule in one algorithm, not a stack's realized per-token weights), endpoint (benchmark scores, not a weight distribution or a mixture shift), comparator (two GRPO variants, not a run's stated objective), and estimand (which rule wins, not how far the realized objective sits from the claimed one). It performs no cross-framework audit at named versions, reports no effective-to-nominal token ratio, and makes no comparison to recipe or data-mixture effect sizes — so limbs (a) and (b) are untouched, and (c) is occupied only in the RL regime and only against a within-RL comparator.

**vs P3 (prompt loss weight).** Studies exactly one of the candidate's weighting mechanisms (prompt vs completion masking) as a tunable hyperparameter, and explicitly describes the weighting it studies as binary at the token level ("weights for next-token prediction loss is therefore binary"), which is the candidate's w_i collapsed to {0,1} on one axis. It differs on unit (fine-tuning runs, not run × stack), endpoint (benchmark aggregate), comparator (PLW 0 vs 1), and estimand (the shape of a performance curve in one hyperparameter). It discusses none of padding, truncation, packing, micro-batch normalization, gradient accumulation or cross-rank averaging; it organizes its length analysis around the mean completion-to-prompt ratio and leaves other length effects open ("The absolute length and size of the dataset will likely play a role in learning dynamics"); and it never touches data-mixture weights or compares its effect size to recipe-paper effects.

**Negative, stated in the method-owning field's words rather than ML's.** The estimand the candidate asks for — the design weights implied by an estimator, their design effect, and whether a unit-level or cluster-level aggregation targets the intended estimand when cluster size is informative — is a mature, named problem family in survey statistics (Kish design effect, effective sample size) and biostatistics (informative cluster size, cluster- vs observation-level estimands; Kahan et al.). Queries 4 and 9 found that apparatus and found zero application of it to a training run. That negative is therefore recorded from the method's field, not only from the data's field.

## Significance — consequence

If the gap is large (ESS ratio well below 1, mixture TV shift of the order of the deltas mixture papers optimize over, loss/benchmark gap outside the pre-registered band): a configured mixture is not the realized mixture, so the mixture-optimization literature is optimizing a quantity the trainer does not implement, and "trained on N tokens of mixture m" ceases to identify an objective. Cross-paper comparisons at matched token counts are then not matched, pooling runs across stacks to fit scaling laws introduces a stack-dependent nuisance, and a share of the recipe literature's non-transferring effects has a named candidate confounder. If the gap is small — the null — then "tokens seen" is established as a comparable unit across stacks, which is itself a licence the field currently assumes without evidence; the concern retires and cross-stack pooling is justified rather than hoped. If the unspecifiability share is high, the finding is that for most published runs the stated objective is not specific enough for the question to have an answer, which is a reporting-standards result: it says what a run report would have to contain.

## Reason for silence

A disciplinary and artifact boundary, reinforced by notation and by incentives. The semantics live in framework source and launch configs, owned by systems engineers who hold them to a correctness criterion ("does gradient accumulation match large-batch training?") rather than treating them as an estimand — which is exactly what the issue histories show: each is fixed as a bug on one path, and no one aggregates them into a statement about what the shipped objective is. The claim about what was optimized lives in papers, owned by researchers who write the objective in mathematics where the denominator is elided (a sum over tokens of −log p looks weight-uniform, so it appears to need no reporting), and who therefore have no notation in which the question is visible. No one owns the join. Compounding it: computing the quantity needs both the config and the realized data order, which is the part least often released, so the natural first study is blocked for exactly the runs whose claims matter most; the method's vocabulary (design weights, design effect, informative cluster size) sits in survey statistics and biostatistics, so the question has no name in ML and does not surface in ML literature searches (queries 4 and 9 confirm the apparatus exists unused); and there is an incentive gap — being first to publish a realized weight distribution makes one's own run look less comparable to everyone else's, with no offsetting credit.

## Scores

Well-posedness: 3/3 — after V1 each limb names a computable quantity against a declared reference (zero-weight share, Kish ESS ratio, mixture TV distance, matched-FLOPs loss gap against a pre-registered band), the frame and its fallback (instrumented replication) are declared, both a yes and a null are bounded, and the case where no gap is definable is converted into a reported share rather than left as an escape hatch. Residual weakness: "the stated objective" is a declared convention, not something papers assert, so the gap is a gap against a stipulated reference — defensible but stipulated.

Significance: 2/3 — consequence and audience are concrete and the audience is demonstrably active, but the significance is capped from two sides: in the highest-compute regime (fully packed pretraining) the perturbation may be near-zero by construction (presupposition 3), and in the regime where it bites hardest (post-training) practitioners already tune these knobs and the RL arm is partly answered, so a material share of the value is in the accounting and comparability limbs rather than in a behavioural surprise.

## Strongest remaining objection (for Gate 4's attention, not a verdict)

Presupposition 3 is the hinge, and it may split the question into two uninteresting halves: where length heterogeneity is large (SFT/RL) the effect is known, named and already acted on, and where the stakes are largest (packed pretraining at trillions of tokens) packing may flatten the weight distribution so far that the ESS ratio is ~1 and the mixture shift is below seed noise. If so, the audit's finding would be "material only where it is already managed," and the remaining content — that reported token counts are not a comparable unit — is an accounting observation that may be answerable in a page from the Megatron and HF code already quoted here, which is attack 1's opening.
