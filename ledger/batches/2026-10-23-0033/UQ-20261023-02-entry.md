Question (canonical): For >= 3 nominally constant-condition (DD or LL) recording chambers in >= 2 facilities, instrumented with passive multi-channel logs (temperature, vibration, sound, CO2, humidity, door/access events) at <= 1 min resolution over >= 2 complete experiments: (a) what is the amplitude and phase of the 24-hour component of each channel, with CIs, and does the temperature channel's amplitude reach or exceed the documented thermal entrainment threshold for the preparation under test; and (b) in a prospective chamber-crossed design running one fixed genotype or preparation across those same instrumented chambers, with chamber assignment randomised or counterbalanced, how much of the observed between-chamber variance in free-running period (tau) is attributable to the chambers' measured 24-hour amplitude, estimated as a chamber-level variance component alongside an imposed-cue-versus-sham arm at the measured facility amplitude testing for period change, phase-angle locking and after-effects?
Reformulation note: this is V2, the one reformulation permitted at Gate 1. V1 asked whether the recording environment carries a 24-hour component large enough to weakly entrain or mask the oscillator and how much of the between-chamber and between-laboratory spread in reported tau it accounts for; it failed on an estimand defined over a covariate published tau reports do not print, and on a per-channel threshold clause with no truth condition for vibration, sound and CO2. V2 measures the environment directly instead of inferring it from published reports and confines the threshold comparison to temperature. Both versions are in the record.

Alternative formulations:
- Is "constant darkness" constant — what residual 24-hour cues do circadian chambers actually deliver, and do they move the measured period?
- How much of a reported free-running period is the building's day rather than the organism's clock?

Motivating gap: Source method, presupposition inversion. Tau measured in constant conditions is treated as an endogenous property of the genotype or preparation; the untested presupposition is that the nominally constant environment carries no 24-hour periodic component of entraining or masking amplitude. Small-amplitude temperature cycles are an established resetting cue for peripheral and cell-autonomous clocks, and methods guidance names residual light leakage and scheduled noise as hazards, but nothing opened across the record and three adversary sweeps logs the 24-hour-periodic content of a recording chamber at facility amplitudes and tests its effect on tau.

What would count as an answer: (i) per-channel 24-hour amplitude and phase with CIs for each of >= 3 chambers in >= 2 facilities over >= 2 complete experiments, with the temperature amplitude placed against the preparation's documented threshold; (ii) the imposed-cue-versus-sham arm's effect on tau, phase angle and after-effects, reported against the measurement precision of the tau estimator actually used; (iii) a chamber-level variance component for tau with its CI, with measured 24-hour amplitude as the candidate predictor. Negative answer: measured 24-hour components below the preparation's documented threshold and an imposed arm shifting tau by less than estimator precision — which licenses explicitly what the field now inherits tacitly.

Significance — consequence: If yes (a 24-hour component at or above the preparation's thermal threshold and a tau shift exceeding estimator precision), tau stops being a property of the genotype or preparation and becomes a chamber-conditional measurement; the immediate casualties are mutant-versus-wild-type, strain and aging tau differences whose arms sat in different chambers or facilities, and the remedy is cheap — log and publish each chamber's per-channel 24-hour amplitude, randomise chamber against genotype. If no, a hazard that methods guidance names without quantifying is retired with bounds and an inherited presupposition becomes a licensed one. Both branches change practice.

(2016), "Climate retainment in carbon dioxide incubators", Current Directions in Biomedical Engineering 2(1):329-332, doi 10.1515/cdbme-2016-0073 — a peer-reviewed, DOI-bearing journal paper with five named authors, a volume and pages, not a TUM document at all; the TUM records at portal.fis.tum.de and mediatum.ub.tum.de are repository entries FOR that paper. Carrying it by institution plus title is what made it look like grey literature and invited leaving it unopened, when a DOI was one fetch from full text. What it contains is a CFD-plus-fog-visualisation study of incubator DOOR GEOMETRY: its abstract scopes the whole contribution to the door-opening event, "A door opening results in a climate breakdown followed by a restoration period during which optimal conditions cannot be guaranteed. The following paper investigates the influence of incubator door design to optimize climate retainment during the above mentioned event."; its longest time axis is 15 s, in Figure 4's CO2 volume fraction, with photographs stopping at 30 s; and it contains no circadian, diurnal or 24-hour content of any kind and no instrumented environmental channel whatsoever. Two descriptive errors are fixed with it. It is not a TUM document, as above; and its CO2-against-time curve is SIMULATED output from ANSYS Fluent with simulation boundary conditions of 10 Vol-% CO2 and 95 % rh, not a log, so it must never be cited as an instrumented incubator record — the paper's only physical measurement is effect fog in a mock-up under a laser plane, a tracer rather than a calibrated environmental channel, and its only duration figure, that the gases had "emanated to the external environment" after "a duration of more than 30 s", is attributed to the authors' experience rather than to an instrument. Had the item been opened carelessly, "CO2 plotted against time in an incubator" was one step from being mistaken for instrumentation. By this entry's OWN stated test — if the published work is only about recovery after door opening, with no 24-hour structure, the gap holds — this item falls squarely in the second case and leaves the gap intact, so it does not threaten limb (a). And that is why it must stop being carried as a live unopened risk: an OPENED-AND-OFF-POINT item is a different thing from an OUTSTANDING RISK. An unopened title is a risk precisely because its content is unknown and could contain the prior that caps the candidate's novelty; once the document has been read and found to be about a fifteen-second transient in a door aperture, the uncertainty is discharged and what remains is a negative result on a named document, which neither threatens the entry nor earns it any credit. Keeping it in the risk column would double-count one read as both a finding and a hazard, and would leave the entry claiming a danger that has already been dispatched. By the same token, the pass notes one asymmetry for whoever next works limb (a), and labels it as plausibility rather than content: Chen et al. 2026's Methods incidentally record that incubator humidifiers and heating and cooling systems produced an artefact — vibration — strong enough to degrade a circadian entrainment result, which is why the Percival Scientific and Power Scientific incubators were abandoned for a fabric greenhouse enclosure. That is a published statement that incubator subsystems perturb circadian experiments, and it supports the PLAUSIBILITY of limb (a) while supplying none of its content, since it concerns vibration, is not periodic in that paper, carries no 24-hour amplitude and is not cell-autonomous. One caution closes the block, in the checker's own terms: nothing in this pass licenses the stronger sentence that incubator environments have never been instrumented. Schuh et al. 2016 names a standard, DIN 12880, in its keywords, and Chen et al. 2026 names a logger, controllers and a specific environmental artefact of incubator subsystems; the literatures those point into are real and nobody read them, so two reads bound what was checked and not what exists.
