## 1. Restatement in my own words

When a program running on a JIT virtual machine keeps changing its per-iteration speed over thousands of iterations instead of settling, is the thing that keeps it unsettled the optimiser's own habit of re-deciding what to compile, on the basis of profiles it keeps collecting? The proposed test: run the same VM and program twice — once letting the optimiser keep re-deciding throughout, once making it stop re-deciding after a chosen point while keeping the compiled code it had — and see whether only the second settles. The alternative formulations on the record ask the same thing as a share: what fraction of non-settling executions would settle if the optimiser stopped re-deciding, against a control that stops it later.

### Attack 8 — Dissolution — **Partial**

**Objection.** (i) The dichotomy "intrinsic to PGO versus exogenous to the compiler" is not a partition: Antoch §1 frames feedback-directed compilation as a propagator that expands exogenous execution differences into future repetitions (VERIFY-10-b verbatim). (ii) "The loop" is not one mechanism — counter-triggered tier-up, deopt→reprofile→recompile, inlining revision, OSR, code-cache flushing, and late first-time compilation at high thresholds (the last named by Antoch §8, VERIFY-10-b; the list is **[MINE]**) — and a freeze removes all of them, so the answer is a mixture whose composition varies by VM and has no referent for tracing JITs. (iii) Non-convergence is horizon-relative, so "the failure of JIT-compiled programs to reach a steady state" as one phenomenon with one cause is what dissolves.

**Best response.** The interventional estimand — a between-arm difference at a fixed horizon over a declared frame — is well-defined whatever the mechanism's composition and whether the loop originates or amplifies; a mixture is still an answer; the record's fecundity section already anticipates decomposition.

**Status: Partial.** The estimand survives; the question's interpretive frame (the two alternatives, and the record's reading of a positive result) does not. Load-bearing for (i): a document (Antoch §1 via VERIFY-10-b), not my reasoning.
