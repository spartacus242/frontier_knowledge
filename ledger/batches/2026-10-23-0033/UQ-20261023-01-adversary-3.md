## 0. Restatement in my own words

Labs that record the same brain region for days with chronic high-density probes (Neuropixels) rely on an algorithm (UnitMatch, EMD) to say which unit on day N "is" which unit on day N+k. Those algorithms make identity mistakes: linking two different neurons (false match), or splitting one neuron into two tracks or merging two into one. The drift literature then reports that matched units' tuning changes across days ("representational drift"). The candidate asks: if NO neuron actually changed its tuning, how much apparent drift would the identity mistakes alone manufacture in the field's own drift statistics (a "matching-error drift floor"), when the mistakes are scored against an identity label that does not itself rely on tuning similarity; and do published drift magnitudes for the same brain area and interval exceed that floor? Gate 1's repair converts "what fraction of each paper's number is bookkeeping" into "compute a floor from ≥2 matchers at declared operating points, propagate it into ≥2 published statistics under a zero-true-drift null, and compare against published magnitudes".
