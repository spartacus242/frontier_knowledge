## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (canonical, V1 as submitted):
What is the end-to-end behavioural modulation transfer function of a shipped vision-language model's image pathway — as a function of spatial frequency (cycles per image and cycles per encoder patch) and contrast, where does forced-choice reporting of a test target's orientation and phase fall to chance, and does that measured cutoff predict the model's fine-detail failures (small text, counting, dense charts) better than input resolution or patch count?

Question (canonical, V2 — the ONE Gate 1 reformulation, permitted only for well-posedness; both versions recorded and V2 is the version graded):
For a pre-declared set of shipped vision-language models crossed with input modes (native resize; each dynamic-tiling / any-resolution setting), probed with synthetic test targets (sine gratings; slanted edges) swept over spatial frequency expressed BOTH in cycles per image and in cycles per encoder patch, crossed with Michelson contrast, orientation (cardinal and oblique) and phase, under a two-alternative forced-choice readout with pre-declared low-frequency positive controls and an enforced chance level:
(i) what is the frequency at which orientation-discrimination accuracy falls to a pre-declared 75%-correct criterion, and what is the contrast threshold as a function of frequency;
(ii) what is the ratio of that cutoff to the nominal patch-Nyquist limit set by patch size and positional-encoding resolution;
(iii) is any above-cutoff correct responding attributable to resize-kernel aliasing (tracking the kernel rather than the target) rather than to true transmission;
(iv) in a pre-registered out-of-sample comparison with the model x input-mode arm as the unit of analysis and a declared estimator and power statement, does the per-arm cutoff explain variance in fine-detail task accuracy (small-text reading, counting, dense-chart QA) beyond input pixel count and vision-token/patch count?

Why the reformulation was needed (well-posedness only, no scope change): V1 calls the object a "modulation transfer function", which presupposes an intensity-linear, shift-invariant chain; this pathway has neither an intensity-linear output to Fourier-transform nor a linear resampler (dynamic tiling is content-dependent). V2 renames the object a psychophysical frequency-response analogue with a declared threshold convention, names the unit of analysis for the "better than" limb (V1 left "predict ... better than" without a unit, an estimator or a power statement, which is answerable either way), and states that limb (ii) is computable only on arms whose preprocessing is recoverable.

Alternative formulations (as submitted, retained):
- What is the visual acuity and contrast-sensitivity limit of a VLM's image front-end, measured the way an imaging system's resolution is measured rather than by accuracy on natural images?
- If a VLM's vision stack is treated as an imaging chain and a test target is swept through it, where is its resolution limit, and how far do dynamic-tiling policies move it?

Motivating gap (REWRITTEN at Gate 2 — the intake's version is falsified in part; see Novelty):
Imaging metrology characterises any imaging chain by a frequency response (slanted-edge SFR/MTF per ISO 12233, or sine-target measurement) and optometry by a contrast sensitivity function, before anyone reports task accuracy, because the cutoff tells you what the chain can carry at all. VLM practice reports accuracy on natural-image benchmarks, and model cards report pixel resolution, patch size and token count, which are NOMINAL limits.
The intake asserted that "VLM evaluation has no such instrument". That assertion is FALSE as of August/October 2025: Hernández-Cámara et al. (arXiv:2508.10367, opened in full) estimate behavioural contrast sensitivity functions for eight open multimodal LLMs end-to-end from their linguistic responses, fit psychometric functions, and claim predictive validity for frequency-filtered and adversarial robustness. The surviving gap is narrower and is what V2 asks: that instrument measures DETECTION of bandpass noise under an assumed viewing geometry in cycles per degree, defines and prints NO cutoff frequency, never mentions patch size, resize kernel, tiling policy, aliasing or a Nyquist limit, and tests prediction only against frequency-filtered Imagenette classification — not against fine-detail task accuracy, and never against input resolution or patch count as rival predictors.

Presuppositions (updated; the intake's versions kept where unchanged):
- P1 (CONTESTED, handled by design): a VLM's image pathway can be probed behaviourally and the linguistic response read as a detection/discrimination measurement. A null could be a reporting failure rather than information loss, so the design needs low-frequency positive controls and a forced-choice format. PARTLY RELIEVED by prior art: Hernández-Cámara et al. run exactly this kind of behavioural readout and obtain orderly psychometric curves (Fig. 3) — but they also report that estimates are SENSITIVE TO PROMPT WORDING across 25 prompt variants, which converts P1 from "can it be done" into "it can be done and the prompt is a nuisance factor that must be a declared arm". V2 inherits that requirement.
- P2 (CONTESTED, and partly what is being tested): information loss in the pathway is organised by spatial frequency at all. A non-monotone or aliasing-dominated response is an informative answer, not a failed measurement. WEAKLY SUPPORTED by prior art (prior 1 obtains band-pass-shaped CSFs for several models); still contested for discrimination of orientation/phase.
- P3 (mostly established for open-weight models): preprocessing (resize kernel, interpolation, tiling rule) is documented or recoverable per model. Closed APIs can only be black-box arms and must be labelled as such; limb (ii) is not computable on them. NOT INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED HERE — I did not open a single model's preprocessing config in this Gate 2, and per the document-kind rule I decline to treat "the config prints the resize kernel and tiling rule" as established. It is a premise about a kind of document (a processor/preprocessor config) that needs two or three ACTUAL instances opened before it grounds anything. Flagged as the cheapest open check.
- P4 (theoretical, and the point of the comparison): patch-based encoders have a nominal sampling limit set by patch size and positional-encoding resolution. Whether the behavioural cutoff reaches that nominal limit is the question, so the nominal limit must not be assumed to be the answer.
- P5 (SOURCED at Gate 2, was "NOT sourced by me"): the fine-detail failures the cutoff is supposed to explain are real and reported. Sourced to Rahmanzadehgervi, Bolton, Taesiri & Nguyen, "Vision language models are blind", arXiv:2407.06581v4 / ACCV 2024, READ LEVEL: FULL TEXT (one capture gap, see below): four frontier VLMs average 58.57% on seven low-level tasks, best model Sonnet-3.5 at 74.01%; per-task image denominators printed in Section 3 (1,800 / 672 / 1,248 / 480 / 120 / 264 / 180). COVERAGE LIMIT: that paper sources the COUNTING limb (nested squares, overlapping circles/pentagons, grid rows/columns) and a small-text-adjacent limb (identifying the circled letter in a word), and cites chart/diagram benchmarks only as a contrast. It does NOT source dense-chart failure. So P5 is sourced for counting and fine spatial detail, title-level-at-best for charts; V2's chart limb still needs a source of its own (OCRBench/ChartQA-class evidence, not opened here).
- P6 (measurand exemption DECLARED AND APPLIED, with its scope corrected): that the quantities in (i)-(iv) have no measured values for these systems is the measurand, and the absence of those numbers is therefore not a false-premise flaw. The exemption is declared for the cutoff, the cutoff-to-patch-Nyquist ratio, the aliasing attribution and the predictor comparison. It is NOT declared, and must not be claimed, for "no frequency-resolved behavioural measurement of a VLM exists" — that one is falsified by prior 1 and the motivating gap has been rewritten accordingly.

What would count as an answer:
Per model and per input mode: a response surface over the pre-registered grating/edge sweep crossed with contrast, orientation and phase, giving (i) the cutoff frequency at the pre-declared 75%-correct 2AFC criterion, in both cycles/image and cycles/patch; (ii) the ratio of that cutoff to nominal patch-Nyquist; (iii) an aliasing test — above-cutoff responding that tracks the resize kernel rather than the target, e.g. by swapping kernels with the target fixed; (iv) an out-of-sample test of whether the per-arm cutoff predicts fine-detail accuracy beyond pixel count and patch count, with the estimator and the unit (model x mode arm) declared in advance.
A negative answer that counts: cutoffs sit at the nominal limit and carry no predictive information beyond resolution/patch count — the instrument is redundant and nominal reporting is vindicated.
Not an answer: accuracy on natural-image fine-detail benchmarks with no frequency axis; a cutoff measured only under one tiling setting and reported as the model's cutoff.

## Gate 3 — Significance

- **Consequence.** If the cutoff sits well below patch-Nyquist and survives limb (iv) as a predictor beyond pixel count and patch count: the fine-detail failures that prior 2 demonstrated and prior 3 localised to ~12 px become attributable, per arm, to information loss in the pathway rather than to reasoning, and the figures model cards report (input pixels, patch size, token budget) are shown to be nominal rather than effective — in the way a lens's aperture is nominal and its MTF is not. Design attention moves from "more pixels" to the resampling and tiling stages, which is where limb (iii) says the aliasing lives. Benchmark builders get an item-level admissibility test: a test item whose critical detail sits above the measured cutoff of the arm under test is not scoring reasoning. If instead cutoffs sit at the nominal limit and carry no incremental predictive information, the instrument is redundant and nominal reporting is vindicated — a clean negative that closes a live hypothesis, and one that prior 2's within-task resolution null (Appendix 0.B.2, heading-level read) already makes plausible enough to be worth settling.
- **Audience.** Three identifiable groups, two of them demonstrably active. (a) Multimodal architecture teams choosing patch size, resize kernel and dynamic-tiling rules: the existence of Res-Bench, CARES, Task-Aware Resolution Optimization and the ICLR 2026 adaptive-patching poster (titles only, not opened) shows a community currently selecting resolution policy by benchmark accuracy with no channel characterisation; a cutoff-per-tiling-setting is directly actionable for them, because it says how much of the extra resolution a tiling policy actually delivers. (b) Benchmark and eval builders, who at present cannot say whether a failure is information loss or reasoning — exactly the dissociation prior 3 reports and cannot mechanistically ground. (c) The machine-psychophysics group that built prior 1, which owns the instrument and has no engineering axis on it; its own stated future work goes to natural images and cognitive suites, not to resize kernels.
- **Fecundity.** An encoder-normalised frequency axis makes several further questions askable that currently cannot be posed: does the cutoff differ between cardinal and oblique orientations, which would be a measurable signature of the patch grid itself; does tiling introduce phase-dependent anisotropy at tile seams; does post-training move the cutoff with the vision encoder frozen (an upper-bound test on projector and LM contribution); does a cutoff-matched pre-filter remove aliasing-driven errors without any model change; and is there a scaling law of cutoff against vision-token budget, which is where V2 meets the fourth source's token-capacity law.
- **Anchoring.** To three anomalies in opened sources: prior 2's demonstrated low-level failures together with its resolution null and line-width effect; prior 3's perception/reasoning dissociation at ~12 px with no mechanism; and prior 1's finding that models match the human CSF in shape or in scale but never both — a result that currently has no engineering explanation and would acquire one if the cutoff tracked patch geometry.
- **Score: 2/3, deliberately not 3.** Consequence, audience and fecundity are argued above and the anchoring is to sources I opened. The deduction is honest rather than modest: prior 1 already supplies a frequency-resolved behavioural instrument, so the candidate's marginal consequence is concentrated in limbs (ii)-(iv), and limb (iv) — the one that would actually change a design decision — has the model x mode arm as its unit of analysis, which realistically means on the order of tens of arms and a predictor comparison that may be underpowered to separate a cutoff from pixel count. A descriptive cutoff table with an inconclusive limb (iv) would be a measurement the field could decline to act on.

## Reason for silence

Two mechanisms, the first primary and specific.

**A disciplinary boundary that runs exactly through this measurement, with the method owned on one side and the knobs on the other.** The instrument is owned by imaging-performance metrology (ISO 12233 slanted-edge SFR, the IS&T Electronic Imaging IQSP community, the AutoSens camera-AI community) and by visual psychophysics. Query 11 shows that the metrology community does ask the predictive question — an AutoSens session is literally titled "MTF as a performance indicator for AI algorithms" — but it asks it of a CAMERA front-end feeding a detection algorithm, because cameras are what that community's instruments attach to; a model's internal resize-and-tile stage is not a lens and has no test chart. Visual psychophysics has now reached shipped multimodal models (prior 1), but it came with vision science's questions — is the CSF human-like — and vision science has no interest in resize kernels, which is why prior 1 contains no mention of patch size, tiling or aliasing despite measuring the very surface those things shape. On the other side, the multimodal architecture community measures its pathway by nominal configuration because configuration is what it sets and ships, and evaluates by natural-image benchmark accuracy because that is what its publication and leaderboard incentives reward; the papers that vary resolution (Res-Bench, CARES, adaptive patching) treat it as a knob to optimise, not as a channel to characterise. Nobody sits at the junction where the frequency axis is divided by the patch pitch — which is the one normalisation that makes the measurement an architecture result rather than a curiosity.

**Secondarily, a new-object mechanism.** The thing V2 measures only recently became the thing that matters: content-dependent dynamic tiling / any-resolution preprocessing is roughly two years old, and it makes the front end a non-linear resampler whose behaviour cannot be read off the nominal patch grid at all. Before it, "resolution" and "patch size" were nearly sufficient descriptions, so the gap between nominal and effective had little room to open. Note what this mechanism does NOT excuse: prior 1 shows the behavioural-psychophysics bridge was crossable by 2025, so the silence is about the engineering normalisation and the rival-predictor test, not about the feasibility of behavioural probing.
