## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (canonical, V1 as intaken):
Over an enumerated frame of deployed time-distribution specifications, data formats and open-source implementations that carry the UTC−TAI or UT1−UTC offset, what is the distribution of the largest offset each can represent and correctly propagate without a format change or an out-of-range failure, and what share of the frame is broken at each candidate maximum value of |UT1−UTC| now in play for continuous UTC?

Question (canonical, V2 — the ONE Gate 1 reformulation, for well-posedness only; this is the version graded):
Over an enumerated, publicly-documented frame of time-distribution specifications, data formats and open-source implementations that carry UTC−TAI or UT1−UTC, what is the distribution of the largest |UT1−UTC| each can represent and correctly propagate — each frame member first classified by the MECHANISM that carries the offset (scalar field of stated width / leap-second table or file / step-flag with no scalar field at all) and then by failure mode beyond its maximum (saturate / wrap / reject / undefined) — and what cumulative share of the frame is non-conformant at the 3,600 s maximum |UT1−UTC| adopted in CGPM Resolution 5 (2026) for continuous UTC from 20 May 2027, with the earliest-breaking artifacts named?

Why reformulated (recorded per Gate 1): (i) the policy value is NO LONGER OPEN — see the Gate 2 presupposition check below — so "each candidate maximum value now in play" denotes an empty set and the estimand had no threshold; V2 fixes the primary threshold at the adopted 3,600 s and keeps 0.9 s and 64 s only as secondary cut points for legacy-conformance reporting. (ii) The document-kind premise was tested in this session on four actual artifacts and FAILED for one of them (NTP has no scalar offset field at all), so "the largest offset each can represent" is not defined artifact-by-artifact until the carrying mechanism is a declared classifier; V2 moves the mechanism taxonomy inside the estimand instead of assuming it away.

Alternative formulations:
- If UTC is allowed to drift from UT1 by minutes or hours, which deployed formats and codebases run out of bits first, and at exactly what value?
- What is the representable dynamic range of the leap-second and DUT1 fields in the world's time-transfer standards — measured over an enumerated frame rather than asserted one body at a time?

Motivating gap:
CGPM Resolution 4 (2022) decided the maximum |UT1−UTC| "will be increased in, or before, 2035" and left the value open; the CCTF/BIPM Task Group then canvassed sector PREFERENCES. ## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

What would count as an answer:
A table over the DECLARED public-artifact frame giving, per member: the field or mechanism that carries the offset; its encoding and width (or "no scalar field" with the mechanism named); the maximum |offset| representable; and the observed failure mode beyond it. Open-source members scored by EXECUTING a declared test-vector suite (a published vector set stepping |UT1−UTC| through 0.9 s, 64 s, 3,600 s and each type boundary, with the four failure modes as the outcome alphabet); specification-only members marked as specification-only and scored on representability alone. From that, the cumulative share of the frame non-conformant at each cut point, with the earliest-breaking artifacts named. The estimand is explicitly SPLIT — representability over the whole frame, propagation over the executable sub-frame only — and the two are never pooled into one percentage.
A clean negative settles it: if every member represents and propagates an hour-scale offset, representability is not a constraint on the 2027 transition and the remaining questions are purely operational.
Worked example of a computable cell, from this session: IEEE 1588 / ietf-ptp `current-utc-offset`, `int16`, maximum 32 767 s (my own arithmetic from the declared type, not a figure printed by any source) — so 3 600 s is representable with ~9.1 h of headroom, and the open question for that member is propagation, not representation. NTP has no cell in the "maximum" column at all and belongs in the flag-only mechanism class. Both cells are reachable; the estimand is therefore computable rather than rhetorical.
Residual well-posedness wobble, stated rather than hidden: the frame is declaration-dependent, and the share-non-conformant figure moves with the frame. Mitigated the way this ledger mitigates it elsewhere — the frame is declared and published before any artifact is scored, with inclusion rules (publicly retrievable specification or source, carries UTC−TAI or UT1−UTC, in force or in distribution on a stated date) — not eliminated.
It is a question, not a thesis: presupposition 6 admits the null result as an answer, and the candidate takes no position on which artifacts break.

## Gate 3 — Significance

Consequence (argued): If the answer is that a non-trivial share of the public-artifact frame cannot represent or propagate 3 600 s, then Resolution 5's 20 May 2027 date has a named, dated list of artifacts needing change, with the earliest-breaking ones identified, and the transition acquires a work-breakdown it does not currently have — the QZSS 64 s remark becomes either confirmed or refuted as a format limit, and Galileo's unnumbered "number of bits" becomes a number. If the answer is that everything in the frame already tolerates hour-scale offsets, then representability was never a constraint on the choice, the sector preferences canvassed in 2024 were preferences about operational convenience and not about bits, and the Task Group's treatment of the question as a preference problem is retrospectively vindicated — which is itself a finding about how the decision was made. Either way the share-non-conformant figure is the quantity transition planners would cite, and at present no one has it.
Audience (specific, and now dated): the CCTF/BIPM Task Group on Continuous UTC and the CIPM actions following Resolution 5; IERS (which publishes the offset); ITU-R SG7/WP7A, whose Recommendations fix the content of disseminated time signals; the GNSS control segments named in the deck (GPS, Galileo, QZSS) facing the assessment slide 14 describes; and the maintainers of the executable members — linuxptp, ntpsec/ntp, chrony, RTKLIB and the GNSS receiver firmware populations behind them. They would act differently in a concrete way: a published per-artifact table with executed failure modes converts "countless pieces of software need re-programming" into a bounded list with owners, and a test-vector suite is the artifact a conformance programme can adopt directly.
Fecundity: once the carrying mechanism is a declared classifier rather than an assumption, the askable questions include — what share of the frame carries the offset out of band (leap-second file, table) rather than in a field, and what is the propagation latency of an out-of-band change; whether failure mode (saturate vs wrap vs reject vs undefined) predicts time-to-remediate better than field width does; what the analogous distribution looks like for the OTHER direction, a negative leap second, which is the nearer hazard before 2027; and whether the preference a body volunteered in 2024 correlates at all with its artifacts' measured maximum — a directly testable question about how standards bodies answer technical questions about themselves.
Anchoring: CGPM Resolution 4 (2022) and Resolution 5 (2026); ITU-R Resolution 655 and Report TF.2511-0; the negative-leap-second hazard; and the Y2K/2038 family of representable-range audits, which is the method ancestry — Seaman's paper explicitly anchors there, and the candidate is the version of that audit the leap-second transition never got.
Significance score: 2/3. The audience is real, named and now operating against a fixed date, which is as strong as this ledger's audience arguments get. Held below 3 because a material part of the value is operational engineering rather than knowledge-producing, and because the GPS/QZSS slice is already recognised as needed work — so the increment the candidate adds is the cross-artifact frame and the executed taxonomy, not the discovery that the question matters.

## Reason for silence

Four mechanisms, with the disciplinary boundary doing most of the work. (1) INCENTIVE AND STANDING GAP: the artifacts belong to operators and vendors, and no body has standing to enumerate another's. The governance instrument available to the Task Group is a canvass, so it asked each body what it would PREFER; each answered with a preference and, where it felt like it, volunteered one claim about its own artifact (QZSS's 64 s, Galileo's "number of bits" with no number). A canvass of bodies cannot produce a distribution over artifacts, and nothing in the process converts one into the other. (2) DISCIPLINARY BOUNDARY between the field that owns the data and the field that owns the method: enumerating a frame, declaring a failure-mode alphabet and executing boundary test vectors is conformance-testing and empirical-software-engineering practice, while the question lives in time metrology and radiocommunication regulation; TF.2511-0 organises its impact analysis by service because that is the unit its community works in. (3) NO THRESHOLD TO TEST AGAINST: while the value was open, "does it break" had no argument, and a census would have had to be re-run per candidate value — Resolution 5's 3 600 s removes exactly this obstacle, which is why the silence is ending now rather than why it existed.
