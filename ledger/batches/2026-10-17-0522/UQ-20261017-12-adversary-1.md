## Restatement (my own words)

A video-trained pose estimator or behaviour classifier is a function of the animal's image. Many treatments under study (sedation, obesity, injury, coat-colour or body-size mutants) change that image. The candidate asks: when such a pipeline is applied inside one experiment, does it misclassify (or mislocalise keypoints for) treated animals at a different rate than controls, judged against blinded multi-rater human labels; and, if so, when you correct the reported treatment effect for that group-dependent error, does the corrected estimate move by more than the published confidence interval? The claim under attack is that nobody has measured per-group error for these pipelines or propagated it into an effect-size bias.
