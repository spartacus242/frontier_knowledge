Domain(s): Mathematics — Diophantine equations and arithmetic statistics (sums of three cubes; pure cubic fields)

Question (canonical, ORIGINAL as submitted): Now that the complete set of solutions to x^3 + y^3 + z^3 = k up to height around 10^15 to 10^16 is known for every admissible |k| <= 1000, is the per-k representation density — the count's slope against log of the height — determined by the local singular series alone, or does the residual after dividing out the local prediction correlate with a global invariant of the pure cubic field Q(k^(1/3)), such as its class number, its regulator, or the 3-part of its class group?

Question (canonical, REFORMULATED at Gate 1 — one reformulation, used only for well-posedness; both versions recorded): Fix B = 10^15 and the Booker-Sutherland counting convention (solutions with min{|x|,|y|,|z|} <= B). For each cube-free k with 0 < |k| <= 1000 and k not congruent to +/-4 mod 9, let n_k(B) be the number of such solutions and let c_k^loc be the Heath-Brown singular-series constant computed from local densities alone, so that the reference prediction is c_k^loc * log B. Does the residual r_k = n_k(B) / (c_k^loc * log B) have a nonzero rank correlation, against a permutation null with the mod-9 congruence class and the cube-factor structure of k conditioned out, with any of three pre-declared invariants of K = Q(k^(1/3)) — the class number h(K), the regulator R(K), and the 3-rank of the class group of K? And, as a labelled prerequisite the estimand depends on: is the per-k prediction actually in use (Booker-Sutherland's rho_sol) purely local, or does its construction already involve the unit group of K?

WHAT THE REPAIR PRESUPPOSES (stated in writing, as required): the reformulation presupposes (i) that a purely-local c_k^loc is well defined and computable per k independently of global invariants of K, and (ii) that the reference prediction can be stipulated by the question rather than inherited from the search literature. Both are open on my read — see "Strongest remaining objection". The repair also DROPS the original's fourth invariant, "the unit index of the associated norm form", because that phrase names neither an index of a specific group in a specific group nor the specific norm form; it is not enumerated and not generated by a stated rule, so it cannot stay in a pre-declared list. The reformulated list has exactly three members, all standard and computable in bulk: h(K), R(K), 3-rank of Cl(K). Which of the two routes I took: I ENUMERATED the members.

Alternative formulations:
1. Do the newly complete three-cube solution tables show a per-k representation density that local densities fail to predict, and does the discrepancy track the unit and class-group arithmetic of Q(k^(1/3))?
2. Is the solution-poverty of small k such as k = 3 a fluctuation of the predicted logarithmic law, or the shadow of a global invariant that the singular series cannot see?

Motivating gap: Exhaustive lattice-based searches have within the last few years produced per-k solution lists to heights near 10^15-10^19, which turns the per-k representation density from a heuristic parameter into a measurable one. The standard heuristic predicts a count asymptotic to c_k * log B with c_k built from local densities, and individual k deviate startlingly: k = 3 has three known solutions, and k = 33 and k = 42 were resolved only at very large heights. The equation's solutions at fixed x + y + z are governed by norm forms in Q(k^(1/3)), where units and the class group act, so the residual is a natural place to look for global arithmetic information.

Reason for silence: a disciplinary boundary astride a new instrument.
