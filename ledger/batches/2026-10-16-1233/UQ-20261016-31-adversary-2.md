## 1. Restatement in my own words

Strip the vocabulary and the claim is: the radio "leakage" an individual Starlink-class LEO satellite throws off in a given band is currently treated, in practice, as an attribute you can read off its model number — v1.5 emits this much, v2-Mini 32x more, DTC this much. The candidate asks whether that is the right kind of thing to attribute. Specifically: take one physical spacecraft, identified by NORAD ID, and measure its band-limited radiated power repeatedly, in several passes, at two or more widely separated times. How much of the spread in those numbers is the satellite-at-a-moment (pass-level, duty cycle, thermal/illumination state), how much is the satellite-at-an-epoch drifting as it ages, how much is this unit versus its siblings, and how much is the model? Second limb: does the per-unit number drift with time on orbit when the model is held fixed? Third limb: of the satellites a past survey saw emitting and that are still flying, how many can be shown — with a stated, matched upper limit, not just "we didn't see it" — to be below where they used to be? And the stake attached: if the per-unit quantity moves around, then population forecasts of satellite sky noise assembled from one-off, early-life survey snapshots are the wrong estimator, and any per-satellite emission limit written as a single number is unverifiable.

So: an identification/level-of-analysis question about a measurement that is already being used for regulation, with a specific published anomaly (previously-detected satellites not re-detected) as its hook.

## 4. The eight attacks

### Attack 1 — Triviality (can it be answered in a page from existing knowledge?)

**Objection.** The headline dichotomy is already disposed of in print, and I can do it in a paragraph from two sources I opened. SNIFFLES I reports unintended radiation in 76.9% of observations of one satellite version at 2700 MHz — i.e. the emission is *absent* in roughly a quarter of observations of that version, which is flatly incompatible with "fixed property of its hardware model" read as an always-on level. Grigg et al. add, within a single campaign, that "the spectral structure changes in time", that the XX/YY polarisation fluxes "are anti-correlated at any given time", and that "the received signal is stronger when the satellite is closest to Geraldton", plus a population detection rate of "~30% of all images". Any competent reader hands back: it varies; it is duty-cycled and state-dependent; next question. Worse for the candidate's proposed design, the replicate structure needed for most of limb (a) *already exists in public*: 112,534 detections of 1,806 identified satellites across a four-month window (my arithmetic: ~62 detections per satellite on average), with launch dates available — Dong et al. 2026 already exploit exactly this to form within-satellite contrasts. A cross-sectional age test with model held fixed (limb b) is likewise available now, because same-model satellites in that catalogue differ in on-orbit age at a common observing epoch. So the candidate asks for a new two-epoch campaign to obtain quantities that a re-analysis of an existing catalogue could partly deliver.

**Best response.** The two things in print are not the two things asked. 76.9% is a *presence rate over observations of a version*, aggregated across units and across one observing programme — it is not a per-unit quantity and it cannot be decomposed, because the design has no unit-level replicate-and-epoch structure (confirmed: SNIFFLES I's unit of analysis is constellation/version, NORAD IDs not used in the abstract's framing). Grigg's within-pass and spectral-shape variability is geometry and instantaneous state at seconds-to-minutes; the candidate's regressor is on-orbit age over months-to-years with model fixed, which no amount of within-pass detail speaks to. On the public-catalogue point: a four-month window gives pass-within-epoch variance and a *cross-sectional* age contrast, but it cannot separate epoch-within-satellite drift from fleet-composition and launch-cohort effects — which is precisely the failure mode Mallama 2026 hits in the optical sibling ("The fading of OneWeb satellites does not appear to be due to aging") and precisely why Dong et al. 2026 declined the decomposition as "not separable in the present sample". And nothing in print answers limb (c): I verified that Grigg publishes **no numerical upper limit** for any of the 68, only a campaign-level "about 1 Jy" sensitivity remark, so the published non-detections are unbounded and the fraction is uninterpretable as stated.

**Status: Partial.** The canonical dichotomy *is* answerable in a paragraph and must be retired; a meaningful part of limb (a) and a weak version of limb (b) are available from existing public data and do not need the proposed campaign. What is not answerable in a page: the epoch-vs-pass partition, the age coefficient net of cohort composition, and any bounded non-detection fraction. Required amendment: carry the Gate 1 reformulation only, and state explicitly that the first deliverable is a re-analysis of the existing catalogue, with the two-epoch campaign justified only for what the catalogue cannot identify.

### Attack 2 — Disguise (is it a known question renamed?)

**Objection.** In eight vocabularies the record did not try, I found a formal home for every analytic limb of this question, and in one case for the whole question. (i) Occupational/environmental exposure science owns limb (a) exactly: within-unit vs between-unit variance components on identified units with many measurements left-censored at a limit of detection — a problem with a 30-year literature and off-the-shelf estimators (Kromhout 1993; multiple NIOSH reports; "Heterogeneity in Sources of Exposure Variability Among Groups of Workers"). (ii) Ecology owns limb (c) exactly: whether a unit detected in one survey and not in the next has changed state or merely evaded detection is the dynamic-occupancy / false-absence problem, with detection probability estimated from repeat visits. (iii) Reliability engineering owns limb (b): random-coefficient degradation models with unit-to-unit variability. (iv) Time-domain astronomy owns limb (a) under irregular sampling: structure functions and ensemble-variance estimation from sparsely sampled light curves. (v) Most damaging, on-road vehicle remote sensing owns the *whole question shape*: individually identified mobile emitters, measured repeatedly across campaigns over years, with published deterioration-with-age rates and an explicit literature on whether a "high emitter" is a stable property of the unit or an intermittent state. If the question has five formal homes and one near-isomorph, what is left is a dataset, not a question.

**Best response.** Two things keep it from being a renaming. First, none of these fields has been pointed at this object: twelve searches, in their own words, returned no measurement of radiated power of identified orbiting units over service life, and the one near-isomorph (vehicle remote sensing) differs in emitted quantity (chemical species vs band-limited RF power), in the censoring mechanism, and in whether a model-level constant was ever the operative assumption. Second — and this is the substantive defence — the transplant is not mechanical in this regime, because the censoring is *informative in exactly the direction of the signal*. The occupational-hygiene case assumes a fixed instrumental LOD independent of the exposure process. Here, Grigg's own text establishes that detectability co-varies with the drivers of the quantity being estimated: "the received signal is stronger when the satellite is closest to Geraldton", so the effective threshold is a function of range, elevation and beam response, i.e. of the same covariates that modulate the measurement. Dropping a standard left-censored variance-components model onto that yields a biased partition. So the imported method has to be rebuilt, not reused.

**Status: Partial.** The question's *analytic content* is a known, named problem in at least five other fields, which demotes its methodological novelty to "transplant plus a censoring repair", and I could not open any of those literatures (fetch budget spent on the on-point astronomy priors), so I cannot exclude that one of them has already been aimed at satellites. It is not a known question renamed about *this object* on anything I found. Not Resolved, and deliberately not Unresolved: a rename would require a located instance, and I located none.

### Attack 3 — Combinatorial novelty (Field A x Field B with no problem at the interface?)

**Objection.** On its face this is "satellite UEMR surveys x repeated-measures variance components" — a method pulled off the shelf and applied to a convenient dataset, with the interesting work all on one side of the join.

**Best response, evidenced from sources I opened.** There are at least three interface problems that neither parent field solves. (1) Informative, covariate-dependent censoring, as above: the detection threshold is a function of range and elevation, and Grigg states the signal is strongest at closest approach, so censoring and signal share their covariates — and the published non-detections carry no numerical limit at all, so the censoring point is not even known per observation. (2) The estimand is normalised to a fixed range ("1000 km"), so the variance partition is conditional on a range/beam-response model that must itself be estimated, and errors in it load directly onto the pass-within-epoch term. (3) Fleet turnover confounds the age regressor with hardware revision: the constellation replaced v1.0 -> v1.5 -> v2-Mini -> DTC inside roughly three years, so "model held fixed" is an assumption about undocumented sub-revisions, and Mallama 2026's optical result shows what happens when it fails. None of exposure science, ecology, reliability or EPFD modelling has a ready answer to the conjunction of the three.

**Status: Resolved.** There is a real, specific, non-cosmetic problem at the interface, and I can point to the line in a primary source that creates it.

### Attack 4 — Presupposition failure

**Objection.** The question's load-bearing clause — "aggregate sky-noise forecasts built from as-launched snapshots are biased" — smuggles in two claims, and both are in trouble. (A) That forecasts treat per-satellite emission as a fixed per-model constant. Against this: arXiv:2609.01995 (abstract, opened) applies the ITU-R EPFD framework directly to measurements and reports **measurement-based EPFD distributions**, with exceedance expressed as a *fraction of samples* (50-70%) against the **2% single-system data-loss criterion of RA.1513-2** — a distributional, time-share quantity, not a mean level. And the community primer I opened says EPFD "accounts for the instantaneous positions of satellites, their time-varying pointing directions, antenna gain patterns and duty cycles" and that "The resulting EPFD value is therefore both time- and direction-dependent." So the forecasting machinery is already time-dependent and already contains a duty-cycle slot. (B) That within-satellite temporal variance would *bias* the aggregate. This is a conflation. My own argument, stated plainly: aggregate band power is a sum, S = sum_i P_i(t_i); by linearity of expectation E[S] = N*mu whatever the within-unit variance, and a cross-section of many satellites sampled at arbitrary phases of their duty cycle is a consistent estimator of mu under stationarity. Variance inflates the uncertainty of the forecast and fattens its tail; it does not bias its mean. The candidate's stated consequence — "every aggregate UEMR forecast ... is estimating the wrong quantity" — does not follow from large within-satellite variance alone.

**Best response.** (i) Checked in this candidate's actual regime, my own linearity argument cuts the other way on decision relevance: because 2609.01995's criterion is a percentage-of-samples exceedance against a 2% data-loss threshold, the decision-relevant functional is a *tail share*, not a mean — so per-satellite temporal variance and duty cycle enter the regulated quantity directly, and the candidate's concern is decision-relevant even though its word "biased" is wrong. (ii) The duty-cycle slot in EPFD is, in the text I opened, described for pointing and intended emission; the primer's own UEMR section (4.4) contains no time-, duty-cycle- or lifetime-variation treatment at all, and says only that "the emissions may not be well characterized". So the machinery exists and is not being fed a per-satellite UEMR state distribution — which is the gap the candidate names, correctly, in the wrong words. (iii) One bias channel does survive intact and I verified it myself: Grigg publishes **no numerical upper limits** for non-detections, so any level estimated from published detections is truncated below at the campaign sensitivity (~1 Jy) and is upward-biased as an estimate of the population mean — genuine bias, arising from reporting practice. (iv) A second survives conditionally: if emission trends with on-orbit age, the fleet's age distribution is non-stationary and as-launched snapshots are then biased for the mature fleet — but that is the question's own limb (b), so the premise cannot be assumed in its statement.

**Status: Partial.** A contested assumption is genuinely smuggled in (variance => bias; snapshots as fixed constants), and one of my two counter-sources is a current paper doing distributional, measurement-based EPFD. But the presupposition is repairable by rewording rather than fatal, and the underlying gap — no per-satellite UEMR state or age distribution anywhere in the time-dependent machinery — is confirmed by the two documents I opened rather than merely asserted. Required amendment: delete "biased" as a blanket claim; name the channel (truncation at unpublished thresholds; non-stationary fleet age; non-representative state sampling) and state that the decision-relevant functional is an exceedance share under RA.1513-2, not a mean. Not Unresolved, which would have been fatal.

### Attack 5 — Vagueness (answerable no matter what the evidence shows, or never?)

**Objection.** As canonically worded, "does it vary over that satellite's operational life" is unlosable: every finite measurement series has non-zero variance, so "varies" is guaranteed; and any null can be charged to insufficient power or to pass sampling. Limb (c) is worse than vague — it is instrument-relative. "Genuinely below detection" is not a property of a satellite but of a (satellite, instrument, geometry, epoch) tuple; since Grigg's own text ties received strength to closest approach, the fraction below a limit can be moved by pass-geometry sampling alone. And "operational life" bundles calendar age, thermal-cycle count, payload loading state and software version, so whatever is estimated cannot be named.

**Best response.** The reformulation removes most of this: the estimand is a *ratio* of variance components (epoch-within-satellite against between-model), not the existence of variance; the age test is specified with model held fixed; limb (c) is specified against "a stated, sensitivity-matched per-satellite upper limit", which converts the instrument-relative quantity into a well-defined comparator (below the level at which this unit was previously detected, at matched range and elevation). A null is explicitly publishable at stated power, so the design can lose. The bundling objection is conceded in the record itself and bounded: the design measures variance it does not attribute, and attribution is listed as downstream fecundity, not as a deliverable.

**Status: Partial.** The reformulation is a well-posed estimation problem, but two stated conditions remain undefined and are load-bearing: "sensitivity-matched" has no operational definition without a per-pass detection-efficiency and beam-geometry model, and "comparable to between-model variance" has no pre-specified decision threshold. Both are flagged in the record's own well-posedness note; neither is fixed. Required amendment: pre-specify a minimum passes-per-satellite-per-epoch, a per-pass detection-efficiency model, and the variance-ratio threshold that would count as a positive, before data.

### Attack 6 — Significance inflation (is the audience real, would they act on either outcome?)

**Objection.** The record's consequence paragraph is overdrawn, and in one specific way: if the regulated quantity is already a percentage-of-time data-loss criterion (2% per RA.1513-2, as used in 2609.01995), then a finding of large per-satellite temporal variance is partly *absorbed* by machinery that already integrates over time and over the constellation. A body that already works in exceedance fractions does not have to "restate every forecast". And the stable-outcome branch triggers no action at all: it vindicates the status quo.

**Best response.** The audience is real and is, right now, asking for the thing the answer would shape. 2609.01995's abstract frames measurement-based EPFD as "a practical route to verifying compliance and monitoring over the long term"; the Quiet Skies primer's UEMR section says operators and astronomers "can develop strategies to monitor and mitigate such effects in orbit" while characterising UEMR as possibly "not well characterized" and recommending only pre-launch EMC testing. That is a community committing to long-term in-orbit monitoring with no per-emitter temporal model to design it against. The concrete action difference is in the monitoring specification, not in rewriting forecasts: whether a compliance regime can certify a unit from one pass, or must sample states to a percentile — which bears directly on the per-satellite limits being proposed (2608.11659, which I did not open and do not grade). The stable branch does act, modestly: it retires the non-detection anomaly, licenses one-off campaign designs as sufficient deliverables, and makes a single-number per-satellite limit defensible.

**Status: Partial.** Audience confirmed real and actively building the dependent infrastructure; the yes-branch has a concrete decision attached. But the record's claim that every aggregate forecast would be "estimating the wrong quantity" overstates the consequence given an already-distributional criterion, and the no-branch action is thin.

### Attack 7 — Reason for silence

**Objection.** A question this cheap to ask of an existing public catalogue, with a published anomaly as a hook, should have been asked. "Disciplinary boundary" is doing a lot of work.

**Best response, with a mechanism I evidenced myself.** Three mechanisms, two of them stronger than the record's. (1) *The anomaly already has a published, sufficient-looking explanation.* I verified that Grigg et al. do not leave the 68/55 non-detections hanging: they attribute them to emission "below the EDA2's sensitivity level in these observations", about 1 Jy. A plausible local explanation removes the puzzle, so no one treats it as an anomaly requiring test — this is an incentive mechanism, not an oversight one, and it also corrects the record, which frames the anomaly as reported-but-unexplained. (2) *A conceptual boundary visible inside a single document.* In the Quiet Skies primer, the EPFD section carries the full time-dependent apparatus — "antenna gain patterns and duty cycles", "both time- and direction-dependent" — while the UEMR section a page later contains no temporal framing whatsoever and routes mitigation to pre-launch EMC testing. The field's own primer puts UEMR on the hardware-characterisation side of a line and intended emission on the statistical side; that line is the silence. (3) *Data recency.* The only catalogue in which identified units carry tens of repeat detections dates from mid-2024 observations published 2025, so a second epoch separated by a year could not yet have been published by anyone starting from it; my sweep in "temporal evolution / longitudinal" vocabulary found none.

**Status: Resolved.** Named mechanisms, at least two of which I evidenced from primary text I opened, and none of which is "no one thought of it".

### Attack 8 — Dissolution

**Objection.** Write the obvious model, P_i(t) = f(model, unit, operating state(t), geometry, age), and the question evaporates: nobody holds the "fixed property of hardware model" horn once the model is written down, and the dichotomy in the title was never a live disagreement — it is an artefact of how survey papers report (one level per generation) rather than a claim anyone defends. SNIFFLES I's 76.9%-of-observations presence rate and Grigg's time-varying spectral structure mean the dissolution has already happened in print. What remains is parameter estimation in a model everyone would accept on sight: not a question, a to-do.

**Best response.** Dissolving the dichotomy does not dissolve three quantities, and each changes a published conclusion. (1) The ratio of epoch-within-satellite to between-model variance decides whether Bassa et al.'s 15 dB (factor 32) generational contrast estimates a hardware property or is partly an operating-state sampling artefact — a live interpretive stake in a 2024 A&A Letter, not a to-do. (2) The sign and size of the age coefficient with model fixed is an empirical fact nobody has, and it discriminates mechanisms for the field's own stated open problem: a synchronised fleet-wide step implicates software, a per-unit monotone trend implicates degradation, a flat result implicates fixed layout — and Grigg states plainly that "the specific component of the satellite that causes this unintended emission is unknown". (3) The censoring-corrected fraction of previously-detected units now bounded below their earlier level is unobtainable from current reporting, because I verified no numerical non-detection limits are published.

**Status: Partial.** The headline framing does dissolve, and the record should stop carrying it. The reformulated estimands do not dissolve; they are estimates with named consequences for specific published numbers.

## 5. Closing: the single strongest surviving objection

The objection I could not dispose of is about the question's consequence clause and its minimal design, taken together. The claim that "aggregate sky-noise forecasts built from as-launched snapshots are biased" is not derived: within-satellite temporal variance does not bias an aggregate mean, and the one bias channel I could actually evidence — that Grigg et al. publish no numerical upper limit for any of the 68 non-detections, so levels estimated from detections are truncated at roughly the 1 Jy campaign sensitivity — indicts survey *reporting practice*, not satellite ageing, and could be fixed by a reporting convention rather than by a new campaign. Meanwhile the forecasting side is moving away from the assumption the candidate attacks: arXiv:2609.01995 already computes measurement-based EPFD distributions and scores them as an exceedance share against the 2% data-loss criterion of RA.1513-2, which is a distributional quantity, not a per-model constant. And a substantial part of the candidate's own limbs is already purchasable from existing public data: 112,534 detections of 1,806 identified satellites across a four-month window, roughly 62 detections per satellite by my arithmetic, with launch dates available, supports the pass-within-epoch variance term and a cross-sectional age contrast today. So what survives the attack is a narrower candidate than the one written: the genuinely unanswered part is the epoch-separated within-unit term, net of launch-cohort composition, plus the bounded non-detection fraction; and the honest first deliverable is a re-analysis of the existing catalogue, not the two-epoch campaign the record proposes. The candidate survives, but only in its Gate 1 reformulation, with the word "biased" replaced by a named channel and with the new campaign justified only by what the catalogue cannot identify.
