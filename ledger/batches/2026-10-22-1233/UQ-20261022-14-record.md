Question (canonical) — V2, after the one Gate 1 reformulation:
Over a declared frame of SWOT-observed reservoirs and lakes whose operator publishes both a water-level record and a DOCUMENTED datum (a named vertical datum, or a published conversion to one), and whose operating rule states a flood-pool, spillway-crest or freeboard threshold: (i) what is the distribution across the frame of the per-water-body residual r = (SWOT geoid-referenced water-surface elevation) − (operator-reported level transformed to the same geopotential datum by the operator's OWN stated conversion), with its uncertainty; (ii) is r stationary over the mission record — tested for steps and trend, against an independent local vertical-motion constraint where one exists; and (iii) within each water-body size class for which SWOT's stated WSE accuracy is smaller than the stated operating margin, for what share of the frame does |r| exceed that margin?

Question (as submitted) — V1, recorded per the Gate 1 rule:
"Over a declared frame of reservoirs and lakes that publish operator gauge levels and are observed by SWOT, what is the distribution of the offset between the operator's reported water level in its own local datum and the SWOT-measured geoid-referenced water-surface elevation, is that offset stationary over the mission record, and for what share of the frame does the offset exceed the margin stated in the structure's own operating rule (flood-pool, spillway-crest or freeboard threshold)?"

Why V1 was reformulated (well-posedness only, no scope change intended): V1's measurand is an undecomposable mixture. "The offset between a level in its own local datum and a geoid-referenced elevation" is not an error until a conversion is declared: with no stated datum the difference is a number of unknown composition (true datum error + the operator's conversion + geoid-model difference + altimeter bias + hydraulic sampling difference). V2 (a) makes the residual auditable by defining it against the operator's own published conversion, so a non-zero r is a defect in a chain somebody owns; (b) makes a documented datum an inclusion criterion, which converts presupposition 1 from an assumption into a frame-construction step whose yield is itself a reportable number; (c) restricts limb (iii) by size class to where the instrument's stated accuracy is below the margin, per presupposition 2. V1 and V2 ask the same thing; V2 can be executed without the answer depending on an unstated convention.

Alternative formulations:

Motivating gap (as revised by what Gate 2 opened; the intake's version was explicitly unchecked — "NO SOURCE OPENED AND NO QUERY RUN"):
The intake's claim was that thirty years of altimetry-vs-gauge comparison estimates the datum offset and REMOVES it, leaving it unreported as a measurand. Gate 2 found this HALF TRUE and in the more interesting way. The convention is real and is stated outright in an opened SWOT validation paper: Köhn & Nielsen (2026) "remove the median offset between SWOT and the gauge on a per–lake–basis, before reporting any metric" and say "investigating the origin of the removed offset lies beyond the scope of this study." But the offsets are not unreported — that paper PRINTS them, per lake, in an appendix column (Table A1, column b−), as the residue of a nuisance step rather than as a result. So the gap is not that the numbers do not exist; it is that they are produced as nuisance, in a form that is not decomposable (the paper does not state how the gauge datum DVR90 and the SWOT reference EGM2008 were reconciled), over frames chosen for altimeter validation rather than for operational consequence, with no stationarity test ("the constant offset per lake"), and never compared with the margin in the structure's own operating rule. The two neighbouring fields that would own the decomposed quantity have each done one limb in a different regime: tide-gauge quality control detects reference-level steps from altimetry (Ray et al. 2023), and gauge-datum harmonisation measures per-gauge offsets by GNSS campaign on one basin at a time (USGS SIR 2016-5109, on two lakes regulated under IJC rule curves). Nothing opened joins the decomposed residual, its time behaviour, and the operating-rule threshold over a declared population.

If the frame collapses — too few operators publish a documented datum and a stated margin — that yield is itself the finding, and it is the finding that most directly indicts current practice, because it says the audit cannot be run on public documents.
