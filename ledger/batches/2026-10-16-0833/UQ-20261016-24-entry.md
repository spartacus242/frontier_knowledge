Domain(s): STEM — quantum computing (cloud-accessible superconducting/trapped-ion processors); metascience of hardware benchmarking; measurement/metrology of reported results

Question (canonical): For a pre-specified corpus of papers reporting results executed on named cloud-accessible quantum processors, and a pre-specified per-device figure of merit F built from archived calibration fields and stable across a declared comparability window (same hardware revision, same recalibration methodology): (a) what fraction of published results can be located in time to the archive's granularity, and what fraction of those fall inside a window the archive still covers; and (b) among the locatable-and-covered results, is the percentile rank of F at the reported execution window — within a pre-specified symmetric comparison window (e.g. ±30 days, truncated at revision boundaries) — uniform on [0,1] or shifted towards favourable ranks, and by how much, reported both as a shift in expected device quality and, on a pre-declared monotone subset of result types, as the implied shift in the headline number? (Gate 1 reformulation, the single permitted one; the as-intaken version is in the record.)

Alternative formulations:
- Do quantum-computing papers report runs made when the machine happened to be good, and can that selection be measured from the vendor's own calibration history?
- What is the percentile rank, within a device's own recent calibration record, of the device state behind each published result obtained on it?

Motivating gap: New-instrument candidate. Vendors publish per-qubit calibration data (T1, T2, gate and readout errors) nominally retrievable for past dates, while papers name the backend and often a date; drift over hours to days is well characterised (arXiv:2105.09472; arXiv:2208.07219; IEEE DataPort temporal-error study; caslab.io/publications/xu2023long.pdf), and error-aware layout / just-in-time transpilation (arXiv:2606.27508; arXiv:2005.12820; Cirq "Qubit Picking") exist to exploit the current calibration state — sensible practice that is also a selection mechanism on the published record. Gate 2 found the normative half already stated in-field (arXiv:2411.08860, Principle 4: "from which best results are selected post hoc") and Gate 4 found the sensitivity half quantified in-field (arXiv:2605.29872: the same ZNE configuration's effect size >3x larger "depending solely on when it is executed"), but no archive-to-literature join and no selection estimand. The gap is narrower than intaken: the worry and the mechanism are on record; the quantity is not.

Presuppositions:
- P1 (CONTESTED, load-bearing, now evidenced as failing in part): historical calibration data are retrievable for the periods the published results fall in. Gate 4 evidence: IBM documents `backend.properties(datetime=...)` ("timestamp closest to, but older than, the specified datetime") and CSV calibration download, with no retention depth stated anywhere; the current API reference raises `NotImplementedError` "If datetime is specified when IBM Quantum Compute is used"; `IBMRetiredBackend.properties()` is stubbed to return `None` (Qiskit/qiskit-ibm-runtime issue #2255, open since 2025-05-08, no maintainer reply; earlier #1069, #1127 record `datetime` unsupported on the cloud channel in 2023). Third-party mirrors are sporadic snapshots (GitHub repo search: 0 systematic mirrors; `fake_provider` one-off `props_<device>.json` files dominate); the one mirror opened covers "90 days" on four unnamed IBM machines (Zenodo 4898393).
- P2 (CONTESTED): papers report enough detail to locate a run in time. The locatable fraction is itself a reportable quantity. arXiv:2607.08348 (127 manual + ~5,000 automated papers) carries a "hardware description" indicator but, in fetched text from two independent adversaries, reports no backend-named / date-given / calibration-snapshot fraction — the number is genuinely not in hand. Supporting: many hardware papers do print a run-time calibration table for the qubits used (A1/S2), so the fraction is plausibly non-trivial.
- P3 (CONTESTED): a time-comparable F can be built from the archived fields; recalibration methodology, qubit retirement and revisions change what fields mean, so comparability windows must be pre-specified and truncated at revision boundaries.
- P4: the null is well defined — absent selection, run-time ranks are uniform. Holds by construction only if (i) the window is fixed before any rank is computed, (ii) calendar time is exchangeable within it, and (iii) F is not selected on the run day's own snapshot. Adversary 3 raised (iii) as an unflagged defect: F as written (median two-qubit error over the qubits actually used) is biased towards favourable ranks under the null, because those qubits were picked on the run-day snapshot and a quantity selected on a noisy estimate regresses to the mean on other days. Status per the adversary's own labelling: its own unsourced reasoning, scoped to snapshot-selected subsets, vanishing for a device-level aggregate or a pre-fixed subset — so Partial, not fatal, and a fallback F removes it.
- P5: headline numbers depend monotonically on F for at least a subset of result types (supported for one class by Senapati et al., IEEE QCE 2023, on their own runs). The monotone subset is still undeclared on any named result class.
- P6 (NOT presupposed, stated to block a misreading): that any selection is misconduct or that re-runs were concealed.

What would count as an answer: A pre-registered pipeline over a named paper sample and named backends reporting, per result, the percentile rank of F at the reported run window within the pre-specified comparison window; test is uniformity of those ranks (KS or one-sided mean-rank with a pre-stated effect size), reported as a shift in expected device quality and, on the declared monotone subset, as the implied shift in the headline number. "Ranks uniform" is a full answer (though attenuated: it bounds the effect within the date-reporting subset, measured through a daily-snapshot proxy, rather than retiring the concern). The locatable-and-covered fraction is part of the answer. A demonstration that archive retention does not reach the literature is a feasibility verdict, not an answer.

Significance — consequence: If ranks shift high, the output is a quantified correction factor on the published NISQ record with a cheap attached reform (report run timestamp plus contemporaneous calibration snapshot), and an archive-based account of poor cross-group replication that currently rests on assertion plus one campaign's re-measurement. Strongest remaining objection: **Most of the literature the question is about ran on devices whose calibration history is now unreachable.** Corrected on the judge's post-Gate-5 verification (2026-10-16, independent fresh-context technical check, 22 queries, 0 refusals), because the record's version of this objection was wrong in its first clause. By-date retrieval IS implemented for currently-live IBM QPUs on the qiskit-ibm-runtime channel: `backend.properties(datetime=...)` maps to the REST parameter `updated_before`, is documented under "Retrieve historical data" in IBM's QPU-information guide, and is exercised by a live CI integration test as of release 0.50.0 (2026-09-24). The record's claim that it raises `NotImplementedError` on the current channel is withdrawn. Two real limits remain, and they carry the objection. (a) For **retired** devices there is no path at all: `IBMRetiredBackend.properties()` is a stub returning None, open issue #2255 (filed 2025-05-08) asks for exactly this capability and is still open, and IBM's retired-QPU guide offers only job retrieval — no bulk download, no published archive; the `fake_provider` snapshots cannot substitute because they carry no date. Since most published IBM hardware results ran on devices now retired, the corpus that motivates the question is largely out of reach. (b) The retained history depth is nowhere documented, no public REST reference lists `updated_before`, and the only CI coverage uses `datetime=today`, so the usable window is unverified — and that single number decides whether the live-device half of the study is viable at all. Two further corrections for a successor's design: IonQ **does** expose dated per-characterization history by REST, so any framing in which no vendor publishes dated calibration history would be wrong; and AWS Braket is the clearest negative, stating outright that historic calibration data is unavailable for all its devices. Well-posedness stays at 2/3 on this corrected basis: the question is answerable in principle and for live devices, and largely unanswerable for the literature it is about.
Promotion requires: (i) a documented by-date retrieval path — live-device history depth on the current IBM Quantum Platform plus at least one other vendor, or a named mirror (Zenodo 4898393, IEEE DataPort, caslab) — whose device/date coverage is shown to intersect a named paper corpus, with the locatable-and-covered fraction reported first (this is what would lift well-posedness to 3/3); (ii) the primary F replaced by a selection-blind construction (device-level aggregate or pre-fixed qubit subset) so the null is exact for the headline estimand, and F shown comparable across at least one documented recalibration-methodology change; (iii) a named result class with a pre-declared monotone F-to-headline mapping, plus the reserved-versus-open-queue contrast carried in the same pipeline so the composite is partly decomposed (this is what would lift significance to 3/3). Novelty is also a standing ceiling: with 2605.29872 and 2411.08860 in-field, Medium is the best available and re-verification is due if either group publishes the archive join itself.

Suggested first step toward an answer (pointer only — do not take it): settle the retention window before anything else — an audit, not a study. Only if a usable window exists does the join to published run dates become worth specifying.
