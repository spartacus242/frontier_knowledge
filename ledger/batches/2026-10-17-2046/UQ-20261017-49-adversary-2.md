## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take a web snapshot you can pin by date (one Common Crawl dump). Take one of the model-based "is this
document good enough to pretrain on" classifiers that real corpora ship — FineWeb-Edu's educational-value
scorer, DCLM's fastText quality classifier, Nemotron-CC's ensemble — and run it at the threshold its
authors ship. Now ask a provenance question about what it kept: of the documents it kept, what share were
written by a machine, and how does that share compare with the share among the documents it started from?
The ratio of those two shares is the thing being asked for. Because the only way to label provenance at
scale is an imperfect detector, the question insists the two shares be corrected for the detector's
sensitivity and specificity, with those two numbers measured on hand-labelled documents drawn separately
out of the kept pile and the thrown-away pile (so that a detector that misfires specifically on the kind
of fluent text the filter likes cannot fake the result), and that the interval on the ratio carry that
correction through. Then: turn the same filter's own dial to keep less and less, and ask whether the ratio
climbs as the dial tightens. Repeat per snapshot date.

In one sentence: do pretraining quality filters concentrate AI-written text, by how much, measured with a
screening-epidemiology correction rather than raw classifier counts, and does the concentration get worse
the pickier the filter gets?

The reason it is supposed to matter: the model-collapse literature parameterises recursive-training risk
by a synthetic mixing fraction, and models see the post-filter set, not the web.

## 3. My own arithmetic (labelled as mine, from the paper's printed numbers)

(a) **The sign limb needs no base rate at all.** With pass rates s_A and s_H for AI- and human-labelled
documents and pre-filter AI share p, the enrichment ratio is
E = p_post/p_pre = s_A / (p·s_A + (1-p)·s_H) = RR / (1 + p(RR-1)), RR = s_A/s_H.
For RR > 1 and p < 1, the denominator 1 + p(RR-1) < RR, so **E > 1 strictly**. The paper prints
RR = 29.3/12.8 = 2.29 and RR = 14.5/1.5 = 9.67. So "is E > 1 at the shipped keep rate" is settled by two
printed numbers and one line of algebra, for any base rate whatever. (My derivation; the paper's own
conclusion states it in words.)

(b) **Magnitude.** Back-solving p from 0.275 = p·0.293 / (p·0.293 + (1-p)·0.128) gives p = 0.1422, hence
E = 1.94 (FineWeb pipeline) and E = 4.36 (DCLM full pipeline). Carried caveat, which is the paper's not
mine: 27.5% is a **token** share while 29.3%/12.8% are **document** shares, so this back-solve mixes units
and the point values should be read as order-of-magnitude only. The sign conclusion in (a) does not depend
on it.

(c) **The candidate's headline methodological differentiator is numerically inert in this regime.**
Rogan-Gladen with the paper's reported operating point (Se = 1 - 0.0199 = 0.9801, Sp = 1 - 0.0005 = 0.9995):
corrected = (0.275 + 0.9995 - 1)/(0.9801 + 0.9995 - 1) = 0.2745/0.9796 = 0.2802. So 27.5% → 28.0%, a
0.5-point move. Worse for the candidate: applying the same correction to the back-solved pool share,
(0.1422 - 0.0005)/0.9796 = 0.1447, moves it by +1.7% relatively while the kept share moves +1.9% — so in
the **ratio** the correction very nearly cancels, changing E by well under 1%.
**Scope check, which is where this claim could have been wrong:** a Rogan-Gladen correction is NOT always
small. At Sp = 0.9995 and a prevalence near the FPR it dominates — on the paper's own 2021 control,
0.062% observed corrects to (0.00062 - 0.0005)/0.9796 = 0.012%, a five-fold change. So the correction
matters in low-prevalence strata. But the candidate's two prevalences are both in the 14-28% band, two
orders of magnitude above Sp's complement, and the published direction of the effect is that tightening
the filter *raises* AI prevalence — i.e. the threshold ladder runs *away* from the regime where the
correction bites. The inertness claim therefore holds specifically in the regime the candidate occupies.
**Uncertainty carried:** Se and Sp are vendor-reported point estimates with no interval in what I read,
and the 0.062% control is footnoted as an upper bound; the inertness result is conditional on those
numbers being approximately right and non-differential, which is exactly presupposition P6.
