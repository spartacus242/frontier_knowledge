## Restatement (my words, not the generator's)

Patients on a chronic self-administered drug take their doses more faithfully in the few days before a scheduled clinic visit. If the outcome biomarker responds fast enough, the reading taken at the visit reflects that pre-visit surge rather than the patient's usual dosing. The candidate asks for a number: what fraction of a trial's visit-measured treatment effect (per arm and in the arm contrast) is produced by the visit-locked surge, computed by taking each patient's electronic dosing record, spreading the same number of doses evenly across the inter-visit interval, pushing both the real and the smoothed record through a pre-declared exposure-response model, and reporting the signed difference against a pre-declared margin, with HbA1c (slow biomarker) as the negative control.

## My own arithmetic (labelled; not any source's conclusion)

Using only P1's figures as the record reports them: overall adherence 85.5 ± 21.2 -> 88.5 ± 23.2 (Pre14-4 -> Pre3-1), i.e. a 3.0 pp surge on an 85.5% base; optimal subgroup 94.0 ± 11.7 -> 97.7 ± 7.4, a 3.7 pp surge on a 94.0% base. Under the exposure-response shape most favourable to the candidate's bias — linear, lag-free, with the arm effect proportional to adherence — the visit-locked component is at most the surge as a fraction of mean adherence: 3.0/85.5 ≈ 3.5% (overall) or 3.7/94.0 ≈ 3.9% (optimal subgroup) of the arm effect. Uncertainty: P1 reports per-window SDs of 21-23 pp; the paired SD of the within-patient change is not available to me, so I can carry only the record's p-values (0.01, 0.001) as evidence the surge is non-zero, not an interval on the fraction. Any pharmacodynamic lag lowers the fraction: for a first-order approach with time constant tau and a surge of duration d, the share of the surge visible at the visit is 1 - exp(-d/tau); with d = 3 days (P1's Pre3-1 window) and tau = 21 days this is ≈ 13%. Saturation at 94-98% adherence lowers it further. Threshold-type responses could raise it only in suboptimal adherers, in whom P1 found no surge (p = 0.69, 0.32). I have NOT sourced any biomarker's time constant, so which named biomarkers fall into the slow regime is my judgment, not a finding.
