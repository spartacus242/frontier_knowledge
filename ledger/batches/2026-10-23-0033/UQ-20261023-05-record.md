## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (canonical, V1 — as submitted):
Over the published phylogenetic record, what is the realized rate at which a clade reported at >=95% support (nonparametric bootstrap, UFBoot or posterior probability) is contradicted at >=95% support by a later independent study of the same taxa, and is that mutual-contradiction rate — which lower-bounds the error rate of the >=95% label — consistent with what the label nominally means?

Alternative formulations:
- Have phylogenetic support values ever been verified against outcomes, the way a weather forecast is verified?
- How often do two papers both claim >=95% certainty for incompatible trees of the same taxa?

Motivating gap: a how-dense / why-empty field. Support values are computed on essentially every published tree and have a large theoretical and simulation literature, but their realized reliability AS PUBLISHED — the label on a paper's branch, under that paper's own data, model and software — has never been verified against the published record. The field's validation culture measures support against a known or reference truth; the candidate's comparator is another published study and needs no truth at all.

## Gate 1 — Well-posedness, with the one permitted reformulation

Two defects in V1, both about decidability rather than interest:
1. "a later **independent** study" makes independence a definitional precondition. The intake's own presupposition list says independence cannot be assumed (shared loci, alignments, software, authors) and must be handled by stratification. As written, V1 requires a decidable independence partition it does not supply.
2. "consistent with what the label nominally means" is underdetermined: a bootstrap proportion, a UFBoot value and a posterior probability do not share a nominal meaning, so "consistent" has no referent until one is declared per method.

Question (canonical, V2 — the one reformulation, used hereafter):
For a pre-declared frame of pairs of published phylogenetic studies sharing at least k taxa after a declared taxonomic-name reconciliation, what is the realized rate at which a clade published at >=95% support is incompatible, on the shared taxon set and under a declared bipartition-conflict test, with a clade published at >=95% support in the other study of the pair — reported separately by support type, stratified by a declared study-to-study data-overlap measure and by era, as a lower bound on the joint error of the two >=95% labels — and how does that rate compare with a per-method nominal benchmark declared in advance?

WHAT THE REPAIR ITSELF PRESUPPOSES (tested as hard as V1, per the standing rule):
- V2 requires a **declared data-overlap measure computable from what papers report** (shared loci/alignments/genes). This is a new document-kind premise that V1 did not carry. I did NOT verify it. The five deposited studies I opened (below) carry a publication reference but I did not establish that locus identity is machine-recoverable from them. **UNVERIFIED — flagged, not credited.**
- V2 requires a non-circular per-method nominal benchmark. For posterior probability this is straightforward. For the nonparametric bootstrap it is contestable, and a source I opened in full says so: Huang, Liu, Zhu & Yang 2020 establish that in the equally-right / equally-wrong regime the bootstrap probability has no point limit (P1 ~ U(0,1) across datasets), so there is no single frequentist quantity a bootstrap label nominally equals. **So the comparison limb is clean for posterior probability and must be declared contested for bootstrap.** This is a real cost of the repair, and it is the strongest internal weakness of V2.
- V2's gain: it no longer presupposes a decidable independence partition (independence becomes a stratifier, which is a measured covariate, not a precondition), and the reported statistic is explicitly the PAIR-level joint-error lower bound rather than a per-label rate, so the arithmetic step is stated as arithmetic.

Presuppositions (status after Gate 2 work):

Partially supported: the nearest between-study prior I opened (bioRxiv 10.1101/123034) does run exactly such a test operationally (reciprocal tip-set overlap on rooted bipartitions), so the conflict half is demonstrably implementable at corpus scale.

4. Support labels from different methods are not interchangeable; analysed separately as primary, one axis only under a declared crosswalk. FLAGGED — and reinforced by the document-kind check: in 0 of 5 deposited studies was the support TYPE declared in metadata, so the crosswalk would have to be inferred from the number's range.
5. Mutual contradiction lower-bounds error (at most one of two incompatible resolutions can be right). Arithmetic, not an assumption; bounds the PAIR's joint error, so a per-label rate needs a declared allocation. FLAGGED and now written into V2's statistic.

What would count as an answer:
An extracted corpus with explicit counts: for every pair of studies sharing >=k taxa, the number of clades at >=95% in one that are incompatible with a >=95% clade in the other, over the number of comparable clade pairs; a reliability curve of contradiction rate against the support label, stratified by support type, data overlap and era; and a sensitivity analysis over the name-reconciliation and conflict rules. A negative answer is a contradiction rate at or below the few-percent level the label implies. **An answer is also reached, differently, if the corpus premise fails: the finding that the published record cannot be audited because support is not deposited with topology is an answer, and must be reported as an availability/granularity gap and NOT as a small contradiction rate.**

## Gate 3 — Significance

**Consequence (argued).** If the realized mutual-contradiction rate at >=95% is materially above the few percent the label implies, then a quantity used as a gate in thousands of downstream analyses is miscalibrated in a measurable, correctable way — and the correction is immediate, because the result is a reliability curve, which maps a published label to an empirical frequency. Every comparative-methods analysis that conditions on a published topology without propagating topological uncertainty inherits a quantified error term it currently treats as zero. If the rate is at or below the nominal level, that is a positive calibration result for the field's most criticised statistic, and it reframes the hard-incongruence literature as a story about a thin tail rather than a systemic flaw. If instead the corpus premise fails — the outcome my own five-study check makes more likely — the finding is that the field's published support labels are not auditable from its own archives, which is an actionable claim about infrastructure rather than about trees.
**Audience (named, and currently active).** (i) Support-method developers: two papers I opened in full, five years apart, are explicitly motivated by "strong support for wrong trees" (Huang et al. 2020) and "overly confident support for incorrect inferences" (Sharma & Kumar 2025), and both validate against simulated or reference truth because that is the comparator available to them. A realized-record figure gives them a target their simulations currently have no way to calibrate against. (ii) Comparative-methods users, who would gain a defensible prior for topology-uncertainty propagation. (iii) Biodiversity-informatics curators (Open Tree, TreeBASE): the granularity gap I documented — support present on 1 of 5 opened studies, support type declared on 0 of 5 — is a concrete, cheap-to-fix curation requirement, and an availability answer hands them the argument for it.
**Fecundity.** Does the contradiction rate fall as data scale grows (Huang et al. predicts it need not)? Does it differ by support type once the crosswalk is declared, which would be an empirical test of the interchangeability that presupposition 4 denies? A Brier decomposition of published labels into reliability and resolution. Whether data-overlap stratification separates "two looks at one dataset" from genuine replication. Whether contradiction rate predicts eventual taxonomic revision — i.e. whether the bound tracks anything the field later acts on.
**Anchoring.** The "well-supported but conflicting topologies" anomaly asserted in Arcila et al. 2021's abstract and across the hard-incongruence literature; Huang et al. 2020's asymptotic non-convergence result; the phylogenetic-data-availability problem (Drew et al. 2013, title-level only here); and, outside biology, the realized-error-rate-of-stated-uncertainty tradition in physics and reliability engineering.
**Significance verdict: 3/3.** Both answer branches change behaviour for a named, currently active community.

## Reason for silence

A **disciplinary boundary plus an instrument/incentive gap**, not an absence of ideas. Two mechanisms, both evidenced in this record. First, systematics validates support against truth: every support-assessment paper I opened compares support with a simulated true tree or a reference tree, because that is the comparator the field's validation culture recognises. The candidate's comparator — another published study — needs no truth at all, and the machinery for using mutual disagreement as an error bound lives in forecast verification and measurement science, where it is routine and where my query 9 found it mature (Henrion & Fischhoff 1986; Shlyakhter 2008) and never pointed at phylogenetics. A question is invisible when the only field that owns its method does not own its data. Second, the audit object has been largely unavailable in machine-readable form: support values are not deposited with topology. That is not speculation here — it is what four of the five deposited studies I opened show, with the type-declaring metadata field empty in all five. So the audit was expensive-to-impossible for most of the period whose record it would audit, and the people positioned to notice are rewarded for proposing better support measures (two such papers in this record) rather than for grading the label the field already prints.
