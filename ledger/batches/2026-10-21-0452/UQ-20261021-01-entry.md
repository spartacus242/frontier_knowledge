Question (canonical): V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted; scored as canonical) — In >=2 independent cohorts or trials holding per-patient time-stamped electronic dosing records over whole inter-visit intervals and a visit-measured biomarker whose pharmacodynamic response time is shorter than that interval (BP, LDL, HIV RNA, IOP, INR), under one pre-declared exposure-response model per biomarker with >=2 model forms as sensitivity: what is the signed difference, per randomised arm and in the arm contrast, between the treatment effect as the visit-measured analysis reports it and the effect predicted when each patient's realised doses are redistributed uniformly across the interval (dose-count-preserving; visit-entrained component removed, total exposure fixed), expressed as a fraction of the observed arm difference against a pre-declared margin, with HbA1c as the pre-specified falsification arm in which the difference must vanish?
V1 (as intaken) differs only in specification, not in object; both texts in batches/2026-10-21-0452/UQ-20261021-01-record.md.

Alternative formulations:
- How much of a drug's measured effect is an artefact of patients taking it more faithfully in the days before the visit at which the effect is measured?
- Treating the visit calendar as a cause of the exposure used to evaluate it, what is the visit-entrainment bias in the ITT estimate, and does it differ between arms?

Motivating gap:
Source method: anomaly mining. The anomaly is established on a primary source read at full text and independently re-read by one adversary: Poleon, Sabbagh & Racette (Front Med 2022;9:867884; 66 POAG patients, MEMS caps, 28 days bracketing one visit) report adherence rising 85.5 -> 88.5% from Pre14-4 to Pre3-1 (p = 0.01) overall and 94.0 -> 97.7% (p = 0.001) in the optimal-adherence subgroup (n = 49), with no pattern in suboptimal adherers (p = 0.69). Two things verification adds. The surge is carried by the already-adherent only BEFORE the visit: after it the full sample falls (87.0 +/- 23.9 -> 84.9 +/- 23.3, p = 0.02) while the optimal subgroup RISES (95.2 +/- 12.0 -> 95.4 +/- 5.7, p = 0.007), and the paper says in terms that "patients with optimal adherence showed an increase in adherence both prior to and after the clinic visit", so no subgroup reproduces the full-sample decline and the suboptimal group's own fall (63.2 -> 54.7, -8.5 pp, p = 0.32) is attributed by the authors to n = 17 and high variability rather than to absence. Separately, an anomaly in the source itself, reproduced not corrected: that +0.2 pp optimal-subgroup post-visit change carries p = 0.007 with the SD more than halving, printed identically in the Abstract, the Results body and Table 2. The SDs matter here: every mean is +/- 21 to 24 points against shifts of 2 to 3 points, and the 88.5-to-84.9 pair across the visit is never tested. The entrainment is owned as a patient trait ("whitecoat adherence") and corrected out of the ADHERENCE estimate; it is not posed as a property of the measurement schedule that biases the EFFICACY estimate. Correction owed to adversary 2's re-read: in the optimal subgroup the post-visit comparison is 95.2 -> 95.4, a slight increase, so the full rise-then-fall cycle is supported only on the pre-visit side in the subgroup carrying the effect — which is the side the bias limb needs. Significance — consequence:
If the entrained component is material in fast-biomarker indications, the visit schedule stops being a logistical parameter and becomes an efficacy-relevant design parameter: part of what a registration trial estimates is a property of its own calendar, which no label reproduces. Non-inferiority margins and dose-ranging slopes calibrated on visit-measured effects would be mis-scaled; part of the efficacy-effectiveness gap would be mechanical schedule geometry rather than a fact about patients; and an E9(R1) estimand would have to declare whether it is defined under the trial's visit schedule, for which the five attributes have no slot. If the answer is no, a plausible threat to every fast-biomarker endpoint is retired on evidence instead of by silence. Adversary 3's correction, accepted: the efficacy-effectiveness limb is weaker than the record states, because routine care also has visits, so only the trial-versus-practice difference in visit frequency can enter that gap.
