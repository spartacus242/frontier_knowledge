Domain(s): Biology — experimental/microbial evolution (target field, whose literature decides novelty). Method transplanted from community ecology (competitive intransitivity) and paired-comparison/tournament statistics (Bradley-Terry, Elo, Hodge/HHD).

Question (canonical): In a microbial long-term evolution experiment with an archived clone series, does the matrix of pairwise competitive-fitness measurements among archived clones admit a consistent one-dimensional ordering — how often are triples intransitive, and at what magnitudes relative to assay error — and does the published power-law deceleration of fitness gain survive re-estimation under a model that does not assume fitness is a scalar?

Alternative formulations:
- Is "fitness" in an evolution experiment a scalar, or a projection of a competition tournament onto one axis, and what does that projection do to the measured shape of adaptation?
- Transplanting the measurement of competitive intransitivity from community ecology to a frozen fossil record: what share of archived clone triples violate transitivity, and does the fitted trajectory law change when the cyclic component is modelled rather than discarded?
- Gate 1 reformulation (the one permitted; both versions stand): for a pre-registered round-robin of archived LTEE clones (>=1 clone per sampled time point, every pair at >=2 starting frequencies, >=3 replicates, propagated replicate-error model) — (a) what share of C(n,3) triples is cyclic under a pre-specified rule and what is the cycle-magnitude distribution against the error null; (b) what variance share of the pairwise selection-coefficient matrix is skew-symmetric (cyclic) rather than gradient/ranking under a Hodge-style decomposition; (c) what are the pre-registered deltas in fitted exponent and projected asymptote between the standard ancestor-referenced power law and a ranking-plus-cyclic fit.

Motivating gap: Published LTEE trajectories are built from ancestor-referenced head-to-head assays, which presuppose a single scalar fitness axis; non-transitive and frequency-dependent fitness has been reported since Paquin & Adams (1983); a 2025 preprint asserts frequency dependence is the general case. It was the first hit on the mandatory own-field/own-words query.

Verification (post-batch, fresh-context checker, log UQ-20261016-57-verification.md): the quote is real, the estimand is not the candidate's, and the rejection stands on the narrower ground.
Confirmed verbatim in de Visser and Lenski 2002 (BMC Evolutionary Biology 2:19): "the ANOVA tests all forms of non-transitivity", with the interaction term at "< 2% of the overall variation" and P > 0.5, against main effects at P < 0.0001 explaining over 93% and residual error about 5%. A second test compared the cumulative fitness gain predicted under transitivity with the observed gain, with a jackknife interval.
Where the batch's characterisation overreached: the design is ONE evolving population (Ara-1), 21 evenly spaced samples (generations 0, 1,000, ... 20,000) each competed against 5 marked Ara+ clones from generations 0, 5,000, 10,000, 15,000 and 20,000, three-fold replicated — 315 competitions on a fixed 21 x 5 grid, NOT a full round robin among archived clones. And the estimand is a sample-by-clone interaction variance share, i.e. non-parallelism of fitness across reference clones. The checker is explicit that it is not a rate of intransitive triples against an assay-error null: no triple counts are reported anywhere, and the residual term reflects replicate error only. "Tests all forms of non-transitivity" is the authors' own scope claim for their interaction term, not a triple-level measurement.
Why the rejection still holds: the question "is LTEE fitness intransitive, and is that why adaptation decelerates?" was asked and answered negatively inside the LTEE's own numbered series, and the power-law paper this candidate targets re-checked its own curve against marked 40,000-generation clones rather than only the ancestor (Wiser, Ribeck and Lenski 2013: 108 competitions, nine populations at 40k and 50k generations, plus all 12 populations at up to 41 time points against the ancestor — confirmed). What this candidate adds is estimator machinery and a wider design (a cyclic-triple rate with an error null, a Hodge decomposition variance share, an exponent refit) on an answered question, which the ledger does not count as a new question.
Recorded for a future run, since it is genuinely unmeasured: nobody has published a triple-level intransitivity rate against an assay-error null for the LTEE clone archive. If that is ever re-filed it must be posed as an estimator question in its own right, with its own Gate 0, and not as a question about whether fitness is intransitive.
