## Restatement (my own words, before searching)

For a hospital quality measure that is built from human chart abstraction and tied to a payment or rating cutoff: (a) can an auditor re-abstract charts one batch at a time, stop early when it is statistically safe, and still guarantee that the chance of stopping while a complete re-abstraction would in fact have flipped the hospital's classification is at most alpha; and (b) in a real cohort, how many hospitals sit so close to the cutoff that this guarantee cannot be had without re-reading most of their charts? A third admissible answer the record keeps open: for shrunken, risk-standardised measures no usable "margin" exists, so such an audit is impossible in principle.
