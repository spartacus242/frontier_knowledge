## 1. Restatement in my own words

Do the stations of one named seismic network go offline *together* more often than chance would predict from each station's own outage rate — and, if so, how much more often than an independent-outage model predicts does the network's *actual* detection threshold sit at least ΔM magnitude units above its *published* capability map for T hours or longer, and what share of the realized threshold's variance is owed to the correlated part of the downtime rather than the independent part? Reported for a declared network and window, with the station frame fixed by FDSN-epoch coverage, against a named published product, over the enumerated grid w ∈ {15 min, 1 h, 6 h}, ΔM ∈ {0.2, 0.3, 0.5}, T ∈ {1, 3, 6, 12} h.

The question has three numbers: (a) a coincidence excess over an independent null; (b) a probability ratio (measured joint / independent null) for ΔM-by-T excursions; (c) a variance share. A null on all three is a pre-named answer. The record declared the measurand exemption; I confirm it applies: "stations go down together" is what (a) measures, not what the question assumes.
