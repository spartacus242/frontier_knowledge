## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (canonical) — V1, as received:
When organ-level compartmental models are fitted independently per organ in the conventional way, do the fitted parameters reproduce the whole-body tracer mass balance that a single total-body PET acquisition now measures directly, and where they do not, which organs' published kinetic parameters are biased and in which direction?

Question (canonical) — V2, the ONE Gate 1 reformulation permitted, for well-posedness only (this is the version graded below):
In dynamic total-body PET (axial FOV >= ~1.9 m) of N subjects per tracer, for a pre-specified tracer set, does the closure residual
  R(t) = A_inj(t) − SUM_organs Ahat_organ(t | thetahat) − E(t) − B(t)
lie within its propagated uncertainty, where thetahat are per-organ compartmental parameters obtained by the conventional independent per-organ fit, A_inj(t) is decay-corrected injected activity, E(t) is measured excreted/left-FOV activity, and B(t) is a PRE-REGISTERED BOUNDED (not freely fitted) non-segmentable remainder; and for each tracer where R(t) is significantly non-zero, which organ's parameters move when the fit is redone under a global conservation constraint, in which direction, and by how much in Ki, Vt, K1 and organ absorbed dose?

Reformulation rationale (recorded per Gate 1): V1 does not say what "reproduce the mass balance" means operationally, and leaves the remainder term unspecified — which is the design threat the intake itself names, since a freely fitted remainder absorbs any residual and makes the test vacuous. V2 fixes the estimand (R(t)), fixes the denominator (propagated uncertainty), and forbids the free remainder. No scope change.

Alternative formulations:
- Does total-body PET's simultaneous view of every organ falsify the independence assumption behind decades of single-organ PET kinetic modelling?
- Is injected activity conserved across independently fitted organ compartmental models, and how large is the closure residual as a function of tracer and of time after injection?
- (Metrology register) What is the misclosure of the whole-body activity balance implied by conventional per-organ PET kinetic fits, and which organ's parameters does a constrained adjustment move?

Motivating gap:
The newly measurable quantity is specific. Total-body PET scanners with ~2 m axial FOV (uEXPLORER class, from 2019) record every organ's time-activity curve AND the arterial input function in one dynamic acquisition, so the tracer's whole-body mass balance is observable at the same time as organ kinetics are being fitted. It was never observable before, because only one organ was ever in the FOV — and the per-organ independent-fit convention that this limitation created has been carried into total-body data unchanged. Two papers opened in this run confirm the convention rather than the check: Chalampalakis et al. 2024 fit eight organs by independent Patlak on a dynamic total-body cohort and never sum them; vPET-ABC (2026) fits ~4.4 million voxels independently at total-body scale, its only cross-unit object being a post-hoc spatial-autocorrelation evaluation. Meanwhile internal dosimetry does use mass balance, but computes MIRD's "remainder of body" as a SUBTRACTION from injected activity — imposing closure by construction and absorbing exactly the residual that would expose a bad organ model. Drug development, by contrast, requires a radiolabeled mass-balance study recovering the administered dose; PET has never had to meet that standard because it could not see the whole body.
HONESTY FLAG CARRIED FROM INTAKE, NOW PARTLY DISCHARGED: the intake flagged as unverified the claim that independent per-organ fitting without a conservation check is the live convention. Two opened sources (Chalampalakis 2024; vPET-ABC 2026) now support it directly, though neither is a methods review, so the claim is evidenced by instance rather than by survey.

Presuppositions:
1. Total-body PET quantifies activity concentration accurately enough at organ level to close a balance to within a few percent, including at the extremes of a 2 m axial FOV where sensitivity, scatter and attenuation corrections are least favourable. CONTESTED — and partly undermined by an opened source: Chalampalakis et al. 2024 report early-frame (<2 min) underestimation at reduced count levels attributed to failure of a fixed 0.75 scatter-scaling factor, and organ Ki changes of −12% / −16% at 25% / 10% of activity. If calibration-and-correction error is of the same order as the expected residual, limb (a) returns a bound rather than a verdict.
2. Organ volumes needed to convert concentration to activity are segmentable with bounded, propagatable error.
3. The non-segmentable remainder (marrow, connective tissue, skin, blood in transit) can be BOUNDED rather than fitted as a free absorber. KEY DESIGN THREAT, handled in V2 by pre-registration; if no defensible bound exists the test is underpowered, not vacuous.
4. Excreted activity — bladder/urine, exhaled, sweat, anything leaving the FOV — is accounted for within the acquisition window.
5. Decay, dead-time and randoms corrections are exact enough that the residual is not swamped by them.
6. The imaged quantity is conserved: labelled metabolites are either imaged identically to the parent or independently measured. CONTESTED and tracer-specific — for a tracer with rapidly cleared labelled metabolites the parent is not conserved and the question must be restated for total label. V2 inherits this as a per-tracer restatement requirement.
7. Independently fitted single-organ compartmental models are the prevailing convention for total-body data. Previously CONTESTED/unverified; now supported by two opened instances (see Motivating gap), not by a methods review.
8. Published Ki/Vt/K1 values feed decisions (occupancy, dosimetry, tracer selection) at a precision where a closure-sized bias would matter. Only partly established — required for significance, and the weakest significance limb.

## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

What would count as an answer:
For each tracer in a small pre-specified set, in dynamic total-body scans of N subjects: compute R(t) as defined in V2, with uncertainty propagated from segmentation, scanner calibration and input-function error. The question is answered by (a) the distribution of |R(t)| relative to that propagated uncertainty, per tracer and per time window, and (b) for every tracer with R(t) significantly non-zero, identification of which organ's parameters move when the fit is redone under a global conservation constraint, and by how much (percentage shift in Ki, Vt, K1 and in organ absorbed dose).
A null answer — closure within propagated uncertainty for every tracer — is a real result: it would license the independent-fit convention with evidence for the first time, and would bound the accuracy of long-axial-FOV quantification as a by-product.
It is a question, not a thesis: both limbs have statable outcomes, and the null is informative rather than a failure.

## Gate 3 — Significance

Consequence:
If R(t) is significantly non-zero for some tracer: published organ kinetic parameters carry a bias of identifiable location and sign, and the constrained refit names the offending organ. The knock-on is concrete in dosimetry — MIRD's remainder-by-subtraction has been absorbing that model error into "remainder of body", so absorbed dose has been misallocated between the remainder and the offending organ, in a direction the constrained fit would reveal. In radiopharmaceutical therapy, organ absorbed dose sets administered activity, so a misallocation of this kind propagates to prescribed treatment.
If R(t) closes within propagated uncertainty for every tracer: the independent-fit convention gets its first evidential licence in roughly four decades of use, and the study yields as a by-product an organ-level bound on long-axial-FOV quantitative accuracy across the full 2 m FOV — a number harmonisation bodies want for its own sake. Either way the run produces a usable quantity, which is what keeps this from being a fishing expedition.

Audience (specific, and what they would do differently):
- Total-body PET kinetic-modelling groups (UC Davis/Wang, Sydney/Meikle, Vienna/Beyer — the last two are authors of the two priors opened here): would add a closure diagnostic to multi-organ protocols, and in the non-null case would re-derive affected parameters.
- MIRD committee and EANM dosimetry committee: a measured residual is an argument to replace remainder-by-subtraction with a measured-and-bounded remainder in the formalism.
- Radiopharmaceutical-therapy dosimetry services: organ-dose shifts translate directly into administered-activity decisions.
- Tracer developers: Vt/Ki are the numbers candidate tracers are selected on; a sign-identified bias changes rankings.
- Harmonisation/accreditation bodies (EARL, QIBA) and LAFOV vendors: the by-product bound is a specification-relevant figure, especially at FOV extremes.

Fecundity (new questions that become askable):
- Does a conservation-constrained multi-organ estimator buy identifiability for badly conditioned parameters — notably k4 in liver and kidney — i.e. is the constraint useful rather than merely diagnostic?
- Can per-scan closure residual serve as an automated QC flag for individual dynamic total-body studies, exactly as misclosure does in survey adjustment?
- Can remainder-of-body be measured rather than subtracted, and what does that do to S-value practice?
- Can closure alone separate parent from total label for a tracer with rapidly cleared labelled metabolites, without arterial metabolite assay?
- Does the same test transfer to total-body SPECT and to 177Lu therapy dosimetry?

Anchoring (existing open problems and anomalies it touches):
Image-derived input-function validation; the MIRD remainder-of-body convention; structural identifiability of 2TCM (query 10's literature supplies the formal machinery); and one concrete opened anomaly — Chalampalakis et al.'s early-frame underestimation traced to a fixed 0.75 scatter-scaling factor, which is exactly the kind of correction error a closure residual would surface as a time-localised non-zero R(t). It also touches the asymmetry that drug development must demonstrate dose recovery while PET never has.

Candidate reason-for-silence mechanism (four strands, none of them "no one thought of it"):
1. NEW INSTRUMENT: before 2019 only one organ was ever in the FOV, so R(t) was not computable. The independent per-organ fit is a fossil of the 15-25 cm axial FOV, and total-body data inherited the convention unchanged — evidenced by two opened instances (eight independent Patlak fits in 2024; 4.4M independent voxel fits in 2026).
2. A CONVENTION THAT IMPOSES CLOSURE BY CONSTRUCTION: the one community that holds the conservation concept, internal dosimetry, defines remainder-of-body as injected minus measured organs. It therefore has no residual to inspect — the quantity is zeroed by definition before anyone could look at it. This is the strongest strand: the concept and the data have been in the same room for years, separated by an accounting identity.
3. A DISCIPLINARY BOUNDARY AROUND THE METHOD: the apparatus that would run this test — data reconciliation with gross-error detection — belongs to process-systems engineering and has no citation traffic with tracer kinetics (query 11).
4. AN INCENTIVE/REGULATORY GAP: no regulator requires dose recovery of a PET tracer, whereas a human radiolabeled mass-balance study is expected in drug development (query 6). Nothing has ever forced the check.
