# UQ-20261016-56 — Adversary 1 log

## Restatement (my own words)
Take every species that has both (a) a chromosome-level genome assembly in a public archive and (b) an old-fashioned microscope chromosome count in a compiled karyotype database, matched by name. Count the assembly's chromosome-assigned sequences under a fixed rule. Limb (i): how often, in which direction, and in which clades does the assembly count differ from the microscope count, with a sub-sample arbitrated by a third method? Limb (ii): fit a ChromEvol-type model on one fixed tree per clade twice — once with microscope counts, once with assembly counts — and compare the shift in polyploidy / dysploidy rate estimates with the shift produced by merely jittering the microscope counts at the error rate measured in limb (i).

The quantity is a two-instrument discordance audit plus an error-propagation experiment; the candidate's novelty claim is that nobody has tabulated the discordance or propagated it, although the direction (assembly below karyotype) is widely suspected.

## Single strongest killing point
Literature karyotypes are gathered as input metadata for large-scale assembly projects (GoaT: "validated genome-relevant metadata ... such as genome sizes and karyotypes"), and whole-chromosome absences are documented technology-vintage artefacts (Kim et al. 2022: eight microchromosomes newly found in the re-assembled zebra finch), so the measured "discordance rate" risks being a statistic of curation practice at a given assembly vintage rather than of measurement error; the record's independence screen is asserted, not shown to be executable.
