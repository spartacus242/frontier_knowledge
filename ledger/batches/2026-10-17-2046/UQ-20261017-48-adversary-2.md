## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Published "AI is getting better at rate R" curves are fitted to a mostly-empty grid of about 200 models by about 40 benchmarks. The empty squares are not accidental. A benchmark gets added precisely because current models do badly on it; it stops accumulating scores once models ace it; and labs publish the numbers that flatter them. So the holes are correlated with the very quantity being measured. Question: once you condition on things you can see (release date, developer, open weights, benchmark age, licence, citations, eval cost), are the holes ignorable? If not, which direction and how far does the fitted capability-vs-date line's height and slope move — far enough to push a published doubling time outside its own error bar?

Two things I checked before attacking, because the whole candidate hangs on them: is the per-cell panel actually public, and does the series actually publish an interval for limb (c) to test against. Both turned out to be answerable, and both answered in the candidate's favour.

## Evidence gathered (verbatim, from sources I opened)

**V1 — P7 is SUPPORTED, and more strongly than the record hoped.** `eci-public` README: "CIs are quantiles of the bootstrap draws"; "Bootstrap resampling holds the set of models fixed and resamples each model's benchmark results with replacement"; outputs carry `eci_ci_low` and `eci_ci_high`. And the method paper reports an interval on the SLOPE itself: "Frontier model capabilities have been improving at 0.55 capability units per year" "with a 95% confidence interval of 0.45 to 0.67 units per year." Limb (c) therefore has a defined denominator. The well-posedness defect the record called "a real defect" is repaired by evidence, not by reformulation.

**V2 — P1 and P2 are SUPPORTED.** `epoch.ai/data/eci_benchmarks.csv` headers, verbatim: `model_id,benchmark_id,performance,benchmark,benchmark_release_date,model,model_version,Model,date,source`. One row per model x benchmark cell (e.g. `m103,b2,0.6,MMLU,2020-09-07,Gemini 1.0 Pro,gemini-1.0-pro-001,Gemini 1.0 Pro,2023-12-13,Stanford CRFM Leaderboard`); unevaluated cells are simply absent as rows, so R_ij is recoverable as the complement over the model x benchmark cross product; `benchmark_release_date` gives benchmark age directly and last-report is derivable as the max model `date` per benchmark. A `source` column exists but, per the same read, "No column flags developer self-report versus independent evaluation" — it is free text. So the panel the candidate needs is public and dated; the selective-reporting covariate has to be hand-coded from free text.

**V3 — P3 is supported in Epoch's own words.** Method paper §3.1: "the newest benchmarks that we've included also tend to have few data points where models perform well." That is the entry-when-hard pattern stated by the index's maintainers about their own panel.

**V4 — the published interval is computed under the assumption in question.** The bootstrap "resamples each model's benchmark results with replacement," i.e. resamples *observed* cells and treats them as exchangeable. So the interval limb (c) tests against cannot itself answer limb (b). This is the candidate's foil, and it is now sourced rather than asserted.

**V5 — the fit is not a likelihood-based IRT fit.** Method paper §2.3: fitted "using standard least squares regression" with L2 regularization, and incomplete coverage is handled by a *filter*: "we further filter out models that have only been evaluated on" three or fewer benchmarks; footnote 5: "We vary this assumption in Appendix E, and find that our results remain very similar." Also "20 of the benchmarks do not have enough data to perform the analysis" (§3.1.1). There is no observation model anywhere in the visible text; none of missing, missingness, MAR, ignorable, unobserved or imputation appears in it.

**V6 — the index is anchored by fiat.** `eci-public` README: "The anchor model names are matched against the `Model` column of the input data," a missing anchor makes the fit fail, and the anchors get NaN CIs (the record's F4 gives Claude 3.5 Sonnet = 130, GPT-5 medium = 150).

**V7 — the index's own authors deny unidimensionality in print.** Method paper: "AI model capabilities can be cleanly captured by a single number, which is of course not the case, but we opt for it nevertheless," and §4.3 is titled "AI capabilities cannot be fully described by a single number." Fit evidence offered is residual inspection and per-benchmark R-squared of 0.71 and 0.81; no formal dimensionality test in the visible text.

**V8 — THE UNREAD RISK I COULD NOT CLEAR.** The method paper's Appendix E carries the headings "Varying benchmark inclusion," "Random selection," and "Detecting Benchmark Gaming." Their bodies are NOT in the returned HTML and my fetch cap was spent. COULD NOT READ; routes that failed: arxiv.org/html/2512.00193v1 returned the body truncated mid-§4.3 with appendices absent. Routes not attempted for want of budget: arxiv.org/pdf/2512.00193, epoch.ai/publications/a-rosetta-stone-for-ai-benchmarks. My reading of the heading pair is that "Random selection" alongside "Varying benchmark inclusion" points to a random-subset ablation, which exercises MCAR/MAR exchangeability rather than testing the MNAR entry/exit mechanism — but that is an inference from headings, and the instructions forbid letting a title ground a grade, so it caps this entry rather than deciding it.

**Minor hazard, my own arithmetic, labelled as such.** The paper reports the 0.55 units/year trend as "roughly corresponds to a time horizon doubling time of around 5 months" while "Footnote 10 computes approximately 4 months," so the paper gives two values for limb (c)'s headline quantity. Carrying the paper's own stated 95% CI of 0.45-0.67 through an inverse-proportionality map (my arithmetic, and the 5-vs-4-month discrepancy shows this is not exactly how they convert) puts the doubling time somewhere near 4.1-6.1 months around the 5-month figure, i.e. limb (c)'s threshold is roughly a plus/minus 20% band on the slope. Determinate, but limb (c) must name which published interval is the denominator.
