## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take a defined set of finished multicentre randomised trials whose primary endpoint is
produced, or whose intervention is titrated, by a device that contains software, and whose
report or protocol names the device model. Two things are being asked.

(i) A base-rate question: for how many of those trials can an outsider, working from
manufacturer release notes and service bulletins, FDA recall/MAUDE records, EU field-safety
notices, a direct enquiry to the vendor, or the trial's own text, establish that the device's
firmware or calibration algorithm was revised at a date falling inside that trial's enrolment
period? The answer is to be read as a floor, not an estimate, with the route recorded per trial
and with the share of device-mediated trials thrown out for not naming a model reported as
attrition.

(ii) A conditional-magnitude question: among the trials where such a revision is datable AND
the patient-level data can be re-analysed, how big is the treatment-effect-by-device-era
interaction on that trial's own effect scale, compared with that trial's own MCID (or, failing
that, half the width of its primary 95% CI), with collinearity between era and calendar time
reported so that trials where the interaction cannot be estimated are counted as such and not
as nulls.

In plainer terms: BOOST-II found that a vendor's mid-trial oximeter recalibration split its
mortality result in two. Is that a one-off, or the one case where anybody looked — and can
anybody even look, from outside, for trials in general?
