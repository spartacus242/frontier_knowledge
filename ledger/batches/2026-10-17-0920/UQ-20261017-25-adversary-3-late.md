## Restated question (own words)
Take every dated, officially issued design value for wind, river discharge or wave height that is explicitly labelled with an annual exceedance probability p. For the years after each issue date, count how often the values were exceeded across many sites, correct for the fact that one storm hits many sites, and ask whether the realised rate is p or something else. Then split any gap into three buckets: the issuing method was biased at the time (measured by re-running the old method against a modern one on the same old data), the old data were too short to pin the value down, and the climate has since moved.
Two separable questions are hiding inside this: Q1 (rate): realised/nominal exceedance ratio for wind, flood, wave issued values. Q2 (attribution): how much of a departure is issuing-method error versus later change.

## The eight attacks

### 1. Triviality
Objection: the answer is a paragraph - "design values are exceeded roughly as often as climate and pooling imply"; per-event AEP is routine USGS output.
Best response: no calculation yields the pooled ratio; it needs a digitised issue-dated frame, a dependence model, and a power analysis. Evidence it is not obvious: the seismic field needed multi-author papers, found power of only <=30% for 0.75-1.5x departures [RECORD, Mak & Schorlemmer], and Stein et al. call the maps "never ... objectively tested" [OPENED-summary D]. Per-event AEP of one observed peak is a different statistic from a pooled realised rate [OWN]. [OWN arithmetic] 842 gauges x 40 yr x 0.01 = 337 expected exceedances before any dependence deflation; 30 yr gives 253 - enough to be non-trivial but not a paragraph.
Status: Resolved. Nothing I found supplies the answer in a paragraph, and the objection is unsourced.

### 2. Disguise
Objection: it is the testing of issued probabilistic hazard maps against observed exceedance counts (Mak & Schorlemmer on four USGS vintages, six return periods, observed-vs-expected site counts, dependence acknowledged, power stated), with the hazard variable swapped.
Best response, split by limb. Q1: yes, this is the known estimand type applied to a new corpus. I will not call it unasked in concept. It is unasked in content: across 10 searches in 8 vocabularies (IDF/rainfall atlases, code wind maps, flood frequency, hindcast verification, cat-model backtests, EVA out-of-sample, seismic attribution, Bulletin 17) I found no item that scores a dated issued wind, flood or wave value against its nominal rate [RESULT-TITLE only; the instrument is a title-level search tool and most hits were unopened, so this is absence of a detected prior, not proof of absence]. The answer is not derivable from the seismic result (different tail, different dependence, different measurement system). Q2: I found nothing in any field that performs the bias-at-issue / later-change decomposition. The seismic side states attribution is open: "no generally agreed-upon criteria" and a short record is the challenge [OPENED-summary D]; the Tasan "more than one explanation" and SSA "too-high models and/or biased data" quotes are [RECORD]. Open, not "asked and answered". Polimi item (query 2) is a code-vs-re-estimate comparison by title; Unverified content.
Residual risk: a hydrology or wind grey-literature or non-English item scoring issued values that a title-level search would not surface. Unrun: opening the Polimi item and the 1995 USGS "computed vs observed" item.
Status: Partial. Not Unresolved: I cannot source a kill. Q1 alone would be Partial-to-weak (corpus swap); Q2 alone is Partial because novelty is shown only by absence.

### 3. Combinatorial novelty
Objection: "coverage test x design values" pastiche; the interface was already crossed in seismology; the rest is data entry.
Best response: there is a real interface problem not solved by the sibling field: storm-footprint and river-network dependence instead of arbitrary declustering (Mak & Schorlemmer's 25/50 km is "fundamentally arbitrary" [RECORD]); count overdispersion in hydrology (Kjeldsen & Prosdocimi, [RECORD]); rating-curve and averaging-time covariates; and the plug-in problem [OWN]: even in a stationary world, a plug-in design quantile estimated from a finite record is exceeded at a rate different from p, so nominal p is not the right null and the baseline must be the estimator's own sampling distribution. That interface is not mechanical. But none of it is evidenced as solved or even tried.
Status: Partial.

### 4. Presupposition failure
Presupposition 5 (bias-at-issue and later nonstationarity separable) and the definition of limb (a).
Objection (sourced part): limb (a) is defined as issue-era estimator versus modern estimator on the identical record. That recovers only the difference between two estimators, not their shared error. Patel & Schneider report that records under 30 years "generally lead to substantial underestimation" via the GEV shape parameter [OPENED-summary A, simulation]; a modern GEV on the same short issue-era record shares that bias, so (a) is blind to it. Whether it lands in (b) depends on whether (b) "parameter and record-length uncertainty" is read as variance or as bias; as worded it is uncertainty. A shared short-record bias then has no bucket and falls into (c) by elimination. Separately [OWN reasoning]: (c) is identified only as a residual after (a) and (b), and over a 20-40 year post-issue window a simulated 100-year-value trend is "small ... compared to sampling uncertainty" [OPENED-summary B, simulated]. The record already concedes (c) needs a "declared structure".
Best response: (b) can be defined as bias plus variance of the issue-era estimator via a resampling or simulation study at the actual record lengths, which puts short-record bias in a named bucket; the decomposition is a pre-declared accounting rather than a claim of unique causal identification; hydrology's estimators are explicit procedures, unlike seismic source-model-plus-GMPE pipelines, so (a) is checkable here in a way it was not there [OWN]. Unevidenced either way.
Sub-check not run: whether issue-era reports document the estimator, record and pooling well enough to re-run (the record's own strongest objection). I did not test it. Unverified.
Status: Partial. I refuse the stronger claim that separation is impossible (see refused objections).

### 5. Vagueness
Objection: with a free choice of dependence model, frame and trend structure, any realised ratio can be absorbed; decomposition shares are undefined when the ratio is near 1.
Best response: Q1 has a concrete estimand (pooled ratio with a declared dependence-aware interval) and pre-declaration blocks post-hoc absorption; the clean null is informative if the interval is narrow enough. [OWN arithmetic] 337 expected counts (above) vs the seismic regime of near-zero per site; effective sample size after overdispersion and storm-footprint clustering is unquantified, so whether the interval can exclude, say, a 1.5x ratio is unknown. Q2: no stated precision for attributing shares, and (c) is a residual, so a discriminating test between "estimator" and "trend" is not stated.
Status: Partial.

### 6. Significance inflation
Objection: audience and consequence asserted; the seismic precedent shows two decades of the statistic with no changed map or adopted margin [RECORD]; the Simiu critique standing since 2003 equally shows non-action.
Best response: Stein et al. state the demand for objective testing explicitly [OPENED-summary D]; the Czech Q100 re-estimation shows an issuing institute asking a neighbouring question [RECORD]; both signs of the answer and the null carry a decision on whether to add a margin or change an estimator [RECORD, OWN]. I could not source that any named committee would change practice. The significance of Q2 depends on its being identifiable.
Status: Partial.

### 7. Reason for silence
Objection: the record's three mechanisms are weakened by seismology (boundary crossing, incentives, scale), leaving only "nobody carried it across"; in hydrology the data are easier, so silence is harder to explain.
Best response: credible mechanisms I can state [OWN, none sourced]: (i) digitising superseded issue-dated documents is nobody's budget line; (ii) engineering practice treats revision as the correction, so scoring the old value changes no decision (this undermines significance, not novelty, per the rule); (iii) the seismic community had a prospective-testing institution (CSEP lineage) that hydrology and wind do not; (iv) issued wind and flood values are often not clean frequency claims, so the frame is smaller than it looks. None evidenced. I also cannot exclude that the work exists and my title-level searches missed it.
Status: Partial. I do not call it Unresolved because the kill (that the mechanisms fail) would itself be unsourced for hydrology and wind.

### 8. Dissolution
Objection: under analysis the question splits into Q1, a known method on a new corpus; (a), an estimator comparison of the kind Simiu et al. already performed for ASCE 7 [RECORD, partial read]; (b), standard uncertainty propagation; and (c), a residual that is nonstationarity only by elimination and is confounded with measurement-system change and unmodelled dependence [OWN].
Best response: the joint accounting is the contribution - subtracting an operational (a) and (b) from a measured ratio so that the remainder is an estimate rather than an assertion - and no source I opened or the brief's readers found does it in any field; the pieces being individually standard does not make their combination done [OWN]. The risk is that the new part is exactly the least identified.
Status: Partial.

## Disposition of the live questions
- Corpus swap on an asked question, new estimand, or two questions? Two questions. Q1 (pooled realised/nominal ratio for wind, flood, wave) is a known estimand type in a new corpus: a legitimate empirical gap but low conceptual novelty, and the result is not derivable from seismology. Q2 (decomposition) is a new estimand with no prior I could find in any field, and the sibling field lists attribution as open rather than closed or impossible; but as worded its identification is weakest exactly where its novelty lies (limb (a) blind to common short-record bias; (c) by residual).
- Novelty grade (mine): Medium. Q1 Low-to-Medium; Q2 higher but resting on absence from title-level searches plus the seismic attribution being stated open [OPENED-summary D].
- The withdrawal does not rescue the bundle intact; it removes the rejection ground. Recommended: re-posed with Q2 as the question and Q1 as its prerequisite measurement, with limb (a)/(b) redefined so short-record bias has a bucket.
