## Restatement (my own words, before any attack)

Take every cube-free k with 0 < |k| <= 1000 that is not +/-4 mod 9. For each, count the integer solutions of
x^3 + y^3 + z^3 = k whose smallest coordinate in absolute value is at most 10^15 (the Booker-Sutherland box), and
divide that count by a prediction built out of p-adic and archimedean solution densities alone, times log 10^15.
Then ask whether the ~1000 leftover ratios line up, in rank, with any of three invariants of the pure cubic field
K = Q(k^(1/3)): its class number, its regulator, or the 3-rank of its class group — tested against a permutation
null that conditions out the mod-9 class and the cube-factor structure of k. Underneath that sits a declared
prerequisite limb: is the per-k prediction the literature actually uses (Booker-Sutherland's rho_sol) purely local,
or does building it already drag in the unit group of K? In plain terms: **does the scarcity or abundance of
three-cube representations of a given k remember anything about the arithmetic of Q(k^(1/3)) that local congruence
conditions cannot see?**

## The document finding the verdict rests on

From **Booker, A. R. and Sutherland, A. V., "On a question of Mordell", PNAS 118(11) e2022377118 (2021),
DOI 10.1073/pnas.2022377118, arXiv:2007.01209**, Section 2, as served at `https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.01209`.
Authors from the paper's own front matter.

**REGIME:** population = cube-free k <= 1000 (positive); unit of analysis = one k; operating condition = search complete
for min{|x|,|y|,|z|} below the stated bound, with "we are reluctant to make any unconditional claims", and (2.2) itself
conditional on GRH with X = 10^9; instrument = local density counts mod p^e plus L-functions evaluated in PARI/GP;
threshold = B in {10^5, 10^10, 10^15}; category = predicted per-k solution density rho_sol.

Definition, as served (segments 1-9 of an ordered transcription, assembled only from consecutive returns):

> "Define $\sigma_p := \lim_{e \to \infty} \frac{\#\{(x, y, z) \pmod{p^e} : x^3 + y^3 + z^3 \equiv k \pmod{p^e}\}}{p^{2e}}$"
> ... "Heath-Brown conjectures that the number n(B) of solutions to (1.1), up to permutation, satisfying
> $\max\{|x|,|y|,|z|\} \leq B$ is asymptotic to $e(B) \coloneqq \rho_{\text{sol}} \log B$ as $B \to \infty$, where
> $\rho_{\text{sol}} \coloneqq \frac{1}{6} \sigma_{\infty} \prod_{p} \sigma_{p}$."

And the field, from the same section:

> "Define $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{k})$ and $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$," ... "We define two modular forms related to
> $F$ and $K$." ... "First, let $f_1$ be the modular form of weight 1 and level $|\operatorname{Disc}(K)|$ such that
> $\zeta_K(s) = \zeta(s)L(s, f_1)$."

And **equation (2.2)**, with the sentence before and after it:

> "Since $-a_p c_p(k)$ is the coefficient of $p^{-s}$ in the Rankin-Selberg *L*-function $L(s, f_1 \boxtimes f_2)$, we
> expect square-root cancellation in the product $\prod_p r_p$. Under the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH), for
> large *X* we have
> **(2.2)** $\prod_{p} \sigma_p = (1 + O(X^{-2} \log X)) L(1, f_1)^{3} L(2, f_2) L(2, f_1)^{-6} \zeta(2)^{-6}
> L(2, (\frac{\cdot}{3}))^{-3} \prod_{p \le X} r_p$
> Applying (2.2) with $X=10^9$ allows us to compute the solution densities $\rho_{\rm sol}$ to roughly 18 digits of
> precision for all cubefree $k \leq 1000$. To evaluate the L-functions, we used the extensive functionality available
> for that purpose in PARI/GP [Par19]."

**Scope and opener check, as required.** The transcription's opening words scope (2.2) to a GRH-conditional
large-X asymptotic for the product over ALL p of the local densities sigma_p. The clause my words sit in **asserts**
the quantity: it is an equation for prod_p sigma_p, not a denial of it and not a record of a move away from it. The
sentence after it is not a definition but a statement that this is **how the published rho_sol values were actually
computed** — performed and printed, not merely defined. The paper's text never names a class number, a regulator or a
fundamental unit; I am not claiming it does. **Internal consistency:** the two independent transcription fetches agree
on rho_sol = (1/6) sigma_inf prod_p sigma_p and on the definition of f_1; I found no inconsistency.

**Leg A (DOCUMENT ONLY, no inference of mine beyond reading the two displayed definitions together).** rho_sol's value
is proportional to L(1, f_1)^3, and the paper defines L(s, f_1) as zeta_K(s)/zeta(s) for K = Q(k^(1/3)). So the value of
the "purely local" per-k constant **is** a value of the Dedekind zeta function of K divided by the Riemann zeta
function, cubed. The comparator is therefore not independent of the global arithmetic of Q(k^(1/3)) — this needs no
class number formula at all, only the paper's own two displayed lines. **If Leg B below fell, the verdict would still
stand on Leg A.**

**Leg B (MY OWN REASONING AND MY OWN ARITHMETIC, NOT A SOURCE FINDING — labelled as required; the search that would have
grounded it in print is S8, which I ran and which returned nobody stating it).** zeta(s) has a simple pole at s=1 with
residue 1 and zeta_K has a simple pole there, so L(s,f_1)=zeta_K(s)/zeta(s) is holomorphic at s=1 with
L(1,f_1) = Res_{s=1} zeta_K(s). For a pure cubic field K = Q(k^(1/3)) the signature is (r_1,r_2) = (1,1) and w_K = 2, so
the analytic class number formula (textbook; I did not open a primary source for it this pass and say so) gives
Res_{s=1} zeta_K(s) = 2^{1}(2 pi)^{1} h(K) R(K) / (2 sqrt|d_K|) = 2 pi h(K) R(K) / sqrt|d_K|. Substituting into (2.2):
**rho_sol is proportional to ( h(K) R(K) )^3 / |d_K|^{3/2}**, times the explicit s=2 L-values, zeta(2)^{-6} and the
rapidly convergent product of r_p. No uncertainty is quoted by the source on these quantities other than the
GRH-conditional O(X^{-2} log X) and the "heuristic estimate of the error", both of which I carry forward: at X = 10^9
they are far below the factor-of-two spread in rho_sol across k that the record's own printed rows exhibit
(rho_sol = 0.049745 for k = 906 up to 0.113449 for k = 42).

So **two of the candidate's three pre-declared invariants, h(K) and R(K), sit inside the comparator the candidate
divides out** — to the third power, in the denominator of the residual r_k.
