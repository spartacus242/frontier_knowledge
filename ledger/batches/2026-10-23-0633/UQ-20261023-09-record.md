## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (canonical): For FDA-cleared autonomous diagnostic AI devices in routine deployment, what is the decision-flip rate when the same patient is re-imaged within the same visit by a different operator on the same camera model, and how does that intra-visit flip rate compare with the uncertainty margins on the sensitivity and specificity in the device's own clearance study?

Alternative formulations:
1. If the patient walked back in and was photographed again, would the autonomous device say the same thing?
2. Clearance measures an autonomous device's accuracy against a reading centre under protocolised imaging; what is its repeatability under deployment imaging, and does the flip rate exceed the margin it was cleared on?

Motivating gap (as intaken): a cleared diagnostic that issues refer / do-not-refer / insufficient-quality with no human read, deployed in primary care where acquisition is done by non-specialist staff rather than the trained photographers of the clearance study; clearance reports accuracy in an acquisition regime deployment does not reproduce, and the patient receives one acquisition.

**The motivating gap is false as stated.** The clearance file of at least one named device in exactly this class contains the re-acquisition measurement, taken at primary care sites by multiple operators on two camera units of the same model, with the decision categories the candidate asks for — see Gate 2.

Presuppositions (intake, with this run's findings):
- P1. Devices issuing an autonomous clinical decision with no human read are in routine use at scale — **established at class level, NOT verbatim-confirmed for the killing device.** The K200667 indications-for-use text I read says EyeArt is "indicated for use by healthcare providers" and, per my reader, "does not explicitly say whether a clinician must review the images." The autonomy wording is therefore UNVERIFIED from the document I opened; the kill does not need it (see "leg the verdict stands on").
- P2. Re-acquisition in the same visit either already happens or can be added at negligible risk — **established, and stronger than intaken.** AEYE-DS pivotal protocol §9.2.2 already specifies up to three imaging attempts, then dilation and "Three (3) more attempts", with "all 4 fundus images ... will be reacquired"; §9.10 adds a dedicated sub-study with "three different novice operators on two different Topcon NW400 fundoscopy devices". K200667 ran repeat acquisition on 62 subjects.
- P3. The device returns a deterministic output for a given image set — **STILL CONTESTED and still unmeasured.** Neither clearance document I opened re-runs identical images through the same device version, so neither separates acquisition-induced from algorithm-induced flips. This is the one presupposition the priors leave untouched.
- P4. A flip is clinically consequential, and insufficient-quality must be its own outcome — **established, and already implemented by the prior.** K200667's agreement is computed on 3x3 tables over "negative, positive, ungradable" with OA = "(PP+NN+UU) / N", plus a reported "Average Ungradable Agreement (AUA)". Ungradable is a category, not folded in.
- P5. The clearance estimate carries a stated uncertainty the flip rate can be compared with — **established by opening two actual clearance summaries** (document-kind rule applied; see Gate 2 §Document-kind check). Both print 95% CIs.
- P6. Device version and camera model at re-acquisition are recorded — **established inside the pre-market sub-study** (one device version, one camera model, named units), **still CONTESTED in routine deployment logs**, which is where the candidate wanted the measurement.

## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

What would count as an answer: per named device, an intra-visit decision-flip rate with a confidence interval, reported by output category and decomposed into acquisition-induced and algorithm-induced components (the latter from re-running identical images), from a pre-declared deployment cohort with a pre-declared re-imaging fraction; plus an explicit comparison with that device's clearance interval.

Reformulation allowance: **NOT USED.** The Gate 1 reformulation is for well-posedness only and must not be spent escaping a Gate 2 kill. Recorded for the runner: the repair that would survive Gate 2 is narrower — *"for a cleared autonomous DR device, what share of intra-visit re-acquisition flips is algorithm-induced rather than acquisition-induced, and does the patient-visit-level flip rate in routine deployment match the eye-level inter-operator agreement printed in the device's pre-market precision study?"* That repair carries presuppositions of its own which I have NOT checked and which must be tested as hard as the original: (a) that a deployed device version can be re-fed its own stored image sets under the same version (needs vendor or site cooperation, and P6 is contested in deployment logs); (b) that the pre-market per-eye contingency tables are recoverable at patient level — the appendix tables that would allow it (K200667 Appendix Tables 13-14) were NOT served to me (see read levels).

Well-posedness defect found: the comparison limb is not operationalised. "The uncertainty margins on the sensitivity and specificity" does not say whether the margin is the CI half-width, the CI lower bound, or the distance from the pre-specified performance goal — and the K200667 file makes this bite, because its accuracy CIs are "computed using the clustered bootstrap method" on an accuracy-against-reference estimand while its agreement CIs sit on a self-agreement estimand with a different unit (eye-pairs). A flip rate and an accuracy interval are not commensurable without a stated bridge, and the candidate supplies none. The question is a question, not a thesis, and the first two limbs are settleable as written.

### THE KILL — what K200667 prints, with locations

Read level: **K200667 510(k) summary — summary-body text read in full through a third-party OCR mirror (Innolitics) via two WebFetch calls; the appendix contingency tables were NOT served ("the reproduced text ... cuts off at Table 10"), so Appendix Tables 13-14 are COULD NOT READ (route: same mirror page, content absent; primary FDA PDF not attempted — budget).**

Design, quoted from that summary text: a "precision (repeatability and reproducibility) study on 62 subjects at 2 US primary care sites"; Cohort P1 used "3 different operators using 2 different camera units of the same model" (Canon CR-2 AF), each eye imaged twice per operator-camera pairing; Cohort P2 used one operator and one camera with each eye imaged three times.

**Opening words and direction of the clause:** the design sentence is scoped to the precision study's Cohort P1 at a US primary care site; the clause **ASSERTS** the quantity (it reports a measured agreement), it neither denies it nor records a move away from it.

Numbers printed, with units and denominators:
- Intra-operator (repeatability) overall agreement, mtmDR, Cohort P1: **93.9% [95% CI: 89.7% - 97.7%]**, denominator **180 per-eye pairs** — "leaving 180 pairs that were performed per protocol", after six pairs were dropped "due to protocol deviations".
- Intra-operator OA, vtDR: **98.9% [96.5% - 100.0%]**.
- Inter-operator (reproducibility) OA, mtmDR: **90.3% [95% CI: 82.3% - 96.8%]** — computed on "the first image from each of the three operators in Cohort P1". **The document does not state an explicit denominator for the reproducibility OA** (my reader says so in terms); so the inter-operator figure's denominator is a gap in the prior, not a number I can name.
- Inter-operator OA, vtDR: **96.8% [90.3% - 100.0%]**.
- Agreement definition: OA on 3x3 tables over "negative, positive, ungradable" as "(PP+NN+UU) / N"; ungradable is a reported category, with an "Average Ungradable Agreement (AUA)" metric.
- Clearance accuracy **in the same document**: Table 2 (sequential enrolment, primary care), mtmDR sensitivity "100.0% [74.1% - 100%]", specificity "92.0% [85.1% - 97.5%]"; Table 3 (enrichment permitted, primary care) sensitivity "92.9% [87.1% - 97.5%]", specificity "85.6% [82.2% - 89.1%]"; "All the 95% confidence intervals (CIs) are computed using the clustered bootstrap method".

**My own arithmetic, labelled as mine:** the inter-operator *disagreement* (flip) rate is 100% - 90.3% = **9.7%, interval [3.2%, 17.7%]** by complementing the printed CI. The printed quantity is agreement; flip is its complement, which is the same quantity renamed, not a different one.

Reproduced as served; not silently corrected. The headline 180-pair denominator is reconstructible (3 x 31 x 2 = 186, minus 6) and is internally coherent.

## Gate 3 — Significance

Consequence: as asked, little — for EyeArt the answer is already on the public record and the comparison is a page of arithmetic, which also exposes the candidate to Triviality. For the residual (P3's decomposition, the patient-visit unit, routine-deployment rather than pre-market sites, and coverage for devices whose files print no precision study at all, such as IDx-DR v2.3) the consequence is real: if deployment flip rates exceed the pre-market inter-operator figure, the cleared accuracy is not the accuracy an individual patient receives.

Audience: real and identifiable — FDA CDRH (whose own staff wrote the guidance paper that specifies the precision study without any measured value), screening-programme commissioners deciding single vs double acquisition, and vendors' labelling. But the audience's own channel already carries the number for one device, so the audience would act on the residual, not on the question as written.

Fecundity: the useful children are narrower than the parent — what share of flips is algorithmic (P3); whether a pre-market inter-operator agreement predicts a deployment one; why precision studies appear in some AI device files and not others (K200667 yes, K213037 no), which is a regulatory-consistency question in its own right.

Anchoring: touches the known complaint that AI device clearances rest on retrospective or protocolised data, and the metrology literature on scan-rescan repeatability of deep-learning classifiers (arXiv 2305.14385; the prostate-ADC test-retest work) — neither of which I opened beyond search level.

Reason for silence: a **document-channel boundary**, not a conceptual one. The measurement exists but lives in a 510(k) summary appendix — grey regulatory literature that PubMed does not index and the clinical AI literature does not cite — and it is named in regulatory-metrology vocabulary ("precision", "overall agreement", "inter-operator variability") that the clinical vocabulary of this candidate ("decision-flip rate", "intra-visit") does not reach. That explains why the question feels unasked while being answered for one device, and it is a mechanism, not an absence of thought. It also predicts the genuine residual: because nothing compels a precision study, coverage across cleared devices is uneven, and nobody has had to reconcile the pre-market figure with a deployment one.
