## Restatement (my words)

Production LLM pretraining loaders are said to build each batch not by drawing B documents independently from the corpus but by streaming shards through a bounded shuffle buffer, so documents sharing a batch tend to share a shard, source, language or crawl segment. The candidate asks, at a frozen checkpoint: how much bigger is the variance of the minibatch gradient (projected on the reference gradient direction) under the stack's real batch-forming scheme than under i.i.d.-with-replacement draws of the same B from the same corpus — the survey-statistics "design effect" — what within-batch gradient correlation rho that implies for a declared cluster size m, whether multiplying the noise-scale variance term by that factor moves the gradient-noise-scale / critical-batch-size number past a pre-registered band, and whether the factor is stable across checkpoints and buffer sizes. Essentially: "how many i.i.d. examples is a real pretraining batch worth, and does anyone's GNS tracker care?"

## Key evidence

E1 (presupposition 1, loader source read first-hand). Megatron-LM `gpt_dataset.py`: `_build_shuffle_index` docstring "Build the range [0, size) and shuffle"; the shuffle index is documented as "A random permutation of index range of the sample index"; implemented as `numpy.arange(...)` followed by `numpy_random_state.shuffle(...)` over the whole sample range, and `_query_document_sample_shuffle_indices` applies `idx = self.shuffle_index[idx]` with random access into memmapped data. OLMo `IterableDataset._build_global_indices`: `indices = np.arange(len(self.dataset))`, `rng.shuffle(indices)` over the entire dataset, then rank sharding `indices[self.rank : self.total_size : self.world_size]`. Both are global random permutations of all samples: within an epoch, a batch is a simple random sample without replacement from the corpus. My own arithmetic from standard survey algebra, not a source's conclusion: for SRSWOR the design effect against with-replacement i.i.d. is the finite-population factor (1 - B/D), which for B of thousands and D of hundreds of millions is 1 to many decimal places — and McCandlish fn. 6 already names this factor (per the record). So the two largest open pretraining stacks, including the stack the record's own "first step" names (OLMo), have deff = 1 by construction. Clustering remains plausible only for streaming loaders (Mosaic streaming, HF streaming, webdataset, litdata).

E2 (Mosaic docs, opened). The shard-based design is real there: "The shard order is shuffled"; py1s "Samples within each shard or shard part are shuffled"; `shuffle_block_size` "determines how many samples within each canonical node are shuffled at once", default `max(4000000/num_canonical_nodes, 1<<18)`; guidance "should set shuffle_block_size to be larger than the number of samples in a single shard (usually, at least 10x)". No definition or measurement of shuffle quality, no loss comparison, no mention of gradient variance or batch size. The engineering audience limb is confirmed as real and as lacking a variance account.

E3 (Disguise hit, CorgiPile, SIGMOD 2022, arXiv 2206.05830, opened). Defines h_D via (1/N) sum_l ||grad f_{B_l}(x) - grad F(x)||^2 <= h_D sigma^2 / b, "an essential parameter to measure the 'cluster' effect within the original data blocks", with h_D = 1 when "all samples in the data set are fully shuffled" and h_D = b when "samples are well clustered in each block"; "the larger h_D, the more 'clustered' the data." Theorem 1: E[F(x_S) - F(x*)] <~ (1-alpha) h_D sigma^2 / T + ..., alpha = (n-1)/(N-1) the buffer fraction; alpha = 1 recovers full-shuffle SGD. Corgi^2 (2309.01640, opened) extends it: h'_D = 1 + (1/n - 1/(nb)) h_D, "h'_D controls the convergence rate, as it linearly impacts the leading term." My own identification, not the papers' words: a quantity bounded by sigma^2/b with range [1, b] that equals 1 under full shuffle and b under full clustering is exactly Kish's deff = 1 + (b-1) rho for a cluster (block) mean, so the design effect of block-based batch formation has already been defined, named (h_D) and tied to the convergence rate in the data-systems literature. Both papers: no empirical measurement of h_D or of any gradient variance (loss/accuracy only), no "design effect/ICC/effective sample size/gradient noise scale/critical batch size" vocabulary, datasets CIFAR-10/ImageNet/yelp, no LLM loader audited.
