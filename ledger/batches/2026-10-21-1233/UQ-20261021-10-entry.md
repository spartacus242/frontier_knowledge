## Verification of the priors and the unverified checks at first hand (post-batch, 2026-10-21)

Three fresh-context readers: one on Dadé et al. 2022, one on the statutory question this entry named as unverified, one on the two sampling-protocol references. **The verdict does not move — Provisional — one novelty correction lands, the hold-off-Validated leg is strengthened, and the measurement limb is left open where the entry put it.**

**The statutory check is DISCHARGED, and it corrects this entry rather than confirming it.** Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2289 does make chemistry a mandatory reporting dimension on the arriving and treated side: Annex III item F.5 is literally "The quantity of batteries from a chemistry supplied to recycling". So nobody may say that no statutory instrument touches chemistry at arrival. But Article 1(4) ties that chemistry axis to the recycling-efficiency target classes, which Annex II shows to be lead-acid, lithium-based, nickel-cadmium and others — "lithium-based" is a single atom in that scheme. The instrument is therefore silent on shares WITHIN lithium-ion (NMC against LFP against LCO against NCA against LMO), which is the resolution this candidate asks about, and its element-level numbers are output-side recovery rates per material rather than an input assay. **The gap is a granularity gap, not the absence of a statutory chemistry axis, and this entry now says so in those terms.** Two limits on the strength of that finding: no filed return was opened, only the instrument prescribing the format, so publication is established as a prescribed obligation and not as an observed publication; and the item F.5 wording was read without its surrounding clause, which is exactly what distinguishes a published figure from a calculation denominator. The 2026-02-20 corrigendum was not opened. UBA was robots-refused and the ZVEI, Eurostat and Slovenian candidates are unexamined, so none of them may be cited as lacking cell-chemistry data.

**Dadé et al. 2022 holds descriptively and contradicts any population-level use of itself.** Front matter exact as cited. Three Black Masses from different unnamed suppliers and recyclers, after "treatments such as pyrolysis, dry shredding and sieving" — a non-exhaustive list, and dry shredding specifically — with per-sample phase and cathode-type abundances in mass percent recalculated from area on polished two-dimensional sections. Corrections: the "<500 µm" figure is an Introduction-level description of Black Mass in general, not a sieve cut applied to these three samples, so it was cut on the wrong side of its clause; the paper's representativity sentence is a general methodological point attributed to its reference [9], and its own two uses are uncited and hedged to two-dimensional sectioning against density segregation and to counting statistics over three million particles — none of which claims three samples represent a feedstock population; and "high statistical analytical precision" with r² > 0.99 is within-sample counting precision and assay agreement on the same material, not sampling uncertainty, with no variance, replicate or interval on any abundance. There is no sampling frame, region, time window, field protocol, tonnage or production-year information of any kind, lithium is inferred from stoichiometry rather than measured, and graphite and plastics come from external C/S analysis which the Conclusions flag as a gap. **This strengthens leg (i) of the crosswalk objection**, which is what holds the entry off Validated: the paper supplies no mass balance, no yield and no input inventory, so its measurand cannot be inverted to arriving-battery mass shares, and it explicitly declines process modelling. No LCA comparison appears anywhere in it.

**The sampling-protocol references are not what this entry called them, and the measurement limb stays open.** Minerals Engineering 169:106924 (2021) is "Automated mineralogy as a novel approach for the compositional and textural characterization of spent lithium-ion batteries" by Vanderbruggen, Gugala, Blannin, Bachmann, Serna-Guerrero & Rudolph — a characterisation study of one 9.6 kg black-mass lot (<1 mm, after vacuum pyrolysis, crushing and sieving) at particle level on grain mounts, with no sampling-variance mathematics at all: no fundamental sampling error, no heterogeneity invariant, no nominal sample mass, and a single generic Gy (1994) citation. It is Dadé's representativity referent, but it is not a sampling-protocol source, and citing it as one misnames what it is. A cut-grammar trap in it is worth recording: "the sampling procedure was carefully designed to obtain the most homogenous sample" ends "...for this characterization study", which is specimen preparation for one analysis rather than a sampling plan. DOI 10.1255/tosf.166 is a WCSB10 item on black-mass phase characterisation — "sampling" in the title, characterisation in the deposited abstract, also post-shredding — and its full text is COULD NOT READ, so any fundamental-sampling-error content in it is formally unverified. And on the independent question: at this search depth nothing publishes a declared variance budget for whole end-of-life cells, modules or packs at a plant gate, with the closest statistical work ("Overcoming Black Mass Variability", 2025) explicitly about black mass. So the narrow form of the measurement limb — a representative-sampling protocol with a variance budget for ARRIVING batteries rather than for black mass — is NOT FOUND rather than unread, which is where this entry already put it. The error pattern to guard throughout this literature is regime substitution: post-shredding black mass cited for pre-shredding plant-gate feedstock.
