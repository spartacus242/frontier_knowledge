## 1. Restatement (my words, before reading the record's framing back)

Take the published in-vitro reconstitution papers for some declared class of cellular processes. For each paper and each purified component, divide the concentration the experimenters actually used by the concentration that component has in the cell where the process is said to happen, and look at the spread of those ratios. Then: in what share of papers is at least one component pushed above 3x, 10x, 100x of cellular? And if you took a sample of those experiments back to the bench with every component pinned at its cellular concentration — buffer, crowding and temperature declared in advance as arms rather than tuned — would the paper's own headline readout still be visible above a detection limit fixed beforehand? Finally: does a paper's worst fold-excess predict that somebody later found the reconstitution was missing a required factor?

So the candidate is three things bolted together: (A) a fold-excess distribution over a literature corpus, (B) a replication-at-cellular-concentration experiment, (C) a corpus-level association between (A) and later-discovered missing factors. The claim of novelty is really a claim about (A) over a frame, with (B) and (C) as extensions.
