# UQ-20261018-03 — Adversary #2 log (independent; first adversary's log not read)

## Restatement in my own words

Take one procedural registry that records, for every case, who did it and when. For each case, measure how long that operator had gone without doing this same procedure (and how many of them they did in the previous 30 and 90 days). Then ask two things. (a) Holding the operator fixed and controlling for how much they do overall in a year and over a career, plus case mix and calendar time, does a longer gap go with a worse patient outcome? (b) If it does, read the fitted curve backwards: how many days off does it take before expected outcome for that operator slips by some pre-stated amount — the number an aviation-style "currency" rule would have to put in a privileging manual — and does the data even contain long enough gaps to identify such a number?

In short: is pilot currency a real thing in surgery, and if so what is the number? Limb (a) is an estimation question about a coefficient; limb (b) is a threshold-derivation question about inverting that coefficient's curve.

My own arithmetic, labelled as mine: on the record's reported SEs, P1's any-procedure vs CABG-specific in-hospital coefficients differ by 0.035 pp/day with a standard error of at most sqrt(0.025^2 + 0.015^2) = 0.029 if independent, so the two are **not** statistically distinguishable even though each is individually non-null. The record's framing that the unrelated-procedure placebo came back "twice" the target is a point-estimate comparison that the stated uncertainty does not support; what *is* supported is that the placebo is itself non-null (0.067, SE 0.025).
