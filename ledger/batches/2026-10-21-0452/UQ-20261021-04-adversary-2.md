## Restatement in my own words

CMS now makes payers publish, once a year, what share of the prior-authorization requests they *received* they approved, denied, and overturned on appeal. Every one of those percentages is computed only over requests that actually arrived. So a payer that makes filing painful enough that doctors give up before filing looks *better* on the published number than a payer that accepts every request and denies some of them.

The candidate asks for two paired numbers, cell by cell, over a set of payer (and where available payer×service) cells fixed before any data is pulled: (i) the printed denial rate, and (ii) a provider-side rate — of clinical decisions that required prior authorization, what fraction died on the provider's side before anything was transmitted. Then: do payers with lower printed denial rates have *higher* pre-submission attrition (negative association), and in what share of cells does adding attrition flip the payer ranking, against a flip threshold written down in advance? A null — attrition small and uncorrelated — is offered as an answer too. A separate, oppositely-signed channel is carried alongside: requests that *were* filed and then auto-denied when the provider did not answer a request for more information, which inflate the printed rate rather than deflate it.

Put plainly: **is the number now being used to rank insurers partly a measure of how hard it is to file with them, and does it rank them backwards?**

## What the KFF 13 Aug 2026 brief actually says (this decides the novelty grade)

The record stakes its Medium grade on this document and says: "If that analysis itself raises care never requested, or pairs the published rate with any provider-side quantity, this candidate drops to Low."

**It does not.** On my read, the brief does not address care never requested, requests never filed, provider deterrence or abandonment, or any provider-side data source outside payer files. Its nearest passages are generic: "this practice may result in delays or denials in receiving necessary care", "as well as administrative burdens for patients and providers", and "Prior authorization denials are rarely appealed"; the only non-payer-file input is a KFF poll figure (24% of insured adults reporting a delayed service), i.e. patient-reported, not a measured provider-side rate. **The demotion condition does not fire, and the record's Medium novelty grade survives the test it set itself.**

Three corrections and one new bite come out of the same read, and they cut against the candidate:

- **The denominator is not standardized, and that is in the instrument, not in the question.** The brief does not state whether its percentages are over requests received or requests adjudicated; it says **"CMS currently provides suggested denominators"**, with standardization only *proposed* in a 2026 rule. P1's arrival-conditioning survives (both candidate denominators are request-based), but the candidate's left-hand variable in year 1 is a differently-constructed number payer by payer.
- **Gold carding is developed further than the record's second-hand version.** Quoted as returned: **"Insurers that rely heavily on gold carding may, in turn, report lower approval rates"**, **"as the approval rate no longer reflects the entirety of the procedures"**, and **"reported statistics from these insurers include only providers that may inherently have lower approval rates"**. Note these are KFF's own hedged formulations ("may"), and the third one is a *provider-composition* selection argument, which is closer to the candidate's selection logic than the record's Becker's-mediated rendering showed. It remains an exposure/composition argument about care that was never *required* to be requested, not about care that was required and never requested.
- **The volume↔denial inverse relationship is misattributed in the record.** The record (via Becker's) treats "insurers processing more requests denied a smaller share" as a finding of the Aug 2026 analysis. The brief instead attributes it to an **earlier** KFF analysis of **2024 Medicare Advantage** data — "the number of prior authorization determinations per enrollee" being "inversely related to the share of requests that were denied" — and says counts were not reported for 2025 at all. Attributing it to the 2026 cross-market analysis is a second-hand error worth striking from the record.
- **Figure to restate at its author's own threshold.** The record's Fecundity section speaks of "the reported 12%–18% denial range". KFF's own brief gives market-level insurer *spreads* of 5% (Elevance) to 17% (UnitedHealth Group) in Medicare Advantage and 3% (GuideWell) to 25% (Centene) in the ACA marketplace. 12%–18% is a market-average range taken from a trade headline, not the insurer-level range; the candidate's cross-payer design lives on the latter.

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional** — nothing Unresolved, and the record's own demotion condition (the KFF 13 Aug 2026 analysis, opened here) did not fire, so Medium novelty holds; but Disguise is Partial one query and one full text short of its sweep, Vagueness and Reason for silence are Partial, and the record's self-assessed well-posedness of 2/3 is below the 3/3 that Gate 5 requires for Validated. Promotion conditions, in order: read Kahan et al. 2006 in full to close the sentinel-effect neighbour and cite it in Gate 2; pre-declare "obstruction ranking" among its four meanings so the flip-share limb has an object; strike the misattributed volume↔denial relationship; and extend the silence mechanism to the parties (ePA intermediaries, integrated payer-providers) that do hold both terms.

**Single strongest killing point:** the cross-payer sign test's left-hand variable is not one quantity in year 1 — KFF's brief states insurers "reported only percentages, not counts" and that **"CMS currently provides suggested denominators"**, with standardization merely proposed in a 2026 rule, so each payer's printed rate is built on a denominator of its own choosing (the record's own F4 payer prints "adjudicated" against a narrative "received"). Denominator heterogeneity across payers is then confounded with exactly the submission-side attrition the design wants to detect, and — my inference from KFF's stated no-counts limitation, not KFF's conclusion — with no counts published, cells cannot be weighted or pooled and a cell-level flip threshold has no sampling interpretation. Taken with the record's own concession that the two attrition channels are oppositely signed and "obstruction" is undefined among four meanings, the flip-share limb is not yet stateable and a null is not yet interpretable. Non-fatal, but it caps this candidate at Provisional.
