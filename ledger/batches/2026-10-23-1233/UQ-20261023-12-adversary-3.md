## 1. Restatement in my own words

Reliability-based calibration-interval analysis takes the as-found results of instruments that come back to the lab, fits P(still in tolerance | elapsed time since last calibration), and sets the recall interval where that curve crosses a declared target. Everyone who does this treats the moment an instrument arrives as uninformative about how far it has drifted. The candidate asks, for at least one real fleet whose management records hold per calibration event the model, the due date (or the interval in force), the actual date, a numeric as-found deviation per characteristic and its tolerance: (a) is arrival timing (early / on time / overdue) independent of the as-found state once elapsed time, model and usage are controlled; and (b) if not, in what direction and by how many days (and what percent of the current interval) is the target-meeting interval mis-set by the selection that this dependence puts into the fitted curve. Two arms are posited: suspect instruments sent in early (excess error at short elapsed times) and healthy-looking instruments run overdue (a selected-healthy sample at long elapsed times).
