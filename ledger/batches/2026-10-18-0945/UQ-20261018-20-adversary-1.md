## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take a normal, healthy adult animal. Cells are dying in it all the time, at some tissue-specific
rate. The cell-death field has a catalogue of named ways a cell can die (apoptosis, necroptosis,
ferroptosis, pyroptosis, and so on), each with its own molecular signature. The question is: of the
deaths that actually happen in a healthy tissue, what fraction belongs to each name? The graded
version (V2) tightens this into a measurement protocol: in one declared tissue of one declared
animal, stain fixed tissue with a marker panel whose per-marker sensitivity and specificity you have
measured in that same tissue against deliberately induced deaths; report how many marker-positive
dying cells per unit tissue you see for each modality, with intervals, plus how many cells light up
for two modalities at once and how many dying cells you cannot classify at all; then, only where
someone has independently measured how long a corpse of that modality lingers before being eaten,
divide the standing count by that persistence time to get deaths per day; then check that your
per-modality deaths per day add up to an independently measured total turnover for that tissue.

So: a census of dying cells, sorted by cause of death, corrected for how long bodies stay at the
scene, and audited against the known total.

## My own arithmetic (labelled as mine, not any source's conclusion)

From F2's point estimates: 2x10^6 erythrocytes/s = 1.73x10^11 deaths/day; 0.5-1x10^6 neutrophils/s =
0.43-0.86x10^11/day; sum 2.2-2.6x10^11/day. Against the record's P1 total of ~0.33x10^12 = 3.3x10^11
cells/day, that is roughly two-thirds to four-fifths of all human daily cell deaths occurring in two
cell types that Brown attributes predominantly to death-by-phagocytosis rather than apoptosis.
Caveats I carry rather than hide: Brown's per-second rates are point estimates with no stated
uncertainty; P1's companion mass figure carries +/-20 g on 80 g/day (~25% relative) and its
per-cell-type intervals are wide; and the two are not independent inputs (both ultimately rest on a
~120-day erythrocyte lifespan and standard blood counts). So this is an order-of-magnitude
reconciliation showing where the mass of deaths sits, not a measured share. It is consistent in sign
with P1's own Methods split ("about half" of the daily total cleared by apoptosis-and-phagocytosis).
