B names three constructions as alternative "estimable quantities": Holford's "curvatures (terms that are orthogonal a linear term)" — that is, deviations from linearity — KNN's "second differences", and Carstensen's set "similar to Holfords curvatures"; and B subsumes second derivatives under curvature ("the second derivatives, or more generally curvatures"). MY OWN REASONING, grounded on A's quoted theorem: ξ is a MAXIMAL invariant under g, so every g-invariant function of θ is a function of ξ; Holford's deviation-from-linearity component of an effect is unchanged by adding a linear trend to that effect, hence g-invariant, hence a function of the second differences (and conversely, each has k−2 degrees of freedom per axis with k levels — my count). (4) THE TRANSPLANT'S OWN TERMS — does p = c + a survive the heap? (my own reasoning throughout, except where a source is named)
======================================================================

Exactness. With birth b and death d both read off the bytes-allocated clock (the record's Merlin quotation, which I did not open: "A perfect trace ... includes the birth and death time of all objects, measured in bytes allocated"), age at death is DEFINED as a := d − b, period as p := d and cohort as c := b. Then p = c + a is a definitional identity at the object level — exactly A's "i + j − 1 is the period" in index form — and nothing in the heap breaks it so long as all three axes share one clock. Mixing a bytes-allocated age with a wall-clock or mutator-time period would break it (the record says this; correct).

Continuity. The bytes clock advances only at allocation events, so observation is discrete and "exact" means exact to the allocation granularity (the entry's Merlin quotation: death time is "the time interval immediately following the one when it was last reachable"). The identity holds exactly on that discrete clock. Deaths arrive in batches at one clock instant (adversary 2's sourced point, Veroy & Guyer §3.6, unopened by me); ties are absorbed by cells of positive width, and the Poisson–piecewise-exponential approximation then needs exposure in object-bytes at risk rather than a head count (adversary 3's point) — none of which touches identification.

Aggregation. Once the analyst bins age and period, each (age-bin, period-bin) cell straddles two cohort bins unless Lexis triangles are used — the same issue demography handles (Carstensen 2007, cited by A and B; NOT OPENED by me). B's result adds that binning age and period in DIFFERENT widths introduces further non-identifiability ("a cyclic pattern in the previously estimable functions"). With an exact trace the analyst controls the lattice entirely and can keep it equal-interval on one clock, in which case A's theorem applies verbatim; the triangular Lexis region a run produces (no old objects early; right-censoring at exit) is the "generalized trapezoid" case A's Corollary 2 is reported to cover (I did not read the corollary's statement).

Interval censoring at GC boundaries — the case where deaths are observed only at collections g_1 < g_2 < .... An object born at b and first found dead at g_k has true death in (max(b, g_{k−1}), g_k]. Then p and a are interval-censored by the SAME interval while c = b is exact: the uncertainty lies along the object's own life-line (the 45° line of fixed cohort in the Lexis plane). The identity p = c + a still holds for the true values and for any imputed pair (p*, p* − b), so the ALGEBRA of identification is unchanged by censoring. What changes is three things. (i) The period axis is coarsened to a collector-defined grid, which in bytes is roughly regular for a fixed-size nursery (minor collections every nursery-size bytes) and irregular for major collections; if the age axis is kept finer than that grid the data are unequal-interval APC data in B's sense, with B's extra non-identifiability; if instead age, period and cohort are all counted in GC intervals (a discrete-time hazard P(die in interval k | alive at g_{k−1}), cloglog or binomial), the lattice is equal-interval in INDEX units and A's result holds on the index lattice, with the physical widths uneven and the interpretation in bytes non-uniform. (ii) Exposure is overcounted within an interval (dead-but-uncollected objects look alive), biasing fine-scale rates late. (iii) The estimand becomes collector-dependent because the collector's schedule defines the period cells — which is precisely the measurement difficulty Dolan's abstract states (record's read; not mine) and the reason presupposition 1 (an exact-liveness oracle) is load-bearing: without it the period axis is the collector's, not the program's. Right-censoring at program exit (immortal objects) is ordinary survival censoring and does not touch identifiability.

Net: the identity transfers exactly; coarsening the period axis does not break the algebra but (a) imports B's unequal-interval problem unless all three axes are coarsened alike, and (b) changes WHOSE period axis is being measured.
