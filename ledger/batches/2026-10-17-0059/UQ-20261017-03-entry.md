Domain(s): Other STEM — mathematics (experimental mathematics, automated conjecture generation); method field: statistics (selective inference / FDR).
Question (canonical): For the enumerable corpus of machine-generated numerical conjectures that passed their generator's own numerical-agreement acceptance threshold, what is the realized rate of non-identities among those adjudicated so far, and does it agree with the rate implied by that threshold under an explicit null model of the generator's search volume?
Alternative formulations:
- (Gate 1 reformulation, one permitted, used) Restricted to engines publishing both a per-conjecture acceptance precision d and a recoverable search volume M, and adjudicating accepted conjectures into {proved new, proved but known, disproved, numerically unverifiable, open}: what censored interval covers the non-identity share, and how does it compare with M*10^-d, scoring rediscovery separately?
- What digit threshold would a conjecture engine need at its actual search volume to hold its false-discovery rate below a stated level?
Motivating gap: Intake held that conjecture engines accept on a digit threshold with no null over the search space, hence uncalibrated multiplicity. Gate 2 falsified the central clause in weak form: RM (arXiv:1907.00205) states "the probability of finding a random match for an enumeration space of 10^9 and result accuracy of more than 50 digits, is smaller than 10^-40" (search space carried in the bound); ESMA (arXiv:2212.09470) accepts at 1000 decimals with "the probability for a false positive ... roughly 10^-1000" (per-candidate only). Residue after Gate 2: the realized side has never been tallied; RM's 10-digit first-stage MITM-RF filter; verification-arithmetic failures; threshold-free LLM conjectures.
Presuppositions: (1) dated enumerable corpus with recorded per-conjecture thresholds — PARTLY FALSE (thresholds engine-level/staged only; no dated snapshot verified). (2) search volumes recorded per conjecture — FALSIFIED for RM and ESMA (and for Ramanujan Library, per adversary). (3) resolution status ascertainable — CONTESTED: no aggregate tally, and no disproved conjecture reported in any published corpus, so the numerator is plausibly empty. (4) a null over the search space is specifiable — PARTLY ESTABLISHED, which cuts against the candidate (Odrzywolek's 1/sigma rule). (5) "false discovery" definable — holds only with a pre-specified adjudication rule plus a "numerically unverifiable" class. (6) thresholds stated in papers/code — VERIFIED at engine level. (7) NOT presupposed that any engine claimed calibration — upheld; RM and ESMA both disclaim it ("does not substitute the need for formal proof").
What would count as an answer: per engine, recover d and M (M by instrumenting released code), build an empirical null by re-running against random matched-complexity targets, adjudicate output into the five classes, report the non-identity share as a censored interval, and state the minimum d holding FDR below a stated level at the measured M. "Thresholds are conservative" is a full answer — and for d >= 100 it follows from M*10^-d without steps (i)-(iii), which is the defect that sinks significance.

Verification (post-batch, fresh-context checker, log UQ-20261017-03-verification.md): the Gate 2 "asked and answered" ground is materially weaker than the entry claimed, and the rejection stands on the triviality ground instead.
Odrzywolek (arXiv:2002.12690) carries the quoted rule verbatim — "Tested unique formulas count must not exceed 1/sigma, where sigma is relative numerical error of the target constant" — but he explicitly declines to answer the probability question the candidate asks: "Above criterion do not provide any numerical estimate for probability of successful identification", offering only a relative likelihood ranking, and noting that the "equivalence problem is undecidable in general". So the threshold-versus-search-volume question is asked there and left unanswered in probability terms, not asked and answered. The Ramanujan Machine's 10^-40 is NOT an acceptance threshold: it is a random-match probability for one worked case at an enumeration space of 10^9 and accuracy above 50 digits, and the paper states no minimum digit count for acceptance. Its procedure is staged — a 10-digit first-stage search, then "Every result found by our MITM-RF algorithm is tested numerically to 100 digits", with tests "for up to 2000 digits of accuracy". The Ramanujan Library (arXiv:2412.12361) does calibrate against random inputs, as the entry said, but hedged and without a rate: the PSLQ cutoff "RoI of 2 seems to comfortably separate random results from false positives", with LLL run on inputs "each with d uniformly random binary digits" and a suggested minimum of 1.3. It reports no numeric false-positive rate and no falsified conjecture; all its outputs "are not theorems, but rather conjectures awaiting proofs".
The second ground is confirmed and is now the one the verdict rests on. The acceptance precisions in actual use — 100 digits as the standard validation, up to 2000 — put the nominal false-identity rate so far below any realized rate that could be observed in a corpus of this size that the comparison is decided by the setup. No published report of a disproved machine-generated conjecture was found. That negative is weak and is recorded as such: four searches, few sources opened, titles only for most hits, and the Nature 2021 version of the Ramanujan Machine paper could not be opened at all. It is enough for attack 1 (triviality), which does not need the negative to be exhaustive — it needs only that no engine reports a disproved conjecture, which is what the engines' own papers say.
So the entry's "asked and answered at per-candidate resolution" is corrected to "asked at per-candidate resolution and explicitly not answered in probability terms", and the Gate 2 Low grade is kept for the narrower reason that the candidate's own alternative formulation is Odrzywolek's rule in inverted form, in the candidate's field and words. Routing is unaffected and remains none: a question whose answer the ledger's own Gate 4 shows to be pre-decided is not an open problem, and Odrzywolek's decision not to give a probability is a declaration about his criteria, not a posed question.
