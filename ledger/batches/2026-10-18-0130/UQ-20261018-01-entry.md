Domain(s): Medicine — primary care / general internal medicine; clinical informatics; diagnostic safety. Adjacent: pharmacoepidemiology (code-based phenotype validity).

Question (canonical): Restricted to label/result pairs with a consensus disconfirming rule computable from routine data — COPD code + post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC >= 0.7; hypothyroidism code + normal TSH >= 6 weeks after last levothyroxine supply; coeliac code + negative serology and negative biopsy on a documented gluten-containing diet — what is the 24-month cause-specific hazard and cumulative incidence of recorded retraction of the diagnosis (problem-list removal, "resolved"/inactivated status, exclusion code; pre-declared fallback, reported separately: >= 180 days without disease-specific prescribing) after the first disconfirming result, against matched person-time in labelled patients with no such result, and what share of each group is still receiving disease-specific therapy at 12 and 24 months? (Asthma + negative challenge and heart failure + normal natriuretic peptide are pre-specified secondary pairs; penicillin allergy + negative challenge is the positive control. This is the Gate 1 reformulation V2; V1 and its two failure points are in the record.)
Alternative formulations: Are clinical diagnoses falsifiable in practice — what is the hazard of a label being removed once the test that defines it comes back against it? / When the chart already contains the result that rules a condition out, how long does the condition stay on the problem list and on the prescription?

Motivating gap: Repeated studies find a large minority of chronic-disease labels unsupported by their defining criteria (Aaron 2017: current asthma ruled out in 33.1%, 95% CI 29.4-36.8, of 613 completers), and de-labelling there was a research intervention that never followed what community clinicians did next. The intake's framing — that nobody asks what routine care does when the disconfirming result is already filed — was FALSIFIED at Gate 2 for the index pair by Fortis et al. 2016, which asked it for COPD and reported both limbs. What survived intake was narrower (multi-pair, event-level, comparator-controlled), and that residue is a design upgrade on an asked question rather than a new question.

Presuppositions: P1 dated histories retrievable INCLUDING removal/resolution events — contested at intake as the weakest point, and partly EVIDENCED against the record by adversary 1: CPRD carries "resolved asthma" codes used to define a 16,023-patient cohort (Jones 2024) and NHS Digital publishes a COPD-resolved QOF refset, so the event is emitted and routinely used in UK primary care; claims data still emit none. P2 an agreed objective disconfirming rule per pair — contested; V2 demotes asthma and heart failure to secondary for this reason, and the COPD rule remains contestable (Fortis used LLN, V2 the fixed 0.7). P3 the result was visible to a clinician — contested; Rimawi 2013 records "no attempt to convey the result" to outside clinicians. P4 labels are intended to be falsifiable — contested; a register with resolved codes is by construction a current-disease register, which partly rebuts it, but "history of" retention may still be convention, so a null is ambiguous. P5 retraction is not assumed preferable. P6 confounding by record continuity is a design problem (prevalent-user and immortal-time structure), not a covariate problem.

What would count as an answer: a pre-registered multi-database cohort (CPRD/OpenSAFELY, a US EHR network, a Nordic registry) reporting per pair the 24-month cumulative incidence of recorded retraction after the first disconfirming result against matched no-result person-time, the share still on disease-specific therapy at 12 and 24 months in each arm, the penicillin-allergy positive control, and the prescribing-cessation sensitivity analysis where removal events are not emitted. A hazard indistinguishable from comparator with therapy continuing answers it one way; a materially raised hazard with therapy stopping answers it the other. A finding that removals are never recorded falsifies P1 and returns it to Gate 1.

Significance — consequence: if the retraction hazard matches comparator and therapy continues, every code-defined chronic-disease denominator — QOF-style registers, EHR phenotype libraries, HCC-style risk adjustment, code-based trial recruitment — carries a false-positive fraction that is absorbing rather than transient, and de-labelling trials need maintenance arms. If the hazard is raised and therapy stops, the one-third prevalence figures measure a queue being cleared and the overdiagnosis reading weakens. Deflated by prior A: for COPD the direction is already published, so the marginal consequence is generalisation and magnitude, not sign.
Significance — audience: EHR-phenotyping and pharmacoepidemiology methodologists; primary-care quality bodies that already own resolved/exclusion coding (NHS Digital refsets; a CPRD-approved resolved-asthma study exists); de-labelling trialists in respiratory, endocrine and allergy medicine; diagnostic-safety researchers; payers using diagnosis-based risk adjustment. Adversary 1 found this audience already acting on the pieces (Jones 2024; AAAAI 2023 on pharmacy records not updated after delabelling; Rimawi's 63% vs 0% beta-lactam receipt by record status).
Significance — fecundity: retraction hazard by who filed the result turns result-acknowledgement auditing into an exposure; faster retraction where the EHR emits an event turns the data model into a treatment; "history of" conversion rather than deletion becomes a measurable intermediate category.
Significance — anchoring: misdiagnosis-prevalence anomaly (Aaron 2017; COPD spirometry series); the problem-list literature's addition/deletion asymmetry; penicillin-allergy delabelling and relabelling; chronic-condition code persistence in claims.

Reason for silence: claimed as a disciplinary boundary plus an incentive/data-model gap (stock measured by clinical epidemiology, flow owned by informatics whose published quantities are all presence-side; no removal event in claims; "history of" as the safe default).
