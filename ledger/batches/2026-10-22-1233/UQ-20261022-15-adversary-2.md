## Restatement in my own words

Pick a frozen release of a shared molecular line-parameter compilation (HITRAN-class) and a declared set of operational greenhouse-gas column retrievals. Keep only the transitions that carry most of the Jacobian-weighted absorption in those retrievals' fitting windows. For each of the six HITRAN-format parameters of those transitions, follow the compilation's own per-parameter reference index back to the publication(s) the number actually came from, and count how many of those are *mutually independent* determinations under a rule declared in advance at two strictness levels (strict: different apparatus, calibration standard, sample, authors, theory/fitting code — each criterion scored separately; weak: different publication). Report three things: the count per parameter and whether the determinations agree inside their stated uncertainties; the fraction of sensitivity-weighted parameter mass that rests on one determination or fewer at each strictness level; and, for each such parameter, which missions and ground networks actually read it, taken from each retrieval's own documentation rather than assumed.

In one line: **is the world's greenhouse-gas remote sensing resting, in sensitivity-weighted terms, on a handful of un-replicated laboratory numbers, and if so which missions share which of them?** It is a provenance census with a cross-instrument sharing map, not a measurement of retrieval error.
