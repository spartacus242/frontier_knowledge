## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take a hospital that can say which bag of which drug went into *this particular* cannula, not merely into this patient. Over many such cannulas, fit a survival model for the cannula dying early, with one random effect per patient, per person who put it in, per ward, and per manufacturing lot, plus a term for what was infused. Two questions. (a) Descriptively, on the log-hazard scale, how big is the infusate's slice of the total between-cluster variance — separately for each way a line dies (phlebitis, infiltration/extravasation, blockage, falling out, infection) — and with an uncertainty interval; any causal reading of "attributable" is demoted to a secondary analysis because sick patients get both the harsh drugs and the bad veins. (b) The incumbent rule that sends a drug to a central line if it is above ~600 mOsm/L or outside pH ~5–9: does that binary rule actually separate the high- from the low-hazard infusates, and is it well calibrated — judged both against the fitted infusate hazard and against extravasation *injury* — compared with an index fitted to the same data and cross-validated across hospitals?

In short: is the chemistry-threshold gatekeeping rule any good at predicting the harm it exists to prevent, and how much of that harm is the chemistry's fault at all?

## What the opened papers actually say

**Bahl 2022 (P2) — read in full.** No pH, no osmolarity, no threshold rule anywhere: exposure is the binary INS vesicant/irritant label ("Specifically, vesicants and irritant infusates as recognized by the INS were noted") plus a dose count. Methods are time-dependent extended Cox ("the effect of vesicants on catheter failure was assessed with time-dependent extended survival analysis"); **no random effects, no frailty, no variance components**, and clustering of multiple catheters per patient is not addressed. Any-vesicant adjusted HR 1.17 (95% CI 0.76–1.81), p=0.477 in Results — the fetched text flags an internal inconsistency with the Abstract's 1.71 on the same CI and p (I report that as what the publisher page showed me, not as my own recomputation). Dose-response flat (1.20 / 1.51 / 0.98 across 1, 2, ≥3 doses). Extravasation was *not* a predefined endpoint; 10 events, none needing invasive treatment. Attribution limitation in the authors' own words: "it was not always clear what access point was used for the caustic infusion". Two consequences: (i) P2 is **not** a near-duplicate and the Gate 2 Medium grade's blocking condition is cleared; (ii) the paper states that the INS list it tests is "based on infusate osmolarity thresholds that increase the likelihood of these complications" — the field itself reads the thresholds as *complication-likelihood* claims, which **corroborates** the candidate's contested presupposition 4 rather than refuting it.

**Ryder et al. 2020 (new prior, missed by Gate 2, in the field's own flagship pharmacy journal).** Randomised, blinded, controlled ovine trial, n=20 sheep, 40 midline catheters, 6 arms: vancomycin 4 and 10 mg/mL (cytotoxicity), doxycycline 1 mg/mL and acyclovir 3.5 mg/mL (pH), Clinimix at 675 and 930 mOsm/L (osmolarity), with contralateral 0.9% NaCl controls. Abstract: "Infusate osmolarity, pH, and cytotoxicity were investigated as risk factors for midline catheter failure." Outcomes: catheter failure plus a histopathological vessel-injury score. Failure 95% test vs 60% control. Four of six arms met the vessel-injury criterion — the exceptions being **Clinimix 675 mOsm/L** and acyclovir. No cutoff analysis, no variance partition, no human calibration, and the page states no numeric pH values. Co-author **Marc Stranz** is the author of the Stranz & Kastango document the Gate 2 record names as the threshold rule's own source.

**Yasuda et al. 2025 (new prior, missed by Gate 2; abstract level only).** 3,410 PIVCs in 1,351 patients, 23 Japanese ICUs, prospective cohort (AMOR-VENUS), **unit of analysis the catheter**, exposure total noradrenaline dose **administered via the PIVC** as a time-dependent variable, time-dependent multilevel Cox regression with DAG-defined confounders and smoothing splines. Linear model null: HR 1.06 (95% CI 0.93–1.20). Spline suggested risk above ~6 mg total dose (post hoc, exploratory). No pH or osmolarity. No variance component, VPC, ICC or frailty variance reported in anything I could read — and the levels of the "multilevel" model are not stated on the page I opened, so I record that as unread, not as absent.
