# UQ-20261015-06 — Adversary #1 log (Gate 4)

## Restatement (my own words)

Take a dense decoder-only LLM on a fixed serving engine. Swap the trained checkpoint for random numbers of exactly the same tensor shapes (either Gaussian matched to each tensor's mean/std, or the engine's own "dummy" fill). Hold numeric format, batch, prompt length and forced output length constant so the GPU executes the same kernels on the same byte counts. Does the board burn a measurably different number of joules per token (beyond noise, with pre-set bands of >3% = yes, <1% = no), and if so does the difference live in prefill, decode or both?

In short: is there a value-dependent energy term in LLM inference big enough to matter, and the trained-vs-random swap is the probe.

## Recommended verdict

Provisional. Nothing is Unresolved, so not Rejected; but attacks 1, 2, 4, 6 and 7 are only Partial, novelty is at best Medium against vendor guidance the record never opened (CUTLASS fill-pattern note; AMD toggle-rate blog), and the significance case leans on a dummy-weight energy practice that no search could find. Promote only if a published energy or power figure derived from dummy/random weights is located, or if a pilot shows the (a)-vs-(b) arm is non-null.

Strongest killing point: the question is a single measurement of an effect NVIDIA and AMD already document as benchmarking doctrine, and its practical stake (energy figures polluted by dummy weights) has no verified instance — MLPerf forbids weight replacement and every energy paper opened uses trained checkpoints — so no one has asked because no one has needed the answer.
