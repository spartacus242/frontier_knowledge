Question (canonical): Within a single ART or cell-therapy facility, among cryostored units of one specimen class frozen under one protocol, what share of the residual between-unit variance in a per-unit post-thaw endpoint is attributable to the unit's static storage coordinate (vessel, canister, rack, height), estimated with cohort, calendar date, donor and operator adjusted and with re-racking and tank-transfer events as the identifying variation, and what is the effect size per 10 °C of a pre-specified time-weighted static storage temperature derived from the facility's own thermal map? (Gate 1 reformulation; it is the version judged here.)
Alternative formulations:
- Gate 0 intake version (compound, recorded and superseded): does a unit's recorded storage coordinate plus the retrieval-event history of its neighbourhood predict post-thaw outcome, and does between-facility variation in in-dewar thermal history account for part of the published between-facility spread in post-thaw survival? The retrieval-exposure limb is answered (Prior 1) and the between-facility limb had no identified estimand; both were dropped by the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation.
- Is a frozen sample's shelf position in the tank an unrecognised exposure variable for its post-thaw viability?
Motivating gap: Vertical temperature non-uniformity inside vapour-phase LN2 dewars is measured and modelled (order 8–14 °C) and transient warming of neighbouring inventory during retrieval is documented, but no study attaches either to per-unit post-thaw outcomes via the inventory coordinate, and no published variance decomposition of post-thaw outcome contains a storage-location term. Gate 2 correction: the intake's claim that upper positions sit near the ~−130 °C glass transition is not supported — the warmest measured position was −181.5 °C (CBS V-1500, nine probes, 24 h) and the warmest modelled monitoring-point average −165.4 °C (Xiong et al. 2026), against Tg −126 to −121 °C. The gradient is real but ~10 °C, not ~60 °C.
Presuppositions:
1. Per-unit storage coordinate recorded and retrievable — ESTABLISHED (Prior 1 logged cane, canister and tank position for all 9,200 cycles); contested for legacy archives.
2. The gradient reaches the devitrification/glass-transition region — CONTESTED, not falsified. Both position-resolved sources put every occupied position 35–65 °C below Tg, but the measured one is a near-empty isothermal vessel marketed for uniformity, and the record's reading of Prior 2 (risk confined to a lifting *neck* transient) is unsupported by the source it cites, which describes −50/−70 °C as vapour-phase temperatures within the dewar and warns that vapour-phase *storage* carries devitrification risk. The reformulated question needs only a non-zero gradient, which is established.
3. Retrieval/opening events reconstructable — ESTABLISHED at one facility, with explicit bystander-counting rules (Prior 1).
4. A per-unit post-thaw endpoint exists and is linkable — ESTABLISHED for ART and cell therapy; false for many research biobanks.
5. Position not fully confounded with calendar time, cohort or donor — CONTESTED; the main design threat, confirmed in Prior 1's data (storage time ≈83 → ≈301 days across exposure groups, P < 0.001). This is why the re-racking/tank-transfer instrument is load-bearing.
6. Sub-Tg degradation cumulative and monotone in temperature and time — CONTESTED; no quantitative sub-Tg rate law for the −195/−180 regime was sourced by either the record or the adversary.
7. Separable from protocol variance at achievable sample sizes — CONTESTED; a 9,200-cycle study could not resolve the larger retrieval exposure.
What would count as an answer: A within-facility analysis of a few thousand units of one specimen class with (a) storage coordinate, (b) a thermal history logged at several heights or reconstructed from a mapped gradient, and (c) a per-unit post-thaw endpoint; outcome regressed on coordinate and on a pre-specified time-weighted thermal summary, cohort/date/donor/operator adjusted, identified off re-racking and tank transfers. Deliverable: a variance component for storage location plus an effect size per 10 °C, judged against a pre-registered null band from the facility's own replicate variability. A null in a facility with a documented gradient counts as an answer.

Verification by the run session (two fresh-context checkers, one over Prior 1 and one over the standards claim behind attack 6, each handed the claim and its source but not the reviewer's reasoning; Prior 1's checker ran 14 searches and 8 fetches with 5 refusals, the standards checker 14 and 6 with 7; full logs in batches/2026-10-16-1433/UQ-20261016-36-verification.md and -verification-standards.md). The novelty negative holds and is now triply sourced. Attack 6 does not hold at the standard it was graded on, and is downgraded from Unresolved to Partial.
(i) PRIOR 1 IS CONFIRMED NOT TO BE ABOUT THE CANDIDATE'S OBJECT, which is the load-bearing check. Zhang et al. 2023 estimates nothing about where a unit sat. The recorded cane/canister/tank coordinates are used ONLY as bookkeeping, to reconstruct which straws shared a cane and therefore which cane movements each straw rode along with; position is an input to building the exposure and never itself an exposure. Table 4's predictor set is EAF plus ten clinical and laboratory confounders, of which the only storage-related one is storage TIME. There is no tank-, canister-, cane-, rack- or height-level term, no random effect for location, no ICC and no variance component anywhere in the paper.
(ii) THE MECHANISM IN THE RECORD IS WRONG, and the correct one is more interesting. The record describes EAF as counting events in which a neighbouring unit was retrieved while the index unit stayed in the vessel. It does not: "the EAF of a straw was decided by the frequency of the cane contained with the straw in and out of the LN2 tank", and "the cane containing a straw was taken out from a canister in the LN2 tank into a LN2 container". The index straw is itself lifted out of the tank, into a Styrofoam LN2 container, every time its cane moves — including when the movement is for a co-located straw. The mechanism is CO-TRAVEL, not bystander exposure. The co-location element is real (up to nine straws share a cane, and "If more than one straw was taken out or put into one cane simultaneously, EAF was added as the rule of one straw"), but the exposure is a count of the index unit's own removals, which makes Prior 1 a study of handling events and leaves static position untouched — strengthening, not weakening, the novelty finding.
(iii) Prior 1's estimand is mis-stated twice in the record. EAF counts air-exposure movements in BOTH directions, out of and into the tank, and is incremented by the index embryo's own vitrification and warming, so it is not a count of other units' retrievals. And it is not modelled as a count at all: it enters as a five-level CATEGORICAL variable (2 / 4 / 6 / 8 / >=10) with EAF = 2 as reference, reported as four category contrasts plus an overall P per embryo-day block, with no per-event slope, no dose-response test and no trend statistic. The authors state the limitation themselves, verbatim: "The third limitation is that EAF is calculated by the number of exposed-to-air during the procedure rather than duration time of exposed-to-air."
(iv) The headline null checks out and must be carried with its own uncertainty. D3, EAF >= 10, clinical pregnancy: adjusted OR 0.867 (95% CI 0.672-1.119, P = 0.274), relative to EAF = 2, in the 3,313-cycle day-3 subgroup, inside a block whose overall EAF P = 0.232. The interval is log-symmetric about the estimate (implied SE 0.1301, z = -1.096, recomputed two-sided p = 0.273 against the printed 0.274) and spans 1: compatible with anything from 33% lower to 12% higher odds. The companion live-birth value is 0.898 (0.688-1.173, P = 0.431). The stratum counts sum exactly (3,313 + 4,530 + 1,357 = 9,200). Two smaller corrections: post-warming survival is DEFINED in Methods and reported descriptively in Tables 1-3 but is not an endpoint of the adjusted regression, so there is no adjusted estimate for it; and EAF = 2's status as reference is printed in Table 4, not stated in any prose sentence.
(v) THE NOVELTY NEGATIVE IS INDEPENDENTLY REPRODUCED A THIRD TIME. Thirteen further searches for any study, in any cryostorage field, estimating an association or variance component between a unit's static in-vessel position and a per-unit post-thaw outcome returned NOT FOUND. Everything that came back fell into an excluded bucket: storage duration, storage phase or temperature, transient warming during retrieval (where Prior 1 itself sits), chamber thermal uniformity with no outcome attached, and hardware design comparisons. Gate 2 found this, the adversary's nine Disguise queries in eight untried vocabularies reproduced it, and this is the third independent context to return it.
(vi) ATTACK 6 IS DOWNGRADED TO PARTIAL, on two grounds. First, its exhaustive negative cannot be supported by what was actually read: of nine document families named, four were never opened (AABB, ISO 20387, ISO 21973/24190, the GMP annexes, SPREC), and of those opened, the storage clauses sat outside the readable text in three (FACT-JACIE C10/D9, NetCord-FACT D6/D7, HFEA GN 17); the only free ISBER full text reachable was the THIRD EDITION of 2012, and that read stopped around section D3, before the Biological Material Tracking section whose own contents line reads "I2.100 Specimen Location". The honest form of the claim is narrower than "nothing shows". Second, the objection's central leg is mis-sourced: ISBER C8.000 does not say to place worst-case material at the warmest point. What it says is that "different locations in the equipment might exhibit different temperatures depending on the size and age of the unit" and that mapping is done "so that warm and cold spots that could be problematic for material storage can be identified". The existing-control argument was the reason attack 6 was graded fatal-adjacent, and the document it was attributed to does not make it.
(vii) The one genuine refutation runs the other way, and it helps the candidate. NetCord-FACT Cord Blood Standards 8th edition B9.2.4 requires "Location within the storage device" and B9.2.3 a "Storage device identifier" as inventory fields held against the unit. So the candidate's exposure variable is already recorded at unit level in accredited cord-blood banks — a feasibility finding, not a significance one, since recording a location for retrieval is category (a) and says nothing about treating it as a quality attribute. No documented instance of any facility, regulator or accreditation body using in-vessel position as a decision input in an investigation, deviation, recall or release decision was found.
(viii) The strongest near-miss in the standards, which the batch did not find: NetCord-FACT A4 defines a "Representative sample" as an "Aliquot of the final cord blood product that is stored under the same conditions as the cord blood unit." That is the natural place for a standard to require co-location or matched position, and in the readable text it requires matched CONDITIONS only. A standard that governs the one aliquot whose job is to stand in for the unit, and does not require it to sit in the same place, is a sharper statement of the candidate's gap than anything in the Gate 2 record.
