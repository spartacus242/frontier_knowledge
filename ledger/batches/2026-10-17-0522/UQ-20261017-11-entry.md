Domain(s): Biology — comparative/evolutionary genomics; reference-genome assembly methodology (EBP/VGP-era pipelines). Method-owning neighbours: statistical screening theory (false-rejection rate of a default-on filter); assembly benchmarking (gene-content metrics).
Question (canonical): In reference genome assemblies produced by the standard Earth BioGenome-era pipeline, what fraction of genuine recent gene duplicates is removed by the haplotypic-duplication purging step (purge_dups / purge_haplotigs plus manual curation), and by how much does that removal shift published estimates of gene-family expansion and contraction rates?
Alternative formulations: (i) When an assembly pipeline is tuned to remove false duplications, how many real young duplicate genes go with them, and does the resulting gene-content bias propagate into comparative-genomic rate estimates? (ii) Is the apparent scarcity of very young, near-identical gene duplicates in reference assemblies a biological fact or a residue of duplicate-purging? Verification (post-batch, four fresh-context checkers, logs UQ-20261017-11-verification-purgedups.md, -guiglielmoni.md, -asmgene.md, -hemizygous.md): **the rejection does not stand.** Both legs of adversary 3's fatal attack-4 objection are unsupported as stated, the Gate 2 ground against it is weaker than claimed, and two of the entry's own prior characterisations were wrong in the candidate's favour. Verdict moves Rejected -> Provisional.
**Leg 1, the purge_dups mechanism.** The kill held that purge_dups only tests contigs below the haploid/diploid coverage transition, so a fixed young duplicate at diploid depth at each copy cannot be purged as a haplotig at all. Coverage gating is real and documented — the README says "Contigs with average coverage below mid coverage are tested for haplotypic duplications", and the paper's heterozygous-overlap path fires only when both contigs fall below the cutoff. But "cannot be removed at all" appears in neither the paper nor the README, and the code as relayed carries an alignment-only haplotig branch (`flt_by_bm_mm`, labelling HAPLOTIG from base-match and mismatch fractions with no coverage test), with the cutoff-index mapping unresolved because `opt.h` was not seen. The tool's own authors concede the failure mode the candidate asks about: dropping `get_seqs -e` "may delete false positive duplications"; of the middle-sequence removals, "Some of them are real, while others may not."; and purge_dups has "only a limited ability to explicitly handle repeats". hifiasm has a coverage ceiling (`--purge-max`: "If the coverage of a contig is higher than this bound, don't apply purge duplication") but its docs warn that a larger value "may collapse repeats or segmental duplications". The checker adds its own mechanism, labelled as its own: near-identical paralogs multi-map, so reads split between copies and each copy shows reduced depth, which can make a genuine duplicate look haplotig-like. purge_haplotigs could not be read. So a diploid-depth fixed duplicate is *unlikely* to be purged on the coverage-gated path, which is a real finding, but the sources do not rule it out and the tool's authors say real duplications can be deleted. A kill that cannot be sourced is Partial at best: **attack 4 drops from Unresolved to Partial.**
**Leg 2, the convention claim, is UNVERIFIED.** The kill also held that hemizygous copies moving to the alternate assembly is the haploid-reference convention rather than an error. Neither primary source could be opened: Rhie et al. 2021 (doi 10.1038/s41586-021-03451-1) failed by DOI and at nature.com, the EBP standards paper was never opened, and the NCBI glossary defines neither "primary assembly" nor "pseudohaplotype" — it defines only "alternate locus" and "linked pseudohaplotype assemblies". The 2020 bioRxiv preprint of Rhie et al. names false duplications as "one of the most common assembly errors" and describes purge_haplotigs removing "retained false duplicated contigs", but the word "hemizygous" does not appear and nothing read defines the relocation either as correct or as a loss. No standard read prescribes a target or acceptable false-removal rate, and no QC statistic is required for over-removal. Per the ledger's own rule this blocks Validated and names a promotion condition; it cannot reject.
**The Gate 2 ground is weaker than the adversary claimed.** Guiglielmoni et al. 2021 (doi 10.1186/s12859-021-04118-3) was indeed missed and is a relevant neighbour, but it is not the dedicated overpurging benchmark that falsifies the gap sentence. It runs on **one genome** (Adineta vaga, ~102 Mb haploid / 204.6 Mb diploid) with 7 assemblers and 3 purging tools, and purging is a near single-factor contrast only within each assembler and read set — the study also varies read filtering, technology and depth. Overpurging is measured **only** as Illumina k-mer completeness (KAT comp; "a lower value indicates that too many k-mers were lost (overpurging)"), with ~50% the target for a well-collapsed haploid assembly and the paper itself conceding that 50% could also come from a mix of missing and uncollapsed regions. There is **no reference genome or ground truth for genuine duplicates**, no annotation, and no gene-family or ortholog analysis; BUSCO is the only gene-content measure. It reports no fraction of genuine gene duplicates lost, and its one overpurging statement is that HaploMerger2 "seemed to overpurge assemblies of the filtered datasets". So it does not supply the candidate's estimand, and the metric-blindness reason for silence survives in narrowed form.
