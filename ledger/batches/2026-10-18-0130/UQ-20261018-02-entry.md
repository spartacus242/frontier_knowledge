Domain(s): Medicine — obstetrics / intrapartum care (target field, whose literature decides novelty and whose guideline bodies would act). Method-owning neighbours: measurement-error biostatistics (coarsened/heaped-data likelihoods), longitudinal methodology (informative observation processes), metrology.

Question (canonical): In a labour-curve dataset with per-examination dilatation records (Consortium on Safe Labor, WHO BOLD, or LaPS), under (i) an error model for digital cervical dilatation estimated from paired same-time-point measurements — replicate independent examiners primary, an instrumented/ultrasound reference secondary — comprising an error variance (as a function of true dilatation), examiner random effects and an explicit whole-centimetre coarsening process, and (ii) a jointly fitted informative-observation-time model for the examination schedule: (a) what is the shift in the median and 95th-centile dilatation-versus-time curves and in the implied action line, stated relative to the published naive fit and to that fit's own confidence bands; (b) what proportion of recorded labour-dystocia / failure-to-progress diagnoses change classification under the error-corrected curve at the published threshold, applied mechanically as written; and (c) how do (a) and (b) decompose between the measurement-error and observation-timing components? Measurand declaration is load-bearing: true dilatation is defined procedurally as the maximal internal os diameter an examiner using a stated standardised technique and examination pressure would obtain. (V1 intake form and the reasoning for the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation: batches/2026-10-18-0130/UQ-20261018-02-record.md.)

Alternative formulations: (1) If cervical dilatation were measured without examiner error, would the partogram's action line and the diagnosis of failure to progress move, and for how many women? (2) Is "slow labour" partly an artefact of how dilatation is measured rather than of how labour proceeds?

Motivating gap: Presupposition inversion. Modern labour curves (Zhang/CSL; WHO Labour Care Guide) and the dystocia thresholds read off them are built entirely from digital estimates of dilatation. The untested presupposition is that digital assessment is a measurement whose error is small relative to the between-woman variation the curves describe. Error magnitudes have been published since 1995 and are routinely cited as a limitation; nothing found propagates an error model back into a labour-curve dataset. Two features make the error non-classical: records are almost exclusively whole centimetres (coarsening, not Gaussian noise) and examinations are more frequent when progress is doubted (informative observation times). Anchors to a live anomaly — the partograph's alert/action lines have near-null diagnostic accuracy for severe adverse birth outcomes, with no accepted explanation.

Presuppositions: (1) A reference substantially more accurate than digital examination exists — CONTESTED; load-bearing for the secondary route only, since replicate examiners are primary. (2) The error distribution is estimable — CONTESTED; rounding toward decision-relevant values (9 cm, "fully") is differential and only weakly identified, declared as a bounded sensitivity analysis, not a fitted term. (3) Examination times are accurately recorded and the schedule conditionally ignorable — CONTESTED and probably false; modelled explicitly, which is why limb (c) exists. (4) Whole-centimetre digit preference is present and measurable in the source data — NOT VERIFIED by Gate 2 or by either adversary; a step-0 data check. (5) Dystocia thresholds are applied as written, so reclassification counts mean something clinically — CONTESTED; limb (b) narrowed to a mechanical count, clinical meaning declared a separate interpretive step. (6) "True dilatation" is well defined at a time point for a deformable cervix — handled by the procedural measurand declaration.

What would count as an answer: Two linked deliverables under a pre-declared plan. (i) An error model for digital dilatation fitted to paired same-time-point measurements in labouring women — replicate examiners (primary) or digital versus reference instrument (secondary) — large enough to estimate error variance as a function of true dilatation, examiner random effects and the coarsening process, with the measurand stated. (ii) Re-estimation of a published labour-curve dataset under that error model and under an informative-observation-time model, reporting the curve and action-line shifts, the dystocia reclassification proportion, and the decomposition between the two artefacts. A negative answer is pre-committed as a result: movement entirely within the published fit's own confidence bands answers the question negatively and retires the field's commonest standing objection to partogram-based dystocia diagnosis.

Significance — consequence: If YES (error and informative timing account for a large share), the published 95th centiles are inflated and the shape partly flattened by examiner noise and coarsening, so the Labour Care Guide's per-centimetre expected durations and any action line derived from them are calibrated partly to the instrument rather than to labour; the implied response is not a recalibrated threshold but a different measurement or a decision rule robust to ±1 cm, and a share of caesareans for dystocia becomes attributable to examination error — a quantity no one has. If NO (movement inside the published fit's own bands), the commonest standing objection to partogram-based dystocia diagnosis is quantitatively retired, attention is redirected to the real source of the alert/action lines' near-null accuracy, and the clinical case for instrumented cervicometry — currently resting on agreement statistics alone — is substantially weakened. Both directions change a decision.

Significance — audience: (1) WHO's intrapartum-care guideline group and the national bodies setting dystocia criteria against these curves (NICE; ACOG/SMFM) — they would learn whether their thresholds are calibrated to examiner noise, and the answer decides whether the fix is a threshold, a measurement or a robust rule. (2) The CSL, BOLD and LaPS analysis teams, who hold the data and already run interval-censored and threshold-regression machinery (P2 proves access and feasibility). (3) Manufacturers and regulators of instrumented cervicometry — an active device trial (DilaCheck, NCT03440723) and the sonopartogram programmes are validated on agreement statistics alone, which is exactly the evidence the candidate would replace or refute. (4) Measurement-error and informative-visit biostatisticians. Audience reality is evidenced, not asserted: both adversaries independently found that Zhang, Troendle, Souza and Oladapo — the CSL/BOLD/WHO-LCG author set, i.e. audiences 1 and 2 — published a signed BJOG letter (2022;129(11):1939-1940) answering a correspondence attack on exactly this question-shape, with a de Vries reply, inside a year. Significance — fecundity: Newly askable — the minimum dilatation measurement precision at which a given action line retains its stated operating characteristics, i.e. a performance specification for cervicometry devices, which currently have none; whether EXAMINER IDENTITY is an exposure in dystocia diagnosis and caesarean risk; whether part of the Friedman-versus-Zhang difference is a measurement-PRACTICE difference (examiner continuity, examination frequency, rounding convention) rather than a population or practice difference, reopening a settled historical dispute on new grounds; and whether station, effacement and position carry the same artefact into the decisions resting on them.

Significance — anchoring: The partograph's alert and action lines have near-null diagnostic accuracy for severe adverse birth outcomes and dilatation over time is a poor predictor of them, with no accepted explanation; measurement error is an obvious candidate nobody has quantified. Also anchors to unexplained inter-unit variation in caesarean-for-dystocia rates, and to P1's standing and unanswered claim that these regression methods "should not be routinely used to define labour progress", which left the field with a criticism and no replacement.
