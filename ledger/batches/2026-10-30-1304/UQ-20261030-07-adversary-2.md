## Restatement (my own words, before any attack)

Take the "transmission bottleneck" — the number of virus particles/genomes that actually found an
infection in a newly infected person, N_b, as estimated by comparing deep-sequenced minor-variant
frequencies in a donor's specimen with the recipient's. The field reports one such number per
pathogen (or per route). The candidate asks whether that number is a property of the *transmitter*:
if one person infects several people, do all of their recipients get similarly sized founding
populations? Formally: fit a variance-components model to log10 N_b over transmission events, with
the donor as the grouping factor, and report the donor-level intraclass correlation — the share of
between-event variance that sits at the donor level — with an interval against a permutation null,
and then report how much of that donor share survives adjustment for donor viral load, symptom
status and days since onset. V2 adds: recipient specimen within 3 days of the recipient's own
symptom onset; one pre-specified beta-binomial exact estimator; 2% iSNV calling threshold; >=100x
coverage; and a frame-admission gate of at least 15 donors each with >=2 qualifying recipients,
below which the result is reported as "not estimable at the pre-declared precision".

So: is the width of the founding-population funnel a trait of the person pouring, or of the route
and the person receiving?
