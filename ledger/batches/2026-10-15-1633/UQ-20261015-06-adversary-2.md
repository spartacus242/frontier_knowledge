# UQ-20261015-06 — Gate 4, adversary #2

## Restatement (own words)
Take a dense decoder-only LLM on a fixed serving stack, with every tensor shape, kernel, batch and token count held constant. If the numbers inside the weights change from trained values to statistically matched noise, or to the engine's tiny "dummy" fill, does the joules-per-token reading on the GPU board move beyond measurement noise? If it does, does the change come from compute-bound prefill, memory-bound decode, or both? Underneath, this asks whether the operand-value (bit-switching) power effect seen in synthetic GEMMs survives into whole-model LLM inference at a size that matters.

## Recommended verdict
**Provisional.** No attack is Unresolved. Validated is excluded because the Disguise attack ran none of the 8 required new queries (search budget exhausted), and because Reason for silence and Significance stay unevidenced. To promote it: run ≥8 new Disguise queries (vLLM/SGLang GitHub issues on dummy-weight power or throughput, Chinese and Japanese ML-systems venues, toggle-aware/glitch-power vocabulary, answer-side phrasing), and check whether Gregersen/Patel/Choukse published any follow-up on real model weights.

Strongest killing point: N1's authors already said they "are trying to modify model weights into value ranges that use less power". The Darmstadt topic asks how "different weight values" affect "power of a decoder-only LLM". The candidate may therefore be the unremarkable first measurement inside an already-announced programme, an open item rather than an un-asked question, and no adversary has independently searched for that.
