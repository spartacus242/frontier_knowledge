## Question (canonical)

**V2 (Gate 1 reformulation — the one permitted; scored as canonical).** In a thin crystal containing isolated substitutional impurities or vacancies of one species, measured by multislice electron ptychography at two or more specimen temperatures on the **same individual defects**: (a) does the per-atom mean-square thermal displacement u²(n) at coordination shell n from the defect differ from the value predicted by harmonic lattice dynamics for the **same defective supercell** (not the pristine host); (b) does the ratio R(n,T) = u²_meas(n,T)/u²_harm(n,T) vary with temperature — which a purely harmonic mass-and-force-constant perturbation forbids and a locally anharmonic or resonant defect mode requires; and (c) over how many shells n does any departure decay to the pristine-host value?

**V1 (as submitted at intake).** Around an isolated substitutional impurity or vacancy in a thin crystal, does the per-atom mean-square thermal displacement measured by multislice electron ptychography deviate from the harmonic-lattice prediction, does its temperature dependence measured on the SAME defect at two or more specimen temperatures depart from linear-in-T as a locally anharmonic or resonant defect mode requires, and over how many atomic shells does the departure decay?

**Why reformulated (well-posedness only, no scope change).** Two defects in V1, both load-bearing.
1. **"Departs from linear-in-T" is the wrong null.** Harmonically, u²(T) = (ħ/2M)∫g(ω)ω⁻¹coth(ħω/2k_BT)dω — a zero-point offset plus a sublinear rise, linear only well above Θ_D. A perfectly harmonic lattice measured at 100 K and 300 K in a host with Θ_D ≈ 400–600 K would depart from a straight line and score as a false positive. V2 tests against the computed harmonic u²(T) **curve**, not a line. *(This is my own reasoning about the harmonic model's algebra, not a source finding; the standard treatment is Maradudin-type impurity Green's-function lattice dynamics, and the nearest item my sweep returned — cond-mat/0512188, "Arbitrary Force-Constant Changes in the Crystal Impurity Problem" — was RETURNED, NOT OPENED.)*
2. **"The harmonic-lattice prediction" was ambiguous between pristine host and defective supercell.** A harmonic calculation that already contains the defect predicts changed neighbour amplitudes from the mass change and the force-constant change alone — the classical harmonic impurity problem. Comparing against the pristine host would score a purely harmonic perturbation as a positive. V2 fixes the null as the harmonic **defective** supercell, under which R(n,T) ≡ 1 at every T; the test is therefore a departure of R from 1 **and** a change of R with T. *(My own reasoning again: both u²_meas and u²_harm are then built from the same mode set, so harmonicity forces the ratio to 1 identically.)*

**What the repair itself now presupposes** (tested as hard as the clause it replaced, per the 2026-10-22 18:33 rule):
- **New, CONTESTED:** harmonic lattice dynamics for a *defective* supercell must be computable with uncertainty below the measured deviation, in a cell large enough that shells 1–5 are unperturbed by the defect's own periodic images. For a **vacancy** this can fail outright: if the relaxed vacancy sits at a saddle point (Jahn–Teller-type distortion), harmonic phonons carry imaginary modes and u²_harm is undefined at the very sites the question asks about. Mitigation available in-design and stated as a rule before any measurement: admit only defect species whose relaxed supercell has no imaginary modes.
- **New, CONTESTED:** the *same individual* defects must be re-found after a temperature change. Thermal cycling drifts the specimen and the defect may be lost, migrate or anneal. Capability evidence exists (Schnitzer et al. 2026, 20 K atomic-resolution ptychography — ABSTRACT LEVEL) but says nothing about re-finding one defect across temperatures.
- Unchanged from V1: beam-induced-motion separability and thin-specimen representativeness, both already flagged contested below.

## Alternative formulations

1. Is the lattice locally anharmonic around a single point defect, and how far does that anharmonicity reach?
2. Does one impurity atom soften its neighbours' vibrations beyond the harmonic mass-and-force-constant change, and does the softening grow with temperature?

## What would count as an answer

R(n,T) ≡ 1 at both temperatures within errors answers in the negative — the lattice is harmonic up to the defect — which would itself exclude resonant-mode accounts of defect phonon-scattering strength in that host.

## Significance — consequence

**If yes** (R(1) > 1 and temperature-dependent, decaying over k shells): the first direct real-space measurement of **how far "local" extends in "local anharmonicity"**. That length currently enters point-defect phonon-scattering models as a fitted parameter with no independent measurement, and resonant-mode accounts of impurity scattering would gain an amplitude field to be fitted against rather than only a bulk thermal conductivity. **If no** (R ≡ 1 at both temperatures within errors): the harmonic mass-and-force-constant perturbation is sufficient in that host, which *excludes* locally anharmonic and resonant-mode accounts of defect phonon-scattering strength there. The question is genuinely two-sided — a null is informative and publishable, which is the mark the Gate 3 rubric asks for.

## Significance — fecundity

Once R(n,T) is measurable, each of these becomes askable and was not before: does R scale with the mass ratio or with the force-constant change — separable by an **isotope control** (same force constants, different mass) in the same host; does R at a vacancy differ in sign from R at a heavy substituent; does the decay length of R coincide with the decay length of the **static** relaxation field, which is independently measurable in the very same images; and does R at a defect pair exceed the sum of the single-defect departures, which would make anharmonicity a defect-interaction channel.

## Reason for silence

**New instrument, with a composition gap, plus a disciplinary boundary.** Per-atom thermal amplitude was a resolution *limit* in 2021 (Chen et al.) and became a *measurand* only in 2025–2026 (Zhang et al.; Gladyshev et al.). Atomic-resolution ptychography at cryogenic temperature was demonstrated separately in 2026 (Schnitzer et al., 20 K, abstract level). **The two capabilities have not been composed in one experiment in anything I opened** — amplitude-as-measurand exists at 300 K, and cryogenic ptychography exists without an amplitude measurement, so the two-temperature limb was not performable until both landed.
