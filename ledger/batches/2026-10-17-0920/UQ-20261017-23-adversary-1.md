## Restatement in my own words

A steel mill tests each heat and labels it with the highest grade whose minimum yield it meets; heats that miss grade G's minimum get retested or relabelled G-1. Certificates for grade G therefore show yields sitting comfortably above the minimum. The candidate asks: how much of that comfortable margin, and of the thinness of the lower tail, exists only because of the labelling decision (aim-high plus removal of failures) rather than because of how the steel was made; can that be measured by matching the mass that "should" sit below G's minimum against the surplus that shows up in the top of G-1 at the same mill; and if you reconstruct the pre-labelling ("latent") distribution, how far do its 5th percentile and the code reliability index beta move from what certificate statistics give. The Gate 1 reformulation drops the single-notch bunching estimator (no left side to fit) and rests identification on a cross-grade mass balance, stratified by thickness class.
