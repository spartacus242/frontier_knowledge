## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Every method used to build interactome maps only registers a pair as interacting when enough
of the pair exists as complex to clear that method's signal floor, at whatever concentration
the method actually presents the two proteins at. So each method implies a frontier in a plane
whose axes are the pair's binding affinity and the proteins' concentration: tight-and-abundant
pairs sit inside, weak-or-rare pairs sit outside. The candidate asks for that frontier to be
*computed* — from protocol working concentrations and detection limits plus an equilibrium
occupancy model — rather than inferred from the edge of a data cloud; then for the union of
the frontiers across method classes, so that the complement is the region no current method can
reach; then for the share of pairs sitting in that complement under two declared denominators;
and finally for a published "we have mapped X% of the interactome" figure to be restated as
coverage of the reachable region rather than of the interactome.

Put uncharitably, the question is: *are the field's completeness numbers measuring the
interactome or measuring its own reach, and by how much?* The first three limbs are
instrumentation metrology; the fourth is the payoff.

## What my sweep actually changed

**The record's nearest-prior list is incomplete, and its dismissal of its own physics neighbour
is partly inverted.** The record disposes of the Maslov/Shakhnovich line in one clause — queries 1
and 5 "returned Maslov/Shakhnovich ... abundance x affinity physics, but evolutionary/thermodynamic
estimands, **not assay detection**" — and graded that dismissal without opening any paper in the
line except one abstract. Opening Heo/Maslov/Shakhnovich shows the dismissal is wrong on the
paper's own text. The paper (i) solves the occupancy problem proteome-wide — *"By solving 3,868
coupled nonlinear LMA equations we obtained all possible binary complex concentrations"* —
(ii) imposes an explicit assay detection threshold, *"capturing only complexes whose concentration
exceeds a certain"* [cutoff relative to bait], and (iii) draws exactly the window conclusion:
pull-down *"can detect functional interactions with high fidelity only for high abundance
proteins"*, with *"missed weak functional interactions"*. That is assay detection, modelled on an
abundance-and-affinity plane, in print since 2011.

It is **not** the candidate's question: the Kd values are *"assigned by formula"* in a model
proteome, not measured or declared as a prior over real pairs; the threshold is a modelled
stoichiometric cutoff, not derived from any published protocol's working concentration and limit
of detection; there is no union over assay classes, no declared cell type with measured copy
numbers, no share-outside-the-window, and no restatement of a coverage figure. So the canonical
question survives the duplication test. But the candidate now has **two** flanking neighbours
rather than one — Hein 2015 in one assay's own units, Heo 2011 in a model proteome's units — and
the qualitative content of the answer ("weak and/or low-abundance is what we cannot see") is
sourced, published, and older than the record's framing allows.

## Inversion check (performed as instructed, and it went against me)

My query-1 instinct was to kill limb (c) with Link's non-identifiability theorem for population
size under heterogeneous detection. On inspection the theorem **supports** the candidate: if N is
not identifiable when detectability is heterogeneous, then a mapped-fraction figure whose
denominator is an estimated interactome size is fragile *precisely because* the window exists, and
computing detectability from physical parameters is a principled response rather than a redundant
one. I therefore do not use it as an attack, and I did not open it, so I ground nothing on it.
Recorded because the instruction to check scope before asserting "theory Y forbids this" is what
caught it.
