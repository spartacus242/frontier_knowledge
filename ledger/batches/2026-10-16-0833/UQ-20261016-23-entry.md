Question (canonical): For a pre-specified list of the drugs most often prescribed to lactating women, (A) what fraction have published human milk concentration-time data satisfying each design criterion a veterinary regulator imposes on a milk residue-depletion study (milked-subject count, number and spacing of post-dose time points, terminal-phase coverage, assay LOQ relative to the limit, licensed-regimen dosing); and (B) for the qualifying subset, what breastfeeding-interruption intervals follow when the veterinary tolerance-limit rule (upper one-sided 95/95) is applied against each of a pre-stated set of candidate infant-exposure limits, and how does that distribution compare, drug by drug, with the interruption advice mothers are given now?
Alternative formulations:
- Could the procedure food-animal regulators use to set milk withdrawal times be applied to human breastfeeding advice, and for how many drugs do existing data already allow the calculation?
- Is "pump and dump" advice reconstructible as a quantitative withdrawal period, or do human lactation data fail a standard the same calculation meets in dairy cattle?
- (Gate 0 intake version, with a single stated infant-exposure limit, reformulated once at Gate 1; both recorded in batches/2026-10-16-0833/UQ-20261016-23-record.md.)
Motivating gap: Veterinary regulation settles a structurally identical decision — how long after dosing milk is unfit for a consumer — with a specified study design, a statutory residue limit and an auditable tolerance-limit rule (EMA CVMP adopted milk-withdrawal guideline rev 1; RIVM MILKINGS calculator), and has even reasoned about children's milk intake (EMA CVMP position paper on MRLs in milk considering daily intake of children). Human lactation advice for the same decision is given in round hours from relative-infant-dose rules of thumb and consensus, with a computed-interval precedent only in radiopharmaceuticals.
Presuppositions: (1) the veterinary between-subject tolerance-limit statistic transfers to milk-volume/fat/schedule variation — graded PLAUSIBLE in the record, re-graded CONTESTED at Gate 4 (the veterinary statistic bounds per-subject crossing times and concentrations; every candidate human limit is a dose, so a volume/bioavailability conversion must be stated); (2) CONTESTED AND CENTRAL: an MRL-analogous infant-exposure limit can be defined — none is agreed, handled by pre-stating a set; (3) CONTESTED: milk concentration-time data are extractable at the required resolution; (4) CONTESTED: milk concentration is the right endpoint rather than infant systemic exposure after oral bioavailability — now contested by three independent groups (concePTION/Milk4baby, Utrecht PBPK, Waterloo UAR); (5) the drug list is enumerable despite poor coding of lactation status; (6) NOT presupposed that current advice is wrong.
What would count as an answer: a pre-registered extraction over a named drug list and named sources reporting per drug, per criterion, pass/fail against criteria fixed in advance from the EMA CVMP milk-withdrawal guideline; then, for qualifiers, intervals computed under the tolerance-limit rule against each pre-stated limit, tabulated beside current advice with a signed discrepancy distribution and sensitivity over limits. A near-zero census would be a complete answer, naming the missing study design in regulator-legible units. Not an answer: advocacy of the transplant, or a framework/decision tree without the census and the computation.

Strongest remaining objection: **Limb B's qualifying subset is near-certainly empty before the first extraction, and limb A then has to carry the question alone.** The one criterion in the imported standard that is both numeric and independent of any infant-exposure limit is the sample-size floor — "Data should be available for a representative sample of n animals, with n>=20", and "a sample size n = 20 is the very minimum" — and the human corpus fails it by construction: FDA's lactation guidance sets no sample size, and the best registered milk-only studies plan 8 to 16 women, with sample sizes declared not to rest on "empirical or hypothesis testing criteria". For the datasets that do exist, the tolerance-limit rule is then inapplicable by its own stated condition, since time-to-safe-concentration cannot be calculated when the last measured concentration is still above the limit. That leaves limb A, whose headline is already in print in adjacent grading (Fomina 2023: 2% of 1,408 LactMed drugs "strong"), and whose advertised payoff — a regulator-legible specification for the study that would fix the gap — is a transcription of the EMA guideline's own numbers and needs no census at all. The genuinely new and genuinely unforeseeable residue is narrow: **which criterion binds for which drug**, a distinction real datasets do vary on.
Secondary, and corrected rather than fatal: the record's claim that limb A is independent of the infant-exposure limit is false for two of its five criteria — the LOQ criterion is stated only as "provided that the LOQ is sufficiently below or equal to the MRL1", with "sufficiently" never defined, and terminal-phase adequacy is not a sample count or a half-life multiple but a limit-relative applicability test. The repair is licensed by the source document rather than invented: footnote 1 states "In the absence of a numerical MRL, another reference value may be used as the relevant limit for residue concentrations", and §4.1.1 adds that for substances without an MRL the relevant safe concentration "might be based on an ADI or other scientifically justified health based guidance value". So limb A becomes a small surface scored once per candidate limit rather than a scalar — an assumption to declare in the design, not a reason to kill the study.
