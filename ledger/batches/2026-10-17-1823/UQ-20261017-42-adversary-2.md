## Restatement (mine, before attacking)

Take one high-throughput DFT campaign where you know the *denominator* — every structure that was
submitted, not just the ones that ended up as database entries. Some tasks died terminally and
were dropped. The question has two limbs. (a) Re-run a random sample of the dropped tasks up a
pre-declared ladder of harder settings (mixing/preconditioner, smearing, k-grid, starting
magnetic configuration, symmetry, step limits), plus count the ones a second code or database
already got through. Among the ones that come back, do formation energy, band gap, magnetic
moment/ordering and dynamical stability look like they do among the entries that survived the
first time, *after* conditioning on composition and structure? That is: is "my solver converged"
ignorable with respect to the quantities the database is mined for, or is the dropped tail
informative? And, by hand, which failures were the solver falling over versus the material having
no minimum to relax into? (b) Put the recovered entries back and see how far a number somebody has
already published moves — a screening hit rate, a count of on/near-hull compounds, or a universal
MLIP's held-out error after retraining.

In one line: is non-convergence MAR or MNAR with respect to the properties high-throughput
databases are used to predict, and does fixing it change published conclusions.

## Recommended verdict

**Provisional** — nothing Unresolved and novelty holds at Medium (9 new-vocabulary queries found only propensity-endpoint or precision-of-retained-endpoint work, and the field's own numerical-QC paper was opened and verified to contain no failure, hull or missingness analysis), but four Partials, led by an identification gap: the canonical question names the ignorability null over the *failed* set while its own answer definition can only identify it over the ladder-*recovered* stratum. Promote on (i) restating the estimand as ignorability over the recovered stratum plus Manski-style bounds over the unrecovered remainder, moved out of "fecundity" and into "what would count as an answer", with limb (b) as the primary endpoint; (ii) replacing the declared category "no local minimum / imaginary modes at the escalated endpoint" with a label that does not assert physics a non-converged run cannot establish; and (iii) reading the full text of Duan et al. 2019 (10.1021/acs.jctc.9b00057; PDF at dspace.mit.edu/bitstreams/2e885ce7-7b43-41e1-a2ca-b77be94a4f27/download), the one unread paper that could still move Disguise.
