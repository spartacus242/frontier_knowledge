# UQ-20261016-31 — Adversary #1 log (Gate 4)

## Restatement (my own words)
Does a given Starlink satellite's low-frequency radio leakage stay roughly the same for as long as it is in orbit, or does it wander (with age, operating state, software), such that a satellite that was clearly leaking in one survey can honestly be invisible in a later one? Nobody, the record says, has re-measured the same NORAD IDs with the same instrument at the same sensitivity months or years apart and partitioned the scatter into pass-level, epoch-level, satellite-level and model-level pieces. The record's motivation is a claimed anomaly: 68 satellites detected by LOFAR in 2022 were not re-detected by EDA2 in 2024 though 55 were still in orbit, and the record says this has been "examined zero times".

## Recommended verdict
Rejected (Unresolved on attack 4). The record's motivating gap rests on an unopened anchor whose next sentences explain the "anomaly"; the first-order stability question is already answered at version level by Bassa 2024; what remains (per-NORAD-ID variance decomposition with upper limits) is a legitimate but unmotivated design proposal, half of which is a reanalysis of the existing Grigg/Dong catalogue.

## Strongest killing point
Grigg et al. 2025 themselves attribute the non-redetection of Di Vruno's 68 satellites to frequency non-overlap (narrowband lines at 125/135/143/150/175 MHz not in EDA2's channels) and to EDA2 sensitivity (~1 Jy v1.5 broadband below the detection level), so the anchor "anomaly" that the candidate is built on, and that the record says was "examined zero times", was examined and explained in the anchor paper the generator never opened.
