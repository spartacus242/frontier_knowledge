## 1. Restatement in my own words (not the record's framing)

Take one EM image volume of a brain that contains both left and right copies of the same circuit, and run it
through one reconstruction pipeline (automated segmentation + automated synapse detection + human proofreading).
Read off, for a pair of cell types, how many synapses connect them on the left and how many on the right. The two
numbers will differ. Part of that difference is the animal; part is the machine and the humans who corrected it.
The question asks for the *machine-and-human part*, expressed as a decision threshold: **how big must an edge-weight
difference be before I am entitled, at a stated confidence, to say it is not an artefact of the pipeline?** And it
asks for that threshold to be *measured* — by reconstructing the same piece of tissue again, independently, and
seeing how far the two reconstructions disagree — rather than *computed* from a model of where errors come from.
The claimed payoff is a number that any comparison of two connectomes (hemisphere vs hemisphere, male vs female,
individual vs individual, species vs species) can be checked against.

Two things in that restatement will matter throughout. First, "measured by re-reconstruction" is not decoration:
it names a *repeatability experiment* as opposed to an *uncertainty budget*. Second, "the detection limit" is
singular, and nothing in the question says which factors are allowed to vary between the two reconstructions.

### My own arithmetic (labelled as mine), on two sentences from F1

Both of these are Schlegel et al.'s own sentences about the same analysis: (a) "differences in edge weights of
+/-30% or less may be entirely due to technical noise"; (b) "25% of all 30-synapse edges on the left will consist
of 15 synapses or less on the right". 15/30 = 0.50, i.e. **a 50% deficit sits at roughly the 25th percentile of
the modelled technical-noise distribution for a 30-synapse edge** — well outside a ±30% band. It follows that
±30% is *not* a 95%, nor even a 75%, bound on technical noise; it is a "do not interpret below this" floor.
Carried uncertainty: (a) may be a central envelope pooled over all edge weights while (b) is conditioned on
30-synapse edges, so the two are not guaranteed to be the same conditional distribution; the record's further
quote ("5% of only 1–7 synapses") would sharpen the point but I did **not** verify it verbatim in my own fetch, so
I do not use it. Even on the conservative reading, the published guidance bounds *incredulity* from below and does
not state the difference one may *believe* at 95%. That distinction is the whole of the candidate's endpoint.

## 4. The eight attacks

### Attack 1 — Triviality: can it be answered in a page from existing knowledge?

**Objection.** Yes, nearly. Open Schlegel et al. 2024 and copy out four sentences: ±30% of edge weight may be
entirely technical noise; 65% of observed left–right edge-weight variability falls inside the modelled noise
range; edges >10 synapses or >1.1% of target input persist with >90% probability; >40 synapses or 3% is "strong".
Add the male-CNS paper's operational thresholds (<8 synapses male CNS, <11 FAFB/FlyWire) and the 30% envelope in
its Fig. 8d. That is a page, it is already written, and it is already being used as a filter on the field's
highest-profile between-connectome biological claim. The remaining content of the candidate — "but measure it
empirically" — is a referee's request for validation, not a question.

**Best response.** The page exists and does not contain the candidate's endpoint. The candidate asks for the
smallest difference distinguishable **at a stated confidence**. Schlegel et al. state a lower bound on
interpretability, not an upper bound on noise, and their own distributional detail contradicts reading ±30% as a
confidence bound: by my arithmetic above on their two sentences, a 50% deficit sits near the 25th percentile of
modelled noise for a 30-synapse edge. A threshold that excludes the lower quartile of its own noise distribution
is not a detection limit; using it as one would pass roughly a quarter of pure-noise 30-synapse comparisons. The
male-CNS paper then shows what the field actually does in the absence of a real limit: it sets *ad hoc* per-dataset
weak-edge cut-offs that differ between datasets by a factor of ~1.4 with no stated derivation, and then falls back
on "expert curation … to separate biological signal from cases of technical noise". Hand curation is the tell: if a
page-length answer existed, it would not be needed. Finally, F5 establishes that the thing the candidate asks for
is, by its own definition, not obtainable from a page: "The repeatability of measurement is determined by
test-retest studies"; "independent replicate measurements are necessary". A forward uncertainty budget and a
repeatability coefficient are different quantities, and only the first exists.

**Status: Resolved.** The headline *summary number* is a page away; the *question's stated endpoint* — a
difference distinguishable at a stated confidence, obtained from replicate measurement — is not, and the field's
own 2025 practice (per-dataset *ad hoc* cut-offs plus manual curation) is evidence that it is not.

### Attack 2 — Disguise: is it a known question renamed? (nine new-vocabulary searches, above)

**Objection.** It is a known question in at least four other vocabularies, and in one of them it is not only
known but routine. (i) **Laboratory medicine** (search 11): the Reference Change Value / critical difference is
the standardized estimand "how large a change exceeds measurement imprecision", decomposed into analytical and
biological components — exactly the candidate's decomposition, with a 60-year-old literature and an ISO/CLSI
apparatus behind it. (ii) **Imaging metrology / QIBA** (search 4, F5): the repeatability coefficient is the same
quantity, and F5 gives the full design theory for obtaining it, down to sample size. (iii) **Diffusion-MRI
connectomics** (searches 6 and 10): per-edge test–retest reliability and *same-data-different-pipeline*
reproducibility of connectome edge weights are published, standard analyses in the sister instrument — the
candidate is the EM field's missing copy of an analysis another connectomics community does as a matter of course.
(iv) **Cryo-EM** (search 12): splitting one dataset and reconstructing it independently is so established that it
is called the gold standard. So the question is not unasked; it is asked constantly, under other names, and the
candidate contributes only the instrument instance.

**Best response.** Every one of those is a *framework*, and a framework is not an answer: the RCV formula does
not tell you the CV_A of a proofreading pipeline, and QIBA's design theory does not tell you the repeatability
coefficient of a cell-type-to-cell-type edge. The value is instrument-specific by construction, and none of the
nine new-vocabulary searches returned an EM-connectome instance of any of them. Worse for the objection, the
transplant is not mechanical in this regime: all four frameworks presuppose replicate measurements whose errors
are independent, and F4 states the opposite for this instrument — technical noise here "is more commonly
correlated across both sides of one brain". An RCV or an FSC computed under violated independence is not the
quantity it names. And the fly field's own vocabulary ("technical noise", a percentage envelope) is why the
transplant has not happened: search 1 shows the nearest *in-field* precision tradition, stereology's coefficient
of error, is attached to sampled population totals, not dense per-edge weights, so even the neighbouring EM
tradition does not supply the estimand.

**Status: Partial.** The question-form is thoroughly known and named in four other fields, which caps novelty:
nothing here is conceptually new, and a competent metrologist would recognize the task on sight. But no source I
opened, and nothing any of the twelve searches surfaced, states the value for a dense EM connectome. Known
question-form, unmeasured instance — disguise damages the novelty claim without killing the question.

### Attack 3 — Combinatorial novelty: Field A × Field B with no problem at the interface?

**Objection.** "Metrology × connectomics." Apply gauge R&R to a segmentation pipeline; publish variance
components. The record itself concedes the machinery is "off-the-shelf in both neighbouring fields and in network
science's measurement-error estimators". Cross-product, no interface.

**Best response.** There are at least three substantive interface problems, each evidenced from sources I opened.
(a) **What counts as a replicate.** F5 defines repeatability as "measurement precision under constant conditions",
but a segmentation pipeline re-run under constant conditions on the same images returns (near) identical output —
variance is zero by construction. So the quantity only exists once you decide which factors vary (proofreader
team, agglomeration threshold, detector version, cell-type conventions), which is a design question this instrument
poses and the textbook case does not. (b) **Correlated, common-mode error.** F4: technical noise "is more commonly
correlated across both sides of one brain". Standard R&R assumes replicate errors are independent; here the
dominant error source — segmentation failures at image defects, missing sections, poor staining — is shared by any
re-reconstruction of the *same* volume, so the usual estimator is biased and needs modification. (c)
**Covariate-dependent error.** F1: completion rate "ranges from 46% in FlyWire right to 77% in hemibrain" in the
mushroom body alone, and the authors explicitly decline to correct for it ("does not correct for other potential
confounds such as differences in the synaptic completion rates"). A single gauge number is therefore the wrong
object; what is needed is a floor as a function of weight, region and completion rate. Those are real problems at
the seam, not a stapling.

**Status: Resolved.**

### Attack 4 — Presupposition failure

**Objection.** Three suspect presuppositions, two of which I can evidence as false or badly contested.
(a) *That no detection limit is in use for connectome-to-connectome comparison.* False: F4 shows per-dataset
noise thresholds (<8, <11 synapses) and a 30% envelope in active use, applied to the field's flagship
dimorphism claims, with results reported as a lower bound. (b) *That "the" detection limit is a single
transferable number.* Contested by both sources: F1's model is scoped by its authors to FlyWire's two
hemispheres, and F4's thresholds differ per dataset — so "the detection limit for any connectome-to-connectome
comparison" presupposes a unity the evidence denies. (c) **The deepest one**, which the record's presupposition 2
gestures at but does not state sharply: *that independent re-reconstruction of the same volume can measure the
noise floor at all.* It cannot measure the part that matters most. Re-reconstructing the same images shares every
image defect, so errors are common-mode; F4 says in terms that technical noise is correlated across both sides of
one brain and that "most apparent differences in male-female connectivity were explained by segmentation issues".
An independent second pass over the same voxels will tend to fail at the same folds, the same lost sections, the
same dark regions — so the candidate's own estimator is structurally biased *downward* on the dominant error term.
The question thus smuggles in the assumption that its estimator is the gold standard, when it is in fact a lower
bound on pipeline variance only.

**Best response.** (a) is a failure of the *stake as originally stated*, not of the question: that thresholds are
in use with no stated derivation and are patched by hand curation is a reason to ask what the right threshold is,
not a reason not to. (b) is answered by rewording: the question's own tightened form already asks how the threshold
*varies* with weight, cell type, neurite length and effort, i.e. it asks for a function, and "the detection limit"
in the canonical sentence is loose shorthand that an amendment fixes at no cost. (c) is the serious one, and the
honest defence is that it narrows rather than voids: a same-volume re-reconstruction measures exactly the
operator-plus-algorithm variance component, which is currently measured by *nothing* and which F1's forward model
does not contain (that model perturbs synapse detection and subsamples by completion rate; it does not model a
proofreader attaching a branch to the wrong soma). Combining a measured operator/algorithm component with the
modelled common-mode component is strictly more than the field has now, provided the design states which component
it captures. That is a required amendment, not a refutation.

**Status: Partial.** One presupposition (no limit in use) is false as stated and must be struck; one (a single
transferable limit) is false and must be reworded to a function; the third (re-reconstruction as the gold standard)
is false in the strong form and survives only in the weak form "measures one variance component and is blind to
common-mode error", which the question must say out loud.

### Attack 5 — Vagueness: could it be answered no matter what the evidence shows, or never?

**Objection.** "The noise floor" is not identified by the question. Vary the design — one team or two, same
segmentation or a different one, same cell-typing conventions or blind re-typing, central brain or optic lobe,
weak edges or strong — and the number moves by what is plausibly an order of magnitude; F1's own numbers already
span "±30%" and "25% of 30-synapse edges lose half their weight" and "approached 100% for weaker synapses". Any
investigator could return almost any value and claim to have answered it, and a sceptic could always say the
replication was not independent enough. That is answerable-no-matter-what and never-settled at the same time.

**Best response.** The vagueness is in the canonical one-sentence form, and the tightened form already fixes most
of it: unit (cell-type-to-cell-type edge), endpoint (synapse count or log weight ratio), comparator (the pipeline's
own error), a stated confidence, and named stratifiers. The metrology frameworks the disguise attack invoked supply
the rest of the specification off the shelf — F5's within-subject SD and repeatability coefficient with a
prespecified specificity, "usually 95%", applied "as cutpoint". Reported that way, the answer is a function with
confidence bands, pre-registered stratifiers and a declared replication design; different designs give different,
*comparable*, labelled numbers, which is how every other measurement science lives with the same multiplicity.

**Status: Partial.** Not unanswerable and not unfalsifiable, but the canonical sentence as it stands is loose
enough that it must carry its replication design and its stratification explicitly, or it will be answered
unfalsifiably.

### Attack 6 — Significance inflation: is the audience real, and would they act on either outcome?

**Objection.** The audience is real but it is the *author* of the answer. The FlyWire/Cambridge–Princeton
consortium built the noise model, published the threshold, and scoped it themselves; the male-CNS team then set its
own per-dataset cut-offs and curated by hand. Telling them to validate their model empirically is a reviewer
comment. And both outcomes are weakly actionable: if the measured floor lands near ±30%, nothing changes; if it
lands worse, the existing claims are already hedged as lower bounds ("Our account therefore represents a lower
bound"), so they absorb the hit without retraction. Meanwhile the cost is a second proofreading campaign over a
volume already paid for once.

**Best response.** The audience is acting on this *right now, badly*, which is the definition of a question worth
settling. F4's own practice is the evidence: *ad hoc* thresholds differing per dataset without stated derivation; a
30% envelope borrowed from a model built for a different volume's two hemispheres; and "expert curation was
required to separate biological signal from cases of technical noise" — i.e. the threshold is doing so little work
that humans must re-adjudicate every candidate difference. The same paper says a "strong quantitative understanding
of that variability" is "also essential" and reports that "some connections in the male were too variable across
hemispheres to be distinguished from zero synapses". Both outcomes change behaviour where behaviour is cheapest to
change: a floor materially worse than ±30% retires a tranche of weak-edge dimorphism claims and redirects
proofreading budget to the regions where completion rate is lowest (F1: 46% vs 77% within the mushroom body alone);
a floor better than ±30% licenses analyses the field is currently declining to run. For the mouse-scale groups the
transfer is explicitly open — F1's authors scope their model to FlyWire — and search 8 found MICrONS documenting
error *correction* without any stated edge-weight limit.

**Status: Partial.** Audience real and demonstrably improvising; but the decision-relevance is bounded by the
existence of a usable modelled number, and the branch where the empirical answer lands near the model (the
likeliest branch) changes little beyond confidence.

### Attack 7 — Reason for silence

**Objection.** There is no silence, and that is itself the finding: the question was asked and numerically
answered inside the instrument's own flagship paper, by the pipeline engineers and circuit neuroscientists who are
the same authors. An "apparent silence" produced by searching with the wrong word ("noise floor" instead of
"technical noise") is a literature-search artefact, not a gap.

**Best response.** The objection is right about the *modelled* answer and wrong about the *measured* one, and the
measured one has a mechanism of the acceptable kind — in fact two, both evidenced. (i) **Incentive/cost:** a
repeatability experiment means paying a second time to proofread tissue already proofread, with no new biology at
the end; no funder or journal rewards a validation whose expected result is "the existing number was about right",
and F5 notes real test–retest designs want "more subjects and more repeated measurements" (its worked example
reaches 139 subjects) which in this instrument means many re-proofread sub-volumes. (ii) **Disciplinary boundary
in the vocabulary, not the personnel:** the record's point that the engineers and the neuroscientists are the same
authors cuts the other way. Precisely because the error model was written in-house, it was written in the house
idiom — a forward propagation of detector FP/FN rates — and never met the measurement-science traditions that
*define* the quantity by replication (F5: "The repeatability of measurement is determined by test-retest studies").
My nine new-vocabulary searches found the frameworks fully mature in lab medicine, imaging metrology, cryo-EM and
diffusion-MRI connectomics, and found no EM-connectome instance of any of them — a boundary with evidence on both
sides of it, not an absence of thought.

**Status: Resolved**, with the concession that the silence to be explained is only the empirical limb; the
headline number is not silent at all.

### Attack 8 — Dissolution: would it dissolve rather than be answered?

**Objection.** Pull on it and "the detection limit" evaporates into a design choice. There is no fact of the
matter about how much a pipeline disagrees with itself until you say which factors vary; the error is
covariate-dependent (completion rate 46–77% within one region), weight-dependent (F1: the noise-explained fraction
"approached 100% for weaker synapses"), and correlated across the very hemispheres it is meant to adjudicate. What
looks like one number is a family of conditional distributions, and the field has already, sensibly, stopped
looking for the number: F4 sets different thresholds per dataset and then curates by hand. The question dissolves
into "characterize your pipeline's error structure", which is engineering, not a research question.

**Best response.** That is dissolution into a *better-posed* question, which is the good case, not the bad one.
Every mature measurement science made the same move and kept the question: lab medicine reports CV_A plus CV_I
rather than one critical difference; QIBA reports a repeatability coefficient with its conditions attached;
cryo-EM reports a resolution curve, not a number. The candidate's own tightened form already asks for the
threshold *as a function of* weight, cell type, neurite length and proofreading effort — i.e. it anticipated the
dissolution and asked for the family. And the family is not vacuous: it has a determinate, currently unmeasured
member — the operator-plus-algorithm variance component of a dense EM reconstruction — that F1's forward model
does not contain and F4's hand curation is a substitute for.

**Status: Partial.** The singular "the detection limit" does dissolve and should be struck; the question survives
as a variance-components estimand, but it survives in amended form, not as posed.

## 5. Closing: the single strongest surviving objection

No attack is Unresolved, so nothing here is fatal; and on attacks 1 and 2 I overturn the generator's Gate 2 ground
in substantial part, on evidence I obtained myself. Schlegel et al.'s ±30% — which the record treats as "verbatim
the number the candidate says does not exist" — is a *do-not-interpret-below* floor derived from a forward
uncertainty budget, and by my own arithmetic on two of their own sentences (a 50% deficit sits near the 25th
percentile of modelled noise for a 30-synapse edge) it cannot be read as a detection limit at anything like 95%
confidence. The field's own latest connectome (Oct 2025) corroborates this from the other side: it sets
undocumented per-dataset cut-offs, borrows the 30% envelope from a model built for another volume, and then needs
"expert curation … to separate biological signal from cases of technical noise". A number that still requires
hand adjudication of every case is not an answer to "what is the detection limit".

The strongest objection that survives is on attack 4(c), and it is an objection to the candidate's *estimator*
rather than to its question: **independent re-reconstruction of the same image volume is structurally blind to the
dominant error source, so the candidate's chosen measurement cannot deliver the quantity its own sentence
promises.** The errors that matter here are made at image defects — folds, lost sections, poor staining — and the
male-CNS paper states that technical noise "is more commonly correlated across both sides of one brain" and that
