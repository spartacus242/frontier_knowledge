## Restatement (my own words, before reading the record's framing twice)

Freeze a version of ProteinGym's substitution benchmark (217 DMS assays). Find the assays whose raw, pre-merge files still contain two or more independently selected replicate measurements per variant. For those assays: (a) measure how well each assay agrees with itself across its own replicates — a test-retest reliability coefficient on the merged-score scale, stepped up by Spearman-Brown to the number of replicates that were actually averaged into the released `DMS_score`, with bootstrap intervals; (b) divide each published zero-shot predictor's Spearman against that assay by the square root of that reliability, and ask what fraction of the distance from the published correlation to 1.0 was never model error at all but noise in the criterion; (c) check whether, after that correction, the leaderboard comes out in a different order (Kendall tau against the published order, plus a count of pairwise flips inside a pre-declared top-k). Report the share of the 217 assays that were even eligible as a headline number in its own right.

Put plainly: it asks how good a predictor can look against a yardstick that disagrees with itself, and whether the current ranking is partly a ranking of which assays were measured carefully.

## Single strongest killing point

Limb (b) presupposes that the gap to perfect prediction splits two ways, into assay measurement error and model error, and the candidate's instrument can only separate the smallest of at least three components. In the candidate's own regime, S2 prints between-experiment Spearman from ρ = 0.21 (BRCA1) to ρ = 0.93 (bla) over 11 measurements of 4 proteins and attributes the low end to setup difference rather than to noise, and S1 — the one protein-science paper that actually performed this ceiling computation — printed two different answers, 0.73 vs 0.83 analytically and 0.74 ± 0.02 vs 0.84 ± 0.02 by simulation (Sec. 3.2), from two defensible ways of estimating the same experimental uncertainty on overlapping data. That ~0.1 spread is Pearson on pooled ΔΔG and transfers to DMS only qualitatively, but qualitatively is enough: the choice of which disagreement counts as "the assay's unreliability" moves the ceiling by far more than the few hundredths of Spearman that limb (c) exists to adjudicate, and the record pre-declares four arms with no rule for what to conclude when they disagree.
