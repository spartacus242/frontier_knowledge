Question (canonical):
(V1 — reformulated at Gate 1, the version assessed) For training runs whose restart history and data pipeline are auditable (open runs such as OLMo/BLOOM-class releases, or a deliberately instrumented replication under the recovery semantics major frameworks actually ship), and taking the run's *claimed* training sequence to be the artifact the run itself asserts — a manifest with an order, or, where order is defined only procedurally, the sequence that (seed, loader version, world size) would have produced absent faults: (a) what is the divergence between the claimed and the realized token sequence induced by dataloader-state recovery at each restart and in total, reported both as a multiset divergence (tokens/documents never consumed, and tokens/documents consumed more times than claimed, as a fraction of nominal) and as an ordering divergence (displacement of documents relative to their claimed position); and (b) under a pre-registered test with a stated detection floor, does per-document memorization or trajectory-specific influence differ for documents adjacent to a restart boundary — a dated discontinuity — relative to matched documents away from any boundary?

Alternative formulations:
- (V0, intake, superseded at Gate 1) In large-scale training runs with documented restarts, what fraction of the nominal training token sequence is actually skipped or re-consumed as a result of dataloader-state recovery, and is the resulting deviation detectable in the trained model — for example as a dated discontinuity in memorization or in influence attributed to the data adjacent to each restart?
- (lay) When a big training run crashes and resumes, how much data gets quietly dropped or seen twice, and does it leave a mark on the model?
- (technical, neighbour vocabulary) Treating the data manifest as the claimed training set, measure the realized-versus-claimed token multiset divergence induced by restarts, and test whether per-document memorization or influence varies with a document's position relative to a restart boundary. Equivalently: what are the *delivery semantics* (exactly-once / at-least-once / at-most-once) of a production training dataloader across failure recovery, and what is the *coverage* of that recovery mechanism measured on the model rather than on the run?

Gate 1 note (one reformulation used, for well-posedness only): V0 left three things undefined that V1 fixes. (i) "Nominal training token sequence" was treated as given; V1 makes the claim itself an explicit artifact and specifies what stands in for it when order is only procedural. (ii) "Fraction skipped or re-consumed" is not a single quantity — a pure skip/duplicate accounting and an ordering perturbation give different answers and bear on different downstream claims — so V1 requires both a multiset divergence and an ordering divergence. (iii) "Detectable in the trained model" could be answered either way without a floor; V1 requires a pre-registered test with a stated detection floor, so that a null is a bounded null. V1 also scopes the question to auditable runs, since frontier-run logs are not obtainable; the frontier gap is then a stated limit of scope, not a hidden failure.

Motivating gap:
The silence is visible in the search result itself: queries for restart-induced skipped or duplicated training data return vendor and framework how-to documentation (Cerebras "Deterministically restart a dataloader", MosaicML Streaming "Elastic Determinism", MindSpore "Resumable Training After Breakpoint", HuggingFace forum threads on batch-skipping, Databricks fault-tolerance write-ups) and no study of consequence. The how-literature on fault-tolerant training is dense — restartable and deterministic dataloaders, distributed and asynchronous checkpointing, elastic training, hot-swap recovery — and its stated success criterion is goodput: the run continues, and little time is lost. Whether the resumed run is the run it claims to be, in the sense of having consumed the token sequence its manifest describes, is posed only indirectly and only once (ElasWave, below, asks it of the loss curve). The anchoring anomaly is in the public training chronicles: the OPT-175B logbook (Zhang et al. 2022, arXiv:2205.01068, with the metaseq chronicles) records dozens of manual restarts including data-position adjustments and rollbacks; Llama 3 (arXiv:2407.21783) reports 466 job interruptions and an effective-training-time percentage; a 504-GPU operational analysis (arXiv:2605.09370) reports median retry intervals and checkpoint-restart bandwidth — none states how much data a restart costs or duplicates, and frameworks differ in whether dataloader state is checkpointed at all. Meanwhile the consumers of exactness have multiplied: data-attribution and influence methods, memorization audits, training-data-extraction claims, copyright and provenance arguments, "was this document in the training set" determinations, and now an explicit auditability tier (OPEN-1B, arXiv:2609.17380) that fixes data batch ordering across a cluster so a run can be replayed step by step.

Presuppositions (each flagged):
1. At least one run exists publicly with both a restart log and a reconstructable data order, or one can be instrumented. CONTESTED for frontier runs; satisfiable for open runs and for a deliberately instrumented smaller-scale replication. V1 scopes to this set, so the presupposition is now in the question rather than behind it.
2. A "nominal training sequence" is well defined — a manifest and order exist as a claim. CONTESTED: some runs define order only procedurally by seed plus loader implementation. V1 handles this by substituting the counterfactual fault-free sequence of (seed, loader version, world size); where even that is not reconstructable, the absence of a well-defined claim is itself the finding.
3. Skips and duplications are large enough to matter at the scale measured. CONTESTED; a parts-per-million answer would itself be the result, and is the one that licenses attribution methods.
4. Memorization or influence is sensitive enough to a document's single-epoch presence and position for a dated signature to be detectable. CONTESTED; needs a power analysis against published per-document memorization curves. Partially supported: Wang et al. 2024 (arXiv:2412.09538) establish that influence is iteration-dependent, so the mechanism is not hypothetical.
5. Restart counts and timings are recoverable from logs rather than inferred. CONTESTED; recoverable for instrumented replications by construction.
6. Recovery semantics are stable within a run, so one accounting applies. CONTESTED, and the sharpest of the set: parallelism degree and world size commonly change across restarts, which changes loader sharding and hence what "resume" means. ElasWave (arXiv:2510.00606) shows resharding is a first-class event with its own RNG-state handling, which both confirms the concern and supplies the handle for it.
7. Shipped framework behaviour is representative of what frontier runs do. CONTESTED; in-house loaders may differ from documented open ones, which bounds the audit to what is observable. V1's scoping makes this a limit rather than an error.

What would count as an answer:
Either (a) an audit of real auditable runs reporting realized-versus-claimed multiset and ordering divergence per restart and in total, with the recovery semantics in force at each restart named (and resharding events separated out); or (b) a deliberately instrumented run with injected restarts under the recovery semantics actually shipped by major frameworks (at minimum: a loader that checkpoints its state, one that does not, and one world-size change), reporting both divergences plus a pre-registered test, with a stated detection floor, of whether per-document memorization or trajectory-specific influence differs for documents adjacent to restart boundaries against matched controls. A null on the model-signature limb with a non-null divergence is decision-relevant: it tells attribution and provenance researchers how much sequence error their methods tolerate, and tells infrastructure teams whether dataloader state must be checkpointed. A non-null signature would mean published data-ordering and memorization results on restarted runs carry an unreported confound. A finding that the claimed sequence is not reconstructable at all for most public runs answers the provenance limb directly.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
1. vs ElasWave — the closest prior, and the reason novelty is Medium rather than High. ElasWave asks the candidate's underlying question of one observable: it injects a failure with a world-size change and measures how far the recovered run's loss drifts from a fault-free baseline, reporting 0.2% → 0.045% deviation after RNG resharding. Three differences. (i) Its estimand is aggregate loss deviation as a *system-quality* metric to be minimised by its own mechanism; the candidate's estimand is the token multiset and ordering divergence itself — an accounting of what was consumed, which loss deviation neither bounds nor reveals (a run can match the loss curve while having skipped and duplicated offsetting amounts of data). (ii) ElasWave treats RNG state as the thing to preserve; the candidate treats the *document identity set* as the thing to audit, which is what provenance and attribution claims rest on. (iii) ElasWave has no model-side per-document test and no audit of real runs' restart logs; its one experiment is a LoRA fine-tune with a single injected failure, not a pretraining-scale restart chronicle.
2. vs Choi/Shavit/Duvenaud — same ultimate concern (is the claimed training data the actual training data) but the opposite direction of travel. Proof-of-Training-Data designs a verification protocol against an *adversarial* trainer, and its seed pre-commitment presupposes that an honest trainer's realized sequence matches the committed one. The candidate asks what the honest-trainer residual is: how much the realized sequence departs from the committed one through ordinary fault recovery, which is exactly the tolerance such a protocol must set and which the paper does not quantify. The candidate is the measurement that would calibrate the verifier.
3. vs Wang et al. — supplies the candidate's mechanism without asking its question. It establishes that influence is iteration-dependent and that removal at a specific iteration has a specific effect, which is why a restart-induced skip or duplicate could leave a dated signature at all. But its perturbations are counterfactual and researcher-chosen; it never considers that the realized trajectory of a real run already contains such perturbations, imposed by faults, at known timestamps. The candidate turns its counterfactual into an observational natural experiment.
(vs OPEN-1B — shares the premise that a run's data order is a claim that could be audited, and fixes batch ordering to make step-level replay possible. But its adversary is floating-point non-associativity and cross-hardware non-determinism, not fault recovery; its abstract does not mention restarts. It is the infrastructure that would make limb (a) cheap, not an answer to it.)

Significance — consequence:
If the divergence is non-trivial (say ≥0.1% of tokens skipped or duplicated per long run, concentrated at restart boundaries): every claim of the form "document D was/was not in the training set, seen once, at position p" acquires an error bar that nobody currently reports, and the "exactly one epoch" premise behind single-epoch memorization and extraction work becomes an approximation of stated size. If the divergence is parts-per-million, that is also an answer with teeth: it licenses the exactness assumption quantitatively, for the first time, and tells framework maintainers that checkpointing dataloader state is a hygiene matter rather than a correctness one. If the model-signature limb is non-null, published data-ordering, curriculum and memorization results computed on restarted runs carry a dated confound aligned with infrastructure events — and the signature becomes a forensic instrument: a way to read restart history out of weights, which cuts both ways for provenance auditing. If it is null at a stated floor, attribution and influence methods get a tolerance budget: sequence error below that floor is invisible, so a verifier need not chase it.

Significance — audience (specific, and what they would do differently):
- Data-attribution and influence researchers (the Wang et al. / TRAK / influence-function community): would either report sequence-error sensitivity alongside influence estimates, or — if a floor is established — stop worrying about it with a citation. Right now they do neither because the number does not exist.
- Memorization, extraction and privacy auditors (Carlini-lineage work, and the per-document memorization curves the power analysis would use): would condition on restart proximity as a covariate, or justify not doing so.
- Provenance, verification and standards work (Proof-of-Training-Data, OPEN-1B's auditability tier, model cards and dataset documentation, EU AI Act training-data summaries): would set a tolerance in the protocol. OPEN-1B is the live case: it fixes data batch ordering to enable step-level replay, and an auditor replaying a restarted run needs to know whether a mismatch at a restart boundary is fraud or fault recovery. Without this number, that distinction is unavailable.
- Training-infrastructure teams: would decide whether dataloader state must be checkpointed, and whether resharding needs sample-level (not just RNG-level) continuity. This is the weakest limb of the audience argument: frameworks already ship restartable loaders and ElasWave already minimises deviation, so part of this audience treats the property as solved by construction and would act only on a non-null model signature.
- Litigation and copyright analysis, where "was this work in the training set" is contested: would gain, or lose, a technical basis for arguing that a manifest does not establish consumption.

Reason for silence:
Two mechanisms, both structural rather than "nobody thought of it".
(1) A disciplinary boundary with a success-criterion asymmetry. The people who can compute the quantity — infrastructure teams with the run logs and loader internals — are evaluated on goodput, and their literature's stated success criterion is that the run continues with little time lost. Time is what they instrument, so time is what they publish; tokens mis-consumed is not on their dashboard because nothing in their incentive set reads it. The people who need the quantity — attribution, memorization, privacy, provenance and legal analysts — work from released checkpoints and manifests and have no access to restart chronicles, so they cannot compute it and have instead been assuming it away. The cross-product belongs to neither community, and the vocabularies do not meet: the delivery-semantics language that would make the question obvious (exactly-once vs at-least-once, recovery coverage) lives in databases and stream processing, and query 4 shows it has never been applied to a training dataloader.
(2) An incentive gap at the point of measurement. The only parties who can audit a frontier run's realized sequence are the labs that ran it, and the publication is a disclosure of imprecision in their own provenance claims — at a moment when training-data provenance is under regulatory and litigation pressure. Framework vendors have the mirror-image incentive: their documentation asserts determinism and restartability as features (Cerebras "deterministic checkpoints", MosaicML "Elastic Determinism"), and a measurement of residual divergence is a measurement of how far the feature falls short. Assertion is cheaper than audit for both.

Strongest remaining objection (for Gate 4 to press):
That limb (a) is arithmetic, not research — once an instrumented run exists, the divergence is a bookkeeping difference computable in an afternoon, and framework maintainers could state the per-restart cost from code inspection alone (a loader that checkpoints its sample index skips zero; one that does not re-consumes exactly from the last checkpoint). If so the only non-trivial content is limb (b), whose power is doubtful at any affordable scale. The response the record can offer: resharding and world-size changes make the per-restart cost not derivable from code inspection, ElasWave's own 0.2% loss deviation shows a recovered run is measurably not the fault-free run, and the audit limb's real finding may be that the claimed sequence is not reconstructable at all for most public releases — which is a provenance result, not bookkeeping. The adversary should test whether that holds or whether the triviality charge survives.
