## Findings
Paper A (the "36%" paper): Rimawi RH, Shah KB, Cook PP. Risk of Redocumenting Penicillin Allergy in a Cohort of Patients With Negative Penicillin Skin Tests. J Hosp Med 2013;8(11):615-618. DOI 10.1002/jhm.2083.
- Retrospective, Vidant Health (10 hospitals, NC). 150 patients with negative PST in Mar-Jul 2012; follow-up Aug 2012-Jul 2013.
- 55/150 (37%) returned to a Vidant hospital within 1 year. 20/55 (36%) had penicillin allergy re-documented, "without apparent reason". Denominator of 36% = readmitted patients, NOT all 150.
- Of the 20: 0 received beta-lactam, 12 (60%) non-beta-lactam, 8 (40%) no antimicrobial.
- LTCF: allergy still listed in 21/21 LTCF records. No attempt to convey PST result.
- Removal by study team: label removed from EMR in the prior intervention (study team inserted procedure note); summariser says the paper does not itself state who did PST/removal; refers to earlier paper (ref 6).
- No time-to-removal, no removal rate.
- Table inconsistencies noted (21 vs 22 LTCF residents; 22 (40%) vs 63%).

Paper B (the intervention): Rimawi RH, Cook PP, Gooch M, Kabchi B, Ashraf MS, Rimawi BH, Gebregziabher M, Siraj DS. The Impact of Penicillin Skin Testing on Clinical Practice and Antimicrobial Stewardship. J Hosp Med 2013;8:341-345. DOI 10.1002/jhm.2036.
- QI study, 861-bed hospital, Mar-Jul 2012; testing by study team (ID fellows supervised). 146 tested, 145 tolerated beta-lactam. 428/4031 (10.6%) charts had penicillin allergy at baseline. No re-label data, no time-to-removal.
