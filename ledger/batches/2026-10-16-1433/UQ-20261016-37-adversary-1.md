# UQ-20261016-37 — Adversary 1 log (Gate 4)

## Restatement (my own words)
Take the "one in a million" sterility assurance level literally, as a prediction of how often a unit sold as sterile is in fact not sterile. Count the terminally sterilized units released per year that carry that claim (order 10^10-10^11), count the cases where such a unit was confirmed non-sterile for reasons intrinsic to the product (failed release sterility tests, non-sterility recalls, intrinsic-contamination outbreaks), correct for the fraction of such units that would ever be noticed, and ask whether the implied rate is at or below 10^-6. If it is above, ask whether replacing the straight-line (log-linear) survivor extrapolation with shoulder/tailing (Weibull) kinetics in the PNSU calculation predicts an excess of the same sign and size.

## Recommended verdict
Rejected — Unresolved on attack 4 (and 8); the Gate 2 asked-but-open finding stands (P1 verified; Winckels & Dorpema 1994 is an earlier unopened lead), and the record's adjacent-field negative is contradicted by the NFPA/CMI canning arithmetic. Routing: open-questions project, with the note that limb (ii) is a modelling exercise and limb (i) cannot identify kinetic-tail survivors.

## Single strongest killing point
The numerator the question specifies (recalls, outbreaks, release-test positives) can only ever be populated by gross lot-level process failures — the sterility test cannot see below ~10% lot contamination (FDA 2004 via source C) — whereas a 10^-6 kinetic-tail survivor is a single unit in a correctly processed lot that generates no detectable event; so even a realized bound above 10^-6 cannot be attributed to the log-linear extrapolation, and limb (ii) is unidentifiable from the data limb (i) collects.
