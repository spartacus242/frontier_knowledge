# UQ-20261017-22 — Adversary #1 log

## Restatement (my own words, before reading the record's framing as authoritative)

Battery labs report that nominally identical cells, cycled under nominally identical conditions, reach end-of-life at different cycle counts, and they call that spread "cell-to-cell variability" and treat it as a property of the cells. But every cell lives on one cycler channel, in one chamber position, for its entire life, so the cell and its channel are perfectly confounded; nothing in the standard design can tell a bad cell from a slightly hot or slightly over-driven channel. The candidate asks two things. (a) If you deliberately move cells between channels on a pre-declared schedule (with sham moves to control for the handling itself), how does the variance in per-interval degradation rate split into cell, channel and cell-by-channel parts, and what does that imply for cycle-life variance under a declared trajectory model? (b) In the Stanford/MIT fast-charging datasets, where the same 48 channels hosted different protocols in successive rounds, how big is the channel random effect, and does entering it change which protocols come out on top?
