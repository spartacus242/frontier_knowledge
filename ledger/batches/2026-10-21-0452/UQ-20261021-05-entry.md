Question (canonical): On declared wards served by identified medical-gas pipeline legs, with leg flow and pressure logged at <=1 min, delivered flow measured at instrumented terminal units on a sampled subset of outlets, and per-patient-hour prescribed device and flowmeter setting from the EHR: (i) is delivered-minus-set flow at an outlet a function of EXOGENOUS leg load — concurrent draw by patients other than the index patient, as a fraction of that leg's engineering design flow, breakpoint pre-declared — and (ii) what share of within-patient variation in a pre-declared oxygenation endpoint (SpO2 time-in-target, FiO2 shortfall, device escalation) is attributable to that load, under within-patient contrasts and a negative-control leg? (V2; V1 and the single permitted Gate 1 reformulation are in the record.)
Alternative formulations:
- Lay: Is a patient's oxygen dose partly set by how much oxygen the rest of the hospital is using at that moment?
- Technical: Using pipeline flow/pressure telemetry as exposure and per-patient oxygenation as outcome, estimate the load-dependent delivery shortfall and its clinical footprint, with design flow as the pre-declared breakpoint.
Motivating gap: The telemetry exists (Leicester/IHEEM: 60 s BMS dashboard, ultrasonic meters, per-leg daily reports, alarm levels derived from copper pipe capacity) with no patient endpoint anywhere in it; the clinical literature treats the wall outlet as an infinite source. The worry is documented (NHS Wales EFA 2020-001, ECRI S0396, NHS Scotland SAN SC2103, AARC 2020). Found in this run and decisive: the worry has been formally investigated once, for one incident, and left unresolved (HSSIB, P1).
Presuppositions: P1 leg flow/pressure logged at usable resolution — established for >=1 trust, generalisability unverified. P2 per-patient-hour prescription retrievable — contested in the record; moved toward established by adversary #2's 2024 medRxiv read (two years of EMR device settings). P3 delivered flow measurable at a terminal unit — contested; instrumented-outlet route demonstrated by Davidson's flow-analyser rig. P4 regulated delivery can fall within the observed load range — contested, the premise most able to dissolve the question; survives as open (HSSIB 4.2 -> 3.1 bar sits below the 50 psi = 3.45 bar flowmeter calibration point, adversary arithmetic). P5 endpoint sampled fast enough — established only where continuous SpO2 exists. P6 exogenous load is not an acuity proxy — contested; the negative-control-leg answer is asserted, not evidenced.
What would count as an answer: a load-response curve of delivered-minus-set flow (secondary: FiO2 shortfall) against exogenous leg demand as a fraction of design flow, on instrumented terminal units in >=2 hospitals over >=1 high-demand period, plus the within-patient variance share from within-patient and negative-control contrasts. A flat curve reported with the minimum detectable shortfall answers it negatively, so both branches are reportable.

Significance — consequence: if delivery degrades with load, a flowmeter setting stops being a dose under surge: MGPS alarm thresholds currently derived from copper pipe capacity acquire a patient-outcome basis, demand-management plans gain a target leg and load instead of hospital-wide restriction, and an HTM revision would have an exposure-response to write design flow against.
