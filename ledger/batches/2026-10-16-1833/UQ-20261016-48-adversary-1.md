# UQ-20261016-48 — Adversary #1 log

## Restatement (my own words)
When two institutionally separate teams red-team the same frozen model under documented access and time, (a) how often do they find the same failure CLASS, once "same" is fixed by a pre-registered matching rule whose own reliability is measured, and (b) does that overlap let a multi-list population estimator (Chao-type for unequal catchability; log-linear for positive list dependence) put a finite, non-trivial upper bound on how many classes discoverable-at-that-effort both teams missed, given that the class population is neither closed nor equally catchable?

## Recommended verdict
Provisional. No attack is Unresolved, but attacks 2, 4 and 7 each require the record to be rewritten: (i) add the HCI evaluator-effect literature (Hertzum & Jacobsen 2003; Jacobsen, Hertzum & John 1998) as the nearest methodological prior and withdraw the claim that evaluator-generated problem populations are unprecedented; (ii) reword limb (b) so that for K=2 the list dependence is a declared sensitivity range (not "fitted") and the vacuity test must pass across that range, citing the untestability of the identifying assumption; (iii) demote "absent construct" from primary reason for silence. Well-posedness stays 2/3 at best until (ii) is done, which alone bars Validated.

Strongest killing point: for the frame the record leads with (two institutes on one checkpoint), list dependence is not identifiable from a 2-list incidence matrix and the identifying assumption is untestable - estimates under different assumptions can be "arbitrarily different ... even when the models have identical fits to the observed data" (Aleshin-Guendel, Sadinle & Wakefield, arXiv:2101.09304, opened) - so "does the overlap support a non-vacuous estimate" is, as worded, decided by the analyst's assumption rather than by the overlap.
