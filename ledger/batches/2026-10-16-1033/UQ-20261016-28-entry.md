Domain(s): Medicine — laboratory medicine, clinical epidemiology; method source field: climatology (station-record homogenization). Source method: cross-field transplant.
Question (canonical): For a pre-specified frame of multi-site laboratory records (>=K sites, >=15 years, analytes restricted in advance to those with documented commutability across the method generations present) and a pre-registered list of published secular-trend claims resting on them: (a) what breakpoints and offsets does a pairwise-reference procedure transplanted from climate-station homogenization (contemporaneous co-catchment sites as reference series; SNHT / pairwise comparison / ACMANT-style detection on differenced series) detect per site-analyte; (b) against independently retrieved analyzer/method/calibrator change logs, what are the sensitivity and specificity of detection and the agreement of estimated with documented offsets; and (c) on the homogenized record, what fraction of each published trend's magnitude is attributable to breakpoint offsets, and how many trends retain sign and significance? [R1, the one Gate 1 reformulation, adopted. V0 as intaken, and the three under-specifications R1 fixed, are in the record.]
Alternative formulations:
1. (lay) How much of the long-term change we think we see in patients' blood test results is actually the laboratory's equipment changing?
2. (technical) Can a homogenized version of a multi-site decades-long laboratory record be constructed, and does it change the sign or magnitude of reported secular trends in haemoglobin, creatinine/eGFR, albumin or total cholesterol?
3. (the narrow residue surviving Gate 2) Do contemporaneous co-catchment laboratories function as valid reference series for each other in the climatological sense — does the differenced series between two sites' same-analyte distributions isolate instrument change from population change well enough that blind detection beats chance against independently retrieved logs?
Motivating gap: Climatology treats an un-homogenized station trend as uninterpretable and repairs it as a matter of routine (neighbour-referenced breakpoint detection and correction, adjustment uncertainty propagated into the published trend). Multi-decade clinical laboratory series in EHRs, biobanks and registries have the same event structure — analyzer replacement, reagent/calibrator lot and method-generation changes are station-history events — and are used raw to support secular-trend claims; IFCC/JCTLM standardization acts forward on new methods and reconstructs no historical series. Gate 2 held the gap PARTLY FALSE, on the ground that two of the three limbs have executed precedent in the candidate's own literature (multi-site blind detection — Poh/de Lusignan; correction plus trend re-estimation with a published magnitude — ARIC/Parrinello). That ground did not survive, and it was overturned twice independently: by the judge's post-Gate-5 verification (2026-10-16; fresh-context checker, 15 queries, 0 refusals, log in batches/2026-10-16-1033/UQ-20261016-28-verification.md) and, separately, by the Gate 4 adversary, which opened Poh for itself. What stands: ARIC/Parrinello's magnitude is published and real (CKD prevalence 21.7% -> 1.3% at visit 1; creatinine bias 49%/47%/13% against the paper's own >10% threshold). What falls: Poh/de Lusignan is not station-record breakpoint detection and ARIC has no detection step at all — see Novelty - how this differs.
Presuppositions: P1 method/calibrator change dates recoverable — CONTESTED (Poh: no Read code for the creatinine assay, no ground truth in a 127-practice extract; non-retention is the normal case). P2 enough contemporaneous sites share the true population signal to serve as reference series — CONTESTED, load-bearing, untested by any opened source. P3 method changes give offsets/scale changes, not a change of measurand — CONTESTED (R1 narrows to commutable analytes rather than establishing it; ARIC's HDL precipitation-to-direct-enzymatic case is a counter-instance). P4 published trends retrievable in re-estimable detail — PLAUSIBLE, untested at frame level. P5 offsets detectable above within-site noise — PARTLY ESTABLISHED for creatinine (ARIC >10% bias for creatinine and uric acid), unknown elsewhere. P6 climatological pairwise methods transport to irregular, censored, indication-driven sampling — CONTESTED. P7 the site's patient population is not itself discontinuous at the method-change date — CONTESTED (analyzer purchase co-timed with a service-line change confounds detection and correction alike, and does so identically in the differenced series). P8 (added at Gate 1) "specificity" is well-defined, i.e. a period with no documented change is a true negative — CONTESTED AND NOT REPAIRED BY R1; incomplete logs (P1) mean absence of a documented change is not absence of a change. P9 (added at Gate 4, on the adversary's requirement) breaks are assumed SITE-IDIOSYNCRATIC — CONTESTED AND FALSE FOR A SUBCLASS, and this is the entry's sharpest objection: a pairwise-reference procedure tests the differenced series d_ij(t) = x_i(t) - x_j(t), so if every site in the frame shifts by the same amount at the same time — a national reagent reformulation, a calibrator re-traceable to a new reference material, IDMS-traceable creatinine calibration propagating through a country's laboratories, a guideline-mandated equation swap — the break cancels identically in every pair and is invisible to relative homogenization by construction (the adversary's own derivation, labelled as such, and an identity rather than an empirical claim). Laboratory method changes are MORE synchronised across sites than weather-station relocations, because manufacturers, standardisation programmes and guideline committees act on a whole country at once; Poh's inferred method change in 80 of 127 practices inside a 2006-2010 window and ARIC's HDL method-generation change (magnesium-dextran precipitation to direct enzymatic) are both of that class. Full markings in the record.
What would count as an answer: an implemented homogenization on a defined multi-site record and pre-specified analyte set reporting (i) detected breakpoints and estimated offsets, validated against independently retrieved change logs as sensitivity/specificity with the true-negative frame defined per P8, and (ii) trends re-estimated with and without correction, with the breakpoint-attributable fraction and a CI. Three decision-relevant outcomes: trends unchanged within CI licenses raw-series claims; trends materially changed obliges correction before any secular claim; detection no better than chance is a negative answer about the transplant itself.

Novelty — how this differs from each: vs Poh — CORRECTED, and it is a weaker prior than Gate 2 conceded; both the verification checker and the Gate 4 adversary opened it and agree. Its unit of analysis is the individual patient's paired series ("We shall call this paired measurement time series the renal function time series of the patient"), its reference is internal and exists only for a DERIVED quantity — it maps a self-recalculated MDRD eGFR onto the laboratory-reported eGFR and counts how many mapping functions are needed — and peer practices are explicitly NOT a reference: "As all practices sent laboratory samples to a single laboratory this change will affect all eGFR measurements reported by that practice." There is no ground truth ("No codes were identified in the Read code system which enable recording of creatinine assay method"), no sensitivity or specificity, no correction ("Identification of these changes in eGFR calculation method is the first step towards backwards calibration") and no trend re-estimation. So it detects a change in the eGFR REPORTING EQUATION, an event class visible only where a derived quantity exists, and it cannot run on haemoglobin, albumin or total cholesterol at all. Limb (a) for equation-free analytes is therefore substantially less occupied than the record allowed, and limb (b) was found uncovered in every vocabulary either reviewer searched. vs ARIC — already produces the magnitude the candidate asks for, for one corpus, so the magnitude cannot be claimed unmeasured; differences are that ARIC has no detection step (visit times are the candidate change dates), its reference is a physical gold standard (banked aliquots reassayed) not a neighbouring site, it is a scheduled-visit fixed cohort with one central laboratory, and it re-estimates within its own cohort rather than a pre-registered frame of external claims. vs Sternberg — change known, dated and singular; subject is the estimator for a given bridge, no detection, no multi-site structure, and it needs the stored specimens routine records lack. Strongest remaining objection: P9, the unlisted presupposition. A pairwise-reference design can only see breaks that are NOT shared across the frame, because a simultaneous common shift cancels identically in every differenced series — and in laboratory medicine the largest and most consequential breaks are precisely the shared ones, since manufacturers, standardisation programmes and guideline committees change a whole country's laboratories at once, whereas weather stations are re-sited one at a time. This inverts the condition that makes the climatological transplant work, it appears nowhere in P1-P8, and it biases the study's most quotable deliverable in the most dangerous direction: limb (c) will systematically under-attribute published trend magnitude to breakpoints, which manufactures the record's own first decision-relevant outcome ("trends unchanged within CI licenses raw-series claims") for the wrong reason. It stands at Partial rather than Unresolved only because the blindness covers a subclass and because no source was opened quantifying the synchronised-versus-idiosyncratic share of fifteen-year laboratory breakpoints; the adversary states plainly that a reviewer with that number could make this a kill. Second, from attack 5 and not conceded in the record: the attributable fraction is detector-dependent, so it is identified only conditional on a detected break set and must be reported that way with sensitivity analyses across detectors.
