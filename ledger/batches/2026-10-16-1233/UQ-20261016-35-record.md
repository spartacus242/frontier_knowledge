Question (canonical) — V1, Gate 1 reformulation (one reformulation used, for well-posedness only):
In a biobank operating a deletion-on-withdrawal mandate, using the custodian's dated withdrawal-ID tranches and a panel of headline estimates fixed in advance (k GWAS associations, k′ epidemiological hazard ratios, one polygenic score's discrimination): (a) what is the pre-deletion standardised difference between withdrawn and retained participants on a pre-specified variable set — the exposures, covariates and outcomes the cohort is actually used to study; (b) how far does each headline estimate move when recomputed on the frame immediately before and immediately after each withdrawal tranche, measured against its Monte Carlo error and against pre-declared decision boundaries (the significance threshold, a clinically relevant effect size, a reclassification threshold); and (c) does the cumulative movement accumulate monotonically in the direction predicted by (a) — i.e. is it systematic drift rather than noise?

Question (V0, intake, retained per Gate 1):
In a biobank that requires researchers to delete the data of participants who withdraw consent, how far do headline estimates move as withdrawals accumulate, and is withdrawal non-random with respect to the exposures and outcomes the cohort is used to study — so that its results drift irreversibly in a direction that, after deletion, nobody can measure?

Reason for reformulation: V0 fuses three limbs with a "so that" and closes on a rhetorical clause ("nobody can measure") that is a claim rather than part of the estimand. V1 separates the covariate contrast (a), the realised estimate shift (b) and the systematic-drift test (c), names the comparator (the frame one tranche earlier) and the decision boundaries, and demotes the irreversibility claim to a presupposition (P3/P6) where it can be tested rather than assumed. No substantive content was dropped.

Alternative formulations:
- What does the right to withdraw do to the scientific record a biobank has already produced?
- Are the people who revoke consent in a cohort study systematically different, on the very variables that cohort is used to study, from those who stay — and how much do published estimates change when they are removed?
- (method-field phrasing) For a biobank cohort, how does the realised influence of the actually-withdrawn subset compare with the worst-case influence of an adversarially chosen subset of the same size, and is the realised influence signed and cumulative?

Motivating gap:
Silence mapping. The "how" of withdrawal is densely documented and operational; the "why/so-what" is absent. Verified in this run: the UK Biobank community article "Participant withdrawals on UKB-RAP" (opened) sets out voluntary withdrawal from October 2025 and enforced withdrawal from 1 April 2026, instructs researchers that withdrawn participants' data must be "no longer accessed, analysed, or retained", states that removal "supports the accuracy and validity of research outputs" — and says nothing about existing results, re-running analyses, or any count or rate of withdrawals. A UK Biobank community forum post from a researcher who noticed participants "automatically removed from the UKB dataset when I updated the dataset" and asked how to strip them from previously derived files is the operational symptom of the question with no trace of its consequences. The two adjacent scientific literatures are about ENTRY (participation/selection bias — Schoeler et al. on correcting participation bias in UK Biobank, and the Birmingham "effect of seeking consent on the representativeness of patient cohorts"; both title-level only, NOT opened) and about OUTCOME ASCERTAINMENT (dropout / loss to follow-up). Neither is retroactive deletion of data already contributed and already analysed. The field that owns the method — finite-sample robustness to dropping data — has the estimand but has never been pointed at consent withdrawal (see prior 1). The governance twist that distinguishes this from attrition: the data vanish, so the quantity may be computable only once, by the custodian, at the moment it is executing the deletion.

Presuppositions:
- P1 — withdrawals are numerous enough for their removal to move anything. **ESTABLISHED at one custodian, by a figure obtained in this run** (previously "to be established"): Ursin et al. 2020 (opened) report 1,187 of 65,291 HUNT2 participants (1.9%) withdrew their samples after a single 2002 letter about genetic research use, with 42 withdrawals from HUNT3 and 126 from HUNT4. 1.9% sits inside the regime where prior 1 demonstrates that dropping data can flip published conclusions (<1% in the Oregon Medicaid example). Qualifier, stated: HUNT2's figure follows an acute trust episode and may be atypically high; UK Biobank publishes no count in the pages opened here, so the typical rate is unestablished. LIMITING for (b): at 0.1–2% removal, well-powered GWAS point estimates are unlikely to move materially (SE scales ~1/√n), so the live cases are subgroup and rare-variant estimates and estimates sitting near a decision boundary.
- P2 — dated releases, archived derived files or frozen analysis files exist that allow one analysis to be re-run on an earlier and a later frame. **CONTESTED**, and the deletion mandate works against it: complying with enforced withdrawal means the earlier frame no longer exists for the researcher. Dissolved only in the custodian-side design (answer-form (a)), where the custodian holds both frames at the moment of deletion.
- P3 — the custodian either retains, at the moment of withdrawal, enough information to describe withdrawers in aggregate, or has already recorded it. **CONTESTED**. If withdrawal terms forbid any further use including aggregate characterisation, the quantity is unidentifiable to everyone — which is itself a publishable and consequential answer, not a failure of the question. Note the asymmetry: most custodian regimes distinguish "no further use of your data" from "no retention of anonymous aggregate statistics already computed", so the legal question of whether a pre-deletion aggregate may be retained is part of what (a) would settle.
- P4 — withdrawal is not the informative censoring that loss-to-follow-up methods already handle. **ESTABLISHED as a structural difference, CONTESTED in magnitude.** Dropout truncates observation prospectively and leaves the already-collected record in place; withdrawal removes the record retrospectively. The sharpest instance of this contrast is the refreshment-sample literature (prior-adjacent, titles only): it identifies non-ignorable attrition precisely *because* attriters' earlier waves are retained. Deletion removes the very data that makes attrition correctable, which is why an attrition method is not a withdrawal method.
- P5 — someone acts on the answer. **CONTESTED (significance limb)**; argued in Gate 3, not asserted. Weakest where the expected answer for headline GWAS estimates is "no detectable movement".
- P6 — deletion is actually executed by every researcher holding a copy. **CONTESTED**; compliance is unverifiable, and this cuts both ways: it weakens the premise that affected results are irreproducible and strengthens the premise that the analysis frame is undefined. The UKB-RAP article's own silence on "user-generated files or previously produced outputs" is direct evidence that the mandate does not reach derived copies.
- P7 — withdrawal reasons are not required. **FLAGGED, and this is load-bearing for novelty**: the estimand is the shift in estimates and the covariate contrast, not the motive. The entire own-field literature found at Gate 2 is about motive (prior 2), which is why it does not answer this.

What would count as an answer:
Either (a) a custodian-side analysis, run once under governance before data are destroyed, reporting the pre-deletion distribution of a pre-specified variable set in withdrawers versus retained participants, together with the change in a pre-specified panel of headline estimates (a handful of GWAS associations, a few epidemiological hazard ratios, one polygenic score's discrimination) computed on the frame before and after each year's withdrawals; or (b) a researcher-side equivalent using dated releases and archived derived files, with withdrawal-ID lists as the only linkage. Decision boundaries pre-specified: a shift inside Monte Carlo error for every estimate settles the matter as a non-issue and licenses current practice; a shift that moves any estimate across a decision boundary establishes that every biobank result carries an unquantified, growing and irreversible erosion term — and, because of the deletion mandate, that the custodians are the only parties who will ever be able to bound it. A third outcome is informative and must be declared in advance: if (a) is legally unrunnable because withdrawal terms forbid aggregate characterisation, the finding is that the erosion term is unidentifiable to every party, which is a governance result with the same audience. For (c), the direction test (does the cumulative shift track the sign predicted by the covariate contrast) separates systematic drift from sampling noise and is what makes "irreversible drift" a measurable rather than rhetorical claim. Benchmarking note that makes the magnitude interpretable: the realised shift should be reported alongside the worst-case shift from dropping the same number of observations (prior 1's AMIP), so that "small" means small relative to what a same-sized adversarial subset could have done.

Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, with sources I actually opened):
**N1. Broderick T, Giordano R, Meager R, "An Automatic Finite-Sample Robustness Metric: When Can Dropping a Little Data Make a Big Difference?", arXiv:2011.14999 (page dated 4 Nov 2021).** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.14999 — OPENED.
 · Unit of analysis: individual observations (households, in the applied examples).
 · Endpoint: a quantity of interest — a coefficient, or an indicator of sign change, loss of significance, or a significant result of the opposite sign.
 · Reference/comparator: the original full-sample estimate.
 · Estimand: the Maximum Influence Perturbation — "the largest possible change induced in the quantity of interest by dropping no more than 100α% of the data", with the dropped subset chosen **adversarially** (worst case over all admissible subsets), approximated to first order in observation weights (AMIP). Motivated explicitly by missingness that "cannot safely be assumed random". Demonstrated: <1% of the Oregon Medicaid sample can flip results; one observation of 16,500 controls the sign of the Mexico microcredit profit effect. Contains nothing on cohorts, biobanks or consent.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
vs **N1** (the most dangerous prior, and the one a Gate 4 Disguise pass should press hardest). Shared: both ask how far an estimate moves when a small, possibly non-random share of the data is removed, and both reject the assumption that removal is random. Four differences, each checked against N1's four attributes. (i) **Estimand**: N1's is a *supremum over subsets chosen adversarially*; the candidate's is the *realised* shift from one specific subset that the world, not the analyst, selected — a point on N1's curve, not the curve's maximum. These answer different questions: N1 bounds what could happen, the candidate measures what did. A small AMIP would settle the candidate's (b) in the negative for free, so N1 can *partly pre-empt* the candidate wherever AMIP has been computed on biobank data — but nothing found has computed it there, and a large AMIP (which N1's examples show is common) says nothing about the realised shift. (ii) **Unit**: N1's unit is an arbitrary observation; the candidate's is a withdrawn participant, a unit defined by a governance act, which is what makes the subset non-randomly selected *in a specific, predictable direction* rather than worst-case. (iii) **Endpoint**: the candidate adds limb (a), the pre-deletion covariate contrast, which has no analogue in N1 — N1 never asks what the dropped observations looked like, because its dropped set is hypothetical. (iv) **Reference**: N1's comparator is the full sample, which it still holds; the candidate's earlier frame is destroyed by the mandate, so the comparison is one-shot and custodian-only. Limb (c), the monotone-direction test, requires repeated tranches and is unavailable in N1's single-perturbation framing.
vs **N2**. Same population (actual biobank withdrawers) and the right field, but every one of the four attributes differs: unit is an interview account rather than a record, the endpoint is a trust theme rather than a statistic, the comparator is other people's talk rather than an earlier data frame, and there is no estimand. N2 is the clearest evidence for the silence mechanism: when the candidate's own field studies withdrawers, it studies their reasons (P7's "not required"), and its only numbers are counts of who left. N2 is a *source* for the candidate's P1, not a competitor to its question.
vs **N3**. Same mechanism, opposite object: N3 is the operational instruction that creates the phenomenon. Its endpoint is compliance, not validity, and its one validity claim ("supports the accuracy and validity of research outputs") is asserted about *avoiding accidental inclusion* — the exact inverse of the candidate's worry, which is that correct exclusion is what moves the estimate. Its silence on previously produced outputs is the gap, stated by the custodian itself.
Structural contrast with the attrition/refreshment family (titles only, so not graded): those methods are identified by *retaining* the attriters' already-collected waves. The candidate's regime deletes them. An attrition correction is therefore not an available answer here, which is P4.

Significance — consequence:
If (b) and (c) are positive — some pre-specified estimate crosses a decision boundary and the cumulative movement tracks the sign predicted by (a) — then biobank results carry an erosion term that is unquantified, monotonically growing, and, uniquely, *unmeasurable after the fact by anyone except the custodian at the moment of deletion*. That converts a governance mechanism into a methodological liability with a named remedy: a mandatory pre-deletion aggregate characterisation retained as anonymous summary statistics, and versioned, citable frame identifiers so that a result names the frame it was computed on. If negative at realistic rates — every estimate moves within Monte Carlo error — the finding licenses current practice, retires a plausible worry with a number instead of an argument, and (reported against N1's AMIP for the same subset size) shows that the realised influence of governance-driven deletion is far below the adversarial bound, which is itself a useful calibration of N1's alarm for this data class. The honest prior, stated because it bears on the score: at 0.1–2% removal in a cohort of n≈500,000, headline GWAS point estimates are very unlikely to move materially. The consequence therefore concentrates in three places rather than in a headline catastrophe — subgroup and rare-variant estimates where effective n is small, estimates sitting within a hair of a threshold, and the identifiability/irreversibility finding itself, which holds regardless of magnitude. The third outcome (P3: aggregate characterisation legally forbidden, so nobody can ever bound the term) is the one with the largest consequence and the least dependence on effect size.
If (a) is positive but (b) is null, the result is still substantive: it establishes that withdrawal is informative selection, bounds its current harm at ~0, and makes the growth rate of the erosion term the thing to monitor — which is exactly the quantity a custodian can monitor and a researcher cannot.

Significance — audience:
(1) **Biobank custodians** (UK Biobank, HUNT, All of Us, Estonian Biobank, China Kadoorie, national birth cohorts): they designed the deletion mechanism, they are the only party holding both frames, and a positive finding changes a procedure they control — specifically whether a pre-deletion aggregate is computed and retained, and whether releases are versioned as citable frames. They act on it by adding one step to a workflow they already run. (2) **RECs/IRBs and data-protection officers** who negotiated the research exemption to the right of erasure: the question supplies the missing empirical input to a trade-off they currently settle on principle alone — how much scientific validity the erasure right costs. Strongest where the answer is the P3 unidentifiability outcome, since that is a direct consequence of how they drafted the withdrawal terms. (3) **Journals, funders and reproducibility bodies**: "the frame on which this result was computed no longer exists" is a category of irreproducibility that current data-availability policies do not name, and the remedy (frame versioning) is a policy lever they hold. (4) **The machine-unlearning community**, whose motivating premise is that a participant's influence can be removed on request without consequence for the rest: N1 already shows the worst-case influence of <1% of data can be decisive, and this question supplies the realised-case counterpart on a real deletion stream — the empirical test of unlearning's own premise, which query 6 confirms its literature does not run.
Honest discount: audience (1) is real but conflicted — a positive finding indicts the mechanism they built, which is also the reason-for-silence mechanism. Audience (4) is the one most likely to take the question up without conflict and least likely to have the data.

Significance — fecundity:
Six follow-ons, of which the first three are unaskable without the measurement. (i) Optimal retention policy: what is the minimal aggregate that must be computed and kept at the moment of withdrawal to keep the frame auditable forever, and is it compatible with the erasure right as drafted? (ii) Is withdrawal predictable from baseline data — a withdrawal-propensity model would let analysts weight, and would also raise its own ethics (modelling people who asked to be unmodelled), which is a new normative question generated by a methodological answer. (iii) Should estimates be reported with a deletion-sensitivity term, i.e. does N1's AMIP belong in cohort reporting standards as a routine robustness statistic? (iv) Are deletion mandates and reproducibility mandates jointly satisfiable, and what does a reproducibility standard look like under irreversible frame change? (v) Does the drift transport across biobanks with different consent regimes — broad consent, dynamic consent, opt-out — which decides whether one custodian's measurement is reusable as a prior or each must measure its own. (vi) Does the measured realised influence calibrate the machine-unlearning literature's assumption of harmless removal?

Significance — anchoring:
GDPR Art. 17 and its research derogations, plus national biobank-act equivalents; the reproducibility and data-availability debate (a frame that cannot be reconstituted is a new failure mode in it); N1's finite-sample robustness literature and the broader influence-function/case-deletion family; participation and selection bias in UK Biobank (Schoeler et al., unopened), which is this question's entry-side twin; informative censoring, MNAR and non-ignorable attrition, including the refreshment-sample identification strategy that deletion specifically defeats (P4); the machine-unlearning / right-to-be-forgotten premise; and HUNT's own 2002 withdrawal episode (N2), which is the field's only documented instance of a large, dated, single-cause withdrawal tranche and therefore the most likely natural experiment for limb (c).

Reason for silence:
Two compounding mechanisms, both admissible under the ledger's list, and both evidenced by the Gate 2 returns rather than asserted.
(1) **An incentive-and-authority gap.** The only party able to compute the quantity is the custodian, and it must be computed at the exact moment the custodian is executing a legal deletion instruction — so the computation is (a) a procedural burden added to a compliance act, (b) legally awkward, since it is one more use of data whose owner has just demanded no further use, and (c) capable of indicting the governance mechanism the custodian designed and advertises. Meanwhile the community that would care — researchers relying on the cohort — has, by construction, had the data removed and cannot ask the question with data in hand. The question is thus unanswerable by those who want it answered and unattractive to those who can answer it. Evidence: N3, the custodian's own withdrawal article, frames removal purely as compliance and asserts without measurement that it "supports the accuracy and validity of research outputs"; it publishes no count, which is the first number the question needs.
(2) **A disciplinary boundary, and specifically a framing boundary at the point of intake.** Withdrawal is institutionally classified as an ethics and governance object — consent, trust, compliance — so the literature that grew around it is bioethics, qualitative social science and operations (queries 1, 5, 9, 11 return exactly that: motives, trust, "Requests to destroy data", "Data Handling When a Subject Withdraws"). The field that owns the estimand classifies the same act as an observation-weight perturbation and never encounters the word (query 4 returns N1 with nothing on consent). Neither field's search terms reach the other's, which is why a mature method and a live phenomenon have coexisted for a decade without meeting. This is the mechanism the Gate 2 rule was written to catch, and here it is visible in the query log rather than inferred.
Not offered as mechanisms: novelty of instrument (withdrawal mandates are old) or taboo (nothing forbids asking, only answering).
