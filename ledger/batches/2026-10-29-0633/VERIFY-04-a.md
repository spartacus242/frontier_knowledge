### (4) The entry's falsification condition: is the 6x deficit specific to interlayer averaging in a 2-D vdW bicrystal, or general? — The paper neither establishes nor excludes it; it names THREE contributors, two tied to hBN's layered structure in its own words and one general, and apportions none.

The paper's own diagnosis (Q3, 13 fragments, F4; segments 1–3 and 9–13 match adversary #2's log verbatim; segments 4–8 were NOT in adversary #2's log):
"Such a discrepancy cannot simply be explained by a lack of z-vibrations in our reconstruction, as the z-resolution of 2 nm in this ptychographic reconstruction is clearly not sufficient to detect fluctuations along this axis. Although for a 2D material, such as hBN, where the layers are bound only by the Van der Waals forces to one another, the z-vibrations might significantly increase the isotropic squared displacements, two additional limiting factors are responsible for reducing the amplitude of reconstructed correlations and the remaining broadening of [the images of] projected atom positions within the layers: 1) the z-distance between atomic layers in hBN is 0.33 nm, which means that per 0.48 nm thin potential slice there are on average 1.4 layers of atoms, the positions of which are averaged over, and 2) the limited number of only 10 object states may not be sufficient to represent all of the TDS and Debye-Waller factor by an incoherent sum of diffraction patterns from statically displaced atom configurations."
(Bracketed "[the images of]": F4 served "...broadening of projected atom positions...", F6 served "...broadening of the images of projected atom positions..." — two passes of the same summariser disagree on three words; I record both and do not resolve it.)
Grammar/polarity: sentence 1's opener "Such a discrepancy" scopes the 6x deficit just stated; the clause DENIES that the missing z-vibrations ALONE ("simply") explain it while PRESUPPOSING that they are missing (the "as" clause gives the reason: 2 nm z-resolution). Sentence 2's concessive opener "Although for a 2D material, such as hBN, where the layers are bound only by the Van der Waals forces" ASSERTS that z-vibrations "might significantly increase the isotropic squared displacements" — a concession that the isotropic comparator is plausibly inflated by out-of-plane motion in a vdW-bound layered material; the main clause then ASSERTS two "additional" amplitude-reducing factors: (1) averaging of 1.4 atomic layers per 0.48 nm slice (stated with hBN's 0.33 nm interlayer distance), (2) 10 object states possibly insufficient to represent the TDS and Debye-Waller factor.
So the paper itself attributes the deficit to: (i) missing out-of-plane vibrations, explicitly framed for "a 2D material, such as hBN" bound by van der Waals forces — hBN/comparator-specific in the paper's own words; (ii) layer averaging within a slice, stated in hBN's numbers (0.33 nm vs 0.48 nm) — layered-structure-specific as worded, though the mechanism (several atomic layers per slice averaged) is a slice-thickness relation that any host with slices thicker than one atomic layer would share (that generalisation is MY reasoning, not the paper's); (iii) the finite number of object states — general to the mixed-state technique. The paper does not quantify any of the three, does not rank them, and does not say "specific to vdW" or "general to the method". NOTHING MORE on the cause was FOUND in the served main text; the paragraph after it moves to the dynamical matrix. This is NOT FOUND, not COULD NOT READ: the main text was served in full.
The paper's own evidence on generality — the SIMULATED 3-D covalent case (Q6, short fragments, F7): for the Si Sigma-9 grain boundary with 20 object states and 4 slices of 0.19 nm (about one atomic layer per slice), no noise, MD ground truth known: "their magnitudes are approximately 25% lower than the ground truth." with cosine-similarity metrics "the CSM value is the highest (0.98), showing an almost perfect match." (no partial spatial coherence) and "reduces the CSM value only slightly to 0.96." (with it). So the technique's demonstrated general underestimate of correlation magnitude in a 3-D host under near-ideal conditions is about 25%, a factor of about 1.3 — not about 6. Also in the simulation section: "Although there is no fundamental bound prohibiting a full 3D reconstruction, the z-resolution currently achievable with STEM-ptychography [21] is not sufficient to quantify the out-of-plane vibrations." (3 fragments).
MY OWN INFERENCE, LABELLED AS MINE: the 25% figure is a correlation-magnitude comparison against a same-projection ground truth, not a B-factor comparison against an isotropic standard, and the hBN experiment differs in object-state count (10 vs 20), layers per slice (1.4 vs about 1), noise, partial coherence and comparator type; so the paper's data cannot say what fraction of the hBN 6x would survive in a 3-D host at experimental settings. What the paper's own numbers DO show is that the part of the deficit demonstrated to be general (factor about 1.3 in simulation) is far smaller than the hBN deficit (factor about 6), leaving the remainder unapportioned among the two hBN-specific contributors, the halved object-state count and experimental effects. A grounding study would reconstruct one specimen region while varying object-state count and slice thickness against an absolute standard — adversary #2 already said it could name none; I did not search for one (not my check).
