Question (canonical): At three or more temperatures in 70-150 K, where the stacking state is quasi-frozen on a laboratory timescale, what is the ratio of mean grain-grain sintered-contact tensile strength for stacking-disordered/cubic (Isd/Ic) water ice to that for hexagonal (Ih) ice, at matched temperature, contact time, load, grain radius and specific surface area, with stacking order measured on the same grains before and after each contact test; and at one declared anneal temperature in 170-200 K, does contact strength change through an isothermal anneal that converts Isd to Ih, measured against a paired Ih control held at the same temperature for the same duration?
Reformulation note: this is V2, the one reformulation permitted at Gate 1 and the version scored. V1 declared a single 70-150 K window in which the Isd-to-Ih conversion was both quasi-frozen and followable; that conjunction is not satisfiable there (Falenty, Kuhs & Hansen, arXiv:1510.08004, introduction: "cubic ice requires low temperatures (below ~ 150 - 160 K) to be stable on a laboratory timescale"). V2 splits the windows and adds the paired same-temperature control. Estimand unchanged. Depth re-applied to V2 by the Gate 0-3 researcher and by both adversaries on their own restatements: Pass each time.

Alternative formulations:
- Is ice grain cohesion a function of stacking order as well as of temperature, time, load and grain radius?
- Does the crystalline polytype of vapour-deposited or droplet-frozen ice change how strongly grains stick, and does annealing to hexagonal ice strengthen or weaken the bond?

Motivating gap: "Does the polymorph control interparticle adhesion and caking?" is routine in powder and pharmaceutical physics and returns no ice item; cryogenic and glaciological ice cohesion is parameterised as force = f(temperature, load, duration, grain radius) with no polytype term (Bahaloo et al. 2022, abstract level); and ice below ~200 K is routinely stacking-disordered. The gap as submitted claimed nobody in the target field raises crystalline phase as a candidate control on grain-grain interaction; the record corrects that, and the adversaries narrow it further. Two in-field papers raise it in their own words and neither varies or measures it: Gärtner et al. 2017 excludes bulk phase by temperature non-coincidence, and Musiolik & Wurm 2019 names "the change of the crystal structure of the ice at lower temperatures" as one of two causes of its measured pull-off-force temperature dependence and then models only the diffuse layer. What survives is narrow and is the candidate's actual target: no source anyone opened measures or prints a contact strength resolved by polytype.

Significance — consequence: if the ratio falls outside the pre-declared band, every parameterisation of the Bahaloo form gains a fifth state variable that is a thermal HISTORY rather than a current condition, so two grain populations at identical temperature, contact time, load and radius differ systematically in cohesion according to whether they were recently deposited or annealed — path-dependent cohesion, with a freshly redeposited surface layer mechanically distinct from annealed bulk at the same temperature. Adversary 1 scales this down with its own arithmetic from Musiolik & Wurm's numbers (0.003-0.008 J/m2 at 175 K against 0.19 or 0.5 J/m2 above 200 K, a 1.5-2 order-of-magnitude diffuse-layer swing): a factor-1.5 polytype effect at 70-150 K is a second-order term on a cohesion already near its floor, so the consequence is real but smaller than "qualitative change" implies. If the null holds, the temperature-only parameterisations are justified against a specifically named and physically plausible alternative instead of by default.
