## Restatement in my own words

Every published ICS dose-ranging trial randomises the number printed on the inhaler. What a
given patient's lung actually receives per actuation is not that number and varies between
patients. The question: if you re-fit the dose-response curve on a per-patient estimate of
the dose actually delivered, instead of on the prescribed label dose, how much steeper does
it get (maximum slope, ED50)? And of that gap, how much is (a) spread in the
nominal-to-delivered multiplier, (b) real pharmacodynamic saturation, (c) patients differing
in steroid responsiveness — with a stated test of whether the multiplier is correlated with
baseline severity? A "no" answer is a bound showing the multiplier's spread is too small to
produce the observed flatness.
