Verification (post-batch, fresh-context checker, log UQ-20261016-60-verification.md): the kill is CONFIRMED on both halves.
Kuntz and Eisen 2014 (PLoS Genetics; arXiv 1306.5297) filmed D. melanogaster embryos at seven temperatures from 17.5 to 32.5 C, scoring 36 landmarks per embryo: "while the total time of embryogenesis varies over two fold", "the relative timing of events from cellularization through hatching is constant across all species and temperatures examined here", and the landmarks occur "in the same temporal order", holding across ten further Drosophila species ("go through the same stages in the same order at all viable temperatures", Fig. 4 caption). So within a species, temperature is a uniform rescaling with order preserved — the candidate's first limb, answered.
The companion, "Oxygen changes drive non-uniform scaling in Drosophila melanogaster embryogenesis" (F1000Research 2015, 4:1102), reports that oxygen does NOT rescale uniformly and that "the order of several developmental landmarks is inverted at different oxygen levels": hypoxia has head involution finishing first, hyperoxia has the heart-shaped midgut forming first, with the inversion point shifting with temperature (about 29% O2 at 27.5 C, about 19% at 17.5 C), over more than 800 embryos across 18 oxygen-temperature conditions. So the candidate's second limb — can an environmental change invert landmark order — is answered too, with a named landmark pair.
Qualifications recorded: the evidence is MORPHOLOGICAL, hand-scored landmarks, not transcriptomic, so it should not be cited as transcriptomic evidence; the oxygen quotes are single-sourced through a page summariser (PubMed returned a captcha, so the abstract string was not independently confirmed); and the temperature paper's order-preservation claim sits in the body text and a figure caption rather than the abstract. On the secondary items: RAPToR reports agreement with chronological age as R-squared (above 0.99 for C. elegans and zebrafish, 0.74 for later fly stages, 0.997 for a Drosophila panel) with per-sample bootstrap MAD intervals, and no overall staging error in time units — so the record's "staging error" framing for RAPToR is imprecise; and the C. elegans epoch-wise temporal-scaling paper (bioRxiv 2020.09.21.306423) was surfaced by title only and NOT opened, so that leg of the Disguise finding is unverified and is recorded as such.
