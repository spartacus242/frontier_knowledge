Question (canonical): [V2, the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation] Over a pre-declared frame of theorem-level declarations in human-authored machine-checked libraries and blueprint projects that carry an attribution to a specific published theorem (library source attributions, docstring references, blueprint-to-paper node maps), and comparing each against the published statement read under a declared blanket-hypothesis rule (standing conventions such as "all rings here are commutative Noetherian" count as stated hypotheses of the published theorem): what is the signed rate of logical-strength divergence — the share formalized strictly weaker (hypotheses dropped or generalized, or a strictly weaker axiom set) against the share formalized strictly stronger (the formalization had to assume more) — decomposed into mechanically detected divergences (axiom set via `#print axioms`, declared-but-unused binders, typeclass weakening relative to the published hypothesis) and hand-adjudicated substantive ones on a random sample with inter-adjudicator agreement; and does the weaker-side rate vary with the source's publication age, length or subfield?

Alternative formulations:
1. How often does formalization reveal that a published theorem's stated assumptions were unnecessary — and how often the reverse?
2. Now that axiom use and hypothesis use are mechanically checkable at library scale, what is the measured logical slack between published mathematics and what its proofs actually require?
(V1, superseded at Gate 1 on three defects — indeterminate "the published statement", a one-sided "fraction weaker" limb, and "over-hypothesization" prejudging attribution — is in the record.)

Motivating gap: Axiom footprint and binder use are now mechanically ascertainable per declaration at library scale; formalizers report generalization-on-formalization as constant anecdote and the community's public case for formalization partly rests on it. Gate 2 narrowed the gap to the join: the mechanical instrument plus library-scale counts ARE published (Best, FMM 2021: 2,877 hits on one ~80,000-declaration mathlib pass), and a signed formal-vs-source fidelity rate IS published for adjacent corpora (arXiv:2606.14000). No measurement found anywhere uses published-paper-attributed human library declarations as its frame.

Presuppositions: P1 a source-attributed frame is constructible (CONTESTED; frame size unverified — mathlib `docs/references.bib` could not be read, and the library has ~308,000 declarations, so the frame may be a sub-1% selected slice). P6 the published hypothesis set is determinate once blanket conventions are ruled on (CONTESTED, probably the dominant error source; V2 makes the rule explicit, not non-arbitrary). P7 discovered slack is distinguishable from deliberate expositional choice (CONTESTED, not mechanically decidable; V2 declines the attribution). P8 someone acts on the rate (partly supported; the autoformalization-scoring link is the firmest and is the one Gate 4 attack 6 left Unresolved).

What would count as an answer: A signed rate with CIs over a pre-declared, pre-registered frame, decomposed into (i) mechanical divergences (axiom set, unused binders, typeclass generalization against the published hypothesis) and (ii) hand-adjudicated substantive divergences on a random sample with inter-adjudicator agreement; both arms reported in both directions so the pre-declared symmetric null can fire; plus covariate analysis by source age, length and subfield with pre-declared strata; plus a sensitivity analysis over the blanket-hypothesis reading rule (P6) and an audit of frame selectivity (P1, P5). Admissible either way: a material weaker-side rate concentrated in older or longer sources; or a rate inside a pre-registered equivalence band with the strengthening rate, or one that collapses under the reading-rule sensitivity analysis. Gate 4 adds a condition the record lacks: without a root-cause arm (discovered slack / library generality policy / later-published generalization / formalizer error) the number is not interpretable as being about the literature.
