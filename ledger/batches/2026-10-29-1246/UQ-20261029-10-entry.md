Domain(s): Computer science — programming-language implementation and managed runtimes (just-in-time compilation; long-run performance dynamics of running systems)
Question (canonical): Is the failure of just-in-time-compiled programs to reach a steady state caused by the profile-guided recompilation feedback loop — does an execution whose compilation decisions are frozen after a declared point converge, while the same (VM, program) pair with the loop live does not?
Reformulation note: Gate 1 used its one permitted reformulation (full text in `batches/2026-10-29-1246/UQ-20261029-10-record.md`): three arms on one machine in randomised order — (A) live profile-guided recompilation, (B) a per-method plan recorded in a pilot execution and pinned from iteration 1 with adaptive recompilation disabled, (C) sham freeze applied only after the pilot's last observed compilation — over a rule-generated subset of the Barrett et al. (2017) VM x benchmark matrix at fixed iteration count, reporting the between-arm difference in the proportion of process executions a pre-declared changepoint rule classifies non-convergent. Both versions stand on the record. The late Gate 4's fatal finding (attack 2) is taken on the canonical wording; its attack 4 finds the reformulation's arm B without a confirmed instrument in any current matrix VM (Presuppositions (1)), so the reformulation does not rescue the entry.
Alternative formulations: Is long-run performance instability in managed runtimes an intrinsic instability of profile-guided recompilation, or exogenous to the compiler (collection, OS, hardware)? / What share of non-converging (VM, program) executions is converted to converging by pinning the compilation plan, against a sham-freeze control?

Corrected by the late Gate 4 in both directions: (i) "no drop locates the cause outside the compiler" is wrong in that direction (reader 1, attack 4 C3, graded Partial) — in a speculative JIT a freeze of compilation decisions cannot hold the executed code fixed: Schiavio et al. 2026 §2.2, "the JVM performs deoptimization, invalidating the compiled code and falling back to interpretation"; with recompilation frozen the method stays interpreted (reader 1's own inference from that sentence), the regime OpenJDK PR #28966 documents as a "deoptimization storm" — "once execution hits a trap, the VM performs deoptimization but does not recompile the method anymore", "thousands of deoptimizations per second", a defect that "has persisted in the codebase for 17 years" — so a frozen arm that fails to converge is the compiler's speculation plus the freeze, not an exogenous cause, and the sham arm shares the fallback and does not control for it; (ii) "a large drop answers in the feedback-instability direction" is invalid as stated (reader 2, attack 8, Partial) — the loop may amplify exogenous differences (Antoch §1; Presuppositions (6)), so the design cannot tell origin from conduit.
