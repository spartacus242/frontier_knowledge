# Area rotation

Jace, 2026-10-16: the ledger was finding most of its questions in medicine or biology, and asked that the area of focus alternate each run. Measured at 65 entries, the skew was real — medicine 35 (54%), other STEM 11, biology 10, AI 9, so medicine and biology together held 69%.

So each batch draws **all five** of its candidates from **one** area, and the areas rotate in a fixed cycle. **The cycle in force, set on 2026-10-28 from Jace's list** (mathematics, physics, computer science, and a narrowed biology and medicine):

1. **mathematics** — pure and applied mathematics; the mathematics of computation where the object is a mathematical structure. Excludes questions whose object is a software system or a database of records (a table of mathematical objects such as OEIS or LMFDB counts as mathematics when the question is about the objects).
2. **physics** — physics and astrophysics, and the physical sciences (physical chemistry, geophysics, planetary science) where the question is about a physical mechanism. Excludes engineering of instruments and networks as such, and biology and medicine even where they use physical methods.
3. **computer science** — theory, algorithms, systems, programming languages, and AI architecture and systems (model and system design, training, evaluation, learning dynamics). Excludes applications of computing to another field, which belong to that field's batch.
4. **biology** — cell behaviour, mechanisms of disease propagation and elimination in organisms, and patterns in biological data that point to an unknown mechanism. Excludes clinical and patient-facing questions.
5. **medicine** — cancer, mechanisms of disease propagation and elimination in patients and populations, and analyses of clinical or biological data aimed at a mechanism or a cause. Excludes audits of records, registries and reporting.

Every area is further filtered by the Depth requirement in `LEDGER.md`: a question about how a field studies its subject (methodology, records, bookkeeping) is rejected at intake whatever its area.

**The cycle from 2026-10-16 to 2026-10-23** was: 1. other STEM (physical sciences, mathematics, engineering, earth and space science), 2. AI architecture, 3. biology, 4. medicine. Its log rows below stand; it was retired after the eighth pass's first step.

Cross-field transplants are unaffected and still wanted: the method may come from anywhere, as long as the TARGET field — the one whose literature decides novelty and whose community would act on the answer — is the batch's area.

## How a run picks its area

Read the last line of the log below, take the next area in the cycle, pass it to the batch runner as its `area` argument, and append this batch's line when the batch is committed. A batch that fails before it commits appends nothing, so the next run takes the same area — which is correct, since that area did not get a batch.

The log is the state. Do not infer the area from entry domains or from the batch count.

**A conflict in LEDGER.md, SETTLED BY JACE ON 2026-10-30. Recorded here because the biology batch is where it first bit and the medicine batch is where it would have stopped the run.** LEDGER.md's Parameters require all five candidates of a batch to come from one rotating area ("A candidate outside the batch's area is a failed candidate however good it looks"), while the Depth requirement's in-scope-fields paragraph then required that "Of the five candidates in a batch, at least two must be in mathematics, physics or computer science and at most two in medicine" — a sentence since withdrawn, see below. Read per batch those cannot both hold: a biology batch has zero candidates in mathematics, physics or computer science, and a medicine batch has zero in those fields AND five in medicine, so trimming to two still leaves zero and the batch could only return nothing. The biology batch generated five biology candidates, as the rotation requires. The reading taken was that the mix clause is a distribution constraint over the CYCLE rather than over the batch: one full cycle is 25 candidates, 15 of them in mathematics, physics and computer science and 5 in medicine, which satisfies both bounds.

**Jace chose that reading on 2026-10-30 at 17:56 UTC**, answering the decision card posted in the ledger thread at 04:02 the same day. It is now written into LEDGER.md — the in-scope-fields paragraph of the Depth requirement carries the operative wording and a new FIELD MIX bullet under Parameters carries the history — and into all three copies of that paragraph embedded in the runner, which went to v18 with the change. The rotation itself is untouched: all five candidates of a batch still come from one area, and medicine and biology batches run as normal.

**One thing recorded here before was false, and it reached Jace.** The runner did NOT already enforce the cycle reading. It carried the per-batch sentence verbatim in three places, and a precedence clause in its generator prompt made that sentence govern "everything else in this prompt" — which includes the area instruction — so the committed script actually told a medicine batch to cap medicine at two. The cycle reading was not in the script at all; for the 13:04 batch it was injected into the runner's `area` argument, and no run parameter for it exists. The claim originated in the biology batch's own report (`ledger/batches/2026-10-30-0033.md`, "The reading used here, and why": "Since the runner is what actually runs, the conflict was already resolved in favour of the rotation before this batch"), was carried into this file, and was then given to Jace as the recommending reason on the card he answered — so he chose with a false fact in front of him. He was told of the correction and was not asked to choose again, because the choice rests on the conflict between the two rules and not on what the runner happened to do. Separately, and correcting nothing: the report that put the question to him was the biology batch's, of 2026-10-30 00:33.

Also withdrawn with the per-batch bounds is the clause that followed them in LEDGER.md, "a batch that cannot manage that returns fewer than five rather than filling the gap". Its trigger was the per-batch quota, and it was unreachable on the runner path in any case, since the generator schema admits exactly five candidates. Its surviving half — replace a failing candidate rather than keep it to make up the number — was folded into the in-scope-fields paragraph so that it still reaches every agent. Jace was not asked about that clause; it is the one judgement in this change that is not his, and it is reversible.
