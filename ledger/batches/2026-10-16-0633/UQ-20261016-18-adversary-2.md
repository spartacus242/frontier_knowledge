# UQ-20261016-18 — Gate 4 adversary #2 (verbatim)

Fresh-context adversary, run at the judge's direction after adversary #1's fatal ground was refuted on verification (see the methods verification log alongside this file). Handed the Gate 0-3 record only. Model: fable; effort: high. 17 Disguise queries, 0 safety refusals.

## Restated question (adversary's own words)

Stripped of the aviation framing, the question is: when you look at the actual minute-by-minute record of how much norepinephrine a pump was commanded to deliver, alongside continuously measured arterial pressure, do the dose changes come at a rhythm set by how often the unit's policy says a nurse should look and chart (once an hour, once every two hours) rather than at any rhythm belonging to the patient's circulation? Three separable claims follow. (a) A measurement claim: the dose series contains more power in a narrow band at the charting frequency than a surrogate/control-band baseline, and dose and pressure are coherent there with a phase relation consistent with a closed negative-feedback loop rather than coincidence. (b) An identification claim: the frequency of that excess moves when a unit's documented cadence moves for reasons unrelated to how sick the patients are. (c) A prognostic claim: bigger swings at that frequency predict worse organ dysfunction, net of average dose, severity and sepsis phenotype — identified off the cadence contrast, not off comparing patients. Underneath is a system-design claim: that the interval at which a human is instructed to observe is itself a determinant of haemodynamic stability, and therefore a cheap, device-free lever. Note what the question is not: it is not a claim about a nonlinear attractor (Gate 1 already surrendered that), and it is not a claim about a person's skill — the controller under examination is a policy-constrained human-plus-protocol.

## Verdict: **Provisional**

## Attacks

### 1. Triviality — **Resolved**

**Objection.** Limb (a) looks like an afternoon's work with a predictable answer. Dose changes are entered when a nurse is at the bedside; charting happens when a nurse is at the bedside; so a comb of spectral power at 1/h in the dose series is close to entailed by the actuation schedule and is bookkeeping, not dynamics. The data-resolution half is a one-page Nyquist argument: at hourly-charted dose data the 1/h signal sits exactly at the sampling frequency and aliases to DC, so it is unobservable — that can be written down without any study. And controller gain/lag for a human titrator is a textbook describing-function computation once you have the series.

**Best response.** The entailment fails on evidence. Hunter/Manias/Considine's naturalistic observation of noradrenaline management (two Melbourne adult ICUs, 14 nurse-patient pairs, 25 h of paired BP and dose data) reports that blood pressure 'fluctuated constantly' while being documented only hourly — i.e. nurses observe continuously and document discretely, so whether their ACTIONS lock to the documentation cadence is an open empirical fact, not a scheduling tautology (https://ivteam.com/?p=253609). The same study is the ceiling of what exists: it classifies dose changes as escalation/weaning/titration and reports step sizes, and reports no inter-event timing, no periodicity, no spectrum and no outcomes. The nearest analogue in a different manual titration loop is the same shape: van der Eijk et al., Acta Paediatrica 2012;101:E97-E104, spent 726 h of continuous FiO2/SpO2 recording with video on 12 infants and reported counts (851 increases, 1309 decreases) and step sizes — no timing, interval, periodicity or frequency-domain analysis at all. Limbs (b) and (c) are not a page under any reading: (b) needs two or more institutions with documented, patient-state-exogenous cadence changes, and (c) needs an outcome model identified off that contrast. Resolved.

### 2. Disguise — **Resolved**

**Objection.** This is a known question wearing new clothes, in at least four vocabularies. (i) Human intermittent control (Gawthrop/Loram/Lakie): intermittent sampling by a human controller provably produces characteristic cycling — the mechanism is already established, so the ICU version is an instance, not a question. (ii) Periodic-review inventory control / bullwhip: oscillation generated by discrete review intervals plus lag is the founding result of that field, and it has already been transplanted into hospitals. (iii) Clinical folk vocabulary: 'chasing the INR' in anticoagulation and 'insulin stacking' in glycaemic management are exactly overcorrection under lagged, discretely sampled feedback, named and taught. (iv) Neonatal automated-oxygen control has been running manual-versus-automated FiO2 titration comparisons for over a decade — the same loop, the same question, a different drug.

**Best response.** Each vocabulary has the mechanism and none has the measurement in this loop. (i) I opened Gawthrop/Loram, 'Intermittent Control in Man and Machine' (ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1407.3543): its named application domains are quiet standing, reaching in a force field, manual tracking, networked control, robotics and the psychological refractory period; minimum intermittent interval 0.5 s, delays 0.1-0.4 s, event delays 90-150 ms. It names no drug dosing, no medication titration, no clinical care, and no timescale slower than about one second. The candidate's sampling interval is three to four orders of magnitude slower and is a written institutional policy rather than a neural refractory property — which is the whole substantive difference, because a policy is identifiable (it is documented and occasionally changed) and modifiable. (ii) Bullwhip in healthcare is operationalised in material flows (Inderscience 'Evidence of bullwhip effect in healthcare sector'; IRMA chapter on bullwhip prevalence in hospitals); nothing transfers it to a physiological controlled variable. (iii) The INR and insulin-stacking corpora returned patient-education and QI material (Joslin, diabetesnet, BMJ Open Quality) and monitoring-frequency-versus-time-in-range work (Chalmers thesis on INR monitoring frequency); there is no frequency-domain operationalisation, no named predicted frequency, no amplitude exposure. (iv) The automated-oxygen corpus is the closest and I probed it twice: it is live and large (NCT06622161, NCT05508308, AreOS NCT04223258, SPOC NCT03785899), and it compares arms on time-in-target-range and extremes of saturation, never on identification of the manual arm as a controller — which independently reproduces the asymmetry the record's Gate 2 query 5 found for vasopressors. Its single best observational study (van der Eijk 2012) quantifies counts and step sizes and attributes large adjustments to procedures, with no timing or spectral analysis. Resolved, with a stated residual: I read the automated-oxygen RCTs at registration/abstract level only, and that corpus remains the likeliest place for an unwitting partial prior.

### 3. Combinatorial novelty — **Resolved**

**Objection.** Control theory x ICU charting logs, with the interface supplied by analogy rather than by a problem. The engineering side already knows how to identify an operator; the clinical side already has the logs; nothing hard happens where they meet, and 'pilot-induced oscillation' is decoration.

**Best response.** There is a specific technical obstruction at the interface, and the record's design is shaped by it. In the source field the operator is identified by INJECTING a reference signal — Gawthrop/Loram's cases are imposed tracking and balance tasks with experimenter-controlled perturbation. Clinical logs offer no injected reference, and direct spectral identification inside a closed loop cannot separate the controller's transfer function from the plant's without exogenous excitation, which is why limb (a) alone cannot deliver gain and lag and why the record has to promote the cadence contrast (limb b) from a nice-to-have into the identification strategy. That is a genuine interface problem, and the answer to it — use an institutional policy as the instrument — is not available in either parent field: aerospace has no institutional sampling policy to exploit, and critical care has no identification machinery to exploit it with. Second, the confound structure is new to the source field and evidenced: van der Eijk 2012 found 126 of 136 large adjustments and 111 of 171 deep desaturations associated with medical or nursing procedures, so in the clinical loop the dominant competing generator of dose change is a scheduled care activity, a disturbance class with no analogue in a tracking task. Resolved.

### 4. Presupposition failure — **Partial**

**Objection.** Two defects, the second worse than the first. (A) The pre-specified primary test cannot work as written. V2 names 'a pre-specified surrogate noise floor (phase-randomised and block-shuffled matched segments)' as the baseline against which EXCESS BAND-LIMITED POWER is judged. A phase-randomised surrogate preserves the power spectrum by construction, so its power in the cadence band equals the data's exactly; the test has literally zero power against that null. This is not a quibble about a detail — the surrogate floor is the headline fix the Gate 1 reformulation was credited with, and it is inert for the statistic it is attached to. (B) The named control band does not separate the exposure from its chief confound, because the confound is collinear with it by design. Nursing care in an ICU is bundled on the same hourly or two-hourly rhythm as charting — turning, suctioning, oral care, hygiene, line checks occur at the care round, not at an independent frequency. So procedure-driven dose change and cadence-driven controller cycling share a frequency signature, and P4's remedy (enter procedures as covariates) fails when the covariate and exposure are at the same frequency. Limb (b) does not rescue this: a unit that changes its documented charting interval changes its care bundle rhythm with it, so the instrument moves both the hypothesised cause and the confound together.

**Best response.** On (A), the defect is real and I verified it at source. tseries::surrogate, implementing Theiler et al. (1992) algorithms 1-2, states: 'surrogate data with the same spectrum as x is computed by randomizing the phases of the Fourier coefficients' (https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/tseries/html/surrogate.html), and the surrogate-methods literature confirms the construction leaves 'the modulus |G(k)| of the Fourier transform ... untouched' (https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1509.00223). But the damage is bounded and repairable: phase randomisation IS the correct null for the OTHER half of the primary test, the dose-MAP coherence and phase criterion, since it preserves each marginal spectrum while destroying the cross-phase relation; and the band-power limb retains two components that do have power, the block-shuffled surrogate (valid if the block length is short relative to the hypothesised period — unspecified in V2, which is the remaining specification gap) and the pre-specified non-cadence control band. So the composite test is not powerless, but the record as written mis-assigns one of its two named nulls and does not state the block length on which the other depends. On (B) the partial separation is dynamical rather than statistical: a procedure-driven dose change is disturbance rejection, in which pressure moves first and dose follows, whereas controller cycling has dose leading pressure at the cadence frequency — so the phase sign in the cross-spectrum discriminates the two generators even at identical frequency, and this is precisely what the coherence/phase requirement buys. Prospective designs can also censor on observed procedure times, as van der Eijk did with video. In retrospective pump-log data, however, procedure timestamps are sparse and unreliable, so (B) is mitigated in principle and unresolved in the design actually specified. Partial: the objections are evidenced, the responses are concrete but incomplete, and the pre-specification needs repair before either branch could be believed.

### 5. Vagueness — **Partial**

**Objection.** A three-limb composite question with a pre-blessed middle outcome cannot fail. The record states that PARTIAL — peak present but benign — would be 'the most instructive outcome', which means all three of present-and-harmful, present-and-benign, and absent are written up as informative. P3 (is oscillation harmful) is explicitly contested, so a null on limb (c) settles nothing about the motivating claim. And the exposure has free parameters a motivated analyst can move: band width, segment length, detrending, which harmonic counts, what 'loop-consistent phase' means numerically.

**Best response.** The pre-specification is genuine where it exists and I can point to it: numeric centre frequencies (2.8e-4 Hz and 1.4e-4 Hz), a sampling-resolution inclusion criterion at 4x the hypothesised frequency, a named control band, named endpoint components (delta SOFA renal/hepatic/cardiovascular, new RRT), a named covariate set, and a requirement of pre-registration for either direction to count. Limb (a) alone therefore has a sharp binary reading against a block-shuffle floor, and the record says in terms that a clean negative at both institutions settles the question. What I cannot evidence is that a negative would be READ as a negative: band width and 'loop-consistent phase' are named as categories but not as numbers, segment length and detrending are unspecified while both directly determine whether a 1/h peak is resolvable or removed, and the record's own fecundity section offers five further loops to look in if this one is quiet — which is the standard route by which a negative becomes a scoping exercise. Partial.

### 6. Significance inflation — **Partial**

**Objection.** The audience that could act will not, and the audience that would care cannot act. The record concedes both: audience (ii), nursing leadership setting cadence and ratios, 'acts on staffing economics', and audience (i), the closed-loop device groups, 'would likely proceed with device development regardless'. Audiences (iii) and (iv) are researchers who would cite it, not decide anything. Strip that out and the consequence is a methods demonstration.

**Best response.** Two consumers survive with evidence. First, the device field is live right now and is making exactly the comparative claim this would supply a comparator for: I found current manual-versus-automated titration trials still recruiting in the oxygen loop (NCT06622161, NCT05508308, NCT04223258, NCT03785899) alongside the vasopressor trials the record names, and every one compares on time-in-target rather than on loop behaviour — so a mechanistic characterisation of the manual arm has an immediate, dated consumer, including regulators assessing superiority claims. Second, the prognostic dose-variability literature (the record's dose-trajectory-versus-AKI neighbour) would have to reinterpret its exposure as partly controller-generated rather than patient-generated, which changes a causal reading, not just a citation. Against that: I found no evidence that any unit has ever changed assessment cadence on haemodynamic grounds, and none that cadence is treated as a clinical rather than a workload parameter — so the headline 'device-free modifiable lever' remains an assertion. The record's own honest discount is to its credit but does not repair it. Partial.

### 7. Reason for silence — **Resolved**

**Objection.** The three stated mechanisms look reverse-engineered, and the sharpest of them — 'analytic habit as a filter', the band gets detrended away — is 'no one thought of it' in methodological dress, since the same analysts choose their detrending and could choose otherwise.

**Best response.** One mechanism is concrete, checkable and decisive, and it is the aliasing one. The field's standard public preprocessing pipeline for exactly this data destroys the signal before anyone could see it: MIMIC-Extract (arXiv 1907.08322) states 'we aggregate the observations from each ICU stay's time-series into hourly buckets', and for interventions it 'extracts hourly binary indicators of when (if ever) common treatments were provided', with vasopressors in that same hourly binary format. At hourly sampling the hypothesised 1/h component sits at the sampling frequency itself and aliases to DC; and an hourly binary indicator discards dose magnitude altogether, so amplitude is not merely aliased but absent. The large downstream corpus built on that substrate — the offline-RL sepsis/vasopressor-policy literature my searches returned — therefore operates on representations in which this question is unaskable, which is a structural filter, not an oversight. The instrument-timing mechanism is also evidenced by what the measurement used to cost: van der Eijk needed three days of continuous recording plus bedside video on 12 infants to obtain adjustment counts alone, in 2012. And the boundary mechanism is evidenced by Gawthrop/Loram naming no clinical application and the automated-titration trials never characterising the manual arm. Resolved.

### 8. Dissolution — **Partial**

**Objection.** The headline concept has already partly dissolved, inside the record itself. V1 asked whether the loop exhibits limit-cycle oscillation; Gate 1 withdrew that because a nonlinear autonomous attractor cannot be established from observational series, leaving 'excess band-limited power with loop-consistent phase'. One more turn of the same handle dissolves the rest: band power at the cadence frequency does not distinguish closed-loop cycling from the hourly discretisation of ANY dose trajectory, since a monotone staircase (steady weaning in hourly steps) carries power at 1/h and its harmonics too. What remains is then 'nurses act at the times they are at the bedside, and dose trajectories are quantised by that schedule' — a fact about work organisation. 'Clinician-induced oscillation' is not a natural kind; it is a metaphor doing the motivating work after the measurable content has been handed back.

**Best response.** The staircase objection is answered by the part of the specification that survives attack 4: a stepped monotone ramp and a cycling loop differ in the dose-MAP cross-spectrum, not in dose-series band power — the ramp has dose and pressure moving monotonically together with no 180-degree relation at the cadence frequency, while sustained cycling does. So the coherence-and-phase criterion, not the band-power statistic, is what carries the dynamical claim, and it does not reduce to a scheduling fact. What I cannot defend is the rhetorical frame: after Gate 1's retreat, the question no longer asks whether bedside titration is a pilot-induced oscillation, and the aviation analogy that supplies its motivation and its claimed significance is no longer what is being tested. The measurable residue — does dose-change timing lock to a documented institutional parameter, does the lock move when the parameter moves, does amplitude carry prognostic information — is well defined and does not dissolve. The question therefore survives, smaller and less interesting than its framing. Partial.

## Strongest remaining objection

The pre-specified primary test cannot separate the exposure from the one confound that shares its frequency, and one of its two named nulls is provably inert. Phase-randomised surrogates preserve the power spectrum by construction (tseries::surrogate, after Theiler et al. 1992: 'surrogate data with the same spectrum as x is computed by randomizing the phases of the Fourier coefficients'), so the band-power limb of the primary test has exactly zero power against the first null V2 names — and that surrogate floor is the headline methodological addition the Gate 1 reformulation was credited with. The deeper problem is collinearity: ICU nursing care is bundled onto the same hourly or two-hourly rhythm as charting, so procedure-driven dose change and cadence-driven controller cycling occupy the same band, and van der Eijk 2012 evidences that procedures dominate large manual titration steps (126 of 136 adjustments of 15% or more were procedure-associated). The record's remedy — enter procedure periodicity as a covariate — fails against a covariate at the exposure's own frequency, and limb (b)'s cadence contrast does not rescue it, because a unit that changes its documented assessment interval moves its care-round rhythm with it, shifting the hypothesised cause and the confound together. The one real separation left is the sign of the dose-MAP phase relation (disturbance rejection has pressure leading dose; controller cycling has dose leading pressure), which rests on a phase estimate that P5 and P7 both independently threaten — charting-time bias on one side, line dead-volume and carrier-flow lag on the other. Before this question is worth funding, V2 needs the surrogate assignment corrected, the block length and band width stated numerically, and a demonstration that any candidate pair of institutions has a cadence contrast that is not also a care-bundle contrast.
