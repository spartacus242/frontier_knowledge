Question (canonical, Gate 0 as submitted):
Within a single model species reared across its viable temperature and nutrition range, is the set of morphological landmark events used to define developmental stage well ordered by one latent axis — that is, does environment only rescale developmental time, as staging series and their temperature-correction formulas assume — or do landmark pairs reverse order, and how much stage-matching error does the measured departure from unidimensionality imply for stage-matched comparative data?

Question (Gate 1 reformulation, one permitted, for well-posedness only; both versions recorded):
For one model species and one pre-specified landmark set L (|L| >= 10) scored per individual across a temperature-by-nutrition grid inside the viable range, estimate three separate quantities under pre-fixed criteria: (E1) the fraction of landmark pairs in L whose temporal order differs between at least two environmental cells, tested against a null built from measured inter- and within-scorer scoring error; (E2) whether a one-factor latent model over the per-individual landmark-time vectors is preferred to a >=2-factor alternative by a fit criterion fixed in advance; and (E3) the share of variance in a named downstream stage-matched comparison (e.g. a single-embryo transcriptomic contrast) attributable to stage-matching error implied by E1-E2.
Reason for the reformulation: the submitted version fuses two estimands from different statistical frameworks ("well ordered by one latent axis" reads both as a strict order/Guttman claim and as a factor-count claim) into one interrogative, so a single answer could not be scored against it. The reformulation separates them. The reformulation does not rescue novelty: see Gate 2.

Presuppositions (status after Gate 2):
1. A landmark set large enough to test order (>=10 scoreable events) can be scored per individual. ESTABLISHED, and now established by demonstration: 36 landmarks in D. melanogaster and 9 refined for quantitative per-embryo analysis (Kuntz & Eisen 2014/2015); ~24 individually imaged zebrafish per temperature followed hourly (Miller et al. 2025).
2. Landmark scoring error is small relative to inter-landmark intervals. CONTESTED and still unmet by the priors: Kuntz & Eisen state manual scoring error is small relative to individual variation but give no inter-scorer or within-scorer reliability estimate and no scoring-error null; Kuntz & Eisen 2015 reports order inversions with no reversal-rate statistic against such a null. This is the one presupposition the priors leave genuinely open.
3. The environment can be varied without pathology, so reversals are developmental rather than teratological. CONTESTED; partly addressed in the priors by staying inside documented viable ranges (17.5-32.5 C; 10-29% O2), and Kuntz & Eisen 2014 note scaling itself fails at temperature extremes, which is exactly the boundary this presupposition needs pre-specified.
4. 'Stage' is intended as an ordinal scale over events, not a convenience label. CONTESTED; the defence stands (stage is used as a matching and covariate variable), and is strengthened by Kuntz & Eisen 2014 drawing the practical inference explicitly — that uniform scaling licenses scaling collection and aging times to stage-match across conditions.
5. Stage-matching error propagates measurably into at least one downstream analysis. CONTESTED and NOT demonstrated by any source opened. No prior found that propagates staging error into a stage-matched transcriptomic or atlas comparison.

What would count as an answer:
Per-individual landmark event sequences across a temperature-by-nutrition grid in one species, yielding (i) the rate and magnitude of landmark-pair order reversals against a scoring-error null, (ii) a one-factor latent-scale fit compared against a >=2-factor alternative under a fit criterion fixed in advance, and (iii) the variance stage-matching error contributes to a named downstream stage-matched comparison. A clean one-factor fit with reversal rates at the scoring-error floor would answer in favour of current practice and justify uniform-rate temperature-correction formulas on evidence rather than convention.

Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, with sources), each with unit of analysis / endpoint / reference / estimand:
PRIOR 1 — Kuntz SG, Eisen MB, "Drosophila embryogenesis scales uniformly across temperature in developmentally diverse species". arXiv:1306.5297 (PLoS Genetics 2014). READ IN FULL at https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1306.5297
  UNIT OF ANALYSIS: the individual time-lapse-imaged embryo (>1,000 embryos; statistics pooled per species x temperature after median outlier correction).
  ENDPOINT: time of occurrence of each of 36 morphological landmark events (8 refined for quantitative analysis plus a membrane-reaches-yolk zero), and each landmark's position after rescaling each embryo between end-of-cellularization and trachea-fill.
  REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: the same species reared at each of 7 temperatures, 17.5-32.5 C in 2.5 C steps (and, separately, 10 other Drosophila species).
  ESTIMAND: whether the normalized relative timing of embryonic landmark events is invariant to rearing temperature — i.e. whether the environment only rescales developmental time — together with whether landmark ORDER is preserved.
  FINDING: "the relative timing of events from cellularization through hatching is constant across temperatures"; "embryogenesis scales uniformly across a two-fold range of total time"; all 36 landmarks occurred in the same temporal order in every species; and "at extreme temperatures, especially high ones, things no longer scale uniformly". The Discussion draws the stage-matching consequence explicitly — uniform scaling licenses scaling collection and aging times to match a chosen stage range across conditions.
PRIOR 2 — Kuntz SG, Eisen MB, "Oxygen changes drive non-uniform scaling in Drosophila melanogaster embryogenesis". F1000Research 2015, 4:1102, doi 10.12688/f1000research.7221.1. READ IN FULL at https://f1000research.com/articles/4-1102
  UNIT OF ANALYSIS: the individual embryo (>800 embryos, 18 conditions; each animal plotted as its own point).
  ENDPOINT: hand-scored time of each of 9 morphological landmark events (pole-bud, membrane-reaches-yolk, pole-cell invagination, amnioproctodeal invagination, amnioserosa exposure, clypeolabrum retraction, clypeolabrum/ventral-lobes even, heart-shaped midgut, trachea fill).
  REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: the SAME single species at 6 ambient oxygen levels (29/25/21/17/14/10% O2) crossed with 3 temperatures (17.5/22.5/27.5 C) — an environmental grid inside the viable range.
  ESTIMAND: whether an environmental change rescales developmental time uniformly across the landmark set, or whether landmark-specific rate changes alter relative timing and order.
  FINDING: non-uniform. "changes in oxygen levels drive developmental heterochrony" and — the decisive line — "the order of several developmental landmarks is inverted at different oxygen levels", with the reversing pair named (cephalic-lobe evenness vs heart-shaped midgut) and the inversion point itself mapped as a function of temperature (~29% O2 at 27.5 C, ~19% at 17.5 C). No reversal-rate statistic, no scoring-error null, no dimensionality test.

Significance — consequence:
If the answer were "landmark order reverses materially and stage is >1-dimensional": every stage-matched design — comparative transcriptomics, cell atlases, ecotoxicology endpoints read at a nominal stage (e.g. OECD TG 236-style zebrafish assays), teratology scoring — carries an unmodelled covariate error whose magnitude is currently unstated, and 'stage' would have to be reported as a vector or a model-based estimate rather than a scalar. If the answer were "one clean factor, reversals at the scoring-error floor": uniform-rate correction formulas gain an evidential warrant they currently lack by convention. Both branches change something, so the question as posed is consequential. But the consequence is substantially discharged: Priors 1 and 2 already establish that the answer is environment-dependent (uniform under temperature in Drosophila within 17.5-27.5 C, non-uniform with order inversion under oxygen), and Miller et al. 2025 already establish that the zebrafish correction formula misestimates rate with stage-dependent residuals. The decision-relevant remainder is the magnitude of downstream error, not the existence of the phenomenon.
Significance — audience:
Real and identifiable: staging-series maintainers and users (ZFIN, FlyBase, Carnegie/Kyoto human-embryo collections), single-embryo and cell-atlas groups that stage-match before contrasting, and regulatory ecotoxicology labs whose endpoints are stage-anchored and whose incubation temperature is specified precisely because rate scaling is assumed. Evidence they would act: Kuntz & Eisen 2014 wrote the stage-matching recommendation into their Discussion; Miller et al. 2025 drew an animal-welfare and assay-classification conclusion ("eleutheroembryos incubated at 26 C should not be classified as 'independent feeders' at 120 hpf") directly from a staging-rate finding. So the audience demonstrably acts on results of this kind — which is also why the question did not stay unasked.
