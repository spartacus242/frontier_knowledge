## Restatement (my own words)

If you pretrain an LLM in a block-scaled 4-bit or 8-bit float format (MXFP8, MXFP4, NVFP4), where 16–32 neighbouring values share one scale fixed by the block's largest value, is the resulting loss penalty relative to an otherwise identical BF16 run spread evenly over the training data, or does it grow as the predicted token (secondarily: entity, language) gets rarer in the training corpus? Concretely: bin held-out tokens by log training-corpus frequency, compute the per-bin loss gap to the BF16 control, fit a line; (i) is the slope non-zero beyond seed-to-seed noise, and (ii) does the slope line up with how often small gradient values inside those same strata get rounded to exactly zero by the shared block scale?

## Strongest killing point (one)

Secondary: the mechanism limb names an instrument (per-stratum gradient flush-to-zero) that is undefined in the named recipe's only cross-token path, because NVFP4 applies a 16×16 Hadamard transform to Wgrad inputs and stochastic rounding to gradients.
