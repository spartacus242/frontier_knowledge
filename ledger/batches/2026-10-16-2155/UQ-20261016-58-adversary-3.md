## Restatement in my own words

Most protostome animals carry a true but "hidden" scission in the 28S rRNA backbone: the two halves stay base-paired until denatured, after which they migrate near 18S, so the 28S peak that mammalian-trained integrity algorithms look for is missing. The candidate asks three distinct empirical things, not one:

- (a) In real submission records that pair an integrity score with an accept / reject / re-extract decision, do break-bearing taxa score lower and get rejected or re-extracted more often than break-free taxa, after preservation route, tissue, protocol, input mass and body size are held fixed or adjusted, with denominators stated?
- (b) In a controlled graded-degradation series on matched RNA from one break-bearing and one break-free species, with true degradation measured by something that does not use the 28S peak (DV200, spike-in fragment length, library 3' bias), what is the numeric score-for-score equivalence — i.e. what does a "RIN >= 7" gate have to become in a break-bearing taxon?
- (c) Exploratory: after conditioning on research effort and collection difficulty, does break status predict whether a taxon has any transcriptome at all?

So: not "does the break exist" (settled, published), not "should someone build a break-aware metric" (asked in print, routed to open problems), but "what did the uncorrected metric already cost, in rates and in a number."

## Fetches (4; access routing: publisher/primary first, PMC never attempted)

- https://peerj.com/articles/20693 (Casanova Gomez 2026, the newest on-point paper) — READ. Confirms the record's claimed absences, which I checked because this ledger has been burned by an unread on-point paper: no numeric RIN/RQN per species or profile class, no graded degradation series, no rejection or re-extraction rates, no proposed metric or threshold correction, no mapping between break-bearing and break-free scores, no transcriptome-availability analysis. It asserts the consequence a fourth time ("conventional RNA Integrity Number (RIN) scores tend to underestimate RNA quality in taxa with a hidden break"; "confirms that RIN-based Quality Control (QC) is unreliable when a hidden break collapses the 28S band") and recommends "rRNA-aware quality control". Profiles are classified single- vs multi-peak with continuous 28Sa/28Sb sizing, i.e. the exposure really is graded, as the record says.
- https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10088586/1/s41598-019-55573-1.pdf (Natsidis et al. 2019, published version) — READ. Verified verbatim: training set "obtained from species without a hidden break"; "will be consistently underestimated in the great majority of animal species with a break"; "an alternative to the standard RIN that takes into account the protostomian hidden break would be a valuable development"; and the asserted mechanism "The absence of two distinct rRNA peaks will give the impression of a degraded sample with a low RIN". No RIN value is computed, simulated or modelled anywhere; the Fig. 1 electropherograms are a colleague's, not an experiment; no QC or data-availability outcome. Prevalence: break in 17 of 19 testable protostome phyla, concluded homologous; 4 of 331 non-animal eukaryotes; absent in 36 non-bilaterians; deuterostomes free apart from non-homologous rodent/cyprinid cases. Break status is called by eye ("an obvious drop in the read coverage between the two conserved flanking 20-mers"), with calls left uncertain for Cnidaria, tardigrades and dicyemids, and only for species with at least 1 Gb of existing RNA-Seq data.
- https://www.protocols.io/view/sanger-tree-of-life-rna-extraction-manual-trizol-cy3kxykw.html — READ. The flagship frame named in the question publishes no numeric integrity gate: the only quality language is "Verify the RNA concentration and quality of the samples with the required QC", with no instrument, metric or threshold named.
