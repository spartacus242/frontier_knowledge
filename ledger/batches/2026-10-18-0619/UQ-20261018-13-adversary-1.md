## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take the per-step logged loss of a pretraining run, strip out the obvious downward learning trend with a pre-declared fit, and treat what is left as a noisy time series the way a clock or gyro lab would: compute how the variance of window-averages shrinks (or stops shrinking) as the window grows (overlapping Allan deviation), read off from the log-log slope whether the residual is white, 1/f, or random-walk in each training phase, and, where it is white-plus-random-walk, use a control-theory result that gives the window length that minimizes tracking error. Then ask whether the windows people actually use to eyeball "recipe A beats recipe B" (EMA 0.9/0.99, mean of last k) are shorter or longer than that, and, using seed-replicated run pairs as ground truth, how often each window rule gets the A-vs-B call wrong. The candidate's own escape hatches: if residuals are white everywhere, the mean of the last k steps is already optimal and the question dissolves into "a measured noise floor"; if 1/f dominates, no optimal window exists and the answer is "replicate, do not smooth".

## Pages opened (4 of 4)

1. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02400 — "An Empirical Study on Noisy Data and LLM Pretraining Loss Divergence" (Zhang, Garg, Foerster, Chatterji, Malik, Lewis). The record's unopened residual (a). CLEARED: noise is injected into the DATA ("By injecting controlled synthetic uniformly random noise into otherwise clean datasets"); the loss curve is the outcome, and no autocorrelation / spectrum / averaging-window analysis of the logged loss is performed. This removes one of the record's three stated blockers to High novelty.
2. https://morganredding.substack.com/p/uniform-smoothing-is-better-than — practitioner post. Argues uniform windows over EMA for training metrics; says EMA on a trending curve is "systematically biased"; recommends overlaying 95% CIs on the smoothed curve to judge whether "differences between runs exceed noise"; justifies uniform weights by "the unweighted sample mean is the best unbiased estimator of the true mean"; notes "Exponential moving averages only really win when the statistical noise from your batch size is small". It does NOT write a signal-plus-noise model, identify a noise type, derive or recommend a window length, or give a formal test; the window is tuned "until the confidence interval is narrow enough". This is the lay question (alt. formulation 1) asked and answered under an implicit i.i.d.-noise assumption.
3. https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10379088 — Haeri, Soleimani & Jerath, "Optimal Moving Average Estimation of Noisy Random Walks using Allan Variance-informed Window Length", 2022 ACC. Fills the record's blank author list. Signal model is EXACTLY and ONLY x_k = x_{k-1} + w_k, w_k ~ N(0, sigma_w^2); y_k = x_k + v_k, v_k ~ N(0, sigma_v^2), independent. Closed-form window m_c = floor(sqrt(3 sigma_v^2 / sigma_w^2) + 1/2) (the radical is garbled in the fetched text; this reading is approximate and is my transcription, not a verified quote). The paper does not treat flicker, bias instability, detrending or deterministic trends; it states only that the vector extension works "as long as the white noise processes are uncorrelated across all dimensions". Its introduction cites trend forecasting, econometrics, communications, cardiac signals, finance, hydrology as domains — so the method's own authors do not regard it as sensor-bound.
4. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.02312 — "Training trajectories, mini-batch losses and the curious role of the learning rate". Models batch noise as additive Gaussian in weight space; shows weight averaging over n points lowers effective LR "by approximately a factor of n"; no autocorrelation, spectrum, or variance-vs-window analysis of the measured loss; no run-comparison rule. Does not pre-empt the candidate.

Promotion conditions: (1) restate limb (ii) so tau* is the optimal window for tracking a single run's level and limb (iii) is the actual decision-rule test, dropping "proved optimal window for deciding two runs differ"; (2) add the Kalman/local-level smoother (Muth/Harvey lineage) as a comparator and ancestor of N3; (3) open MaP full text and run the census query the record never ran; (4) address the detrending-span artefact with a pre-declared check (e.g. compare slopes at tau << span only, or use a method that does not require detrending).

Single strongest killing point: the imported theorem (Haeri, Soleimani & Jerath, ACC 2022, opened) assumes exactly x_k = x_{k-1} + w_k, y_k = x_k + v_k and optimizes the error in estimating x_k, i.e. it treats the random walk as the signal to track in ONE series; the candidate's headline use is an A-vs-B decision in which run-specific wandering is noise, so "Allan-variance-informed optimal averaging window for deciding that two runs differ" is not something N3 proves, and the record's limb (ii) rests on a mis-transplanted estimand. Not fatal because limb (iii) is empirical and estimand-agnostic, but it is the defect that most needs fixing before any status above Provisional.
