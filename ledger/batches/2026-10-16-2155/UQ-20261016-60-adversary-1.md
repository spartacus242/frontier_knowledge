## Restatement (my own words)

Take one species. Score a fixed list of at least ten visible developmental events on each individual animal, rearing the animals across a grid of temperatures and food levels that all lie inside the range where development is normal. Then ask three things. (1) Does changing the environment merely stretch or compress a single developmental clock, so every event still happens in the same order at the same fraction of total time, or do some pairs of events trade places? (2) Is a single "how far along" number enough to describe each animal's event-time vector, or do you need two or more? (3) When people match samples by nominal stage before comparing them (e.g. single-embryo RNA-seq), how much of the difference they see is really staging error produced by (1)-(2)?

The record's two load-bearing priors (Kuntz & Eisen 2014 and 2015) answer (1) within Drosophila for temperature (uniform, order preserved) and oxygen (non-uniform, a named pair inverts). The record left (2), the scoring-error null for (1), and (3) as open. My Disguise sweep targeted exactly those three residues in vocabularies the record did not use.
