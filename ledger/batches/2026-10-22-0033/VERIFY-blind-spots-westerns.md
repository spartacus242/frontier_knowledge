### (d) Concentration in ug/mL or molar units, or a computed DOSE — ABSENT. The source records REPORTING, not amount.

The article nowhere reports, measures or computes an antibody concentration in µg/mL, mg/mL, nM or any molar unit, and nowhere computes an antibody dose. I searched the served text for "concentration", "µg", "ug/ml", "mg/ml", "nM" and "dose".

The single "concentration" sentence concerns the **blocking reagent**, not an antibody — Results, "Membrane blocking protocol":

> "Almost all papers that reported the type of blocking reagent also stated the concentration of the blocking reagent."

What the source does instead: it **MEASURES the prevalence of reporting** a dilution — a per-paper categorical item ("reported for all / some / none"), as the Methods criterion states:

> "Do the authors report the antibody company or supplier, catalog number, lot number, RRID, dilution," + "duration, and temperature of incubation for primary and secondary antibodies?"

It never converts a reported fold-dilution (e.g. 1:1,000) into a concentration, and it never possesses the stock concentration that such a conversion would require. On the taxonomy asked for: with respect to antibody concentration or dose the paper neither DEFINES, BOUNDS, PREDICTS, ANNOUNCES nor MEASURES it — it is **silent on the quantity** while measuring something adjacent (whether the dilution ratio was disclosed at all).

Which is it, granularity gap or absence? **Both, at two different grains, and the distinction matters:**
- As to the **dilution value**: a GRANULARITY gap. The paper classifies disclosure (all / some / none) and does not tabulate the values that were disclosed.
- As to **concentration in mass or molar units, and to dose**: an **ABSENCE**, not a granularity gap. The quantity is not a finer cut of anything the paper collected; the paper's instrument (abstraction of reporting items from published text) cannot produce it.

### (f) Unit of analysis and endpoint, in the authors' own words

**Unit of analysis: the published full-length paper.** "In total, 551 papers included western blots and were eligible for abstraction"; "We studied western blot image display practices in a sample of 551 full-length papers".

Items are yes/no or all-some-none questions, e.g. "Cropping: Are the blots in the paper cropped?" and "Membrane blocking protocol: Do the authors report details concerning the blocking reagent, concentration, and duration?"

**Comparator: none inferential.** Neurosciences (n = 151) vs cell biology (n = 400) are descriptive strata reported side by side; the two fields are not contrasted with a test statistic in anything I read, and the primary-vs-secondary wording ("more frequently", "more often") is a descriptive comparison of proportions.

**Source type:** a **primary meta-research study** (cross-sectional, observational, of the published literature) that carries prescriptive recommendations. It is labelled "Meta-Research Article" and is not a review, protocol, guideline, editorial or column. ## REGIME

The source establishes its proportions in this regime, and only this one:

- **Population:** full-length research papers **containing western blot images**, published in journals in the **top 25% by impact** in two fields — **neurosciences (n = 151)** and **cell biology (n = 400)** — with earliest publication date "between July 1 to 31, 2021".
- **Unit of analysis:** one published paper.
- **Instrument:** human abstraction of the paper's own figures and methods text, dual-abstracted, consistency-checked on a random 10%.
- **Measurement:** presence / completeness category of a named reporting item, per paper.
- **Threshold / categories:** reported for **all** antibodies vs **some** vs **none** — the 25% and 52% are the "**all** antibodies' dilution omitted" category, which is why the sentence reads "The dilution of all antibodies was omitted".
- **Endpoint:** a percentage of papers.

Claims that sit **inside** this regime: that 551 papers with westerns from July 2021 in top-quartile neuroscience and cell biology journals were examined; that 25% / 52% of those papers (neurosciences / cell biology) omitted the dilution of all primary antibodies; that catalog numbers and lot numbers are frequently missing.

Claims that sit **outside** it, on which this source is **silent, not supportive**:

- Any statement about antibody **amount, concentration or dose** actually used at the bench. The instrument reads text; it never touches a reagent.
- Any statement that a reported dilution is **wrong, excessive or inadequate**. The paper measures disclosure, never appropriateness.
- Any statement about fields other than neurosciences and cell biology, journals outside the top quartile, or periods other than July 2021.
- Any statement about papers **without** western blot images — those were screened out.
- Any arithmetic conversion of the percentages into **counts of papers** (e.g. "38 neuroscience papers"): that conversion is the converter's arithmetic. The source prints percentages at this sentence and prints no count, and prints no denominator at this sentence either.

## DEFECTS FOUND

1. **The earlier agent's "authorship not served at any route" is wrong, and wrong at a route the ledger itself names.** The complete 17-author byline is served on the publisher's typeset PDF and all but a UI-collapsed middle is served on the HTML article page. This is COULD-NOT-READ reported where the correct finding is FULLY READ. If any downstream record rejected or discounted this source on the strength of unestablished authorship, that reasoning rests on an error.
2. **The denominator in sub-claim (c) is supplied, not quoted.** "with denominators of about 151 and 400" attaches to the 25%/52% figures a base that the source does not print at those figures, and that the figure's own caption (Fig 4) does not print either. 151 and 400 are printed elsewhere as the field sizes of the abstraction sample. The inference is plausible — the Methods describe no reduced sample for the methods protocol — but it remains the reader's inference. **Report the percentages as printed and say the denominator is unstated at the point of use.**
3. **Cut-grammar risk on the 25%/52% sentence.** The sentence's grammatical subject is a **comparison between secondary and primary** antibodies: "omitted more frequently for secondary (58%; 72%) than primary (25%; 52%)". Lifting the primary half alone does not falsify the numbers — they are correctly attached to primary antibodies — but it discards the sentence's actual point, which is that secondary antibodies are reported **worse** (58% / 72%). A ledger entry that cites 25%/52% as the headline omission figure understates the paper's own finding by roughly a factor of two, because the worse number is the one the sentence is built around. **Carry all four figures or say explicitly that primary is the better-reported of the two.**
4. **PUBLISHED IS NOT PERFORMED, confirmed in the direction the task anticipated.** The paper is frequently invoked around antibody *amount*; it measures antibody-information *disclosure*. There is no µg/mL, no molar figure, no titration, no dose anywhere. Any entry that treats this paper as evidence about concentrations or doses has crossed from what it measures to what it is about.
5. **No vendor-datasheet linkage at all.** Catalog-number *presence* is abstracted; no product record is ever retrieved or compared. An entry claiming this paper demonstrates a mismatch between reported dilutions and vendor recommendations would be asserting an analysis the paper did not run.
6. **A numeric tension I could not resolve within the query cap, flagged not corrected.** The consistency check is stated as "a random selection of 10% (n = 62) of papers from the sample". 10% of 551 is 55, not 62; 62 is 10% of ~620. This may mean the 10% was drawn from a larger screened set before the 551-paper eligibility cut, or it may be an inconsistency in the paper. I did not have budget to read the screening flow diagram, and I am **not** silently reconciling it. It does not bear on (b) through (f), but anyone quoting the consistency check should resolve it at the source.
7. **Front matter styling, minor:** the publisher's own citation line abbreviates the journal "PLoS Biol" while the site brands itself "PLOS Biology". Both forms are the publisher's; neither is an error.

Treat any use of this paper outside that regime as unsupported rather than as supported.

**Net effect on the entry:** the source is real, fully open, correctly placed in PLOS Biology September 2022, and its headline count and percentages are as described. Two edits are required — drop the asserted denominators, and stop recording the authorship as unestablished — and one framing point should be added: this paper is evidence about *reporting completeness of dilution ratios*, and is silent on antibody concentration and dose, which is the stronger form of the ledger's gap rather than a weaker one.
