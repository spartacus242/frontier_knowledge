## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Comparative biologists fit allometric and life-history regressions over compiled species-trait tables
(AnAge, Amniote, PanTHERIA/TetrapodTraits, FishBase, TRY, Genoud's BMR compilation), count one row per
species as one independent observation, and correct non-independence for phylogeny only. The question is:
if you follow each row's citation chain back to the publication or measurement that originally produced the
number, (i) how many rows turn out to share an origin with another row in the same regression (and how many
cannot be traced at all), (ii) by how much does that shared-origin clustering cut the regression's effective
sample size, (iii) how far do the published slope and its interval move when the same fit is re-run with
errors clustered by origin, and (iv) how much of that clustering is already inside the phylogenetic
covariance matrix PGLS fits anyway. In short: is the degrees-of-freedom count of comparative biology
inflated by citation chains converging on the same original paper, and does fixing it move the exponents
the field argues about.
