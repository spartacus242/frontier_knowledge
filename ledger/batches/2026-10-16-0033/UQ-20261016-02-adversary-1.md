## Restatement (my words, before engaging the record's framing)

Commercial drug-information databases stamp each drug pair with a severity label, and hospitals wire those labels to decide which pop-ups interrupt a prescriber mid-order. The question asks whether that label actually sorts pairs by how often the combination hurts people. Concretely: pick a pre-specified sample of pairs, over-weighting the pairs the vendors grade differently; in two or more linked claims/EHR databases, estimate the harm caused by taking both drugs over and above taking either one alone (serious harm: ADR-coded or all-cause hospitalisation, or death, within 90 days); then report (a) how the pair-level attributable risks are spread inside each vendor's severity class and how much pair-to-pair variation the class explains, (b) whether the class ranks pairs by that measured harm better than chance, against a pre-committed threshold, and whether the vendors rank them the same way, and (c) if a health system re-sorted pairs by measured harm instead of by label, how much alert volume would fire at the same harm capture. Pairs that cannot be identified are reported as a named stratum rather than dropped.

Two things my restatement makes visible that the record's phrasing softens. First, limb (b) compares a label to a *population incidence*, while the label the vendors publish is a judgement about how bad the event is *if it happens*, crossed with how well documented it is. Second, the whole informativeness of (b) rests on the discordant stratum, which is also the stratum most likely to be too rare to estimate.
