Domain(s): Biology — translation fidelity, protein quality control, proteostasis. Adjacent: aggregation cell biology; biomanufacturing sequence-variant control; molecular evolution of coding sequences.

Question (canonical): Using single-molecule protein sequencing of full-length chains in an unstressed cell, what is the distribution of the number of amino-acid misincorporation errors per individual polypeptide molecule, does it depart from the site-independent (Poisson-binomial) null built from position- and codon-specific rates measured in the same cells — i.e. are errors clustered onto a minority of molecules over a window longer than one tryptic peptide — and what fraction of molecules carry two or more errors?
Reformulation note: this is V2, the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation, and the version scored. V1 (intake) named a single-lambda Poisson null and claimed peptide-level MS cannot see co-occurrence at all; both were corrected from opened sources (per-site rates span orders of magnitude, so the null must be Poisson-binomial; two to four substitutions inside one tryptic peptide are detectable and were tested against the independence product under drug). Full text of both versions in batches/undefined/UQ-20261022-04-record.md.

Alternative formulations:
- Does mistranslation produce many slightly wrong proteins or a few badly wrong ones?
- Is the proteome's per-molecule error load site-independent, and what fraction of molecules carry two or more errors — measured, not inferred from the bulk rate?

Motivating gap: measured mistranslation is an aggregate ratio because the instrument has been bottom-up MS, whose unit of observation is the tryptic peptide. Three sourced parts: the canonical unstressed map (P3) runs no co-occurrence or Poisson-departure test and averages error rates within a protein; the stressed cluster study (P1) states its own clusters are "likely to supersede the length of average tryptic peptides", so the window is shorter than the object; and the per-molecule quantity exists in print only as a prediction under assumed independence (P2).

Presuppositions: (1) single-molecule protein sequencing can read a full-length chain end to end with per-residue reading error far below the ~10^-3 translation signal — CONTESTED, and the one the verdict turns on; (2) errors are frequent enough to count per molecule at achievable throughput (lambda ~0.3 on a 300-mer) — CONTESTED; (3) sampled molecules are full-length mature products — CONTESTED; (4) degradation has not already removed multi-error molecules, and the estimand (errors per synthesised vs per surviving molecule) is left unchosen — CONTESTED; (5) the independent-error null with same-cell position- and codon-specific rates is the right comparator — established, and already a published construct; (6) bulk per-site frequency and per-molecule count are commensurable under (5); (7) "an error" is individuated identically by both instruments — stated in words, not operationalised.

What would count as an answer: a measured per-molecule error-count distribution with CI, tested against a pre-declared Poisson-binomial null from same-sample position- and codon-specific rates; known-sequence standards bounding instrument reading error; a stated detection limit for 2+ errors on one chain; the fraction of molecules with 2+ errors with uncertainty; and a declared estimand with respect to presupposition 4. A null-consistent distribution reported with power to exclude clustering above a declared effect size answers it negatively.
