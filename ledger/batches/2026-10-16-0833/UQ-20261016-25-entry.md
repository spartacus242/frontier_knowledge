Domain(s): Medicine — clinical pharmacology, pharmacoepidemiology, drug regulation (adjacent: oncology pharmacy, health economics)
Question (canonical): Because a molecule's marketed tablet strengths define a lattice of achievable daily doses, what fraction of population-level variation in prescribed dose is imposed by that lattice rather than by patient characteristics, and when an individual strength is discontinued, does the resulting shift in the dose distribution produce measurable changes in clinical outcomes or adverse events?
Alternative formulations:
- Is the dose a patient takes chosen by the prescriber or by the manufacturer's catalogue of strengths, and what happens when the catalogue changes?
- Treating strength discontinuations as natural experiments on dose, how much do a drug's available strengths matter to outcomes?
- Is the marketed strength ladder a clinically consequential design object, or slack that prescribing absorbs without outcome cost?
- Gate 1 reformulation (permitted, both versions retained): with the achievable lattice defined empirically from dispensing data (scored halves, multi-tablet regimens at >=1%) and an unconstrained target dose from a pre-registered drug-specific target-dose model — (a) what share of the variance of dispensed daily dose is attributable to rounding onto the lattice, and what share of patients have no reachable lattice point in their target interval; (b) for strength discontinuations passing an exogeneity screen, what is the ITS/DiD effect on the dose distribution and on pre-registered clinical and adverse-event endpoints against a pre-set equivalence margin.
Motivating gap: Silence mapping — dosing guidance is dense on *how* to dose and apparently silent on why the choosable strengths are what they are; strength-level marketing-status registers looked like an unused natural experiment on dose. Gate 2 finding: **the gap does not hold.** The question was published in the candidate's own field in 1996, and strength-removal-as-exposure has been executed twice since (Ontario, 2018 and 2019).
Presuppositions: (1) dispensed daily doses and dated strength-level marketing histories retrievable at scale — contested as to completeness; (2) LOAD-BEARING: strength discontinuations are frequent and exogenous enough to instrument dose — contested, and Gate 2 worsens it: the two executed examples are payer delistings chosen *because* high doses were thought harmful, so the real supply of events is enriched for the exact endogeneity the design must exclude; (3) prescribers round rather than split/combine — partly relaxed by the empirical lattice, still contested; (4) non-flat exposure-response over the perturbed range; (5) endpoints ascertainable in the same data; (6) NEW and load-bearing: a per-patient unconstrained target dose is well defined — for titrate-to-response drugs the guideline target is an interval or not computable, so limb (a) is identified only relative to a chosen model. Not presupposed: that the lattice is badly chosen, or that regulators should mandate strengths.
What would count as an answer: (i) a pre-registered variance decomposition of dispensed daily dose into lattice-rounding and target-dose components for a stated drug set, with sensitivity across target-dose models, plus the share of patients whose target is unreachable on the empirical lattice; (ii) for exogeneity-screened strength discontinuations, ITS/DiD estimates on the dose distribution and on pre-registered clinical/adverse-event endpoints against an equivalence margin fixed in advance. A heaping plot is a premise, not an answer.
