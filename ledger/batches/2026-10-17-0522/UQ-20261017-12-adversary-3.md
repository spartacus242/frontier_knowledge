## Restatement (my own words)

When a learned video pipeline (a keypoint tracker such as DeepLabCut/SLEAP feeding a supervised or unsupervised behaviour classifier) scores rodent behaviour in an experiment whose manipulation changes how the animal looks or moves (sedation, obesity, injury, a coat-colour/size-divergent line), does the pipeline err at a different rate in treated animals than in controls — and if it does, how much of the reported treatment effect is instrument artefact once the group-specific sensitivity/specificity (or keypoint error) is pushed through standard differential-misclassification correction, measured against the effect's own CI and the assay's usual effect size? The record claims nobody in animal behaviour phenotyping has asked this.
