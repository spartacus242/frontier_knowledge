Domain(s): Biology — population and conservation genomics of long-lived modular organisms (trees, corals, clonal plants); somatic mutation. Neighbouring: cancer genomics (multiregion sampling), survey sampling theory (cluster designs), forest/aquaculture breeding (clone identity).

Question (canonical, Gate 0 as intaken):
In long-lived modular organisms, how much do standard population-genomic estimands — individual heterozygosity, pairwise relatedness/kinship, allele frequencies and the effective population sizes derived from them — change depending on which module of the same individual happened to be sampled, and is that within-individual sampling variance large relative to the between-individual variance those estimands are meant to measure?

Question (Gate 1 reformulation, the version carried forward; one reformulation used, for well-posedness only):
For a pre-specified set of population-genomic estimands (per-individual observed heterozygosity; pairwise kinship coefficient; per-site allele frequency; one LD-based Ne), computed on a crossed design of N individuals x M >= 3 spatially separated modules per individual per taxon, sequenced at matched depth with module provenance recorded, in at least two taxa (one long-lived tree, one colonial or clonal marine organism): what is the ratio of the between-module-within-individual variance component to the between-individual variance component for each estimand, with an interval, after a technical component is estimated from replicate libraries of the same module and chimeric individuals are classified and reported separately from mosaic ones? The target of estimation is explicitly the module-conditional estimate plus its between-module variance, not a single "true" per-individual value.

Alternative formulations:
- Does it matter which branch of the tree you sequence?
- Treating within-individual somatic mosaicism as a measurement-error term rather than as biology: how large is the between-module component of variance in population-genomic estimates for trees, corals and clonal plants, and which published conclusions are sensitive to it?

Motivating gap:
Within-individual somatic variation in large modular organisms is now directly measurable and has been mapped in named organisms (Pando aspen clone mosaic, eLife reviewed preprint 110244, 2025 — COULD NOT READ, see below), and theory exists for INDEXING it (Iwasa, Tomimoto & Satake 2024, Quantitative Plant Biology 5:e11, doi 10.1017/qpb.2024.9; read at intake via Cambridge Core by the generating agent, not re-opened here: entirely theoretical, three phylogenetic-distance indexes for variation among a tree's reproductive organs, no heterozygosity/kinship/allele-frequency/Ne, "no empirical data are available", frames somatic variation as having fitness consequences rather than as a confound). The literature describes mosaicism as biology, or voices it as a Discussion-section caveat, but no opened source propagates module choice into the estimands the field reports. This Gate 2 found that the caveat has in fact been stated explicitly in the coral literature since at least 2012 and never quantified — which lowers the grade and is recorded below rather than smoothed over.

Presuppositions:
1. Within-individual somatic variation is detectable above sequencing error at the depth typical of population studies. CONTESTED — mosaic variants are the hardest class to call; a dedicated-depth arm and a variant-calling validation arm are required. Supporting (title-only, not opened, grades nothing): "Minimizing detection bias of somatic mutations in a highly heterozygous oak genome" (2025), which exists because this is a live methodological problem.
2. Somatic variants reach within-module frequencies high enough to move an individual-level heterozygosity or kinship estimate. CONTESTED and the crux: if they are rare and subclonal the answer may be "negligible", which is itself the answer. NEW RISK FOUND AT GATE 2: published per-branch somatic variant counts already exist (Populus trichocarpa somatic genetic/epigenetic mutation rate work, Genome Biology 2020, title-only — not opened, so no number is quoted here), so for the heterozygosity limb specifically an order-of-magnitude answer may be derivable in a page from published counts over a genome-wide heterozygous-site denominator. This is the candidate's main exposure to a Triviality attack and is named as such, not hidden.
3. Multiple modules per individual can be sampled and sequenced at matched depth with module provenance recorded. Established — Maier et al. 2012 already did 2-3 samples per colony across 155 colonies with marker data.
4. The estimands remain defined for a mosaic individual. FLAGGED as a dissolution risk; handled by defining the target as the module-conditional estimate plus its between-module variance.
5. Module provenance is recoverable for at least some published datasets, so a re-analysis arm is possible. PARTLY CONTESTED — Maier's thesis records per-colony sample counts but the Springer record does not specify the anatomical origin of each sample, which is the exact field a re-analysis arm needs; provenance recoverability must be verified dataset by dataset, not assumed.
6. ADDED at Gate 2: chimerism (fusion of distinct genotypes) is separable from mosaicism (somatic mutation). Necessary, because in corals chimerism injects whole second genotypes rather than single mutations and would dominate any pooled between-module variance while answering a different question. Schweinsberg et al. 2015 report both in every species tested.

What would count as an answer:
A variance-components analysis on multi-module sequencing of a pre-specified number of individuals in at least two taxa (one long-lived tree, one colonial or clonal marine organism), partitioning each estimand's variance into between-individual, between-module-within-individual, and technical components, with the between-module share and its interval reported per estimand and chimeras reported separately; plus a sensitivity arm that re-runs a published analysis (a kinship matrix, a genetic-diversity indicator, or a diversity-based conservation threshold) with modules swapped, reporting how often a conclusion-relevant threshold is crossed. A measured near-zero between-module share answers the question in the negative and licenses current single-module practice explicitly rather than tacitly. A per-estimand answer is required: the limbs can come apart (plausibly negligible for genome-wide heterozygosity, non-negligible for identity- and threshold-based analyses).

Significance — consequence:
If the between-module share is non-negligible for an estimand, then every single-module study reporting that estimand carries an unreported variance term, and protocols change in a specific, cheap way: record module provenance, and sample >1 module for identity- and threshold-based analyses. The highest-value targets are not genome-wide diversity summaries but identity- and threshold-dependent outputs, where prior A already predicts the bite: clone-mate assignment, parentage, "number of genotypes" in a coral nursery or a restoration plot, a kinship matrix used for broodstock or seed-orchard management, and any conservation indicator with a hard cut (an Ne threshold, a genotypic-richness target). If the share is near zero, the answer licenses single-module sampling explicitly, which has real value precisely because the caveat has circulated unquantified in the coral literature since 2012 and currently costs design hesitancy and reviewer objections without any number to settle them. Both outcomes are publishable and act-upon-able, which is what a well-posed variance question should deliver.
A real asymmetry across limbs is argued rather than hidden: for genome-wide observed heterozygosity the denominator (millions of heterozygous sites) is so much larger than published per-branch somatic variant counts that a near-zero answer is the strong prior and may be derivable in a page; for kinship near a decision threshold, for allele frequencies at the specific sites that happen to be somatically variable, and for any coral analysis where a chimeric partner genotype enters, the prior is much weaker. The question earns its keep on those limbs, and it should be judged on them.

Significance — audience:
(1) Conservation genomicists computing genetic-diversity and Ne indicators for long-lived trees and corals, who need a defensible uncertainty statement for an indicator computed from one tissue sample per organism. (2) Coral restoration programmes that genotype nursery fragments to track "genotypes" and maintain genotypic diversity targets — a fragment is a module, and the currency of the practice is module-level identity. (3) Forest tree breeders and clonal seed-orchard managers, who already run ramet clone-verification assays (query 6) and would learn whether a mismatch means mislabelling or somatic divergence — these are opposite operational responses to the same observation. (4) The somatic-mutation-rate community itself, which must choose how many modules per individual to sequence and currently has no variance budget to justify a number. Audience (3) is the one that would act fastest because it already runs the assay.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: module-aware estimators and a design-effect/ICC analogue for modular organisms (how many modules buy how much precision, and at what cost relative to adding individuals); whether somatic load is systematically related to module age, height or position, so that convenience sampling (reachable low branches, fragment tips) is non-random with respect to the estimand rather than merely noisy; whether mosaicism and chimerism need different corrections; whether the between-module share scales with organism age or size, which would make it a predictable, correctable term rather than an unknown; and whether the same decomposition applies to epigenetic estimands, where the within-individual term is likely far larger.

Significance — anchoring:
The coral chimerism/unit-of-selection problem (prior B's own framing); the genet-vs-ramet sampling-unit problem in clonal plant Ne (prior C); the design of somatic mutation-rate studies (how many branches); the Kunming-Montreal GBF genetic-diversity indicators, which put a decision threshold on exactly these estimands; and the clone-identity-verification practice in breeding, where a somatic-divergence interpretation and a mislabelling interpretation are currently not distinguished.

Reason for silence:
A disciplinary boundary that is also a design incompatibility, plus a genre effect. The people who measure within-individual somatic variation are mutation-rate biologists whose design is a few deeply sequenced modules from one to three individuals — a design with no between-individual axis, and therefore no denominator for the ratio the question asks about. The people who compute heterozygosity, kinship, allele frequencies and Ne use one module from many individuals — a design with no within-individual axis, and therefore no numerator. The crossed design (many individuals x several modules x matched depth) costs a multiple of either and, until resequencing got cheap, had no funding rationale; neither community's grant pays for the other's axis. The genre effect compounds it: in the coral literature the problem has lived since 2012 in Discussion sections as a named caveat (prior A), and caveats are the part of a paper that gets cited rather than tested, so the concern propagated as a sentence while never acquiring an estimand. That is a mechanism, not "no one thought of it" — the record shows they did think of it and wrote it down.
