## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take one named seismic network and one named time window. Reconstruct, hour by hour, which channels were actually recording, push that through a stated model that turns "these stations are up" into "this is the smallest magnitude the network can detect, here", and then ask three statistical questions of the resulting series: (a) do stations go dark *together* more than they would if each went dark on its own schedule — what share of downtime hours sit in multi-station clusters above an independence null; (b) how much more likely is a multi-hour stretch in which the network is really worse than its *published* capability figure by at least 0.2/0.3/0.5 magnitude units, once you use the measured joint downtime process instead of the independence assumption — reported as a ratio; and (c) what share of the variance of realized capability is correlated downtime rather than independent downtime. In one line: the field governs availability one station at a time (a 98% floor per station) and states capability for the network as a whole; nobody has measured the covariance that connects them.
