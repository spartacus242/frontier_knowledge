## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Take one fixed network recipe (architecture, init, token budget, optimiser settings) and train it many times on Gaussian synthetic inputs whose covariance eigenvalues fall off as i^-beta, for six values of beta and nine (learning-rate, batch-size) pairs. For every large hidden weight matrix, compute the eigenvalues of W^T W, take the top 10% and run the Hill estimator to get a "tail exponent" alpha. Question: when you lay those alphas out over the (beta, eta/B) grid, is the surface really one-dimensional (a function of beta only, of eta/B only, or of one power-product of the two) or genuinely two-dimensional, and what are the two slopes at the grid centre? The record also pre-commits to a check that cells sharing eta/B but differing in (eta, B) give the same alpha, failing which eta/B is the wrong temperature variable.
