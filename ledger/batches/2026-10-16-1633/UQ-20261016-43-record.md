Question (canonical):
VERSION A (as intaken): Treating each published electrocatalyst durability test as a right-censored lifetime observation whose censoring time is the experimenter's chosen test duration, what lifetime distribution does the published corpus actually support, and is the censoring informative — does the stopping time depend on the degradation the experimenter was watching?

VERSION B (ONE Gate 1 reformulation, for well-posedness only; this is the version graded):
For a pre-specified frame of published electrocatalyst durability tests — restricted in advance by reaction (OER), electrolyte pH class, current-density band and cell type so that one failure definition is comparable (e.g. first time the overpotential at fixed current exceeds its own run-initial value by a pre-fixed Delta, with Delta fixed before extraction) — and treating each run as an observation that either reached that failure definition (event) or ended at the author's chosen stop (right-censored):
 (i) what is the empirical distribution of stopping times, and what share of its mass lies exactly on convention values (10, 12, 24, 100, 500, 1000 h);
 (ii) what survival function (Kaplan-Meier) and parametric lifetime fit (Weibull / lognormal) does the frame support under that failure definition, with pre-registered sensitivity analysis over the comparability restriction and over a stated publication-selection model;
 (iii) does the degradation rate over the final pre-specified fraction of each run predict that run's stopping time, after conditioning on pre-registered design covariates (lab, year, journal, reaction, current density, cell type) — which under non-informative censoring it should not;
 and what test duration would be required to separate two candidate catalysts' lifetimes at 80% power, two-sided alpha = 0.05, under the fitted distribution?
Both versions are recorded as required; Version B adds the frame, the failure definition, the covariate set and the power limb without changing the object of the question.

Alternative formulations:
1. Are headline durability claims such as "stable for 100 h at 10 mA cm-2" statements about catalysts, or about the conventional durations at which this field stops testing?
2. If the oxygen-evolution durability literature is analysed as censored life data, how many reported "stable" results carry a lifetime estimate distinguishable from one another, and what test duration would be needed to separate two candidates at stated power?

Motivating gap:
Durability is reported as "held X mV for Y hours", with Y clustering on human-convenient values (10, 12, 24, 100, 1000 h). The field's documented response to this has been to propose better metrics and shorter surrogates (S-number, mass-loss monitoring, accelerated stress tests) or better protocols — i.e. to escape the duration problem — rather than to treat its own stopping times as a statistical object with a censoring distribution. The transplanted machinery is mature and specific: right censoring with suspended items, competing failure modes, and formal tests for informative censoring. The interface friction is real rather than decorative: the censoring mechanism here is an author's decision plausibly correlated with the outcome being censored, which is exactly the case standard life-data analysis excludes by assumption. Communities that would act: electrocatalysis groups and the journals refereeing their stability claims; electrolyzer developers bridging 100-hour laboratory tests to 40,000-hour deployment targets; and materials-informatics groups whose literature-derived stability datasets would need a censoring field.

Presuppositions:
P1. Test durations, and whether degradation was still in progress at stop, are recoverable at scale from published reports. CONTESTED: durations are usually readable from text or figure axes, but "degrading at stop" generally requires reading a figure, so extraction error must be measured against a hand-coded subsample and reported.
P2. Stopping times are chosen by the experimenter rather than imposed. CONTESTED: equipment, shift, cell-failure and funding constraints make some stops non-informative by construction; separating these classes is part of the question, not a nuisance, and requires a censoring-reason taxonomy coded from text/SI where stated and left missing where not.
P3. A failure definition can be pre-specified that is comparable across reports. CONTESTED: current density, electrolyte, substrate, loading and cell geometry all differ, so comparability needs a stated restriction rule that may shrink the usable frame severely; the shrinkage is itself a reportable result.
P4. Publication selection acts on which tests are reported. CONTESTED and only weakly identifiable; any lifetime estimate must be stated as conditional on the publication filter, with a sensitivity band rather than a point claim.
P5. "Lifetime" is a meaningful latent quantity for a material-plus-conditions pair. CONTESTED: degradation can be non-monotone or partly recoverable (activation periods, reversible surface reconstruction), in which case the competing-risks formulation is required and a single distribution is the wrong object. The protocol must pre-commit to the competing-risks fallback and to reporting which regime the data fall in.
P6 (added at Gate 1). The failure definition is reached often enough for a non-degenerate survival estimate. CONTESTED: if almost no run reaches it, Kaplan-Meier is near-degenerate — which is an admissible and informative answer, not a failure of the design.

Gate 1 well-posedness check: Version B states, for each limb, the evidence that settles it. (i) is a histogram with a pre-fixed convention-value set and a stated heaping statistic. (ii) is a fitted survival function with pre-registered sensitivity bands. (iii) is a regression coefficient with a pre-specified null (independence of censoring time and final-fraction degradation rate given covariates) and a stated test. The power limb is a derived number from (ii). The question is a question, not a thesis: "the censoring is uninformative, and the corpus supports no lifetime statement beyond the modal stopping time" is an admissible, pre-declared, publishable answer, so the design cannot be answered the same way regardless of evidence. The load-bearing risk is P1/P3 (extraction feasibility and comparability), both flagged contested with measurement built in.

What would count as an answer:
A pre-registered extraction over a defined frame of reports producing: (i) the empirical stopping-time distribution with spikes at convention values quantified; (ii) a Kaplan-Meier estimate plus a parametric fit under a stated failure definition, with sensitivity analysis over the comparability restriction and the publication-selection assumption; (iii) a direct test of informative censoring (final-fraction degradation rate predicting stopping time, conditioned on design covariates); and (iv) the test duration needed to separate two catalysts at stated power. Plus a measured extraction-error rate on a hand-coded subsample, and a censoring-reason taxonomy with its missingness rate. A null on (iii) with a degenerate (ii) is an admissible answer.

Significance — consequence:
If the censoring is informative (stopping time depends on observed degradation): every literature-derived durability ranking is biased, and the bias has a sign that can be estimated — authors who see degradation begin have a reason to stop at the nearest convention value and report the pre-degradation window, which inflates apparent stability of the worse catalysts relative to run-to-failure data. Benchmark tables of the form "stable for Y h" would have to carry a censoring indicator, and the materials-informatics datasets being built from this literature (the Q3 hits show such databases exist and are growing) would need a censoring field or their stability labels are mislabelled survival times. If the censoring is uninformative: the field gains something it currently does not have — a legitimate pooled lifetime estimate conditional on the publication filter, and a design number of the form "to distinguish catalyst A from B at 80% power you need N hours", which directly settles arguments about protocol length. The pre-declared null answer ("the corpus supports no lifetime statement beyond the modal stopping time") is itself consequential: it would mean that a decade of durability claims is a measurement of community convention, and would justify a reporting requirement for either run-to-failure or an explicit censoring reason.

Significance — audience:
(1) Electrocatalysis groups: would change what they record and report — a censoring-reason field and a "degrading at stop?" flag, and for key claims a run continued to a pre-specified failure rather than to a round number. (2) Journal editors and referees who set stability-reporting requirements (the Nature Catalysis / Chem Soc Rev / ACS Energy Letters best-practice line that Q6 and Q11 surfaced, including the PSU "Best Practices for Reporting Electrocatalytic Performance" guidance): a concrete, auditable checklist item replacing an exhortation to "test longer". (3) Electrolyzer and fuel-cell developers and the funding programmes that set 40,000-80,000 h targets (the CORDIS/Clean Hydrogen records from Q5): a quantified statement of how far the laboratory corpus can be extrapolated, and a power-based minimum test duration for down-selection. (4) Materials-informatics groups training stability predictors on literature-extracted labels: their labels are right-censored times being used as if they were outcomes.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: a competing-risks formulation separating dissolution, amorphization/reconstruction, substrate corrosion and bubble-induced mechanical loss, each with its own hazard; a censoring-reason taxonomy (equipment, shift, cell failure, funding, publication deadline) recoverable from text and SI, which is a new covariate class for the field; informative-censoring-corrected catalyst rankings; whether accelerated-stress-test protocols shift the censoring distribution rather than the lifetime distribution; whether the heaping structure differs by journal, lab seniority or year, which turns stopping behaviour into a measurable sociology of the field; and direct transfer of the whole design to other fields with an analogous convention — perovskite photovoltaic T80 reporting, membrane fouling, catalyst time-on-stream in thermal catalysis.

Significance — anchoring:
Touches the activity-stability trade-off debate; the metric debate the S-number was invented to settle (Prior 2); the long-standing lab-to-stack durability gap and the extrapolation problem it forces on reliability engineering; the reproducibility/benchmarking literature in electrocatalysis; and the general statistical problem of lifetime inference from heavily censored short tests, where this would supply an unusually clean real-world case of censoring set by the observer.

Reason for silence:
A disciplinary boundary plus an incentive gap, with a weaker new-instrument component. (a) The method is owned by biostatistics and reliability engineering; electrocatalysis trains neither, and its statistical reflex is per-sample error bars and replicate counts, not corpus-level time-to-event analysis with a censoring distribution. (b) The field's rewarded response to the durability problem has been metric invention — build a short surrogate so duration stops mattering (Prior 2 is this move, stated in the authors' own words) — which yields a publishable product, whereas auditing the corpus's own stopping behaviour yields no new material and implicates colleagues' headline claims, including those of the referees. (c) Ontologically, a durability curve's stopping point is not an "experimental result" in this field but a figure boundary, so it was never a variable anyone would tabulate; nothing in the field's data model has a slot for it. (d) Extraction of durations and end-of-run slopes at corpus scale has only recently become practical, which supplies the new-data component but is not the main mechanism — the main mechanism is that no one in the field owns the method and no one who owns the method reads the field.
