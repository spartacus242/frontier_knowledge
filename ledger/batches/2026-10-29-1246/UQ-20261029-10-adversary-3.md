## 1. Restatement in my own words

Programs running on just-in-time-compiling virtual machines (HotSpot, Graal, V8, PyPy, LuaJIT, HHVM, Truffle) are known, since Barrett et al. 2017 and now Antoch et al. 2026, to often fail to settle into a steady level of per-iteration performance even after thousands of in-process repetitions. The candidate asks whether the CAUSE is the VM's own adaptive recompilation cycle (profile → compile → deoptimise → re-profile → recompile) rather than something outside the compiler (collector, kernel, hardware, memory layout). Its test: take one (VM, program) pair and run it (A) normally; (B) with the compiler's per-method decisions fixed in advance from a recording made in a pilot run and with adaptive recompilation switched off; (C) with the same fixed plan applied only late, after the point at which the pilot had finished compiling (a placebo arm); classify each execution's iteration-time series as convergent or not with a changepoint rule fixed before any timing; report the between-arm difference in the non-convergent share, over every pair in Barrett's 2017 VM × benchmark matrix whose VM currently supports arm B.
