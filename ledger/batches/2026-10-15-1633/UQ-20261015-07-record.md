Question (canonical): [Gate 1 reformulation, v2] In proteins crystallised in the same crystal form and measured at both cryogenic (~100 K) and room temperature (≥270 K), at residues whose major conformer differs between the two temperatures by more than coordinate error, do AlphaFold 2, AlphaFold 3 and open AF3-class predictors (sampled over several seeds) reproduce the cryogenic major conformer more often than the room-temperature one, and does any such preference persist when proteins are stratified by training-set exposure?

Alternative formulations:
- v1 (intake wording, kept for the record): "For residues whose conformations differ between paired cryogenic (~100 K) and room-temperature crystal structures of the same protein, do AlphaFold-class predictors systematically reproduce the cryogenic conformation rather than the room-temperature one?"
- Has training on a Protein Data Bank that is overwhelmingly cryocooled taught structure predictors the cryocooling artefacts, so that their "near-experimental accuracy" partly means accuracy to 100 K structures? (mechanistic version; not the canonical question)
- When cryogenic and room-temperature crystal structures of one protein disagree, which one do AlphaFold 2/3 predictions match, and do pLDDT or seed-to-seed spread flag the temperature-sensitive residues?
- Gate 1 note: v1 left "conformations differ", "AlphaFold-class" and "reproduce" undefined, and folded a mechanism ("learned the artefacts") into a descriptive question. v2 defines the residue set (major-conformer change larger than coordinate error, same crystal form), names the predictor set, requires multi-seed sampling, and moves the mechanism into a stratified secondary test. v2 is a question with a yes/no/effect-size answer, not a thesis.

Motivating gap: The claim that AlphaFold 2/3 reach near-experimental accuracy is benchmarked against PDB crystal structures. Most were collected at ~100 K (a St Jude summary of Bradford et al. 2021 puts it at about 95%), and cryo-EM models are also vitrified at cryogenic temperature. That benchmark assumes the cryogenic reference is the right target. Cryocooling remodels conformations: Fraser et al. 2011 PNAS (PMID 21918110) studied ~30 paired cryo/room-temperature structures; Keedy et al. 2014 Structure (PMID 24882744) examined DHFR; and in Bradford et al. 2021 Chem. Sci. (doi 10.1039/d1sc02751d; opened) "more than a third of the 164 residues in the protein responded to temperature in at least 1 of the 9 structure pairs" (T4 lysozyme L99A), mostly near the ligand site, and this biased docking. Chemistry World's coverage of Bradford 2021 (opened) quotes E. Garman: "PDB cryo-structures will not be as productive a training set as room temperature-structures would be … for AI-based computational modelling tools". That is a stated expectation, not a test. In the 37 searches below, no paper tests AlphaFold-class predictions against paired cryo/room-temperature structures at the level of residue conformers.

Presuppositions:
1. Enough paired cryo/room-temperature datasets exist with identical sequence and crystal form. ESTABLISHED for dozens of proteins: Fraser 2011 (~30), Keedy multitemperature PTP1B (2018), Bradford 2021 (9 T4L pairs and 4 other classes), Ebrahim 2022 Mpro, Mehlman 2023 PTP1B fragments, Günther 2025 FosA. CONTESTED for statistical power once the set is restricted to residues that switch major conformer, and further restricted by training exposure.
2. Cryo/room-temperature differences are real redistributions of conformations, not artefacts of refinement or model building. LARGELY ESTABLISHED (Fraser 2011; Keedy 2014; Bradford 2021 used maps, not just models). FLAGGED: a residual risk from differences in resolution or refinement between members of a pair. A mitigation is to re-refine both members of each pair through one pipeline (e.g. qFit).
3. The PDB's recorded collection temperature is reliable. FLAGGED: the mmCIF ambient-temperature field is sometimes missing or a default value and must be checked against the primary paper.
4. A single predicted conformer can be compared meaningfully with the major conformer of a multi-conformer model. CONTESTED: AF2 and AF3 emit one state per sample, and room-temperature models often add alternate conformers rather than switching the major one. The design therefore needs several seeds and samples, plus a secondary occupancy-level readout (below).
5. Memorisation can be separated from learned bias. CONTESTED: few proteins have both datasets after the training cutoff (AF2 PDB cutoff 30 Apr 2018; AF3 30 Sep 2021). The workable strata are: both in training, cryo only in training, or neither (sequence-cluster level).
6. INTERPRETATION ONLY, not needed for the descriptive answer: a cryo preference means the predictor learned cryo-specific states. CONTESTED: AF2 is already reported to favour PDB-prevalent rotamers (bioRxiv 2025.02.10.637534), and those prevalences are themselves cryo-derived. A cryo preference could therefore arrive by way of a generic rotamer prior. This is why a prior-only baseline comparator is required.
7. Room-temperature structures are closer to physiological conformations. CONTESTED (~290 K is not 310 K, and lattice contacts remain). Only the significance argument needs this; the descriptive question does not.

What would count as an answer:
(a) Curate pairs with the same sequence, space group and similar cell, cryo ≤110 K and room temperature ≥270 K. Re-refine both members of each pair with one multi-conformer pipeline.
(b) Define temperature-sensitive residues before looking at predictions: a χ1/χ2 rotamer-bin change of the major conformer, a peptide flip, or a loop alternate state, each larger than coordinate error estimated from resolution and DPI. Temperature-insensitive residues form the control set.
(c) Predict with AF2, AF3 and at least one open reproduction (e.g. OpenFold3, Boltz-1/2, Chai-1, Protenix), using ≥5 seeds × multiple samples, with templates both on and off.
(d) Per residue, classify each sample as matching the cryo state, the room-temperature state, or neither.
(e) Primary test: among discriminating residues, H0 is P(cryo match | matches one state) = 0.5. Benchmark this against a prior-only comparator, i.e. the most-probable backbone-dependent rotamer. As a calibration check, AF's match rate at temperature-insensitive residues gives its baseline accuracy.
(f) Secondary test: is the per-residue distribution of AF samples closer, by Jensen–Shannon divergence, to the cryo or to the room-temperature occupancy distribution?
(g) Stratify (e) and (f) by training exposure and by distance to ligand site and lattice contacts.
"Yes" = a significant cryo preference beyond the prior-only comparator, especially in the "cryo-only-in-training" and "neither" strata. "No" = no preference, or a room-temperature preference.
Secondary readout: whether pLDDT or seed spread is lower at temperature-sensitive residues than at the control residues.

Significance — consequence:
- If YES (cryo preference beyond the prior-only comparator, persisting outside memorised proteins): part of "near-experimental accuracy" is accuracy to a 100 K state. Benchmarks such as CASP/CAMEO/PoseBusters-style comparisons should report collection temperature or stratify by it. AF-derived pockets used for docking inherit the cryo remodelling that Bradford 2021 showed biases ligand-binding predictions. Developers have a concrete fix: a temperature token, analogous to Boltz-2's method conditioning, or reweighting of room-temperature/serial structures.
- If NO (no preference, or a room-temperature preference): AF-class models have not absorbed a pervasive acquisition artefact in their training labels. That is evidence the learned preferences transfer across measurement conditions. It defuses the Garman expectation and lowers the priority of room-temperature reweighting for predictors, without weakening the case for room-temperature data in experimental interpretation.
- Either way: if pLDDT or seed spread flags temperature-sensitive residues, that becomes a cheap prior for choosing where to collect room-temperature data.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism):
(1) Disciplinary boundary. The room-temperature crystallography community frames cryo artefacts as a problem for experimental interpretation, docking and force fields (Bradford 2021; Cavender 2023 targets force fields explicitly). ML evaluation treats "X-ray" as one ground-truth class; even Boltz-2's method conditioning stops at X-ray/NMR/MD.
(2) Benchmark incentive. Leaderboards reward agreement with deposited, mostly cryogenic structures, and asking whether the target is mis-specified cuts against headline accuracy claims.
(3) A resolution belief. AF's typical side-chain and backbone error (median Cα ~0.6–1.0 Å) was assumed to swamp cryo-induced shifts. Discrete rotamer switches and peptide flips are not below that resolution, but that assumption plausibly discouraged the comparison.
(4) Recent enablers. Automated multiconformer modelling (qFit, eLife 2024), the serial-crystallography expansion of room-temperature datasets after 2020, and cheap multi-seed sampling in AF3 and open reproductions (2024–2025) only recently made the per-residue, exposure-stratified test feasible.
Gate 4 should test whether (1) and (2) are real mechanisms or post-hoc.
