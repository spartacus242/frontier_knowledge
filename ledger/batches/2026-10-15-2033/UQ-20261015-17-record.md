Domain(s): STEM — atmospheric science / numerical weather prediction; AI (data-driven forecast models). Neighbours: data assimilation (observation impact / observation influence), forecast verification science.

Question (canonical) [R1, adopted at Gate 1]:
For a fixed period and a fixed set of initial times, with both model families started from the same operational analysis and scored under one pre-specified metric set (RMSE, bias, CRPS, and a pre-specified extreme-event score) per variable, level and region: does the data-driven-minus-physical skill difference measured against the analysis/reanalysis the data-driven models were trained on (Delta_analysis) equal, within a pre-specified equivalence band, the same difference measured against observations that did not inform that analysis at that time and place (Delta_obs) — where "did not inform" means blacklisted, post-cutoff/late-arriving, networks not assimilated, or withheld in an observing-system experiment; and does the paired discrepancy Delta_analysis − Delta_obs scale with a per-location/level/variable measure of how weakly the analysis is constrained by observations (observation influence, DFS, or analysis-ensemble spread)?

Question (V0, intake version, superseded at Gate 1):
For identical forecast sets, is the skill gap between data-driven weather models and the operational physical model the same when scored against the analysis or reanalysis the data-driven models were trained on as when scored against independent in-situ observations, and does any discrepancy grow with how weakly the analysis is constrained by observations at that location, level and variable?

Reason for the single permitted Gate 1 reformulation (well-posedness only): V0 left three things undefined that decide the answer. (i) "Identical forecast sets" does not fix initialisation: in practice data-driven models are started from an analysis and the physical model from its own analysis, and arXiv:2305.00048 shows that this confound alone can absorb the effect — R1 pins both families to the same analysis. (ii) "Independent observations" is not operational, since most conventional observations are assimilated into ERA5 — R1 gives four admissible independence routes. (iii) V0 asked whether the gap "is the same" with no decision rule — R1 names a pre-specified equivalence band and a paired statistic. No change of subject: same quantity, same inversion.

Motivating gap:
The settled claim inverted is "data-driven models now beat the operational physical model". It presupposes that skill scored against the analysis/reanalysis on which those models were trained is an unbiased estimate of skill against the atmosphere. A reanalysis is a model product: where observations are sparse its values are largely set by the assimilating model's own physics, so a learner trained to reproduce it inherits that model's structure as "truth". GraphCast, Pangu-Weather, FourCastNet, AIFS and Aurora are trained on ERA5 and headline-scored under analysis-based protocols. Observation-space verification is routine in operational NWP and now appears in several AI-model papers; what the Gate 2 sweep did not find is the *paired* quantity — the difference between the AI-minus-physical gap in analysis space and the same gap in observation space, for the same forecasts, resolved by observational constraint.

Presuppositions:
1. Analyses and reanalyses are model-dependent in a spatially, vertically and variable-dependent way that can be quantified. ESTABLISHED — observation-influence and DFS diagnostics exist (e.g. Cardinali-type observation influence; Hu et al. 2025, J. Adv. Model. Earth Syst., doi 10.1029/2024MS004809, "Assessing the Influence of Observations in Ensemble-Based Data Assimilation Systems", search result opened only as a listing, not read in full).
2. A body of observations independent of both the analysis and the training data exists. CONTESTED IN PRACTICE — most conventional observations are assimilated into ERA5; R1 therefore enumerates four independence routes rather than assuming one.
3. Observation error and representativeness error can be handled so an observation-space gap is interpretable. STANDARD BUT NON-TRIVIAL; explicitly contested for surface and boundary-layer variables and for precipitation (gauge undercatch is the untested explanation offered in arXiv:2509.01879).
4. Both families can be scored on the same initial times and lead times. ESTABLISHED; R1 additionally forces a shared initial state, because free initialisation is itself part of the circularity under test.
5. "Skill gap" is well defined per variable/level/region under a pre-specified metric set. ESTABLISHED once the metric set and band are pre-registered (R1 does this).
6. A per-location/level/variable observation-constraint measure can be obtained and joined to verification output. CONTESTED ON ACCESS, not on existence: these diagnostics are computed inside operational assimilation systems and are not published as gridded fields.
Not presupposed: that the discrepancy favours either family, or that it is non-zero.

What would count as an answer:
A verification study over a fixed period and a fixed forecast set that, per variable/level/region, computes Delta_analysis and Delta_obs for the same forecasts, reports the paired difference Delta_analysis − Delta_obs with uncertainty (block bootstrap over initial times; paired because the two references score the same forecast fields), and regresses that difference on an observation-influence measure. The presupposition holds if the paired difference lies inside the pre-specified equivalence band across variables and regions; it fails if the band is exceeded, with the sign and the slope on observational constraint reported. A third closed outcome must be admitted: observation and representativeness error inflate both Deltas enough that the paired contrast is not resolvable at the available sample size — in which case the answer is that the comparison cannot currently discriminate, which is itself decision-relevant for verification standards.

Significance — consequence:
If the paired difference lies inside the band: the published analysis-space league tables can be read as estimates of atmospheric skill, and the circularity objection — which is currently argued rhetorically (N1) rather than measured — is retired with a number attached. That is a real change, because the objection is live enough that a data-driven model's operational adoption can be contested on it.
If the band is exceeded: every headline comparison acquires a reference-dependent correction, and the correction's sign matters differently in each direction. If Delta_analysis > Delta_obs (the hypothesised direction, consistent with N1), part of the reported AI advantage is agreement with the training target rather than with the atmosphere, and the quantity procurement and deployment decisions are made on is biased — worst exactly where forecasts are hardest to replace (data-sparse oceans, polar regions, the land boundary layer, tropical humidity). If Delta_obs > Delta_analysis, data-driven models are being *under*-credited by analysis-space scoring, which is equally consequential and would redirect effort toward obs-space training targets. A non-zero slope on observation influence converts either finding from a global caveat into a map of where analysis-space scores can and cannot be trusted — which is the operationally usable object.
Third outcome (not resolvable at available sample size) would itself justify investment in reserved, unassimilated reference networks, which is a budget decision WMO members actually make.

Significance — audience:
(i) WMO lead centres and verification bodies that write the standards — the WMO Lead Centre for Deterministic NWP Verification (wmolcdnv.ecmwf.int) and the WIPPS verification framework — would have to decide whether obs-space verification becomes mandatory for data-driven systems rather than supplementary; a measured reference-dependence slope is the kind of evidence such a decision needs, and its absence is why the current standards (query 5) say nothing about it.
(ii) National meteorological services choosing whether to put a data-driven model on the operational bench, who presently weigh analysis-space scorecards against an argued-but-unmeasured circularity objection.
(iii) AI-model developers choosing reference datasets and training targets: a region/variable map of where analysis-space scores mislead is a direct instruction about where to train or verify against observations instead, and several groups are already moving this way (obs-assimilating and obs-trained models surfaced in queries 1 and 11).
(iv) Downstream buyers who procure on published scores (energy trading, aviation, reinsurance) — the only group whose decisions are made *entirely* on the analysis-space number.
(v) Reanalysis producers, for whom a measured dependence on observation influence is a statement about where their product should not be used as truth.
Reality check: this audience is demonstrably real rather than asserted — N1 was written by a commercial forecast provider, N2 by an academic group, and N3 by the operational centre deploying the model, i.e. all three constituencies have already published on the first clause.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: should data-driven models be trained on observation-space loss in exactly the regions where the slope is steep, rather than globally? Does the reference-dependence slope shrink as a model's training target moves from ERA5 to an obs-assimilating or obs-trained analysis (the arXiv:2407.15586 / 2607.07879 line)? Is there an analogous slope for the physical model, i.e. does the operational model also look better against its own analysis than against observations, and by how much less? Does the slope predict which AI-model failure modes (blurring, extremes under-dispersion, boundary-layer bias) are artefacts of the training target? And the generalisation: for any learner trained to reproduce a model-based product, how much of its measured skill is inherited structure — the NWP instance of a question asked separately in ML evaluation (query 9) and never joined to it.

Significance — anchoring:
Touches (a) the observation-impact/observation-influence and DFS literature, which supplies the regressor and is currently used only for assimilation-system monitoring (Hu et al. 2025, doi 10.1029/2024MS004809); (b) the long-standing verification-science problem of a model-dependent verification reference, which predates AI and is taught as a caveat without a measured size; (c) the live anomaly that AI models' reported advantage is large in analysis space and small-to-absent in observation space (N1, N2) with no accepted explanation and competing candidate causes (initial-state quality, representativeness error, training-target inheritance) that nobody has separated; (d) the deployment decisions now in front of operational centres (N3).

Reason for silence:
Not "no one thought of it" — N1 and N3 both come within one subtraction of it. Two mechanisms, both structural.
(1) Disciplinary boundary with an asymmetric data holding. The regressor the second clause needs — per-location/level/variable observation influence or DFS — is computed inside operational assimilation systems as a monitoring diagnostic and is not published as a gridded field that can be joined to verification output. The people holding it (DA groups at operational centres) do not run AI-vs-physical headline comparisons; the people running those comparisons (AI developers, benchmark maintainers, academic evaluators) cannot obtain it. N1's authors argued the circularity instead of measuring it, which is what the boundary predicts: the argument is available to an outsider, the measurement is not.
(2) An incentive gradient in the verification protocol itself. Analysis space gives complete, gridded, globally uniform, reproducible fields and therefore cheap, comparable, publishable scores; observation space gives sparse, error-laden, representativeness-confounded, network-dependent ones, and the independence requirement (unassimilated observations) makes them scarcer still. Benchmark maintainers are rewarded for comparability, model developers for headline numbers, and operational centres report obs-space scores for their own operational monitoring in a different document from the one making the AI-vs-physical case. Nobody's reward function pays for the subtraction, and the subtraction is the only thing that would show whether the convenient reference is the biased one. The pre-registration the candidate requires (equivalence band fixed before the data are seen) is also exactly what a party with a stake in the answer is least inclined to commit to — an incentive gap rather than a conceptual one.
Caveat consistent with Gate 2: because a credible reason for silence exists for the *second* clause only, and the first clause has in fact been asked, the silence story supports treating this as a hardening of an asked question rather than as an un-asked question.
