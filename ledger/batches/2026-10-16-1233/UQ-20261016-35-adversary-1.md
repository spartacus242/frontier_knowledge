## Restatement (my words, not the generator's)

When a biobank participant withdraws consent and the rules say their record must be deleted from every analysis copy, (a) do the people who withdraw differ systematically from those who stay on the very exposures, covariates and outcomes the cohort is used for; (b) when you recompute a fixed panel of headline results on the dataset just before and just after each batch of deletions, do any of them move by more than sampling noise or across a decision threshold; and (c) does the movement accumulate in the direction the covariate differences predict. The record's added claim is that, because the deleted records are gone, only the custodian can ever measure this and only at the moment of deletion.

Stripped of framing, this is three things: a withdrawer-vs-stayer Table 1; a realised case-deletion influence computation; and a governance claim about who holds the frames.

## Own arithmetic (labelled; standard nested-subsample result, my derivation)

For an efficient estimator on nested samples, Var(theta_sub - theta_full) = Var(theta_sub) - Var(theta_full) = SE_full^2 * f/(1-f) where f is the removed fraction. Under random removal: f = 0.019 (HUNT2 sample-withdrawal rate per N2) gives SD of shift about 0.14 SE; f = 0.003 gives about 0.055 SE. Under informative removal of a mean, the expected shift is about f*d*sigma/(1-f) where d is the withdrawer-stayer standardised difference; with n = 65,000, f = 0.019 and d = 0.3 that is about 1.5 SE for a raw mean, but for a regression or GWAS coefficient the first-order shift requires withdrawal to covary with genotype, which it plausibly does not except through the outcome. I did not verify these against a primary source within budget, so they support only a Partial, not an Unresolved, on Triviality.
