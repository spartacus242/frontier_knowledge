## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

When a hospital or health system swaps one EHR vendor for another, two things are being asked. First, a bookkeeping question: of the structured facts (problems, med orders, allergies, numeric labs with units, immunisations, procedures, notes) that sat in the OLD system's tables during a chosen look-back period, what share shows up in the NEW system as a structured, queryable, value-identical fact, when the mapping between old and new data models is fixed in advance and "there is no legitimate place for this in the new model" is counted as its own bucket. Second, a measurement question: if you compute standard longitudinal research quantities (new-diabetes rate, ACE-inhibitor exposure, Charlson prevalence) from the new system's research extract, do they jump or bend at the switch date beyond a threshold set beforehand, and can the jump be pinned on the missing history by recomputing the same quantities for the same patients from the retired system's archive, and by comparing against a same-length window with no switch (and a non-switching site if one exists).

What the question CONDITIONS ON: (a) the retired system remains queryable (not merely viewable) at the time of measurement; (b) the new system's research extract draws only on the new system's tables; (c) the switch date is labelled; (d) analysts do not already truncate at the switch; (e) absence in the target is one undifferentiated quantity rather than a mixture of designed scope exclusion and migration failure. I tested (a), (b) and (e) against something I opened; (c) and (d) I could not test within budget.
