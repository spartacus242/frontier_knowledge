## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take some class of infrastructure you can actually list unit by unit — bridges on a national road network, say, or wind turbines in a register — where you know when each unit went in and when (or roughly when) it came out, and why it came out. Three things are then asked. (i) How long did they really last: a proper survival estimate, with the units still standing counted as censored and the units already old at the start of the window handled as left-truncated, ending in the probability that a unit reached the design working life its code declared. (ii) Of the units that left, what fraction left for the reason the design working life is about, versus for reasons that have nothing to do with how strong it was — with an inter-rater agreement figure from a hand-checked subsample, and the share that cannot be classified at all reported. (iii) What does the mismatch do to the arithmetic of code calibration: integrate the code's target reliability over the service lives that actually occurred rather than over the nominal 50- or 100-year reference period, and report the shift in implied reliability index — plus the same calculation under the rival reading in which the reference period was never meant to be an exposure duration at all, which by construction gives no shift.

In plain terms: **do bridges leave service because they wore out, or for other reasons, in what proportion, how long did they actually last, and does that break the code's safety arithmetic?**

### C7 is the kill. Three legs, each labelled.

**Leg A — textual, my own reading of the record's quoted clause (labelled as my reasoning, not a source finding).** The rule is *exhaustive at two classes* and then posits a third. "the structural class is **exactly** those recorded reasons whose satisfaction would have been prevented by greater design resistance, **everything else is non-structural**" leaves no residue; "an explicit unclassifiable class absorbs the residue" therefore absorbs an empty set on the rule's own terms. So a deterioration-driven removal is not unclassifiable — it has already been assigned, by the second clause, to **non-structural**. The unclassifiable class cannot be the escape hatch for it. The search that would have grounded this leg does not exist: it is an internal inconsistency in the question's own wording, not a claim about the world.

**Leg B — sourced. "The class the design working life encodes" includes deterioration, so the rule's class is the wrong class.** From F3 (SCIA's EN 1990 training manual, exact fragments as served; **EN 1990 itself not opened by me**):
- Design working life: *"The design working life is the assumed period for which a structure is to be used for its intended purpose"* / *"with the anticipated maintenance but without major repair being necessary."*
- Durability: *"The structure shall be designed that deterioration over its design working life does not impair"* [the sentence continues to performance requirements; I am not detaching the clause's consequent, and I quote it cut only because the summariser's cap stopped there].
- Design working life again: *"Design working life needs to be considered for material property deterioration, for life cycle costing"*.
Read these three for what the opening words scope and whether the clause asserts the quantity: the first **asserts** what the design working life is; the second **asserts** a durability requirement tied to it; the third **asserts** that material deterioration is one of the things the design working life is for. None denies it, none records a move away from it. So the class the design working life encodes is resistance **and** durability. The rule's class is resistance only. V1's own enumeration said the same thing the code does — "structural deterioration, fatigue, loss of load capacity" — and **the single permitted Gate 1 repair silently dropped deterioration out of it.** Per rule (iv) the repair is tested as hard as the clause it replaced, and this is where it fails.
**REGIME (Leg B source):** population = EN 1990 as the basis-of-design standard for building and civil engineering works in CEN states; unit of analysis = the structure; operating condition = design stage, anticipated maintenance, no major repair; instrument = the standard's text, here as reproduced in a vendor training manual rather than the standard; threshold = the reliability reference period T, stated as one that may or may not coincide with the design working life; category = design working life, durability, reliability management. The candidate's clause "the class the design working life encodes" sits squarely inside this regime.

**Leg C — sourced, and this is the materiality.** The block the repair reassigns is large and it is durability.
- On NILIM TN 444 表-2.2.3 panel (d) the printed counts are 688, 319, 179, 60, 23, 22, 51 over a printed denominator of 1,342 bridges (verification pass, full text at the document). 上部構造の損傷 179 + 下部構造の損傷 22 = 201, i.e. **201/1342 = 15.0% of removals** (*my own arithmetic on printed counts*). Limb (ii)'s headline share then reads **4.5% (60/1342)** under the rule as written and **25.0% (335/1342)** if the damage and residual blocks go the other way — the verification pass's own arithmetic, a factor of 5.5. Note what is and is not printed, per the published-is-not-performed test: NILIM prints **counts**, not a percentage-of-total column; the shares are arithmetic on printed counts and I say so.
- That damage block is dominated by durability mechanisms, printed, at a document **I read in full**: Ichikawa et al. 2008 **Figure 5**, FY2006 damage sub-causes — steel (n=59) 鋼材の腐食 **49.2%**, 床版の破損 28.8%; RC (n=75) コンクリート桁の亀裂・剥離 **32.0%**, 床版の破損 26.7%, 塩害によるコンクリート桁の破損 14.7%; PC (n=33) 塩害によるコンクリート桁の破損 **51.5%**. **Denominator check: these are shares of the material-type sub-counts n=59 / 75 / 33, NOT of 1,342.** They establish composition within the damage class, not a share of all removals.

The defect here is not coarseness: NILIM's records are granular to the named mechanism (塩害, 中性化, アルカリ骨材反応, 疲労 each a printed sub-row, and one sub-row defined by **explicit exclusion** of load capacity, "耐荷力不足以外の要因で床版が損傷する全てのケースに該当する"). These removals are classifiable, and the question's own rule classifies them into the class the code's design working life says they do not belong in. Nothing in the question reports *that*. ## 5. Recommended verdict

**Rejected** — Unresolved on attack 4 (the Gate 1 repair's generating rule defines the wrong class, materially, on sources opened) and on attack 2 (limb (ii)'s core is a published known on a second national register in a vocabulary Gate 2 never tried); attack 5 is the same defect as 4 and is not counted as an independent leg. **Single strongest killing point:** the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation replaced V1's structural class ("structural deterioration, fatigue, loss of load capacity") with "exactly those recorded reasons whose satisfaction would have been prevented by greater design resistance, everything else is non-structural" — but EN 1990's design working life explicitly encodes deterioration ("The structure shall be designed that deterioration over its design working life does not impair"; "Design working life needs to be considered for material property deterioration"), so the rule assigns the code's own durability-driven removals to the *non-structural* class. On the only bridge register whose per-reason cells are printed (NILIM TN 444 表-2.2.3, 1,342 bridges) that is 201/1342 = 15.0% of removals and swings limb (ii)'s headline from 25.0% to 4.5%, and Ichikawa et al. 2008 Figure 5 shows the reassigned block is durability-dominated (鋼材の腐食 49.2% of steel, n=59; 塩害 51.5% of PC, n=33). The repair is not available a second time.

## 6. What I nominate, and why it is the right KIND

What would settle the one attack I could not close — attack 1's and attack 6's dependence on limb (iii) — is a document that (population) covers a code-calibrated structural class, (perturbs) the integration period of the target reliability, i.e. varies the exposure duration rather than the load model, and (prints) an implied reliability index or lifetime failure probability at more than one period with the periods named. **Heron 68-2 (2023), "Annual reliability requirements for bridges and viaducts", https://heronjournal.nl/68-2/1.html** is that kind on its face: its title names the conversion between annual and lifetime reliability requirements, which is exactly the quantity limb (iii) manipulates. **I did not open it** — it was returned by my own S7 and S10 and I had no fetch budget left — so this nomination is justified by kind and venue and **not** by anything I read, and I say so. The same applies to the structurae item "Differentiation of Target Reliability and Design Life..."
