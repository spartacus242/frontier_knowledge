## Restatement (my words, before engaging the record's framing)

Take the strains that two or more independent public culture collections each claim to hold as "the same strain" — the pairs that catalogues cross-reference, or that StrainInfo's identifier graph welds together. Fix that list before looking at any sequence. Then buy ampoules from each collection and sequence what actually arrives in the post. Report three things. (a) How far apart the genomes are, collection against collection, split by what kind of change it is: point mutations, indels, insertion-sequence jumps, plasmid gain or loss, prophage gain or loss. (b) What share of those cross-collection pairs are further apart than two ampoules of one lot from one collection are from each other — a yardstick measured inside the same study with the same pipeline, not a threshold chosen in advance. (c) Whether the distance grows with the number of subcultures since deposit and with years since deposit, under a rule written down in advance for telling "it drifted after deposit" apart from "these two were never the same isolate and the cross-reference is simply wrong."

In one line: does "equivalent accession" name one physical lineage, and if the claim fails, how often and by how much — as a distribution, not an anecdote.

## What the two pages I opened actually say

**NCIMB (F1), the collection's own curator page.** Scope, stated by the page itself: it is an explanation of how to search the NCIMB catalogue using other collections' strain numbers, plus a curator's note on why strains overlap between collections. It is NOT an equivalence policy document, and I state that separately from the strings below. On the contested point it is nonetheless explicit. The page's accepted standard is that when a collection states strains are equivalent, "these cultures will be indistinguishable from one another." Its caveats: "No culture collection can absolutely guarantee strain equivalency"; "genetic drift may alter the properties of the strains between collections"; such cases are "rare occurrences." It says nothing about passage history or subculture practice. (These quotes came back from an open, un-primed prompt — I did not supply the strings — but they are single-source and single-pass, and I had no fetch left to re-check them alone with their neighbouring sentences, which this ledger's rule (4) asks for. Treat them as one page's wording, verified once.)

**DSMZ (F2), the catalogue field guide.** Scope, stated by the page: "how the strain data fields are arranged in the DSMZ online catalogue" — a field-definition guide, not a provenance or equivalence policy. Two field definitions matter. "Other collection numbers" is defined only as "Accession numbers in other collections" — the page does not say whether a listed number means the strain was received from that collection or merely that it is equivalent. And the History field is defined as "History of the strain before deposit into the DSMZ."

Three consequences, and they do not all point the same way.

1. **P8 resolves in the candidate's favour, on one side of the frame.** The record says P8 is "not resolved by anything opened" and that if collections only ever claim same-type or same-original-isolate, "a large divergence distribution refutes nothing the collections assert." That worry is now answered for NCIMB: the claim on the table is indistinguishability of the cultures. The question therefore has a real target.
2. **But the frame is semantically heterogeneous, and V2 does not stratify it.** V2 builds the frame from link EXISTENCE — "catalogue cross-reference or StrainInfo identifier matching" — while limb (b)'s interpretation needs link MEANING. The two collections I could read publish incompatible meanings: NCIMB asserts indistinguishability; DSMZ's own field guide asserts only that a number exists in another collection. A single pooled divergence distribution over a frame built from both link types does not test one claim. This is the objection I would press hardest, and it is not in the record's presupposition list in this form.
3. **P5 is worse than "contested": the catalogue field that records transfer history is on the wrong side of the deposit event.** Limb (c)'s primary covariate is transfers-SINCE-deposit. The DSMZ History field is defined as history BEFORE deposit. So the one publicly documented transfer-history field I checked cannot supply limb (c)'s variable, which converges with the record's own concession that prior (A) could not count transfers for the most documented strain in the world. I checked one collection's field guide, not all of them, and I say so.
