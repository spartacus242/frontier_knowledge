## Restatement (my words, before engaging the framing)

Take one pathology archive and one diagnosis. Re-diagnose every case from every decade under today's criteria, re-staining from the block, so that the label means the same thing for a 1975 case and a 2020 case. Then run one panel of molecular measurements, fixed in advance, on all of them in a single run with batch order randomised, and alongside them run controls chosen to be era-matched but not the tumour of interest: that same patient's own non-neoplastic tissue from the same block, and same-era blocks of unrelated diagnoses. Ask three things. (a) How much does the tumour's molecular profile differ by decade of accession? (b) How much decade signal do the controls show — that is the block-age/fixation artefact. (c) Is the era difference, after subtracting (b) and reported with an interval, bigger than the between-cohort molecular distance that historical- and external-control borrowing already treats as acceptable? Finding nothing above artefact at feasible sample size counts as an answer.

In one line: is a decade-of-accession effect on a fixed diagnosis's molecular interior distinguishable from the archive's own ageing, and is it big enough to matter for borrowing controls?

## Single strongest killing point

Limb (c) carries essentially all the claimed significance and is the one limb with no definition: the "between-cohort molecular distance that borrowing already tolerates" has never been published (the record concedes this), and the borrowing literature expresses tolerance on the outcome scale while (a)-net-of-(b) is a distance in molecular measurement space, with no established conversion — so the comparison that is supposed to make the answer matter must be invented by the answerer, and either answer to it can be engineered by choice of metric. Compounding it: (a)-net-of-(b) is identified only under an artefact-transport assumption the design measures nowhere, and the record's own pivotal neighbour is cited as evidence against it.
