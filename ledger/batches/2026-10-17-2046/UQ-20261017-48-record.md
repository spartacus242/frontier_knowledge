Question (canonical) — V0, as intaken:
In the model x benchmark score panel that underlies published AI capability trend series, is the pattern of missing cells ignorable given observed covariates — given that benchmarks enter the panel because they are hard for current models, leave it once they saturate, and are reported selectively by developers — and what are the magnitude and sign of the induced bias in the fitted trend's level and slope, such as an estimated capability doubling time?

Question (canonical) — R1, the one Gate 1 reformulation, ADOPTED (recorded alongside V0, not replacing it; the reformulation is for well-posedness only and adds no new object):
For ONE named, dated model x benchmark score panel published as the basis of a capability trend series — the Epoch Capabilities Index, ~40 benchmarks x ~200 models, which states it is "our ongoing public implementation of this approach" — with each cell's observation indicator R_ij recorded and each benchmark's first- and last-reported dates recovered:
 (0) does a unidimensional latent model fit the panel well enough for "the trend" to be a defined object (pre-step, not assumed);
 (a) IGNORABILITY TEST: does Pr(R_ij = 1) depend on the fitted latent capability theta_i and the fitted benchmark difficulty b_j after conditioning on a pre-declared covariate set Z = {model release date, developer, open/closed weights, benchmark age, benchmark licence/access, citation count, evaluation cost};
 (b) SENSITIVITY: under a pre-declared family of MNAR departures — a shared-parameter selection model with sensitivity parameter gamma on theta, and a pattern-mixture alternative indexed by the mean shift delta between observed and unobserved cells — what are the SIGNED changes in the fitted theta-vs-date trend's intercept and slope, expressed as a change in the implied doubling time, with Manski-style worst-case bounds;
 (c) DECISION RULE: does that change exceed the series' own reported uncertainty?

Alternative formulations:
1. Do AI progress curves bend because models improved, or because benchmarks were replaced whenever they stopped discriminating?
2. Is the benchmark panel behind published capability indices missing-at-random, and what does non-ignorable missingness do to the AI timelines extrapolated from it?

PRIOR 2 — Parker Whitfill, "Note on Selection Bias in Observational Estimates of Algorithmic Progress", arXiv:2508.11033 (August 2025), https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11033v1 (OPENED).
 UNIT: a language-model training run (loss, parameter count, dataset size, year), from Ho et al. 2024. No benchmark dimension at all.
 ENDPOINT: cross-entropy training loss; derived quantity is the annual rate of algorithmic progress beta_year/beta.
 COMPARATOR: a fixed reference year and the loss-at-fixed-compute trajectory; experimentally estimated scaling exponents (Hoffmann et al. 2022, Besiroglu et al. 2024, beta ~ 0.37) as the reference for beta.
 ESTIMAND: the SIGNED bias in the estimated progress rate from endogenous compute/data choice. Theorem 1: bias sign = -sign Cov(ln D, eps_D); positive selection underestimates, negative selection overestimates. Simulations: a true 45% rate is estimated at 16.5% under correlation 0.5 and 93% under -0.5, with the author cautioning "the exact mangitudes should not be taken too seriously". First-pass empirical claim: progress overstated by roughly a factor of nine (beta ~ 0.04 observational vs ~0.37 experimental), which the author calls "very much a first-pass argument". Framing is endogeneity/omitted variables ("the familiar omitted variable problem in econometrics"); remedies are experiments or instruments. No corrected doubling time is reported.
