## Restatement (my own words, not the record's)

Take published protein-engineering campaigns that run in rounds, where each round screens a batch of
variants with at least two replicate measurements each, reports how many were screened and how many
were kept, and builds the next batch out of the kept ones (mutagenesis, recombination, or a surrogate
model sampling nearby sequence space; fully generative redesign excluded).

Two questions. (a) Can you forecast, using only round r's own numbers, how much the *average*
measured activity of the library will rise from round r to round r+1, using the animal-breeding
formula "gain = selection intensity x accuracy of selection x among-variant standard deviation",
where accuracy is sqrt(assay repeatability) when you select on the assay itself and the model's
prediction-vs-measurement correlation when you select on a model? Score it as a calibration slope,
a bias, and interval coverage. (b) Given the same total number of wells, would the breeding-theory
optimal split between "how many variants" and "how many replicates each" differ by >=20% of
predicted gain from what the campaign actually did?

In short: does the breeder's equation forecast per-round library-mean gain in protein campaigns, and
are campaigns spending their wells on the wrong margin? Note what (a) is *not*: it is not about the
best variant found, which is what these papers actually report.
