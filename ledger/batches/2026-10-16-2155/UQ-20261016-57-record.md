Domain(s): Biology — experimental evolution / microbial evolution (target field). Method transplanted from community ecology (competitive intransitivity) and paired-comparison / tournament statistics (Bradley-Terry, Elo, Hodge/HHD decomposition).

Question (canonical, Gate 0 as intaken):
In a microbial long-term evolution experiment with an archived clone series, does the matrix of pairwise competitive-fitness measurements among archived clones admit a consistent one-dimensional ordering — how often are triples intransitive, and at what magnitudes relative to assay error — and does the published power-law deceleration of fitness gain survive re-estimation under a model that does not assume fitness is a scalar?

Question (Gate 1 reformulation — the one permitted reformulation, used only for well-posedness; both versions stand on the record):
For a pre-registered round-robin set of archived clones drawn from one or more LTEE populations across generations (>=1 clone per sampled time point, every clone competed against every other, each pair assayed at >=2 starting frequencies, >=3 replicates), and with a propagated per-assay error model estimated from replicate competitions:
  (a) what share of the C(n,3) triples is cyclic under a pre-specified decision rule, and what is the distribution of cycle magnitudes (in selection-rate units) against the replicate-error null;
  (b) what fraction of the variance in the fitted pairwise selection-coefficient matrix is carried by the skew-symmetric (cyclic) component rather than the gradient/ranking component under a Hodge-style decomposition;
  (c) when the same competition data are fit (i) as the standard ancestor-referenced power law and (ii) as ranking-plus-cyclic components, what are the pre-registered deltas in fitted exponent and in projected asymptote?

Alternative formulations:
- Is 'fitness' in an evolution experiment a scalar, or a projection of a competition tournament onto one axis, and what does that projection do to the measured shape of adaptation?
- Transplanting the measurement of competitive intransitivity from community ecology to a frozen fossil record: what share of archived clone triples violate transitivity, and does the fitted trajectory law change when the cyclic component is modelled rather than discarded?

Motivating gap (as intaken, and as corrected by Gate 2):
As intaken: published LTEE fitness trajectories (the power-law fit) are built from ancestor-referenced head-to-head assays, a design that presupposes a single scalar fitness axis; non-transitive and frequency-dependent fitness has been reported since Paquin & Adams (1983); a 2025 preprint asserts frequency dependence is the general case; and nothing was found that measures a transitivity-violation RATE over an archived series or refits a trajectory law without a scalar.
CORRECTION ESTABLISHED AT GATE 2: the premise that the deceleration has never been re-examined without a scalar/transitive fitness axis is false. de Visser & Lenski (2002), the eleventh paper of the LTEE series, is titled "Rejection of non-transitive interactions as cause of declining rate of adaptation", builds an explicitly non-transitive alternative model of the trajectory, breaks the single-reference convention by competing every sampled time point against five reference clones from five different generations, and reports the non-transitive model rejected. The intake's own Gate 2 partial record missed it; one query in the field's own words surfaced it as the first hit. Separately, the 2025 preprint that the intake recorded as COULD NOT READ was opened here (bioRxiv PDF route) and does test additive transitivity across strain triads at four starting frequencies, so it is a near prior on limb (a) rather than mere background.

Presuppositions:
1. Archived clones are revivable and comparable across time points — ESTABLISHED (frozen LTEE collections; de Visser & Lenski 2002 competed 21 time-point samples from -80 C glycerol stocks).
2. Pairwise competitive fitness is measurable at a precision finer than the triple-violation magnitudes of interest — CONTESTED, and now partly quantified against real numbers: de Visser & Lenski report residual error ~5% of variance on ln-fitness with the sample x reference-clone interaction under 2% (P > 0.5); Ascensao et al. report measurement error at ~40% of the variance in their frequency-dependence slope. So the error term is of the same order as, or larger than, the structure sought, and the honest deliverable is more likely a bound than a rate.
3. A tournament model supplies a meaningful alternative target — PARTLY CONTESTED and correctly flagged at intake: Bradley-Terry "strength" is itself scalar, so the design must fit ranking plus an explicit skew-symmetric component and report each one's variance share. The machinery exists and is named outside biology (Hodge/HHD tournament decomposition; Elo under intransitivity), which is where the transplant's only real residue lies.
4. Intransitivity is not wholly an artefact of a single starting frequency — CONTESTED, and now substantially undercut as a gap: Ascensao et al. already assay four starting frequencies (~1%, 20%, 80%, 99%) on LTEE-background strains and find ~75-80% of competitions significantly frequency-dependent, with the transitivity deviations reported in that same frequency-resolved design.
5. The published trajectory law is sensitive to non-scalar structure at the magnitudes measured — NOT ESTABLISHED, and the one place where the candidate's framing survives contact with the literature: the 2002 rejection was aimed at the hyperbolic deceleration over 20,000 generations, not at the Wiser et al. (2013) power-law exponent and asymptote over 50,000+ generations, and no skew-symmetric variance share has been computed for any of it.

What would count as an answer:
A measured distribution of triple-violation magnitudes from a pre-registered round-robin of archived clones within and across replicate populations and time points, set against a propagated assay-error null; plus two fits of the same trajectory data — the standard ancestor-referenced power law, and a decomposition into transitive-ranking plus cyclic components — reporting the cyclic component's share of fitness variance and the resulting change in fitted exponent and projected asymptote. A null (violations indistinguishable from assay error, exponent unmoved) is a positive result for the scalar-fitness convention. NOTE: this answer specification is largely satisfied in weaker form already — limb (b) by de Visser & Lenski's model-rejection test, limb (a) by Ascensao et al.'s triad-additivity test — which is why the candidate fails Gate 2 rather than Gate 1.

P1. de Visser, J.A.G. & Lenski, R.E. (2002) "Long-term experimental evolution in Escherichia coli. XI. Rejection of non-transitive interactions as cause of declining rate of adaptation." BMC Evolutionary Biology 2:19. doi:10.1186/1471-2148-2-19 — https://bmcecolevol.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2148-2-19
  UNIT OF ANALYSIS: 21 frozen population samples of LTEE line Ara-1, one per 1,000 generations, 0-20,000 (whole-population samples, not single clones).
  ENDPOINT: ln relative fitness from 315 head-to-head competitions (21 x 5 x 3); tests = the sample x reference-clone interaction term in a two-way ANOVA on ln-fitness, plus a comparison of cumulative ln-fitness gains with each model's prediction.
  REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: five neutrally marked Ara+ reference clones from generations 0, 5,000, 10,000, 15,000 and 20,000 — i.e. the design deliberately does NOT use the ancestor as sole reference.
  ESTIMAND: whether a non-transitive model of competitive interactions can account for the observed deceleration in the rate of fitness gain, versus a transitive model matched to the same ancestor-referenced trajectory.
  RESULT: interaction term < 2% of variation, not significant (P > 0.5), residual error ~5%; non-transitive model rejected; "non-transitive effects are either absent or much too weak to account for the pronounced overall deceleration in the rate of fitness gains"; "Non-transitive competitive interactions were not a major feature of evolution in this population." No triple count, no round-robin clone matrix, no varied starting frequency, no Bradley-Terry or skew-symmetric decomposition, and the hyperbolic curve was not itself re-fit.
P2. "Frequency-dependent fitness effects are ubiquitous." bioRxiv doi:10.1101/2025.08.18.670924 (Ascensao, Abedi, Prasad, Hallatschek) — https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.18.670924.full.pdf
  UNIT OF ANALYSIS: reconstructed mutants on the LTEE ancestral background REL606 — five early-LTEE beneficial mutations (G, R, P, S, T) as singles and in double combinations; n = 4-8 biological replicates per competition. NOT archived evolved clones and not a time series.
  ENDPOINT: per-cycle selection coefficient as the slope of logit-transformed frequency over two growth cycles, measured at four starting frequencies (~1%, 20%, 80%, 99%); plus an explicit additive-transitivity test across strain triads, s_ij = s_ik - s_jk, under eight definitions (two shown in the main text).
  REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: REL606 and each other strain in the set (all pairwise within the constructed panel).
  ESTIMAND: the prevalence of frequency dependence (~75% of competitions significant at FDR-corrected p < 0.01; "approximately 80%" in the abstract; 3/5 single mutants vs 27/30 double-mutant competitions) and the magnitude of departure from additive transitivity (deviations typically around +/-20%, significant in several strain combinations). Measurement error ~40% of the variance in the frequency-dependence slope. No fraction-of-cyclic-triples statistic, no tournament/skew-symmetric decomposition, no trajectory-law re-fit, no discussion of the LTEE power law.
P3. "Adaptive evolution of nontransitive fitness in yeast." bioRxiv doi:10.1101/700302 (authorship not confirmed from the document — see COULD NOT READ above) — https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/700302.full.pdf
  UNIT OF ANALYSIS: three clones (Early, Intermediate, Late) along the line of descent of one yeast population (BYS1-D08) from a 1,000-generation evolution experiment; killer phenotype assayed in 142 populations, viral genomes from 67.

  REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: each other clone along the same lineage, plus killer-cured derivatives.

RESULT: one explicit ordering violation (Intermediate > Early, Late > Intermediate, yet Late often loses to Early), bistable and frequency-dependent (Late beats Early only when started at high frequency), mechanistically a nuclear-genome/killer-virus multilevel-selection interaction. Significance — consequence:
If cyclic structure is large: ancestor-referenced relative fitness is a projection that discards a measurable component, and every quantity built on it — the power-law exponent, the unbounded-versus-asymptotic adaptation argument, cross-population fitness comparisons — inherits an unmodelled bias term. If it is small (which P1 already reports for the deceleration, and which P2's +/-20% triad deviations complicate at the level of individual pairs), the convention is licensed and the field gains a published bound rather than a working assumption. The asymmetry matters less than it looks precisely because P1 already published the null for the trajectory: the marginal consequence of the candidate is a tighter, modern, power-law-era version of a result the field already acts on.

Significance — audience:
Experimental-evolution laboratories that report ancestor-referenced relative fitness (LTEE and its descendants, yeast and Pseudomonas evolution groups) would change their reporting only if the cyclic share were non-trivial; theorists fitting trajectory laws (power law, macroscopic-epistasis models) would gain or lose a nuisance parameter; community ecologists and tournament statisticians would gain a new application domain. The audience is real but has already received the headline answer once, which caps how differently it would act.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: does cyclic structure grow or shrink with divergence time between clones; is it concentrated in the ecologically differentiated populations (the known frequency-dependent polymorphisms); does the cyclic share predict long-term coexistence within a population. These remain askable, and P2's mechanism (resource-depletion-time feedback explaining 10-50% of frequency dependence) gives them a handle. Reduced fecundity, though, because the most generative version of the question was already asked in 2002.

Significance — anchoring:
Touches the open argument over whether LTEE fitness gains are unbounded (power-law asymptote), the macroscopic-epistasis explanation of deceleration (Good & Desai, arXiv 1408.4108), the status of frequency dependence as the general case (P2), and the coexistence role of intransitivity in ecology (P4).
