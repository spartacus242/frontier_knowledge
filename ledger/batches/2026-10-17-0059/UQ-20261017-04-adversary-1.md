## Restatement (my own words)

Take every ordered experimental inorganic crystal-structure entry that carries a measurement temperature. Compute harmonic phonons with a named universal MLIP on the *exact deposited coordinates and cell* (no relaxation), with some declared finite-temperature correction, and raise a flag when imaginary modes survive an artefact screen. How well does that flag pick out the entries crystallographers themselves later found wrong (corrected, retracted, re-solved at lower symmetry, or shown to be a space-averaged model of a superstructure) when compared against matched controls that were independently confirmed at the same symmetry? What is the instrument's own error rate in this regime, measured by DFT on a subsample? And does the flag add anything to what a checkCIF-type report already says about the same cases?

## Recommended verdict

Provisional. No attack Unresolved; attacks 4, 6 and 8 Partial; novelty Medium holds with a close neighbour (prior A). Promotion conditions: (1) add two comparators to the incremental-value limb — residual force/stress magnitude at the deposited geometry, and the same MLIP phonon flag after relaxation — so the lattice-dynamical content is isolated; (2) pre-specify an energy-derived-force uMLIP (MatterSim/MACE class per arXiv:2412.16551), since direct-force models give 85-93% spurious instability even at relaxed geometries; (3) verify P6 against the full PLATON test list, not only the IUCr category page; (4) resolve the tension between the "unrelaxed" frame and any sampling-based finite-temperature effective-harmonic treatment.

Strongest killing point: the instrument is defined outside its own validity domain — the phonon tool's documentation requires relaxation "so that the residual forces, and the residual stress on the lattice, vanish", the DFT subsample at the same geometry cannot repair that, and the best current benchmark (relaxed geometries) already shows the imaginary-mode rate swings from 5% to 93% with the choice of universal MLIP, so the flag risks measuring the potential rather than the structure.
