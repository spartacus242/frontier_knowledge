## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

In most invertebrates the big ribosomal RNA subunit is physically in two halves, so on an electrophoretic
trace their *intact* RNA shows no 28S peak and a doubled 18S-position peak. The Bioanalyzer's RIN is a
classifier trained on profiles that always have a 28S peak. The candidate asks two separable things:

(a) **Did the mis-scoring cost anything that can be counted?** In a frame of RNA submissions with a recorded
integrity score and a recorded accept / reject / re-extract decision, is there a break-correlated excess of
rejections and re-extractions, and (secondary, weaker) do break-bearing taxa have fewer transcriptomes at all,
once effort, body size, preservation and protocol are accounted for?

(b) **What is the exchange rate?** In matched RNA from one break-bearing and one break-free species degraded
in the same graded series, what RIN in the break-bearing taxon corresponds to the same *true* degradation
(DV200 / spike-in fragment length / 3' bias) as RIN 7 in the break-free one — i.e. by how much should a
facility move its cutoff?

Limb (b)'s *metric* half is admitted by the record to be an asked-but-open problem; the novelty claim rests on
limb (a) (the realized deficit) and on (b)'s empirical mapping.
