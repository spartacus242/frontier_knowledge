## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Take the magnitude number an official agency had actually published by some fixed cutoff after an earthquake (the moment a contract or an automated rule reads it). Treat that number as a prediction of the number the same agency ends up standing behind later. Two things are asked. (a) Is that prediction *honest in the statistical sense* — does it miss high and low equally, and is the miss unguessable from everything already on the table at the cutoff (depth, region, how many stations saw it, which magnitude formula was used, local time, the size of the number itself)? (b) Taking the real, publicly documented list of parametric insurance contracts and automated response rules that fire off that published number, how many of those fire/don't-fire decisions would have gone the other way had the later number been used instead, and in which direction?

So: limb (a) is a bias-and-predictability test on a published statistic's own revision; limb (b) is a counted counterfactual over an enumerated decision population. Neither is "do magnitudes change" (everyone knows they do) nor "is the rapid algorithm accurate" (that is a different instrument).
