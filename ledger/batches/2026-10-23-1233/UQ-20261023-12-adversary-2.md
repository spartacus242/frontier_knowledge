## 1. Restatement in my own words

Calibration labs decide how often to recall an instrument by fitting a curve — probability still in tolerance against time elapsed since the last calibration — to the as-found results of the instruments that came back, then picking the interval where that curve crosses a declared target (e.g. 85% or 95%). That fit treats each returned instrument's as-found reading as a fair draw from the drift distribution at the elapsed time it happened to arrive at. The candidate asks two things about one real fleet whose records carry the needed fields: (a) once you hold elapsed time, instrument model and a usage proxy fixed, is *when an instrument shows up* statistically independent of *how far out it is*; and (b) if it is not, how many days — and what percent of the current interval — does the uncorrected fit move the interval that hits the declared target, and in which direction. Early arrivals of instruments somebody already distrusts would load excess error at short elapsed times; instruments that look fine and run overdue would load clean readings at long elapsed times. A measured null inside a declared equivalence band counts as an answer.
