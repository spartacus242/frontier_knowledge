Question (canonical): For q in {2,3,5}, with monic polynomials of degree n ordered by the base-q value of their coefficient string (constant term least significant) and two monic irreducibles of degree n called consecutive when none lies between them in that order, is delta(a,a;m,n,q) = P(a,a)/(N_pairs/phi(m)^2) - 1 systematically negative for monic m of degree 1-3, as the Lemke Oliver-Soundararajan deficit in Z is reported to be, how does it behave as n grows at fixed q, and do its sign and magnitude change when the same irreducible set is re-ordered by the reverse digit significance (leading coefficient least significant)? [This is the Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted one; the submitted wording is the first alternative formulation below.]
Alternative formulations:
1. (As submitted at Gate 0.) In F_q[t], with monics of each degree ordered by their coefficient string and consecutive irreducibles defined by that order, does the pair of residue classes of consecutive irreducibles mod a fixed monic m show the same signed under-representation of equal classes found for consecutive primes mod q, or is the bias absent?
2. Is the consecutive-prime residue bias a consequence of local k-tuple correlations alone, or does it require the archimedean ordering of Z, as decided by the function-field model where the needed correlation statements are theorems rather than conjectures?
3. For monic irreducibles over a finite field, do successive irreducibles repeat their class modulo a fixed monic less often than chance, and how does the deficit behave as the degree grows?
