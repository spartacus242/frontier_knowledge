Question (canonical, V2 — Gate 1 reformulation, the one permitted; V1 below):
For a pre-declared gene set, a pre-declared isoform-individuation rule (splice-junction-chain identity at a declared 5'/3' end tolerance), and >= 2 independent deep long-read libraries per tissue: treating each library as an incomplete sample from that gene's set of distinct spliced-isoform classes, (a) what does a false-positive-aware richness estimator — coverage-based rarefaction/extrapolation and Chao/Good-Turing extended with a contamination term calibrated per read class from spike-in synthetic transcripts and technical replicates — give for the number of unobserved REAL isoforms per gene, with intervals; (b) does the estimator's prediction of how many new isoforms a further independent library will reveal match a held-out library; and (c) is the fitted per-gene asymptote finite and depth-stable across all admissible contamination models, or does no admissible model close the curve — in which case "isoforms per gene" is a depth-indexed quantity and not an estimand at all?

Question (V1, as intaked): Treating a long-read transcriptome as an incomplete sample from a community of distinct spliced isoforms, does the per-gene isoform repertoire have a finite asymptote, and what do false-positive-aware richness estimators (coverage-based rarefaction, Chao/Good-Turing extended with a measured contamination term) give for the number of unobserved real isoforms per gene?

Reason for the V1 -> V2 reformulation (well-posedness only): V1 left three things undeclared that decide whether the question has an answer at all — the individuation rule (which changes the count by large factors), the predictive falsification test, and the disjunctive form of the finiteness limb. V2 adds no new content; it fixes the estimand, names the falsifier, and makes the "not an estimand" branch an admissible answer rather than a failure.

Alternative formulations:
- Is "the number of isoforms of a gene" an estimable quantity with a finite asymptote, or an unbounded function of sequencing depth?
- What is the unseen-isoform richness of a human gene once single-observation reads are partitioned into genuinely rare isoforms and technical artefacts?

Motivating gap:
Every large long-read transcriptome study adds thousands of novel isoforms dominated by single-observation transcripts, and the discovery curve does not visibly close. The field's own 2025 review of this instability (Genome Research 35(4):583, opened for this record) states plainly that "sampling depth will increase and many novel transcripts will be captured," that transcripts addable to reference sets might rise "perhaps by many millions," and that its own SIRV-based artefact estimate ("between 10% and 15% of the sequencing output") "only represents a possible upper limit" — and contains no richness estimator, no rarefaction, no asymptote, and no per-gene unobserved count. Meanwhile the interpretive frame (splicing noise vs functional diversity; Pickrell et al., PLoS Genetics 2010 — NOT OPENED here) is argued qualitatively while annotation bodies must decide what to admit. Statistical ecology solved the structurally identical problem (richness from an incomplete sample dominated by singletons) with explicit assumptions. The interface is not a pastiche: in transcriptomes the singletons are inflated by a separately measurable technical process (reverse-transcription template switching, intrapriming, intron retention, degraded 5' ends), which directly violates the estimators' "singletons are rare but real" assumption, so the transplant REQUIRES a contamination-aware estimator rather than an off-the-shelf one. The strongest evidence that the gap is live rather than imagined: the transplant has already been made in this exact field at Hill order q=1 (Schertzer et al., per-gene isoform "perplexity") and stopped there — order q=0, the richness order, is precisely the order that false singletons destroy, and that paper removes 7.9 million collapsed isoforms (81% single-read) by threshold rather than modelling them.

Presuppositions (each marked):
1. Distinct isoforms are individuable from long reads at a declared tolerance. CONTESTED, and declared rather than assumed: collapsing and splice-site-matching rules change the count by large factors. V2 pre-declares the rule and the answer is conditional on it.
2. The per-read-class technical false-positive rate can be estimated independently (spike-in synthetic transcripts, technical replicates). LOAD-BEARING, and only PARTIALLY SUPPORTED by what was opened: the Genome Research review's SIRV-derived figure is explicitly "a possible upper limit," so the contamination term may be identifiable only as an interval. V2 absorbs this by asking for stability across admissible contamination models rather than a point estimate; if even interval identification fails, the question is not answerable as posed.
3. Reads are near-independent draws after duplicate handling. CONTESTED for PCR-based cDNA protocols; direct-RNA is cleaner but shallower. Affects which estimator family is admissible, not whether the question is posed.
4. The real per-gene repertoire is finite. NOT ASSUMED — this is the question's own limb (c), posed as a hypothesis with a stated negative branch.
5. "Real" is definable without reference to function. DECLARED OPERATIONALLY: reproducible across independent libraries and donors.
6. Richness estimators retain informative behaviour at per-gene sample sizes. SMALL-SAMPLE RISK, FLAGGED: at per-gene counts Chao-type estimators may degenerate to a weak lower bound. A finding that they are uninformative per gene and informative only pooled is an answer, not a failure, but it blunts the practical consequence.
7. Available datasets have enough independent deep libraries per tissue for a held-out falsification test. Plausible for human brain/heart/K562-class data; must be verified before committing to a gene set.

What would count as an answer:
Fitted sample-coverage curves plus an estimated unobserved-real-isoform count per gene with confidence intervals, under a pre-declared contamination model calibrated from spike-ins and technical replicates per read class, for a pre-declared gene set across >= 2 independent deep long-read datasets per tissue; PLUS a falsification test in which the estimator's prediction of how many new isoforms one further independent held-out library will reveal is checked against that library. The question is answered either by corrected curves that close (a finite, depth-stable asymptote per gene) or by a demonstration that they close under no admissible contamination model — which would mean the field's "isoforms per gene" statistic is not an estimand and annotation must report a depth-indexed quantity instead. Both branches are reportable; neither is a null result.

PRIOR 1 — Schertzer MD, Park SH, Su J, Reese F, Sheynkman GM, Knowles DA. "Perplexity as a Metric for Isoform Diversity in the Human Transcriptome." bioRxiv, DOI 10.1101/2025.07.02.662769 (page lists 2026; preprint, no journal named). OPENED: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.02.662769.full.pdf
- UNIT OF ANALYSIS: the gene (also protein-coding transcript and ORF level); 124 samples.
- ENDPOINT: effective number of isoforms per gene (perplexity = exp(Shannon entropy) of observed isoform proportions), its evenness (perplexity / "potential"), and reproducibility across 5 K562 replicates.
- REFERENCE/COMPARATOR: observed isoform counts ("potential", i.e. Hill order q=0 of the OBSERVED set) and counts surviving global TPM thresholds of 0.1/1/3/5.
- ESTIMAND: the order-1 Hill number of the sample-averaged observed isoform-usage distribution per gene — explicitly a property of the OBSERVED repertoire, not of the population.
This is the closest prior found and it is in the candidate's own field, with the same unit (gene) and the same transplanted framework family (Hill numbers from ecology). It is nevertheless a different question, and says so: it does not model a finite asymptote, runs no rarefaction or extrapolation, uses no Chao or Good-Turing estimator, states only that the catalogue "almost certainly underestimates" diversity without modelling that, and CONSIDERED entropy bias corrections (Miller-Madow, Nemenman-Shafee-Bialek) and declined to apply them. Its artefact handling is a threshold, not a model: isoforms need >= 10 reads across 124 samples, removing ~7.9 million collapsed isoforms of which 81% are single-read — i.e. it DISCARDS exactly the single-observation class the candidate proposes to partition into real-rare and artefactual. It also argues order-1 perplexity is insensitive to those singletons while "potential" would be inflated by them — which is an argument for why order 0 is hard, not an answer at order 0.

- ENDPOINT: total richness C = observed non-singletons + predicted f1 + unobserved f0, with a standard error.

Significance — consequence:
If YES (finite, depth-stable, contamination-robust asymptote per gene): the field gains a per-gene completeness statistic and a stopping rule. "We have observed 60% of this gene's repertoire" becomes sayable, sequencing depth becomes plannable against a target rather than a budget, and the arbitrary filters now in use (e.g. >= 10 reads across 124 samples, which discarded ~7.9M collapsed isoforms in PRIOR 1) can be replaced by thresholds justified against an estimated contamination rate. Negative clinical calls ("no pathogenic splice isoform detected") acquire a quantifiable completeness caveat.
If NO (no admissible contamination model closes the curve): the headline statistic "N isoforms per gene" is not an estimand, and annotation databases, long-read method papers and isoform-level benchmarks are reporting a function of their own depth and protocol as a property of biology. That forces depth-indexed reporting — a change in what GENCODE/RefSeq-style resources publish, and a reinterpretation of every cross-tissue or cross-species "isoform diversity" comparison made at unequal depth.
Either branch is informative, and the held-out predictive test means the estimator cannot pass by fitting alone.

Significance — audience:
(1) Reference-annotation consortia (GENCODE/Ensembl, RefSeq) — would gain a completeness statistic to report alongside transcript counts, and in the negative branch would have to change the form of what they publish. (2) Long-read method and benchmark authors (LRGASP, SQANTI, TALON, IsoQuant, bambu, FLAIR) — precision is currently measured against a reference annotation that is itself depth-limited; a per-gene completeness estimate changes the denominator of recall. (3) Design of large long-read studies (ENCODE4, GTEx long-read, population isoform atlases) — depth and replicate planning against an estimated target rather than convention. (4) Clinical RNA diagnostics — the strength of a negative isoform finding. (5) Statistical ecology / diversity-estimation methodologists — a new application class with a measurable false-positive process, which their own FP-robust estimators currently handle by discarding singletons rather than modelling them.
Why this is argued and not asserted: PRIOR 1 and PRIOR 2 both demonstrate the audience acting on this quantity already — one by choosing a read-count threshold that deletes 7.9 million candidate isoforms, the other by reporting an artefact share as an explicit upper limit while declining to convert it into an estimate. These are decisions made under exactly the uncertainty the candidate proposes to quantify.

Significance — fecundity:
Per-gene completeness becomes a covariate: does low completeness explain the known irreproducibility of isoform-level quantification and isoform eQTL calls? Coverage-standardised comparison replaces depth-confounded comparison across tissues, species and single-cell vs bulk. A principled filter threshold replaces arbitrary read cutoffs. The same machinery extends to TSS/TES repertoires, to allele-specific isoform sets, and to the question of whether "splicing noise" is a fixed per-gene rate or a depth artefact. In the negative branch, a new question becomes askable: what IS the right depth-indexed annotation statistic, and how should it be standardised?

Reason for silence:
A disciplinary boundary that runs exactly along the estimand, plus a missing method, plus recent data — not inattention.
(1) BOUNDARY: transcriptomics frames novel isoforms as a PRECISION problem — is this transcript model real? — and has built its entire toolchain around that per-transcript question (SQANTI structural categories, FSM/non-FSM classification against a reference, AIDE's "high precision", replicate-based filtering). "How many are there, including the ones I did not see?" is a different question with a different mathematics, and nothing in the field's instrumentation asks it. The statistical-ecology community that owns the estimators writes in OTU/microbiome vocabulary (PRIOR 5's note cites HMP, EMP and soil datasets and never mentions RNA) and has no stake in genome annotation.
(2) MISSING METHOD: the off-the-shelf estimators assume singletons are rare-but-real, which is false here by a separately measurable mechanism, and the one FP-robust variant that exists discards f1 rather than ingesting a measured artefact rate — and is an unpublished note whose author declined to publish it and advised against firm conclusions from it. So the bridge the candidate needs has not existed in citable, usable form.
(3) POSITIVE EVIDENCE THAT THE BLOCK IS CONCEPTUAL, NOT ACCIDENTAL: the field DID transplant the ecological diversity framework to per-gene isoform repertoires (PRIOR 1, Hill numbers) and stopped at order q=1, arguing explicitly that order 1 is insensitive to single-read isoforms while observed richness would be inflated by them. The field reached the edge of this question, saw that the richness order is the one false singletons destroy, and moved away from it rather than solving it.
(4) DATA: >= 2 independent deep long-read libraries per tissue with spike-ins and documented replicate structure are only recently available at the scale a held-out falsification test needs.

Suggested first step toward an answer (pointer only — not taken): before any estimator is fitted, open LRGASP and establish whether spike-in-calibrated per-read-class false-positive rates are already published at the granularity the contamination term requires; if they are, the question's load-bearing presupposition is satisfied from existing data and the first real step is a feasibility audit of which tissues have >= 3 independent deep libraries (2 for fitting, 1 held out).
