## Restatement in my own words

Take the WHO International Standards that have been replaced at least twice (so there are three or more generations) and whose collaborative-study reports can still be obtained. At each replacement the study measures how potent the new candidate material is relative to the outgoing standard, and then a committee assigns the new standard a unitage. Three things are then asked:

(a) multiply together, across all the replacements in a chain, the small gap between what the study measured and what was actually written on the label (the gap left by convention and by rounding the label to a tidy number);
(b) combine, across the same replacements, the per-study scatter figures that the "continuity" convention throws away rather than carrying forward into the new standard's stated value;
(c) ask whether (a) and (b) together are bigger than the slack in clinical cut-offs and pharmacopoeial potency limits that are written in IU, and bigger than the ordinary run-to-run reproducibility of the assays used to apply those numbers.

In plain terms: has the meaning of "1 IU" wandered by a bookkeeping amount nobody has ever added up, and if so is the wander big enough to matter at the bedside? The generator concedes that the obvious version of this is zero by construction, and so re-aims the question at the paperwork residue plus the discarded error bars.

### What the Gray deck actually says (fetched, slide 17)

All generations assayed **against the 5th IS in 2008**:

| Standard | Material / year | Labelled IU/mg | Anti-Xa (95% CL) | Anti-IIa (95% CL) | APTT (95% CL) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st IS | Bovine lung, 1941 | 130 | 136 (128–144) | 145 (133–157) | 140 (131–149) |
| 2nd IS | Bovine lung, 1956 | 130 | 135 (127–144) | 136 (125–146) | 139 (131–147) |
| 3rd IS | Porcine mucosa, 1973 | 173 | 176 (161–191) | 178 (161–195) | 173 (160–186) |

So each generation **recovers its own labelled value** to within +0% to +11.5% (1st IS +4.6% to +11.5%; 2nd IS +3.8% to +6.9%; 3rd IS 0% to +2.9%) after up to four replacements and 67 years. The record states that Gray shows "the 3rd IS reading about 173 IU/mg against about 130 IU/mg — ... exactly the generation-level non-continuity the candidate predicts." That is a misreading: 173 is the 3rd IS's **own labelled value**, and the 130→173 step is a change of source material (bovine lung → porcine mucosa) accompanied by a relabelling, not an undetected drift. The deck's only interpretive statement is that International Standards "should not impact the dosing and product labelling."

## Single strongest killing point

The record's own strongest cited evidence says the opposite of what the record reports. Gray's GaBI deck, fetched and quoted above, is the field performing the all-generations retrospective re-assay against the current standard — and it shows the 1941, 1956 and 1973 heparin standards recovering their own labelled potencies to within +0% to +11.5% with 95% confidence limits, i.e. continuity holding to within ordinary assay reproducibility across the longest chain in the portfolio. The record reads the 130→173 IU/mg step as "exactly the generation-level non-continuity the candidate predicts" when 173 is the 3rd IS's own label and the step is a change of source material with a relabelling. Correcting that misreading removes the motivating anomaly, supplies the empirical answer for the candidate's strongest case, and shows the field already owns a direct estimator that strictly dominates the proposed documentary reconstruction.
