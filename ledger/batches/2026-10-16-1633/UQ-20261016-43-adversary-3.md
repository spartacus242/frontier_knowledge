## 1. Restatement in my own words

Take every published OER durability run that fits a pre-fixed frame (pH class, current-density band, cell type). For each run record (a) how long the author ran it and (b) whether a pre-fixed overpotential-increase threshold was crossed before the author stopped. Then ask: (i) do the stop times pile up on round numbers (10/12/24/100/500/1000 h) and how much; (ii) if the runs are treated as right-censored lifetimes, is the Kaplan-Meier/Weibull fit non-degenerate and what does it say; (iii) do authors stop sooner when the curve is visibly degrading at the end (informative censoring), tested as "end-of-run degradation rate predicts stop time, given design covariates"; and (iv) given the fitted distribution, how many hours does a test need to separate two catalysts at 80% power. In short: are "stable for 100 h" claims statements about catalysts or about when the field stops looking, and can the corpus be re-read as censored life data?

## 3. Evidence extracted from opened sources (quoted lines)

Spöri et al. 2017 (F3): "We summarize the most frequently occurring test parameters, reported in the literature in frequency histograms" — for "cell temperature, experiment runtime, current density, choice of galvano- or potentio-static modes" (histograms in SI, not opened). Finding stated in the main text: "the majority of published reports have focused on operation at 80 °C, 1-2 A cm-2 in galvanostatic mode" with a runtime of "either 24 or 100 h"; "the stability test parameters are extremely varied with little to no justification"; "only about a third of the PEM tests reported in the literature give additional analysis"; "stability itself, in turn, can therefore be strongly dependent on the time of measurement". Table 3 compiles runtimes from 2 h (McCrory benchmarking protocol) to 60,000-80,000 h. Scope: the 80 °C / 1-2 A cm-2 conditions are PEM-electrolyzer single-cell tests, so the runtime histogram is for that subset; number of publications surveyed not stated in the main text I could read.

Graniero et al. 2023 (F1): Perovskite Database "more than 15,000 publications", about 42,400 devices; "Only 1,834 of those devices report a measured T80"; Fig. 1 caption: "The vast majority of devices reported, 1,790 out of 1,834, have a short lifetime of at most 2,000 h." On censoring: "we could use the PCE at the end of the stability experiments and the length of such experiments" to estimate T80, but "the wide variety of aging behaviors would make such extrapolation highly uncertain." Reporting bias: "the low statistical relevance of data entries which report only the best-performing devices." No histogram of test durations, no survival model, no test of stopping vs observed degradation.

Knöppel et al. post (F4): stability in aqueous cells is, "in comparison to practical application, gravely underestimated"; if aqueous-cell estimates were right, known OER catalysts "would fully degrade in several hours to a year at a typical current density of 1 A mgIr-1"; same iridium oxide shows a discrepancy of "several orders of magnitude" (S-number) between aqueous SFC-ICP-MS and MEA; drivers named: "a likely higher pH in MEA than generally accepted in the literature" and "a stabilization of the MEA after several hours of operation time"; the post offers "a critical view about the relevance of widespread aqueous model testing for industrial application". Scope: Ir-based acidic OER; alkaline OER not covered by this source.
