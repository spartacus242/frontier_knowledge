## Question (canonical)

**[R1, adopted at Gate 1 — the one permitted reformulation; V0 below]**
For a closed-loop autonomous inorganic-synthesis campaign whose raw diffraction patterns and per-sample automated decision scores are archived (e.g. the A-Lab campaign of Szymanski et al. 2023, whose patterns have twice been re-read): taking as reference standard the majority verdict of >=3 crystallographers who each classify every pattern as *target phase present / absent / indeterminate* against a pre-specified minor-phase weight-fraction threshold, blinded to the automated verdict, to each other, and to the published per-target conclusion —
(a) what is the automated-vs-expert-majority disagreement rate, with CI, on **all** attempted targets (not only the reported successes), reported against the measured expert-expert agreement (Fleiss kappa / Gwet AC1) on the same patterns as a noise floor; and
(b) is the loop's own deployed phase-identification score calibrated against that blinded majority well enough — pre-specified calibration-slope, Brier and AUC bands — for a running campaign to estimate its own false-discovery rate on-line?

Scope restriction (added at R1): *phase identification only*. Whether a correctly identified phase is new to science or to a database is a separate decision step and is excluded.

**V0 (intake, superseded):** "In closed-loop autonomous inorganic synthesis campaigns, what fraction of the loop's automated 'target phase synthesized' decisions survive blinded expert re-analysis of the very same diffraction data, and is the loop's own automated phase-identification score calibrated well enough for a campaign to estimate its own false-discovery rate on-line?"
*Reason for reformulation (well-posedness only):* V0 left four things undefined that decide the answer — (i) "survive" had no threshold, so a continuous minor-phase weight fraction was being treated as a binary; (ii) it did not fix the number or independence of experts, nor require an expert-expert noise floor, so any disagreement rate would be uninterpretable; (iii) it silently conflated phase identification with novelty adjudication, which is where the published dispute actually diverged; (iv) "fraction of decisions" over reported successes alone yields no specificity and therefore no FDR. R1 fixes all four and adds a control set. No scope was widened.

## Alternative formulations

1. *Lay:* Can a robot lab measure its own error rate — does the match score it already computes predict whether an independent crystallographer, handed the identical pattern with the robot's answer hidden, agrees the target was made?
2. *Technical (diagnostic-accuracy framing):* Treat the loop as a diagnostic test and a blinded >=3-reader majority as the reference standard: estimate sensitivity, specificity and FDR for the "target synthesized" call, decompose reader-vs-loop disagreement against reader-vs-reader disagreement (MRMC variance components), and test the loop's score for calibration against the reference.
3. *Dependence form:* How large is automated-versus-expert disagreement on identical autonomous-campaign diffraction data, and does it scale with the number of fitted phases, the amorphous fraction, the pattern SNR, or the target's structural prototype?

## Motivating gap

A-Lab (Szymanski et al., *Nature* **624**:86-91, 2023) reported 41 of 58 targets synthesized in 17 days with fully automated XRD phase identification. Leeman, Palgrave et al. (*PRX Energy* **3**(1):011002, 2024 — verified at Gate 2) re-read the same data and concluded that **no** new materials had been discovered, with roughly two thirds of the claimed successes likely known compositionally disordered analogues. *Nature* then published an Author Correction (**650**(8100):E1, 19 Jan 2026) in which the original authors manually re-analysed their own patterns and reported that the platform "reached the correct conclusion in 36 of its 40 reported successes", with four inconclusive from XRD alone, reframed the novelty claims as "new to the prediction platform, not necessarily new to science", and removed one compound found in the training data.

So the same diffraction data have now produced **two incompatible readings in print** — "no new materials" and "36/40 correct" — and neither is a measurement. Both re-reads were unblinded, done by parties with a stake in the outcome, on the reported successes only, with no confidence interval, no second independent reader, no expert-expert agreement baseline, and no evaluation of the loop's internal score. The anomaly that automated and expert readings of identical data diverge is therefore fully on the record *and already consequential enough to move a Nature correction*, yet the quantity itself — a disagreement rate against a noise floor, and a calibration curve for the score the loop already computes — has never been estimated. Meanwhile success counts from such loops continue to be cited by funders, platform builders and reviewers with no error bar. (Gate 2 note: the record is now richer and more adversarial than at intake; see Novelty.)

## Presuppositions

1. **Campaigns retain raw patterns and per-sample automated decision scores.** ESTABLISHED for A-Lab specifically — its patterns were released and have been independently re-analysed twice (Leeman et al. 2024; Author Correction 2026) and 20 of its reaction patterns were re-used in the Dara paper. CONTESTED/unknown for proprietary platforms, and *partly contested even for A-Lab*: whether the per-sample internal score is archived for all 58 targets, including failures, is not established by anything opened at Gate 2 and must be checked before the study is designed.
2. **A blinded expert majority can serve as a reference standard.** CONTESTED, and deliberately so: inter-crystallographer agreement on weak, multi-phase patterns is itself unmeasured, which is why R1 requires the expert-expert kappa to be estimated *inside the same study* as the noise floor. The design therefore uses an imperfect reference standard knowingly — standard in diagnostic accuracy, unusual in crystallography.
3. **"Target phase present" is a definable binary near the detection limit.** CONTESTED — minor-phase weight fraction is continuous. Handled, not assumed away: R1 requires a pre-specified weight-fraction threshold plus an explicit *indeterminate* category, and sensitivity analysis across two or three thresholds.
4. **Automated scores are comparable across samples within one campaign.** NEEDED for any calibration statement; untested. If the score is only meaningful within a target (e.g. a heuristically weighted FoM, as in Dara), (b) must be answered with a within-target rank statistic instead of a global reliability diagram.
5. **Automated and expert verdicts address the same proposition.** CONTESTED at intake, now RESOLVED BY SCOPING: the published dispute diverged substantially on *novelty and disorder* rather than on phase presence, so R1 restricts both verdicts to phase identification under a stated threshold.
6. **A control subset with independent ground truth is obtainable** (patterns whose phase content is fixed by an independent method — stoichiometric standards, synchrotron/neutron data, or known single-phase products). ADDED at R1; needed to separate genuine indeterminacy from reader noise. Flagged as a feasibility risk, not established.
7. **Not presupposed:** that the loop is wrong. A well-calibrated score is an equally informative answer, and the Author Correction's 36/40 is consistent with a low disagreement rate.

## What would count as an answer

A pre-registered, blinded re-read of an existing campaign archive (all released A-Lab patterns for all 58 attempted targets, or a comparable published campaign), with:

- **Readers:** >=3 crystallographers, independent, each returning target-present / absent / indeterminate per pattern plus a confidence, blinded to the automated verdict, to one another, and to the published per-target conclusion. Pattern order randomised; a replicate subset re-read by the same reader for intra-reader agreement.
- **Threshold:** one pre-specified minor-phase weight-fraction criterion (primary), with 2-3 sensitivity thresholds.
- **(a) Disagreement:** automated-vs-majority disagreement rate with CI over *all* attempted targets, so sensitivity, specificity and FDR are estimable; reported beside the measured expert-expert Fleiss kappa / Gwet AC1 and intra-reader agreement. MRMC variance decomposition of reader-vs-loop against reader-vs-reader variance.
- **(b) Calibration:** reliability curve, calibration slope and intercept, Brier score and AUC for the loop's own deployed score against the blinded majority, with pre-specified acceptance bands; plus the implied on-line FDR estimate compared with the realised one.
- **(c) Dependence:** regression of disagreement on number of fitted phases, amorphous fraction, SNR, and target structural prototype.
- **Control:** the independent-ground-truth subset (presupposition 6) to bound reference-standard error.

**Closed outcomes.** YES = calibration inside the pre-specified bands → an autonomous campaign can estimate its own FDR from data it already collects, and self-reported success counts can carry an error bar. NO = flat or inverted reliability curve → success counts require external audit, and journals/funders need a human-verification requirement rather than a score threshold. THIRD CLOSED OUTCOME = expert-expert kappa is so low that the disagreement rate sits inside the noise floor → the binary "target synthesized" decision is not well defined at the detection limit for either humans or machines, which refutes the reference-standard premise and redirects the field toward reporting weight fractions with uncertainty instead of binary calls. All three are informative and none can be reached by argument alone.

**Communities that would act:** self-driving-lab builders (LBNL A-Lab, Acceleration Consortium, Argonne Polybot), funders of materials-acceleration programmes (DOE, ARPA-E, EU), journals setting evidence standards for autonomy claims (demonstrated: *Nature* corrected on exactly this axis), crystallographic validation committees (IUCr CPD).

## Novelty — how this differs from each

**vs P1 (Author Correction).** This is the closest prior and the reason novelty is not High. It is a dispute resolution, not a measurement: a single interested team, **unblinded** (they knew their own verdicts and the published claims), re-reading **only the 40 reported successes**, so the design cannot yield specificity, cannot yield an FDR, and cannot detect missed successes among the 17-18 declared failures. It reports a bare count with no CI, no second independent reader, and therefore no expert-expert noise floor — and the existence of P2's incompatible reading of the identical data is precisely the evidence that a single unblinded re-read does not settle the quantity. It says nothing about the loop's internal score, so it cannot answer part (b) at all, and nothing about dependence on phase count, amorphous fraction, SNR or prototype. The candidate's (a) is the blinded, all-targets, noise-floored version of what P1 did unblinded on a subset; its (b) is untouched by P1.
**vs P2 (Leeman et al.).** Same data, opposite conclusion, and the same metrological deficits: one expert group, unblinded and reading toward a stated thesis, no agreement statistic, no reader count, no noise floor, no threshold pre-specification. Critically, P2's verdicts are substantially about *compound novelty and cation disorder* — a different proposition from "is the target phase present above a stated weight fraction", which is why R1 scopes to phase identification. P2 demonstrates the anomaly; it does not measure it, and by its own framing it cannot (an argued re-interpretation is not an estimate with an error bar).
**vs P3 (CrystalShift).** P3 is the closest prior for part (b) and the second reason novelty is not High: calibration of phase-presence probabilities, with reliability diagrams and ECE, is already asked and answered — **for a candidate algorithm, against simulated ground truth**, at controlled noise levels. The candidate differs on four axes that decide whether a campaign can police itself: (i) reference standard is a *blinded multi-expert majority on real campaign patterns*, not synthetic labels (P3's one experimental set is hand-labelled by an unstated number of unblinded experts); (ii) the object calibrated is the score a *deployed loop actually used to make its decisions*, not a new method's posterior; (iii) the target quantity is a campaign-level **on-line FDR estimate** compared against the realised FDR, which P3 explicitly does not develop; (iv) dependence on fitted-phase count and amorphous fraction, absent in P3. P3 also cannot speak to the expert-expert noise floor, without which a disagreement rate is uninterpretable.

## Significance — consequence

**If yes (score calibrated inside pre-specified bands):** an autonomous campaign can convert the number it already computes into a running FDR estimate, so success counts acquire an error bar generated by the loop itself; score-thresholded auto-triage of which samples need human reading becomes defensible, and the human-verification budget can be spent where the score says the risk is. **If no (flat or inverted reliability curve):** the internal score is not evidence about correctness, so self-reported success counts cannot be audited from inside the loop; evidence standards must then require external human reading of a random sample (a design, not an exhortation — the disagreement rate tells you the sample size), and platform comparisons based on headline success counts become uninterpretable. **If the third outcome (disagreement inside the expert-expert noise floor):** the binary decision is not well defined at the detection limit for humans either, which tells the field to report refined weight fractions with uncertainty instead of "synthesized / not", and retires the success-count metric rather than fixing it.

## Significance — audience

Demonstrated rather than asserted: *Nature* has already issued a correction on exactly this axis (P1), which is the strongest available evidence that journals and editors act on this quantity; the A-Lab team itself responded by building a new phase-identification tool with an expert comparison (Dara, arXiv:2510.19667), i.e. the builders are already reaching for the measurement and currently doing it with one unblinded reader; the critics (P2) published in a journal rather than a comment thread. Beyond those: funders of materials-acceleration programmes (DOE, ARPA-E, EU) who currently have no error bar on the throughput claims they fund; Acceleration Consortium and Argonne Polybot, who must choose whether to ship a calibrated score or a human-in-the-loop gate; IUCr CPD / crystallographic validation committees, who validate structures but have no instrument for validating *decisions*. What each would do differently is concrete: a calibration band is something a journal can require and a platform can report.

## Significance — fecundity

Opens: (i) what is the cheapest audit design — how many blinded readers on what fraction of samples — that bounds a campaign's FDR to a stated width, which is a reader-sampling optimisation nobody can pose before the noise floor is known; (ii) whether the same protocol transfers to other automated readouts that gate autonomous claims (automated structure verification in NMR, automated cryo-EM map interpretation, LLM-as-judge screens), giving a general "decision-level metrology for self-driving labs"; (iii) whether selective prediction / conformal abstention (arXiv:2508.07556, 2603.24704) can be bound to an expert reference standard rather than synthetic labels; (iv) whether disagreement concentrates in identifiable structural prototypes or in disordered systems — if so, prediction pipelines can be steered away from targets their own verification cannot adjudicate; (v) whether "weight fraction with uncertainty" should replace binary success as the unit of reporting.

## Significance — anchoring

Anchors directly on: the A-Lab dispute and its correction (P1, P2); the reproducibility of computational materials predictions and the ordered-vs-disordered-compound problem that P2 identifies as two thirds of the disagreement; the long-standing between-analyst variability problem in quantitative phase analysis that the IUCr/ICDD round robins were set up to probe (search 3, not opened — flagged as a lead for the noise-floor literature); the blind-test tradition in automated structure determination (CASD-NMR, search 9) as the cross-field precedent for auditing automation by withholding the answer; and the open problem of uncertainty quantification for ML phase labelling (P3).

## Reason for silence

Two mechanisms, both credible and neither "no one thought of it":

1. **Disciplinary boundary.** The measurement requires a methodology crystallography does not use: multi-reader multi-case diagnostic-accuracy design, agreement statistics against an *admittedly imperfect* reference standard, and reliability diagrams. Crystallographic quality culture validates *structures* against physical criteria (checkCIF, IUCr validation, Rwp), on the premise that the structure is the truth; it has no tradition of treating a human reading as a fallible rater whose agreement must be estimated. Conversely, the ML groups who own calibration machinery benchmark against simulated ground truth precisely because expert labels are expensive and, in this regime, disputed — P3 does exactly this. The question sits in the gap: it needs the crystallographers' patterns and the metrologists' design, and belongs to neither literature's reviewers.
2. **Incentive gap, sharpened by the correction mechanism.** No party gains from running it. Platform builders would be publishing their own error rate, and the cheaper move — demonstrated — is to build a better tool and compare against one cooperating expert. Critics gain more from a case-by-case refutation, which is publishable without recruiting a blinded panel, than from a rate that might come out reassuring. And the journal correction process rewards resolving *this* dispute over *these* compounds, which is what happened: once P1 settled the specific claims, the general quantity stopped being anyone's deliverable. A third, weaker contributor: the "same data, two independent readings" corpus only came into existence when a public dispute forced pattern release, so before 2024 there was no material to run the study on.

**Well-posedness: 3/3.** R1 states the threshold, the reader count and independence, the blinding, the indeterminate category, the noise-floor statistic, the calibration metrics with pre-specified bands, the feature regression, and a control subset with independent ground truth; all three outcomes (yes / no / inside the noise floor) are closed and informative, and none is reachable by argument. Residual weaknesses recorded rather than hidden: the reference standard is imperfect by construction (presupposition 2), the per-sample internal score may not be archived for failed targets (presupposition 1), and if the score is only within-target meaningful, (b) must be answered with a rank statistic instead of a global reliability diagram (presupposition 4).
