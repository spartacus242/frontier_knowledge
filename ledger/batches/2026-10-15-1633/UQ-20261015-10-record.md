Question (canonical): [R1, adopted at Gate 1] In open knockout-based characterisations of commercial antibodies (YCharOS: about 1,200 antibodies against about 120 human proteins as of December 2024, plus later releases), and separately for each application (WB, IP, IF):
(i) what share of the variance in antibody pass/fail lies between target proteins, after adjusting for antibody-level factors (renewable recombinant, monoclonal or polyclonal; vendor; host; immunogen-region features where the vendor discloses them) and for assay context (target protein abundance in the parental line, research attention to the target)?
(ii) do pre-specified target-intrinsic features account for that between-target share? The features are: number of transmembrane segments, primary subcellular compartment, fraction predicted disordered, maximum paralog identity, annotated PTM-site density and protein length.
  [V0, as submitted] Which intrinsic properties of a target protein (membrane topology, subcellular location, abundance, intrinsic disorder, paralog sequence similarity, post-translational modification) predict whether commercial antibodies against it pass knockout-based validation, independently of antibody type and vendor?
  Reason for reformulation (well-posedness only):
  (a) V0 counts "abundance" as intrinsic. It is not. It depends on the cell line, and YCharOS partly controls it by design: parental lines are chosen with RNA ≥ 2.5 log2(TPM+1) (Ayoubi et al. 2023). R1 treats it as an assay-context covariate.
  (b) V0's "which ... predict" has no decision rule, and with about 120 targets the power for six covariates is limited. R1 separates a well-powered primary question (the target variance share) from a secondary one (attribution to named features) and gives pre-set outcome bands, including "inconclusive".
  (c) V0 does not say what an all-fail target means. Biddle & Virk 2023 attribute all-fail targets to insensitive antibodies, a faulty knockout line, non-expression, or translation from a downstream ATG. Those are measurement failures, not target difficulty. R1 handles them in a sensitivity analysis.
  (d) V0 does not mention immunogen choice. Immunogen features are the closest prior's predictors (Waury et al. 2025), so R1 asks what targets explain beyond them.
Alternative formulations: (1) Are some human proteins systematically "hard targets" for commercial research antibodies once reagent type and vendor are held fixed, and is the hardness carried by the protein's topology, disorder, paralogy or modification? (2) In knockout-validated antibody data, do target-level differences in pass rates exceed what antibody-level factors explain, and which protein features carry that variance?

Motivating gap: The antibody-reliability literature is dense with how-to answers. These include the IWGAV validation pillars (Uhlén et al. 2016, Nat. Methods; not opened in-session), vendor knockout validation and the YCharOS knockout protocol. It also reports antibody-level predictors.
- Ayoubi et al. 2023 (eLife RP91645; bioRxiv 2023.06.01.543292, full text opened) tested 614 commercial antibodies against 65 targets. Recombinant antibodies performed best. The paper contains no analysis by target feature. Targets were "chosen by disease charities, academia, and industry", mostly in neurodegeneration (56 intracellular, 9 secreted). About 40% of targets lacked any successful IF antibody, and 9 targets (14%) had only a non-selective WB antibody.
- Biddle & Virk 2023 (F1000Research 12:1344, "YCharOS open antibody characterisation data: Lessons learned and progress made"; opened) analyse only antibody- and vendor-level predictors (renewability, vendor genetic controls, orthogonal controls, literature support). They note that "On rare occasions, for specific proteins, all antibodies fail to detect the wild-type specific signal" and list candidate causes without testing any.
Correction to the intake: Biddle & Virk do not say the cause is "unclear". They list insensitive antibodies, the knockout line, non-expression and downstream-ATG translation. None of these is a structural property of the target.
Existing target-level hints, never decomposed: GPCR antibodies as a class are unreliable (Michel et al. 2009), and proteins lacking validated HPA antibodies are enriched for transmembrane proteins (Waury et al. 2025, Fig. 1). No source found estimates how much of antibody failure is attributable to the target rather than the reagent, using knockout ground truth and commercial antibodies.

Presuppositions:
  P1 Knockout-based pass/fail is valid ground truth. ESTABLISHED for the YCharOS protocol. FLAGGED: truncated products or translation from downstream ATGs can escape a CRISPR knockout (Biddle & Virk 2023). A sensitivity analysis excludes targets where no antibody detects the wild-type signal and orthogonal (MS or RNA) evidence of expression in the parental line is absent.
  P2 The data hold enough distinct targets. PARTLY ESTABLISHED. Counts: 78 proteins / 812 antibodies (Aug 2023; Biddle & Virk); 96 / 859 (Mar 2024; CST blog, opened); "approximately 1,200 antibodies against 120 protein targets" (McGill news, 18 Dec 2024, opened). The F1000 YCharOS gateway lists 59 articles, the latest dated 13 Oct 2026, so there are probably more targets now (current count UNVERIFIED). About 120 targets is adequate for a variance component but modest for six target-level covariates.
  P3 Each target has enough antibodies for a target random effect. ESTABLISHED: about 9–10 per target (614/65; about 1,200/120).
  P4 Target features can be separated from assay context and from vendor immunogen choice. CONTESTED.
    - Abundance is partly fixed by YCharOS line selection and enters as a covariate. Research attention (publication count) enters as a covariate: Ayoubi 2023 notes more antibodies for well-studied proteins.
    - Commercial immunogens are often undisclosed (FLAGGED), so immunogen adjustment is partial.
    - A residual "target" effect may partly run through the immunogen options the target allows. That is a target-intrinsic route, not a confound, but it must be reported as such.
  P5 The YCharOS target set spans enough variation in the features. CONTESTED. Targets are nominated by charities and industry, are neurodegeneration-heavy and mostly intracellular, and IF was run only for intracellular targets. This range restriction may leave some covariates (e.g., multi-pass membrane topology) unestimable. Inference generalises only to YCharOS-like target sets.
  P6 Pass/fail is comparable across targets within an application. ESTABLISHED within the YCharOS scoring scheme. WB has graded categories (e.g., specific vs. non-selective), so an ordinal model is an option.
  P7 No prior target-level analysis exists. PARTLY CONTRADICTED.
    - Class-level: Michel et al. 2009 (GPCRs); the anti-GPCR selectivity screen of 205 receptors.
    - Coverage-level: Waury et al. 2025 (HPA coverage gaps enriched for TM proteins).
    - HPA pages report no success rates by protein class.
    None of these is a knockout-based, cross-class, commercial-antibody variance decomposition. The HPA-by-class check is incomplete (queries 8–9 not run).

What would count as an answer: Assemble per-antibody, per-application outcomes from the YCharOS Zenodo community and the F1000 data notes. Join target features:
  - UniProt: TM segments, signal peptide, PTM-site annotations, length.
  - HPA: subcellular location, RNA level in the tested line.
  - AlphaFold / pLDDT-based disorder fraction.
  - Ensembl/BLAST: maximum paralog identity overall and, where the immunogen is disclosed, over the immunogen region.
Fit a mixed logistic (or ordinal) model for each application with antibody-level fixed effects, a target random intercept and a crossed vendor random intercept.
  (i) Report the latent-scale target ICC with a 95% CI.
  (ii) Add the six target covariates. Report each odds ratio with a 95% CI (Holm-corrected across 6 features × 3 applications) and the proportional reduction in target variance.
Pre-set bands:
  - YES (target effect): ICC CI lower bound > 0.10 in at least one application.
  - NO: ICC CI upper bound < 0.05 in all applications.
  - Otherwise: "small or uncertain", reported with its size.
  - Attribution: a named feature is a carrier if its corrected CI excludes 1 and its inclusion cuts target variance by ≥ 25%.
  - If no feature meets this and the ICC is YES, the answer is "target effect present, not carried by these features". If the power for a feature is too low to exclude an OR of 2, that feature is "inconclusive".
Sensitivity analyses: drop measurement-failure targets (P1); restrict to antibodies with disclosed immunogens; leave-one-target-class-out.

Significance — consequence:
  YES (a clear target share carried by named features), three practical changes:
    - A researcher could receive a prior probability that any commercial antibody against a given protein works, before buying. For predicted hard targets (e.g., multi-pass membrane proteins with close paralogs), the rational move shifts from "buy a recombinant, knockout-validated antibody" to epitope-tagged knock-in or MS-based detection.
    - Characterisation consortia and funders (YCharOS/ABIF; MJFF and ALS charities, which nominate targets) could triage nominated targets and route predicted-hard ones to de novo renewable-binder generation instead of screening catalogue products.
    - Vendors could predict which catalogue items most need knockout data.
  YES with no feature carrying it: an unexplained hardness term that is itself worth explaining.
  NO (negligible target share): failure is a reagent-quality problem. Then:
    - The current policy push (recombinant antibodies, vendor genetic controls) is sufficient whatever the target.
    - The "hard target" lore, including GPCRs, would be re-read as an artefact of immunogen or vendor choice.
    - Target-stratified validation burdens would be unjustified.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism):
  (1) New data. Multi-target knockout ground truth for commercial antibodies under one protocol only exists since YCharOS reached about 65–120 targets (2023–2024). Earlier evidence was single-target studies, single-family screens (GPCRs) or HPA in-house validation, none of which supports a target variance component.
  (2) Incentive framing. The reproducibility movement's goal is reagent accountability (recombinant production, vendor genetic controls). Antibody- and vendor-level predictors serve that goal, while a target-level "some proteins are just hard" finding reads as exculpatory for vendors. Characterisation groups also partner with vendors and publish per-target data notes rather than cross-target syntheses.
  (3) Disciplinary boundary. Antigen-design work (HPA, Waury) asks which immunogen region to pick. Reagent-performance work (YCharOS) asks which product to buy. The structural-bioinformatics features that would characterise the target sit with neither group, and the needed join (YCharOS Zenodo × UniProt/HPA/AlphaFold) crosses all three.
  Weakest link: (2) is inferred, not evidenced. Silence may also be partly apparent, an artefact of the incomplete search.
