Domain(s): Medicine — clinical genetics and diagnostic laboratory practice (long-read / read-backed phasing); neighbours: laboratory quality assurance, screening epidemiology, pharmacogenomics, precision oncology

Question (canonical):
V1 (intake): Among archived clinical reports that diagnosed an autosomal-recessive condition on two heterozygous variants whose phase was never established by parental testing or read-backed phasing, what proportion are in cis when the stored DNA is re-phased by long-read sequencing — that is, what is the diagnostic false-positive rate attributable to unresolved phase?
V2 (Gate 1 reformulation, adopted; one reformulation used, for well-posedness only): In a defined archive of issued diagnostic reports asserting an autosomal-recessive molecular diagnosis on two heterozygous variants in one gene whose phase was not established by trio testing or by read-backed phasing, (a) PRIMARY: among cases whose stored DNA is retrievable and re-phaseable, what fraction have both variants on one haplotype under long-read (or targeted linked-read) phasing, with a confidence interval, stratified by inter-variant distance, gene, report era, and whether a population cis/trans prior was cited; and (b) SECONDARY: among those cis cases, what fraction have no alternative second allele on the other haplotype after a pre-specified search for deep-intronic, structural and methylation events — the strict false-diagnosis fraction? The estimand is explicitly defined on the retrievable archive, with retrievability reported against the issued-report denominator.
Reason for reformulation: V1 conflates two outcomes ("proportion in cis" and "false-positive diagnosis rate") into one number, leaves the sampling frame equivocal between issued and retrievable reports, and states no equivalence margin for a "no". V2 separates the outcomes, fixes the frame, and makes the null decidable. V1 is retained in the record.

Alternative formulations:
- Now that phase is directly observable at arbitrary variant separations, how many legacy "compound heterozygous" molecular diagnoses were wrong because both variants sat on one haplotype?
- What is the positive predictive value of a recessive molecular diagnosis issued without phase information, stratified by inter-variant distance and by whether population-based cis/trans priors were invoked?

Motivating gap:
Phase is load-bearing for a recessive diagnosis and, until long reads, was inferred rather than observed: trio testing when parents were available, otherwise population priors, assumption, or an explicit "phase unknown" caveat (ACMG/AMP PM3 encodes trans as the evidential requirement). Long-read and linked-read phasing now settle the question directly on the same stored DNA, and single-case demonstrations are appearing (haplotype phasing of biallelic WNT10B variants, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11973024/). What is missing is the population-level quantity: an error rate over an archive of reports already issued. Gate 2 confirmed that the field's two adjacent programmes run in the opposite directions — population-genetic phase INFERENCE for prospective variant pairs (Guo et al. 2024), and long-read reanalysis applied to UNSOLVED cases to raise yield (Smith et al., ASHG) — so no one is re-testing solved cases to find out how many were wrong.

Presuppositions:
1. A non-trivial share of issued recessive diagnoses had phase unresolved at report time. PLAUSIBLE BUT UNQUANTIFIED HERE; load-bearing. Supported indirectly: Guo et al. 2024 (Nat Genet) state that outside parental testing few methods establish phase in clinical settings, which is why they built an inference tool; Cao et al. 2026 report that 78.7% of 17,519 multi-hit gene pairs exceed the ~500 bp short-read phasing limit (somatic, but the same read-length physics). If nearly all such reports were trio-confirmed, the estimand is near-empty.
2. Stored DNA is still available, consented for re-testing, and of adequate quality for long-read phasing. CONTESTED, and a selection hazard: surviving samples are not a random sample of issued reports. V2 handles this by defining the estimand on the retrievable archive and reporting retrievability against the issued denominator, rather than claiming the issued-report rate.
3. Long-read phasing is accurate enough at the relevant separations that a cis call is not itself an artifact (mapping error in segmental duplications, allelic dropout, switch error). Requires a stated error model plus orthogonal confirmation of cis calls; the equivalence margin for "no" must be pre-specified against that model.
4. A cis pair genuinely falsifies the diagnosis. CONTESTED and explicitly split in V2: cis variants plus an undetected second-allele event can leave the diagnosis right for a different reason, so "phase was wrong" (primary) and "diagnosis was wrong" (secondary) are separate outcomes.
5. The archive's report text records how phase was established, well enough to define the eligible subset. Partly testable within the study (inter-rater agreement on eligibility coding); if report text is too thin, the eligible subset is itself biased toward labs with better documentation.
6. Implicit: a cis finding is actionable — the lab can recontact and reissue. Jurisdiction- and consent-dependent, and not required for the measurement, only for its consequence.

What would count as an answer:
A retrospective re-phasing study in one or more diagnostic laboratories: define the eligible subset from report text (two heterozygous variants in one gene, recessive condition, phase not established by trio or by read-backed phasing); re-phase retrievable DNA by long-read or targeted linked-read sequencing with a stated switch/dropout error model and orthogonal confirmation of cis calls; report the cis proportion with a CI, stratified by inter-variant distance, gene, era, and whether population priors were cited; report the share of cis cases with no alternative second allele (strict false-diagnosis rate); and give an explicit account of availability bias, with retrievability as a reported denominator rather than an assumption. "No" = the cis fraction lies inside a pre-registered equivalence margin around the phasing method's own error rate, which would retire a standing worry and license continued phase-free reporting in defined circumstances (distance, gene, prior strength).

Novelty — how this differs from each:
1. vs Guo 2024: same load-bearing premise, opposite direction and opposite selection. Guo build a population-genetic estimator of P(trans) for rare variant pairs from gnomAD v2 (125,748 exomes) and validate it at ~96% accuracy against trios and against patients with PRESUMED CAUSAL compound heterozygous variants. That validation set is pre-selected for pairs believed to be in trans — the mirror image of the candidate's eligible subset (reports where phase was never established). Guo supply a prospective prior; the candidate asks for a retrospective, archive-framed error rate on diagnoses already issued, with a second-allele adjudication step and an availability-bias account, neither of which exists in a population-inference paper. Caveat an adversary must check first: if Guo's validation includes a count of presumed-causal pairs their method or trio data called cis, that count is a partial answer in miniature and would push novelty to Low — I could not read the full text or supplement (PMC blocked, fetch budget spent).

Asked-but-open: No, not in this form. The underlying WORRY is explicit and standing in the field's own words (PM3 requires trans; Guo et al. open by saying few methods establish phase clinically; reports carry "phase unknown" caveats), but the worry is stated as a motivation for better prospective inference, never posed as a measurable error rate over issued reports. The question is un-asked; its premise is uncontroversial.

Significance — consequence:
If the cis fraction is materially above the phasing method's error rate, a class of molecular diagnoses acquires a measured error rate for the first time, and three things move: (a) reporting practice — "phase unknown" stops being a caveat and becomes a hard bar on a biallelic call when trio testing is unavailable, or a trigger for mandatory read-backed/long-read confirmation; (b) a recontact obligation falls on a definable subset of past reports, with a distance/gene/prior-based rule for prioritising it; (c) the downstream decisions built on those diagnoses get re-examined — recurrence risk quoted to parents (25% vs near-0), carrier testing of relatives, gene-therapy and newborn-screening eligibility, and trial enrolment on molecularly defined cohorts. If instead the fraction sits inside a pre-registered equivalence margin, the result is equally usable: it shows that population priors plus phenotype fit were already doing the work, retires the worry, licenses continued phase-free reporting in defined circumstances, and redirects scarce reanalysis capacity from phase confirmation to second-allele discovery (deep intronic, structural, methylation), which the secondary estimand also measures.

Significance — audience:
Diagnostic laboratory directors and variant-curation teams, who hold both the archives and the reporting templates; ClinGen's Sequence Variant Interpretation working group and the disease-specific VCEPs that own PM3's wording and its phase-unknown downweighting, who have a direct lever (the CSPEC specifications found at query 4); accreditation and proficiency-testing bodies (CAP, ISO 15189, EMQN), who could add observed phase to checklists and build a phase-resolution proficiency challenge; clinical geneticists and genetic counsellors who quote recurrence risks; payers and national genomics programmes deciding whether long-read confirmation is reimbursable; and the long-read cohort consortia (GREGoR, Genomics England) who hold instruments, archives and consents simultaneously — the Smith et al. abstract shows exactly that combination already assembled at one lab and pointed at unsolved cases instead.

Significance — fecundity:
(i) Calibrates the distance threshold beyond which population-prior phasing stops being clinically safe, converting Guo-style P(trans) estimates into a decision rule with a measured miss rate. (ii) The secondary estimand yields the first direct estimate of the undetected-second-allele rate in recessive disease — a quantity currently invisible because cis cases are never systematically searched for a hidden second hit. (iii) Because cis/trans priors are LD- and ancestry-dependent, the stratified result immediately raises an equity question: is the phase-attributable error rate higher in under-represented ancestries where the priors were weakest? (iv) Generalises to a programme of assumption-attributable diagnostic error rates — penetrance (Smith et al.'s 53% is the dominant-condition sibling number), mosaicism, population rarity — each measurable by the same archive-audit design. (v) Asks whether report text can support eligibility coding at scale, a concrete NLP/report-structuring question for labs, with the answer determining whether this audit is feasible anywhere but at labs with structured reports.

Significance — anchoring:
Touches PM3's trans requirement and ClinGen's phase-unknown handling; the reanalysis/reclassification literature, which measures change (18% clinically relevant change at five years, PMC10545662; long-read reanalysis raising NDD yield) but has never partitioned it by phase and only ever counts diagnoses gained; the gene-specific complex-allele literature (CFTR G378X-I148T and kin), which is the anecdote class this would generalise into a rate; the ACMG "nine categories of pitfalls" taxonomy, which names inappropriate positive results without rating them; and the missing-second-allele problem in recessive disease (deep intronic, structural, methylation), which the secondary estimand measures from the other side.

Reason for silence:
Two mechanisms stacked, plus one framing effect. (1) New instrument: direct phase at arbitrary variant separation only became routine and affordable with long reads — Cao et al. quantify the old limit at ~500 bp, beyond which 78.7% of their multi-hit pairs fell — so before that the question could be worried about but not answered, and the field's response was to build better inference (Guo et al. 2024) rather than to measure past error. (2) Incentive and accountability gap: the parties holding the archives, the stored DNA and the consents are the clinical laboratories whose own issued reports would be shown wrong; there is no regulatory requirement to audit issued molecular diagnoses retrospectively, and a non-zero number carries liability and recontact cost, while the academic groups with the instruments hold neither the archives nor the consents. (3) Framing: reanalysis programmes are funded and published as yield-increasing exercises — the measured outcome is diagnoses gained, never diagnoses retracted — so re-testing solved cases sits outside the funded workflow. Smith et al. is the clean illustration: one lab with lrWGS, a 13,000-case exome archive and an explicit interest in failed Mendelian assumptions pointed the long reads at its 25 UNSOLVED cases. "No one thought of it" is not the mechanism; the thought is written into PM3.
