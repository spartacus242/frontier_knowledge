Question (canonical — Gate 0, as intaken):
In statutory plant-health eradication programmes where a positive test triggers mandatory destruction of the host and its neighbours, what fraction of destroyed hosts carried infections that would never have become transmissible or damaging within a stated horizon — the overdiagnosis fraction — and what destruction radius would an overdiagnosis-aware policy choose?

Question (canonical — Gate 1 REFORMULATION, the version that goes forward; one reformulation used, for well-posedness only):
Taking Xylella fastidiosa in Apulian olive under Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/1201 as the index programme, and defining the estimand on the TEST-POSITIVE subset only: among hosts destroyed because they themselves tested positive, what proportion would not have reached a stipulated endpoint — (E1) successful pathogen acquisition by a competent vector from that host, or (E2) a stipulated damage threshold (named yield loss or host death) — within a stipulated horizon T (e.g. T = 10 years) under the counterfactual of no destruction; and, treating that proportion as a parameter in a stated loss function over destroyed hosts versus averted onward transmissions, what removal radius does that loss function select?
Second, separate quantity (NOT the overdiagnosis fraction): among the untested neighbour hosts felled by the radius rule, what proportion were uninfected or likewise would not have reached the endpoint within T.

Why the reformulation was needed (recorded per Gate 1): the intake version said "destroyed hosts", which silently pools three disjoint categories that the screening literature keeps apart — (i) false positives (test wrong, host not infected), (ii) precautionary radius culls (untested, mostly uninfected by design), and (iii) true positives whose infection would never have mattered. Only (iii) is overdiagnosis. (i) and (ii) are already named, measured and litigated in plant health; pooling them makes the question answerable by restating known numbers and would have let a reviewer "answer" it without touching the novel quantity. The reformulation also supplies the horizon and endpoint whose absence the intake itself flags as leaving the quantity undefined, and separates the endpoint into transmissibility (E1) and damage (E2), which can diverge sharply in this system.

Alternative formulations:
1. Transferring the excess-incidence logic of cancer screening to plant biosecurity: how much of the detected disease burden in a Xylella fastidiosa or citrus-greening eradication zone is disease that would never have mattered?
2. What is the number-needed-to-destroy to avert one onward transmission in statutory plant-disease eradication, as a function of removal radius, and has it been estimated for any programme?
3. Does statutory plant-health eradication have a lead-time/length-bias problem — does symptom-led and proximity-led survey sampling systematically oversample the fast-progressing tail of infections, so that progression hazards estimated from survey-detected hosts overstate the risk posed by the median test-positive host?

Motivating gap:
Cancer screening built a measurement apparatus for exactly one question — what share of detected cases would never have progressed — with excess-incidence designs, unscreened-arm follow-up and lead-time modelling. Plant biosecurity destroys hosts at very large scale on test positivity: EU Regulation 2020/1201 prescribes destruction of host plants within 50 m of an infected plant inside a 2.5 km demarcated area for Xylella fastidiosa, and olive felling in Apulia has run to very large numbers. EFSA Plant Health Panel opinions and the eradication cost-effectiveness literature argue about radius, detection sensitivity and latency. The interface is substantive rather than cosmetic because the ingredients of the plant analogue of "would never have progressed" are separately reported and separately modelled — asymptomatic and latent hosts, host-species differences in titre and in vector acquisition efficiency, incomplete vector acquisition, climate suitability limits, remaining host lifespan — but have not, as far as the intake's own prior searching found, been assembled into a single overdiagnosis estimand with a horizon and an endpoint attached. The intake records that its searching returned eradication cost-effectiveness modelling, EFSA opinions and the Apulia controversy, and no overdiagnosis estimand.

P3. A counterfactual is obtainable. CONTESTED and programme-dependent. Note the structural obstacle: holding a test-positive host under observation is itself prohibited by the regulation being evaluated, so the cleanest design is legally foreclosed to any compliant actor. Substitutes would have to be containment-rather-than-eradication zones, endemic regions, pre-programme spread records, or episodes where destruction lagged or was refused.
P4. "Would never have become transmissible or damaging" is definable once a horizon T and an endpoint are stipulated; without that stipulation the quantity is undefined, exactly as in screening. ESTABLISHED as a methodological point; the reformulation discharges it.
P5. Regulators and growers would act on the number. PLAUSIBLE — the radius is a tunable legal parameter under active scientific dispute and compensation litigation, and the proportionality of felling host plants around a positive has been tested in court.
P6 (added here, not in intake). The welfare structure differs from screening and the transplant must be adjusted for it: in screening the overdiagnosed person bears the harm and would have received the benefit; in plant health the destroyed host's owner bears the loss while the benefit accrues to third parties and to the public interest in pest freedom. CONTESTED-as-framing: the estimand survives this, but "overdiagnosis" cannot be read straight off as a welfare loss, and any policy conclusion needs the external-benefit term written down.

What would count as an answer:
A stated estimand — the proportion of destroyed test-positive hosts whose infection would not have reached named endpoint E within horizon T — plus at least one of:
(a) an excess-incidence or natural-experiment estimate from areas where destruction was delayed, refused or replaced by containment, with counterfactual cumulative incidence modelled from pre-programme spread;
(b) a containment cohort of test-positive hosts held rather than destroyed and followed to the endpoint, yielding a progression hazard stratified by host species, titre and vector pressure (legally available only where eradication has already been abandoned for containment, or under explicit derogation);
(c) a programme-level model calibrated to (b) returning number-needed-to-destroy per averted transmission as a function of radius, with the loss function and the external-benefit term written out.
The horizon and the endpoint must be stated with the number. An estimate near zero settles the question in the regulators' favour and is as useful as a large one. An answer that reports only false-positive rates or only the uninfected share of radius culls does NOT answer it.

Novelty — how this differs from each:
vs N1 (Loureiro 2025): N1 is an accounting of categories (i)-(ii) - uninfected preventive removals and programme cost - in a real 2020/1201 programme. It never defines a counterfactual-progression estimand on test-positive hosts, states no horizon T and no endpoint E, and does not connect any such fraction to the choice of radius. It does place every ingredient side by side (destroyed count, confirmed-infected count, asymptomatic share among infected, unclear pathogenicity) and stops at a cost comparison of eradication versus surveillance. It is one step from the candidate and is the reason the confidence is Medium rather than High.
vs N2 (Hyatt-Twynam 2017 / Vicent & Blasco 2017): the quantity optimised is total hosts removed (infected plus uninfected) for a given eradication outcome, with a host's "risk" meaning its probability of being infected given position and dispersal. Every infected host is a future source by construction of the model, so the fraction of infected hosts that would never reach an endpoint is not representable, let alone estimated. It answers "which hosts to remove", not "how many of the removed infected hosts never mattered"; the candidate's loss function would take N2's machinery and add the missing non-source term.
vs N3 (EFSA 2016 / Parnell 2019): EFSA holds the E1 overdiagnosis fraction to be approximately zero by premise (every infected plant a lifelong reservoir) and does not quantify it; the radius evaluations are scenario outcomes, not a loss function containing a non-source fraction. N3 is where the candidate's P2 is contested by the regulator's own scientific body, which makes the candidate a direct test of a stated EFSA premise rather than a restatement of it.

Significance — consequence:
Bidirectional and in both directions it moves a legal parameter, which is what distinguishes this from novel trivia. If the overdiagnosis fraction at a decade horizon is near zero for the index host-pathogen pair, the destruction rule is vindicated on its own terms: the grower complaint collapses to the uninfected-neighbour fraction (a known quantity, already the subject of proportionality argument) and the scientific dispute over radius reduces to a pure detection-and-spread problem with no hidden overtreatment term. That is a real result, not a null: it would remove from circulation the most damaging available criticism of the programme, and it is the answer regulators would expect. If instead the fraction is substantial for some strata — ornamental and wild hosts, low-titre positives, hosts outside vector phenology or climate suitability, hosts near the end of productive life — then the current rule is destroying assets for no epidemiological return in an identifiable subset, and an overdiagnosis-aware loss function would prefer titre- or host-stratified thresholds, test-and-hold under surveillance, or a radius that varies with the stratum, over uniform destruction. The number also enters compensation directly: a defensible estimate of how much destruction bought no averted transmission is the quantity a damages calculation or a proportionality review has been missing, and the absence of an estimate currently works as a default in the programme's favour.
If no: if the estimand turns out not to be estimable in any programme (P3 fails everywhere), that is itself consequential — it would mean the destruction radius is a legal parameter that cannot in principle be calibrated against the quantity that justifies it, which is an argument for building derogated containment cohorts prospectively rather than for leaving the rule unexamined.

Significance — fecundity:
Answering it makes a whole methodological cascade askable in plant health, each with an existing screening counterpart: lead time of statutory detection and what it is worth; the plant analogue of length-biased sampling, since symptom-led and proximity-led surveys preferentially catch fast-progressing infections and so bias progression hazards estimated from survey-detected hosts; the status of "incidentaloma" positives found in non-crop and wild vegetation with no economic endpoint at all; the right endpoint hierarchy when transmissibility (E1) and damage (E2) diverge; the overdiagnosis-aware optimal re-survey interval, which is the screening-interval problem transplanted; whether compensation schemes should be indexed to an estimated overdiagnosis fraction; and the same estimand in animal health, where test-positive culling and preemptive culling have the identical structure and, as far as I know, the identical missing quantity. It also sharpens an existing argument: it gives the "how much of olive quick decline is attributable to the pathogen" dispute a quantitative sibling about how much of the detected infection matters.

Significance — anchoring:
Attaches to live disputes rather than to a quiet corner: the contested removal radius and buffer dimensions for Xylella fastidiosa; the contested sensitivity and latency of detection, which is exactly the lead-time term the estimand needs; the fact that host-status lists exist at all, which concedes that host identity governs transmissibility and so concedes P2's mechanism; the eradication-versus-containment decision that several programmes have already faced and in some places resolved toward containment, which is where a legal counterfactual cohort could exist; and the proportionality test that courts apply to precautionary destruction, a legal standard currently argued without the quantity that would discipline it.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism, argued):
A disciplinary boundary compounded by a structural and an incentive obstacle — not "no one thought of it".
(i) Boundary: the measurement apparatus (excess-incidence designs, lead-time models, the overdiagnosis estimand itself) was built inside cancer-screening epidemiology and is taught there; the decision problem sits in plant-health regulation, whose quantitative tradition is spread-rate estimation and optimal control. Optimal-control framings ask which removal policy minimises a cost functional and answer it inside a model where every infected host is a future source by construction; they therefore never need to name the fraction of infected hosts that are not. The estimand is invisible from inside that formalism rather than rejected by it. [Gate 2 follow-up correction: for X. fastidiosa it is not only invisible, it is foreclosed by an explicit premise. EFSA PLH 2016 (N3) states as a finding that infected plants remain reservoirs for life regardless of symptoms, which sets the E1 fraction to zero by doctrine. Mechanism (i) should therefore read: a disciplinary boundary plus a stated regulatory-science premise that the quantity is zero. This strengthens the reason for silence and makes the candidate a test of that premise.]
(ii) Structural: the design that would identify the quantity — hold test-positive hosts and watch whether they ever reach the endpoint — is prohibited by the instrument under evaluation. A question whose answer requires breaking the law it would inform does not get asked by the people with standing to ask it, and the datasets that would support it are not generated. This is the strongest part of the mechanism and is of the same kind as the reason overdiagnosis in screening was only quantifiable once long-running unscreened arms and population registries existed: the counterfactual arm has to be institutionally available before the question becomes askable.
(iii) Incentive: in a programme under compensation litigation, no regulator gains from commissioning an estimate of how much of its own destruction bought nothing, and growers' advocates have an easier and legally better-supported argument available — that healthy, uninfected trees were felled — which crowds out the harder claim that infected ones were felled pointlessly. Both sides are thus steered to categories (i) and (ii) and away from (iii). [Gate 2 follow-up correction: partially contradicted. The crowd-out is confirmed for Italy (every Italian hit in S5 and S12 argues healthy trees or low incidence). But Loureiro et al. 2025 (N1) show that academic authors in another Member State running the same regulation (Portugal, subsp. multiplex, pathogenicity on olive unclear) did publish a programme-level audit of destruction against confirmed infection and asymptomatic share, framed as cost-effectiveness. The incentive barrier holds for regulators and for litigants, not for outside academics in low-damage settings, and the adjacent question is already being asked from the cost side. Mechanism (iii) should be downgraded from "nobody commissions it" to "the regulator does not, and growers' counsel prefer the easier claim"; mechanism (ii), the legal foreclosure of the identifying design, is untouched and remains the strongest part.]
