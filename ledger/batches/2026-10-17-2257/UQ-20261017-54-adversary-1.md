## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Inside a frozen intact cell you can now take a complex, sort its individual copies into
structural classes by what is sitting in its binding sites, and count them. That gives you a
measured "fraction of copies in bound state S". Separately, textbook biochemistry predicts that
fraction from two numbers: the dissociation constant measured in a test tube, and the
concentration of unbound ligand floating in the cytoplasm. The question is: do those two numbers
agree? Take the log of (counted fraction / predicted fraction) for several different complexes
and ask whether it is zero, whether it is consistently off in one direction by a common factor
(which would mean the cell interior imposes a single correction every modeller could apply), or
whether each complex is off in its own way (which would mean no correction is transferable and
every complex must be measured). V2 adds that machines burning ATP/GTP are only admitted if you
declare up front that the reference is a flux-balance steady state rather than an equilibrium.

So: a prediction-versus-measurement audit of in-vitro-to-in-cell parameter transfer, with the
measurement done by counting structures rather than by any assay.

## The dilemma that drives several rows below

From the verified text of P1: the drugs were dosed "much higher than the reported saturating
concentrations", and intracellular concentration "cannot be determined". Mass action gives
occupancy theta = [L]/(Kd + [L]), so at [L] >> Kd, theta -> 1 and d(theta)/d(ln Kd) -> 0: the
prediction stops depending on the affinity. **My own arithmetic on the record's and P1's published
numbers:** predicted occupancy ~1 under saturating dosing, observed 0.72, so
log10(0.72/1.0) = -0.14 (Spt -0.17, PUM -0.23). Those log-ratios are computable today, in a
paragraph, from published numbers — and they carry no information about affinity transfer, because
at saturation any Kd within orders of magnitude predicts the same thing. They measure how much
counting, heterogeneity and classification lose, which is P2's question, not the cell-biology
question. The regime with power (ligand within ~an order of magnitude of Kd) is exactly the regime
where the free-concentration term is undeterminable, per P1's own sentence. **Scope check, stated
honestly: this dilemma is proven for the drug-ligand cases of the one ample census. It is not
universal** — for endogenous nucleotides and tRNA, cellular concentrations can sit nearer the
relevant constants and free pools are partly measurable (though free-versus-Mg/protein-bound
partition is itself contested). So it eliminates the best-documented instance and narrows the
viable regime; it does not prove the class empty.

## Single strongest killing point

The question's informative regime and its measurable regime do not overlap in any instance so far
demonstrated: the one ample census dosed its ligands "much higher than the reported saturating
concentrations", where mass action predicts occupancy ~1 regardless of the Kd, so the computable
log-ratios (-0.14/-0.17/-0.23, my arithmetic) test classification fidelity rather than affinity
transfer; and the regime that would test affinity transfer is the one where the same paper says
"the exact concentrations of antibiotics inside cells cannot be determined".
