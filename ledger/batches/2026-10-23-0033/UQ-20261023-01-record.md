## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (canonical, ORIGINAL as intaken): In chronic high-density extracellular recordings where putative single units are matched across days by a published matcher, what fraction of the reported day-to-day change in unit tuning ("representational drift") is generated by the matcher's own identity errors, measured as false-match and split/merge rates at a declared operating point against an identity ground truth that is independent of the functional response used to validate matches?

Question (canonical, GATE 1 REFORMULATION — the version Gates 2–3 were run against; one reformulation, both recorded): For >=2 published cross-day unit matchers at declared operating points, applied to chronic high-density extracellular recordings in which unit identity is labelled by at least one construction that does NOT use the functional response later used to measure tuning, (a) what are the matcher's false-match and split/merge rates, and (b) what apparent change do those rates ALONE induce in >=2 published drift statistics under a declared null of zero true drift — i.e. what is the matching-error drift floor — and (c) do published drift magnitudes, matched on brain area and interval, exceed that floor?

Alternative formulations: How much of representational drift is a bookkeeping error about which neuron is which? / What drift floor is implied by cross-day unit-matching error alone, and do published drift magnitudes exceed it?

Motivating gap (as intaken, now verbatim-confirmed — see Gate 2 read levels): cross-day unit matchers publish their own error rates; the drift literature that consumes matched units never propagates them. The intake's figures were taken from a summariser-mediated read of the eLife reviewed-preprint PAGE. All of them are now CONFIRMED against a full-text read of the eLife 92495 v1 PDF (routes and quotations below), and one intake conjecture — that UnitMatch "may already report a ground-truth false-match rate" — is CORRECTED: its main text does not, and the numbers it points to are in a supplement this route did not serve.

Presuppositions (status after Gate 2 reads):
1. Cross-day "the same neuron" is a well-defined object over the recording interval — CONTESTED (electrode drift, cell death, and the possibility that a unit is a stable waveform cluster rather than one cell). MEASURAND EXEMPTION DECLARED, NOT MERELY CONSIDERED: the question's object is a matcher's decision scored against a declared identity labelling. If "same neuron across days" turns out not to be well defined at the interval in question, that is part of the answer and is itself the finding — it is not a false premise, and an attack that calls it one is attacking the question for reporting the failure it exists to measure.
2. At least one published matcher reports, or permits computation of, an error rate at a declared operating point — ESTABLISHED at full-text read level for the EMD matcher ("z=10 μm threshold has a false positive rate = 27% for KSgood units", Figure 4 caption), but that number is MODEL-BASED, not ground-truth-measured — the paper's own method sentence is "The full distribution can then be integrated up to any given z-threshold value to calculate the false positive rate." So established-as-published, FLAGGED as not-ground-truth.
3. An identity ground truth independent of functional response exists or can be constructed — CONTESTED, and the Gate 2 reads sharpen why: BOTH matchers I opened validate across days with functional response. UnitMatch states in terms "However, neither can be considered ground truth." Candidate constructions (within-session splits propagated across a surrogate null; probe-repositioned acute controls; optotagging of a genetically defined unit; two probes on one cell; paired juxtacellular or imaging-plus-ephys) each have coverage limits that an answer must declare.
4. Published drift magnitudes were computed on matched-unit sets whose matcher, version and operating point are recoverable from the papers — FLAGGED in the ORIGINAL; the Gate 1 reformulation DROPS this presupposition, because it compares published magnitudes against an independently computed floor instead of decomposing each paper's number.
5. Matching errors are approximately independent of tuning — FLAGGED; if false matches are tuning-biased the induced drift is not a simple inflation, which makes the question harder rather than ill-posed.
6. "Drift" as reported is a change in a measurable tuning statistic, so an induced-drift floor can be expressed in the papers' own statistic and units — ESTABLISHED in the weak sense that the drift literature reports measurable statistics; not separately verified per paper at this read level.

PRESUPPOSITIONS INTRODUCED BY THE REPAIR (tested as hard as the clause they replace, per the rule that a reformulation carries presuppositions of its own):
R1. At least one functional-response-independent identity labelling can be constructed with non-trivial coverage. This is presupposition 3 moved into the question rather than assumed away — still CONTESTED, and it is the repair's weakest point.
R2. Published drift statistics can be recomputed, or analytically perturbed, under an injected error rate. This requires either released matched-unit tables or a reimplementation of the drift statistic. NOT VERIFIED — I opened no drift paper's data-availability statement, so this is Unverified, not established.
R3. A zero-true-drift surrogate is realisable. If within-session split-halves stand in for "same neuron, no drift", that presupposes no within-session drift at the split timescale — which the matcher papers themselves do not establish and which UnitMatch's own within-day check cannot test, since the split IS the check. FLAGGED as a new contested assumption the repair creates.

## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

What would count as an answer: For >=2 published matchers at declared operating points: measured false-match and split/merge rates against >=1 identity ground truth that does not use the functional response; a propagation giving, under a declared null of zero true drift, the apparent drift those error rates alone produce in each of >=2 published drift statistics, with CIs; and a comparison against >=2 published drift magnitudes matched on brain area and interval, reporting the share attributable to matching error. A CLEAN NEGATIVE — the induced floor is small relative to published drift at all plausible error rates and all ground-truth constructions — settles it the other way and retires the concern. Both branches are informative, which is what makes it a question and not an agenda: at present the field's reassurance is an argument, not a number (see the EMD paper's "the impact of mismatches is low", quoted and scoped below).

It is a question, not a thesis: the candidate does not assert that drift is an artefact; it asks for the floor and for the comparison, and names the outcome that would retire its own concern.

## Gate 3 — Significance

Consequence: If the floor is comparable to published drift magnitudes at plausible error rates, then part of the electrophysiological representational-drift literature's effect size is identity bookkeeping, and drift papers acquire an obligatory matcher-error term, as imaging papers already report registration false-positive/false-negative rates. If the floor is small, the concern retires and drift in ephys is positively strengthened — which matters because the field's current reassurance is an argument from a non-generalising subset accuracy ("since the accuracy is very high (Figure 4), the impact of mismatches is low") rather than a number. Either branch replaces an argument with a measurement.
Audience: (a) chronic high-density recording labs whose drift claims rest on matched units; (b) the matcher developers themselves — the UnitMatch and EMD groups already write the confound into their discussions and would be the natural producers of the floor; (c) drift theorists who fit time constants to measured drift rates, since an instrument-limited rate would mis-set those constants. Concrete behaviour change: a drift paper reports a matcher-error floor alongside its drift magnitude and states its matcher's operating point, which at present neither matcher paper's consumers are prompted to do.
Fecundity: the same floor logic transfers to cross-day decoder-stability claims, to "stable subset" claims, to separating cell death from drift, and to whether reported drift TIMESCALES are instrument-limited. It also opens the import of track-purity / ID-switch metrics from multi-target tracking and of latent-identity estimators from capture–recapture into ephys, where Q5 and Q11 found no such import.
Anchoring: the standing is-drift-real-or-recording-instability debate (Rule et al., eLife 51121, returned at Q4/Q9 and NOT OPENED); the two matchers' explicitly disclaimed ground truth; and the imaging-registration-error literature that already performs half of this operation on a different instrument.
Significance score: 3/3 — argued from quoted text in both matchers rather than asserted, with a named audience that has already written the confound down.

Reason for silence: A disciplinary-and-incentive boundary with a new-instrument component. The matcher developers own the error rate and have no stake in its consequence for someone else's estimand; the drift consumers own the estimand and treat the matcher as a cited black box. Neither party is penalised for the gap, and the party best placed to close it would be publishing a number that discounts its own tool. The propagation method lives in record linkage, capture–recapture-with-misidentification and multi-target tracking — fields with no citation contact with systems neuroscience in anything Q5, Q6 or Q11 returned, so the method is not sitting in the consuming field's hands. And the instrument is young: routine cross-day matching at this scale arrived with chronic Neuropixels and its matchers (2023–2025), so the consuming literature is younger than the error rates it would need to propagate.

Scores: Well-posedness 3/3 · Significance 3/3
