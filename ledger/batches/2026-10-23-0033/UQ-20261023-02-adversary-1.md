## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

A "constant darkness" or "constant light" circadian experiment is called constant, but nobody weighs the room. The question has two halves. Half one is pure measurement: stick passive loggers (temperature, vibration, sound, CO2, humidity, door openings) inside at least three light-tight chambers in at least two buildings, run them at minute resolution through at least two real experiments, and ask how big the exactly-24-hour component of each channel is, with a confidence interval — and for temperature only, ask whether that amplitude reaches the amplitude known to reset the particular preparation being recorded. Half two is attribution: run one fixed genotype across those same instrumented chambers with chamber assignment randomised, plus a within-facility arm that deliberately imposes a 24-hour cue the size of the one actually measured against a sham, and ask how much of the chamber-to-chamber spread in measured free-running period is traceable to the chambers' own 24-hour content. In one sentence: **is the number we call the organism's endogenous period partly the building's day, and if so how much of it?** A negative result — amplitudes below threshold, imposed arm shifting tau by less than the estimator's own noise — is an answer the question explicitly accepts.

Note before attacking: the record declares the **measurand exemption** on its presupposition 1 (that a 24-hour component exists at all), and it is right to. Limb (a) reports per-channel amplitude with CIs including a null, so that clause is a quantity the question measures, not an assumption it smuggles. Attack 4 below does **not** reach it, and I say so rather than grading around it.
