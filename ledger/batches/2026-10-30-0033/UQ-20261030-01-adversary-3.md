## 1. Restatement (my own words, before any attack)

An ordinary mammalian cell in culture divides into two daughters that look alike. Three different kinds of cellular garbage are present at once: worn-out/depolarised mitochondria, clumps of misfolded cytosolic protein, and pigment-loaded (lipofuscin-autofluorescent) lysosomes. Each of the three is split slightly unevenly. The question asks whether, **within one division**, the three unevennesses point the same way — the same daughter gets more of all three — or are unrelated, or point opposite ways.

Operationally: decide which daughter is "daughter 1" by a rule having nothing to do with the garbage (the one that keeps the older mother centriole); per division and per species compute a signed, volume-normalised asymmetry `a_s = (x1 − x2)/(x1 + x2)`; report the 3×3 matrix of within-division Spearman rank correlations with CIs, in ≥2 symmetrically dividing lines at ≥150 tracked divisions each, against a null made by permuting species labels across divisions, plus an unstressed arm. All-positive = one sorting axis; null-indistinguishable = three independent mechanisms; negative = active routing of different junk to different daughters.

So the thing being asked is: **is there one junk-disposal program per division, or three?**
