## Domain(s)

Medicine — clinical documentation and diagnostic error; primary care and clinical informatics; health equity. Method-owning neighbours: summarization evaluation / information sufficiency (NLP), clinical risk prediction, measurement-bias and algorithmic-fairness methodology, speech technology (ASR) evaluation.

## Question (canonical — Gate 0 intake version, recorded as submitted)

Using the paired encounter transcript and final note that ambient documentation now produces at scale, how much clinically predictive information does the record discard relative to what was said — measured as the gap in predicting a later, independently established diagnosis or outcome from the transcript versus from the note — and is that gap distributed unevenly across patient groups?

## Question (canonical — Gate 1 reformulation, ONE permitted, for well-posedness only; this is the version graded)

In a corpus of primary-care or ambulatory encounters for which both the verbatim encounter transcript and the final signed note are retained, and in which a pre-specified set of index diagnoses/outcomes is independently established from the EHR at or after a pre-specified lag L (adjudication rule fixed in advance, incorporation bias controlled by excluding the index encounter's own coding), what is the difference in out-of-sample discrimination (AUROC/AUPRC), calibration and decision-curve net benefit between a predictor whose input is the transcript and one whose input is the note — estimated within encounters that have both artefacts, under (a) length-matched and summary-matched input controls, (b) a human-transcribed reference subsample isolating ASR loss from documentation loss, and (c) pre-specified subgroup-by-artefact interaction terms (interpreter use, limited language proficiency, documented dysarthria or cognitive impairment, speech rate, accent/first-language group) — and does that difference persist on pre-ambient, human-written notes from recorded encounters?

Both versions are recorded. The reformulation changes nothing substantive; it fixes the estimand (a difference in a named performance functional for a named predictor family, not an unqualified "information" quantity), the lag and adjudication rule, the matched controls, and the subgroup interaction as a pre-specified contrast rather than a post-hoc look.

## Alternative formulations

A1. "Is the clinical note a lossy channel whose discarded content carries diagnostic signal that no downstream reader, human or algorithmic, can recover?" — **REJECTED at Gate 1 as not well-posed.** "No downstream reader can recover" quantifies over an unbounded class of readers and is not settleable by any finite experiment; it should not be carried forward as a formulation of this candidate.
A2. "Now that what was actually said in consultations is recorded at scale, which artefact predicts what turned out to be wrong with the patient better: the transcript or the note written from it?" — retained; this is the lay form of the graded version.

## Motivating gap

The instrument that first makes the spoken encounter auditable at scale is being used almost exclusively to ask whether the machine writes as well as the clinician. Foo et al., 'A comparative analysis of AI scribes versus human documentation in simulated general practice consultations', Aust J Gen Pract 2026;55(4):226–232 compared four commercial AI scribes with GP-written notes across four simulated consultations scored by three blinded raters on a modified ten-domain PDQI-9; omissions entered only as a 'thoroughness' Likert item, with no item-level omission count against the transcript, no consequence grading and no outcome measurement.

**This Gate 2 revises the motivating gap materially.** The omission-against-transcript limb of the gap is no longer open: by August 2026 two companion papers (Fox, Markham, Lail, Karotsieris; arXiv:2608.31016 and arXiv:2608.31017) had built exactly that instrument — item-level omission counting against a transcript-derived audited fact sheet, severity-graded critical/supporting/peripheral, and a verified census of three deployed commercial scribes over 142 consultations and 565 notes reporting 31.3% (95% CI 27.0–35.6) of notes carrying a verified failure. What remains open after reading them is narrower and sharper: nobody has anchored documentation loss to a **downstream outcome** (the predictive-gap estimand), and nobody has stratified loss by **patient attributes**. The candidate should be understood as those two limbs, with the content-inventory limb demoted to a replication/extension of Fox et al.

## Presuppositions (status after Gate 1/2)

- **P1** Paired transcript–note corpora exist at research-usable scale with governance permitting analysis. **CONTESTED and LIMITING.** Fox et al. 2608.31017 assembled 142 consultations × 3 vendors by recording encounters themselves rather than by obtaining retained vendor transcripts, which is indirect evidence that retained production transcripts are hard to get. The irretrievability of transcripts is itself part of what the question exposes, but it caps near-term feasibility: the realistic first instantiation is a prospectively consented, locally retained corpus at one or two health systems, not a population-scale extract.
- **P2** An independently established later diagnosis/outcome exists for the same encounters under a pre-specified lag and adjudication rule. Plausible via EHR linkage; incorporation bias must be handled by excluding the index encounter's own problem-list/coding from the outcome definition.
- **P3** Predictive performance is a defensible proxy for clinically relevant information. **CONTESTED.** A transcript could predict better through paralinguistic, stylistic or interactional correlates of severity (speech rate, hedging, who initiates) rather than through clinical content; the content/style ablation and the human-adjudicated discordant subsample in the answer criteria are therefore load-bearing, not optional. Note that the estimand is also predictor-class-relative: the answer is "how much a stated model family can extract", not an intrinsic channel capacity.
- **P4** The note, not the encounter, is what downstream decisions and note-trained models consume. Plausible and partly checkable from access logs; also supported by the fact that every prior found here uses the transcript only as an audit reference and never as a consumed artefact.
- **P5** Loss is non-random across patient attributes. This is the hypothesis, not an assumption; it does presuppose the attributes are recorded well enough to stratify on, which is weak for accent/first language and for interpreter use (documentation of interpreter use is itself known to be unreliable — one of the Gate 2 hits was a quality-improvement study on improving physician documentation *of interpreter use*).
- **P6** The comparison is not an artefact of input length or predictor capacity. Requires the length- and summary-matched controls now written into the reformulation.
- **P7** Transcription itself is not the dominant error source. **CONTESTED**; requires the human-transcribed reference subsample. Fox et al. 2608.31017's finding that errors concentrate in telephone consultations is consistent with an audio-quality channel that must be separated out.
- **P8** Clinicians, vendors or standard-setters would act. Plausible; scribe procurement, documentation standards and medico-legal review are live 2026 decisions, and the coding/revenue world is already framing omissions as consequential (an omitted chronic condition as a lost HCC).

## What would count as an answer

On a paired corpus under a pre-registered protocol: (i) the predictive gap (discrimination, calibration, net benefit) for a pre-specified set of later-established diagnoses, transcript versus note, with length- and summary-matched controls, a human-transcribed subsample isolating ASR loss, and human adjudication of discordant cases establishing that transcript-only signal is clinical content rather than style; (ii) an inventory of the recoverable content (symptom qualifiers, explicit negatives, time course, social context, patient-raised concerns never addressed) — **now largely pre-empted by Fox et al. 2608.31016, so this limb counts as replication plus outcome-anchoring, not as discovery**; (iii) the gap's interaction with pre-specified patient attributes; (iv) the same analysis on human-written notes from encounters recorded before ambient deployment, so the result is about documentation rather than about AI. A null — notes carry what transcripts carry, for the stated predictor family and outcome set — would be a strong and currently unavailable defence of documentation practice and of note-trained models.

- Estimand: whether automated judges detect omitted facts as well as added/altered content, and what prompt design recovers detection. ## Gate 3 — Significance

**Consequence.** If the gap is large: the note is not a sufficient statistic for the encounter with respect to outcomes clinicians care about, which has three distinct consequences, each with a decision attached. (1) Retention policy: transcripts become part of the record that must be kept, which is a governance and medico-legal change, not a quality-improvement one. (2) Documentation targets change from "faithful and readable" to "predictively sufficient" — a scorable objective that scribe vendors and standard-setters could specify, replacing PDQI-9-style rater scores that Prior 2 shows move by a factor of eight when the review instruction changes. (3) Every note-trained clinical model inherits a known, quantified ceiling, and the ceiling is measurable rather than speculative. If the gap is null: that is a strong and currently unavailable defence of documentation practice and of note-trained models, and it retires the retention argument. If the gap is large *and* differential by patient group: documentation becomes an identified mechanism of inequity that sits upstream of every model-fairness intervention in the field — the fairness literature found at query #11 audits models over notes and has no instrument for a differentially lossy input channel.

**Audience, and what they would do differently.** Scribe purchasers and health-system informatics teams (procurement criteria shift from rater-scored note quality to retained-signal measures; the 31.3% failure census already gives them a number, this would give them a consequence). Documentation standard-setters and specialty bodies (RCGP/ACP-style documentation guidance; the AJGP comparative-evaluation genre is already in their literature). Clinical-AI developers who train on notes (would have a reason to train on, or at least evaluate against, transcripts). Health-equity researchers and language-access programmes (a measurable target upstream of model fairness). Medico-legal reviewers and regulators (a note demonstrated to discard outcome-relevant content changes what "the record" means). Each of these is a community with live 2026 decisions, not a hypothetical audience.

**Fecundity.** New askable questions: what is the predictively sufficient note — i.e. can a documentation target be optimized against retained signal rather than rater score? Should the record be two-tier (transcript + note) and what does that do to clinician and patient behaviour once both know it is kept? Does the loss concentrate in the content classes Prior 2 identifies (allergies, medications, history-written-as-examination) or in the classes communication research identifies (patient-initiated psychosocial content, per Prior 3)? Does a transcript-trained model's advantage survive deployment, or does it decay as clinicians learn the transcript is read? What is the analogous gap for other summarizing artefacts — handover notes, discharge summaries, radiology reports (ledger UQ-20261016-07's object)?

**Anchoring.** Diagnostic error and missed/delayed diagnosis (the omitted qualifier or time course is the documented failure mode in malpractice review). The note-bloat-versus-note-loss debate, which currently has volume metrics and no loss metric. Distribution shift and input-degradation ceilings for EHR-trained models. Language-access and interpreter-use disparities, where the measurement instrument has been missing. The summarization-sufficiency problem (COSMIC) — this would be its first clinical, outcome-anchored instance.

**Significance score: 3/3** for the reformulated two-limb question (predictive gap + differential loss). Noted honestly: the content-inventory limb alone would now score 1/3, because Fox et al. have done it.

## Reason for silence (candidate mechanism)

Three mechanisms compound, and none of them is "no one thought of it".

1. **New instrument, used for the procurement question it was built to answer.** Paired transcript–note artefacts at scale are 2023–2026 objects. The question that paid for the instrument is "does the machine write as well as the clinician", so the whole first wave of work — Foo et al., the Fox et al. census, the vendor note-completeness products — takes the transcript as the *gold standard* and asks whether the note matches it. Treating the transcript instead as a *rival input* and asking which artefact predicts the patient's future is a different use of the same data that the procurement question does not generate.

2. **Disciplinary boundary between the field that owns the data and the field that owns the method.** Documentation-fidelity research descends from communication and qualitative primary-care research (Prior 3's author list is a communication-research group) and its native endpoint is "was it documented". Outcome-anchored prediction with calibration, net benefit and pre-specified subgroup interactions lives in clinical risk prediction; the sufficiency-of-a-summary framing lives in summarization evaluation (COSMIC). The people with the transcripts do not use the second field's endpoints, and the people with the endpoints have never had transcripts — they have only ever had notes, which is exactly presupposition P4 and is why the comparison never occurred to the second group as available.

3. **An incentive-and-retention gap that destroys the comparator.** Vendors retain transcripts variably and often transiently, partly for medico-legal reasons; a deleted transcript makes the loss unmeasurable by construction. The actors who could fund the study are the ones whose exposure grows if the answer is "the note discards outcome-relevant content" — and the audit genre they do fund (rater-scored note quality) is, per Prior 2, elastic to the review instruction in a way an outcome endpoint would not be. The equity limb carries an additional version of this: it requires stratifiers (interpreter use, first language) that are themselves documented badly enough that query #9 returned quality-improvement work on *getting interpreter use documented at all*.
