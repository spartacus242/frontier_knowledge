## Gate 0 — Intake

**Question (canonical), V2 — the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation, made solely for well-posedness; V1 below.**

For a pre-declared set of payer-by-service cells drawn from the first years of CMS-0057-F public prior-authorization reporting, what fraction of prior-authorization-requiring clinical decisions terminate on the provider side *before any request reaches the payer*, and does cross-payer variation in the published denial rate predict that pre-submission attrition rate with a negative sign — in what share of cells does adding attrition reverse the ranking of payers by obstruction, against a flip threshold fixed before any data pull?

**V1 (as intaken, retained per Gate 1):** Now that payers must publish prior-authorization approval, denial and appeal percentages on denominators of requests they received, what fraction of prior-authorization-requiring clinical decisions terminate before a request ever reaches the payer, and is the published denial rate informative about obstruction once that pre-submission attrition is measured — does cross-payer and cross-service variation in reported denial rate predict pre-submission attrition with a negative sign, and in what share of payer-by-service cells would adding attrition reverse the ranking of payers?

**Why V2 differs, and both changes are corrections of fact established first-hand at Gate 2, not a change of object:**

1. V1 said the deterrence channel includes "a request abandoned mid-flight" and that neither channel "can appear in" the metric. **That is false for at least one real published file.** The DHCS Medi-Cal CY2025 report (opened; see Gate 2 F4) prints a line "Request auto-denied after extension* 9,439 78,987 12%" with the footnote "Extensions requesting more information are denied automatically by the system if the provider does not respond." Mid-flight provider abandonment is therefore *visible* in the published metric and *counted as a denial* — it is misclassified, not invisible. (9,439/78,987 = 11.95%, my own arithmetic, consistent with the file's printed 12%.) V2 restricts the object to strictly **pre-submission** attrition and records the mid-flight channel as a separate, oppositely-signed finding (below).
2. V1's "cross-service variation" limb has **no denominator in the instrument as actually published in year 1**: per KFF (opened) the reports are "aggregated across all items and services" with "no breakdown by service type." V2 keeps payer-by-service cells as the pre-declared design target but the cross-service limb is now conditional on a payer reporting at service granularity (Washington and Massachusetts are named by KFF as doing more by service category); its non-availability in year 1 is itself reportable and is scored against well-posedness below.

**Alternative formulations:** (a) Does a low published prior-authorization denial rate mean a payer approves more care, or that fewer requests were ever filed? (b) Treating the new public prior-authorization metrics as an instrument with an endogenous denominator, estimate pre-submission attrition and the bias it induces in the metric as a measure of access to care.

**Motivating gap.** The instrument is established at first hand. The CMS "Prior authorization metrics reporting overview template" page (READ LEVEL: full page via fetch, summarised, at intake) requires impacted payers to publish, from the previous calendar year and beginning in 2026, the list of items and services requiring prior authorization and, for standard and expedited requests, percentage approved, percentage denied, percentage approved after appeal, mean and median response times, and the percentage extended-and-approved; denominators are total standard or expedited requests, total appeals and total requests, and response time is "measured from when the payer receives the request, not when the provider sends it." Every published figure is conditioned on a request having arrived, so care never requested cannot appear in it, and a payer could in principle lower its reported denial rate by making submission harder. Gate 2 both strengthened and complicated this: analysts are *already* ranking named payers on these percentages (Becker's, 13 Aug 2026, off a KFF analysis of the first CMS-required reports for CY2025), and the nearest prior has *already* identified one denominator-side confound — gold carding — while naming nothing on the provider side.

**Presuppositions (status after Gate 2):**

- P1. The public metrics' denominator is conditioned on a request having arrived at the payer. **ESTABLISHED, but NOT as uniformly "requests received."** The CMS template says received; the DHCS Medi-Cal file prints its denominators as requests "that were adjudicated (decided) within the measurement period" while its own narrative says DHCS "received a total of 78,987 prior authorization requests" — the file is internally inconsistent. The arrival-conditioning survives either way (adjudicated ⊂ received); the clean statement "the denominator is requests received" does not. Any design must declare per-payer which denominator it read.
- P2. Prior-authorization-requiring decisions that never become requests are identifiable from provider-side records. **CONTESTED, unchanged.** Requires a pre-declared rule over cancelled orders, documented "not pursued," and electronic prior-authorization transactions begun and not completed; the measurable fraction is a lower bound and must be reported as such. Gate 2 found no study that has done this (see F6).
- P3. Payers' published requirement lists can be matched to clinical orders. **PARTLY ESTABLISHED and cuts both ways**, unchanged; matching error needs a sensitivity arm.
- P4. Payers have an incentive to move the metric through submission friction. **CONTESTED — this is the hypothesis the sign test addresses, not an assumption the design may make.** Gate 2 adds a reason to doubt the predicted *sign*: the auto-denial-on-non-response category pushes provider non-completion *into* the numerator as a denial, so post-submission abandonment raises the reported rate while pre-submission attrition lowers it. The two channels oppose each other and a design that does not separate them can recover a null for the wrong reason.
- P5. Clinically equivalent substitution is distinguishable from abandonment. **CONTESTED**, unchanged; needs a pre-declared classification.
- P6. Enough payers and services report comparably for a cross-sectional comparison. **WEAKENED, not merely unverified.** Opened sources establish: no service-type breakdown in the national files; percentages only, not counts ("Payers are required to report only percentages, not the number of prior authorization requests they received or denied"); inconsistent methodologies and reporting locations across payers; prescription drugs excluded. The payer-by-*payer* cross-section is constructible in year 1; the payer-by-*service* cross-section largely is not.
- P7 (new, from the source method). The quantity "became public for the first time in 2026." **FALSE as stated.** State-level public prior-authorization reporting predates CMS-0057-F: Mississippi Insurance Department annual prior-authorization reports (2025 and March 2025 files, TITLES ONLY) and a Hawaii SHPDA prior-authorization reporting guide (TITLE ONLY) surfaced in search, and KFF names Washington and Massachusetts as already publishing more detailed service-category analysis. The correct claim is "first time nationally, on a uniform federal specification."

## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

**What would count as an answer.** For a pre-declared set of payer cells (and service cells where a payer reports at that granularity), paired estimates of (i) the published denial rate as printed, and (ii) the provider-side pre-submission attrition rate — prior-authorization-requiring decisions terminating before transmission, over PA-requiring decisions identified by a matching rule declared before any data pull — with the sign and magnitude of their cross-cell association under one primary estimator plus declared sensitivity arms for matching error, for the substitution-versus-abandonment classification, and for the received-versus-adjudicated denominator; and the share of cells whose obstruction ranking flips when attrition is included, against a flip threshold fixed in advance. A null (attrition small and uncorrelated with the published rate) answers the question and validates the metric as specified. A negative association of stated size says the published metric is partly a measure of submission friction — a respecification question CMS alone can act on. The design must report the post-submission auto-denial share separately, since it loads the same metric with the opposite sign.

It is a question, not a thesis: the sign is the object of the test, the null is a publishable answer, and P4 is carried as a hypothesis rather than an assumption.

**Well-posedness score: 2/3.** Against: one named limb (cross-service variation) has no denominator in the instrument as published in year 1, so part of the question is currently unanswerable rather than merely hard; the attrition denominator rests on a contested matching rule (P3) over requirement lists that CMS has deliberately made non-machine-readable in the sense that a bare procedure-code list does not satisfy the rule; and "obstruction ranking" needs its own pre-declared definition, since the reported rate, attrition, gold-carding exposure and auto-denial misclassification are four different things any of which could be called obstruction. For: the primary estimand, the sign test, the flip-threshold and the sensitivity arms are stateable in advance, and both answers are informative.

## Gate 3 — Significance

**Consequence (argued).** If attrition is substantial and negatively associated with the published denial rate, then the metric CMS specified is partly a gauge of submission friction, a published ranking of payers by denial rate is partly inverted, and the fix is a respecification CMS alone can make — an exposure denominator (decisions subject to authorization, as the gold-carding point already implies) or a reported attrition/withdrawal category, plus separating the auto-denial-on-non-response cases that currently load the numerator the other way. If attrition is small and uncorrelated, the metric is validated as specified against its sharpest available criticism, which is worth having on the record precisely because the trade press is already ranking payers on it. Either answer changes what a reader is entitled to conclude from a number now being published annually.

**Audience (named, real, and already acting).** CMS owns the specification and has a live PA rulemaking to attach it to (a MAPRx comment letter on a June 2026 PA proposed rule surfaced in search; TITLE ONLY). KFF and the trade press are ranking payers on these files *now* (P3, 13 Aug 2026) and have already published one denominator critique, so the audience for a second one is demonstrated rather than assumed. Provider organisations and specialty societies cite PA burden and would gain a quantity on their own side of the wire. Payers would face a metric that no longer rewards friction.

**Fecundity.** Makes askable: whether submission friction *responds* to publication (CY2025 is a pre-publication baseline, so a Goodhart test has a clean pre-period); whether the auto-denial-on-non-response category should be reclassified, and how much of the reported 12%–18% denial range it is; whether the appeal-side analogue ("only 7.3% of denials were appealed") has the same structure; whether an exposure denominator can be built from gold-carding rosters plus requirement lists; and whether the queueing estimators for unobserved balking (F5) can be applied to a regulatory metric at all — a transplant with no precedent found.

**Anchoring.** Touches the administrative-burden/ordeal literature's core claim that procedural denials and non-take-up are produced by burden rather than by eligibility (F6); the queueing problem of inference under unobserved balking and silent abandonment (F5); the measured abandonment literatures in pharmacy and oncology, which give levels but never the metric's invariance; and, inside this ledger, the "a CMS measure may not measure what it is read as measuring" family (UQ-20261017-18 and the four entries flagged with it).

**Significance score: 3/3.**

## Reason for silence (candidate mechanism)

A **measurement-boundary split reinforced by an incentive gap, on an eight-month-old instrument.** The metric was created by a rule about payer APIs, so it is specified, computed and published entirely by payers; the complementary quantity exists only in provider EHR and ePA logs. The analytic community that works from payer files — health-policy analysts, KFF, consultancies — literally cannot see the attrition term and has reached for the confounds it *can* see from those files (granularity, denial reasons, gold carding, volume), which is exactly the pattern the opened sources show. The community that can see attrition is provider-side revenue cycle and clinical informatics, for whom the quantity is a measure of their own submission performance and therefore not something to publish, and who have no standing to ask a question about a federal metric's specification. Neither side owns both terms. Add that the first files appeared in 2026, that P7's earlier state-level reporting was fragmentary enough not to provoke the comparison, and that the natural paper title ("prior authorization abandonment") is already taken by the pharmacy and oncology levels literature, which absorbs the search traffic that would otherwise land on this question.
