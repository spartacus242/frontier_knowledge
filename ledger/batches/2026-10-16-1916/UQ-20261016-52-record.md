Domain(s): AI architecture — pretraining/post-training pipeline design, and the construct validity of the released "base" checkpoint used as the pre-post-training control throughout post-training research. Method-owning neighbours: trial/causal-inference methodology (control-arm contamination and effect attenuation), measurement validity / metascience of ML.

Question (canonical, Gate 0 as intaken):
Across the released "base" checkpoints that research uses as the pre-post-training control, how much post-training-like behaviour (refusal on harmful requests, chat-template preference, zero-shot instruction following, assistant-persona self-reference) is already present relative to a documented pre-instruction-era reference base, how far does that presence track instruction-formatted content in the pretraining corpus where the corpus is open, and by how much does it bias published estimates of what post-training adds?

Question (Gate 1 reformulation — the single permitted reformulation, for well-posedness only; both versions retained):
On a pre-registered frame of released base checkpoints dated by release and paired with their own instruct/chat siblings: (i) what is the rate of each of four pre-specified post-training-like behaviours (refusal on a fixed harmful-instruction set under fixed decoding, preference for the sibling's chat template over a neutral format, zero-shot instruction compliance, first-person assistant-persona self-reference) in the base checkpoint, with CIs, expressed as a fraction of the base-to-chat gap, and how does that fraction trend against release date and against the releaser's disclosed mid-training/annealing practice; (ii) on the open-data subset only, what is the instruction/chat-formatted token share of the pretraining corpus under a stated detector with reported precision and recall, and does it predict (i) across checkpoints; and (iii) for at least two specific published "post-training adds X" effect estimates, how much does the point estimate move, relative to its own stated uncertainty, when the released base is replaced by a cleaner control (a pre-mid-training/pre-anneal checkpoint, or a dated pre-instruction-era base)?
Gate 1 note: the reformulation separates two things the intake version fuses — PRESENCE of the behaviour in the control (which is what control validity turns on, whatever its cause) from PROVENANCE of the behaviour (contamination by instruction-formatted data vs imitation of web text in which requests are declined). Limb (ii) is the only provenance evidence on offer and it is available only where the corpus is open, so the question must be posed as presence-first with provenance as a separable sub-finding. The intake phrase "post-training contamination" presupposes provenance and is dropped from the canonical wording.

Alternative formulations:
- Are "base" models still base — and if not, is every chat-minus-base comparison measuring a shrunken difference?
- What is the degree of post-training-like behaviour in the released pretraining-stage artifact, and what happens to published "alignment tuning does X" effect sizes when a cleaner control is substituted?

Motivating gap (source method: presupposition inversion):
A measured anomaly nobody has turned into this question. The AlignmentForum post "Base LLMs refuse too" (read in full via WebFetch at intake, 2026-10-16; https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/YWo2cKJgL7Lg8xWjj/base-llms-refuse-too) scores 100 JailbreakBench harmful instructions under greedy decoding and reports released BASE models refusing ~48% on average against ~90% for their chat siblings, with ~3% refusal on harmless prompts in both, across Qwen1.5-0.5B, Gemma-2-9B and LLaMA-1-7B. The post explicitly declines to attribute a cause (post-ChatGPT leakage and synthetic/filtered pretraining data appear only as caveats; it notes LLaMA-1 predates ChatGPT and still refuses ~half) and does NOT argue that base models are invalid controls — its claim is only that refusal is not solely a fine-tuning product, offered as motivation for model diffing. Commenters raise, without measuring, whether some released bases are truly untuned. Meanwhile labs now openly run curated mid-training/annealing stages with instruction-like and synthetic data before releasing the base, while a large literature — superficial-alignment claims, crosscoder/model-diffing studies, "fine-tuning removes safety", elicitation and "what RL adds" work — uses that released base as the untreated arm.

Presuppositions:
1. A credible "clean" reference base exists — a model whose pretraining corpus is documented free of instruction/preference-formatted data. CONTESTED, and the hardest premise. Candidates are pre-2023 models and open-data models whose corpora can be searched. Failing an absolute zero the question must be posed as a gradient across release dates and disclosed mid-training practice, which the reformulation does.
2. The named behaviours are measurable in base models above a stated null. ESTABLISHED by the cited measurement (~48% vs ~3%), for refusal only; the other three behaviours are presumed measurable but the intake cites no measurement of them in base checkpoints.
3. Instruction/chat-formatted content in a pretraining corpus is detectable by corpus search with reportable detector precision. PLAUSIBLE, UNVERIFIED — see the Gate 2 note on the unread WIMBD/Dolma-search line of work; the nearest published detector-over-reference-corpus number I did open (Pew/Open Pangram, below) is a different construct.
4. Published studies do in fact use released bases as the untreated control. ESTABLISHABLE by a countable literature audit; partially corroborated here, since arXiv:2507.10532 (opened) treats "beyond the base model's performance boundary" as the estimand of the RL literature it criticises, and arXiv:2312.01552 (opened) takes "the aligned model versus its base model" as its comparator.
5. Behavioural presence implies control bias in a direction that matters. CONTESTED — a base may decline requests by imitating web text in which requests are declined; behaviour alone cannot separate imitation from instruction-data contamination. This is why limb (ii) is load-bearing rather than decorative. Note that for control VALIDITY the distinction may not matter (a control arm already exhibiting the outcome attenuates the contrast either way), while for the PIPELINE-DESIGN claim it is decisive.
6. Base and chat siblings are otherwise comparable artifacts. CONTESTED — some releasers ship a base that is not the exact parent of the released chat model.

What would count as an answer:
(i) A pre-registered behavioural battery run across a dated frame of released base checkpoints and their chat siblings, reporting each behaviour's rate with CIs, as a fraction of the base-to-chat gap, and its trend against release date and against the releaser's disclosed mid-training practice.
(ii) For the open-data subset, corpus-search estimates of instruction/chat-formatted token share with detector precision/recall reported, tested against the behavioural rates.
(iii) Re-estimation of at least two published "post-training adds X" effects using the released base versus a cleaner control (e.g. a released pre-anneal checkpoint), reporting the change in effect size against its stated uncertainty.
YES: presence rises with release date, tracks instruction-formatted corpus share, and re-estimation moves published effects beyond their intervals.
NO: base behaviours are flat across eras, uncorrelated with corpus content, and substituting the control moves nothing.

Significance — consequence:
If YES: the untreated arm used across a large literature is itself partly treated, and increasingly so with release date, so every chat-minus-base quantity — crosscoder/model-diffing feature differences, "fine-tuning removes safety guardrails" deltas, "what RL adds" boundaries, elicitation and pass@k ceilings, and superficial-alignment estimates including URIAL's own — is a shrunken difference with an era-dependent shrinkage factor. That converts a qualitative worry into a correction term and creates a cheap, concrete ask of the labs: release the pre-mid-training/pre-anneal checkpoint as a research control and state which stage the shipped "base" is. It would also make "base model" a graded, disclosed pipeline stage rather than a binary label.
If NO (flat across eras, uncorrelated with corpus content, substitution moves nothing): the released base is vindicated as a control, and the AlignmentForum anomaly is localised to pretraining text itself — refusal would be a property of the web-text genre (requests get declined in human text) rather than a product of either fine-tuning or mid-training curation. That is not a null result: it redirects safety work from "fine-tuning instils refusal" toward "pretraining distribution contains refusal", and it tells interpretability groups that base-vs-chat diffing is measuring style and format rather than the acquisition of the behaviour.

Significance — audience:
(a) Interpretability / model-diffing groups running crosscoder base-vs-chat studies — would have to add a control-stage covariate or re-run against a pre-anneal checkpoint, and would re-read features currently attributed to post-training. The motivating post itself cites base-vs-chat diffing as its use case, so this audience is already looking at the artifact.
(b) Safety-fine-tuning researchers publishing "fine-tuning removes safety" and "alignment is superficial" effect sizes — would report deltas against a stated control stage.
(c) RL-for-reasoning researchers using base pass@k as the capability ceiling — the same group arXiv:2507.10532 already addressed, who have shown they act on control-validity arguments.
(d) The labs and hubs that release base checkpoints (AI2/OLMo, Qwen, Meta, HF) — the actionable ask is publishing a pre-mid-training checkpoint and a stage label; OLMo-style open-data releases make this a low-cost change for at least one releaser.
(e) ML metascience / eval-methodology reviewers — a reporting requirement ("which base stage was the control?") is the kind of norm this community does install.

Significance — fecundity:
Opens: what is the dose-response of mid-training instruction-like data on measured post-training gains (is the apparent diminishing return of post-training over releases partly a drifting control)? Can a base checkpoint be de-contaminated for control purposes, and does de-contamination restore the old effect sizes? Should "base" be a disclosed graded stage with a published behavioural fingerprint? If refusal is partly a web-text genre property, which other "alignment" behaviours are genre properties, and can they be predicted from corpus composition alone? Does the behavioural fingerprint of a base checkpoint let a third party infer undisclosed mid-training practice — i.e. a pipeline-audit instrument built from behaviour rather than disclosure?

Significance — anchoring:
Touches the superficial-alignment dispute (LIMA, URIAL arXiv:2312.01552, arXiv:2502.04602); the live argument over whether RL expands a base model's capability boundary (arXiv:2507.10532, arXiv:2601.11061); the "fine-tuning strips safety" literature; refusal-direction mechanistic work (arXiv:2504.16980 and the single-direction result); benchmark-contamination methodology (arXiv:2601.06103); and the broader emergent-abilities-as-measurement-artifact debate, of which this is the control-side analogue.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism — disciplinary boundary plus artifact unavailability plus an incentive gap; not "nobody thought of it"):
(1) Conceptual/disciplinary: ML treats base-and-chat as a MODEL PAIR, not as a treatment-and-control design, so the validity question lives in vocabulary (control-arm contamination, attenuation of a contrast, crossover) that the field does not import — query 11 shows the framing returns nothing in ML's own words. Where the worry has surfaced, the field had a ready-made concept, "benchmark contamination", that absorbed it (arXiv:2507.10532) and stopped it generalising from the benchmark to the control arm itself.
(2) Artifact unavailability: the cleaner control does not exist publicly. Releasers ship the post-anneal base only, so the obvious design is unrunnable without lab cooperation or an open-data releaser's intermediate checkpoints — and questions whose answer requires an unreleased artifact are not asked, they are shelved. Mid-training as an openly described, curated stage is also only two or three release generations old, so the gradient the question needs has only recently become long enough to measure.
(3) Incentive: a positive finding deflates published effect sizes across a large literature, including the asker's own prior work, and deflates releasers' "post-training adds X" narrative; nobody's publication is improved by discovering that their control was contaminated.
