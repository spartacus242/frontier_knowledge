Domain(s): Computer science — programming-language runtimes and memory management (method transplanted from demographic age–period–cohort analysis)

Question (canonical): For a managed-runtime program traced to exact object death times on a single declared clock (bytes allocated), does the death hazard of a heap object depend only on its age, or do the identifiable curvatures of the death-time (period) and allocation-epoch (cohort) axes differ from zero — reporting (i) second-difference curvature sequences for the period and cohort axes with intervals, (ii) a pre-declared likelihood-ratio test of the age-only submodel, and (iii) nested deviance reductions under ALL orderings of the three axes, since under p = c + a no unique split exists? (This is V2, the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation, and the version scored; V1 asked in its third clause for a single deviance "split" that the exact dependence it invokes makes non-identifiable.)

Alternative formulations:
- Is the age-specific mortality of heap objects stationary in program progress, or does the rate at which objects of a given age die change with when in the run they are dying and with when they were allocated?
- Lay heap-object deaths on a Lexis surface with an at-risk denominator: do the off-age axes carry estimable curvature beyond age, and how much deviance?

Motivating gap: The generational hypothesis is an age-only statement about mortality. The field has defined the hazard, measured its age marginal, and printed the raw two-way death tabulation — but no at-risk-denominated rate off the age axis, no period or cohort curvature, no deviance decomposition, and no statement of the identification obstruction. Dolan (ISMM 2025, abstract) states that of the generational hypothesis "it is not obvious how it can be measured as a property of a program (independent of a particular GC strategy)" and proposes lifetime dispersion (Gini) instead — a move away from a hazard framing. Correction carried from adversary 3: the record's claim that the field "has not named the cohort axis" is struck — Yak (OSDI 2016) names it as the "epochal hypothesis".

Depth (object test; deletion test): Object — the answer would be a fact about the heap objects of running programs and their mortality (a running system behaving; on the pass list). Deletion — if every paper, database and protocol vanished tonight while runtimes and released software stayed as they are, running programs and their heaps would still fix the hazard surface. PASS at Gates 0–3 and PASS on all three adversaries' independent restatements; one row is one allocated heap object with its birth and death byte-times. Adversary 2's reservation, routed to vagueness and not to depth: limb (iii) alone is a property of a declared model plus an ordering convention, so limbs (i)–(ii) carry the Pass.

Presuppositions:
1. Exact object death times are obtainable on a bytes-allocated clock. ESTABLISHED, sourced at full text by two adversaries on Merlin (TOPLAS 2005/2006): "A perfect trace ... includes the birth and death time of all objects, measured in bytes allocated"; death time is "the time interval immediately following the one when it was last reachable". Cost 70–300x tracing slowdown; "exact" means exact to the allocation at which the clock ticks.
2. p = c + a exactly on one clock. True by arithmetic, and sourced in that both birth and death are measured in bytes allocated.
3. Under that exact dependence only curvature-type quantities are identifiable. UNVERIFIED by the record and by all three adversaries — nobody opened an age–period–cohort identification primary source; corroborated only at title level (arXiv:2112.08299, 2312.09698) and, at abstract level, as "the structural link identification problem".
4. Within-cell exchangeability sufficient for a plain Poisson/GLM with CIs and an LRT. CONTESTED on an opened source: Veroy & Guyer, Onward!'17 Sec. 3.6, "While all objects in a garbage cluster die at the same time, they are all allocated at different times"; Jones & Ryder cite "objects that are allocated together, die together"; Yak Sec. 1.1, "many data-path objects have the same life span and can be reclaimed together at the end of an epoch". Repairable by a frailty or cluster-robust specification without changing the estimand.
5. The record's "pre-declared phase rule" (its self-named sharpest presupposition) is NOT a clause of V2: V2's period axis is death time on the bytes clock, so only a bin width need be pre-declared. All three adversaries found this independently. The record's "phase" prose should be aligned to V2's "death time"; it is a self-description defect, not a false premise.
6. Undeclared and load-bearing: cell width and exposure unit. Every curvature and deviance number scales with them.

What would count as an answer: For at least five programs traced under an exact-liveness oracle, a fitted hazard model of object death reporting the three axes' curvature sequences with intervals, a pre-declared likelihood-ratio test of the age-only submodel, and nested deviance reductions under all orderings. Non-zero period or cohort curvature with the age-only submodel rejected answers in the multi-axis direction; curvature confined to age vindicates the age-only mechanism with the first measured bound on what the other axes can carry.

3. Stefanović, McKinley & Moss, "On Models for Object Lifetime Distributions (short paper)", full text (cs.unm.edu/~darko/papers/on-models.pdf; venue inferred from a URL path, flagged not asserted). DEFINES the quantity — m(t) = f(t)/s(t), "the age-specific death rate", "also known as the hazard function" — and BOUNDS the generational argument with it, but tests distributional families by skewness-versus-CV moment ratios and fits no hazard. Fourth neighbour, added on adversary 3's finding and absent from the record: Nguyen et al., "Yak", OSDI 2016, full text. ANNOUNCES the cohort axis qualitatively — "We call this the epochal hypothesis"; "The lifetimes of objects in the data path often align with epochs creating them" — and scopes it to the data path of data-intensive applications while saying the generational hypothesis "holds for the control path". On a dedicated sweep it prints no hazard, no age decomposition and no epoch-alignment fraction; its one printed share (0.0028%–1.3%, Table 4) is objects escaping a region at retirement — a different numerator on a different denominator. Fifth, from adversary 2: Merlin Sec. 8.1 Fig. 12(c) prints a "demise map" (bytes allocated x age-ordered heap position, death density) — a second unmodelled picture with no denominator, which strengthens rather than defeats the gap claim.

Novelty — how this differs from each: The record can name NO prior number for the candidate's quantitative limbs, and three adversaries re-checking the two closest neighbours at full text confirm it. What the sources do, graded on their actual acts: prior 3 and TR 98-81 DEFINE the hazard and assume stationarity; prior 2 MEASURES AND PRINTS the age marginal in ppm/byte (so "no heap hazard-of-age has ever been measured" is false and is struck); prior 1 and Merlin PRINT the raw two-way tabulation as volume/density with no at-risk denominator; Yak ANNOUNCES the cohort axis without a rate. The candidate's estimand — the period and cohort curvatures of an exposure-denominated rate and the deviance they carry beyond age — appears in none of them. Without a denominator one cannot separate a high death rate at an age from a large number of objects reaching it, which is the whole distance between what is in print and what is asked.

Significance — consequence: If the off-age curvatures are materially non-zero and the age-only submodel rejected, the one variable a generational collector observes cheaply is not the one carrying the mortality signal, and promotion and pretenuring policy condition on the wrong axis by a measured amount. If curvature is confined to age, the age-only mechanism is vindicated with the first measured bound on what the other axes could contribute, and Dolan's stated measurement difficulty gets an answer in collector-independent hazard terms.

Significance — audience: Runtime and collector implementers (OpenJDK G1/ZGC, .NET, V8, OCaml 5 — Dolan's ISMM 2025 paper comes from that quarter), the ISMM community, and the pretenuring and lifetime-prediction line descending from Jones & Ryder's site clustering. Real and currently active on this exact measurement question; the consequence drawn from it is overstated, per attack 6.

Significance — fecundity: Turns "which allocation epochs produce long-lived objects" into a prediction problem with a correct denominator; supplies a collector-independent definition of a GC-relevant phase change where Jones & Ryder's phases are hand-identified from their own plots; and opens competing risks (Veroy & Guyer's causes of death as risk types), frailty models for site heterogeneity, and Dolan's Gini recovered as a functional of a fitted hazard.

Significance — anchoring: Dolan's ISMM 2025 measurement gap; the strong generational hypothesis stated as a monotonicity condition on m(t) that nobody has tested as such; Jones & Ryder's and Merlin's unmodelled death surfaces; Yak's age-versus-epoch tension, argued qualitatively and never decomposed; older-first collection, whose premise is an age-axis anomaly.

Reason for silence: A vocabulary half-crossing survives all three sweeps — the runtime field imported demography's nouns (demographics, mortality, generations, infant mortality) but took the lifetime DISTRIBUTION as its object rather than the rate-with-exposure, and the identification result has no currency in programming languages; demographic apparatus has zero computing footprint in English, French or German. The instrument-timing mechanism is sourced (brute-force death times cost "over 3 months" per suite before Merlin; 70–300x even with it) but weakened by adversary 3: Merlin predates the 2008, 2016 and 2017 trace studies, which had the traces and asked about sites, epochs and causes instead. The honest third mechanism is that qualitative and volume evidence sufficed for the engineering decisions actually taken — which explains why nobody needed the decomposition rather than why nobody asked.

Strongest remaining objection: V2 reports curvature sequences and nested deviance reductions as its headline quantities while declaring neither the cell width nor the exposure unit, and both scale with that choice — second differences over 1 KB period cells and over 1 MB period cells are different sequences, and a deviance reduction rises with the number of cells an axis is given. The verdict stands on this leg ALONE: it is a reading of the question's own text, landed independently by all three adversaries, and needs no source. The other two legs would fall without disturbing it — (b) the exchangeability defect, whose empirical half is sourced verbatim to Veroy & Guyer Sec. 3.6 at full text but whose load-bearing half (clustering makes the LRT anti-conservative and the intervals too narrow) its author labels his own statistics reasoning with no primary source and no search run; and (c) Yak as a renamed neighbour, which reaches the hypothesis and not the quantity. Vulnerability of this capping objection, recorded because nobody measured the thing it leans on: the frame it criticises — that only second differences are identifiable — is an ALGEBRAIC identity about an APC design matrix under p = c + a, not a bitwise or empirical finding, and it is asserted from memory by the record and left UNVERIFIED by all three adversaries, who each judged other clauses likelier to fail.
