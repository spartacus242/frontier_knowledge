## Question (canonical, Gate 0 as submitted)

In laboratories and pilot plants with a documented handling history of a compound that has a metastable and a stable polymorph, is crystallographically active seed material of the stable form recoverable from air and surfaces at quantities above the threshold that determines which form crystallizes, and with what persistence after handling stops?

## Question (canonical, Gate 1 REFORMULATION — the one permitted reformulation, used, for well-posedness only)

For a compound with a still-preparable metastable form and a stable form, and a panel of rooms stratified by documented handling history of the stable form: (a) what quantity of the stable form, introduced as recovered surface/air residue into a standardized seed-controlled supersaturated crystallization that otherwise yields the metastable form, flips the outcome to the stable form — the flip threshold, established within the same study by a spiked dose-response rather than presupposed; (b) does residue recovered from exposed rooms carry seeding activity at or above that threshold while residue from unexposed and from cleaned rooms does not; and (c) what is the decay time of that recovered seeding activity after handling ceases and after defined cleaning protocols?

Reason for reformulation: the submitted version refers to "the threshold that determines which form crystallizes" as if it were a known quantity, but no such threshold is established in the literature — Bucar, Lancaster & Bernstein (2015) state verbatim that "there is no consensus about the size and range of activity of such seeds." The submitted question therefore quantified against an undefined reference. The reformulation makes the threshold an endpoint measured inside the study, and makes seeding activity rather than detected mass the primary readout. It also drops the requirement that the metastable form be truly "disappeared" (for ritonavir the original conditions are not recoverable), substituting a compound whose metastable form is still preparable somewhere.

## Alternative formulations

- Is the "disappearing polymorph" phenomenon caused by an ambient seed burden that can be measured directly — swabbed, counted, form-identified, and shown to decay — rather than inferred after the fact?
- For a given compound and room, what is the dose-response between recovered environmental residue (mass and polymorphic form) and the probability that an unseeded crystallization yields the stable rather than the metastable form?

## Motivating gap

Disappearing polymorphs are a named, repeatedly reported anomaly: once the thermodynamically stable form has been obtained somewhere, a previously reproducible metastable form often becomes hard or impossible to prepare, across unrelated laboratories. The ambient-seeding explanation is asserted in the canonical reviews but, on the evidence I opened, never measured.

Bucar, Lancaster & Bernstein (2015) state that "the working atmosphere inevitably becomes contaminated with seeds of the particular material," quote Mullin that "[a]tmospheric dust frequently contains particles of the crystalline product itself," and say the presence of seeds "cannot be casually dismissed; indeed its presence would be hard to avoid." The same review concedes, quoting the 1995 review, that such seeds "have never actually been directly observed," and states that "there is no consensus about the size and range of activity of such seeds." The quantitative support offered is calculation (up to 1e10 seeds in a single invisible particle) and generic dust counts (~1e6 airborne particles >=0.5 um per cubic foot of urban air; ~1e6 particles per minute from a seated person), not sampling of the compound itself. Persistence evidence is anecdotal: for benzocaine:picric acid the low-melting form could again be isolated after discarding all samples, "washing the equipment and laboratory benches and waiting for 8-12 days"; in another case the authors relocated to a laboratory a few kilometres away with new reagents and glassware and recovered the original polymorph on the first attempt; for ritonavir, Abbott "finally accepted that we could not get rid of" Form II. The one threshold-like figure in the review is for deliberate seeding, not environmental residue: ritonavir Form II forms when "seeded with Form II — even at amounts as low as 1 ppm."

Deliberate seeding is quantitatively engineered in industry, and cleaning validation already swabs surfaces for drug residue by HPLC — both halves of the measurement exist as routine practice. No source I opened puts them together to measure the involuntary seed burden of a working room: mass, polymorphic form, seeding activity, and decay time.

## Presuppositions

1. A compound exists with a documented metastable form that became hard to prepare after the stable form appeared, and laboratories with contrasting handling histories can still be identified and sampled. — **Partly established.** Bucar 2015 documents several such compounds. The sampling-access half is an unverified practical assumption.
2. Seed-free (or seed-controlled) crystallization reproducibly gives the metastable form in a naive environment. — **CONTESTED, and the reformulation's main motivation.** The historical records are often informal and original conditions may not be recoverable; Bucar 2015 notes Form I of ritonavir was later obtained even in laboratories previously exposed to Form II under controlled conditions, which cuts against a simple exposure-determines-outcome model.
3. Seeding by trace environmental material is a real causal route for the disappearance. — **CONTESTED.** Competing explanations include unrecognised impurities acting as nucleation inhibitors or templates (Bucar 2015: for sulfathiazole an impurity at "as low as 10 mol %" stabilised metastable Form I), humidity and scale changes, and publication-side attrition of negative results. Note the review itself asserts the seeding influence "is thus well established" while simultaneously conceding the seeds were never directly observed — an internal tension the candidate exploits.
4. Recovered residue can be form-identified at the quantities present (ng-to-ug on a swab) by Raman/XRD on collected particles, or functionally by a seeding bioassay. — **CONTESTED for XRD and probably for Raman.** The bioassay route makes this a functional rather than structural assay, which is the defensible version.
5. "Crystallographically active" is operationalisable as a flip in the outcome of a standardised supersaturated crystallization, not merely as detected mass. — **Established as a definition**, and strengthened by the reformulation; but see the cross-nucleation objection below.
6. Handling history of candidate rooms is documentable well enough to rank exposure. — **Unverified.** Plausible in GMP facilities with batch records; weak in academic laboratories.
7. **NEW presupposition introduced by the measurement design, and the most fragile:** the flip threshold lies at or above the recovery-plus-quantitation limit of the sampling chain. — **CONTESTED and possibly false.** The review's own arithmetic (1e10 seeds inside one optically invisible particle; deliberate ritonavir seeding effective at 1 ppm) suggests the active quantity may sit orders of magnitude below HPLC LOQ on a swab. If so, a "no" result is uninterpretable: indistinguishable rooms would be consistent both with no seed burden and with a seed burden that is active but undetectable. This must be flagged explicitly rather than assumed away.

## What would count as an answer

A sampling campaign over rooms stratified by documented exposure to the stable form: surface swabs and air samples, extracted, quantified for mass (HPLC, the existing cleaning-validation chain), and assayed for seeding activity by introduction into a standardised supersaturated solution held under conditions that yield the metastable form in naive, seed-controlled apparatus. The answer is a dose-response curve from recovered quantity to probability of the stable form, with (i) a flip threshold calibrated in the same study by a spiked dilution series of authentic stable form down to and below the sampling chain's LOQ, (ii) a persistence half-life from re-sampling rooms after handling ceased and after defined cleaning protocols, and (iii) structural form identification of recovered particles where mass permits (single-particle Raman being the plausible route).

- **"Yes"** = exposed rooms carry recoverable seeding activity at or above the flip threshold, naive and cleaned rooms do not, and the dose-response is monotone.
- **"No"** = exposed and naive rooms are indistinguishable in both recovered activity and crystallization outcome *while the spiked positive-control series demonstrates the assay would have detected activity at the threshold* — which would redirect the explanation of disappearance to impurity, humidity or scale and make the seeding account unsupported rather than merely unproven.
- **"Uninterpretable"** is a real third outcome and must be pre-registered as such: rooms indistinguishable *and* the spiked series showing the flip threshold lies below the sampling chain's detection limit.

## Novelty — nearest prior formulations (3, all opened)

**Prior A — the nearest by a wide margin.** Bucar, D.-K., Lancaster, R. W. & Bernstein, J., "Disappearing Polymorphs Revisited," *Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.* 2015, 54(24), 6972-6993. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4479028 (opened in full). Asserts the ambient-seed mechanism as the leading explanation, quantifies it by calculation, reports anecdotal persistence and decontamination (benzocaine:picric acid: wash benches and wait 8-12 days, repeated several times; laboratory relocation restoring the original form; ritonavir: could not be eliminated), and gives a deliberate-seeding threshold for ritonavir (1 ppm). Explicitly concedes the seeds "have never actually been directly observed" and that "there is no consensus about the size and range of activity of such seeds."

**Prior B.** Nicoud, L., Licordari, F. & Myerson, A. S., CrystEngComm 2019, DOI 10.1039/c8ce01428k (first published 16 Oct 2018). https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2019/ce/c8ce01428k (opened). No environmental sampling, no threshold quantity, no contamination decay time (its only durability data concern seed storage stability).

**Prior C.** Nahas, L., Haw, M., Sefcik, J. & Lee, M. (Strathclyde / GSK), "(301b) Does Seeding Always Preserve Solid Form? Cross-Nucleation Study of alpha- and gamma-Glycine," 2025 AIChE Annual Meeting, Separations Division, Nucleation and Growth session. https://proceedings.aiche.org/conferences/aiche-annual-meeting/2025/proceeding/paper/does-seeding-always-preserve (opened). Seed-on-a-stick isolation of fluid-shear effects on secondary nucleation; finds "fluid shear alone can drive secondary nucleation of glycine" for both seed forms, and that seeding with gamma-glycine led to cross-nucleation producing alpha-glycine crystals. Deliberate seeding in controlled apparatus; no air or surface sampling.

## Novelty — how this differs from each

**vs Prior A.** This is the closest call in the record and the reason novelty is capped. The review poses the mechanism, asserts the atmosphere is contaminated, offers a seed-quantity calculation, reports anecdotal persistence and anecdotal decontamination, and names a 1-ppm deliberate-seeding threshold. What it does not contain, and cites nothing containing, is a measurement: no sampling of a room, no form identification of recovered particles, no quantity-to-outcome dose-response, no measured decay time. The candidate asks for exactly the observation the review says has never been made. **But this cuts two ways**: the review's own sentences ("never actually been directly observed"; "no consensus about the size and range of activity of such seeds") are the field naming this gap in print. That makes the candidate closer to **asked-but-open** than to un-asked at the level of "has anyone observed these seeds". What I found no source stating is the specific *estimand* — a stratified-room dose-response with a calibrated flip threshold and a persistence half-life. So: the deficiency is on the record; the research question built on it is not, among the sources I opened.

**vs Prior B.** Same underlying physics (a trace quantity of the unwanted form dictates the outcome) and the same analytical instruments (Raman, IR), but the domain of the seed is entirely different: Prior B's seeds are deliberately weighed into a crystallizer and its contaminants are inside the vessel. The candidate's seeds arrive from the room and the estimand is a property of the room, not of the process. Prior B also supplies an argument *for* the candidate's design (trace unwanted form is causally potent at undetermined levels) and one *against* its inferential simplicity (seeding does not determine outcome by itself; temperature and stirring modulate it, so a room-level dose-response will be confounded by crystallization conditions unless they are held fixed).

**vs Prior C.** Prior C is the most serious technical threat to the candidate's readout rather than a competitor for its question. If seeding with one form can cross-nucleate the other, then "recovered stable-form seed introduced into supersaturated solution" does not cleanly indicate "this room's residue causes the stable form to appear," and a functional seeding bioassay may misreport. It shares no estimand with the candidate: no environmental sampling, no mass recovery, no persistence.

## Significance — consequence

**If yes:** polymorph control becomes partly a facility-engineering problem with measurable specifications — a surface-loading limit, a decay time, a demonstrated cleaning efficacy — in place of the current folklore remedies the review actually documents (discard everything, wash the benches, wait 8-12 days, or move to a different building). Disappearing polymorphs become preventable and in principle reversible on a measured timescale. It would also convert an acknowledged manufacturing hazard into a monitorable one: facilities could decide on evidence whether a room is fit to make a metastable form, instead of discovering it is not.
**If no** (with positive controls demonstrating adequate sensitivity): the mechanism asserted as "well established" in the canonical review of the phenomenon is unsupported at the quantities actually present, which redirects causal attention to impurity templating (for which Bucar 2015 already reports a concrete sulfathiazole case at 10 mol %), humidity, and scale. That is the higher-information outcome, and it would require rewriting the standard explanation of a named anomaly.
**Asymmetry, stated against the candidate:** a "yes" largely confirms received wisdom, so the expected information gain is carried disproportionately by the "no" and "uninterpretable" branches. This discounts the score.

## Significance — audience

- **Pharmaceutical solid-form development and CMC groups** — would gain a basis for deciding whether a metastable target form is manufacturable in a given suite, and whether a failed campaign was a facility problem or a chemistry problem.
- **Cleaning-validation and QA analysts** — the strongest "would actually act" argument in the record, because the infrastructure already exists and is mandated. Query 6 established that this community already swabs surfaces and quantifies drug residue by HPLC, but exclusively against toxicological carryover limits; form identity and nucleation activity are outside the assay's purpose. The marginal cost of adding a form or bioassay endpoint to an existing, already-funded, already-scheduled swab programme is low, which is what makes the action plausible rather than hypothetical.
- **Facility and HVAC/containment engineers** — would gain or lose a reason to treat filtration and airflow as polymorph controls.
- **Regulators and inspectors** drafting cross-contamination expectations — currently have no quantitative basis for form-specific carryover.
- **Academic crystallization laboratories** — bears directly on the reproducibility of published metastable-form preparations.

## Significance — fecundity

Answerable follow-ons that are not currently askable: the decay kinetics of surface seeding activity against humidity and surface material; whether seeding activity survives dissolution-and-drying cycles (i.e. whether cleaning redistributes rather than removes it); whether HVAC filtration class predicts room seed burden; whether the documented irreproducibility of specific metastable preparations correlates with institutional handling history, which would turn the anecdotes in Bucar 2015 into a testable epidemiology; whether the flip threshold is compound-general or compound-specific; and whether cross-nucleation (Prior C) means some rooms are primed for a form they have never hosted.

## Significance — anchoring

Attaches to: the disappearing-polymorph anomaly itself; the explicit "no consensus about the size and range of activity of such seeds" gap stated in Bucar 2015; the reproducibility problem in metastable-form preparation; cross-nucleation as a live 2025 research front (Prior C); and the unexamined boundary between cleaning validation as a toxicology exercise and cleaning validation as a crystallization control.

## Reason for silence

**A disciplinary boundary plus an incentive gap — not "no one thought of it."**

The measurement requires three communities that share neither journals, instruments, personnel nor regulatory endpoints. Solid-state chemists own the polymorph question but work at bench scale and treat the room as background; their canonical review's response to the missing observation was to calculate how many seeds *would* be in a dust particle rather than to sample one. Cleaning-validation and QA analysts own the swab infrastructure, the recovery-study methodology and the facility access, but their mandated endpoint is total residue mass for patient-safety carryover — polymorphic form is not merely unmeasured but outside the purpose of the assay, so no one in that function has a reason to ask. Aerosol scientists own single-particle form identification but have no reason to look for an API in indoor air. The question lives in the gap between a discipline with the motive and two disciplines with the means.

The incentive gap compounds it, and is specific rather than generic: for a manufacturer, documenting that its rooms carry crystallographically active seed of a particular form creates a discoverable record and a finding that cannot be un-known at the next inspection. The informative "no" requires demonstrating your own assay's detection limit against the active threshold, which risks establishing on the record that your cleaning validation cannot see the thing that matters. That is a measurement nobody is funded to make and several parties are quietly funded not to make. Meanwhile the academic side has a cheaper route to publication — the anecdote is already publishable as a review observation, and Bucar 2015 shows an asserted mechanism passes peer review in *Angewandte Chemie* without the measurement.

## Scores

**Well-posedness: 2/3.** The core measurement is specifiable end to end and the reformulation removed the undefined-threshold defect. Three deductions, one of them structural: (a) a genuinely "naive" room is close to unobtainable for any compound that has ever been handled, so a negative can always be attributed to insufficient naivety unless naivety is defined by assay rather than by history; (b) Prior C's cross-nucleation result means the functional bioassay's readout is not a clean indicator of what the recovered residue is; (c) most seriously, presupposition 7 — if the flip threshold lies below the sampling chain's detection limit, as the review's own 1e10-seeds and 1-ppm figures suggest it may, then "no" is not reachable and the question has a live uninterpretable branch. Pre-registering that branch and calibrating with a spiked series is what keeps this at 2 rather than 1.

**Significance: 2/3.** Consequence is real, the anchoring is to a named anomaly with an explicitly acknowledged evidential hole, and the audience argument is unusually concrete because the cleaning-validation community already owns the instruments, the access and the schedule — the marginal ask is an added endpoint, not a new programme. Held below 3 by the information asymmetry (a "yes" confirms the textbook account), by the dependence of the headline "no" on a sensitivity demonstration that may not be achievable, and by the fact that the practical remedies a "yes" would license (clean harder, wait longer, use a different room) are already in informal use, so the decision change is calibration rather than redirection.
