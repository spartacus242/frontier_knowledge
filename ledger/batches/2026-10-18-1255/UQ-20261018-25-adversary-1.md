## Restatement (my own words)

Take people who were in a randomised trial of a long-term treatment. Starting from each person's own last trial visit, use pharmacy and clinical records to ask: (a) at 3, 6 and 12 months, what are they on — the trial drug, whatever they took before the trial, something else, or nothing — compared with the 12 months before they were randomised; and (b) does the quantity the trial was measuring jump or change slope at that last visit, judged against some comparison series (matched non-trial patients, or their own pre-trial record), and does that differ by which arm they were in? The implied claim of the intake is that the field has written about *how* to close trials and *whether* post-trial access is promised, but nobody has measured what actually happens to the participants.

## Recommended verdict

**Rejected** — Disguise (attack 2) Unresolved: the limb Gate 2 left as the sole un-asked residue has been asked in the pharmacoepidemiology "trial participation effect on adherence" vocabulary (van Onzenoort et al., Hypertension 2011; pharmacy refill records of trial participants before, during and after the trial, persistence after trial end), and post-trial treatment by arm was recorded in WOSCOPS for 5 years; the Gate 2 Low grade stands and is strengthened. The surviving residue (four-state distribution dated to each participant's own last visit, with a declared control series) is a design refinement and should be re-entered only as a fresh candidate with its own Gates 0-2, and with the 2007 WOSCOPS NEJM follow-up and the Harvard HCP HIV systematic review opened first.

Strongest killing point: van Onzenoort et al. 2011 measured trial participants' dispensing before, during and after a randomised trial from pharmacy refill data with a pre-trial look-back — the method-field question the record declared un-asked.
