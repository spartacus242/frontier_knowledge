Domain(s): Medicine — physiology / infectious disease / clinical thermometry. Secondary: measurement metrology; screening epidemiology.
Question (canonical): In a multi-site EHR corpus spanning roughly 1995–2025, restricted to episodes of documented infection (coded syndrome plus culture- or NAAT-confirmed pathogen) and taking as the episode peak the maximum temperature recorded strictly before the first antipyretic administration timestamp, with the contemporaneous reference being the afebrile temperature distribution of non-infected patients in the same health system, device class and time-of-day window: within strata of age band, syndrome, pathogen, severity-at-presentation and device class, is the calendar-year slope of (episode peak − contemporaneous afebrile reference) zero, and how does it compare with the calendar-year slope of the afebrile reference itself? (Additive primary scale, mandatory bounded/multiplicative sensitivity analysis; any pre-1950 archival arm reported only as a bias-bounded estimate.) One Gate 1 reformulation used; intaked version and rationale in the record.
Alternative formulations:
- Is fever amplitude (peak minus that era's own afebrile baseline) in documented infection constant across birth cohorts, or has it declined?
- Of the difference between historical and modern fever observations, how much is a shifted baseline and how much a smaller rise per unit of infectious stimulus?
- Has the acute-phase temperature response per unit of measured stimulus (°C per log CRP, per log pathogen load) changed by calendar year within matched syndrome and device class?
Motivating gap: Anomaly mining. Mean US body temperature has fallen ~0.03 °C per birth decade since the 1890s (Protsiv et al., eLife 2020;9:e49555). The downstream policy limb — should 38.0/38.3 °C be lowered? — is in print as unevidenced conjecture (Wright & Auwaerter, OFID 2020;7(5):ofaa132, Conclusions; quote verified verbatim by both adversaries). The un-asked quantity is the decomposition Δpeak = Δbaseline + Δamplitude, which separates "the ruler moved" (re-referencing restores sensitivity) from "the response weakened" (re-referencing does not). Ledger adjacency: UQ-20261016-13 (in-vitro MIC at site temperature) distinct; UQ-20261016-09 (cross-era biospecimen comparability) same failure mode, Rejected, checked against the archival arm.
Presuppositions: P1 (partly contested) usable historical infection series with stated denominators exist — raw material confirmed digitised, no denominated series found. P2 (CONTESTED) cross-era thermometry comparable after calibration/site/dwell correction; offsets are the same order as the effect — not load-bearing for the modern-era primary arm. P3 (CONTESTED) cohorts matchable across eras on syndrome/severity/age. P4 (contested) pre-antipyretic peak identifiable from EHR administration timestamps. P5 (CONTESTED, physiological) "amplitude" defined independently of baseline — assumes additive set-point displacement; set-point-vs-gain is live and actively revised (Romanovsky, PMID 15970487), so the Gate 1 scale pre-specification is a repair of statement, not of physiology. P6 (CONTESTED) the secular baseline fall is real, not instrument artefact (disputed by Jones, SBM 2020) — the reformulated question does not inherit it. P7 (deduction against well-posedness) peak attained is a function of amplitude, stimulus magnitude AND time allowed before truncation; earlier presentation and earlier antimicrobials lower observed peak with no physiological change, so a negative Δamplitude is unidentified unless time-from-onset and a stimulus measure are conditioned on. P8 (added by adversary #1) both slopes are estimable at useful precision inside one instrument era.
What would count as an answer: A calendar-year-stratified distribution of pre-antipyretic peak in matched documented infection with thermometric bias carried as a term with uncertainty, yielding Δpeak decomposed into Δbaseline and Δamplitude with intervals on the pre-specified scale plus bounded-scale sensitivity analysis, conditioned on time-from-onset and a stimulus measure (P7). "Baseline only" if the Δamplitude interval contains zero while Δbaseline is clearly negative; "response attenuation" if Δamplitude is clearly negative and survives that conditioning; a null bounded away from |Δamplitude| < 0.2 °C also answers it. Feasible without any archival arm.

Significance — consequence: If Δamplitude ≈ 0 while Δbaseline < 0 ("ruler moved"), the deficit is metrological and fever criteria should be re-referenced to a contemporaneous or per-person baseline in SD units. If Δamplitude is clearly negative ("response weakened"), re-referencing does not restore sensitivity and the implication is secular change in innate immune activation or set-point regulation. A bounded null (|Δamplitude| < 0.2 °C) closes the ambiguity cheaply.
Significance — audience: Bodies setting fever criteria (AAP febrile-infant 38.0 °C, Sepsis-3/SIRS, IDSA/NCCN neutropenic-fever 38.3/38.0 °C, FUO definitions, CDC/WHO entry screening); the clinical-thermometry standards community (ISO 80601-2-56, tympanic and temporal-artery vendors) for bias budgets against a drifting population mean; EHR CDS builders firing sepsis alerts on temperature thresholds (adjacent to UQ-20261015-21); thermoregulation physiologists and inflammaging immunologists.
