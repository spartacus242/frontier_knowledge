## 0. Restatement in my own words (R1 as it now stands, repair included)

Pick a set of alloy systems for which at least two different research groups have each published a complete CALPHAD (Gibbs-energy) description. Build one digitised pile of the experimental measurements for those systems, tagging each measurement with the paper it came from. Now score every competing description twice under one agreed error metric: once on the whole pile (its "apparent" error, which the candidate concedes must be RECOMPUTED by the analyst because assessment papers do not print a fit number), and once in a leave-one-study-out fashion so the error is "predictive". Then ask three things: do the two scorings put the descriptions in the same order (Kendall tau); how much bigger is the predictive error than the apparent one (absolute and as a ratio); and in how many systems does the winner change. A finding of "same order, small gap" is declared in advance to be an answer, not a failure. The avowed analogy is crystallography's free-R factor and clinical prediction models' apparent-vs-external performance.

MEASURAND EXEMPTION — DECLARED AND APPLIED, not merely noted: the record's P6 pre-commits that tau indistinguishable from 1 with a small gap is a complete answer. I therefore do NOT attack this candidate for allowing that apparent fit is already a sufficient selector, and no status below rests on that possibility. I also do not attack it for reporting that CALPHAD papers print no fit statistic — that is a reported fact inside its protocol, not a smuggled premise.
