Question (canonical): For a pre-declared set of payer-by-service cells drawn from the first years of CMS-0057-F public prior-authorization reporting, what fraction of prior-authorization-requiring clinical decisions terminate on the provider side before any request reaches the payer, and does cross-payer variation in the published denial rate predict that pre-submission attrition rate with a negative sign — in what share of cells does adding attrition reverse the ranking of payers by obstruction, against a flip threshold fixed before any data pull?
Alternative formulations: (a) Does a low published prior-authorization denial rate mean a payer approves more care, or that fewer requests were ever filed? (b) Treating the new public prior-authorization metrics as an instrument with an endogenous denominator, estimate pre-submission attrition and the bias it induces in the metric as a measure of access to care. (V1, as intaken, retained in the record; the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation narrowed the object to strictly pre-submission attrition after the Medi-Cal file was opened.)
Motivating gap: Every figure CMS-0057-F requires payers to publish (percentage approved, denied, approved after appeal, response times) is conditioned on a request having arrived — response time is explicitly "measured from when the payer receives the request, not when the provider sends it" — so care never requested cannot appear, and a payer could lower its reported denial rate by making filing harder. Analysts are already ranking named payers on these percentages (KFF issue brief, 13 Aug 2026; Becker's, 13 Aug 2026) and have named one denominator-side confound (gold carding) while naming nothing on the provider side.
Presuppositions: P1 arrival-conditioned denominator — ESTABLISHED, but not uniformly "requests received" (the Medi-Cal CY2025 file prints "adjudicated" against its own narrative "received"; KFF confirms CMS gives only "suggested denominators", standardization merely proposed in a 2026 rule). P2 pre-submission terminations identifiable from provider records — CONTESTED; measurable fraction is a lower bound. P3 requirement lists matchable to clinical orders — PARTLY ESTABLISHED, needs a sensitivity arm. P4 payers gain from submission friction — CONTESTED, carried as the hypothesis under test, not an assumption. P5 clinically equivalent substitution distinguishable from abandonment — CONTESTED. P6 comparable reporting across payers/services — WEAKENED: percentages only (no counts), no service-type breakdown nationally, prescription drugs excluded, inconsistent methods and locations. P7 "first public in 2026" — FALSE as stated; state-level reporting predates it (Mississippi, Hawaii, Washington, Massachusetts, California workers'-comp UR). Correct claim: first nationally, on a federal specification that is itself not yet uniform.
What would count as an answer: Paired estimates per pre-declared cell of (i) the published denial rate as printed and (ii) the provider-side pre-submission attrition rate over PA-requiring decisions identified by a matching rule fixed before any data pull, with the sign and magnitude of their cross-cell association under one primary estimator plus declared sensitivity arms (matching error, substitution-vs-abandonment, received-vs-adjudicated denominator), and the share of cells whose obstruction ranking flips against a pre-fixed threshold. The post-submission auto-denial-on-non-response share must be reported separately, as it loads the same metric with the opposite sign. A null (attrition small and uncorrelated) is a publishable answer; a negative association of stated size says the metric is partly a gauge of submission friction, which only CMS can respecify.

Novelty confidence: Medium
Significance — consequence: If attrition is substantial and negatively associated with the published rate, the metric CMS specified is partly a gauge of submission friction, a published payer ranking is partly inverted, and the fix is a respecification only CMS can make — an exposure denominator, or a reported attrition/withdrawal category, plus separating the auto-denial-on-non-response cases that load the numerator the other way. If attrition is small and uncorrelated, the metric is validated against its sharpest available criticism, which matters because payers are already being ranked on it.
Significance — audience: Demonstrated, not assumed: KFF published a seven-author issue brief on exactly these files on 13 Aug 2026 with named-insurer ranges, picked up within days by Healthcare Dive, HME News, HomeCare, LeadingAge and InsuranceNewsNet; a Senate HSGAC letter to Humana dated 14 Jul 2026 surfaced in search; and KFF reports a 2026 proposed rule that would standardize the denominators, so the respecification lever is open now. Provider organisations gain a quantity on their own side of the wire; payers face a metric that no longer rewards friction.
Significance — fecundity: Makes askable whether submission friction responds to publication (CY2025 is a clean pre-publication baseline for a Goodhart test); whether the auto-denial-on-non-response category should be reclassified and how much of each insurer's reported rate it is; whether the appeal-side analogue (7.3% appealed) has the same structure; whether an exposure denominator can be built from gold-carding rosters plus requirement lists; and whether queueing's unobserved-balking estimators can be applied to a regulatory metric at all.
Significance — anchoring: The administrative-burden/ordeal literature's claim that procedural denials track burden rather than eligibility; inference under unobserved balking and silent abandonment in queueing; the allowance-rate and discouraged-borrower analogues in disability insurance and lending; measured abandonment in pharmacy and oncology, which give levels but never the metric's invariance; and in this ledger the "a CMS measure may not measure what it is read as measuring" family (UQ-20261017-18, with UQ-20261016-32, UQ-20261016-42, UQ-20261017-39 — now a fifth member; the family-level redundancy flag goes to the curator).
Reason for silence: A measurement-boundary split on an eight-month-old instrument. The metric is specified, computed and published entirely by payers; the complementary quantity exists only in provider EHR and ePA logs. The payer-file analytic community cannot see the attrition term and has reached only for confounds visible inside those files — which is exactly what KFF's gap list shows. The new-instrument half of the mechanism survives adversarial testing; the incentive-gap half is weakened, since provider-organisation coverage-request data have been studied academically (Kapur, Gresenz & Studdert 2003, ~half a million requests at two capitated medical groups, abstract only) and ePA intermediaries and integrated payer-providers hold both terms, so "neither side owns both terms" is false for the parties best placed to ask.

Strongest remaining objection: The sign test is not identified against the confound the field has already named. A negative volume↔denial relationship is already published (KFF, 2024 Medicare Advantage data) and already attributed to gold carding, whose own mechanism — "reported statistics from these insurers include only providers that may inherently have lower approval rates" — is a provider-composition selection argument of the same shape as the candidate's; and the candidate's predicted sign additionally requires attrition to be merit-selective (removing cases that would have been denied), which no opened source supports and the record never states. As designed, a negative result would not discriminate the candidate's mechanism from KFF's, and a null would not discriminate a valid metric from two oppositely signed channels cancelling. Compounding it on the data side: in year 1 the left-hand variable is not one quantity — CMS gives only "suggested denominators" (standardization merely proposed), insurers publish percentages and not counts, and the record's own payer prints "adjudicated" against a narrative "received" — so denominator heterogeneity is confounded with the submission-side attrition the design wants to detect and a cell-level flip threshold has no sampling interpretation.
