## Restatement (my own words, before engaging the record's framing)
Freeze a list of every public post-training dataset you can actually download on a stated day. Flatten them all into one common shape — instruction, optional context, response or preference pair — throwing away each dataset's own template and field names. Then ask how much of this pile is the same content wearing different dataset labels: how many genuinely distinct examples the whole open pool holds (as a curve, since "same" depends on how loose a match you allow); which datasets overlap which, and by how much; and for a typical distinct example, how many named datasets it lives in. Then take a few famous published recipes that say "this much dataset A, this much dataset B" and ask what share of the *distinct* content each named ingredient really contributes once you stop counting the same example twice — including whether the ingredients change rank order. Finally, say what fraction of the pool you could not retrieve at all on the day you looked.

So: an overlap-and-unique-coverage census of the open post-training data pool, plus a declared-vs-realized ingredient audit of three recipes.
