## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (V0, as supplied at intake): Treating a pretraining run's logged loss as a frequency-metrology time series, which noise types (white, flicker, random-walk, identified by the slope of the overlapping Allan deviation of detrended residuals) dominate at which averaging times, and what is the Allan-variance-informed optimal averaging window for deciding that two runs differ — compared with the smoothing windows and decision points that published ablation practice actually uses?

Question (canonical, V1 — the one Gate 1 reformulation, used only for well-posedness; V0 retained above):
For a pre-registered frame of public pretraining runs with step-resolution loss logs (>= 10 runs across >= 3 model scales, e.g. OLMo, Pythia, Marin/WandB public reports, nanoGPT-speedrun logs), with the detrending pre-declared as one primary estimator (local linear / Savitzky-Golay over a declared span) plus two sensitivity arms (a scaling-law fit; a penalized spline), and with tau restricted in advance to the range over which the overlapping-Allan estimator's confidence interval at that run's length excludes the neighbouring canonical noise-type slopes:
(i) what is the fitted log-log slope mu of the overlapping Allan deviation sigma_y(tau) of the detrended residuals, computed separately for pre-declared training phases (warmup / stable / cooldown), and which canonical noise type does it identify in each (white mu = -1/2, flicker mu = 0, random-walk mu = +1/2), and at what tau does sigma_y(tau) attain its minimum;
(ii) where the identified mixture is white-plus-random-walk, what is the Allan-variance-informed optimal averaging window tau*, and how does tau* compare with the windows published ablation reports actually use (EMA 0.9 / 0.99, mean of the last k steps) and with the token/step counts at which those reports call the A-vs-B decision;
(iii) measured against ground truth from seed-replicated run pairs (same recipe, different seed = null; a recipe change of pre-declared effect size = alternative), what are the realized false-decision rates of a tau*-window decision rule (difference of window means exceeding a declared threshold) versus the conventional windows?

Alternative formulations:
(1) At what averaging time does smoothing a loss curve stop removing noise and start hiding real drift?
(2) Does a training loss curve have a measurable noise floor and a drift regime, and are recipe decisions being made inside the regime where averaging cannot help?

Motivating gap: "Is recipe A better than recipe B" is decided constantly from short runs by eye on smoothed loss curves, with windows (EMA 0.9/0.99, mean of last k steps) chosen by convention and never justified. Metrology has a standard answer to "how long should I average": the Allan deviation's log-log slope identifies the dominant noise type, locates the averaging time at which white-noise averaging gives way to drift, and — for a white-plus-random-walk signal — supplies a proved optimal window (2022 American Control Conference pp. 1646-1651, NSF-PAR 10379088, opened). The intake search (2026-10-18) returned the metrology method in its home field plus "Learning Curve Theory" (arXiv:2102.04074) and "Measuring all the noises of LLM Evals" (arXiv:2512.21326, evaluation noise, not within-run loss telemetry) and no application of the diagnostic to training-loss series; this Gate 2 reproduces that negative across eleven vocabularies. Ledger differentiation: UQ-20261017-27 is between-configuration / between-seed variance; UQ-20261017-28 is within-step batch-formation design effect. Both are between-run or within-step. This is the third, missing axis — the within-run temporal noise structure and the averaging-time question that follows from it. No ledger entry (155 checked, plus a grep for allan / power spectral / 1-over-f / flicker / random walk / averaging time / EMA) touches it.

Presuppositions:
P1. Logged loss is sampled regularly enough and over long enough runs for an overlapping-Allan estimate. CONTESTED — logging cadence varies and tau coverage is capped by run length; Chang et al. 2308.15419 (opened) in fact log on a deliberately non-uniform grid ("We begin pre-training with 100 steps per checkpoint, and we end pre-training with 25K steps per checkpoint"), which is a direct instance of the hazard. V1 answers it by restricting tau to an estimator-CI-bounded range and by frame pre-registration on step-resolution logs only.
P2. Allan variance may be applied despite a strong deterministic trend, provided the analysis runs on detrended residuals. CONTESTED and load-bearing: a scaling-law fit, a spline and a local linear fit do not give the same residuals, and the detrending span itself sets a high-tau cutoff. V1 pre-declares one primary plus two sensitivity arms and reports all three.
P3. Noise type is stable enough within a run for a slope to mean anything. CONTESTED — warmup, stable phase and cooldown plausibly differ; V1 stratifies by pre-declared phase rather than assuming stationarity across the run.
P4. Step-resolution loss logs are retrievable for a usable number of public runs. SATISFIED in principle (OLMo, Pythia, Marin/WandB public reports, speedrun logs); not independently verified in this gate.
P5. "Two runs differ" is the decision the field actually makes from these curves. DEFENSIBLE; the alternative estimand is "extrapolate to a larger scale", which is a different question and is not what limb (iii) scores.
P6. Seed-replicated run pairs can supply ground truth for decision error rates. CONTESTED in availability — requires pairs that exist publicly or must be commissioned at small scale. Chang et al. (opened) is an existence proof that five-seed replicated pretraining sets are built and published, though at one scale.
P7 (added at Gate 1, not in intake, and the sharpest one). The optimal-window theorem the transplant imports assumes a white-measurement-noise-plus-random-walk signal. CONTESTED: if limb (i) identifies flicker as dominant, tau* is not defined by that theorem and limb (ii) needs a different estimator or must report "no optimal window exists in this noise regime". V1 makes limb (ii) conditional on the noise type limb (i) returns, so this is a branch rather than a hidden assumption.

## Gate 3 — Significance

Consequence (YES/NO both informative):
YES — if detrended residuals show a white-to-drift transition at an accessible tau, then every ablation decision read off an EMA-0.99 or mean-of-last-k curve has a window that is either too short (throwing away free averaging) or past the Allan minimum, where more averaging tracks drift instead of suppressing noise and makes the decision worse. The deliverable is not a sentiment but three numbers a practitioner can use the same afternoon: tau*, the realized false-decision rate at tau*, and the realized rate at the window they currently use. The diagnostic is also nearly free: it runs on logs that are already stored, with no new training compute, which matters because the audience's current remedy for "is this real?" is more seeds, which costs a run each.
NO (the dissolution branch, recorded as a real answer) — if residuals are white at every tau the estimator can reach, the mean-of-last-k is already optimal, the transplant dissolves, and what survives is still new: a measured noise floor for pretraining loss telemetry and a positive licence for the conventional window, which no published source currently supplies. Either branch closes a question that is presently decided by convention.
Intermediate and most likely branch: flicker dominates over the accessible decade, in which case the imported optimality theorem does not apply (P7), there is no finite optimal window, and the correct conclusion is that averaging *cannot* buy the resolution the field assumes it buys — the strongest possible statement in favour of replication over smoothing, and one that would redirect effort from longer runs to more seeds on principled grounds.

Audience, and how they would act differently: (1) Pretraining teams who run recipe ablations at small scale and promote the winner — AI2/OLMo, Marin, EleutherAI, the nanoGPT-speedrun community, and industrial pretraining groups — would change the window they average over and, more consequentially, would report a decision-error rate alongside an A-vs-B claim, which none currently does. (2) The training-telemetry tooling layer (W&B, TensorBoard, internal dashboards) whose current primitive is an EMA slider with no statistical semantics: sigma_y(tau) is a panel, and tau* is a default. (3) The ML experimental-methodology community that has built the between-run variance-accounting literature; this supplies the within-run axis that literature does not have, and tells it when more seeds beat longer runs. The reaction risk is real and is named in the strongest-objection slot: a practitioner may answer "I already know to use more seeds", and the question's value then rests entirely on limb (iii)'s error rates being surprising.
Fecundity: once sigma_y(tau) exists for loss, the identical diagnostic applies to gradient norm, update norm, MFU and downstream eval series, making "is this run drifting or just noisy?" a measurement rather than a judgement; it yields a run-length design rule (train long enough that tau* is inside the run, which is a different stopping criterion from the ones in use); it connects testably to the critical-batch-size and gradient-noise-scale line, since the white-noise floor's batch-size dependence is predictable and therefore falsifiable; and it turns A-vs-B into a hypothesis test with stated power, which makes pre-registration of ablations possible at all.
Anchoring: the standing anomaly that small-scale ablation winners do not survive scale-up; the scaling-law-fit-sensitivity literature, where the detrending choice (P2) is the same load-bearing choice under another name; N1's undefended 25-spline GAM as a concrete instance of unjustified smoothing in a flagship paper; and ledger entries UQ-20261017-27 and UQ-20261017-28, to which this is the missing third variance axis (between-configuration, within-step, and now within-run temporal).

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism — disciplinary boundary compounded by a non-obvious prerequisite and an incentive gap, not "nobody thought of it"):
The Allan deviation is a house tool of time-and-frequency metrology and inertial navigation, where the measured object is a stationary residual around a nominal and the canonical question is literally "how long should I average". Query 11 confirms the method's literature has stayed inside oscillators and MEMS gyros. ML training telemetry arrives through dashboarding tools whose smoothing primitive is an EMA slider with no statistical semantics, so the window is presented as a display preference rather than an estimator choice, and nobody is scored on it: no reviewer has ever asked for a justified smoothing window, so there is no incentive to derive one. Compounding this, the loss curve is visibly dominated by a deterministic trend, which makes it look like an object to which stationary-noise diagnostics obviously do not apply; the detrending step that makes the transplant legal (P2) is a non-obvious prerequisite that blocks the idea at first glance. And the field's trained reflex for "is this difference real?" is to add seeds — the between-run frame the reproducibility literature built, and the frame of the two adjacent ledger entries — which answers a different question well enough that the within-run temporal structure was never interrogated.
