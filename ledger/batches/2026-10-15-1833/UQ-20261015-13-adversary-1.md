# UQ-20261015-13 — Adversary 1 log (Gate 4)

## Restatement (own words)

Take the pooled table of MedDRA preferred terms recorded as adverse events in a drug's pre-approval controlled trials, with each term's count. Treat the terms as species and the patients as sampling effort, and apply unseen-species estimators (bias-corrected Chao1 minus observed richness; the Good-Turing singleton share f1/n) to say how many AE types the programme missed. Then ask whether that "missed types" number predicts how many new reaction terms regulators add to the label over the next five years, beyond what patient-years, observed term count, drug class, biologic status and accelerated approval already predict. Secondary: are the terms later added to the label disproportionately ones seen exactly once in the trials? A phase-2 → phase-3 hold-out is the positive control that separates "estimator useless on AE data" from "trial sample and market sample are different populations".

## Recommended verdict

No attack Unresolved; novelty holds at Medium after 10 further disguise queries; Partial on 3, 4, 6, 7, 8. Recommended: **Provisional** — promotable to Validated if the record (a) replaces ClinicalTrials.gov with CSR/Policy-0070 sources as the primary data basis and sizes the feasible cohort, (b) adds the all-cause-vs-attributed estimand presupposition with the coverage-deficit response, and (c) pre-specifies exclusion of class-labelling additions.

Strongest killing point: the estimator is computed on the all-cause treatment-emergent AE spectrum (mostly background morbidity, and with singletons unavailable from ClinicalTrials.gov because non-serious events under a sponsor-chosen threshold of up to 5% are omitted), while the readout counts causally attributed reactions that regulators add partly for class reasons — so the estimand and the readout are drawn from different universes and the record does not say why the one should track the other.
