## Restatement (my own words)

Take every paper that ran something on a named cloud quantum computer and says, at least roughly, when. Look up what the vendor's own calibration log said that machine's quality was on that date, and compare it with what the same log said for the surrounding two months. If papers were produced on random days, the run-day quality should land anywhere in that two-month spread with equal probability. The question is whether it instead lands disproportionately near the top — i.e. whether the published record is drawn from the machine's good days — and how big that tilt is. Side-question: how many papers can even be pinned to a date, and how many of those dates still have a surviving calibration record.
