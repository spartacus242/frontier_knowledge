Question (canonical): Over a pre-declared window and airspace set, with exposure defined from non-GNSS-derived evidence only (regulator bulletins, NOTAMs, spectrum-monitoring records) and each aircraft report joined to an archived NWP observation-feedback record, do aircraft-derived meteorological observations (AMDAR and Mode-S EHS/ADS-B-derived u/v wind and temperature) differ between exposed and matched unexposed airspace in (i) observation-minus-background bias and standard deviation, stratified by aircraft navigation configuration, (ii) the rate of gross positional misassignment detectable as kinematic inconsistency between successive reports from one airframe, and (iii) the fraction of such reports that survives operational quality control into the analysis and the analysis increments they produce — or are the exposed and unexposed departure statistics indistinguishable inside a pre-declared equivalence band with no excess QC rejection rate?
(V2; reformulated once at Gate 1 from a V1 that left exposure definition, comparator and null unspecified. V1 and the diff are in the record.)

Alternative formulations:
- Has GNSS interference been putting corrupted aircraft weather reports into operational weather analyses, and if so where and how many?
- Treating documented GNSS interference as an exposure, what is its effect on the O-B error statistics of aircraft-derived wind and temperature, stratified by aircraft navigation configuration, and on the analysis increments those observations produce?

Motivating gap: Dated, geolocated GNSS jamming/spoofing has been documented since 2022 (EASA SIB 2022-02R4; ICAO APAC 2025, SAM 2026). An aircraft-derived wind is the ground vector minus the air vector, and the surveillance-sourced stream's position tag is partly GNSS-derived, so interference can corrupt the value and separately mis-place a valid value. Volume at stake ~55M wind + ~32M temperature obs/day over Europe (de Haan et al. 2025), and ECMWF took the global Mode-S stream operational on 8 May 2025. Aviation quantifies the exposure at fleet scale; the meteorological outcome is quantified nowhere. AMENDED at Gate 4: the join is not wholly absent — Ingleby, ECMWF Newsletter 184 (2025) records it qualitatively ("In parts of eastern Europe and the Middle East, there are gaps due to GPS interference ... and also occasional bad data that was not detected"), with no statistics.

Presuppositions (E established / C contested): 1 (C, in degree) aircraft wind depends on a GNSS-informed ground-velocity solution — per-fleet navigation fit must be documented, not assumed; de Haan et al. 2025 does not state BDS 5,0 ground-vector provenance either way, so this stays contested. 2 (CORRECTED at Gate 4, was wrongly graded E) position and time stamps are NOT uniformly GNSS-derived: de Haan et al. 2025 §2.3 — "The timestamp is not generated by the aircraft; instead, it is created by the radar or receiver at the time of reception", and "For ATC radar, the position is determined by ATC radar"; receiver-sourced reports are CPR-decoded from ADS-B and AMDAR positions come from the FMS. 3 (C, load-bearing) exposure can be geolocated/dated independently of the outcome — usual proxies (PIC/NIC, GPSJAM hex severity) are themselves GNSS-derived, so V2 requires a non-GNSS index with Aireon's TDOA Independent Position Check as validation only. 4 (E, per centre) per-report observation-feedback archives with QC flags exist. 5 (C) sufficient exposed sample — traffic collapse/rerouting confounds and censors. 6 (C, and the object of limb (iii)) operational QC is imperfect. 7 (C) the background is usable in exposed regions — break circularity with reanalysis or a denial reference.

What would count as an answer: a matched-exposure analysis on archived observation feedback (ECMWF/Met Office/NOAA) joined to a non-GNSS exposure index, reporting (i) exposed-vs-unexposed O-B bias and sd for u/v and T stratified by navigation configuration, (ii) a gross-misassignment rate from within-airframe kinematic inconsistency, cross-checked against an independent TDOA track, (iii) the QC-survival fraction, resulting increments and a data-denial OSE. A clean null inside a pre-declared equivalence band with no excess rejection rate answers it equally and closes it.

Significance — consequence: if yes, variational QC and blacklisting are organised by platform ID and variable, never by airspace exposure (confirmed: de Haan et al.'s screen is per-airframe 14-day sd plus message-level sanity checks), so the fix is a new kind of exposure-conditioned QC predictor over tens of millions of obs/day in the region containing the exposure; the worst case is a mis-placed but physically valid temperature that passes a departure check at the wrong location. Regulators gain a quantified non-safety cost term they currently lack — evidenced: IFALPA's Dec-2025 "Effects of Manipulated GNSS" enumerates thirteen downstream effect classes and never mentions wind, temperature or meteorological data. If no, the null licenses the ongoing expansion of the Mode-S/ADS-B stream into exactly the airspace where interference is worst.

Significance — audience: NWP observation-monitoring and DA groups (ECMWF, Met Office, DWD, Meteo-France, KNMI, NCEP), who already run the machinery that would change; EUMETNET E-AMDAR/EMADDC and WMO JET-ABO as stream and guidance owners; EASA/ICAO/national CAAs and spectrum regulators; secondarily the ADS-B integrity community, whose products would become inputs to a meteorological QC chain.

Significance — fecundity: how should assimilation QC treat an observation whose position rather than value is in error; should observation error covariances carry a navigation-integrity term for any navigation-dependent platform (ships, buoys, GPS radiosondes, UAS, mobile-phone pressure); should interference exposure be routine archived report metadata; and inverted — can meteorological O-B departures serve as an independent interference detector, since they rest on a non-GNSS physical reference.
