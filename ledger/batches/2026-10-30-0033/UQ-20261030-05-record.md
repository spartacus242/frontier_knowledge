## Question (canonical)

**V1 (as submitted at intake, recorded per Gate 1's both-versions rule):** Across cell types whose nuclear pore numbers differ severalfold, is the import flux per pore of one declared cargo invariant — so that pore number tracks transport demand — or does per-pore flux vary between cell types, making pore number a limiting resource in some of them?

**V2 (the one reformulation permitted at Gate 1, used ONLY for well-posedness; this is the operative wording):** For one pre-declared cargo (a 2×GFP–SV40-cNLS reporter expressed from one promoter) measured in one laboratory under one assay, what is the absolute import flux per nuclear pore, in molecules·pore⁻¹·s⁻¹ with a confidence interval, in each of five pre-declared cell types; and is the between-cell-type coefficient of variation of that per-pore flux materially smaller than the between-cell-type coefficient of variation of counted pore number per nucleus (CV ratio ≤ 0.5, declared in advance), comparable or larger (CV ratio ≥ 0.8), or indeterminate (between the two)?

**What the reformulation changed, and what it newly presupposes.** V1 carried "transport demand" inside the question; no agreed measure of demand exists, and the intake conceded this was its weakest clause. V2 removes demand from the *test* and keeps it only as the *interpretation* of a result, so the headline number no longer depends on an undeclarable quantity. V2 also pre-fixes the CV-ratio threshold that V1 left as the undefined word "materially". **The repair's own presupposition, stated as the ledger requires:** a CV ratio is informative only if the denominator — the between-cell-type spread of pore number — is itself large. If pore number across the declared panel turns out nearly constant, the ratio is dominated by a small denominator and the test is uninformative whatever the flux data show. That spread is *not* established to my read level (see Presuppositions and Prior 1), so V2's test is conditional on the study's own counting arm delivering a severalfold spread, and the study must report the pore-number CV before the ratio is interpreted.

## Significance — consequence

**If invariant (CV ratio ≤ 0.5):** pore number becomes a read-out of a cell's transport demand, which turns a structural census into a physiological measurement — counting pores would estimate traffic. It also forces the existence of a flux-sensing mechanism that sets pore number during interphase and differentiation, and makes Prior 3's Tpr axis a candidate effector of such a set-point rather than an isolated regulator. **If it varies (≥ 0.8):** pore number is a candidate limiting resource, and some cell types are running their envelope near saturation while others hold spare capacity. That yields an immediately falsifiable prediction — raising pore number raises flux specifically in the low-flux cell types — and it would mean that the many disease settings where NPC density falls are not equivalent to each other, because the same proportional pore loss would cost flux in a saturated cell type and cost nothing in one with headroom.

Three communities would act differently, and the action is specific in each. **NPC-number biologists** (the Prior 3 group and successors) would gain the missing outcome variable: they currently perturb pore number and report pore number, because, in their own words, they can only speculate about the reason, and a per-pore flux read-out gives their perturbations a functional endpoint. **Single-molecule nuclear-transport biophysicists** would have a reason to leave the one or two model preparations the assay was built in and run matched panels, which is a change of experimental design, not of instrument. **Cell-biologists of nuclear ageing and neurodegeneration**, where NPC loss and nucleoporin decline are reported, would have a basis for deciding whether a measured pore deficit is functionally silent or flux-limiting in the affected cell type — currently that inference is made with no per-pore flux figure for any of them.

## Significance — fecundity

New questions become askable, each sharper than the parent: does per-pore flux change during a differentiation time course in one lineage, so that the set-point is dynamic rather than cell-type-fixed? Does the CV-ratio answer hold for a second cargo class with different kinetics — which is the declared-cargo caveat turned into an experiment? Is there an envelope-area versus pore-number trade-off, i.e. do cells that need more traffic add pores or add envelope? If flux varies, what saturates first — pore occupancy, receptor availability, or the Ran gradient? And the inverse of Prior 1's negative: if neither surface, volume nor DNA content determines pore number, does *flux* determine it?

## Significance — anchoring

It attaches to a named open problem conceded in the literature (Prior 3's "we can only speculate"), to Prior 1's unexplained 1977 negative that no single geometric or genomic variable sets pore number, to the organelle-scaling literature's question of what quantity organelle abundance is proportional to, and to the supply-and-demand framing of metabolic control analysis, whose flux-control vocabulary my query 11 found has never been applied to the nuclear envelope.

## Reason for silence

A **disciplinary boundary reinforced by an instrument split**, not an absence of interest — and Prior 3's own Discussion rules out "nobody thought of it", since the thought is printed there.

The candidate needs two measurements in one laboratory on the same cells. Absolute pore **counting** per nucleus belongs to electron microscopy and, recently, super-resolution imaging; absolute per-pore **flux** in live cells belongs to single-molecule biophysics. Those communities have different preparations: the flux community works predominantly in whichever preparation its assay tolerates — permeabilised cells, *Xenopus* oocytes, yeast, or one or two flat human lines — and the counting community works in whatever fixes and images well. The result is that the two numbers exist for *different* cells, so the ratio has never had a denominator and a numerator from the same nucleus. Prior 2 shows the shape of this exactly: it needed a per-pore figure, had no pore count, and supplied an assumed 86 NPCs.

Two further mechanisms compound it. The *normative* framing — is a structure's abundance matched to its throughput? — is owned by metabolic control analysis and supply–demand physiology, a vocabulary the nuclear-transport field does not use, which is why query 11 returned nothing: the question's natural method-owning field never looked at this object. And the field's dominant question has been *selectivity* — how the permeability barrier discriminates — which is a per-pore mechanism question whose answers are the same in every cell type by construction, so cell-type contrast has not been the variable anyone needed to vary.
