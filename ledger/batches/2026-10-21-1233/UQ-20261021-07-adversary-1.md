## Restatement in my own words

Take a defined set of heat exchangers that are actually running in one kind of service (say, crude preheat, or cooling-water condensers). For each one, work out from the plant's flow and temperature readings how much thermal resistance is there beyond the clean-tube value, with error bars, and say below what value you cannot distinguish it from zero. Divide that by the fouling-factor number the designer wrote on the datasheet (typically copied from the TEMA table). Question 1: across the set, is that ratio centred above, below or on 1, and how widely does it scatter? Question 2: for the exchangers where that fouling-factor number is what set the tube area, how much area did it buy that the real fouling did or did not need, with sign?

Two things in the record I am not taking on trust: that the "fouling-allowance-controlled" subset is a thing a datasheet can identify, and that the plant-derived quantity is fouling rather than everything the design model got wrong.
