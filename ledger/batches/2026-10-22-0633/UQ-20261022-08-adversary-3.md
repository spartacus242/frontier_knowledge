## Restatement (my own words, before reading the record's framing as authoritative)

When a hospital or health system swaps one EHR for another, part of the old record is converted into the new system's structured tables, part becomes PDFs or scanned images, and part stays only in a legacy archive. The candidate asks two things. (i) For each of seven kinds of clinical fact, of all the facts recorded in the OLD system's structured tables during a chosen look-back window, what share appears in the NEW system as structured, queryable and value-identical, under a mapping rule written down before any data are pulled, with "cannot be mapped" tallied as its own category? (ii) If you compute standard longitudinal research measures (incident diabetes, ACE-inhibitor exposure, Charlson prevalence) from the new system's standard research extract, is there a jump or kink at the switch date bigger than a threshold declared in advance, where "artefactual" is established by recomputing the same measures for the same patients from the old archive and by running the same test on a window containing no switch (plus a non-switching control site where one exists)?

What the canonical wording CONDITIONS ON (each tested below under attack 4): a legacy archive that is queryable at fact grain; a single cutover date T; a research extract that sees only the target system's tables; a research-network convention that does not already neutralise the artefact; patient identity carried across systems so the same patients can be re-derived; a control window of equal length that contains no cutover.
