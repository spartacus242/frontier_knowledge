Question (canonical) [R1, adopted at Gate 1]:
In occupied rooms (chamber and field), with occupant number, activity (quiet breathing / speech / light exercise) and recirculating HEPA filtration under experimental control and air-exchange rate measured independently by tracer decay: (a) is the ratio R = (size-resolved airborne human-DNA copies per m3, reported separately for an oral/respiratory-marked and a skin-marked fraction) / (CO2-derived rebreathed-air fraction) invariant to occupant number within a pre-registered equivalence band, as any per-occupant-normalised tracer must be; and (b) does R fall under HEPA filtration and shift across particle-size cuts as a particle-borne tracer must and a gas tracer cannot, with the oral/respiratory-marked fraction responding differently from the skin-marked fraction? Third, closed outcome: sampling recovery and between-person shedding variance exceed the filtration and activity contrasts, i.e. the tracer is not quantifiable at the precision the comparison needs.

Question (V0, intake wording, superseded at Gate 1): In occupied rooms, does airborne human-DNA concentration per occupant track the rebreathed-air fraction inferred from CO2, or does the human-DNA-to-CO2 ratio vary systematically with occupant activity, filtration and particle size in a way that yields a direct particle-phase measure of inter-occupant exposure that CO2 cannot provide? (Superseded for well-posedness only: "track", "vary systematically" and "yields a direct measure" carried no decision rule; the yes-branch and no-branch were not separated by stated bands; and the oral-vs-skin source split, which is the whole discriminator, sat in the presuppositions rather than in the question. R1 moves the source split into the estimand and names the third closed outcome. One reformulation used; none remaining.)

Alternative formulations:
- Is airborne human DNA a quantitative tracer of shared-breath exposure, and where does it diverge from CO2?
- Can a measured human-DNA-to-CO2 ratio quantify the gas-versus-particle discrepancy that CO2-based infection-risk models currently assume away?

Motivating gap:
CO2-based rebreathed-air models (Rudnick & Milton 2003) underpin ventilation guidance and airborne-infection risk estimates, and the uncertainty of point CO2 measurements for exposure prediction is itself live (PMC11496947, classroom CO2 exposure predictions; not opened within this run's fetch budget, so cited as the intake's motivation, not as verified content). CO2 is a gas tracer: filtration, deposition and size-dependent loss leave it untouched, and those are the terms that dominate particle exposure. Quantitative recovery of human airborne eDNA became routine only recently (Whitmore et al. 2023 report species-specific qPCR of human eDNA including occupied versus unoccupied room air), supplying a human-origin tracer carried on particles. Searching the eDNA vocabulary against the rebreathed-air/CO2 vocabulary returned CO2-proxy work on one side and forensic/ecological human-air-DNA work on the other, and nothing joining them.

Presuppositions:
1. Airborne human DNA is quantitative, not merely detectable (CONTESTED; this is the instrument question: recovery efficiency, qPCR calibration, limits of quantification). Partial support: Whitmore et al. 2023 applied species-specific qPCR to room air; Fantinato et al. 2023 report air DNA in nanograms, not copies per m3, with air medians (0.1 ng) an order below dust (2.3 ng) - i.e. near the low end of quantifiable.
2. Shed human DNA reaches the air on particles whose size distribution overlaps respiratory aerosol (CONTESTED, and the main confounder: much airborne human DNA may come from skin flakes and resuspended dust, so the design must mark oral/respiratory versus skin sources). Note a sharper problem than the intake states: the proposed markers are not clean. Oral-microbiome markers are not human DNA, and human mitochondrial versus nuclear DNA does not separate oral from skin origin. The partition is an unsolved assay-design problem, not a settled technique.
3. CO2 gives a well-defined rebreathed-air fraction for the room under test (established, with documented point-measurement uncertainty).
4. Occupancy, activity and ventilation rate can be measured independently (established: CO2-release and exhaled-CO2-decay tracer methods used routinely, e.g. Qian et al. 2012; occupancy logging).
5. Human DNA shedding rate per person is stable enough across individuals for per-occupant normalisation (CONTESTED; between-person variance may swamp the contrasts and must be estimated, not assumed. Kappelt et al. 2021 found the analogous between-person variance in viral load spans orders of magnitude and defeated their own CO2-to-RNA conversion).
6. Collecting human DNA from air is ethically and legally admissible in the proposed settings (FLAGGED; Whitmore et al. 2023 foreground consent, privacy, surveillance and data ownership for exactly this material; consent design is part of feasibility, not a footnote).

What would count as an answer:
Chamber plus field experiments with known occupancy, ventilation measured by tracer decay, controlled activity (quiet breathing, speech, light exercise), controlled recirculating HEPA filtration (on/off), and size-resolved air sampling (impactor stages or equivalent), with blanks and spike-recovery controls for the DNA assay and a per-person shedding-variance estimate from repeat single-occupant runs.
Reported outcomes: human-DNA copies per m3 (total, and separately for oral/respiratory-marked and skin-marked fractions), the CO2-derived rebreathed-air fraction, and an independent physical particle tracer.
- YES: R is insensitive to occupant number within pre-registered equivalence bands, and falls under filtration and shifts across size cuts outside those bands, with the oral-marked fraction responding differently from the skin-marked fraction.
- NO: R is constant across filtration, activity and size within the equivalence bands, in which case airborne human DNA carries no information beyond CO2 for this purpose and the question closes.
- NOT QUANTIFIABLE (also closed): pooled recovery and between-person shedding variance exceed the filtration and activity contrasts, which is the instrument's own verdict.
Bands must be fixed from pilot variance before the confirmatory runs; the record notes that this pilot does not yet exist, which is what keeps well-posedness at 2/3.

Novelty - how this differs from each:
vs P1 (Kappelt 2021): P1 is the closest prior in intent and asks the parent question - can CO2 proxy for aerosol exposure - and answers it partly for physical particles. Four differences. (i) P1's tracer is a particle count with no human-origin label, so in any real room it cannot be separated from background, skin-derived or resuspended particles; the candidate's tracer is human-origin by construction and further split by source marker. (ii) P1 varied neither filtration nor particle size - precisely the loss terms on which a gas tracer and a particle tracer must diverge, and the candidate's whole decision rule. (iii) P1 is single-person accumulation in a 2 m3 chamber, not occupancy scaling in occupied rooms, so it cannot test invariance of the ratio to occupant number. (iv) P1's negative result is about pathogen-load variance between people, a different quantity from whether a human-origin particle tracer tracks rebreathed fraction; the candidate makes the analogous shedding variance a measured parameter with a closed "not quantifiable" outcome rather than a defeater discovered afterwards.
vs P2 (Whitmore 2023): P2 establishes that human eDNA in room air is recoverable and qPCR-quantifiable and that taking it raises consent problems. Its room-air contrast is binary presence (occupied versus unoccupied). It reports no ventilation rate, no CO2, no activity or filtration contrast, no size resolution and no per-occupant normalisation, and it frames the material as bycatch and an ethics object, not as a dose or exposure variable. The candidate takes P2's instrument as its enabling condition and asks a question P2 does not pose: whether the quantity scales as an exposure tracer must.
vs P3 (Qian 2012): P3 is the closest methodologically and already has size-resolved qPCR, per-occupant emission rates and CO2 in the same classroom - yet the two never meet. Significance - consequence:
If YES: ventilation practice gains a measured, human-origin, particle-phase exposure index where it currently has only a gas tracer plus modelled loss terms. The concrete change is to filtration credit: equivalent-clean-airflow allowances are justified by model, and a DNA-to-CO2 ratio that moves when a HEPA unit is switched on would let a room's filtration credit be checked empirically with no tracer release. It also converts the gas-versus-particle discrepancy inside Rudnick-Milton-type models from an assumption into a measurable quantity, and the oral-versus-skin split would resolve the long-standing source-apportionment ambiguity in indoor human bioaerosol (P3, Adams 2015).
If NO: a clean negative that CO2 is sufficient for shared-air indexing and that airborne human DNA is dominated by skin and resuspension sources. That is directly useful twice over: it closes a line that eDNA method developers would otherwise pursue, and it strengthens the forensic contamination interpretation Fantinato et al. raise (air DNA as a record of presence and shedding, not of breathing).
If NOT QUANTIFIABLE: bounds what any air-DNA metric can support, including forensic claims about occupancy intensity, and tells ethics committees that the surveillance-grade quantitative use they worry about is not currently achievable - a result with its own audience.

Significance - audience:
ASHRAE (SSPC 62.1, Standard 241) and REHVA committees, who must currently defend filtration-based clean-air credit without a field-measurable biological endpoint, and who would act by adding or declining a verification method. Hospital infection control: programmes already running air qPCR for pathogens (hospital ddPCR and the Thuresson-type size-resolved series) have a numerator and no denominator, and a human-DNA normaliser would let them express pathogen air concentrations per unit of rebreathed air across wards. Aerosol-transmission modellers, who would replace assumed deposition and filtration loss terms with a measured ratio. eDNA and forensic method developers, for whom the quantitative-versus-detectable verdict determines what air-DNA evidence can claim. Ethics and privacy reviewers, for whom the feasibility verdict bounds the surveillance risk Whitmore et al. raised.
Score: 2/3. The audiences are real and identifiable and the decision they face is concrete, but the yes-branch payoff is conditional on the instrument clearing a precision bar that current evidence (air DNA near 0.1 ng, P1's order-of-magnitude between-person variance) does not establish, and the no-branch informs a narrower methods community than the ventilation-standards framing suggests.

Significance - fecundity:
New askable questions on a YES: can the ratio serve as a release-free field measure of equivalent clean air delivery, replacing tracer-gas decay tests; does the oral-marked fraction track speech time and volume closely enough to index a "talking dose" for a room; does between-person shedding variance set a hard floor on any personal-exposure eDNA metric, and is that floor lower for oral than skin markers; can archived HVAC filters be read retrospectively as occupancy-and-exposure dose records; does the DNA-to-CO2 ratio behave differently in displacement versus mixing ventilation, giving a cheap test of air-distribution effectiveness for particles. On a NO or NOT QUANTIFIABLE: what fraction of airborne human DNA is resuspended versus freshly shed, and what does that imply for forensic air-DNA interpretation.

Significance - anchoring:
Touches (i) the documented divergence between tracer-gas and aerosol-particle distributions indoors, which the engineering literature reports but does not resolve into a correction for CO2-based risk models; (ii) the point-measurement uncertainty of CO2 for exposure prediction; (iii) unresolved source apportionment of indoor human bioaerosol, skin versus oral, left open by P3 and Adams 2015; (iv) the human-genetic-bycatch consent debate opened by P2, to which a feasibility verdict is directly relevant; (v) forensic air-DNA interpretation and laboratory contamination, where Fantinato et al. found detectable DNA in ultra-clean forensic labs.

Reason for silence:
Primary mechanism - a disciplinary boundary located at the outcome variable, not at the method. The groups that quantify human DNA in room air are forensic geneticists and eDNA ecologists whose outcome is identification (a profile, a likelihood ratio, presence versus absence); Fantinato et al. report DNA in nanograms per sample rather than copies per cubic metre, which is the forensic unit and not an exposure unit. The groups that use CO2 as an exposure tracer are ventilation engineers whose biological measurements stop at bacterial and fungal rRNA genes; in P3 the two quantities sit in the same classroom and the same paper, with CO2 confined to the methods section as a ventilation instrument. Both literatures run the same chambers and the same qPCR, with non-overlapping outcome variables - the configuration that leaves a ratio between them unformed.
Reinforcing mechanisms: (a) new instrument - routine quantitative human airborne eDNA dates to roughly 2021-2023 (P2), so the tracer did not exist when CO2-based rebreathed-air modelling was established in 2003, and the CO2-proxy comparison that did get run in 2021 (P1) used the physical particle counters already on the bench; (b) an incentive and taboo gap - proposing routine human-DNA sampling of occupied classrooms or wards carries the consent and surveillance burden P2 foregrounds, with no established protocol, which deters an engineer from formulating the question before any pilot data could justify the trouble.
