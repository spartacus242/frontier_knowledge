## 1. Restatement in my own words

Food-animal regulators answer a question with the same shape as "when can this mother nurse again?" — after a cow is dosed, how long is her milk unfit for a consumer — and they answer it by a fixed recipe: run a residue-depletion study whose subject count, sampling grid, terminal-phase coverage and assay sensitivity are specified in advance; fit the depletion curve; take an upper one-sided tolerance bound on it (95/95); read off the time it drops under a legal residue limit.

The candidate asks two things about doing that to people. **(A)** Take a pre-named list of the drugs lactating women are actually given. For each, go to the published human-milk concentration-time literature and score it pass/fail, criterion by criterion, against that veterinary study-design standard. What fraction passes? **(B)** For the drugs that pass, actually run the tolerance-limit calculation against each of several candidate infant-exposure limits (since no legal one exists), and lay the resulting "wait this many hours" numbers next to the hours currently printed in LactMed and told to mothers. How far apart are they, and in which direction?

So: a data-adequacy census against a borrowed yardstick, plus a recomputation of standing advice using a borrowed statistical rule. The candidate deliberately refuses to say what the right infant limit is; it reports a sensitivity surface instead.
