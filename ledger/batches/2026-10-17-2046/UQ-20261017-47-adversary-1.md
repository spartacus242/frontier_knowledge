## Restatement (my own words)

Take a list of models fixed in advance. For each, split the compute that went into *making* it into (a) the FLOP spent on gradient updates and (b) the FLOP spent on inference-shaped work that fed the recipe — RL rollouts and verifier/reward scoring, generating synthetic training text, running a teacher for distillation, and runs that were thrown away. Ask three things: what is the spread of b/a across the list; if you rank models by a+b instead of by the "training compute" number people currently quote, does the order change (Spearman ρ); and how many models move across the EU's 10^25 FLOP line when you switch between the Commission's inclusion list, a wider list, and a GPU-hours/energy denominator. The intake also framed this as "is training compute even an additive quantity, or does it depend on an undeclared boundary?"
