## Restatement in my own words

As it now stands (V2 = Gate 1's reformulation R1, which is the clause I test): take general clinical laboratories that are accredited today, run automated analysers, and keep middleware audit trails from at least two vendors. Pick in advance a list of routine quantitative analytes for which the study material is documented to behave like a patient specimen. Send each laboratory the *same* material twice: once openly labelled as an external-quality-assessment sample, once smuggled in through the ordinary patient workflow by one pre-named, governance-approved route. For each analyte, how much higher is the share of results falling outside a pre-set acceptability band around a reference-method target when the laboratory does *not* know it is being tested? Also report, as primary results, what share of the smuggled specimens the staff spotted, and how much of the gap lines up with middleware-logged operator grade, repeat runs before release, and time to report.

In one line: *do laboratories score better on the exam they know they are sitting, by how much per analyte, and is it because declared samples get special handling?*

## The document that decides this entry

**LaMotte LC Jr, Guerrant GO, Lewis DS, Hall CT. "Comparison of Laboratory Performance With Blind and Mail-Distributed Proficiency Testing Samples." Public Health Reports 1977;92(6):554–560.** (PMID 200968. No DOI recovered; I do not assert one.)
**READ LEVEL: FULL TEXT**, one fetch, at stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/65385/cdc_65385_DS1.pdf. Authors taken from the **front matter as printed** on that PDF, not from anyone's short form. Caveat on my own read: the PDF was read through the fetch summariser; I did not independently inspect Tables 2 and 3, and the figures below are the summariser's extraction of the Results text with Tables 2–3 named as support.

**REGIME:** population = US laboratories performing urine drug-abuse screening for hospitals and methadone treatment centres (1973 arm, 22 laboratories; 1975 arm, nine per the text and "24 laboratories" per Table 3's heading — the paper is internally inconsistent here); unit of analysis = the **detection opportunity** (drug × sample × laboratory); operating condition = identical material sent twice, once mail-distributed and declared, once submitted *through hospitals or methadone centres as ordinary patient specimens*, **with fictitious patient identities in the 1975 arm**; instrument = mid-1970s drug-screening methods; threshold = correct qualitative detection of a drug known to be present; category = qualitative detection error. 1973 and 1975.

**What it prints, and where.** Per-arm paired counts per drug, in the Results text with Tables 2 and 3 as support. 1973: barbiturates mailed 110 of 110 opportunities detected, blind 110 opportunities with "only 79 correct detections" (31 missed); morphine mailed "110 opportunities", "missed only once", blind "111 opportunities", "the drug was missed 41 times"; methadone mailed 110 opportunities missed twice, blind "106 opportunities" missed 21 times; d-amphetamine blind "82 opportunities" with "only 39 correct detections". 1975 repeats the design over morphine and metabolite, barbiturates, methadone and metabolite, amphetamine and benzoylecgonine. Its summary sentence as served: *"the laboratories detected a greater percentage of the drugs in the mailed samples than in the blind samples."* The clause **asserts** the gap; it is not a denial and not a move away from the quantity.
**MY OWN ARITHMETIC on those printed counts** (labelled as mine; the paper prints no confidence intervals I was shown, so no uncertainty can be carried through): miss-rate difference blind minus declared ≈ **+28.2 pp** barbiturates, **+36.0 pp** morphine, **+18.0 pp** methadone, 1973 arm. **Internal consistency, checked before leaning on it:** the 1975 morphine blind counts (31 + 24 = 55) exceed the stated 45 opportunities; the 1975 methadone blind line as served contradicts the paper's own discussion; the 1975 laboratory count differs between text and table heading. So the 1975 arm cannot be decomposed from what it prints, and I lean only on the 1973 arm and on the design.
**What it does NOT do:** it does not measure the handling mechanism — the explanations it offers (staff reticence about reporting positives; routine specimens "processed en masse without strict quality controls") are, in the paper's own framing, assumptions from laboratory directors' statements, not measurements; and it does not report the share of blind specimens recognised by staff. Both of R1's added limbs are genuinely absent here.
**OVERLAP IN BOTH DIRECTIONS.** What LaMotte **misses** that the candidate includes: every quantitative analyte; error defined as a result outside an acceptability band around a **reference-method-assigned** target; the modern accredited-general-laboratory and automated/middleware regime; bias and imprecision as separate estimands; the concealment audit; the mechanism decomposition. What LaMotte **includes** that the candidate excludes: qualitative drug-of-abuse detection in 1970s drug-screening laboratories — which R1's restriction to "routine quantitative analytes" on automated analysers excludes outright.
**So I do NOT claim LaMotte prints the candidate's quantity.** Its numerator class is missed detection opportunities for a qualitatively present drug; the candidate's is results outside a quantitative acceptability limit. Its denominator is detection opportunities, not results, not specimens, not laboratories. What LaMotte settles is **that the question was asked, designed, executed and published** — and it settles that at full text, under its own title, which is the candidate's question in the field's own words.

**Second document opened: HHS DAB decision No. 1951 (2004).** READ LEVEL: full text via summariser. REGIME: a CLIA enforcement appeal upholding revocation of one laboratory's certificate for PT referral; the regulation is quoted as it stood in 2004, **not** as it stands in the 2026 eCFR, which I did not open. Short fragments as served (the summariser split them at its 125-character cap; I assemble only consecutive returns and mark this a reconstruction from two fragments per clause): §493.801(b)(1) — PT samples *"must be examined or tested with the laboratory's regular patient workload"* / *"by personnel who routinely perform the testing in the laboratory using the laboratory's routine methods"*, and *"The individual testing or examining the samples and the laboratory director must attest to the"* / *"routine integration of the samples into the patient workload using the laboratory's routine methods."* §493.801(b)(5) — records including the attestation statement *"documenting that [PT] samples were tested in the same manner as patient specimens"* (brackets as printed in the decision). §493.801(b)(4) — *"The laboratory must not send PT samples or portions of samples to another laboratory"*, with revocation *"for at least one year"* for an intentional referral. Scope of these sentences: they are **requirements imposed on the laboratory**, inside a decision about referral; they assert the identical-handling duty. They say nothing about blind submission by a third party in either direction.

**Third document opened, and it is a negative worth recording:** stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/84629 is **not** a blind-PT document at all — it is Earley MC, Astles JR, "Proficiency testing program providers respond to client concerns", MLO Med Lab Obs 2016;48(6):12–17 (CDC authors). READ LEVEL: full text. It contains no blind-versus-declared comparison, no error rate, and nothing on why blind PT is or is not used under CLIA; its only "blind" is blind-coding by a provider, a different concept. The record listed this item unopened and my own query D4 returned it; opening it cost one fetch and removes it from the unopened pile in the correct direction.

## Attack 4 in the required form — the conditioning clauses of R1 as it now stands, written out before grading

## What kind of document could still save this candidate, and whether I can name one

To overturn Leg 1 one would need a document showing that the *question* R1 asks is materially different from LaMotte's and Parsons' — not a document showing the *answer* is unknown in the modern regime, which I accept. The kind of document that would settle whether the residue is un-asked rather than merely unreplicated is a **reference list or review of declared-versus-blind EQA studies covering analytes other than blood lead and drugs of abuse**: it must cover clinical laboratories under an accreditation regime, must distinguish declared from covert submission, and must **print** or enumerate studies by analyte. The 1998 own-field review behind PubMed 9448012 is that kind of document, and its reference list has been opened by nobody including me — **but I am naming it from its indexing record and two readers' abstract-level reads, not from its type label or its contents, so this is a lead and partly a guess, and I say so.** The TB blinded-rechecking cluster from my query D1 is the other place a fourth group's measurement could be hiding; five openable items there are unopened, and I did not open them.
