## Restatement (my own words, not the generator's)

Take published protein-engineering campaigns that run more than one round, where each round's screened variants were measured at least twice, the paper says how many variants were screened and how many were kept, and the next round's library is built by mutating/recombining/surrogate-sampling around the kept ones. Two questions:

(a) If you compute, from round r's data only, the animal-breeding prediction "expected gain = selection intensity x accuracy of selection x among-variant SD" (accuracy = sqrt of replicate-based repeatability when you select on the assay, or the surrogate's prediction-measurement correlation when you select on a model), does that number match the actual shift in the *library mean* from round r to round r+1 — judged by calibration slope, bias, and interval coverage on a pre-declared, censoring-handled log scale?

(b) At each campaign's own well budget, would the breeding-theory-optimal split between "how many variants" and "how many replicates each" have been noticeably better (>=20% more predicted gain) than what the campaign actually did?

In short: *is one round of directed evolution a breeder's cycle, numerically, and were the wells split the way a breeder would have split them?*

## Recommended verdict

**Rejected** — Unresolved on attack 4 (presupposition failure) is fatal under Gate 4's rule; attack 2 only Partial, so novelty rests on arm (a), the arm the presupposition failure disables.

Strongest killing point: R-hat = i x rho x sigma_g with rho defined as sqrt(assay repeatability) assumes a selected variant's value passes intact to the library made from it; in mutagenesis- or surrogate-sampling-based rounds the realized change in library mean is dominated by the negative mean effect of newly introduced mutations, a first-order term the predictor has no slot for and that cannot be estimated from round-r data alone — so the calibration test is misspecified by construction and its (predictable) failure is uninformative.

Promotion path, if the generator wants one: re-pose arm (a) as estimating the *transmissibility* of a selected variant's measured value to its derived library (a protein parent-offspring regression), with i x rho x sigma_g as the no-load null; and re-pose arm (b) honestly as "do real campaigns' (n,m) splits deviate from the Cochran-Utz-Longin optimum", citing that prior art. Even then, presupposition 1 (>=20 qualifying campaigns) must be demonstrated by a corpus count before either arm is askable.
