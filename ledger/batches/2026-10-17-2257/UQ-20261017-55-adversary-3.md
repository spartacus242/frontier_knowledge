## Restatement (my words, not the record's)

Cells are almost always grown in a 37 °C box. Plenty of tissues are not at 37 °C in the living animal — testis and skin run cooler, working muscle and some birds run hotter. So: pick at least three cell types whose home tissue is reliably off 37 °C (some cooler, some warmer), grow each one four ways (its own tissue temperature vs 37 °C, crossed with low vs room oxygen), and measure how far each culture sits from the same cell type taken straight out of the animal, using a yardstick written down before any data exist. The number wanted is: how much of that gap does fixing the temperature close, per cell type, with error bars — printed next to how much fixing the oxygen closes on the identical yardstick in the identical experiment, plus whether the two fixes interact.

Two things to notice about that. First, the question is not "does temperature change cells" (settled, yes) but "is the inherited setpoint a named, estimable bias term in every published phenotype of a cool-or-warm-tissue cell type." Second, the whole rhetorical force comes from a comparison — temperature vs oxygen on one scale — and the record itself concedes the oxygen half of that comparison has never been computed either. The candidate is therefore asking for a two-armed measurement where neither arm exists, and justifying it by analogy to a critique literature (physioxia) that is itself qualitative.

## What the two opened papers actually say

**Gregg et al. 2023 (read in full).** Compared a "standard" 38 °C against a "physiological" 41 °C for primary broiler pectoralis major satellite cells, and measured the donors' own muscle internal temperature at **41.14 ± 0.08 °C** to set the physiological arm. States the widely used standard "may not be reproducing physiological conditions of the broiler SC niche in vivo," and that the point is "avoiding misrepresentations of in vitro experimental results when applying conclusions back to in vivo myogenesis." No comparison to a freshly isolated or in vivo cell profile; no fraction of any in vitro-to-in vivo gap computed; oxygen fixed at 18% throughout, so no oxygen arm and no factorial; doubling time was an *outcome*, not a matching variable, so no rate matching. Its "standard" is 38 °C, not 37 °C, and its rationale for that standard is protocol inheritance from murine work.

**Klein et al. 2022 (read; the record's PRIOR 1 and the load-bearing source for its silence claim).** Every temperature quote the record attributes to it checks out verbatim: "including temperature, typically adjusted to 37°C"; "It is implicitly assumed that culture temperature is controlled at 37°C"; "thermal regimes of cell cultures may be reliably inferred from calibrated incubators." It gives tissue-specific values for oxygen only ("O2 levels vary across human tissues and range from 13% O2 in the lung-pulmonary vein to 1–3% O2 in the uterus") and gives no in vivo temperature values, no tissue-matching recommendation and no challenge to 37 °C for any cell type. The record's central negative survives audit. **Two details the record did not report, and they cut against it:** Klein states "**42% reported temperature and CO2 incubator settings**", and notes that "**a number of studies used different culture temperatures**," which "highlights the need to declare and monitor the incubation temperature."

**van den Brink et al. 2017 (read).** The reference side of the candidate's distance metric has a documented artefact and it is a heat-shock/immediate-early-gene artefact: "Single molecule FISH analysis shows that Fos expression is induced during the SC isolation procedure"; "Hspa1b is also higher expressed in 2-hour collagenase-treated than in 1-hour collagenase treated cells"; "Dissociation time experiment confirms that the dissociation procedure influences the transcriptome of SCs"; "Similar Fos/Jun expressing subpopulations can also be found in other single-cell datasets." Its offered fixes are cell-selection fixes — "remove most of the dissociation-affected cells by selecting against MitoTracker-high cells during FACS" and "discarding cells with high FSC-H values" — and "The gating strategy successfully reduces the detected expression levels of HSP and IEG genes in bulk." **Scope note, stated honestly: the paper does not state a dissociation temperature** (it reports collagenase exposure times, 1 h vs 2 h), and it mentions neither cold-active protease nor nuclei-based alternatives. So I can source that the reference-side artefact lives on the HSP/IEG axis; I cannot source from anything I opened a quantification of how much the candidate's thermal arms move those same transcripts. The collinearity inference below is **my own**, labelled as such.

**Priors spot-check.** Klein 2022 and van den Brink 2017: verified by me, authors and quotes.
