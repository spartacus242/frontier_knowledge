## Gate 0 — Intake

Question (canonical, V1 as submitted):
Across the small bodies for which a direct mechanical interaction has now been recorded — Itokawa, 67P, Ryugu, Bennu and Dimorphos — how wide must a prediction interval derived from pre-encounter remote observables be to cover the in-situ determined near-surface strength, and is that interval narrow enough to discriminate the strength regimes that mission design actually distinguishes?

Question (canonical, V2 — the one Gate 1 reformulation, taken for well-posedness only; V2 is the version scored):
For the small bodies carrying both a dated pre-encounter remote-observable record and a post-encounter in-situ mechanical constraint (Itokawa, 67P, Ryugu, Bennu, Dimorphos), and under a measurand map and a reference-standard TIER declared per body before any numbers are pulled — tier A direct mechanical measurement, tier B one-sided bound, tier C parameter inverted through an impact- or slope-model — (a) reconstructing from dated pre-encounter publications only, what is the width of the narrowest honest prediction interval on near-surface cohesion (and on penetration resistance at a declared depth) that a pre-named estimator family would have issued: thermal inertia -> particle size -> cohesion, spin-limit cohesion, radar circular-polarisation ratio, boulder size-frequency, slope stability; (b) does that interval contain the post-encounter constraint, per body and per probing mode, with the sign of the error recorded; and (c) is the interval narrower than the gap between the strength regimes that sampling, landing and deflection design distinguish, with those thresholds quoted from mission requirement documents rather than assumed to exist?

Why V1 was reformulated: V1's phrase "the in-situ determined near-surface strength" presupposes a single commensurable measured quantity per body. One of the papers opened at Gate 2 (Rivkin/Barnouin et al. 2024, Nat Commun 15:6202) states the opposite in its own words for Dimorphos — the strength numbers there "reflect input parameter values for the strength scaling relationships, rather than a physically measured value of the strength of Dimorphos." V1 would therefore be answerable only by silently promoting a model input to a reference value. V2 fixes this by tiering the reference standard and by requiring the design thresholds in limb (c) to be quoted, not assumed. Both versions are retained.

Alternative formulations:
- What is the calibrated uncertainty of remote-sensing-based regolith strength prediction for small bodies, as distinct from its reported qualitative agreement with in-situ results?
- Could a pre-registered blind prediction of near-surface strength from remote data alone be stated with intervals tight enough to be a design input for Hera, Ramses or OSIRIS-APEX, and would it cover?

Motivating gap:
In-situ mechanical constraints on small-body surfaces are a seven-year-old class of measurement: the Hayabusa2 SCI crater and MASCOT at Ryugu, the OSIRIS-REx TAG that found near-zero cohesion and loose packing at Bennu (Walsh et al. 2022, Sci Adv), and DART at Dimorphos in 2022. Whether remote observables predicted them is therefore newly answerable. The comparison that exists is qualitative. Barnouin's 2024 ISAS curation-symposium abstract S41-3 (read in full at Gate 2; an abstract is all there is) says the techniques are "successful when compared to assessments of surface interactions" "in many instances," that SCI and TAG "demonstrate that the analyses derived from remote sensing work," and that Bennu and Ryugu regoliths are "effectively cohesionless" — with no error bars, no prediction intervals and no coverage statement; it names Bennu, Ryugu and Dimorphos but not Itokawa or 67P, and notes only that Ryugu sample densities "push the estimates of macro-porosity to smaller values" (20-30%). The stake is dated: Hera arrives at Didymos in 2026-27, and Ramses and OSIRIS-APEX must design Apophis operations in 2029 from remote data alone.

Presuppositions (each marked established / contested / partly false):
1. The in-situ and remote quantities refer to comparable measurands. CONTESTED, and the hardest part of the question. TAG penetration, SCI crater scaling and beta-derived cohesion probe different depths, volumes and strain rates; "strength" is a family, not a scalar, and the map between members must be declared in advance. V2 makes the map a declared object rather than an assumption.
2. Pre-encounter remote predictions can be reconstructed as they stood, without hindsight. CONTESTED but checkable from dated pre-encounter publications and mission design documents.
3. Five bodies suffice for a coverage statement. CONTESTED, and weaker than V1 implies: on the evidence opened here only Ryugu and Bennu plausibly reach tier A/B, Dimorphos is tier C by its own authors' statement, 67P rests on a single uncommanded Philae contact, and Itokawa's touchdown did not measure strength at all. The answer criterion must accept a sign and an order of magnitude over 2-3 usable bodies rather than a coverage rate over 5.
4. Mission design distinguishes strength regimes at a stateable resolution, so "narrow enough" has a referent. NOT VERIFIED HERE. Established in a general sense for sampling, landing and deflection, but the query aimed at named numeric thresholds (bearing capacity, cohesion, lander design requirement) returned lunar/Martian simulant geotechnics and a penetrometer-simulation paper with no stated design values. Limb (c) therefore carries a load-bearing presupposition this Gate 2 could not confirm.
5. In-situ determinations carry characterised uncertainty rather than order-of-magnitude bounds. PARTLY FALSE, and worse than the intake stated. Several are one-sided bounds (Didymos surface cohesion <=1 Pa, Dimorphos <0.9 Pa) and at least one is a model input presented with a five-decade range (crater strength Y = 10 to 10^5 Pa in Rivkin/Barnouin et al. 2024). These must be carried through as the tier they are.
6. Pre-encounter remote observables (thermal inertia, radar CPR, rotation-derived cohesion limits, boulder SFD) were available at comparable quality for each body. CONTESTED across the five; radar CPR and boulder SFD in particular were not available pre-encounter at comparable quality for 67P or Dimorphos.

## Gate 1 — Well-posedness

What would count as an answer:
A table, per body and per probing mode, of the pre-encounter remote prediction with its own stated uncertainty against the post-encounter constraint tagged by reference tier, giving a signed error in log units and an empirical containment count (not a "rate" dressed up from n<=5); plus a pre-registered blind prediction interval, deposited before arrival, for Hera at Didymos and then Apophis in 2029, so containment can be tested prospectively instead of retrofitted. The informative negative is stated in advance: intervals that contain the constraint but are already wider than the named design gap answer the question in the negative and tell mission designers to stop treating remote-derived strength as a design input. It is a question, not a thesis: both the positive and the negative are pre-specified and either is publishable.

## Gate 3 — Significance

Consequence (argued): If the answer is yes — reconstructed pre-encounter intervals both contain the constraints and are narrower than the design gap — then remote-derived strength becomes a defensible design input, and the specific decisions it would feed are dated: Ramses and OSIRIS-APEX must size Apophis surface operations in 2029 from remote data alone, with no prior encounter. If the answer is no, the consequence is sharper and is pre-stated: the review-literature sentence that remote analyses "work" must be restated as a sign-agreement claim, impact modellers who adopt cohesion and Y priors from remote inference (prior 2 shows the priors are chosen, and prior 4 shows Y ranging over five decades) lose the justification for narrowing them, and mission teams must design to the full regime envelope rather than to a nominal remote estimate. A third outcome is live and is what makes the question non-rhetorical: intervals that contain but are useless, which is an answer with an action attached.

Audience (specific, and the deflation named): (i) small-body mission design teams with dated decisions — ESA Hera and its Juventas/Milani deployables at Didymos in 2026-27, ESA Ramses and NASA OSIRIS-APEX at Apophis in 2029; (ii) planetary-defence impact modellers, who set strength and cohesion priors by hand and whose beta-to-cohesion inversion is non-unique by prior 2's own finding; (iii) the review community that writes the "remote sensing works" sentence, which is currently sourced to a conference abstract with no error analysis. The honest deflation: these teams may already design to envelopes rather than to nominal remote strength (TAGSAM was built for both hard and soft surfaces), in which case the negative tells them something they already practise. What survives the deflation is audience (ii) and (iii): a published containment count would change which priors are admissible in deflection modelling and would force a quantified restatement of a claim now carried qualitatively.

Fecundity: answering it makes askable (a) which single remote observable carries the strength information, since a containment table decomposes by estimator; (b) whether the strength family is reducible to fewer latent parameters than its members, i.e. whether the measurand map of presupposition 1 is low-rank; (c) whether tier-C model-inverted strengths should be admitted as reference values at all, which is a question about the epistemics of impact scaling rather than about asteroids; (d) whether deposited pre-arrival prediction intervals should become a standing mission-science norm, as blind-prediction exercises are in geotechnics.

Anchoring: the Ryugu macroporosity anomaly (sample densities pushing macroporosity to 20-30%, below what the rubble-pile appearance suggested — prior 3); the "unexpected surface" of Bennu, where thermal inertia implied fine regolith and the surface was boulder-strewn; the beta-to-cohesion degeneracy at Dimorphos (prior 2's non-uniqueness, prior 4's Y = 10 to 10^5 Pa); and the long-standing gap between spin-barrier cohesion estimates and the near-zero in-situ cohesion found by TAG.

Reason for silence (candidate mechanism — new instruments plus a disciplinary boundary plus an incentive gap, not "no one thought of it"):
1. New instruments, arriving serially. Every in-situ mechanical constraint dates from 2019-2024 and each was reported by its own mission team in its own paper. A coverage statement needs the set, and the set only closed with DART; no single team owned the cross-mission comparison, and the first attempt at it (prior 3) is a curation-symposium abstract, the lowest-stakes venue in the field.
2. A disciplinary boundary at exactly the method. Containment of a pre-declared interval is a geotechnical and statistical practice (Class A prediction, Ballina-style blind exercises, conformal intervals), confirmed live in query 6 and absent from the small-body literature in query 8. Planetary science reports agreement as "consistent with" and has no convention for publishing a signed prediction error; the bridging literature (asteroid geotechnics for ISRU) predates the data it would need.
3. An incentive gap, with the structure visible in the sources. The people who would aggregate are the people whose pre-encounter predictions would be audited — prior 3 and prior 4 share an author — and a published signed error documents one's own miss on a mission one led. The qualitative verdict is reputationally free; the quantitative one is not.
4. A reference-standard problem that makes the work arbitrate rather than measure. Prior 4 disqualifies its own Dimorphos strength numbers as measurements, so anyone computing coverage must first rule on which model's inversion counts as truth. That is a thankless adjudication, and declining it is cheaper than making it.

Scores: Well-posedness 2/3 · Significance 2/3

Well-posedness 2/3, with the deduction argued rather than asserted: V2 states an answer criterion, pre-specifies the informative negative, and tiers the reference standard, so it is answerable in principle. It loses a point for one load-bearing presupposition this Gate 2 could not confirm — presupposition 4, that mission design distinguishes strength regimes at a stateable numeric resolution. Limb (c)'s "narrow enough" has no verified referent: the query aimed squarely at named cohesion or bearing-capacity design thresholds returned none. If those thresholds exist only as qualitative regimes, limb (c) degrades from a measurement to a judgement call. Secondarily, the usable n is 2-3 rather than 5 once tiers are applied, so "coverage rate" must be downgraded to a containment count, which V2 does but which leaves the question answering less than its V1 wording promised.

Significance 2/3: consequence is real and dated on both branches and the negative is pre-stated with an action, but the primary named audience may already follow the negative's advice by designing to envelopes, so the change in practice lands mainly on impact modellers' priors and on the review literature's quantified restatement rather than on mission hardware requirements.

Strongest remaining objection (pre-Gate-4, recorded for the adversary): the question may be unanswerable at the resolution it asks for, not because the comparison is hard but because the reference standard does not exist. Prior 4 says in its own words that the Dimorphos strength numbers are scaling-relation inputs rather than measurements; several others are one-sided bounds. A containment count computed against bounds and model inputs measures the prediction against another prediction, and the exercise could reduce to comparing two model outputs with no ground truth anywhere in it — which is the dissolution risk, and the adversary should press it.
