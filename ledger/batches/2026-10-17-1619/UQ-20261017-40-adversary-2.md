## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Everybody who counts diagnostic errors counts them by having doctors read the chart afterwards. But the chart's later pages are partly a photocopy of the first doctor's guess: once a label is written it gets copied forward, and the tests that get ordered are the ones that label calls for. So the "right answer" the reviewer measures the first doctor against was partly written by the first doctor. The candidate asks: take one set of cases, adjudicate it twice — once normally, once with the index visit's diagnostic assertions and every later copy of them blacked out but genuinely new findings left in — and report how far the classified error rate moves, in which direction, with uncertainty, with a measured check that the blinding actually held, and with the same contrast run on cases that have an outside truth source (autopsy, external re-adjudication, independent registry) so the direction can be pinned down.

In one line: *is the yardstick for diagnostic error made partly out of the thing it is measuring, and by how much?*

Two things I notice immediately. (1) The question is a transplant: "was the reference standard read without knowledge of the index test?" is a stock appraisal question elsewhere. (2) The candidate's own escape hatch — that a sign reversal "falsifies P3 rather than answering the question" — means no outcome of the experiment can embarrass the question. Both go into the attacks.

"Effect of Prior Diagnoses on Dermatopathologists' Interpretations of Melanocytic Lesions: A Randomized Controlled Trial." JAMA Dermatol. 2022;158(9):1040–1047. doi:10.1001/jamadermatol.2022.2932.** READ (abstract/article page via publisher route; full text not read — flagged). Unit of analysis, quoted: *"The unit of analysis was a pair of diagnoses of a case by the same dermatopathologist in phases 1 and 2."* Manipulation, quoted: *"Each phase 2 case was randomly assigned to 1 of 3 conditions: PD+, PD−, and PD-absent"* — i.e. the reviewer was randomised to see a more severe prior diagnosis, a less severe one, or none.
