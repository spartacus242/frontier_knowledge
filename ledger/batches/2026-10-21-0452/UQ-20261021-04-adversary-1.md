## Restatement (my own words, before attacking)

Of all the clinical decisions a payer's own rules say need prior authorization, what share dies in the provider's office before anyone files anything with the payer? Then: across payers (and services, where a payer reports them), do payers whose published denial rate looks low turn out to have high pre-filing attrition — a negative correlation — and if you fold attrition back in, how often does the published "most obstructive payer" ordering flip, judged against a cutoff fixed before looking? Underneath it: is the CMS-0057-F public denial percentage, whose denominator is only requests that arrived, a measure of payer behaviour or partly a measure of how hard the payer makes filing?
