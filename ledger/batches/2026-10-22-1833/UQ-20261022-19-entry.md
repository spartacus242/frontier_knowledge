Domain(s): AI architecture — test-time compute, reasoning-model design and evaluation methodology

Question (canonical): Using a deployed model's thinking-budget dial as an exogenous manipulation on a fixed item set, how much of the reported accuracy-versus-reasoning-token relationship is a within-item causal dose-response and how much is between-item difficulty selection — and is a model capped at N reasoning tokens behaviourally equivalent to the same model naturally stopping at N?
Reformulation note: one Gate 1 reformulation was used and both versions are retained. The graded version splits the compound into (a) DECOMPOSITION — on a pinned item set, report the signed selection share (b_p − b_w)/b_p per difficulty stratum and per truncation convention, where b_w is the within-item slope of correctness on log reasoning tokens under randomly assigned forced budgets and b_p the pooled slope on naturally emitted length over the same items; and (b) EQUIVALENCE — on items where the model naturally emits approximately N tokens, test correctness-distribution equivalence between forced-at-N and natural-at-N at a pre-declared margin.

Alternative formulations:
- Is the test-time-scaling curve a treatment effect or a selection artefact of item difficulty, and does a forced cap behave like a natural stop?
- Confounding by indication for inference compute: harder items draw longer traces and also fail more, so what share of the pooled accuracy-length slope is dose and what share is difficulty selection?

Motivating gap: intake claimed exogenous within-one-model budget variation "makes a separation available that was not available before", at read level "title + URL only; I opened nothing". The gap does not survive.
