## Restatement (my own words, before reading the generator's framing again)

Run a managed-runtime program and record, for every heap object, the byte-offset at which it was allocated and the byte-offset at which it became unreachable, both on one clock (bytes allocated since program start). Lay the deaths out on a grid whose two coordinates are the object's age at death `a` and the time of death `p`, with each cell's exposure being the objects alive at that age at that time, so the cell quantity is a *rate* (deaths per object-at-risk) rather than a count. Because allocation epoch is `c = p − a` exactly, the three axes are linearly dependent and only their second differences (curvatures) can be estimated without an arbitrary constraint. Report: (i) whether the period-axis and cohort-axis curvature sequences differ from zero, with intervals; (ii) whether a pre-declared likelihood-ratio test rejects the submodel in which the rate depends on age alone; (iii) the deviance reductions from nesting the three axes, under every ordering, since no one ordering is privileged. Plain version: *is an object's instantaneous chance of dying a function of how old it is, or does it also depend on when in the run we are and on when the object was born?*

## Single strongest killing point

V2's declared inference cannot deliver the "age-only vindicated" outcome it advertises. Deaths in a managed heap arrive in simultaneous clusters spanning a wide range of ages — Veroy & Guyer §3.6, full text, verbatim: "While all objects in a garbage cluster die at the same time, they are all allocated at different times." — and Jones & Ryder cite "objects that are allocated together, die together"; so both off-age axes are qualitatively non-zero before any model is fitted, while the scored wording still fits a plain Poisson/GLM over cells of unspecified width and reports its CIs and its likelihood-ratio test as the answer. The empirical leg is sourced from a full-text read; the statistical leg — that clustering plus millions of cells makes rejection near-certain and the intervals too narrow — is **my own reasoning with no primary statistics source behind it**, which is why this is Partial and not a kill. If that leg fell, the verdict would still stand on attack 5's unpinned cell width, which is a reading of the question's own text.
